# Entrance — Flooring & Lighting

Part of [[01_Entrance/Entrance_Guide|Entrance Guide]].

## Heated Flooring: Usage-Dependent, Not a Default Win

Konstantin Kruglov recommends moisture-resistant quartz-vinyl or
porcelain stoneware for entryway flooring (never laminate/parquet/
engineered board — an entryway seasonally tracks in snow, de-icing
chemical residue, dust, or mud that wood-based flooring can't tolerate),
and specifically recommends electric heated floor here to actively dry
residual moisture. [source: [[_Sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]

Two of eight households featured in one INMYROOM TV compilation
independently recommend heated entryway flooring for the same reason
(speeds drying of wet/snowy shoes) plus a second reason (a pass-through
entry corridor is otherwise noticeably cold underfoot) — one explicitly
regretted skipping it. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xPxusEh-gow_inmyroom_top8_entryways|YT_xPxusEh-gow]]]

**Against this**, a household in a different INMYROOM TV compilation
installed heated flooring in three zones (bathroom, entryway, insulated
balcony) but reports never actually using the entryway zone in practice —
their shoes don't get wet/dirty enough to need active drying, and the
measured monthly electricity cost was "quite expensive." [source: [[_Sources/YT_jxx99px88HA_inmyroom_4_entryways_styles|YT_jxx99px88HA]]]

**Synthesis**: three independent households favor heated entryway
flooring and one independent household found it not worth running —
the deciding variable each side points to is the household's own real
shoe-wetness/dirt exposure (climate, how muddy/snowy the entry actually
gets) weighed against the ongoing electricity cost, not a property of
the flooring choice itself. Treat "always install heated entryway
flooring" as a leaning, not a rule.

## Threshold-Free Material Transitions

A level, threshold-free transition between two different entry floor
materials (porcelain tile + engineered wood board) can be achieved with a
liquid-cork joint rather than a conventional metal/plastic transition
strip — avoids a raised trip hazard entirely and keeps a flush,
single-plane floor. Independently corroborated by a second household
using a "cork-compensator" joint between tile and wood/laminate — same
underlying idea, two independent sources. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xPxusEh-gow_inmyroom_top8_entryways|YT_xPxusEh-gow]]; [[_Sources/YT_oFGkW7wZsLo_inmyroom_top5_khrushchevka_entryways|YT_oFGkW7wZsLo]]]

Kruglov additionally recommends a rigid (not soft/flexible) baseboard for
an entryway specifically, so debris/slush can't migrate under a gap.
[source: [[_Sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]

## Lighting

- **Deliberately over-light an entryway rather than under-light it** —
  never rely on a single fixture. [source: [[_Sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]
- **Battery-powered, motion-sensor LED puck lights (no wiring required)**
  mounted inside entryway storage/shelving — turn on automatically on
  entry, auto-off after a timeout; a low-cost no-wiring lighting option
  for a storage niche. [source: [[_Sources/YT_oFGkW7wZsLo_inmyroom_top5_khrushchevka_entryways|YT_oFGkW7wZsLo]]]
- **Add an electrical outlet near in-closet shoe storage for a boot/shoe
  dryer** — near-zero incremental cost, avoids drying wet footwear on a
  balcony or in a living space. [source: [[_Sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]
- **Hide an electrical panel behind a removable decorative wall panel
  built with a ~10cm air gap** specifically for panel ventilation
  (prevents overheating once enclosed) — the gap also doubles as small
  storage niches on each side. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xPxusEh-gow_inmyroom_top8_entryways|YT_xPxusEh-gow]]]

## Mirrors

A full-height (full-body) mirror is close to a universal recommendation
across this batch's sources: Kruglov's rule ("flexible on placement —
wall-mounted, recessed, on a wardrobe door leaf, or on the entrance
door's own interior face — but full-body view is the requirement"); a
door-leaf-mounted mirror as a specific space-saving solution for a small
entryway (a second household, independently); and Olga Kachanova's
equivalent full-wall bathroom mirror deliberately chosen to visually
expand a small room, corroborating the same underlying technique in a
different room.

**Mirror orientation is a separate question from whether to include
one**: Anuta Vlady notes a mirror on a corridor's *long wall* expands its
*length* specifically, which can make an already-long hallway feel
endless — orient/place a mirror to expand width instead if length is
already the corridor's problem, not its asset. See
[[02_Hallway/analysis/Layout_and_Zoning|Hallway — Layout & Zoning]] for the
full proportion-control context this recommendation sits inside.

## Visual Design Techniques

- **Tall doors (2.70m, floor-to-ceiling)** make ceiling height read as
  taller than it is — cited independently by two different INMYROOM
  TV-featured households as a deliberate visual technique. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xPxusEh-gow_inmyroom_top8_entryways|YT_xPxusEh-gow]]]
- **In a small entryway, prefer bold/dark colors plus mirrors over
  defaulting to light colors** to visually enlarge the space — explicitly
  stated as a counter to the common assumption that small spaces need
  light colors. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xPxusEh-gow_inmyroom_top8_entryways|YT_xPxusEh-gow]]]
- **Dark ceilings matching the wall color** are a deliberate technique for
  a cozier, enclosed feel in a small entryway — extends the bold/dark-
  color-for-small-spaces technique to ceilings specifically. [source: [[_Sources/YT_oFGkW7wZsLo_inmyroom_top5_khrushchevka_entryways|YT_oFGkW7wZsLo]]]
- **Don't treat wavy/uneven walls in an older building as a defect
  requiring full re-plastering** — one project trued corners only where
  built-in furniture needed a flat surface, left the rest wavy after
  cleaning/painting; faster/cheaper with no stated quality-of-life cost.
  [source: [[_Sources/YT_oFGkW7wZsLo_inmyroom_top5_khrushchevka_entryways|YT_oFGkW7wZsLo]]]
- **A wallpaper or finish accent deliberately reserved for the entry/
  corridor zone specifically**, reasoning stated directly: "a corridor
  takes up square meters and gives no beauty by default, so this is
  where we decided to add real visual impact" — a generalizable design-
  allocation rationale for treating a hallway as worth a deliberate finish
  choice rather than an afterthought. [source: [[_Sources/YT_YarbTzTfq6I_interior_nalitso_old_fund_replan_case|YT_YarbTzTfq6I]]]
- **Entrance-door reveals/jambs are the single most vulnerable wall
  surface in an entryway** (repeated bag/parcel scuffing) — protect with
  decorative plaster (the most durable non-tile finish) or a protective
  wood panel/trim; decorative plaster generally recommended for the
  entryway's other walls too, since the total area (~5-6 m²) makes the
  added cost negligible against a full renovation budget. [source: [[_Sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]
- Match an entryway's accent color to an already-chosen accent color
  elsewhere in the apartment for intentional whole-home visual
  continuity. [source: [[_Sources/YT_jxx99px88HA_inmyroom_4_entryways_styles|YT_jxx99px88HA]]]
- Always physically test a specified paint color at full scale on the
  actual wall before finalizing it, even when the color was already fixed
  in an approved design project — real on-site lighting/reflection can
  make a color read noticeably differently than the design swatch.
  [source: [[_Sources/YT_jxx99px88HA_inmyroom_4_entryways_styles|YT_jxx99px88HA]]]
- Weathered/pre-aged wood cladding is a durability choice for a small,
  heavy-traffic entryway wall — paint/wallpaper wears and gets dirty
  regardless of care taken, while cladding is far more durable. [source: [[_Sources/YT_jxx99px88HA_inmyroom_4_entryways_styles|YT_jxx99px88HA]]]
