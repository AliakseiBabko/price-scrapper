## Durable Facts

- **Relocating a "wet point"** (e.g. moving a kitchen to a different room than the developer's original layout) **requires a technical replanning project and approval from the local исполком (executive committee)** and other relevant authorities before work can proceed. `confirmed` (level 1, directly spoken). [source: yt__hYkf4UkCqw]
- **Merging two separate bathrooms, each on its own plumbing riser, typically means one riser is capped** ("глушится") while the other remains in use and absorbs the combined plumbing load. `confirmed` (level 1). [source: yt__hYkf4UkCqw]
- **There is a specific height threshold above which a suspended/wall-hung fixture installation (e.g. a wall-hung toilet built into a false wall) cannot be approved/raised without going through the replanning approval process** — plumbing stacks above that height can't be freely relocated without authorization. `confirmed` that this threshold exists and matters (level 1, stated as the host's own practical experience); the **actual height value was never stated in the source** — see Numeric Data below. [source: yt__hYkf4UkCqw]

## Rules / Heuristics

- Before planning any wet-zone relocation (moving a kitchen or bathroom to a different room) in a Minsk apartment, budget time and cost for a technical replanning project and исполком approval — this is not optional paperwork, it gates whether the work can proceed at all. [source: yt__hYkf4UkCqw]
- When merging two bathrooms sharing separate risers, plan on one riser being capped rather than assuming both remain active. [source: yt__hYkf4UkCqw]
