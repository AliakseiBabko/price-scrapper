# Renovation Budgeting Master Guide

Airplane-view guide to renovation budgeting. For row-level pricing and sourced detail, see the case studies linked at the bottom — this page deliberately stays high-level.

## 1. What Must Be Budgeted

Build the budget from the physical space, not from guesswork:
1. **Layout first** — a plan (Homestyler, AutoCAD) fixes fixture/cabinet/outlet positions before anything is priced.
2. **Confirm the design project package is complete** — a full package should include dimensioned as-built, demolition, and clean (unfurnished) montage plans; wall elevations cross-checked against the plans; visualizations for sign-off; and node/section drawings for junction details. MEP (electrical/HVAC) engineering is typically a separate deliverable, not assumed to be included by default. *(secondary reference — see intermediate knowledge store)*
3. **Room-by-room breakdown** — group all costs (finishes, furniture, engineering) by room.
4. **Volumes** — m² of floor/ceiling/wall, perimeter for baseboards, counts for doors/windows/fixtures.
5. **Target tiers** — set a class benchmark (basic/comfort/premium) rather than pricing item-by-item first. See [[11_Budget_and_Planning/analysis/Budget_Tiers_Cheap_Optimal_Premium|Budget Tiers: Cheap / Optimal / Premium]] for a worked cheap/optimal/premium breakdown across 11 systems (ceiling, electrical, walls, tile, flooring, baseboard, window reveals, doors, ventilation, smart home, plumbing) — the tier per system need not match; watch for genuine technical dependencies between systems (not just taste) before mixing tiers on physically-connected choices.
6. **Buffer** — add 10–15% to the total.
7. **Verify and optimize** *before* demolition (e.g. laminate over parquet), not after.

## 3. What Drives Cost

- **Timeline pressure** — compressing the schedule (e.g. "turnkey in 3 months") forces premium/weekend-work pricing. See [[11_Budget_and_Planning/Renovation_Sequence|Renovation Sequence]] for the high-level sequencing logic and why order matters, and [[11_Budget_and_Planning/analysis/Project_Duration_and_Scheduling|Project Duration & Scheduling]] (added 2026-08-25) for a full realistic-duration breakdown — three independent turnkey companies converge on 6-9 months as the real floor for a design-project renovation, with the underlying material-curing-time mechanism explained in detail.
- **Demolition/re-planning** — every wall moved is paid for four times (demolish, haul, rebuy, rebuild); working with the existing layout is the single biggest lever. [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Sourcing choices** — retail vs. wholesale materials, direct factory sourcing, and procurement specialists can shift costs significantly without changing the design. See §5 below for concrete tactics. [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Built-in vs. surface-mounted installs** — the visible device-price gap between a surface-mounted and a built-in fixture is often much smaller than the real installed-cost gap once pipework, fittings, and labor are counted; price the full install, not just the fixture. *(secondary RUB-pricing reference, not Minsk-comparable — see intermediate store)* [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Stretch ceiling vs. drywall (GKL) — a risk factor, not just a cost one** — beyond being cheaper, a stretch ceiling can contain water from a leak above long enough to limit damage; a drywall ceiling saturates and lets water through to furniture/floors/walls below. *(secondary reference, single-account — see intermediate store)* [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_zemskov_premium_class_tips_71691249.txt|20260804_zemskov_premium]]]
- **A personalized, high-end renovation rarely pays for itself at resale** — a good interior is generally *not* an investment that returns its cost; tying renovation budget to apartment price as a fixed percentage is a common but flawed rule of thumb, since material/labor cost doesn't actually track the property's own price. **Corroborated independently by three unrelated practitioners** — treat as a reasonably solid pattern, not one opinion. *(secondary reference — see intermediate store)* [source: [[_Sources/YT_6Z7uH2_rXsw_buro_segment_pricing_2026|6Z7uH2_rXsw_buro_segmen]]]
- **"Wishes" (a specific named product/brand target) and "expectations" (a described functional requirement) each drive cost independently of overall budget tier** — either can swing a single line item's cost by up to 10×. Relatedly, small execution/detail decisions ("nuances" — e.g. a material-junction detail, a finish pattern) compound: individually minor, but dozens of them across a project can shift total cost by a meaningful percentage. Get specific about both before pricing, not just the overall quality tier. *(secondary reference — see intermediate store)* [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **AC/climate system placement and drainage are easy to get wrong** — indoor units need clearance above/below for airflow and shouldn't blow directly on occupants; condensate drainage needs a proper dry-trap valve, not a straight sewer connection or a simple water trap (a plain trap's water eventually evaporates, losing its seal). *(technical reference only — the source's pricing is from 2017 and not usable for current budgeting; see intermediate store)* [source: [[_Sources/YT_H61xa8n2nTk_flatart_ac_installation_guide|H61xa8n2nTk_flatart_ac_i]]]
- **"Ликвидность" (resale liquidity/broad marketability) as a distinct planning concept from ROI, per Kruglov/Ontario (added 2026-08-28)** — a good renovation should stay broadly appealing/resalable for years regardless of current sale intentions, since circumstances can change; this is a design-toward-broad-appeal argument, **not the same claim** as this guide's existing "a personalized renovation rarely pays for itself at resale" caution above (that one is about financial ROI not tracking spend; this is about design choices staying marketable over time) — worth tracking alongside it, not merged into it. A companion framing from a second same-channel source: distinguish "недорогой" (cost-efficient) from "дешёвый" (cheap/low-quality) — the goal is controlling cost and preserving resale value through stylistic choices, not simply minimizing spend. [source: [[_Sources/YT_x7wiBaReFN8_kruglov_quality_reno_2024|YT_x7wiBaReFN8]], [[_Sources/YT_dBn4nhn8d9c_kruglov_budget_stylish_reno_2024|YT_dBn4nhn8d9c]]]

- **Source year controls currency conversion** — never convert historical prices at today's rate; use the source's own year (see [[00_Master/exchange_rates_reference|exchange_rates_reference]]).
- **Region matters** — Minsk/Belarus pricing is the primary baseline here; Russian/RUB data is secondary reference only, not Minsk-equivalent (e.g. a RUB-denominated contractor-interview source, dated April 2025, uploaded by a Moscow-based channel with its own price-per-m² tier vocabulary — the uploader's location is not the same as a confirmed claim about which region's market the prices describe; see intermediate store for the full breakdown. Not assumed equivalent to the Minsk figures above.). [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Sequence matters as much as price** — some decisions (concealed door frames, shadow baseboards, partition build order) must be locked in before rough works start or become expensive to change later — see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/Renovation_Sequence|Renovation Sequence]].
- **Confirm the budget is real before setting a tier** — clarify how much of a stated total is guaranteed cash in hand versus loans, credit lines, or future income not yet earned; a budget that shrinks mid-project is a common source of disputes. [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Know the difference between a plumbing cladding box and a ventilation shaft** — a "zashivka" (cladding built around risers, sometimes oversized) can often be rebuilt more compactly to reclaim space; a "venshakhta" (ventilation shaft, identifiable by grille openings) runs as a single shared column from ground floor to roof serving every apartment on that riser line and must never be touched or damaged. *(secondary reference — see intermediate store)* [source: [[_Sources/YT_QHl1YEHMfgE_doma_minska_severny_bereg_ep2_layout|QHl1YEHMfgE_doma_minska_]]]
- **Vet a contractor before committing, then keep verifying after** — check genuine (non-arranged) reviews, visit a real completed past project in person, and review the actual signed contract before hiring; once work starts, use independent technical supervision (an inspector unaffiliated with the contractor, signing off stage-by-stage) to catch defects before the next stage builds over them. *(secondary reference — see intermediate store)* [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Watch for a construction-middleman scam pattern (added 2026-08-19)**: an operator with no real crew or verifiable track record, using a generic/boilerplate contract, quoting a large upfront deposit (documented pattern: 40-50% of total project value) relative to any actual work-in-progress, and an office that turns out to be borrowed/rented by the hour rather than genuinely occupied — ask directly to confirm a contractor's office and track record are real, not just visited once. `single-account`, `unverified` methodology, but a checkable red-flag pattern. *(secondary reference — see intermediate store)* [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_first_shopping_list_consumables_78f45b8e.txt|20260804_first_shopping_]]]
- **Insist on a smeta priced by outcome and fully itemized, not by vague or raw-quantity line items** — water/heating rough-in priced by fixture "points" (not pipe length) and electrical rough-in priced by floor area (not cable length) both close off a common upsell pattern ("turned out we needed more, please pay extra"). A vague one-line entry like "wall putty" can hide up to ~9 real sub-steps (priming, base coat, sanding, mesh, finish coat, more sanding/priming) — demand them itemized so you can verify what was actually done. The same applies to flooring installation pattern (straight/diagonal/herringbone): get it specified before work starts, since pattern materially changes labor cost. *(secondary reference — see intermediate store)* [source: [[_Sources/YT_cdNwbqsLUK4_remonthochu_smeta_methodology|cdNwbqsLUK4_remonthochu_]]]
- **A named taxonomy of smeta wording-ambiguity traps, per Kruglov/Ontario's own 4-year "professional smeta analysis" service (added 2026-08-28)** — distinct from the itemized-vs-vague-line-item caution above: even a fully itemized smeta can hide a scam in the *wording* of an individual line. Named traps: a screed or plaster line capped at a stated thickness ("up to 5cm"/"up to 2cm") with no quoted rate above that band or for a ceramzite-backfill/reinforcement-mesh variant; a "ceramic tile" floor line that silently becomes a much pricier "porcelain" (керамогранит) line once the client's own purchased tile arrives — since virtually 100% of real floor tile sold is porcelain, not true ceramic (worked example: a 2,000 RUB/m² ≈$20/m² "ceramic" line, up-charged to as much as 8,000 RUB/m² ≈$90/m², settled at 5,000 RUB/m² ≈$60/m²); a tile-size band ("up to 60cm long side") ambiguous about whether a 60×60cm tile itself is inside or outside it, with no rate for larger formats a design project might actually call for; and a flooring-installation-*method* ambiguity distinct from this guide's existing pattern (straight/diagonal/herringbone) caution — the same material installed floating (≈1,000 RUB/m² ≈$10/m²), glued (≈2,000 RUB/m² ≈$20/m²), or glued-to-plywood (≈3,000 RUB/m² ≈$30/m²) are three different prices a design project frequently doesn't specify between. **Mitigation stated for all of these**: request quoted rates above any stated band, and confirm installation method/size range explicitly, before signing. Two further named gaps: wall area (unlike floor area) has no independent document a client can cross-check it against, making it a common place to quietly under-count volume; and a bathroom smeta can list gypsum plaster on a wet-zone wall while omitting the waterproofing line that should accompany it. Converted at the trailing-6-month USD/RUB mean before 2025-06-27 (87.0015 RUB/USD). [source: [[_Sources/YT_rzpkTJYsY0Q_kruglov_smeta_top_mistakes|YT_rzpkTJYsY0Q]]]
- **⚠️ Rough-work-inflated/finish-work-deflated bait-and-switch, with a named sabotage-to-get-fired mechanism (Kruglov/Ontario, added 2026-08-28)** — a more granular mechanism than this guide's existing lowball-then-inflate schemes above: a competing smeta's *total* looks only modestly cheaper while its internal rough/finish split is inverted (rough-stage work overpriced by roughly the amount the finish stage is underpriced). Once the client signs at the lower total and pays through the rough stage — the stage where a construction company's real profit concentrates — the crew deliberately delivers poor-quality finish work (the source's example: intentionally crooked tile-laying) to get fired before doing the unprofitable finish-stage work at its quoted rate. **The trap closes because no other contractor will pick up an abandoned finish-stage object at the original smeta's low finish price** (it's worth meaningfully more), leaving the client stuck mid-project. Practical takeaway: the single most important vetting question is whether a contractor will complete a project start-to-finish, not just the lowest headline total. [source: [[_Sources/YT_rzpkTJYsY0Q_kruglov_smeta_top_mistakes|YT_rzpkTJYsY0Q]]]
- **⚠️ Two sharper smeta-wording variants and a new example, per a second Kruglov/Ontario source (added 2026-08-28)** — a concrete electrical cable-length sanity-check ratio: for roughly 50 outlets/switches, a realistic cable run is ≈450-500 running meters; a smeta quoting only ≈100 running meters for the same point count is inadequate. A more specific screed-thickness standard than the banding trap above: only two real screed-thickness standards exist — 2cm on a hard/rigid base (concrete/monolithic slab) and 4cm on a soft base (keramzit, soundproofing, waterproofing) — worked example: a 4cm floor-height differential means the low point needs 4cm+2cm=6cm, already outside a smeta line reading "screed up to 3cm," triggering an extra per-centimeter charge. A genuinely new slippery-wording example: a wallpaper line reading "installation without pattern matching" (без подбора) is misleading since almost 100% of real (non-paint-ready) wallpaper requires pattern matching per its own label (repeat interval typically 10-30cm) — virtually no real wallpaper qualifies as needing no matching, so this wording is bait for an under-quote. [source: [[_Sources/YT_lPmjWTwNVQA_kruglov_contractor_scams|YT_lPmjWTwNVQA]]]

See [[11_Budget_and_Planning/analysis/Estimate_and_Contract_Templates|Estimate & Contract Templates]] for the full breakdown — this fills what had been a genuine gap (smeta-literacy guidance existed above, but no template *structure* to build one from).

Business-class work typically specifies Q3 or Q4 (see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/Renovation_Sequence|Renovation Sequence]]'s Pre-Finish Stage Acceptance Checklist for what each tier actually means and which finishes need which tier). `single-account`. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_business_class_five_attributes_19385e7a.txt`]

### Drawing Construction Plans: the "Floating Dimension" Technique (added 2026-08-19)

**⚠️ On any construction drawing with a chain of dimensioned wall segments (a reveal, an opening, another reveal, a wall thickness, and so on), specify only the dimensions that are genuinely functionally critical — and deliberately leave one non-critical segment's width unlabeled.** A crew builds outward from whichever reference point is easiest for them (typically a load-bearing wall, once they've decided where the plaster layer will sit); if every segment in the chain is dimensioned, cumulative construction/plastering tolerance error compounds through the whole chain and eats into exactly the *last* dimensioned segment — often the one that most needs to stay firm (a wardrobe's depth, say). **Fix**: dimension only the true anchor points (worked example: "600mm offset from this corner" and "150mm wall thickness" on one sheet; "500mm reveal" and "800mm opening width" on a separate openings sheet) and leave one segment's width deliberately unlabeled — its only implicit requirement is fitting its own minimum function (e.g. a door casing). Since the crew has no target to build that segment to, all real-world tolerance error lands there instead of consuming a dimension you actually need held. **A general, broadly reusable technique for any DIY construction drawing this project produces**, not specific to one room. `single-account`, `unverified` methodology, but directly applicable. *(secondary reference — see intermediate store)* [source: [[_Sources/YT_78FeaeMZHOI_2.5m_thanks_developer_275|78FeaeMZHOI_2.5m_thanks_]]]

### Tracking Expenses (added 2026-08-19)

**Track expenses through a dedicated running spreadsheet from day one, not retroactively from a pile of receipts.** Five methods exist, ranked worst to best: doing nothing and reconstructing from memory (worst — loses most information); collecting receipts and tallying at the end (misses every cash/no-receipt payment — informal demolition labor, movers, market deliveries — and can't answer "how much so far" mid-project); delegating tracking to the crew/contractor (risky — a direct incentive to pad receipts, and it degrades back into the receipt-pile method at reporting time anyway); a fixed-price rough-materials arrangement with the contractor (creates an incentive for them to buy cheaper materials and pocket the difference — only safe with a highly trusted company backing a long warranty); and a dedicated spreadsheet system, tracked live. **A spreadsheet built around 4 blocks works well**: Primary Expenses (rough/finish materials, electrical, plumbing, windows, doors), Secondary Expenses (appliances, furniture, lighting, accessories — deferrable up to a year post-move-in), Works (contracted total / paid-to-date / remaining), and Balance (cash handed over vs. spent vs. still on hand) — plus a dedicated Payments log where **every payment is tied to a memorable event** ("handed over 200,000 RUB when we came to inspect the tub install") for later dispute-prevention, and an Overrun sheet isolating the exact price delta whenever a planned item gets swapped for a pricier one (e.g. laminate → parquet), so budget overruns are traceable to specific decisions instead of a vague "we're over somewhere" feeling. `single-account`, `unverified` methodology, but a directly actionable practice for this project's own self-managed model. *(secondary reference — see intermediate store)* [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]

## 5. Cost-Saving Strategies

Each cost-saving bullet below names its source where the extraction note is decisive; otherwise it begins with `channel not confirmed` rather than inferring a channel from filename or company context.

Tactical moves for trimming budget without cutting quality (compressed from 13 sourced strategies — see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/analysis/cost_saving_strategies_full|Detailed strategy notes]] for full sourcing and figures):

- **Talk to neighbors before over-investing in soundproofing** — shared-wall soundproofing may already be covered on their side. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_zemskov_premium_class_tips_71691249.txt|20260804_zemskov_premium]]]
- **Consider soundproofing the upstairs neighbor's floor instead of your own ceiling (added 2026-08-04)** — one practitioner's stated preference: ceiling soundproofing adds a real cost (200,000–500,000 RUB cited on an 88.3 m² apartment) and every later ceiling penetration (a vent, an electrical mount) degrades it; a leak from above is also worse under a stretch ceiling with hidden soundproofing (traps water/mold) than under a bare stretch ceiling (drop it, dry it, re-hang it). Paying to soundproof the floor above achieves the same acoustic result without either drawback — though it depends on the upstairs neighbor's cooperation, unlike ceiling work on your own unit. Both are still done together in premium-tier work, per the same source. `single-account`. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_zemskov_premium_class_tips_71691249.txt`]
- **Wall soundproofing protects the neighbor from you, not just you from the neighbor (added 2026-08-04)** — a business-class framing worth weighing alongside the point above: one cited client initially declined soundproofing, confident it didn't matter to him personally, then added it after renovation specifically because a neighbor complained about noise from his side. Soundproofing choices affect both directions, not just the occupant's own comfort. `single-account`. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_business_class_five_attributes_19385e7a.txt`]
- **Skip protecting a window during rough/dirty work if its full replacement is already planned later in the schedule (added 2026-08-04)** — ideally after the messiest work (e.g. after painting) — avoids paying for protection material that gets discarded anyway once the window is swapped. `single-account`. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_never_take_this_from_masters_70m2_4b3c72b6.txt`]
- **Limit demolition/re-planning** — every wall moved is paid for four times; prefer smart zoning (slats/panels) over new walls. [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Don't rush the timeline** — compressed schedules directly inflate cost (see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/Renovation_Sequence|Renovation Sequence]]).
- **Use a procurement specialist ("комплектатор")** for expensive designer fixtures — often finds near-identical alternatives for a fraction of the cost. [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Buy rough materials wholesale**, e.g. via a contractor who supplies materials, rather than retail. [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Skip non-structural wardrobe backing panels** and order direct factory board-cutting — cuts custom millwork cost ~30%. [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Source lighting directly from overseas factories** — cuts hardware cost 50–70% vs. local resellers. [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Choose a 2-in-1 washer-dryer combo** over a separate dryer stack in comfort-class/space-constrained projects. [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Use online marketplaces (e.g. OZON)** for standard loose furniture items instead of custom/designer pieces. [source: [[_Sources/YT_BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_budgeting_interview|BMmPLHVmnqw_renovation_b]]]
- **Spray-paint existing PVC window frames** instead of replacing them (~$730 vs. $3,000+). [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_never_take_this_from_masters_70m2_4b3c72b6.txt|20260804_never_take_this]]]
- **Substitute stretch ceilings and paintable wallpaper** for drywall ceilings and smooth painted walls — 40–60% labor/material savings. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_zemskov_premium_class_tips_71691249.txt|20260804_zemskov_premium]]]
- **Pay for rough materials directly through verified distributors**, not contractor cash advances, to avoid markup/fraud risk. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_first_shopping_list_consumables_78f45b8e.txt|20260804_first_shopping_]]]
- **Consider a "micro-baseboard" instead of a fully concealed baseboard** — approximates the concealed look at a fraction of the installation cost and schedule time, trading a small amount of visual fidelity. *(secondary reference — see intermediate store)* [source: [[_Sources/YT_DsdLa87Acz4_prolife_invest_moscow_flipping|DsdLa87Acz4_prolife_inve]]]
- **Question any "free" site consumables offer (added 2026-08-04)** — a temporary toilet/sink/shower and a wall-chasing dust extractor are billable customer consumables in reality, not free; companies advertising them as free are reported to mark up materials elsewhere by roughly 2x to cover the cost. A related concrete line item: hardboard sheeting to protect common-area walls during move-in/material delivery is worth budgeting for directly (~5,000 RUB) rather than risking a ~10,000 RUB damage-repair bill if skipped. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_first_shopping_list_consumables_78f45b8e.txt`]
- **Split rough and finish contracting into two separate hires (added 2026-08-04)** — the rough phase (demolition, framing, rough electrical/plumbing/heating, floor sound/waterproofing, screed) is the technically complex phase worth paying an experienced specialist crew for; the finish (cosmetic) phase is comparatively simple and can be done by a cheaper crew afterward without materially harming quality, since a well-executed rough phase is hard for a lesser finish crew to mess up. One company's own cited Moscow 2023 rough-only pricing: ~20,000 RUB/m² labor + ~13,000–16,000 RUB/m² rough materials over ~3–4 months, then ~2 more months for a cheaper finish crew. `single-account`, and note the company pitching this also profits more from selling the finish phase separately — weigh the incentive alongside the strategy. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_split_rough_finish_contracting_and_rough_stage_checklist_9a4efa5e.txt`]
- **When budget is tight, sequence spending by how costly each item is to redo later, not just by nominal price (added 2026-08-15)** — fully fund and complete concealed/"fundamental" work first (wiring, plumbing, tile, windows), because reopening finished surfaces to fix any of these later is far more expensive than doing it right the first time; defer non-structural, purchasable-later items instead — a stretch ceiling can be added weeks/months after move-in, door casings and the doors themselves can wait until last, and a kitchen can typically be delayed up to roughly six months without disrupting anything already finished. Framed by the source as a normal budget-stretching strategy, not a sign of a compromised project. `single-account`. [source: `_Sources/YT_FO9mQWUeSuA_best_solutions_4_094_client_qa_tour.md`]
- **Cutting and reinforcing a load-bearing-wall opening yourself (materials + independent labor) instead of hiring a specialized crew (added 2026-08-17)** — one practitioner's real cost comparison: a specialized company quoted **>50,000 RUB** for one opening (2019 Moscow pricing); DIY materials (steel angle, rebar, welding consumables) cost only **~15,000 RUB**, a >3x gap. The source recommends hiring cheaper independent labor to execute the DIY-priced version rather than doing the physical work personally — the real saving comes from the materials/company-markup gap, not from eliminating labor cost. Requires replanning approval *before* cutting (some openings are structurally prohibited regardless of reinforcement) and careful technique (see `13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Walls_and_Paint.md`'s Load-Bearing Wall Opening section) — not a shortcut to skip approval or reinforcement. `single-account`, `unverified`, 2019 prices — do not blend with more recent RUB figures without inflation adjustment. [source: `_Sources/YT_DHjZm9lugow_load_bearing_wall_opening_technique_017.md`]

- **Deliberately lower the screed "zero reference" a couple cm below the apartment's true highest raw-floor point, filling the small resulting wedge with thin self-leveling compound instead of full-depth cement-sand screed (added 2026-08-17)** — since a 2cm level difference across more than half a room's floor isn't visually detectable once finished flooring is down, this avoids paying full screed depth (min. 3cm, plus however far below the true zero a point sits) across the whole apartment. Two independently worked examples from the same source arrived at the same order of magnitude: a 2cm lowering on a real 140m² project saved ~140 bags (~30,000 RUB, 2019 Moscow prices) and also avoided an entrance-threshold step defect; a 3cm-thickness reduction on a virtual 100m² example saved ~150 bags (~30,000-35,000 RUB) net of the pricier self-leveling top-up. **Route electrical/data cable trunking on the ceiling, not the floor, for the same reason** — a floor-routed bundle (often ≥5cm thick) forces a local 10cm screed buildup (5cm bundle + 5cm minimum cover) that can single-handedly blow this technique's savings and create a threshold-height defect; the same source estimates an extra ~120,000 RUB in avoidable screed volume if electrical were routed on the floor on that 140m² project. `single-account`, `unverified` pricing (practitioner's own stated rates, no invoice shown), 2019 Moscow prices — do not blend with this guide's more recent RUB figures without inflation adjustment. [source: [[_Sources/YT_KNY-XfgbGog_kruglov_top8_save_money_tips|KNY-XfgbGog]]]

Cement grout ≈250-300 RUB/m² (well under $10) versus epoxy ≈1,000-2,000 RUB/m² (≈$10-$20/m²) — up to ≈7× more expensive for the same result outside true wet zones. [source: [[_Sources/YT_KNY-XfgbGog_kruglov_top8_save_money_tips|KNY-XfgbGog]]]

[source: [[_Sources/YT_KNY-XfgbGog_kruglov_top8_save_money_tips|KNY-XfgbGog]]]
- **⚠️ Large-format tile's real cost drivers beyond a higher per-m² material price, per Kruglov/Ontario (added 2026-08-28)**: delivery cost for large-format slabs can run nearly as much as the material itself; lifting/hoisting cost is similarly disproportionate; breakage risk during delivery is meaningfully higher (priced into delivery cost); installation needs an unusually flat wall substrate; and every hole cut into a large slab for plumbing/mirror mounts/a faucet risks cracking the entire slab — a small mistake destroys an expensive unit. Large-format tile is also frequently specified across a whole wall in a design project, then ends up mostly hidden behind towels/mirrors/furniture once finished — wasted spend on invisible area. [source: [[_Sources/YT_KNY-XfgbGog_kruglov_top8_save_money_tips|KNY-XfgbGog]]]
- **Tiled-niche shower shelving vs. glass shelving, a concrete cost comparison, per Kruglov/Ontario (added 2026-08-28)**: recessed shelf niches lined in tile (cutting/finishing tile edges per opening) cost roughly ≈10,000 RUB (≈$110) per shelf — 5 shelves this way total ≈50,000 RUB (≈$560) — versus a single larger tiled niche fitted with several built-in tempered-glass shelves at a meaningfully lower total (the video's own exact glass-alternative total is ASR-garbled, not extracted precisely). The glass used is tempered/safety glass, comparable to automotive windshield glass — won't cut on breakage. [source: [[_Sources/YT_KNY-XfgbGog_kruglov_top8_save_money_tips|KNY-XfgbGog]]]
- **⚠️ Three material-procurement rules, per Kruglov/Ontario (added 2026-08-28)**, directly protective of a self-managed budget: (1) approve rough-material volumes/types before purchase — material shouldn't be delivered to site until the client has signed off on the proposed list and quantity; (2) keep material cost as a separate, distinctly-tracked line from the labor contract sum, never bundled into one fixed total — bundling removes the client's ability to independently source or substitute materials, and a pre-quoted bundled quantity (e.g. "100 bags at 400 RUB each") typically carries enough built-in price buffer to protect the seller's margin months later, while still billing the client by actual quantity used; (3) agree on an explicit reference retail-price benchmark up front (e.g. a named big-box chain) — if delivered pricing lands more than ~15-20% above that benchmark, the company isn't honoring the agreement and self-purchasing directly is a reasonable response. [source: [[_Sources/YT_hfJa_QNaN6c_kruglov_consumables_25_percent_budget|hfJa_QNaN6c]]]
