# Renovation Budgeting Master Guide

Airplane-view guide to renovation budgeting. For row-level pricing and sourced detail, see the case studies linked at the bottom — this page deliberately stays high-level.

## 1. What Must Be Budgeted

Build the budget from the physical space, not from guesswork:
1. **Layout first** — a plan (Homestyler, AutoCAD) fixes fixture/cabinet/outlet positions before anything is priced.
2. **Room-by-room breakdown** — group all costs (finishes, furniture, engineering) by room.
3. **Volumes** — m² of floor/ceiling/wall, perimeter for baseboards, counts for doors/windows/fixtures.
4. **Target tiers** — set a class benchmark (basic/comfort/premium) rather than pricing item-by-item first.
5. **Buffer** — add 10–15% to the total.
6. **Verify and optimize** *before* demolition (e.g. laminate over parquet), not after.

## 2. Main Cost Categories

- **Rough materials** (~30% of material spend): screed, plaster, wires, pipes, blocks.
- **Finishing materials** (~70%): tiles, parquet, paint, fixtures, furniture.
- **Contractor labor**: typically 30–35% of total end-to-end spend; the remaining 65–70% is materials, doors, ceilings, HVAC, and millwork.
- **Hidden/logistical costs** (commonly 10–15% of the whole budget, often forgotten): garbage removal, delivery/lifting of heavy materials, consumables, and doors/stretch ceilings excluded from base "per m²" labor quotes.

For a fully worked example of these categories on a real project, see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/case_studies/minsk_world_60m2_design_cost_case|Minsk World 60 m² Case Study]].

## 3. What Drives Cost

- **Timeline pressure** — compressing the schedule (e.g. "turnkey in 3 months") forces premium/weekend-work pricing. See [[11_Budget_and_Planning/Renovation_Sequence|Renovation Sequence]] for the high-level sequencing logic and why order matters.
- **Demolition/re-planning** — every wall moved is paid for four times (demolish, haul, rebuy, rebuild); working with the existing layout is the single biggest lever.
- **Sourcing choices** — retail vs. wholesale materials, direct factory sourcing, and procurement specialists can shift costs significantly without changing the design. See §5 below for concrete tactics.

## 4. What to Check Before Estimating

- **Source year controls currency conversion** — never convert historical prices at today's rate; use the source's own year (see [[00_Master/exchange_rates_reference|exchange_rates_reference]]).
- **Region matters** — Minsk/Belarus pricing is the primary baseline here; Russian/RUB data is secondary reference only, not Minsk-equivalent.
- **Sequence matters as much as price** — some decisions (concealed door frames, shadow baseboards, partition build order) must be locked in before rough works start or become expensive to change later — see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/Renovation_Sequence|Renovation Sequence]].

## 5. Cost-Saving Strategies

Tactical moves for trimming budget without cutting quality (compressed from 13 sourced strategies — see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/analysis/cost_saving_strategies_full|Detailed strategy notes]] for full sourcing and figures):

- **Talk to neighbors before over-investing in soundproofing** — shared-wall soundproofing may already be covered on their side.
- **Limit demolition/re-planning** — every wall moved is paid for four times; prefer smart zoning (slats/panels) over new walls.
- **Don't rush the timeline** — compressed schedules directly inflate cost (see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/Renovation_Sequence|Renovation Sequence]]).
- **Use a procurement specialist ("комплектатор")** for expensive designer fixtures — often finds near-identical alternatives for a fraction of the cost.
- **Buy rough materials wholesale**, e.g. via a contractor who supplies materials, rather than retail.
- **Skip non-structural wardrobe backing panels** and order direct factory board-cutting — cuts custom millwork cost ~30%.
- **Source lighting directly from overseas factories** — cuts hardware cost 50–70% vs. local resellers.
- **Choose a 2-in-1 washer-dryer combo** over a separate dryer stack in comfort-class/space-constrained projects.
- **Use online marketplaces (e.g. OZON)** for standard loose furniture items instead of custom/designer pieces.
- **Spray-paint existing PVC window frames** instead of replacing them (~$730 vs. $3,000+).
- **Substitute stretch ceilings and paintable wallpaper** for drywall ceilings and smooth painted walls — 40–60% labor/material savings.
- **Pay for rough materials directly through verified distributors**, not contractor cash advances, to avoid markup/fraud risk.
