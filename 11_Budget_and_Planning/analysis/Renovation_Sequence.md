# Renovation Sequence of Events

Properly sequencing a renovation project is critical for budget management. Deviating from the logical order or rushing the timeline drastically increases costs due to overlapping trades, overnight drying, and fixing mistakes.

## General Order of Events

1. **Design and Layout Ideation**:
   - Establish floor plans.
   - Decide on 2D layouts for plumbing, electrical, and HVAC.
   - Create the initial Bill of Materials.

2. **Demolition and Preparation**:
   - Dismantling old walls, floors, and finishes (primarily in secondary housing).
   - Organizing garbage removal and hauling.

3. **Rough Works & Structural Changes**:
   - Erecting new partition walls.
   - Floor leveling and pouring new screed.
   - *Note: Walls in new builds are generally straight, whereas secondary housing may require heavy plastering and reinforcement.*

4. **Engineering and Utilities**:
   - **Plumbing**: Routing pipes for bathrooms and kitchens.
   - **Electrical**: Wiring, setting up junction boxes.
   - **HVAC**: Installing ducting or air conditioning conduits.

5. **Finishing Preparation**:
   - Puttying and plastering walls to perfectly flat surfaces.
   - Preparing floors for finishing materials.

6. **Finishing Works**:
   - Laying down final flooring (laminate, parquet, tile).
   - Painting walls, applying wallpaper.
   - Installing stretch ceilings (usually handled by specialized contractors).

7. **Installation of Fixtures & Doors**:
   - Installing interior doors (best done by the door manufacturer/supplier for warranty).
   - Installing electrical sockets, switches, and lighting fixtures.
   - Installing sanitary ware (sinks, toilets).

8. **Furniture and Final Cleaning (Cleaning)**:
   - Moving in and assembling furniture.
   - Final professional cleaning.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Don't Rush the Timeline**
> Demanding an artificially fast timeline (e.g., "turnkey in 3 months" for a massive project) forces contractors to charge high premiums for weekend work and accelerated curing compounds. Let the sequence flow naturally.

## 2. Alternative Sequence Approaches & Trade-Offs

Depending on wall construction types, pre-existing subfloor conditions, and custom furniture designs, key sub-sequences can be executed using different methodologies:

### A. Partition Wall Construction & Utility Routing Order

| Approach | Sequence | Advantages & Circumstances | Trade-Offs / Risks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Option 1: Drywall Frame First (Cavity Routing)**<br>*(Source: [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_mistakes_video_21ade3f6\|21ade3f6]])* | 1. Plaster perimeter walls<br>2. Build drywall metal frame<br>3. Skin one side & install plywood backings<br>4. Route cables & pipes inside cavity<br>5. Fill sound insulation & close second side | **Zero wall chasing**. Faster execution, cleaner job site, excellent acoustic insulation, easy built-in plywood backing for heavy TVs/cabinets. Ideal for new builds or full re-planning. | Requires skilled framing labor; improper stud spacing can cause joint cracking if not taped properly. |
| **Option 2: Block/Brick Walls First (Wall Chasing)**<br>*(Source: [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_mistakes_video_21ade3f6\|21ade3f6]])* | 1. Erect gas-block/brick walls<br>2. Plaster all walls<br>3. Wait 2-3 weeks for plaster to dry<br>4. Chase (groove) slots for electrical & plumbing | Solid masonry feel everywhere; high point-load capacity for heavy wall anchors without pre-planned backings. | Messy wall chasing through cured plaster/block; generates dust; requires 2-3 weeks plaster drying wait before chasing. |

### B. Full-Height Custom Cabinetry vs. Stretch Ceiling Order

| Approach | Sequence | Advantages & Circumstances | Trade-Offs / Risks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Option 1: Cabinetry First (False Top Panel)**<br>*(Sources: [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_tips_video_f23c504a\|f23c504a]], [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_mistakes_video_21ade3f6\|21ade3f6]])* | 1. Complete wall finish & floor laying<br>2. Install full-height wardrobes & kitchen tall units with top false panels<br>3. Mount stretch ceiling track directly to cabinet top false panel | **Seamless built-in look** with zero top dust gaps. Eliminates risk of tearing/damaging ceiling membrane during cabinet assembly. Saves ceiling material square footage. | Cabinet dimensions must be finalized early; requires coordination between furniture installer and ceiling contractor. |
| **Option 2: Stretch Ceiling First**<br>*(General practice; not transcript-specific)* | 1. Install stretch ceiling across full room area<br>2. Install wardrobes underneath with filler strips | Standard sequence; allows ceiling contractor to finish entire apartment in one visit before furniture delivery. | Risk of damaging ceiling membrane when lifting heavy cabinet panels; leaves top gap requiring decorative filler strips. |

### C. Electrical Wiring Routing: Overhead vs. Sub-Screed

| Approach | Circumstance | Method |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ceiling Routing**<br>*(Source: [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_mistakes_video_21ade3f6\|21ade3f6]])* | Pre-existing subfloor screed already poured by developer | Route corrugated conduit across ceiling slab before stretch/drywall ceiling installation. Avoids damaging existing floor screed. |
| **Floor Screed Routing**<br>*(General practice; not transcript-specific)* | Bare concrete slab without pre-existing screed | Route conduit across floor slab first, then pour wet screed over wiring. Reduces overhead ladder work. |

### D. Plaster Drying Acceleration Options

* **Natural Air Curing (Recommended)**: Wait 2 to 3 weeks for gypsum plaster to cure naturally before applying putty or tile. Prevents plaster delamination and mold.
* **Controlled Dehumidification**: Use air dehumidifiers or heat guns to accelerate moisture extraction if timeline requires faster curing.

### G. Baseboard Profile Installation Order

| Approach | Sequence | Advantages & Circumstances | Trade-Offs / Risks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

## 3. Full 24-Step Turnkey Workflow

The comprehensive turnkey renovation lifecycle is organized into 24 distinct chronological steps across 4 major phases (Planning, Rough Works, Surface Finishing, and Turnkey Handover).

| Step | Stage | What Happens | Why It Matters | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Design & Technical Project | Create detailed technical drawings, electrical/plumbing layouts, and select exact flooring and fixture types | Defines screed depths, socket locations, and prevents costly re-work | [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1\|21a6e3c1]] |
| **2** | Worksite Preparation | Set up temporary power, water, sewer, living quarters, protection films, and consumable tools | Ensures site safety, hygiene, and worker productivity | [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1\|21a6e3c1]] |
| **3** | Demolition & Wall Openings | Dismantle old non-bearing walls, adjust door opening heights, apply initial subfloor waterproofing | Establishes exact room geometry early before ordering custom doors/windows | [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1\|21a6e3c1]] |
| **4** | Windows & Entrance Door Order | Take precise window/entrance door measurements and place factory orders; protect floor waterproofing | Long manufacturing lead times run in parallel with messy demolition without risk of damage | [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1\|21a6e3c1]] |
| **5** | Ceiling Prep & Kitchen Choice | Prime/paint bare concrete ceiling slab, route duct outlets, finalize kitchen vendor and ceiling box needs | Prevents concrete dust falling onto stretch ceilings and resolves top kitchen cabinet clearances | [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1\|21a6e3c1]] |
| **6** | Partition Walls & Soundproofing | Erect inner walls, apply acoustic insulation (projecting back-boxes outward), route AC lines, build radiator niches | Creates structural room boundaries and sound insulation before wet plastering | [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1\|21a6e3c1]] |
| **7** | Wall Plastering & Alignment | Set plaster beacons, plaster masonry/block walls, and remove beacons immediately upon drying | Produces 90° true corners for cabinetry and flush wall surfaces | [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1\|21a6e3c1]] |
| **8** | Windows & Entrance Door Install | Mount entrance door, exterior windows, and balcony glazing | Seals building envelope from wind, rain, and cold before screed and plumbing works | [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1\|21a6e3c1]] |
| **9** | Subfloor Piping & Primer | Map AC ducts, lay subfloor heating pipes, apply bonding primer across concrete floor slab | Prepares floor slab for screed pouring without trapping loose dust | [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1\|21a6e3c1]] |

| **11** | 1st Kitchen Tech Measurement | Invoke kitchen designer after screed walkability for 1st technical verification | Provides exact socket, plumbing, and wall backing requirements for kitchen assembly | [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1\|21a6e3c1]] |
| **12** | Rough Electrical & Low-Voltage | Groove plaster, mount back-boxes, pull power/ethernet cables, schedule utility valve shutoff dates | Conceals wiring safely inside walls before final wall puttying | [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1\|21a6e3c1]] |
| **13** | Rough Plumbing & In-Wall Valves | Install manifolds, pressure regulators, check valves, and wall-hung toilet frames | Establishes reliable pressure-balanced water supply before closing walls/tiling | [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1\|21a6e3c1]] |
