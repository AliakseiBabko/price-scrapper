# Master Budgeting Guide for Renovation

This guide synthesizes the core principles of creating a robust renovation budget, ensuring you capture hidden expenses and avoid the dreaded "budget creep."

## 1. The 7-Step Layout-First Approach

Instead of guessing costs, build your budget systematically based on the physical space:

1. **Floor Planning (Layouts)**: Always start with a physical or 3D plan (e.g., Homestyler, AutoCAD). Determine exactly where the sofa, shower, cabinets, and major electrical outlets will be. This is your foundation for all material calculations.
2. **Room-by-Room Breakdown**: Group expenses by room (Kitchen, Bedroom, Bathroom, Entrance). List everything going inside: finishing materials, furniture, and engineering solutions (AC, radiators, ventilation).
3. **Volume Calculation**: Calculate exact volumes based on layouts (m2 of floors/ceilings/walls, perimeter for baseboards, counts for doors/windows).
4. **Room Filling**: Count the number of fixtures, cabinets, and appliances per room.
5. **Setting Target Tiers**: Set a benchmark price based on the class of your renovation (e.g., basic, comfort, premium) rather than getting stuck on exact items immediately.
6. **The Buffer**: Always add a 10% to 15% buffer to the total sum.
7. **Verification and Optimization**: Compare the final sum against your reality. Optimize *before* demolition starts if you are over budget (e.g., choosing laminate over parquet).

## 2. Non-Obvious & Hidden Costs

Many people calculate the cost of a sofa and laminate but forget the logistical and organizational costs. These typically consume **10% to 15%** of the entire budget.

- **Garbage Removal**: Essential for secondary housing. Renting containers and hiring loaders for demolition waste can be a massive unbudgeted expense.
- **Delivery and Lifting**: Heavy materials like screed, blocks, and tiles cost money to deliver and bring up to your floor.
- **Consumables**: Spatulas, tape, drill bits, and covering film.
- **Doors & Stretch Ceilings**: Often excluded from base "per square meter" labor quotes because they require specialized third-party installation for warranties.

## 3. Material Budget Breakdown

When allocating funds for materials, a general rule of thumb is:
- **Rough Materials (30%)**: Screed, plaster, wires, pipes, blocks.
- **Finishing Materials (70%)**: Tiles, parquet, paint, fixtures, furniture.

## 5. Contractor Labor vs Total Budget Allocation Ratios

In a comprehensive turnkey renovation budget, base contractor labor typically constitutes **30%-35%** of the overall end-to-end expenditure, while rough materials, finishing materials, doors, stretch ceilings, HVAC, and custom millwork account for the remaining **65%-70%**.
*(Source: [[90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_a8e90887\|a8e90887]])*

## 6. Regional and Year-Aware Price Policy

To maintain strict price comparability and historical context across all budgeting documents:

1. **Source-Year Controls Conversion Basis**:
   - The year of the source content controls currency conversion assumptions. Historical price estimates MUST NOT be converted using current spot exchange rates.
   - Source year priority order: (1) Explicit year stated in content/title (e.g. "2025"); (2) Verified publish date year; (3) If neither is available, mark `source_year` as `unknown` and refrain from presenting converted values as directly comparable.

2. **Currency Conversion Standards**:
   - Original currency amounts and units are always preserved.
   - Converted BYN/USD values are calculated using the annual average exchange rate for the specific `source_year` recorded in [[00_Master/exchange_rates_reference|exchange_rates_reference]].
   - Unknown-year sources are marked low-confidence for financial comparisons.

3. **Regional Market Priority**:
   - **Primary Baseline**: Local Minsk / Belarus pricing data has primary priority.
   - **Secondary Reference**: Russian / RUB market data is treated as secondary reference data only (labeled as "currency-normalized reference", not Minsk-equivalent pricing) due to regional labor and material market variations.
