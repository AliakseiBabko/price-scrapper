## Why a Single Cleaning Pass Usually Falls Short

Both channels represented here converge on the same core claim: post-
renovation cleaning done as one single pass right after work finishes is
a common client assumption (Директор Клининга: ~90% of clients expect
this) that trades away real quality. The reasoning given is mechanical,
not just "more is better" — dust and residue get physically sealed behind
fixtures once they're installed, and a single pass has no opportunity to
catch dust that resettles after cleaning finishes. A budget renovation can
reasonably accept the tradeoff of a single combined pass; a higher-end
renovation, per Директор Клининга's own framing, generally shouldn't.
[source: [[_Sources/YT_oF62qDhYkuw_direktor_klininga_3stage_cleaning|oF62qDhYkuw]], [[_Sources/YT_MJE43WkLP4_direktor_klininga_harsh_review|-MJE43WkLP4]]]

## The Staged Sequence

Директор Клининга frames the process as three formal stages plus an
informal fourth:

1. **After rough works, before finish works** — full de-dusting of walls,
   floor, *and* ceiling (ceiling explicitly called out as commonly
   skipped: dust trapped above a stretch/suspended ceiling before
   installation isn't sealed airtight, and gradually escapes through
   gaps/fixture openings over months, becoming a delayed dust source in
   an already-finished apartment).
2. **After finish works, before furniture/fixture installation** — a
   second full de-dusting pass, because dust on walls/tile at this point
   becomes permanently trapped once a fixture (e.g. a shower cabin) is
   mounted over it — "only a finger fits" in the resulting gap.
3. **After furniture/fixtures are installed** — final de-dusting and
   floor wash, the point the apartment is genuinely move-in ready.
4. **Informal fourth pass, post-move-in** — a light wet-mop after movers'
   foot traffic, explicitly distinguished from true construction-dust
   cleaning, which should already be resolved by stage 3.

Ирина Илёнова's independent, real-time walkthrough of an 85m² final clean
corroborates the *mechanism* behind staging without restating the same
three-stage vocabulary: she describes dust resettling visibly during any
single cleaning pass, and frames a staged process as the only way that
resettled dust gets caught by a later pass rather than left in place —
the same underlying logic from a different practitioner's own words.
[source: [[_Sources/YT_oF62qDhYkuw_direktor_klininga_3stage_cleaning|oF62qDhYkuw]], [[_Sources/YT_MJE43WkLP4_direktor_klininga_harsh_review|-MJE43WkLP4]], [[_Sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]

## Protective Technique During Construction (Upstream of Cleaning)

What the renovation crew does (or fails to do) directly drives how hard
the eventual cleaning is, per Директор Клининга: window frames left with
factory protective film too long become brittle (UV/weather exposure) and
need mechanical scraping that risks the frame — cover the original film
with an additional taped-down layer instead. Tile floors need multi-layer
protection (cardboard + film, not film alone) during tiling work, since
dried mortar/grout on bare flooring is slow and labor-intensive to
scrape off later. **Grout haze removal timing matters, especially for
epoxy grout**: wipe excess grout film off tile immediately after
grouting — left to dry, it "smears and stays on the tile permanently,"
only partially removable afterward. Facade crews should tape windows or
rinse spilled mortar/mix off glass immediately, since dried mix on
textured glass can be effectively unremovable. Correct protective-taping
order for door/window handles: cover in plastic film first, *then* tape
over the film — taping directly onto bare hardware leaves adhesive
residue that's harder to remove. Floor transition thresholds are a
commonly-missed protection point, scuffed by debris bags dragged across
them if left uncovered. [source: [[_Sources/YT_oF62qDhYkuw_direktor_klininga_3stage_cleaning|oF62qDhYkuw]], [[_Sources/YT_MJE43WkLP4_direktor_klininga_harsh_review|-MJE43WkLP4]]]

## Commonly-Missed Spots (QC Checklist)

Директор Клининга's on-camera QC walkthrough names specific spots that
pass a casual visual check but fail closer inspection: kitchen-drawer
undersides/back cavities; radiator fin interiors (needs a steam cleaner
with a narrow brush attachment — some cleaners fake this step by
spraying/wiping only the visible front); windowsill undersides;
cabinet/door handle grip surfaces; door and window-frame top edges above
the sash (every interior door, not just windows — remove via dry vacuum
with a brush nozzle from a ladder, since wet-wiping construction dust on
wood "smears rather than removes"); the window rubber gasket/seal
(dust hides underneath, invisible without physically pulling the seal
back); and a bathtub access screen, which the source flags as a genuine
skill/liability gap — not always something a general cleaner can safely
detach, with real options being a specialist, extra time, or the client
accepting it stays uncleaned behind it. **QC technique**: check surfaces
under strong/direct or accent lighting, not just ambient light — grout
haze and dust on a backsplash can be fully invisible under normal room
lighting. [source: [[_Sources/YT_MJE43WkLP4_direktor_klininga_harsh_review|-MJE43WkLP4]]]

## Window Cleaning: a Distinct Three-Sub-Step Process

Ирина Илёнова's independent account gives the most detailed window-
cleaning mechanism in this batch: (1) a first soapy-plus-acid-cleaner
pass (she uses "Азелит") removes bulk construction grime, streaks
accepted at this stage; (2) a **steam generator flushes fine construction
dust packed into the frame track ("межрамье") and hardware** — the step
she says makes post-renovation window cleaning meaningfully harder than
routine cleaning, since construction dust is fine enough to pack into
every gap and gasket a wipe alone can't reach; (3) a final squeegee/
magnetic-window-tool polish pass done from **both** interior and exterior
sides of the glass, since spots missed from one side only become visible
from the other. **Real time benchmark: ~1.5 hours for one especially
dirty post-renovation window.** Never let the renovation crew wash
windows themselves — a crew scraping dried paint off glass with a flat
putty knife/scraper permanently scratches it. An interior-only clean on a
sealed/non-opening high sash leaves faint streaks visible only at certain
lighting angles — a genuinely streak-free result needs exterior access
(e.g. a rope-access washer), a real, accepted service-tier limit rather
than a defect to chase indefinitely. [source: [[_Sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]], [[_Sources/YT_MJE43WkLP4_direktor_klininga_harsh_review|-MJE43WkLP4]]]

## Surface-Specific Chemical Cautions

A pattern repeated three times across this small batch, from two
independent channels: **the wrong chemical on the wrong finish causes
real, sometimes irreversible damage.** Директор Клининга records a
fireplace door's factory coating stripped alongside contractor paint
splatter by the same solvent, and a separate acid-cleaner spill that
damaged a faucet fitting after contact with hot water. Ирина Илёнова adds
a third, distinct case: **never use solvent or acid-based cleaner on
acrylic bathtubs or on organic glass ("оргстекло")/plexiglass shower
panels** — solvent visibly clouds organic glass almost immediately; use a
dedicated weak-acid, plumbing-specific product instead. **Polished/high-
gloss porcelain tile needs a fully dry pre-wipe before any wet chemical**
— wiping while already wet on a dusty glossy surface produces near-
permanent white haze/streaks; dry-wipe first, then apply solvent (she
uses "646 растворитель") via a damp cloth and immediately dry-polish
before it evaporates. Lower wall sections accumulate disproportionately
more dust than higher ones (gravity settling) — clean zone by zone.
[source: [[_Sources/YT_oF62qDhYkuw_direktor_klininga_3stage_cleaning|oF62qDhYkuw]], [[_Sources/YT_MJE43WkLP4_direktor_klininga_harsh_review|-MJE43WkLP4]], [[_Sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]

## Handover Standard and Professional Conduct

Ирина Илёнова states a fixture-handover rule not present in the other two
sources: **every plumbing fixture must be fully dry, not just clean, at
handover** — visible dryness is what actually reads as "clean" to a
client. Mosquito/insect screens need their own multi-step process (steam,
soapy-plus-acid treatment, hose rinse) and a seasonal rule — remove and
store them indoors roughly December-March, since outdoor winter exposure
degrades the synthetic mesh faster. Professional-conduct notes: cleaners
don't move furniture or appliances themselves (movers' scope, even on
direct client request); warn occupants before re-entering a room
mid-task. [source: [[_Sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]

## Sourcing a Cleaner as a Renovation Client — a Fourth, Independent Perspective (added 2026-08-28)

Konstantin Kruglov (Ontario, a turnkey renovation company — the buyer
side of this relationship, distinct from the three cleaner-practitioner
sources above) states his own company deliberately does **not** offer
post-renovation cleaning in-house, after trialing "a large number" of
private cleaners and both small and large cleaning companies over time
without finding one it could work with long-term free of recurring
damage complaints (chemical-solution damage to laminate, wallpaper,
walls; scratches). Clients must source their own cleaner. **A named
cost-tension example**: a cheap cleaner (~20,000 RUB, ≈$220) scratched a
cooktop during an hourly clean, with the resulting damage-repair cost
framed as falling back on the client despite the low quote, versus a
cleaner confident enough to quote roughly double (~40,000 RUB, ≈$440)
for reliably careful work — offered as the real cost/risk tradeoff
behind hiring a post-renovation cleaner, not evidence that either figure
is a safe threshold. `single-account`, converted at the trailing-6-month
USD/RUB mean before 2024-10-11 (90.0715 RUB/USD). [source: [[_Sources/YT_hfJa_QNaN6c_kruglov_consumables_25_percent_budget|hfJa_QNaN6c]]]

## Occupational Health and Equipment

Ирина Илёнова's account is the only one of the three to explicitly frame
routine chemical/solvent exposure as a genuine occupational-health
consideration — stating that people with allergies or asthma should
reconsider professional cleaning work specifically because of it, not as
a throwaway caution. Cheap (~80 RUB, under $2 USD, "fix price"-tier)
disposable gloves are reported to degrade quickly (tear, partially
dissolve) under the solvents/acids this work uses. `single-account`.
[source: [[_Sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]
