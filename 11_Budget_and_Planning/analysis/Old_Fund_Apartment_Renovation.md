# Old-Fund ("Старый Фонд") Apartment Renovation

Old-fund apartments — pre-revolutionary and Soviet-era buildings with masonry/timber-joist construction, often no central hot water, and legacy layouts that predate modern plumbing/electrical codes — carry a distinct set of renovation risks and rules not shared by newer-build ("новостройка") stock. This page consolidates the three sources this vault has on the topic so far (all from ДЕЛАТЬ НЕ ПЕРЕДЕЛАТЬ / Vladimir Amelchenko, business/premium-segment turnkey renovation, St. Petersburg), crossing this project's 3+-source page-creation threshold on 2026-08-28.

## Buy-Time Red Flags

Checkable at a viewing, before purchase:

- **Through-riser wall penetration into a neighboring unit** — at a plumbing-stack wall, an actual through-hole into the adjacent apartment is a real, found defect, not hypothetical.
- **Multi-strand soft aluminum wiring visible at an outlet** — this era's aluminum wiring is already past its safe design service life by default, making it unsafe regardless of apparent condition.
- **Lath-and-plaster ("дранка") partition construction** — a genuine structural weak point by period construction standards, contrasted with modern aerated-concrete (газобетон) block replacement partitions.
- **Gas pipe boxed inside a wall cavity** — a code violation: gas pipe must remain either fully visible/exposed or concealed only behind an easily removable furniture-type construction, so it stays inspectable and serviceable.
- **Crumbly, hollow-sounding original screed** ("бухтит" when tapped) — a default-assumption defect on old-fund apartments, not an occasional finding; tile removal reliably reveals poor-condition plaster beneath it too. Corroborated with an added acoustic detail: existing lath-and-plaster wall surfaces often sound audibly hollow/loose ("вся звенит") even before demolition begins. ## Regulatory / Approval Constraints Specific to Old-Fund Work

Old-fund replanning/systems work routinely requires formal official approval, and that process can override what would otherwise be a free design or client choice:

- **Load-bearing-wall opening enlargement**: a metal reinforcement frame around a widened/heightened opening indicates prior work — any such modification requires a formal engineering project developed first, reinforcement executed according to that project, and formal sign-off/acceptance of the completed reinforcement, not a contractor's own judgment call. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_AGqrxEtyrdI_sbk_old_apartment_renovation_case|AGqrxEtyrdI]]]
- **Gas pipe re-welding/rerouting**: must be performed by licensed gas-utility technicians ("газовщики") according to a formally developed engineering project — never by a general contractor. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_f8lzn1KEIww_sbk_old_fund_full_gut_case|f8lzn1KEIww]]]
- **⚠️ Fixture-location constraints tied to the approval process itself, capable of overriding a client's own design preference**: in one real case, a client wanted to retain half of an original masonry stove ("печка") as a small heated nook — the approvable replanning required a sink at that exact location instead (apparently tied to existing plumbing/utility stub-out positions the approval process references), and the stove had to be given up. A concrete example of how legalization requirements, not just aesthetic or budget tradeoffs, can force a specific room layout. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_nma3zHRugsM_sbk_no_bathtub_oldfund_replan|nma3zHRugsM]]]
- **General implication**: a design project for old-fund replanning work is not just a visual/ergonomic exercise — it must be built to be *approvable* under these constraints from the outset, since retrofitting an already-built-out layout to satisfy a rejected approval is far more costly than designing for approvability up front.

## Structural/Substrate Technique

- **Substrate must be leveled (flat, not necessarily perfectly level) before laying XPS insulation board (пеноплекс)** — a prior contractor's failure to do this let the board sit unsupported over "wave" sections of an uneven floor; under a poured screed's weight, those unsupported sections crushed/deformed. Correct sequence: clean/level bumps first, pour leveling compound where needed, then lay XPS. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_AGqrxEtyrdI_sbk_old_apartment_renovation_case|AGqrxEtyrdI]]]
- **Expanded clay (керамзит) under screed, two distinct old-fund use-cases for the same material**:
  - *Dead-load avoidance*: where floor height varies significantly (one real case: ≈15cm variance room-to-room), filling low areas with expanded clay mixed with cement "milk" (added so the aggregate doesn't stay loose) up to grade, then a normal-thickness screed on top, avoids adding several tons of dead load a full-depth screed pour would put on an old structural slab not designed for it. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_AGqrxEtyrdI_sbk_old_apartment_renovation_case|AGqrxEtyrdI]]]
  - *Reaching a target floor height over a structurally capable slab*: where the original wood-on-joists floor (≈25cm thick) is removed and the underlying slab is monolithic (from a prior capital repair, able to bear a full-depth pour), a thick expanded-clay-concrete screed restores floor height to properly clear the door threshold — and the resulting screed depth can be deep enough to bury a re-routed sewer stack beneath the finished floor. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_f8lzn1KEIww_sbk_old_fund_full_gut_case|f8lzn1KEIww]]]
- **Conduit tied to (never fastened through) the Шуманет soundproofing/waterproofing membrane** — fastening through the membrane punctures the sealed "trough" it forms, and a punctured membrane means a later screed pour floods the apartment below. Conduit is instead tied to a separate wire mesh laid loosely over the membrane. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_AGqrxEtyrdI_sbk_old_apartment_renovation_case|AGqrxEtyrdI]]]
- **Protective metal sleeve at a floor-conduit-to-threshold crossing** — a "fool-proofing" measure so a future occupant drilling into the floor to fit a threshold strip hits the tube, not the cable, and stops to investigate rather than unknowingly severing a live circuit. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_AGqrxEtyrdI_sbk_old_apartment_renovation_case|AGqrxEtyrdI]]]
- **A candid tool-substitution anecdote, recorded as realistic site management, not an endorsed technique**: when the intended chase-cutting tool was unavailable on a given day, a crew cut the chase with a concrete core (hole-saw) bit instead, on the principle that a crew must keep the schedule moving with whatever tool is on hand as long as the end result meets spec. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_f8lzn1KEIww_sbk_old_fund_full_gut_case|f8lzn1KEIww]]]
- **⚠️ Wood-joist-on-steel-channel ("швеллер") support gap requires a welder, a real named cost figure**: where an added/extended floor joist section falls short of the second support channel, a welder must weld on an additional channel section for a third bearing point before flooring can proceed — **stated cost ≈15,000-20,000 RUB (≈$210-$280 USD, trailing-6-month average before this 2020-09-03 source)**, purely for this one fix.
- **Floor-height-gain tradeoff on floor replacement, two named build-up options, both real cost drivers**: removing old wood floors typically drops the sub-floor level ~15-20cm, which must be built back up. Wet screed is heavy and slow to mix/pour by hand; dry build-up (plywood/OSB sheet layers) is lighter and faster but needs a large quantity of sheet material that is itself not cheap — either path costs a materially significant sum.
- **⚠️ Wall substrate can include a bituminous/roofing-felt layer under the lath, not just plaster-on-lath alone**: stripping plaster and lath from one wall revealed a roofing-felt-like layer ("рубероид") plus the original wood boards beneath — fresh plaster will not adequately bond to this substrate, so the wall must first be sheathed in sheet material (drywall/GVL) before any leveling/skim-coat finishing, adding real material and labor cost beyond a simple re-plaster.
- **Unstably-bonded original brick masonry, a distinct in-wall defect from the lath/plaster/roofing-felt issue**: some original brick coursing is only loosely/poorly bonded — remediation requires either reinforcing it in place or knocking out and replacing the affected bricks, both adding real time and cost.
- **⚠️ Demolition-stage risk to the downstairs neighbor's ceiling**: once old floorboards are removed, the crew is standing directly on what is, from the unit below, the neighbor's lath-and-plaster ceiling — jumping, dropping tools, or hammering on the exposed sub-floor boards can visibly crack/shed the neighbor's ceiling plaster below. Site practice: no jumping, no dropped tools, no sledgehammer strikes at this stage. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_pFrQLRQ37pY_sbk_old_fund_price_case|pFrQLRQ37pY]]]

## Systems Workarounds Specific to Old-Fund Buildings

- **Gas water heater ("газовая колонка") as the standard hot-water source where no central hot water exists** — cold water reaches a wall-mounted gas heater, is heated there, and returns through the apartment's plumbing as its actual hot-water loop; a collector/manifold distribution system at the entry point serves this loop the same way it would a modern central-hot-water setup. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_f8lzn1KEIww_sbk_old_fund_full_gut_case|f8lzn1KEIww]]]
- **Gas-to-electric conversion as an alternative modernization path**: in one real case, a client chose to remove gas supply entirely (including pipe running through a repurposed niche near a former stove) and switch cooking/heating to electric, rather than retain and legalize the gas system. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_nma3zHRugsM_sbk_no_bathtub_oldfund_replan|nma3zHRugsM]]]
- **⚠️ Heritage fixtures (fireplaces/stoves) can only be restored, never demolished or rebuilt**: a still-present decorative masonry stove ("печь") can be cleaned, insulated, and cosmetically restored (e.g. repainted), but not removed or altered structurally, if the owner wants to keep it as a heritage feature — a distinct constraint from the approval-driven fixture-location case above.
- **⚠️ Chimney/flue inspection is a real prerequisite before using or connecting anything to an old-fund flue**: old-fund flues are described as routinely clogged by default — using a stove, or connecting a modern gas water heater ("газовая колонка") to an existing flue, requires a chimney sweep ("трубочист") to inspect/clean the system first. **Named failure mode if skipped**: connecting a modern water heater to a clogged/underperforming flue causes insufficient draft; when draft reverses, the heater cuts out and hot water reverts to cold — a recurring real problem in old-fund apartments in winter specifically. Inspecting one flue typically obligates inspecting the whole building's other flues too (usually similarly clogged), adding to the cost. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_pFrQLRQ37pY_sbk_old_fund_price_case|pFrQLRQ37pY]]]

## Electrical and Plumbing Cost Drivers (added Round 6, `pFrQLRQ37pY`)

- **⚠️ Three named electrical problems, near-universal in old-fund apartments**: (1) old aluminum wiring already near/past its safe service life; (2) wiring routed by an unknown installer with no documented layout, hard to trace/diagnose; (3) very low allocated electrical capacity from the building — one real apartment's allocation was only 3 kW, insufficient for a modern water heater plus a 4-burner electric cooktop together.
- **⚠️ Real capacity-upgrade case with a concrete before/after figure**: the same apartment's allocation was raised to 5 kW across three phases via several calls/applications to the utility — an added cost (approval process plus new breakers sized for the increased capacity) beyond the base electrical rewire budget.
- **Aluminum wiring replacement is a fire-safety mandate, not a discretionary upgrade** — required regardless of budget, since degrading aluminum wiring is a genuine fire risk, especially in wood-frame-wall/ceiling buildings like this one.
- **⚠️ A harder version of the neighbor-coordination constraint on sewer riser replacement**: properly replacing a cast-iron sewer riser requires also replacing its shared cross-fittings and tees, which requires the neighbor's cooperation/access. Where a neighbor has already renovated and won't grant access, only the accessible riser section can be replaced — the old cast-iron tee at the inaccessible boundary must be left in place, a real, uncontrollable risk of a future crack/leak at that joint.
- **General plumbing framing, stated directly**: full re-piping of old supply/waste lines buys ~30 years of reliability but is unavoidably costly — both sides of the same tradeoff, not a false choice.

## Materials — Period-Authentic Details as Named Cost Drivers

- **Arched (semicircular) window openings**: curved plaster/paint window-reveal ("откосы") lines are always more expensive than straight lines; for sheet-material cladding (e.g. MDF), a curved reveal cannot be a stock item — it must be custom-fabricated individually, is non-returnable, takes longer, and costs more. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_AGqrxEtyrdI_sbk_old_apartment_renovation_case|AGqrxEtyrdI]]]
- **PVC vs. paper protective packing tape on new windows**: paper tape leaves adhesive residue within about a month and is hard to remove cleanly — a real "tragedy" on wood-framed windows specifically, where residue removal after ~6 months forces stripping/refinishing/repainting the wood. PVC tape (commonly orange or blue) can reportedly stay 6 months to a year and come off cleanly, provided the manufacturer's own safe-duration spec is checked. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_AGqrxEtyrdI_sbk_old_apartment_renovation_case|AGqrxEtyrdI]]]
- **⚠️ Mid-demolition design-project revision, a real worked example**: stripping plaster from a facade-facing wall revealed structurally sound, aesthetically attractive original brick — the design project was revised on the spot to seal/varnish the brick and leave it exposed as a deliberate design feature, rather than re-plastering as originally planned. A concrete example of the design project remaining a living document through demolition rather than fixed in advance. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_nma3zHRugsM_sbk_no_bathtub_oldfund_replan|nma3zHRugsM]]]
- **Old period fixtures worth restoring rather than replacing**: a functioning original electric doorbell was cleaned/restored and incorporated into the finished interior on the designer's own suggestion — a small, low-cost heritage-preservation technique. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_nma3zHRugsM_sbk_no_bathtub_oldfund_replan|nma3zHRugsM]]]

## Real Dysfunctional Legacy Layouts and Their Fixes

- **A real found layout defect used to justify commissioning a design project even for a small (35m²) apartment**: bathtub and kitchen combined in the same space, directly off the entry, with no functional separation. Replanning (partition removal + rebuild) produced a genuinely separate entryway, a complete bathroom (shower, toilet, sink, door swinging away from the entry door), and a standalone kitchen. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_nma3zHRugsM_sbk_no_bathtub_oldfund_replan|nma3zHRugsM]]]
- **Partition removal for light and furniture placement**: combining two original rooms brought existing large windows into the merged space (more natural light, apartment reads larger than its footprint) and freed a wall segment sized for a large television that the original division didn't allow. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_nma3zHRugsM_sbk_no_bathtub_oldfund_replan|nma3zHRugsM]]]

## Company Policy

- **⚠️ Full gut demolition only, no partial/selective renovation offered, for old-fund work** — even a partition/screed/plaster surface that looks "plus or minus fine" is treated as unreliable, and this company's stated 5-year workmanship warranty is judged incompatible with building over unverified original substrate. Buyers hoping to keep an "OK-looking" original surface to save cost are explicitly declined under this policy. `single-account`, one company's own stated business policy — not confirmed as an industry-wide standard. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_f8lzn1KEIww_sbk_old_fund_full_gut_case|f8lzn1KEIww]]]
