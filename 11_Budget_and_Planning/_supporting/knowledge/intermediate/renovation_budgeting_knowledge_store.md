the surrounding market data is treated as normal evidence, but the

## Durable Facts

- A complete interior design project package should include: a dimensioned as-built plan, a demolition plan, and a clean (unfurnished) montage/installation plan; a full set of wall elevations cross-checked against the plans; visualizations used for client sign-off; and node/section drawings resolving junction details. Detailed MEP (electrical/HVAC) engineering schematics are typically a *separate* deliverable from a standard interior design project, not included by default. `confirmed` [source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]
- The visible price gap between a surface-mounted and a built-in (behind-the-wall) plumbing fixture is commonly much smaller than the real installed-cost gap once pipework, fittings, sleeves, and labor are counted — comparing device price alone understates built-in installs. `confirmed` as the source's claim; the specific RUB multiplier behind it is `unverified` (single source). [source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]
- A renovation budget should be planned from the client's actual, reliable available funds (excluding loans, credit lines, and not-yet-earned future income) rather than an aspirational total figure; not clarifying this up front is reported as a common cause of mid-project shortfalls and client/designer disputes. `confirmed` [source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]
- Higher-priced contractor service tiers (on this source's own pricing scale) are described as buying more project-management staffing and process (foreman, senior master, procurement coordinator, supervising designer), not necessarily higher-skilled physical labor. `confirmed` (stated as the speaker's own view/opinion, not verified independently). [source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]

[source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]
- `yt_jAdRyL6KFxs` is a 7komnat.by company case study: Vladimir Ermakov (founder) interviews Aleksey (company director) about Aleksey's own just-completed apartment. Both roles are stated directly in the transcript's opening lines. `confirmed` (level 1) — stronger speaker attribution than most sources processed for this topic, though note this is an internal/marketing case study (the client is the company's own director), not an independent third party. [source: yt_jAdRyL6KFxs]
- **Usable room count is constrained by window count** (a balcony door counts as a quasi-window) before client preference is even considered — more rooms than usable light sources tends to produce impractically small, poorly-lit rooms; a combined "euro-format" layout (one bedroom + one large kitchen-living room) is preferable to splitting a modest apartment into three small rooms. `confirmed`, general design principle. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- **Relocating a kitchen/sink must account for drainage slope, not just supply-line routing**: pressurized water-supply lines can be hidden in screed with little constraint, but gravity-fed sewage lines need a consistent slope toward the riser — the farther a sink is from the riser, the more screed depth is needed to conceal the sloped pipe; placing a sink along a wall (drain routed inside the wall to the riser) avoids this problem better than a mid-room placement. `confirmed`, non-obvious and genuinely useful. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- **Toilet placement should drive bathroom layout, not the reverse**: place the toilet first, as close to the riser as practical, because it requires a 100 mm-diameter drain pipe — the hardest pipe size to conceal — then plan the sink/shower positions around it. `confirmed`, a concrete sequencing rule. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- **"Zashivka" (plumbing cladding box) vs. "venshakhta" (ventilation shaft) is a critical, non-obvious distinction**: a zashivka (developer-built cladding around risers, sometimes oversized) can be demolished and rebuilt more compactly to reclaim space; a venshakhta (ventilation shaft, identifiable by grille openings, typically ~60×40 cm) runs as a single shared vertical column from ground floor to roof serving every apartment on that riser line and **must never be touched, damaged, or removed** — doing so cuts ventilation for every unit sharing the column, not just the renovating one. `confirmed` — the single most important safety-relevant technical fact in this store to date. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- A design project deliverable in a real Minsk turnkey project included 3D visualization and covered replanning approval (moving partitions/radiators, a bathroom doorway shift, a waterproofing sub-project, and obtaining a new technical passport via BRTI) as one bundled service. `confirmed` (level 1). [source: yt_jAdRyL6KFxs]
- A stretch ceiling can contain/retain water from a leak above (forming a temporary pocket) before it's drained or punctured, limiting damage compared to a drywall (GKL) ceiling, which saturates and lets water through to furniture/floors/walls below. `single-account`, anecdotal (one specific flood example given), but it's a distinct risk-based argument for stretch ceilings beyond cost. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- Recommendations for appliance/finish "prestige" level are commonly scoped to a property's market segment (e.g. comfort/business/premium/elite) by resale-focused practitioners, rather than applied as a blanket "always buy cheap" or "always buy premium" rule. `confirmed` as the speaker's stated framework; whether buyers actually perceive/value this distinction is not independently verified. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- A cheap, thin "micro-baseboard" profile exists as a lower-cost visual approximation of a fully concealed in-wall baseboard, trading a small amount of visual/tactile fidelity for a large reduction in installation cost and time (speaker estimates roughly half a day vs. about a month of added schedule for a 100 m² apartment). `confirmed` as the speaker's stated claim; not independently verified. This directly nuances the existing store fact (from `yt_BMmPLHVmnqw`) praising fully concealed baseboards without mentioning this alternative. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- Indoor split-system AC units should follow three placement rules: don't blow cold air directly on occupants (cool the room evenly instead); leave clear space above and below the unit for airflow; position it to be visually unobtrusive. `confirmed`. [source: yt_H61xa8n2nTk]

[source: yt_6Z7uH2_rXsw]

### Doors / Trim (new topic area, added 2026-07-31)

- **Door unit (дверной блок) has five components**: leaf (полотно), frame (коробка), jamb extensions (доборы), casing/trim (наличники), hardware (фурнитура). Standard leaf height 2.0 m; standard leaf widths 60/70/80/90 cm. `confirmed`. [source: yt_ludvy76HGSU]
- **A 60 cm-wide leaf will not pass a 60 cm-wide appliance (e.g. washing machine) through a 60 cm door**, despite matching nominal widths — the frame's rebate/stop (прихлоп, ~1 cm) plus the ~2-3 mm perimeter installation gap reduce the true clear opening below the nominal leaf width. `confirmed`, concrete non-obvious mechanism. [source: yt_ludvy76HGSU]
- **Adding a bottom threshold/rebate (порог с прихлопом) for extra soundproofing is not recommended** — modern interior door soundproofing is already adequate (the audible gain is imperceptible), while a ~3 cm threshold becomes a lasting trip hazard. `confirmed` as speaker's recommendation. [source: yt_ludvy76HGSU]
- **Telescopic jamb extensions (доборы) are superior to standard flat-board doborы** — they slide into a channel in the frame and self-adjust to an uneven wall or a slightly twisted frame, producing better junctions. `confirmed` as speaker's stated preference. [source: yt_ludvy76HGSU]
- **Door installation-plane offset must match the swing direction** — the frame should sit flush with the wall on the side the door swings into (clean foam joint under a flat casing), with the full reveal treatment on the opposite side. Getting this backwards causes the open door to strike/split the opening's corner, limits the swing to ~90° instead of ~95°, and is linked to more serious injuries. `confirmed`, safety-relevant mechanism. [source: yt_ludvy76HGSU]
- **Sliding interior doors have materially worse soundproofing than hinged doors**, even when hidden behind a false wall — a hinged door seals on 3 of 4 sides via its frame rebate; a basic sliding door has essentially no seal. `single-account`, ~80% of the speaker's own sliding-door clients complained about sound, complaints stopped when switched to hinged. [source: yt_1YiVgB9jqyU]
- **Sliding-door mechanisms (rollers, stoppers, soft-close dampers) commonly begin failing en masse ~3-4 years after installation**, and repair requires disassembling substantial finished work (furniture, ceiling, flooring, part of the wall) to reach the hidden mechanism. `single-account`, but the speaker's stated primary reason for abandoning sliding doors in his own practice after using them heavily from ~2009. [source: yt_1YiVgB9jqyU]
- **Sliding doors are appropriate as a narrow exception**: a private ensuite bathroom/shower serving only 1-2 people (e.g. attached to a bedroom for a couple) where a hinged door's swing arc has no viable path — soundproofing/reliability downsides are tolerable specifically because (a) only a small, intimate user group is affected and (b) usage frequency is low. Outside this case, the channel's standing recommendation is not to use sliding interior doors. `confirmed`, real project example plus explicit decision logic. [source: yt_x4TlTKt211Q]
- **A doorless, casing-trimmed opening between two rooms still needs a small wall stub (~10 cm, "при стенок") preserved at each outer corner** so casing can terminate cleanly on both faces of the wall — removing it (without checking with the design technologist first) leaves one face impossible to trim cleanly. `confirmed`, real jobsite mistake-and-fix example. [source: yt_c4b7iyg8v5U]
- **A full-length ("growth") mirror can be mounted directly on an entrance door or entry-area wardrobe leaf** when there's no separate wall space for one — described as cheaper than buying the door and a separate mirror individually, and the entryway makeup/grooming zone is the correct place for it (not the living room or a guest WC). Never hang a mirror directly opposite another mirror (infinite-corridor reflection effect). `confirmed`. [source: yt_b2rxvzj55Q0]
- **Entrance door minimum functional clear/light opening width: ~74 cm (740 mm)**, stated by the speaker as the practical floor below which passing through with furniture (e.g. a non-collapsible armchair) becomes impractical — this floor should take priority over cosmetic rules (e.g. casing standoff) when a hard site constraint (neighboring wall, embedded electrical conduit) forces a choice. `confirmed`, concrete numeric floor from a real constrained case. [source: yt_D2Qz6s8eNoU]
- **The ~10 cm casing-standoff rule is a default, not absolute** — a real documented case kept casing nearly flush to a wall because the opposite directions were blocked by a neighboring apartment's wall and a fixed electrical conduit box; the explicit prioritization rule given is "functional door width over cosmetic junction detail" when the two conflict. `confirmed`. [source: yt_D2Qz6s8eNoU]
- **Relocating a developer-installed entrance door opening (light partition wall) is generally low-complexity**: cut/rebuild the wall section, weld a steel angle-iron frame (~8 mm stock) around the new opening for reinforcement, backfill with cement-sand mortar, weld the door frame to the angle-iron, plaster to conceal it, and finish reveals with standard interior-door jamb-extension panels to match. Cited downsides limited to needing anti-vandal stairwell-plaster repair; no cost figures given for the work itself. `single-account`, real project (~30 cm shift, light aerated-concrete partition — may not generalize to load-bearing walls or larger shifts). [source: yt_oVf6AyxJjd0]
- **Interior door swing-direction rule (a coherent, safety-grounded 5-reason system)**: living-space doors (bedroom, living room, study, kids' room, kitchen, library) should open INWARD, for safety (fewer people pass through the room itself vs. the shared corridor), light-switch ergonomics (handle and switch on the same inside wall, push-to-enter keeps orientation in the dark), open-position convenience (parks against an interior wall vs. blocking a often-only-~1m-wide corridor), floor-stopper placement (inside the room vs. a corridor trip hazard), and closing convenience (reachable from the corridor). WC/bathroom doors (and small utility rooms/closets) should open OUTWARD, for space (bathrooms have less interior area than the corridor) and — the safety-critical reason — so an incapacitated occupant blocking the door from inside can still be reached without the door striking them. Quick heuristic given: if the light switch is inside the room, the door opens inward; if outside, it opens outward. `confirmed` as speaker's consistently-applied rule; speaker states <1% of his own projects use an inward-opening WC door for unexplained edge-case reasons. [source: yt_7Vd95idVXak]

### Electrical / Lighting (new topic area, added 2026-08-04)

> All 29 entries in this subsection come from one channel (Zemstandart / Alexey Zemskov) — `single-account`/`unverified` per this store's corroboration rule throughout, even where a claim repeats across several of the channel's own videos. Feeds `12_Engineering_and_Systems/Electrical_and_Lighting.md`.

**Mounting heights & positioning**

- **Switch height is a fixed 90 cm from finished floor, independent of occupant height** — a repeated on-camera demonstration (four people, heights 150–185 cm) shows the resting hand-drop height from standing is essentially constant across adults; switch center is placed 15 cm from the rough door-opening edge (moving it to 10 cm causes the switch to conflict with door casing). A household with unusually tall occupants may use 95 cm instead of 90 cm. `confirmed`, repeated demonstration. [source: yt_uLJGsTfTj3A, yt_l_rjjPlPkRo]
- **Bedside electrical points scale off furniture, not a fixed number**: bedside lamp switch ~120 cm (headboard-mounted) or ~70 cm (nightstand-mounted) from floor; bedside outlets ~70 cm; an over-bed pendant/lamp cable drop at ~100 cm; a formula combining false-wall offset + curtain-zone clearance + nightstand width + half the bed's width sets the horizontal position. `confirmed` as the channel's own design formula. [source: yt_uLJGsTfTj3A, yt_VK8rCdChdbY]
- **Countertop outlets sit 110 cm from finished floor by default**, and shift by the same delta as the countertop height when a non-standard (e.g. +10 cm) countertop is planned — measured from floor, not from the not-yet-installed countertop, because the countertop's exact future height is harder to plan against reliably. Window-slope outlets are the explicit exception, measured from the slope/sill instead of the floor; ceiling-referenced points (e.g. AC unit alignment) use the ceiling as datum. `confirmed`. [source: yt_uLJGsTfTj3A, yt_12o621100MQ]
- **A small set of point types should track occupant eye level rather than the fixed heights above**: video-intercom monitors and underfloor-heating thermostats, both read visually while standing/using them, unlike switches which are operated by feel. `confirmed`. [source: yt_uLJGsTfTj3A]

**Cable sizing, circuits & panel design**

- **Cable-gauge-by-load table (copper, this channel's standard)**: 1.5 mm² for lighting circuits, 2.5 mm² for socket/outlet circuits, 4 mm² for ovens, 6 mm² for cooktops/high-draw appliances; a 4–6 mm² high-load circuit should never be split or branched partway. `confirmed`. [source: yt_JrClDJb8WTM]
- **Breaker curve selection**: curve B for socket circuits (tolerates appliance inrush current without nuisance-tripping), curve C for lighting and AC circuits; curve D is not recommended for residential use. Breaker amperage is paired to cable gauge (roughly 10A/1.5mm², 16A/2.5mm², 20A/4mm², 32A/6mm² in this channel's convention). `confirmed`, internally consistent with the circuit-formula fact below. [source: yt_JrClDJb8WTM, yt_ciXeqvKDKSI]
- **Panel circuit-count formula**: minimum 2 circuits per room (one lighting, one sockets), +1 dedicated circuit for AC; oven and cooktop each get their own dedicated circuit; wet rooms (bathroom/WC/kitchen) require an RCD/differential breaker. `confirmed`, consistent with the wire/breaker pairing table above. [source: yt_ciXeqvKDKSI]
- **Route electrical conduit above plumbing runs on the ceiling, not stacked on the floor** — avoids compounding conduit+pipe thickness inside the screed depth near door thresholds, a common source of a raised threshold lip. Conduit should clear adjacent walls by 15 cm minimum (20–25 cm preferred) for installation and future baguette/trim clearance. `confirmed`, guest electrician's stated practice. [source: yt_Onu15qOeWGA]
- **On walls slated for later acoustic insulation, recess socket boxes deeper than a standard wall during rough-in** — otherwise the finished box ends up proud of the insulated wall surface once the soundproofing layer is added (this generalizes an already-recorded fact about back-box offset in this store's Do's/Don'ts; here it's the recessed-box-depth version of the same underlying problem). [source: yt_Onu15qOeWGA]
- **Sample only the ground (yellow-green) conductor when checking an existing de-energized service-entry cable's gauge** — avoids disturbing live/neutral conductors unnecessarily; extending an undersized service cable uses crimp ferrule sleeves (to convert stranded wire to a solid-equivalent end before screw-terminal connections) and heat-shrink gel splice kits for any in-line joint. `confirmed`, procedural detail. [source: yt_alnV3DYCDlg]

**Temporary / construction-stage wiring**

- **Temporary (rough-in-stage) wiring uses a different, deliberately simpler spec than permanent wiring**: 2×2.5 mm² ungrounded (vs. permanent 3×1.5 mm² for lighting / 3×2.5 mm² for sockets, both grounded) — grounding is treated as unnecessary for the temporary stage. Install temp wiring after the design/electrical plan exists but before interior walls go up, and after ceiling priming (not before). Keep temp cable runs continuous/unbroken (no in-line splices) and maintain ≥1 m between a temporary bulb socket and any outlet block (heat and cable-tugging risk). Reserve a dedicated ~10A circuit for a job-site charging station. `confirmed`. [source: yt_VT-zl1Xay4A, yt_957RF_DcjwA]
- **Off-the-shelf retail extension cords are commonly undersized for job-site power draw** (this channel cites ~0.75 mm² conductors) — build a job-site extension from proper 2.5 mm² cable instead of relying on a retail cord for sustained tool/heater loads. `confirmed` as the channel's stated practice; no independent verification of the 0.75 mm² figure. [source: yt_VT-zl1Xay4A]
- **Leave 1–2 working temporary outlets plus one working light per room during rough-in**, even on a multi-floor build before any finish work starts — improves site usability/safety during the build itself. `single-account`, thin technical detail (customer-testimonial-style source). [source: yt_9L9xTWQLZZ8]

**Switches & controls**

- **All standard toggle-style switches in an apartment should share the same ON/OFF orientation** (e.g. down = on, consistently) — a mixed-orientation install is a common, avoidable annoyance. Prefer several single-gang switches over one crowded multi-gang switch plate where space allows. Never position a switch inside a future door's swing zone (checked against the door leaf's full opening arc, not just its closed position). `confirmed`. [source: yt_8eECI5sWEy4]
- **Two-way switches work in pairs; a three-or-more-point control run adds "intermediate" (crossover/перекрёстный) switches between the two end switches**, not more two-way switches — a distinct component from a standard two-way switch. Wire joints must terminate in a proper junction box; twisted-and-taped joints left exposed outside a junction box are flagged as a common, unsafe jobsite shortcut. `confirmed`. [source: yt_VXfpDJruyVs]
- **A bathroom/WC exhaust fan's switch belongs inside the room it serves, not in the adjoining hallway.** Automating a fan is acceptable for auto-OFF (a timer that shuts it off after a delay) but not for auto-ON (an occupancy/humidity sensor that starts it without warning) — the auto-ON case is described as startling/unpleasant for the occupant. `confirmed`, opinion-based but mechanism-specific. [source: yt_KFBS2wgOzrs]
- **Buy finish-stage switches/outlets/frames roughly one week before installation, not months in advance** — reduces storage damage/loss risk and avoids having already bought fixtures invalidated by a mid-project design change. Count the actual number of finish electrical points room-by-room after rough-in is complete (not from the original drawing, since e.g. kitchen point counts commonly shift once final cabinetry is confirmed); keep a small spare stock (roughly 3–4 spare outlets and a few spare switches/frames of each type used, for a ~100 m² apartment) for later breakage/loss. `confirmed`. [source: yt_PIYzs2b4UDU]
- **Prefer spring-clamp (screwless, self-clamping) terminals over screw terminals on finish-stage outlets/switches** — screw terminals are more prone to loosening from thermal cycling over time; evaluate a device by country of manufacture and terminal type rather than by brand name alone. Avoid switches with an integrated LED backlight/indicator (reported flicker/glow issue on some LED bulb types); finalize the actual bulbs being used before buying dimmers, since dimmer compatibility depends on matching leading-edge vs. trailing-edge dimming to the bulb/driver type. `confirmed` as the channel's stated preference. [source: yt_nmVie-r0iis]

**Lighting design & light source types**

- **Four-category lighting-design framework**: main/general light, task light, decorative light, and night light, each with a distinct recommended color temperature (~4000K for main/task, ~2700–3000K for decorative/night) and a ≥500 lux comfort target for main/task lighting. Night light must be present in hallways and WCs but should **never** be installed in bedrooms (disrupts sleep); decorative lighting is explicitly not a substitute for a dedicated night-light circuit. `confirmed`, coherent taxonomy — the clearest lighting-category framework in this store to date. [source: yt_iRbTlv5Ka9o]
- **Perimeter LED strip lighting (in a stretch-ceiling baguette channel) is decorative/supplementary only** and is never sufficient as a room's main light source, even when a client is told otherwise. Two distinct "main light" fixture types are described: flat light panels (bright, energy-efficient, but difficult to retrofit onto an existing stretch ceiling since they need mounting holes the ceiling wasn't prepped for) and softboxes (fixtures that emit light from their full surface/sides, not just the underside — cited as giving the most even room-fill light at comparatively low wattage, and the channel's own stated preference). `confirmed` as the channel's stated preference and mechanism explanation. [source: yt_GkpOGG_cxM0]

**Rough-in materials, quality checks & hiring red flags**

- **A hired electrician's own tool kit is a usable vetting signal**: a dust-extraction vacuum attached to the drill during wall-chasing/socket-hole cutting is treated as a mandatory red-flag check (its absence means the crew is spreading masonry dust through the unit's cavities, a later air-quality issue); round, cleanly drilled socket-box holes (vs. hacked/oversized square holes) indicate a competent installer. Standard on-center spacing for a ganged multi-socket box run is 71 mm; modern outlets commonly use self-clamping (screwless) terminals. `confirmed`, checkable homeowner-facing signals. [source: yt_XmeoZUiGwBM]
- **Strip cable insulation only with a dedicated wire-stripping tool, never a utility/box knife** — a utility knife risks nicking the conductor's copper strands, a latent failure point. Remove a device's factory-installed mounting screws before rough-finish work (plastering/puttying), not at final finish-electrical install — screw holes left in during finishing get clogged with paint/putty and have to be re-drilled later. `confirmed`, jobsite technique. [source: yt_hqkdbicp994]
- **A window-slope-mounted outlet/switch panel must be glued (foam adhesive) to the slope structure, not merely clipped in** — a loose/wobbly slope outlet when unplugging a cord is a simple, homeowner-testable sign of a bad glue job. ## Rules / Heuristics

*The wiki pages already contain synthesized heuristics (buffer %, rough/finish material split, labor-vs-total ratio) traced to the Minsk case studies above. Newly added from this pass:*

- Before setting a renovation budget tier, confirm which portion of the client's stated total is guaranteed cash on hand vs. loans/credit/future income. [source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]
- When comparing built-in vs. surface-mounted fixture options, price the full installation (pipework, fittings, labor), not just the device sticker price. [source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]
- Vet a contractor via genuine (non-arranged) reviews, an in-person visit to a real completed past project, and review of the actual signed contract. [source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]
- A complete design project package should include dimensioned as-built/demolition/clean-montage plans, cross-checked elevations, visualizations, and node/section drawings; MEP engineering is typically out of scope by default. [source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]
- Hire independent technical supervision (not affiliated with the renovation contractor) to sign off on each construction stage before the next stage builds over it. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- A single multi-trade "brigade" contracting model is often cheaper than hiring per-trade specialists individually, for a comparable quality outcome most owners won't perceive the difference on. `unverified`. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- Where hardware quality genuinely affects longevity (e.g. drawer slides/hinges), paying a premium for a known-durable brand can be worth it even under an otherwise "don't overpay for brand" approach — treat this as an explicit, narrow exception, not a blanket rule. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- For entryway flooring specifically, prefer a hard, scratch-resistant surface (e.g. porcelain tile) over a wood-look surface, even where wood-look flooring is otherwise the preferred choice elsewhere in the home — tracked-in grit is the reason given. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- Before finalizing a room-count layout, check window count first (a balcony door counts as a quasi-window) — more rooms than usable light sources tends to produce impractically small, poorly-lit rooms. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- When relocating a sink, place it along a wall (not mid-room) so the gravity-fed drain line can run inside the wall to the riser without losing the required slope. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- Interior door rough openings should be sized ~10 cm wider than the leaf (5 cm clearance per side); check door-swing paths for furniture conflicts before defaulting to an 80 cm leaf everywhere. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- A wet zone (kitchen or bathroom) generally must stay stacked above the same wet zone in the apartment below; even a free-planning/flexible-layout unit still needs formal approval to deviate from this. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- Avoid a bathroom entrance directly off a kitchen/dining area; keep the bathroom door at least ~1–1.5 m from the entryway to avoid frequent crossing of the "dirty" entry zone. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- Never touch, damage, or attempt to remove a ventilation shaft ("venshakhta," identifiable by grille openings, typically ~60×40 cm) — it serves every apartment on that riser column, not just the unit being renovated. A plumbing cladding box ("zashivka") is a different structure and can often be rebuilt more compactly. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- Default to leaving developer-installed radiators in place — relocating one requires its own approval project and an energy-efficiency recalculation, a real bureaucratic cost most projects can avoid. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- Engage a specialist for replanning approval from the start rather than attempting to self-manage the process to save money — a documented attempt to DIY it cost one project a full month with nothing to show for it. `single-account`. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- A dedicated laundry/utility room, where floor plan allows, is a comparatively low-cost, high comfort-value addition; where space doesn't allow it, a combo washer-dryer unit is a reasonable substitute for a separate stacked dryer. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- Rough AC sizing rule of thumb: ~1 kW cooling capacity per 10 m² of room area, minimum practical unit size 2 kW, with a ~20% capacity buffer as standard practice — adjust upward for sun exposure or rooms that share airflow with an open door. `confirmed` as the source's own stated rule of thumb, explicitly flagged by the source itself as approximate. [source: yt_H61xa8n2nTk]
- Buy AC equipment and installation from the same company where possible, to get a single bundled warranty rather than having to attribute fault between separate equipment and installation vendors after a failure. [source: yt_H61xa8n2nTk]
- Photograph in-wall drain/refrigerant line routing during installation, to reduce the risk of later wall work (e.g. hanging shelves, drilling) damaging a hidden line. [source: yt_H61xa8n2nTk]
- Price water/heating rough-in by fixture "points" (e.g. a sink needs 2: hot + cold), not by linear meters of pipe — pricing by pipe length is a common vector for "turns out we needed more, pay more" upselling. [source: yt_cdNwbqsLUK4]
- Price electrical rough-in by floor area, not by cable length, for the same anti-upsell reason. [source: yt_cdNwbqsLUK4]
- Verify a smeta specifies plaster/putty quality grade and flooring installation pattern (straight/diagonal/herringbone) explicitly — both materially affect cost and are common sources of "we assumed the cheaper option, pay extra now" disputes if left unspecified. [source: yt_cdNwbqsLUK4]
- Watch for scope commonly added mid-project rather than itemized upfront (partition-wall reinforcement/opening framing, plaster corner-bead/window-reveal work, ancillary tile-cutting work) and common-area/lobby protective-covering requirements some residential complexes impose — verify these appear as explicit line items in the original smeta. [source: yt_cdNwbqsLUK4]
- Match renovation ambition to the property's own class — don't commission a premium designer renovation in a low-value unit, and don't do bare cosmetic work in an expensive new-build. [source: yt_OrKB6uSKRyk]
- If budget-constrained, prefer buying an already-finished (even unremarkable) developer unit over a bare-shell unit you might not have enough funds to fully renovate — running out of money mid-project (e.g. stalling at tiling) produces a worse outcome. [source: yt_OrKB6uSKRyk]
- If self-managing on a tight budget, it's reasonable to reduce the plumbing equipment set itself (fewer valves/reducers = fewer failure points), as distinct from just downgrading brand within a fixed set. [source: yt_OrKB6uSKRyk]
- Don't save incrementally toward a "someday" quality renovation — renovation costs (materials, furniture, labor) tend to inflate faster than typical personal savings accumulate; commit to a simplified scope within current means now, or defer the decision entirely. `single-account`, opinion. [source: yt_OrKB6uSKRyk]
- Never quote or compare a renovation by "price per m²" alone — always ask what specific material/build-quality/complexity level that figure assumes, since the same visual outcome can vary 2–10× in real cost. [source: yt_6Z7uH2_rXsw]
- When evaluating a material for a given application, separate "will it survive functionally" (durability) from "will it look/install correctly" (quality) — a budget material can be equally durable while being harder or impossible to install well due to poor geometry/consistency. [source: yt_6Z7uH2_rXsw]
- Don't prioritize spending on labor/execution quality over materials, and don't cut cost on rough materials or concealed systems (heating, plumbing, junctions) — poor execution undermines even good materials, and concealed-system replacement later is expensive or impossible. [source: yt_6Z7uH2_rXsw]
- When budgeting, separately account for "wishes" (a specific named product/brand target) and "expectations" (a described functional requirement) as their own cost drivers — both can swing a single line item's cost by up to 10×, independent of overall quality tier. [source: yt_6Z7uH2_rXsw]
- Prefer a local wall-mounted "breather" ventilation unit over a full mechanical supply-and-exhaust system for most apartments — breathers are claimed to solve the large majority of comfort/air-quality needs at a fraction of the cost; reserve a full system for apartments on loud arterial roads where windows can't be opened for ventilation. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- A specific, checkable upsell red flag: a company that deliberately underbids an initial quote by ~10–15%, then adds roughly 40% more once work is underway — treat an unusually cheap initial quote from a company with skepticism rather than as a win. [source: yt_cdNwbqsLUK4]
- For a child's room specifically, place the bed away from the window/heat source and the desk closer to the natural light source. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- When budgeting a multi-month self-managed project in a currency loosely pegged to USD, confirm and track exchange-rate drift explicitly rather than assuming a fixed rate — a real project's own rate moved from 2.6 to 2.8 BYN/USD over its duration, meaningfully affecting the USD-equivalent total even with local-currency costs unchanged. [source: yt_8zm_5NjbGNA]
- Budget contingency isn't only relevant to turnkey/company-managed projects — even a disciplined, itemized self-managed renovation can meaningfully overshoot its own target (here, ~30%); treat a stated self-managed target figure as a floor to plan around, not a ceiling to expect. [source: yt_8zm_5NjbGNA]
- When switching a major appliance's install method late (e.g. freestanding to built-in) after rough electrical is already done, budget for a same-day emergency callout to relocate the affected outlet — a small, concrete cost of late decisions. [source: yt_8zm_5NjbGNA]
- Default interior door leaf widths: 80 cm for living spaces (70 cm only if 80 doesn't physically fit; never 90 cm), 70 cm for wet rooms/utility rooms as the speaker's revised preference (60 cm only if space is very tight). Size the rough opening 10 cm wider than the leaf, and never let the finished casing sit flush against an adjacent wall/corner. [source: yt_ludvy76HGSU]
- Avoid sliding interior doors by default (soundproofing, reliability, and repairability are all materially worse than hinged doors); reserve them for a narrow exception — a private ensuite serving only 1-2 people where a hinged door's swing has no viable path — and even then expect roller/stopper/damper mechanism failures within a few years. [source: yt_1YiVgB9jqyU; yt_x4TlTKt211Q]
- When forming any casing-trimmed opening (with or without a door leaf) between two rooms, preserve a small wall stub (~10 cm) at each outer corner so casing can terminate cleanly on both faces — don't let a site crew remove it without checking against the original design/technologist plan. [source: yt_c4b7iyg8v5U]
- Treat a door's own casing-standoff/junction-detail conventions as defaults, not absolutes — when a hard physical constraint (a neighboring wall, an embedded utility box) forces a trade-off, prioritize a door's functional clear width (never go below roughly 74 cm for an entrance door) over the cosmetic standoff rule. [source: yt_D2Qz6s8eNoU]
- Set interior door swing direction by room type: living spaces (bedroom, living room, study, kids' room, kitchen) open inward; WCs/bathrooms/small utility rooms open outward (partly for space, and critically so an incapacitated occupant blocking the door from inside can still be reached). Quick check: a room whose light switch is inside gets an inward-opening door; a room whose switch is outside gets an outward-opening door. [source: yt_7Vd95idVXak]

### Electrical / Lighting (new topic area, added 2026-08-04)

- Set switch heights (90 cm from floor, 15 cm from door edge) and bedside/countertop outlet heights (70 cm / 110 cm) as fixed defaults across an entire apartment, not per-occupant — they're ergonomic constants (hand-drop height, furniture standards), not something that scales with who lives there. [source: yt_uLJGsTfTj3A, yt_l_rjjPlPkRo]
- Size and select circuit breakers by pairing curve type to load (curve B sockets, curve C lighting/AC) and amperage to cable gauge, not by amperage alone — an oversized breaker on undersized cable defeats the breaker's protective purpose. [source: yt_JrClDJb8WTM, yt_ciXeqvKDKSI]
- Count and buy finish-stage electrical devices room-by-room from the as-built rough-in, roughly a week before install, not from the original drawing months in advance — kitchen point counts in particular commonly shift once final cabinetry is confirmed. [source: yt_PIYzs2b4UDU]
- When vetting an electrical contractor, check for a dust-extraction vacuum on their tools and inspect drilled socket-box holes for clean, round cuts — both are checkable-by-a-non-expert signals of installation quality. [source: yt_XmeoZUiGwBM]
- Plan electrical layout against a 5-15 year family-change horizon (not just move-in day), and leave a few extra outlets wired-but-hidden behind the finished wall surface as a low-cost future-proofing move. [source: yt_k7VUlw8gHZs]
