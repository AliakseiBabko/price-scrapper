## Rules / Heuristics

*The wiki pages already contain synthesized heuristics (buffer %, rough/finish material split, labor-vs-total ratio) traced to the Minsk case studies above. Newly added from this pass:*

- Before setting a renovation budget tier, confirm which portion of the client's stated total is guaranteed cash on hand vs. loans/credit/future income. [source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]
- When comparing built-in vs. surface-mounted fixture options, price the full installation (pipework, fittings, labor), not just the device sticker price. [source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]
- Vet a contractor via genuine (non-arranged) reviews, an in-person visit to a real completed past project, and review of the actual signed contract. [source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]
- A complete design project package should include dimensioned as-built/demolition/clean-montage plans, cross-checked elevations, visualizations, and node/section drawings; MEP engineering is typically out of scope by default. [source: yt_BMmPLHVmnqw]
- Hire independent technical supervision (not affiliated with the renovation contractor) to sign off on each construction stage before the next stage builds over it. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- A single multi-trade "brigade" contracting model is often cheaper than hiring per-trade specialists individually, for a comparable quality outcome most owners won't perceive the difference on. `unverified`. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- Where to economize on a two-inlet-node apartment (kitchen + bathroom stacks): a secondary inlet node's build cost could roughly halve if simplified/skipped, while other plumbing work should still be done properly if funds allow — "if money doesn't allow, get by with the minimum." `single-account`, `unverified`, no absolute figure given. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_q4SSpq9jc3g_sidorik_metal_plastic_pipes_ep26|q4SSpq9jc3g_sidorik_metal_plastic_pipes_ep26]]]
- General fixture-placement research method: search "сантехника, разметка, размер" (image search) for widely-shared standard-height reference diagrams, then always cross-check the specific equipment's own installation manual before finalizing marking. `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_S4fOYedAruE_sidorik_sewer_installation_ep24|S4fOYedAruE_sidorik_sewer_installation_ep24]]]
- General "euro-renovation" cost heuristic (qualitative, no currency figure attached): total renovation cost runs roughly a third of the apartment's own market value. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_N36scNqRwII_sidorik_water_heater_installation_errors|N36scNqRwII_sidorik_water_heater_installation_errors]]]
- **A dedicated "plumbing equipment project" (проект на сантехническое оборудование), distinct from a design project**: have an engineer draft a project specifying exactly what plumbing equipment will be installed and the installation *sequence*, separate from a designer's project (which only shows supply/drain outlet locations and installation-frame position). Framed as protection against plumber error — including from an unqualified plumber found through classifieds — since every nuance is worked out and agreed between engineer, homeowner, and installer in advance. `single-account`. No dedicated page fits this planning-process concept yet; flag in Pending Wiki-Page Decisions if a third source raises the same concept. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_Cj2U_wVlG-I_sidorik_plumbing_intro_node_errors|Cj2U_wVlG-I_sidorik_plumbing_intro_node_errors]]]
- Where hardware quality genuinely affects longevity (e.g. drawer slides/hinges), paying a premium for a known-durable brand can be worth it even under an otherwise "don't overpay for brand" approach — treat this as an explicit, narrow exception, not a blanket rule. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- For entryway flooring specifically, prefer a hard, scratch-resistant surface (e.g. porcelain tile) over a wood-look surface, even where wood-look flooring is otherwise the preferred choice elsewhere in the home — tracked-in grit is the reason given. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- Before finalizing a room-count layout, check window count first (a balcony door counts as a quasi-window) — more rooms than usable light sources tends to produce impractically small, poorly-lit rooms. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- When relocating a sink, place it along a wall (not mid-room) so the gravity-fed drain line can run inside the wall to the riser without losing the required slope. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- Interior door rough openings should be sized ~10 cm wider than the leaf (5 cm clearance per side); check door-swing paths for furniture conflicts before defaulting to an 80 cm leaf everywhere. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- A wet zone (kitchen or bathroom) generally must stay stacked above the same wet zone in the apartment below; even a free-planning/flexible-layout unit still needs formal approval to deviate from this. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- Avoid a bathroom entrance directly off a kitchen/dining area; keep the bathroom door at least ~1–1.5 m from the entryway to avoid frequent crossing of the "dirty" entry zone. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- Never touch, damage, or attempt to remove a ventilation shaft ("venshakhta," identifiable by grille openings, typically ~60×40 cm) — it serves every apartment on that riser column, not just the unit being renovated. A plumbing cladding box ("zashivka") is a different structure and can often be rebuilt more compactly. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- Default to leaving developer-installed radiators in place — relocating one requires its own approval project and an energy-efficiency recalculation, a real bureaucratic cost most projects can avoid. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- Engage a specialist for replanning approval from the start rather than attempting to self-manage the process to save money — a documented attempt to DIY it cost one project a full month with nothing to show for it. `single-account`. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- A dedicated laundry/utility room, where floor plan allows, is a comparatively low-cost, high comfort-value addition; where space doesn't allow it, a combo washer-dryer unit is a reasonable substitute for a separate stacked dryer. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- Rough AC sizing rule of thumb: ~1 kW cooling capacity per 10 m² of room area, minimum practical unit size 2 kW, with a ~20% capacity buffer as standard practice — adjust upward for sun exposure or rooms that share airflow with an open door. `confirmed` as the source's own stated rule of thumb, explicitly flagged by the source itself as approximate. [source: yt_H61xa8n2nTk]
- Buy AC equipment and installation from the same company where possible, to get a single bundled warranty rather than having to attribute fault between separate equipment and installation vendors after a failure. [source: yt_H61xa8n2nTk]
- Photograph in-wall drain/refrigerant line routing during installation, to reduce the risk of later wall work (e.g. hanging shelves, drilling) damaging a hidden line. [source: yt_H61xa8n2nTk]
- Price water/heating rough-in by fixture "points" (e.g. a sink needs 2: hot + cold), not by linear meters of pipe — pricing by pipe length is a common vector for "turns out we needed more, pay more" upselling. [source: yt_cdNwbqsLUK4]
- Price electrical rough-in by floor area, not by cable length, for the same anti-upsell reason. [source: yt_cdNwbqsLUK4]
- Verify a smeta specifies plaster/putty quality grade and flooring installation pattern (straight/diagonal/herringbone) explicitly — both materially affect cost and are common sources of "we assumed the cheaper option, pay extra now" disputes if left unspecified. [source: yt_cdNwbqsLUK4]
- Watch for scope commonly added mid-project rather than itemized upfront (partition-wall reinforcement/opening framing, plaster corner-bead/window-reveal work, ancillary tile-cutting work) and common-area/lobby protective-covering requirements some residential complexes impose — verify these appear as explicit line items in the original smeta. [source: yt_cdNwbqsLUK4]
- Match renovation ambition to the property's own class — don't commission a premium designer renovation in a low-value unit, and don't do bare cosmetic work in an expensive new-build. [source: yt_OrKB6uSKRyk]
- If budget-constrained, prefer buying an already-finished (even unremarkable) developer unit over a bare-shell unit you might not have enough funds to fully renovate — running out of money mid-project (e.g. stalling at tiling) produces a worse outcome. [source: yt_OrKB6uSKRyk]
- If self-managing on a tight budget, it's reasonable to reduce the plumbing equipment set itself (fewer valves/reducers = fewer failure points), as distinct from just downgrading brand within a fixed set. [source: yt_OrKB6uSKRyk]
- Don't save incrementally toward a "someday" quality renovation — renovation costs (materials, furniture, labor) tend to inflate faster than typical personal savings accumulate; commit to a simplified scope within current means now, or defer the decision entirely. `single-account`, opinion. [source: yt_OrKB6uSKRyk]
- Never quote or compare a renovation by "price per m²" alone — always ask what specific material/build-quality/complexity level that figure assumes, since the same visual outcome can vary 2–10× in real cost. [source: yt_6Z7uH2_rXsw]
- When evaluating a material for a given application, separate "will it survive functionally" (durability) from "will it look/install correctly" (quality) — a budget material can be equally durable while being harder or impossible to install well due to poor geometry/consistency. [source: yt_6Z7uH2_rXsw]
- Don't prioritize spending on labor/execution quality over materials, and don't cut cost on rough materials or concealed systems (heating, plumbing, junctions) — poor execution undermines even good materials, and concealed-system replacement later is expensive or impossible. [source: yt_6Z7uH2_rXsw]
- When budgeting, separately account for "wishes" (a specific named product/brand target) and "expectations" (a described functional requirement) as their own cost drivers — both can swing a single line item's cost by up to 10×, independent of overall quality tier. [source: yt_6Z7uH2_rXsw]

### RemProektMD (Chișinău, Moldova — first non-Belarus/Russia market source, added 2026-08-24)

> [!NOTE]
> Chișinău is a genuinely different national market from every other source in this store (Moscow/Minsk/provincial-Russia) — per this store's location-AND-year rule, none of the figures below are comparable to any RUB/BYN benchmark elsewhere in this store. EUR was the currency actually stated (not the anticipated MDL); `tools/pricing/currency_converter.py` supports neither, so no USD equivalent is computable for either.

- A small apartment (30-35 m²) carries a higher $/m² renovation cost than a large apartment (~200 m²) of the same building type, for a structural reason (not opportunism): every unit needs one full bathroom regardless of size, and the bathroom is the single most expensive room-type per m² — its fixed cost is spread over fewer total m² in a small unit. [source: yt_zBF3iPJfbaw]
- New-build costs less than old-fund ("старый фонд") turnkey renovation for a checkable set of reasons, not just "older building = more expensive": old-fund additionally requires full plaster removal to the base structural material (old plaster won't reliably bond new plaster or will eventually detach with it), full screed removal to the structural slab (not partial), added thermal/sound insulation in the new screed (expanded clay or extruded polystyrene), and frequent sewage-riser/radiator replacement — none of which a new-build needs. [source: yt_zBF3iPJfbaw]
- Screed flatness QC: check with a 2-meter straightedge; if deviation exceeds 2-3mm over 2 meters, level it (self-leveling compound preferred over building up tile adhesive). A new-build's screed being marketed "ready for laminate" is very rarely actually true once measured. [source: yt_zBF3iPJfbaw]
- If a building is more than 20 years old, never keep the existing screed even if it looks flat — it becomes friable over time and will eventually delaminate under new flooring. [source: yt_zBF3iPJfbaw]
- A new-build's white-painted ceiling is not evidence it's level — check with a laser level; a bare monolithic slab passed only with dry-mix (no leveling) is the norm, and turns out out-of-level "most often" once actually measured. A drywall-boxed ceiling and a plastered-on-lath ceiling cost roughly the same for the same slab once genuinely out of level — there's no cheap "just repaint" shortcut once that's confirmed. [source: yt_zBF3iPJfbaw]
- When a developer's new-build pipe replacement is in question (polypropylene vs. Rehau), a pressure test ("опрессовка") can justify leaving existing pipes if the client declines replacement — but pipes holding pressure today can still fail in year 3-4; this company has seen at least one real leak occur after a client declined replacement. [source: yt_zBF3iPJfbaw]
- Build 10-15% contingency into an itemized quote for unforeseen expenses — not always the contractor's fault; sometimes client-initiated changes, sometimes genuinely unforeseen. [source: yt_zBF3iPJfbaw]

### RemProektMD — 5 Contractor-Scam Red Flags (added 2026-08-24, fully region-agnostic)

- Refusal to sign a written contract ("we're not a company, we're a good crew") is not a valid excuse — an individual, not just a registered company, can be bound by a contract, and it should state the agreed sum. [source: yt_OcqynathaX8]
- Refusal to produce an itemized quote (only a rough total or bare $/m² rate) is a red flag — a common precursor to "this wasn't included" disputes once the client has paid the full agreed sum; unforeseen work arising mid-renovation should be accounted for in the smeta process, not left as a later surprise. [source: yt_OcqynathaX8]
- Be wary of a crew claiming to be "universal" (equally good at electrical, plumbing, painting, plastering, tiling) — per this source's professional experience, a real career rarely masters more than 1-2 trades; a self-described generalist is often either weak across most trades or genuinely skilled in one while mediocre in the rest. `single-account`/opinion. [source: yt_OcqynathaX8]
- Refusal to show completed past projects is a red flag — a legitimate, long-operating contractor typically retains workable relationships with former clients and can arrange a viewing with their permission. [source: yt_OcqynathaX8]
- An unrealistically short quoted timeline (2-3 weeks, or even a month) for a full renovation spanning electrical + plumbing + plastering + painting together is a red flag — the source's stated floor for that combined scope is a minimum of 2-3-4 months; a much shorter quote means either a fictional timeline or rushed, lower-quality work. [source: yt_OcqynathaX8]
- Prefer a local wall-mounted "breather" ventilation unit over a full mechanical supply-and-exhaust system for most apartments — breathers are claimed to solve the large majority of comfort/air-quality needs at a fraction of the cost; reserve a full system for apartments on loud arterial roads where windows can't be opened for ventilation. [source: yt_DsdLa87Acz4]
- A specific, checkable upsell red flag: a company that deliberately underbids an initial quote by ~10–15%, then adds roughly 40% more once work is underway — treat an unusually cheap initial quote from a company with skepticism rather than as a win. [source: yt_cdNwbqsLUK4]
- For a child's room specifically, place the bed away from the window/heat source and the desk closer to the natural light source. [source: yt_QHl1YEHMfgE]
- When budgeting a multi-month self-managed project in a currency loosely pegged to USD, confirm and track exchange-rate drift explicitly rather than assuming a fixed rate — a real project's own rate moved from 2.6 to 2.8 BYN/USD over its duration, meaningfully affecting the USD-equivalent total even with local-currency costs unchanged. [source: yt_8zm_5NjbGNA]
- Budget contingency isn't only relevant to turnkey/company-managed projects — even a disciplined, itemized self-managed renovation can meaningfully overshoot its own target (here, ~30%); treat a stated self-managed target figure as a floor to plan around, not a ceiling to expect. [source: yt_8zm_5NjbGNA]
- When switching a major appliance's install method late (e.g. freestanding to built-in) after rough electrical is already done, budget for a same-day emergency callout to relocate the affected outlet — a small, concrete cost of late decisions. [source: yt_8zm_5NjbGNA]
- Default interior door leaf widths: 80 cm for living spaces (70 cm only if 80 doesn't physically fit; never 90 cm), 70 cm for wet rooms/utility rooms as the speaker's revised preference (60 cm only if space is very tight). Size the rough opening 10 cm wider than the leaf, and never let the finished casing sit flush against an adjacent wall/corner. [source: yt_ludvy76HGSU]
- Always demolish and rebuild standard brick boxing ("короб") around a plumbing/heating riser rather than leaving it in place, for three reasons: it hides leaks/defects until they surface after finishes are complete (by which point only mitigation, not repair, is possible); it blocks soundproofing the riser itself; and it typically costs ~15 cm of usable floor area versus building the finish wall directly at the riser's own inspection-valve plane. `single-account`, sourced from a commercial (dental clinic) project — the demolish-and-inspect rationale generalizes, but the specific corridor-width code figure cited alongside it (120 cm minimum) is a medical-facility standard, not confirmed residential. [source: yt_PO9kd1cqCyA]
- Avoid sliding interior doors by default (soundproofing, reliability, and repairability are all materially worse than hinged doors); reserve them for a narrow exception — a private ensuite serving only 1-2 people where a hinged door's swing has no viable path — and even then expect roller/stopper/damper mechanism failures within a few years. [source: yt_1YiVgB9jqyU; yt_x4TlTKt211Q]
- When forming any casing-trimmed opening (with or without a door leaf) between two rooms, preserve a small wall stub (~10 cm) at each outer corner so casing can terminate cleanly on both faces — don't let a site crew remove it without checking against the original design/technologist plan. [source: yt_c4b7iyg8v5U]
- Treat a door's own casing-standoff/junction-detail conventions as defaults, not absolutes — when a hard physical constraint (a neighboring wall, an embedded utility box) forces a trade-off, prioritize a door's functional clear width (never go below roughly 74 cm for an entrance door) over the cosmetic standoff rule. [source: yt_D2Qz6s8eNoU]
- Set interior door swing direction by room type: living spaces (bedroom, living room, study, kids' room, kitchen) open inward; WCs/bathrooms/small utility rooms open outward (partly for space, and critically so an incapacitated occupant blocking the door from inside can still be reached). Quick check: a room whose light switch is inside gets an inward-opening door; a room whose switch is outside gets an outward-opening door. [source: yt_7Vd95idVXak]

### Electrical / Lighting (new topic area, added 2026-08-04)

- Set switch heights (90 cm from floor, 15 cm from door edge) and bedside/countertop outlet heights (70 cm / 110 cm) as fixed defaults across an entire apartment, not per-occupant — they're ergonomic constants (hand-drop height, furniture standards), not something that scales with who lives there. [source: yt_uLJGsTfTj3A, yt_l_rjjPlPkRo]
- Size and select circuit breakers by pairing curve type to load (curve B sockets, curve C lighting/AC) and amperage to cable gauge, not by amperage alone — an oversized breaker on undersized cable defeats the breaker's protective purpose. [source: yt_JrClDJb8WTM, yt_ciXeqvKDKSI]
- Count and buy finish-stage electrical devices room-by-room from the as-built rough-in, roughly a week before install, not from the original drawing months in advance — kitchen point counts in particular commonly shift once final cabinetry is confirmed. [source: yt_PIYzs2b4UDU]
- When vetting an electrical contractor, check for a dust-extraction vacuum on their tools and inspect drilled socket-box holes for clean, round cuts — both are checkable-by-a-non-expert signals of installation quality. [source: yt_XmeoZUiGwBM]
- Plan electrical layout against a 5-15 year family-change horizon (not just move-in day), and leave a few extra outlets wired-but-hidden behind the finished wall surface as a low-cost future-proofing move. [source: yt_k7VUlw8gHZs]

### Quality Cosmetic Renovation Technique (new topic area, added 2026-08-24 — Petrishin-Stroi Round 5, `izhaUHRKViw`)

- This company only accepts cosmetic-scope jobs on units that already had
  a genuinely good capital renovation with a modern base, explicitly
  declining Soviet-era units with old-style capital work — corroborates
  and extends this channel's own principle from `s27qG_Eg3SY` (a
  cosmetic-only renovation on a unit that never had a capital one carries
  real hidden-systems risk) with an active company policy, not just a
  caution.
- Before any cosmetic-scope demolition/sanding/filling, fully declutter
  the space, then wrap all remaining furniture and cover floors/doors in
  plastic sheeting/bubble wrap — even careful work produces microscopic
  airborne dust that a wood floor absorbs permanently into its gaps if
  left unprotected, ruining nearby furniture.
- When painting a ceiling, fully remove (or disconnect and lower, wrapped
  in cellophane) any light fixture rather than taping around it — taping
  is slow per fixture and still risks a visible unpainted halo once
  removed.
- To paint a wall without disturbing wiring, remove only the outlet/
  switch faceplate, tape over the exposed mechanism, paint, then
  reinstall the clean faceplate.
- **"Cosmetic" renovation means point-repair, not blanket recoating**:
  locate and repair specific damaged spots (chipped/scratched corners,
  dents, furniture-contact marks) via fill → sand → true the edge →
  prime → paint, room by room, spot by spot — not just rolling new paint
  over old paint.
- Even when an old baseboard, sun-faded/cracked windowsill, or worn/loose
  door could theoretically be kept in a budget-limited cosmetic job,
  replacing them is a cheap operation with a disproportionately large
  visual payoff — leaving them creates an "ugly contrast" against freshly
  redone walls that undercuts the whole job's value.
- A renovation is not complete without a professional cleaning pass
  afterward, even on the cleanest-run job — it removes microscopic
  construction dust that settles everywhere and can't be reached by a
  normal cleanup.

`single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_izhaUHRKViw_petrishin_quality_cosmetic_reno|YT_izhaUHRKViw]]]

### Secondary-Housing ("Вторичка") Renovation-Estimate Risk Checklist (new topic area, added 2026-08-24 — Petrishin-Stroi Round 5, `s27qG_Eg3SY`)

Real hidden-defect discoveries from an on-site cosmetic-renovation object
plus a general secondary-housing capital-renovation risk list from the
same source. `single-account`.

- Hidden developer protective film behind curtains, coated in
  accumulated dirt/paint, can cost real unplanned labor hours to remove
  (a real example: ~4-5 hours across 3 windows in one room) — worth
  checking behind curtains before finalizing a window-cleaning estimate.
- Removing an old, thicker baseboard can reveal a wall-floor gap the new,
  thinner baseboard profile (MDF/polyurethane) won't cover — an
  invisible sizing mismatch until the old baseboard is actually removed.
- Deep, hidden picture-hook holes (found once wall art is removed) can
  need multiple fill-dry-sand cycles depending on depth — genuinely
  time-consuming even on a small wall area, and painting can't proceed
  until every layer has fully dried and been sanded.
- **Tile-adhesion tap-test before deciding not to replace existing
  tile**: tile that looks fine visually can still be detached from its
  substrate — tap-testing reveals a hollow sound where adhesion has
  failed, versus a solid sound where it hasn't. This source's own
  practice: bring a rotary hammer/hammer drill to the estimate visit
  specifically to test-drill suspect areas, to avoid a bad surprise for
  the client later.
- **Mixed aluminum-copper wiring risk from a prior partial electrical
  renovation**: when only some rooms in a unit previously had wiring
  replaced, undiscovered aluminum-to-copper splices can exist behind
  walls elsewhere; demolishing/disturbing a supposedly untouched circuit
  risks losing power to that room mid-project. **Key principle**: a
  renovation can only genuinely be called "cosmetic only" if a full
  capital renovation (electrical, plumbing, heating) was already done at
  least once in that unit's history — a cosmetic-only renovation on a
  unit that never had one carries real hidden-systems risk.
- **Corroded mixer/faucet wall connection point ("водорозетка") failure
  mode**: an old, corroded/seized connection point can itself break when
  removing an old mixer/faucet, especially with no isolation
  valve/cover plate present — remediation can require removing tile and
  re-piping (a full resolder if the line is polypropylene) — a
  genuinely large, easy-to-underestimate cost/scope item.
- **Furniture-packing/moving deserves its own explicit smeta line item**:
  clients commonly underestimate what furniture will actually remain
  during a renovation (initially say "nothing stays," then a couch,
  wardrobe, and chairs turn out to still be present) — define and price
  a furniture-packing-and-moving line explicitly with the client and
  estimator upfront, to avoid a later conflict over unplanned labor.
- **Door-frame demolition plaster-crack risk**: removing screwed-in door
  frames often causes surrounding plaster to crack/detach from vibration
  — especially in Stalin-era and Khrushchev-era buildings — potentially
  forcing a full re-plaster the original estimate never accounted for.
  Tap-test plaster around door frames yourself before signing a smeta.

### Designer/Foreman Coordination & QC on Complex Custom Builds (new topic area, added 2026-08-24 — Petrishin-Stroi Round 5, `w6_e7nySEXI`)

- A messy/disorganized job site is a reliable red flag for a messy process elsewhere on the same job (skipped steps, technology violations) — check site cleanliness as a first-pass signal on any visit. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_w6_e7nySEXI_petrishin_designer_mistakes_delays|YT_w6_e7nySEXI]]]
- For a complex backlit two-tier drywall ceiling, confirm up front with the foreman whether corners will be milled ("фрезеровка," sharper/flatter, costlier) or classic butt-jointed-and-taped — this affects cost, timeline, and durability, and matters more than usual when a lit reveal will visually magnify any corner imperfection. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_w6_e7nySEXI_petrishin_designer_mistakes_delays|YT_w6_e7nySEXI]]]
- Any custom drywall/niche geometry (a decorative trim/baseboard piece sized to a niche, an inset shelf, etc.) needs a fully dimensioned drawing from the designer, not a verbal description — a real defect on this object (a niche gypsum trim piece that didn't fit) traced directly to skipping this. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_w6_e7nySEXI_petrishin_designer_mistakes_delays|YT_w6_e7nySEXI]]]
- Ask a designer directly how a proposed material/finish is cleaned, whether it collects dust, and how durable it is before accepting it — rank practicality first, durability second, aesthetics third; if a designer can't answer a practicality question about their own choice, treat that as a reason to find a different designer. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_w6_e7nySEXI_petrishin_designer_mistakes_delays|YT_w6_e7nySEXI]]]
- Before dependent trades proceed off an original material spec (e.g. a tile border sized to a specified glass-block thickness), re-verify the actual delivered material's dimensions against spec — a real 1cm delivery deviation (5cm specified glass block arriving at 4cm) produced a visible asymmetric podium because the tiler had already worked to the original spec. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_w6_e7nySEXI_petrishin_designer_mistakes_delays|YT_w6_e7nySEXI]]]

### First-Meeting Vetting Checklist for an Individual Tradesperson (added Round 6, `ziOsy7OGQ2Q`)

A distinct, broader behavioral checklist from this store's existing contractor-vetting content (reviews, past-project visits, contract review, electrical-specific tool checks) — applied holistically, no single item alone is disqualifying:

- **Punctuality at the first meeting** — arriving on time signals professional self-respect and respect for the client's time; lateness is a leading indicator.
- **Personal tidiness/appearance**, explicitly hedged as not 100% reliable — a tradesperson who keeps himself and the client's space clean (removes muddy boots, uses shoe covers) correlates with careful general work habits.
- **Tool inventory quantity over brand** — reject "tool-brand snobbery"; judge instead by breadth of tools brought (a professional owns and brings task-specific tools for different jobs; one tool for a multi-tool job is a red flag).
- **Tool condition as a provenance signal** — clean, well-maintained, properly-stored tools suggest genuine ownership/livelihood; tools looking freshly borrowed or scavenged are a reason to question whether the person is a real tradesperson.
- **⚠️ Smell of alcohol or visible hangover — an explicit hard pass**, not just a caution, since precision work is impossible in that state.
- **⚠️ Named long-term-alcohol-abuse indicator: visibly poor dental condition** — reasoned via poor diet, neglected dental care, and cost/priority; not a demand for cosmetic perfection, just "not badly rotted."
- **⚠️ Same-day/first-day advance-payment request is a red flag, with an explicit legitimate-exception carve-out**: requesting a trivial sum (e.g. 1,000-2,000 RUB) before any work/contract is a reliability red flag — a real emergency would realistically need "tens of thousands," not a trivial sum. **Legitimate exception**: a standard ~30% deposit requested *after* a written contract is signed is normal practice, distinct from an ad hoc pre-contract cash request.
- **⚠️ A named "disappearing act" pattern**: failing to show up, going unreachable for hours/a day, then resurfacing with an implausible excuse — linked to the alcohol-related concerns above as the likely underlying cause, and framed as highly likely to recur.

### Design Concept (new topic area, added 2026-08-15)

- **A materials/color palette can be deliberately designed for cross-scale reuse rather than bespoke-fitted per apartment** — choosing finishes/colors specifically because they harmonize with each other regardless of room proportions lets one design concept transfer to a different footprint without a full redesign. `single-account`, presented as this company's own product mechanism, but the underlying principle (palette chosen for cross-scale harmony, not just per-project fit) is a genuinely reusable design idea. - **Design custom kids'-room furniture to scale to the room's actual footprint while preserving consistent seating usability for schoolwork**, rather than a fixed-dimension piece resized proportionally — keeps the furniture functional regardless of the specific apartment's kids-room size. ### New-Build Handover / Developer Acceptance (new topic area, added 2026-08-24 — Pavel Sidorik channel trial)

- **Never sign the acceptance act before receiving keys, regardless of how the request is framed** — a developer or its reps may ask for a signature first ("get the keys after"), or soften it as a short time-boxed viewing window; a buyer is legally entitled to inspect for as long as needed and bring any specialist to the walkthrough. Region unresolved for this object (see source note's Region check) — treat as Russian/CIS-market practitioner practice, secondary reference only. [source: yt_hpU_xEXmdvE]
- **Expect and ignore fabricated technical justifications offered under time pressure during a handover walkthrough** (e.g. a tilted windowsill claimed as "intentional for drainage," hollow plaster claimed as "intentional for soundproofing") — record every genuine defect in the acceptance act regardless of pushback. [source: yt_hpU_xEXmdvE]
- **Watch for a "agree on paper, stall in practice" developer tactic** — agreeing to every listed defect but never acting on it, running out the clock until the buyer signs anyway just to be able to start renovation. Described as the most common of three observed developer pressure tactics. [source: yt_hpU_xEXmdvE]
- **Inspect windows, radiators, and windowsills specifically under raking/side light, not flat daylight** — chips and fine scratches are frequently invisible under direct/flat lighting and only show up at a raking angle; developers are also observed masking such marks with film, stickers, or filler before a walkthrough. [source: yt_hpU_xEXmdvE]
- **Treat a pre-whitened (skim-coated) raw-concrete apartment as a net negative, not a bonus** — a developer's own cosmetic skim-coating done purely to look more finished/appealing to a buyer is typically the cheapest material and lowest-quality workmanship, meaning it has to be stripped and redone rather than saving any real work. [source: yt_hpU_xEXmdvE]
- **Ventilation quick-test**: open a window, then hold an A4 sheet against the kitchen/bathroom vent grille — if it's sucked against the grille, the vent draws air correctly (the test only works with a window open). [source: yt_hpU_xEXmdvE]
- **Electrical quick-test at handover**: with typically only the distribution panel and one outlet live, test that outlet directly; for a fully-wired panel building, walk the apartment with a simple bulb-in-socket test lamp and confirm live power at every individual outlet. [source: yt_hpU_xEXmdvE]
- **In winter, use a thermal-imaging camera during acceptance** to find window-seal gaps (a cold streak indicates poor closure) and to trace actual underfloor/wall heating-pipe routing by chalk-marking the floor — the developer's own as-built schematic is not always accurate, and this matters before any later penetration work. [source: yt_hpU_xEXmdvE]
- **Cross-checking the project/estimate documentation ("проектно-сметная документация") against actual as-built work is a real, financially material check, not a formality** — a real anecdote (Brest, level-1-located but an unrelated earlier apartment, undated): one buyer's own documentation audit recovered several thousand USD per apartment for an entire building (and buildings handed over before it) once overbilled/unperformed work was documented. [source: yt_hpU_xEXmdvE]
- **A paid-inspection-refunded-into-contract model is a workable acceptance option**: pay a specialist (or the crew who will do the renovation) for a stand-alone acceptance inspection, with the fee refunded against the renovation contract if that same specialist/crew is then hired for the actual work. [source: yt_hpU_xEXmdvE]
- **Developer-supplied entry doors and most sanitary fixtures are not worth inspecting closely** — near-universally the cheapest available and replaced during renovation regardless (water meters are the one sanitary item worth keeping); only confirm the entry door still opens/closes for the interim period, and check for a hidden pre-existing failure (e.g. a cracked sewer stack concealed under tape) rather than finish quality. [source: yt_hpU_xEXmdvE]

### Construction-Phase Prep & Logistics (new topic area, added 2026-08-24 — Pavel Sidorik Round 2, New Building A-to-Z ep.2)

- **Insure the apartment and neighbor-liability coverage *before* starting renovation, not after** — renovation work is a real, common cause of flooding in both directions (a neighbor damaging your already-finished ceiling/skim coat, or your own plumbing accident damaging the unit below); the liability-to-neighbors half of the policy is the more important of the two. Region: Belarus level 1 (Minsk named directly elsewhere in this same source — see source note). [source: yt__TGU8C0u010]
- **Obtain the building's utility-routing schematic (heating-pipe routing under the screed) from the HOA/condo association, plus keys to the electrical and heating-network distribution panels, before starting demolition or rough-in** — avoids working blind around embedded services. [source: yt__TGU8C0u010]
- **A self-built heavy-duty hand cart is a real, cheap alternative to a store-bought one**: 15×15mm square steel tube frame, 4× 125mm rubber-tired swivel casters (protects stairwell/lobby tile), reused-particleboard deck; sized to fit a standard elevator while carrying 3 bags of dry mix side by side. Practical one-person load ceiling ≈150-200kg regardless of the cart's structural capacity — sizing larger sacrifices maneuverability for no real benefit. [source: yt__TGU8C0u010]
- **Build a temporary on-site handwash station (sink + faucet + simple wood-batten frame) connected to the apartment's capped water/drain stub-outs** rather than washing hands/tools from a hose in a toilet bowl — a cheap setup with a real stated productivity/hygiene payoff for the crew. [source: yt__TGU8C0u010]
- **A single durable PVC protective tape (cited brand: "Blue Dolphin," red) can mask windows/doors/radiators once for an entire renovation** instead of the more common practice of re-taping several times as ordinary tape degrades — speaker's own real-world test left it on a sun-facing window over 10 months with zero adhesive residue on removal. Sun-facing windows additionally get a foil layer over the masking film, to block heat gain and prevent sun glare from washing out a laser level's beam. [source: yt__TGU8C0u010]
- **A glazed loggia functions as an effectively airtight, dust-free storage/staging room during demolition** — move tools and belongings there beforehand to avoid having to wipe dust off everything afterward; cited as a specific advantage of new-build apartments (which reliably have PVC-glazed loggias) over older housing stock. [source: yt__TGU8C0u010]
- **Buy secondhand polypropylene bags (ex-packaging for grain/sugar/bulk material) for demolition debris instead of new construction-store garbage bags** — see Numeric Data for the real per-bag and per-project cost comparison. [source: yt__TGU8C0u010]

### Demolition (new topic area, added 2026-08-24 — Pavel Sidorik Round 2, New Building A-to-Z ep.3)

- **Two-person masonry-partition demolition technique**: one person on a jackhammer (medium size, easier to hold up against a vertical wall), the other on a sledgehammer (called equally essential, not just backup); rotary hammer as an auxiliary tool, angle grinder for embedded rebar. Full PPE required: dust mask, safety glasses, hearing protection. [source: yt_H6atjh_g1jQ]
- **Repurpose an airless paint sprayer to mist down dust during demolition** — a cheap, effective improvised dust-suppression technique reusable across future demolition jobsites whenever the sprayer isn't needed for its normal purpose elsewhere. [source: yt_H6atjh_g1jQ]
- **Remove the toilet bowl before demolishing surrounding bathroom walls**, to avoid it being broken by falling debris. [source: yt_H6atjh_g1jQ]
- **Shut off the heating riser before breaking up a screed** — limits an accidental pipe strike's water release to just what's in that apartment's own pipes, not the full building system. **Never strike blindly once a pipe is located under a screed** — break the screed on both sides of the pipe and clear debris near it by hand instead. [source: yt_H6atjh_g1jQ]
- **Use a heavier rented jackhammer for full screed removal, resting its weight on the screed itself, distinct from the lighter hand-held-preferred hammer used for vertical wall demolition.** [source: yt_H6atjh_g1jQ]
- **Grind monolithic concrete surfaces (walls, ceilings, ventilation ducts) to remove cement laitance and open the surface pores before plastering or applying a concrete-contact primer** — a concrete-contact primer commonly fails to bond specifically at the laitance layer, so removing it first avoids a later adhesion failure; applies to ceilings too, done in alternating sections by a two-person crew to manage overhead-work fatigue. [source: yt_H6atjh_g1jQ]
- **Clean and flatten the exposed floor slab after screed removal before installing a soundproofing membrane** — leftover construction-phase mortar/plaster splatter (~5mm) needs grinding + vacuuming first, since the membrane needs a clean, relatively smooth substrate. [source: yt_H6atjh_g1jQ]
- **A real accidental structural finding**: a tipped-over, spilled toilet tank during demolition caused no damage to the unit below, illustrating that a monolithic floor slab itself blocks water transfer between floors independent of any applied waterproofing membrane. `single-account`, incidental rather than a design guarantee. [source: yt_H6atjh_g1jQ]
- **A worked 5-reason framework for deciding to demolish bathroom/WC partition walls even when structurally sound**: room-footprint resizing, reclaiming ~4cm/side by rebuilding an oversized wall thinner, removing crooked/non-90°-corner plaster that must come out anyway, right-sizing an oversized developer false wall (fphalshstena) down to just the pipe diameters it needs to conceal (and omitting it where a furniture back-panel can do the same job), and confirming the outer wet-zone boundary isn't changing even as the internal split is. [source: yt_H6atjh_g1jQ]
- **A worked 5(+1)-reason framework for deciding to demolish an already-relatively-flat developer screed**: soundproofing underlayment needs the bare substrate, an oversized screed can be thinned to recover ceiling height, floor-routed electrical needs the space underneath when the ceiling isn't being dropped, pouring a new screed level from the start avoids needing a later self-leveling compound, and chases for new partition walls are easier to cut into an exposed slab than through an existing screed (plus: full heating-system inspection/easier radiator replacement without a screed layer in the way). [source: yt_H6atjh_g1jQ]
- **Informal student labor is a real, usable option for bulk debris hauling** — the most physically repetitive, lowest-skill part of demolition — though not fully reliable (a no-show requires the practitioner to cover the gap personally). [source: yt_H6atjh_g1jQ]

- **Temporary lighting as a QC requirement**: insufficient light during demolition can cause incomplete removal of old plaster/oil-paint layers, besides being a basic safety issue. [source: yt_zxTbtAbuXFs]
- **Temporary construction sink (water + drain), with a named flooding risk it prevents**: crews repeatedly forgetting to close a tap on an un-drained temporary water point has flooded the object and the downstairs neighbor — a proper drain connection avoids this. [source: yt_zxTbtAbuXFs]
- **Neighbor-relations practice, three tactics**: notify neighbors on both sides/above/below before demolition; ask households with small children about nap/sleep schedules; give the building concierge a small gift (≈200-300 RUB/person candy) — stated 18-year experience that this reliably de-escalates future noise/access complaints. Daily hallway cleanup (damp rag at the door, clean the landing after every debris trip) and a door-posted apology note with a contact number round out the same neighbor-relations practice. [source: yt_zxTbtAbuXFs]
- **Debris-disposal cost lifehack**: negotiate debris removal/disposal directly with the building's own janitorial staff — can be meaningfully cheaper than a standard dumpster container if terms/timeline are agreed explicitly upfront. [source: yt_zxTbtAbuXFs]
- **Pre-priming dedusting requirement, adhesion mechanism**: fully dedust walls/ceilings with a brush before priming — priming over a dusty wall prevents the new plaster from bonding to the brick/concrete underneath. [source: yt_zxTbtAbuXFs]
- **Flooring-removal-to-bare-substrate rule, adhesive-residue failure mode**: remove all laminate/plywood/baseboard remnants and leftover linoleum adhesive residue down to bare substrate — unremoved adhesive residue can crack later once a self-leveling floor is poured over it. Old bitumen in older apartments can stay only if well-adhered, and then needs ≥4-5cm of screed over it; remove it entirely if dried out/poorly adhered. [source: yt_zxTbtAbuXFs]

[source: yt_zxTbtAbuXFs]

### Small-Apartment Planning & Code Compliance (new topic area, added 2026-08-24 — Pavel Sidorik channel trial)

- **Don't chase a full bathroom-enlarging replanning on a very small apartment just because a bathroom feels cramped** — weigh the disproportionate cost/bureaucracy (hidden-works acts, official replanning approval, waterproofing sign-off) against the small fraction of total area a bathroom expansion would actually reclaim on a genuinely tiny unit; sometimes the right call is to fit the required fixtures compactly into the existing footprint instead. [source: yt_0sJPlpi8I2U]
- **Clad a wall-hung-toilet installation frame in removable material (e.g. laminated chipboard) rather than a permanent solid box** when a building-wide capital repair (капремонт) with riser replacement is expected in the foreseeable future — lets the frame be disassembled and reconnected to a new riser without demolishing a permanent enclosure. [source: yt_0sJPlpi8I2U]
- **Defer a fix that would require its own separate design/approval process if the building's already-scheduled capital repair (капремонт) will address it anyway** — avoids duplicate paperwork/cost for the same eventual outcome (real example: replacing an old heating-system-connected towel rail). [source: yt_0sJPlpi8I2U]
- **A glass-and-metal loft-style partition between an open kitchen and living space must still satisfy the gas-appliance-open-space code** — a full floor-to-ceiling glass panel (not a partial screen) can satisfy the code while preserving an open, visually connected loft aesthetic. Independently corroborates the same gas-code partition constraint already in this store from a different channel (Category 5, `yt_2E7YVK6PhIM`). [source: yt_0sJPlpi8I2U]
- **A sloped/pitched top-floor ceiling can be treated as a design feature worth preserving rather than a defect to level** — deliberately not leveling a sloped ceiling avoids losing height gain and can give a converted-attic character to the room. [source: yt_0sJPlpi8I2U]
- **A single water-tolerant flooring material (e.g. quartz-vinyl/quartz-laminate) can be run as one continuous surface across both wet and dry zones** (kitchen, hallway, living space) instead of zoning different flooring materials by room — simpler installation (same click-lock method as ordinary laminate) with higher wear resistance than standard laminate. [source: yt_0sJPlpi8I2U]
- **A wet-zone resize stays outside formal replanning-approval territory as long as it stays within the room's pre-existing outer footprint** (only moving the internal split between adjoining wet-zone rooms, e.g. toilet vs. bath) — only expanding the wet zone's outer boundary into an adjacent *living* room triggers the formal replanning project + hidden-works-act paperwork. A concrete decision lever for weighing a bathroom-enlarging remodel, corroborating this store's existing small-bathroom-replanning caution from a different Sidorik source above. [source: yt_ZW-dNzbCREI]
- **Buy dense-block wall material (e.g. silicate partition block) by the full pallet directly from the manufacturer's own website, not retail** — a real ≈2× retail markup was found this way; factory sale is pallet-multiples only and needs a crane/manipulator delivery truck. Hire experienced movers to carry heavy pallletized material up into the unit rather than doing it personally, and split delivered stock across multiple locations in the apartment rather than one concentrated pile. [source: yt_ZW-dNzbCREI]
- **A practitioner's own on-screen cost estimate ("смета") may not include the value of their own labor time on a self-executed project** — flagged explicitly by this speaker in response to viewer disagreement over whether his figures read as too cheap or too expensive; worth checking before treating any of this channel's self-managed totals (including the khrushchevka finale case study) as a genuinely all-in figure. [source: yt_ZW-dNzbCREI]

### AAC/Foam-Concrete Partition Mistakes (new topic area, added 2026-08-24 — Pavel Sidorik Round 2, New Building A-to-Z ep.5; ⚠️ real topical overlap with ep.4's silicate-block partition build — see that source note's Overlap section, not double-counted)

- **Chase (cut back) the screed before building a non-load-bearing partition on top of it, in a free-plan apartment**: the block must sit on the structural base slab, not the screed — an intact screed sits on a soft underlayment that can compress ~0.5cm under a partition's weight (cracking), and a floating-screed acoustic design requires zero rigid screed-to-wall contact (sound transmission). [source: yt_HqmQaZ1y1UM]
- **A softer/lower-density block (aerated/foam concrete) needs a soft, flexible wall-tie (мягкий подвес) to the load-bearing wall, not a rigid metal angle bracket** — a rigid tie is a real cause of cracking in this material specifically; tie every third course, joint filled with foam. **Material-specific distinction from this store's existing silicate-block partition entry** (denser silicate block can take a rigid metal-angle tie). [source: yt_HqmQaZ1y1UM]
- **Correct a proud/uneven block edge by sanding/planing it down, never by adding a thicker glue layer** — glue isn't a gap-filler; coarse (grit 30) sandpaper on a wood block is the cheapest, simplest fix (≈$1 for 0.5m, real price stated directly) — cheaper and more effective than a dedicated block plane, which struggles on higher-density block. [source: yt_HqmQaZ1y1UM]
- **Check block level/plumb from only one consistent side while laying, never both simultaneously** — blocks vary in flatness by 1-3mm (up to 5mm) even at "first-grade"; push all irregularity to one reference side, since the opposite face gets straightened out by the later plaster coat anyway. [source: yt_HqmQaZ1y1UM]
- **A cheap DIY door-lintel alternative to a factory block-lintel unit**: glue 2-2.5 standard blocks together with foam adhesive, built flat on the partition's own already-level top surface (not the uneven floor), cured ~10 minutes before lifting into place — roughly 7-8× cheaper than a factory lintel unit. See Numeric Data for the real price comparison. [source: yt_HqmQaZ1y1UM]
- **Don't fill an oversized top-course gap (e.g. ~5cm near the ceiling) with foam alone instead of cutting a properly-sized block piece** — the resulting foam-filled void has nothing solid to screw into later, causing problems mounting drywall-profile track or anything else attached during finish work. [source: yt_HqmQaZ1y1UM]
- **Use removable colored tape (not pencil/marker) to lay out future partition/furniture footprints on the floor in a free-plan apartment** — previews room/opening placement at real scale and repositions with no residue, unlike marker lines which accumulate into visual clutter after corrections. Same "Blue Dolphin" PVC tape as this channel's episode-2 window-masking technique. [source: yt_HqmQaZ1y1UM]
- **A working, multi-material block adhesive**: "Holcim Expert" glue, usable across foam concrete, aerated concrete, and silicate block alike. [source: yt_HqmQaZ1y1UM]
- **An alternative, more-precise base-leveling method than a mortar first course**: cut 6mm×15cm plaster "beacons," bed them on cement plaster along the laser-leveled line, then pour a thin sand-cement screed over the beacon strip as a precisely level partition "foundation" — slower but more accurate than eyeballing a mortar bed, and doesn't require mortar-laying experience. [source: yt_HqmQaZ1y1UM]

### Hot-Glue-Gun Rough-Stage Techniques (added 2026-08-24 — Pavel Sidorik Round 4, YouTube zaW8XagX72U)

- **Dislodged plaster/screed beacon-lump repair**: if a beacon ("маяк") is accidentally knocked loose from its mortar/plaster support lump mid-job (common — a beacon's set to millimeter precision, so pressing it back onto fresh plaster doesn't restore that precision), clean plaster residue off both the beacon's back face and the lump, reseat it in its exact original position, then bond with a hot-melt glue gun — sets in ~3 minutes and reportedly holds more securely than the original plaster mounting.
- **Beacon-splicing for a room longer than a single beacon**: join two beacons end-to-end with a short filler/splice segment glued on top of both ends with a hot-melt glue gun — fast, cheap, and immediately workable once set, no cure-time wait.
- **Drywall-profile positioning-drift fix**: a hammer-drill bit can wander sideways on hitting aggregate in the substrate, nudging a metal track profile off its marked reference line before the fastener is driven. Fix: after positioning the profile exactly on the line (shims if needed), tack it with a dab of hot-melt glue through the fastener hole (or against the profile's back face) before drilling — holds it perfectly still while the actual fastener holes are drilled.
- **Wall-hung-fixture installation-frame nut position-memory trick**: after leveling an installation frame's height-adjustment nut, fix its position with a dab of hot-melt glue — if the frame is removed and reinstalled later (e.g. during other trades' work), the nut stays at its already-correct setting instead of needing to be releveled from scratch; peel the glue off to intentionally readjust.

### Entrance / Hallway general layout & storage (added 2026-08-25, Kruglov/Ontario) — no dedicated wiki page yet, 2nd source toward the 3+ threshold (see Pending Wiki-Page Decisions)

- For entryway flooring, use moisture-resistant quartz-vinyl or porcelain
  stoneware only, not laminate/parquet/engineered board — an entryway
  seasonally tracks in snow/de-icing chemical residue, dust, or mud that
  wood-based flooring can't tolerate; not all quartz-vinyl products are
  the moisture-resistant type. Electric heated floor is recommended here
  specifically to actively dry residual moisture rather than let it sit.
  Use a rigid (not soft/flexible) baseboard so debris/slush can't migrate
  under a gap. `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]
- Deliberately over-light an entryway rather than under-light it — never
  rely on a single fixture here. `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]
- Include a full-height (full-body) mirror in the entryway — flexible on
  placement (wall-mounted, recessed, on a wardrobe door leaf, or on the
  entrance door's own interior face), but the full-body view is the
  stated requirement. `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]
- Provide a seat (pouf, or a built-in seated spot inside a wardrobe) for
  putting on/removing shoes while tired or unwell. `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]
- Prefer closed storage over open shelving/hooks for entryway shoe/coat
  storage, despite the extra "open door" step — open shelves/hooks
  inevitably accumulate small clutter (keys, mail, fobs) regardless of
  intent, while closed storage preserves a sense of order and reduces
  cleaning friction. `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]
- Design entryway storage cabinetry to either seal fully to the floor (no
  gap) or sit raised clear of it (legs, or wall-mounted/floating) so a
  robot vacuum can clean underneath — a mid-height obstruction blocks the
  robot and forces manual shoe removal to clean. `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]
- Add an electrical outlet near in-closet shoe storage for a boot/shoe
  dryer — near-zero cost, avoids drying wet footwear on a balcony or in a
  living space. `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]
- Entrance-door reveals/jambs are the single most vulnerable wall surface
  in an entryway (repeated bag/parcel scuffing) — protect them with
  decorative plaster (the most durable non-tile finish) or a protective
  wood panel/trim; decorative plaster is also recommended for the
  entryway's other walls generally, since the total area involved (~5-6
  m²) makes the added cost negligible against a full renovation budget.
  `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]
- Entryway security/smart-home options worth considering: a video door
  intercom or peephole camera; a door-open sensor integrated with a
  smart-home hub (no extra wiring, ~10 minute install) for a use case like
  notifying a parent when a child leaves for or returns from school; a
  smart lock (e.g. multiple unlock methods including temporary codes for
  a cleaner, open/close logging, redundant low-battery warnings) as a
  reusable selection-criteria list independent of brand. `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]
- A "master light switch" (one button kills all lights in the apartment)
  only suits a household with synchronized schedules (single occupant, or
  a couple who leave/return together) — it works one-directionally from
  the entry point, so it's a poor fit for a larger family with staggered
  schedules; if installed anyway (e.g. only for a vacation-departure
  routine), mount it out of normal reach (inside a closet) to prevent
  accidental triggering. `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_cM0AndkKdVk_kruglov_top_entryway_solutions|YT_cM0AndkKdVk]]]

  mounted/flush fixtures lower it (stronger); pendant/hanging fixtures
  lower it furthest and put the upper wall/ceiling into shadow, maximally
  hiding real ceiling height. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT__6E5wt-PfqQ_anuta_vlady_narrow_hallway_design|YT__6E5wt-PfqQ]]]
- To visually shorten an over-long corridor, color-block it into distinct
  zones rather than one uniform run: paint just the end wall dark; extend
  that color onto an adjoining surface (e.g. ceiling) to visually "cut
  off" a segment; or align a color division with a genuine architectural
  feature (e.g. a locally lower-ceiling section gets its own color) rather
  than an arbitrary point. Real applied case: an unremovable structural
  beam became the natural boundary for a color transition from entryway
  to kitchen accent color in a friend's in-progress budget renovation
  (region unresolved, no cost data — project incomplete at filming).
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT__6E5wt-PfqQ_anuta_vlady_narrow_hallway_design|YT__6E5wt-PfqQ]]]
- Create a focal point directly in a corridor's forward sightline (accent
  wall/artwork/object group straight ahead) rather than leaving a "boring
  dead-end wall" — reasoning: people pay far less visual attention to
  side walls while moving through a pass-through corridor than to
  whatever's directly ahead, so the far wall's treatment matters more than
  the side walls'. Avoid large-format artwork in a narrow corridor (no
  room to stand back and view it) — prefer small-format pieces.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT__6E5wt-PfqQ_anuta_vlady_narrow_hallway_design|YT__6E5wt-PfqQ]]]
- When a corridor segment sits directly opposite an always-open (doorless)
  living-room entrance, treat it as effectively part of that room's own
  interior — share its decorative-lighting switch rather than adding a
  separate one. **General rule**: don't give a pass-through corridor's
  accent/picture lighting its own switch by default — combine with the
  main ceiling-light switch. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT__6E5wt-PfqQ_anuta_vlady_narrow_hallway_design|YT__6E5wt-PfqQ]]]
- Choose mirror orientation deliberately relative to the direction you
  actually want to expand — a full-length mirror on a corridor's long
  wall expands its *length* (an already-long corridor reading as even
  longer), which may not be wanted; orient/place a mirror to expand width
  instead when length is already the problem, not the asset.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT__6E5wt-PfqQ_anuta_vlady_narrow_hallway_design|YT__6E5wt-PfqQ]]]
- Furniture (console, chest of drawers) sitting below the height of a
  person's extended-arm fingertips reads as leaving the space "free" even
  at the same footprint as a taller piece — people perceive personal
  space at roughly arm's-length height; furniture below that line doesn't
  register as encroaching on it. A wall-mounted/hung or tall-legged shoe
  cabinet reads as more spacious than a floor-standing one of the same
  capacity, per the general principle "the more visible floor, the more
  spacious the room feels." [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT__6E5wt-PfqQ_anuta_vlady_narrow_hallway_design|YT__6E5wt-PfqQ]]]
- Shallow-depth furniture (bookshelves especially) suits a corridor's
  limited depth; mixing floor-standing sections near the entry with
  wall-hung sections further down the same run keeps progressively more
  floor visible. A wall-hung (not floor-standing) cabinet is specifically
  practical for a corridor's "wet zone" (wet outdoor gear/umbrellas) since
  it keeps the floor dry/cleanable underneath — pairing with heated floor
  speeds drying further, corroborating this batch's heated-floor-for-
  drying theme with a hung-storage-plus-heated-floor combination.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT__6E5wt-PfqQ_anuta_vlady_narrow_hallway_design|YT__6E5wt-PfqQ]]]

### Kitchen-Living Combination Zoning (now 3+ sources, added 2026-08-25 — crosses the 3+-source page-creation threshold; see the Living & Dining Guide page created this session)

- Combined kitchen-living-room zoning is gated by total room area, with
  concrete thresholds: under ~20m² is too small for three distinct zones
  (kitchen, dining, living/TV); ~10m² supports kitchen+dining but not a
  separate living zone; ~50m² gives real room to spread all three out;
  **~35m² is cited as workable for a family of four (two children)** —
  kitchen+dining on the side nearer the entry, living/TV zone on the
  other. `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_ncOI16-meB4_omikor_kitchen_living_rules|YT_ncOI16-meB4]]]
- Dining-table clearance rule: leave a minimum of 1.1m clear on both
  sides of any dining table for chair pull-out/seating, 1.2m preferred
  for genuine comfort — a fixed rule regardless of table size. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_ncOI16-meB4_omikor_kitchen_living_rules|YT_ncOI16-meB4]]]
- Size the kitchen-zone footprint against actual household cooking
  intensity, not a fixed default: an active cook needing a food
  processor/juicer/full oven/large fridge justifies a larger (possibly
  L-shaped/corner) kitchen footprint at dining's expense; a light/
  occasional cook can work with a minimal footprint (small sink, ~60cm
  worktop, 2-burner cooktop, fridge), freeing floor area for dining
  instead. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_ncOI16-meB4_omikor_kitchen_living_rules|YT_ncOI16-meB4]]]
- Tall column-style cabinets are the most space-efficient storage option
  in a combined kitchen-living zone, holding more volume than an
  equivalent footprint of standard base + wall cabinets. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_ncOI16-meB4_omikor_kitchen_living_rules|YT_ncOI16-meB4]]]
- When a kitchen and living room form one continuous open space, unify
  the design language across both — kitchen cabinet facades should
  coordinate with living-room furniture facades (matching, or different
  materials that complement each other); don't treat the kitchen as a
  stylistically separate zone once the space is physically open. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_ncOI16-meB4_omikor_kitchen_living_rules|YT_ncOI16-meB4]]]
- Plan distinct lighting scenarios per zone within the same open space:
  brighter task lighting plus general ceiling light over the kitchen
  work zone; a pendant/chandelier specifically over the dining table;
  dimmer, softer ambient lighting in the living zone. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_ncOI16-meB4_omikor_kitchen_living_rules|YT_ncOI16-meB4]]]
- Flooring-material zoning is area-dependent: a large combined space can
  use different flooring per zone (e.g. tile in kitchen, parquet in
  living); a small space should use one flooring material throughout for
  visual continuity, generally favoring a wear-resistant option (ceramic/
  tile) as the safer unifying default. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_ncOI16-meB4_omikor_kitchen_living_rules|YT_ncOI16-meB4]]]

**2nd source, added 2026-08-25 — Diskill/Nadia Karmin (individual designer, 15+ years), the densest source in this batch:**

- Design storage/scope to the household's actual priorities, not a
  generic default (families with kids need more concealed/child-safe
  storage; avid cooks need large built-ins + prep space; big-group
  hosts need a large table/sofa/media zone) — if a kitchen-living space
  is really about entertaining rather than serious cooking, the real
  functional kitchen can live in an entirely separate room instead.
  Real client-pattern caution: many clients request a large
  sliding/extendable table for hypothetical big gatherings but in
  practice host only 1-2 guests/year — weigh actual entertaining
  frequency against a table sized for a rare best case. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_TnYExlfWw9c_diskill_kitchen_living_10_rules|YT_TnYExlfWw9c]]]
- Outlet-count planning by zone: kitchen ~8-10 (fridge, hood, dishwasher,
  microwave, stove, kettle, small appliances, plus a sink-disposal
  outlet); living zone ~5-6 (TV, consoles, decorative lighting). Plan
  more outlets upfront than seems necessary to avoid extension cords.
  Major appliances (fridge, dishwasher, oven) should each get a
  dedicated circuit for safety and to avoid voltage drops — label every
  breaker correctly since these outlets sit in the cabinet plinth.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_TnYExlfWw9c_diskill_kitchen_living_10_rules|YT_TnYExlfWw9c]]]
- Hood shouldn't connect to a shared building ventilation stack in a
  new-build (odors otherwise spread between units) — plan a
  carbon-filter recirculating hood plus a standard vent with a
  backdraft/check valve to block a neighbor's odors coming back. Even
  with a strong hood, cooking smells will still spread through a combined
  kitchen-living space to some degree. **Hood airflow-capacity sizing
  formula**: kitchen floor area (m²) × ceiling height (m) × 12 =
  required capacity (m³/h). Hood mounting height: 60-75cm above a gas
  cooktop, 50-60cm above electric. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_TnYExlfWw9c_diskill_kitchen_living_10_rules|YT_TnYExlfWw9c]]]
- Multi-layer lighting, three explicit layers: primary/general (bright
  enough for real cleaning), accent/task (under-cabinet or shelf
  lighting for a specific surface), and decorative (sconces/floor lamps,
  soft warm light, doubling as a legitimate main-light stand-in) — more
  decorative sources in a kitchen-living zone is generally better for
  atmosphere. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_TnYExlfWw9c_diskill_kitchen_living_10_rules|YT_TnYExlfWw9c]]]
- Flooring zoning with a structural reason, not just aesthetics: put
  hard flooring (tile) under kitchen cabinetry specifically because
  heavy cabinetry can dent/compress a softer material (quartz-vinyl or
  laminate) over time — a dent in one spot forces the material to bulge
  elsewhere. If a shared dining table spans the kitchen/living flooring
  transition, position chairs off the material seam. Each zone can have
  its own ceiling treatment and light — but give each zone its own
  switch. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_TnYExlfWw9c_diskill_kitchen_living_10_rules|YT_TnYExlfWw9c]]]
- Storage planning: size concealed storage to actual usage (not
  ceiling-height cabinets you can't fill — wasted volume becomes a junk
  dump); vary opening mechanisms by position (lift-up upper fronts,
  pull-out lower drawers) and never economize on mechanism quality; use
  internal dividers/organizers; keep concealed kitchen storage well-lit
  and ventilated. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_TnYExlfWw9c_diskill_kitchen_living_10_rules|YT_TnYExlfWw9c]]]
- **Corroborates** the sofa-bed aesthetic/practicality trade-off already
  recorded from `YT_qHuFie5U65g` (Fedotova): many clients request a
  pull-out sofa bed for occasional guests, but most pull-out sofas look
  unattractive extended — independently confirmed by a second
  practitioner. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_TnYExlfWw9c_diskill_kitchen_living_10_rules|YT_TnYExlfWw9c]]]

### Living Room + Bedroom Combination Zoning (new topic area, added 2026-08-25 — Iolanta Fedotova, 1st source, no dedicated page yet)

> [!NOTE]
> Directly relevant to this project's own Phase 2 household plan (a wall-
> bed/Murphy-bed transformer, see `04_Living_and_Dining_Room/Living_and_
> Dining_Index.md`) — recorded here as independent general reference
> material corroborating that category of solution, not as an edit to the
> household's own already-decided plan notes.

- Two fundamental approaches to combining a living room and bedroom in
  one room: **sofa-only** (fold-out sleeper, no separate bed) vs. **bed +
  separate sofa** (two distinct zones). Sofa-only gives more open floor
  space and room for extra furniture, but requires daily folding/unfolding
  and forces a 3-way trade-off between real orthopedic quality, attractive
  folded-profile design, and affordability (realistically 2 of 3). Bed+sofa
  gives a permanently-made real orthopedic mattress with no daily chore,
  but **a full double bed plus adequate access needs a minimum of ~9m²
  dedicated to the sleep zone alone** — in a typical 16-22m² combined
  room this leaves only ~9-12m² for the living/sofa zone. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_qHuFie5U65g_fedotova_living_bedroom_combo_rules|YT_qHuFie5U65g]]]
- Decision heuristic: choose based on actual guest frequency/size and
  personal tolerance for daily folding, not a default preference; if
  storage need dominates, consider dropping a dedicated bed zone entirely
  in favor of large wardrobes/a walk-in closet instead. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_qHuFie5U65g_fedotova_living_bedroom_combo_rules|YT_qHuFie5U65g]]]
- Bed-vs-sofa placement relative to the entry is geometry-conditioned, not
  fixed: if the room has a recessed niche near the entry, put the bed
  there and the living/sofa zone nearer the window; if the room is a
  straight walk-through with no recess, put the living/sofa zone in the
  entry/walkway path and the bed further in near the window, away from
  entry traffic. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_qHuFie5U65g_fedotova_living_bedroom_combo_rules|YT_qHuFie5U65g]]]
- Zone-divider options ranked with trade-offs: heavy curtains matching
  the window treatment (cheapest, openable at will); a glass partition
  (keeps daylight reaching the far zone, some sound-dampening); glass +
  curtains combined (more complete — sound-blocking plus visual privacy);
  a raised platform/podium (mentioned, not elaborated); furniture used as
  a divider (this designer's own least-favored option — blocks too much
  light to the far zone). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_qHuFie5U65g_fedotova_living_bedroom_combo_rules|YT_qHuFie5U65g]]]
- **This designer's own top recommendation for this room type**: a
  wall-mounted transformer/foldaway furniture system (a sofa attached to
  a wall-mounted mechanism) — full daytime living-room floor space, folds
  down at night to a complete bed with its own mattress, no daily
  sheet-stripping. Cites multiple Russian-market manufacturers producing
  this as of filming (2022), and suggests a custom furniture maker if no
  ready-made unit is available locally. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_qHuFie5U65g_fedotova_living_bedroom_combo_rules|YT_qHuFie5U65g]]]

**3rd source, added 2026-08-25 — Ekaterina Popova (individual designer), a worked 3x5m real-dimension example with three furnished variants:**

- Three sleep-solution options with a different trade-off framing than
  `YT_qHuFie5U65g`: folding sofa (reads most like a living room, but a
  genuinely comfortable sofa-bed isn't cheap and needs daily assembly);
  regular bed + good mattress (most budget-friendly best sleep quality,
  no daily effort, but pushes the room's identity toward "bedroom"); a
  sofa-bed transformer (same daily effort as a sofa, plus a style
  constraint - looks "alien" in a classical interior, suits modern rooms).
  Decision heuristic: guests/living-room-look priority leads to sofa
  (test in-person first); best-sleep/minimal-effort priority leads to
  bed; unwilling to give up either leads to transformer, accepting the
  daily effort. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_SG0nbNRdl4c_popova_two_rooms_in_one_bedroom_living|YT_SG0nbNRdl4c]]]

  it matters if the unfolded/disassembled bed would be visible from the
  corridor through an accidentally-open door. Furniture minimalism for a
  small dual-purpose room: only truly necessary pieces, avoid full
  packing. Wardrobe placement/camouflage: place on the wall farthest
  from the window, camouflage its facade as wall paneling. Accent chair
  should not match the sofa's own collection (avoids a "hotel room"
  read). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_SG0nbNRdl4c_popova_two_rooms_in_one_bedroom_living|YT_SG0nbNRdl4c]]]
- Shared cross-zone table-lamp placement (between the sleep and living
  zones, lighting both at once); deliberate open-floor reserve left
  uncommitted for future furniture rearrangement without a full redesign.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_SG0nbNRdl4c_popova_two_rooms_in_one_bedroom_living|YT_SG0nbNRdl4c]]]
- Transformer-specific construction/styling cautions, extending
  `YT_qHuFie5U65g`'s transformer recommendation: avoid ordering a
  transformer bundled with extra built-in cabinets (looks overloaded -
  keep to just bed+sofa, small side shelves only); order the transformer
  and its sofa/chair in a matching light color to look visually lighter
  (an explicit practicality-vs-looks compromise); **avoid a low-hanging
  pendant near a transformer's bed zone - the bed mechanism can strike it
  when opening; use track lighting instead.** Visual-lightness styling:
  small neutral headboard + tall/exposed legs on a bed, matched by an
  armless sofa on tall legs; match leg color to unify the two, or use a
  bold accent leg color to draw the eye away from the bed; switch
  material for a supporting piece (e.g. metal nightstand) when the room
  already has several wood-toned surfaces. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_SG0nbNRdl4c_popova_two_rooms_in_one_bedroom_living|YT_SG0nbNRdl4c]]]

### General Living-Room Layout & Furniture Arrangement (new topic area, added 2026-08-25 — 500LUX/Marina Zvereva, 1st source)

- Define the living room's purpose (rest, entertainment, work,
  socializing) before placing furniture — the intended use should drive
  selection/placement, not the reverse.
- Establish one central focal point per room (fireplace, large TV, dining
  table, or even a window view) and arrange furniture so it visually
  draws attention there without anything blocking it.
- Organize the room around the soft-seating zone (sofa) for comfortable
  conversation and TV viewing; a larger living room can be divided into
  sub-zones, but walkways/circulation paths must never be blocked by that
  zoning.
- Match furniture scale to the room's actual size — don't overload a
  smaller living room with oversized furniture.
- **Lighting minimum-3-layer rule**: general (even ambient illumination),
  decorative (table/floor lamps for specific tasks like reading), and
  accent (directional fixtures/art-lighting) — a third independent
  specialist source (after `YT_TnYExlfWw9c` and `YT_hW1hlDoqDR0`)
  converging on the same 3-layer minimum, again under its own layer
  names. **Rearrangement-planning caution**: if furniture is likely to be
  periodically rearranged, plan only the basic general-lighting layer at
  construction time (fixed wiring/outlets don't move with furniture) and
  add accent/decorative layers later with flexible fixtures (lamps, not
  hardwired spots) instead of over-committing to fixed accent positions.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_gEykawbVyYQ_500lux_zvereva_top10_living_room_layout|YT_gEykawbVyYQ]]]
- Furniture must be functional: side tables next to seating for
  drinks/books, adequate storage to keep the room tidy.
- **Rugs are a genuine zoning tool, not just decoration** — a rug
  delineates a zone on its own; furniture should ideally not sit fully on
  the rug (leave a clearance gap). A real applied example: two separate
  rugs independently zone a dining area and a living/sofa area within one
  combined room, with no physical partition at all.
- Maintain balance between paired elements (sofa+art, sofa+chair,
  sofa+table) and keep color palette/furniture style harmonious; avoid
  overloading a room with too much furniture regardless of room size.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_gEykawbVyYQ_500lux_zvereva_top10_living_room_layout|YT_gEykawbVyYQ]]]

### Room-Zoning Partition Methods (new topic area, added 2026-08-25 — 13DS/Olesya, 1st source, no dedicated page yet — cross-cutting, applies beyond Living/Dining)

- Wood slat/batten partitions let light through but give no full acoustic/
  visual privacy; need a ceiling mounting point ("закладная"), especially
  under a stretch ceiling. Cheap dimensional lumber (e.g. hardware-store
  battens) is dimensionally unstable and will likely warp — fine for pure
  wall decor, but a real structural partition should use custom-ordered
  MDF slats (pricier, pre-finished, far more reliable). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8oZexrWcMUY_13ds_10_partition_zoning_ideas|YT_8oZexrWcMUY]]]
- Curtain zoning is the cheapest/fastest option (blackout fabric for full
  light-blocking, fully flexible open/close); multiple curtains sharing a
  wall with window curtains can look like "a pile of fabric" — pair with
  a Roman blind on the window to lighten the visual load. Curtains are
  dust magnets requiring periodic removal/washing, not just wiping. A
  plain curtain track looks unfinished — conceal it in a ceiling niche
  (drop the ceiling on both sides, or build two drywall stub walls at an
  existing cornice's height) so the curtain glides through the middle.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8oZexrWcMUY_13ds_10_partition_zoning_ideas|YT_8oZexrWcMUY]]]
- Shelving-unit dividers need real household discipline to avoid becoming
  "a vertical junk pile" — mix open and closed cabinet sections instead of
  fully open shelving to reduce visible clutter while still letting light
  through. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8oZexrWcMUY_13ds_10_partition_zoning_ideas|YT_8oZexrWcMUY]]]
- **Sliding glass/plastic partition mounting caution, generalizable to any
  wardrobe-style sliding door system**: mounting on both a top AND bottom
  track (like a standard closet door) is not recommended — the bottom
  track creates a trip-hazard threshold and visually cheapens the
  interior; prefer top-track-only mounting, but confirm the ceiling
  backing/blocking requirement with the manufacturer before the ceiling
  is closed up, since a top-track-only mechanism is heavier and needs a
  genuinely solid structural mounting point. Real project price point
  (this company's own project, region/date unconfirmed, ~2020): a glass
  sliding partition separating bedroom from kitchen-living cost ~100,000
  RUB; adding stub walls on both sides of a glass partition reduces the
  glass width needed and lowers cost. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8oZexrWcMUY_13ds_10_partition_zoning_ideas|YT_8oZexrWcMUY]]]
- A wardrobe/cabinet used as a room divider adds closed storage but
  exposes an unattractive rear panel — mitigate by combining it with
  wood slats or strung rope/cord behind it; build a divider-wardrobe to
  full ceiling height (not partial) since it's freestanding — a low unit
  looks incomplete, usually requiring a custom order. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8oZexrWcMUY_13ds_10_partition_zoning_ideas|YT_8oZexrWcMUY]]]
- **Two-sided mirrored static partition**: a static partition covering
  ~2/3 of a room's width (leaving a walk-through gap) with a mirror on
  *both* faces — one enlarges the kitchen/utility-side zone visually, the
  other the room-side zone, compounding to make the whole apartment read
  as roughly double its size. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_DO67YHV5uAU_gusev_two_rooms_from_one_partitions|YT_DO67YHV5uAU]]]
- **Arched double swing-leaf partition with curtain layering**: fixed
  side sections plus a center pair of hinged leaves opening like a formal
  double-door; adding a curtain rod above the opening lets the opening
  be curtained off for full closure/light-blocking on top of the leaves.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_DO67YHV5uAU_gusev_two_rooms_from_one_partitions|YT_DO67YHV5uAU]]]
- **"Book"/rotary-fold partition door**: instead of a single swinging
  leaf that needs a large floor-space radius to open (blocked by nearby
  furniture), a book-fold door folds in half into the wall opening,
  needing far less swing clearance; can use up to 3 hinged leaves per
  side (small accordion) for a wider opening while still folding flat —
  a small-apartment space-saving alternative to a standard hinged
  partition door. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_DO67YHV5uAU_gusev_two_rooms_from_one_partitions|YT_DO67YHV5uAU]]]
- **Windowless habitable-room registration caution (code-adjacent, region
  unstated)**: a room with no natural light source generally cannot be
  officially registered as a "жилая комната" (habitable/living room) per
  applicable building norms — relevant when partitioning off a windowless
  carve-out as a bedroom: an *opaque* partition on that carve-out's only
  borrowed-light path would create exactly this problem, while a glass/
  transparent partition avoids it by letting light through. Region
  unstated at level 1 (not routed to `16_Legal_and_Regulations/`).
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_mrnTQL0eyBc_studio57_glass_partitions_for_against|YT_mrnTQL0eyBc]]]
- **Partition material-selection heuristics (generic content-aggregator
  source, thin but non-duplicative)**: glass/glass-block partitions suit
  small or poorly-lit rooms specifically because they don't block light
  transmission and don't visually overload the space (complements, does
  not contradict, the glass-partition entries above); textile/curtain
  partitions are the easiest option to maintain (simple to install,
  change, and clean); a metal partition adds real individuality but needs
  a deliberate matching design style, not a neutral default the way
  glass/drywall are. `low-value source — most of its content duplicated
  existing, more detailed coverage above and was not re-recorded`.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_Mf2A7cbTq3s_dela_domashnie_partition_types_materials|YT_Mf2A7cbTq3s]]]

## Planning Rules — Four Renovation-Organization Models Compared (Vladimir Amelchenko / ДЕЛАТЬ НЕ ПЕРЕДЕЛАТЬ, added 2026-08-28, Round 5)

A structured comparison this store didn't yet have in this shape — not a repeat of existing design-process-workflow content, which covers process *within* a chosen model rather than comparing the models. Medium-high promotional ratio (concludes by recommending the speaker's own company's own business model — flagged as tier-steering; the individual pros/cons below are recorded as real, checkable mechanisms independent of that conclusion).

- **Model 1, no design project at all — 8 named concrete disadvantages**: constant unanswerable field questions (outlet/switch counts, plumbing fixture choices, ceiling/wall/floor material) blocking work start; client forced to personally develop the layout solution; verbal-only agreements with no documentation create unresolvable he-said/she-said disputes (worked example: an agreed 300mm outlet height installed at 350mm); constant later revisions become more expensive after finishes are in (worked example: a forgotten hood outlet or TV-internet point discovered late, forcing a long cable run or fresh wall-chasing, plus real risk the crew refuses rework); client must personally source all materials; a delivery delay can make the crew demand down-time payment or simply leave for another job, with real risk of never returning; post-completion furniture/cabinetry conflicts discovered only once installers arrive (blocked outlets, doors that can't open, because layout wasn't coordinated against furniture footprint in advance); and an unclear final cost that can run **1.5-2x** the originally agreed figure, with real risk of an unfinished renovation if no budget reserve existed. One named advantage: saves the design-project fee.
- **Model 2, client has a design project with no author supervision — 3 advantages / 2 disadvantages**: advantages are a documented expected result (eliminates the disputed-agreement pattern from Model 1), a computable cost basis (materials/labor scope known in advance), and fewer disruptive field questions. Disadvantages: the designer has no financial incentive to stay engaged post-handover without a separately purchased supervision package, and the project itself is an added cost.
- **Model 3, design project plus paid author supervision ("авторский надзор") — 1 advantage / 2 disadvantages**: the designer stays actively engaged through construction, taking most procurement/layout decisions off the client. Disadvantages: supervision is a real added cost (typically a fixed monthly fee on top of the design fee), and **a designer-crew coordination gap is a real named risk when the two haven't worked together before** — independent parties can fail to find common technical ground, generating conflicts the client has to personally mediate.
- **Model 4, design and construction from the same company (the speaker's own stated preferred model) — 4 named advantages, tier-steering, mechanism recorded not the endorsement**: designer and crew already work together routinely with pre-resolved technical solutions; **the design is actively checked against the client's real budget during design itself**, catching an unrealistic/overscoped proposal before construction (real cited counter-example: a client's externally-produced, already-paid-for design specified a built-in track-lighting ceiling system that turned out to be unaffordable once priced, and was simply dropped — the design fee paid for that element was wasted, illustrating the disconnect this integrated model claims to prevent); the client is substantially removed from day-to-day procurement/technical decisions; and the client retains standing access to the designer throughout the project.

[source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_FA7paWxy_JE_sbk_why_design_project|YT_FA7paWxy_JE]]]
