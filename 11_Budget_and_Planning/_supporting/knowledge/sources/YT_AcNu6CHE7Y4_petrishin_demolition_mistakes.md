## Mistakes / Warnings — Four Named Demolition Mistakes, in Severity Order

1. **⚠️ Dust protection**: fully wrap doors/windows not being replaced in
   protective film with taped seams — otherwise expect scratches, chips,
   and dirt with "100% probability," and dust clogging window/door
   hardware mechanisms to the point of being nearly impossible to clean
   out later.
2. **⚠️ Waste-volume miscalculation**: actual demolition debris volume is
   almost always far greater than a client imagines — one of the most
   common mistakes across all renovation clients. Construction waste
   cannot legally go into an ordinary yard dumpster (risk of a fine,
   real neighbor pushback) — a specialized waste container must be
   ordered separately (cited cost **≈15,000-20,000 RUB** per container,
   `ASR-uncertain` — the spoken figure is garbled in the transcript as
   "15-20 сся руб," most plausibly "15-20 тыс. руб"/thousand rubles),
   plus separate paid labor to load it. **Named dishonest-contractor
   practice**: an incompetent/dishonest crew deliberately underquotes
   total demolition cost specifically so as not to scare off a
   potential client — the client then discovers and pays a large
   additional sum later just to haul the debris away.
3. **⚠️ Hidden-utility damage**: old plaster can conceal a heating riser
   or a hot/cold water pipe; a hammer-drill/jackhammer strike can easily
   rupture one, flooding the unit and the neighbors below, pausing the
   renovation, and requiring payment to the affected neighbors.
   Mitigation stated: confirm the crew checks walls for hidden pipes/
   wiring before starting demolition.
4. **⚠️ Named as the single scariest of the four**: a damaged water-fed
   heated towel rail ("полотенцесушитель") — common, old, and fragile in
   secondary-market ("вторичка") apartments. **Explicit incidence
   estimate: in 3-4 out of 10 cases**, a careless/irresponsible crew
   damages one and causes a flood, then disappears rather than taking
   responsibility.
5. **⚠️ Named as the fourth listed mistake, load-bearing-wall
   demolition, framed as the most dangerous overall**: a wall that
   superficially resembles a non-structural partition can actually be
   carrying ceiling/slab load; removing it can crack or deform the
   ceiling. **Regulatory mechanism, level-1 region evidence**: any
   layout change must be approved via Мосжилинспекция (Moscow Housing
   Inspection) — an unapproved change makes later reselling the
   apartment meaningfully harder. Before demolition: check the BTI floor
   plan and consult a Мосжилинспекция specialist.
6. **Load-bearing-wall nuance, distinct from the blanket "never touch
   it" framing above**: an opening can sometimes be legally cut into a
   load-bearing wall (a real, regulated partial weakening of the
   structure), and in some circumstances — e.g. a wall that is less
   structurally critical, such as on a building's top floor — even a
   door opening, widening, or full removal can be permitted, but only
   following load calculations reviewed against the building's original
   structural documentation (architect/engineer of record) via
   Мосжилинспекция. Never assess this by eye; it depends on a documented
   structural review, not visual judgment.
