## Frame Construction (Economy vs. Premium)

- **Economy frame material**: particleboard (ДСП), low density —
  confined to economy tier for that reason. **Marketing trick named
  directly**: a sofa built ~99% from ДСП with a single plywood
  reinforcement strip can be labeled "ДСП + plywood frame" — technically
  true, but implies far more plywood content/reinforcement than exists.
- **Premium frame material**: thick, high-quality plywood (or solid
  timber beam); premium sofas may use solid dry hardwood (birch, oak,
  beech) for the frame.
- **Plywood thickness by tier**: economy-tier plywood (where used at
  all) is thin, **6-10mm**; premium-tier plywood is **15-18mm**, thick
  and strong.
- **Structural reinforcement ribs**: absent entirely in economy tier
  (cost-cutting); present in premium tier, reinforcing the frame.
- **Armrests/backrests**: hollow or cardboard-filled in economy tier;
  reinforced wood-framed in premium tier.
- **Joinery/fasteners**: economy tier uses self-tapping screws, threaded
  studs, corner brackets, and staples; premium tier uses tenon/mortise
  joinery ("шип-паз" technique), furniture bolts, and glue.
- **Resulting lifespan gap**: economy frame lasts roughly **3-5 years**;
  premium frame lasts **10-20 years** with no structural degradation.

## Transformation Mechanism (Sofa Bed) — a Deliberate Comfort Tradeoff

- **⚠️ Core recommendation, stated directly**: if the sofa is primarily
  for everyday sitting/lounging (not a spare bed), **buying a sofa with
  no fold-out mechanism at all is better** — the mechanism itself is
  described as an inherent design compromise that constrains cushion
  fill choice and construction, forcing manufacturers to sacrifice
  everyday comfort for the folding function. Explicit analogy: like a
  combined washer-dryer unit, which trades away pure-washing
  performance for the added drying capability.
- **Cultural counterpoint, named directly as the reason most Russian
  buyers still choose a fold-out anyway**: hosting guests overnight is a
  common expectation in this market; the source's own alternative
  suggestion is to buy a separate cheap folding cot or air mattress kept
  for actual guest visits (which the source notes happen far less often
  — a handful of times a year — than the sofa's own daily use), rather
  than compromise the household's primary daily-use seating.
- **A first-hand comparison anecdote**: the presenter reports sitting on
  a premium mechanism-free sofa and calling the comfort difference
  versus a folding sofa "colossal" — recommending visiting a premium
  showroom just to experience the difference before deciding whether
  it's worth paying for.
- **Mechanism type taxonomy by tier**: economy — книжка ("book"),
  клик-кляк (click-clack), еврокнижка (euro-book), cheap "dolphin"
  (дельфин) mechanisms. Premium — седафлор/"седаф" (sedaf-type),
  "lamp LED"-branded mechanisms (as named by the source), high-end
  dolphin variants, and accordion mechanisms.
- **Mechanism material/manufacturing gap**: economy mechanisms use thin
  metal, mediocre metal finishing, and weak welds — **play/looseness can
  appear after just 10-30 fold/unfold cycles**. Premium mechanisms use
  thick-wall metal, precision welding, and powder-coat finishing —
  **no looseness even after 10-20 years**.
- **Sleeping-surface flatness/comfort**: economy mechanisms typically
  leave step-offs, gaps, or ribs that press into the sleeper's side;
  premium mechanisms give a genuinely flat surface, sometimes with an
  added 12-18cm mattress layer and spring or latex support blocks.
- **Fold effort**: economy mechanisms are stiff/hard to operate and fail
  quickly; premium mechanisms fold/unfold easily, one-handed, and stay
  reliable long-term.

## Seat and Backrest Fill

- **Foam density by tier**: economy — low-density polyurethane foam,
  **18-28 kg/m³**, often a single monolithic block; premium — high-
  density polyurethane foam, **35-45 kg/m³**, sometimes HR foam and
  multi-layer "sandwich" constructions.
- **Sag/compression-resistance gap**: economy cushions begin visibly
  sagging/compressing within **~6 months** of use; premium cushions
  resist this over many years.
- **⚠️ A specific consumer trap named directly**: an economy and a
  premium sofa can feel genuinely identical the moment you sit down in
  the showroom — the real difference (compression resistance over time)
  only shows up after roughly 6 months of home use, by which point the
  purchase decision is long made.
- **Anatomical/lumbar support**: absent in economy tier (resulting in
  either a uniformly hard or uniformly sagging feel); present in premium
  tier, built from materials that may include latex, "холлофайбер"
  (hollow-fiber) batting, "трутто," coconut coir, felt, or pocket-spring
  ("pocket-spring") units.
- **Cushion covers**: economy — thin fabric, non-removable covers;
  premium — high-density fabric, typically removable and washable.

## Upholstery Fabric Selection Criteria — Price ≠ Durability

- **⚠️ Core caution, stated directly and worth flagging distinctly from
  the frame/mechanism/foam sections above**: unlike frame quality (where
  more expensive reliably means more durable), **fabric price does not
  reliably predict fabric durability or fitness for purpose** — a cheap
  economy/comfort-tier fabric can be genuinely durable, while an
  expensive imported (the source's example: Italian) fabric can pill,
  fade within 6 months, and have a low abrasion rating despite its
  price. **Select fabric by its stated technical properties, not by
  price tier.**
- **Martindale abrasion index**: the key durability metric — number of
  rub cycles before visible wear-through. **15,000-25,000 cycles is a
  low/short-lived rating; 80,000-100,000+ cycles is a genuinely
  long-lived rating.**
- **⚠️ Pilling resistance is an independent property from the Martindale
  rating — not correlated**: a fabric can have a very high Martindale
  rating (e.g. 80,000 cycles) and still pill quickly; these are
  explicitly two separate properties, not a contradiction when both
  occur together.
- **Colorfastness/UV-fade resistance**: varies significantly by fabric —
  some fade quickly in direct sun, others tolerate near-outdoor UV
  exposure with no visible fading. Relevant specifically for a sofa near
  a loggia or panoramic window; largely irrelevant for one in a room
  with minimal direct sun exposure.
- **Optional fabric treatments**: Teflon-type stain-repellent coatings,
  "aquaclean"-type treatments, antibacterial treatments, and pet-claw/
  stain-resistant treatments are all available add-ons beyond plain
  fabric.

## Design Customization and Warranty

- **Customization gap**: economy tier is template/catalog-only (fixed
  frame + mechanism + a limited fabric color range); premium tier
  allows full custom specification of frame, fabric, mechanism, and
  cushion fill — some manufacturers will produce a bespoke series to a
  client's specification.
- **Warranty and assembly-QC gap**: economy tier — typically 1-year
  warranty, higher defect/complaint rate; premium tier — 3-10 year
  warranty, top-tier assembly quality.
- **Three named buyer psychology profiles** (not a technical claim, the
  source's own framing): (1) budget-minimizer — "good enough, in
  budget, if it lasts 3 years that's fine"; (2) premium buyer — wants
  the sofa as a genuine interior centerpiece matching the whole design
  concept, values durability/individuality/comfort highly; (3) the
  "mixed compromise" segment (stated as ~50% of the market) — picks and
  chooses tradeoffs across the same criteria (e.g. a strong frame paired
  with cheaper fabric, or vice versa).
