## Regulations / Permits / Approvals — Durable Facts & Rules (Russian consumer-protection process, level 1 confirmed location: this dispute is Moscow-region-based)

- **Two distinct legal-expertise categories determine what can be claimed**, per a guest lawyer's explanation: **товароведческая экспертиза** (product/goods expertise) applies to a free-standing furniture purchase; **строительно-техническая экспертиза** (construction-technical expertise) applies when the furniture is built-in and its installation/removal affects the apartment's own interior finishing. The distinction matters because the construction-technical category additionally allows claiming **restoration costs** for finish damage caused by removing defective built-in work — a purely product-expertise claim would not cover that. `confirmed`, stated by a named legal professional in the video, a genuinely reusable process fact for anyone in a similar dispute.
- **Before commissioning any formal expert inspection, the other party (contractor) must be formally notified of the inspection's date/time** (the source cites using a registered telegram) so they have a documented opportunity to attend — skipping this step lets the contractor later contest the expert report's validity in court, which can add months to the case. `confirmed`, a concrete procedural safeguard.
- **Recommended process sequence**: engage a lawyer first (to review the contract type — contract-for-work vs. sale-of-goods — since Russian consumer-protection law treats these differently) → determine whether interior finishing was affected (built-in vs. free-standing) → formulate specific, documentable questions for the formal expert examination → notify the other party → conduct the examination → calculate the claim. `confirmed`, stated as the lawyer's own standard sequence.
- **Claim components illustrated in this specific case** (Moscow region, ~2020, RUB): original deposit refund; restoration-cost claim for finish damage from removing the defective built-in wardrobe; cost of the expert examination itself; moral-damages/legal-fee compensation. If unresolved via a pre-court settlement demand, additional claims become available: a **consumer penalty of 50% of the combined goods/damages/moral-harm total** (per Russian consumer-protection law, `unverified` against the actual statute text in this note, but stated as a specific legal mechanism, not an opinion), plus a **contractual penalty (неустойка) of 3% of the contract value per day** of non-payment, which the law caps at a stated maximum. `confirmed` as spoken; the specific percentages should be verified against the actual law text before being relied on for a real dispute, since this is a single secondhand account of the statute, not a citation of the law itself.
