## Furniture / Built-ins — Durable Facts & Rules

- **A single-sided walk-in closet (гардеробная) wastes space relative to two back-to-back wardrobes (шкаф) sharing a thin dividing wall**, because a walk-in's aisle is dead space a plain wardrobe doesn't need — accessing hung clothes from directly in front (wardrobe) uses the same footprint more efficiently than walking past a one-sided storage run (walk-in). `confirmed`, core rule of this source.
- **Worked example**: original client brief wanted a small single-sided walk-in (entered from the living room) adjacent to the bedroom, which would have forced the bed and the bedroom door opening to each shift ~700mm to accommodate the aisle. Instead, the practitioner installed **two separate wardrobes sharing one ~100mm-thick dividing wall** — one for outerwear near the entry zone, one for shirts/blouses/dresses inside the bedroom — losing only the 100mm wall thickness total while gaining two full-depth storage runs and **improved soundproofing** between the entry zone and the bedroom (a wardrobe run acts as an acoustic buffer). `confirmed`, real project, client-confirmed positive outcome on camera.
- **Rule of thumb given**: before defaulting to a walk-in closet, check whether two adjoining rooms could each get their own wardrobe against a shared wall instead — especially valuable when the two rooms need acoustic separation anyway (e.g. entry corridor vs. bedroom). `confirmed` as the practitioner's standing recommendation; explicitly softened to "minimize walk-ins, especially single-sided ones," not an absolute ban.
