## Doors/Trim (Windows) — Standard Window Measurement

- **All windows visible simultaneously in one sightline should have sills at the same height from the floor.**
- **Tool: a laser level on an elevation tripod** (one with a fine-adjustment crank/rod), not a standard tripod (whose only height control is leg length, impractically tedious for precise matching).
- **Zero-reference-point method for multiple windows**: find the single highest point across all windowsill areas via laser survey; set the "zero mark" **15mm below** that highest point (the minimum foam-joint thickness under the sill/base profile needed to avoid the joint freezing — can be 3, 4, 5cm+ elsewhere if the sill is uneven, but never less than 15mm anywhere). Physically mark this zero point at multiple locations around every opening (including hard-to-reach spots) so installers share an unambiguous reference.
- **Measurement-from-zero method**: measure from the marked zero point up to the opening's top, then subtract the standard top-joint allowance (30mm) to get the frame's actual order height — avoids the installer separately subtracting the bottom-joint thickness later.
- **Sketch-first rule**: before measuring, sketch the future window unit on paper, including mullion/pillar (impost) positions.
- **Worked example (height)**: opening height 1600mm − 30mm top joint = **1570mm order height**.
- **Width-measurement rule**: measure width at three points (bottom, middle, top); take the minimum reading; subtract 60mm total (30mm joint allowance per side).
- **Worked example (width)**: 2000mm minimum measured − 30mm − 30mm = **1940mm order width**.
- **Scope caveat, explicitly stated**: this simplified method applies specifically to replacing an existing PVC window with a new PVC window (measuring off the old frame's footprint) — replacing a wooden window with PVC needs a materially different, more complex measurement approach (a separate video referenced, not covered here).
- **Always independently re-verify every sash/section's real measurement rather than trusting the previous installer's numbers** — specifically to catch a facade-code violation: enlarging a window beyond its original opening on a building with a regulated facade can force removing and redoing the new windows at cost. Always measure to match the existing frame position; never intentionally enlarge one window relative to its neighbors.

## Doors/Trim (Windows) — Balcony-Block Measurement

- **Reference "zero" offset is taken from the project documentation's stated future-finished-floor level** (worked example: 110mm above rough floor). Method: set the laser level's horizontal plane at 1m + that offset above rough floor (example: 1.11m) as a convenient working mark, mark it clearly and distinctly on the wall (elongated so it's not confused with any other mark), then measure both up and down from it, subtracting joint allowances as before.
- **Worked example**: rough floor to future-screed zero = 110mm; +1000mm convenience offset = working mark; from the mark, measure up and subtract 30mm (top joint) for frame top; measure down and subtract 15mm (bottom joint) — note the frame sits directly 15mm above the future screed with **no base/support profile** in this configuration, so that 15mm gap is covered later by the finished flooring.
- **Critical width-measurement rule for a balcony block with an integrated door**: there are always exactly **two** widths measured — total opening width and door width — **never** a separately-measured "window width." Window width is always the total minus the door width, calculated, never independently tape-measured. Measuring it independently is described as a guaranteed source of error.
- **Worked example**: total opening width 1600mm − 30 − 30mm = 1540mm; door opening width 800mm − 30 − 30mm = 740mm; the window portion's width is the derived remainder.
- **Critical height rule for a second/adjacent window measured against a first**: never independently tape-measure a second window's height — match it exactly to the first window's already-established height, because even a 1cm difference between two visually adjacent windows' upper reveal lines becomes glaringly obvious, especially under a later-installed stretch ceiling (leaving only a thin visible strip above). Cross-check with a tape measure only as a safety verification; if that check implies a foam joint under 30mm, adjust by using the first window's dimension as the fixed reference and changing the second window's joint allowance instead — never both windows' heights independently.
- **Loggia/balcony-block glazing color must match the building facade exactly** — requires manufacturer color-swatch fans (separate video referenced, not covered here).

## Doors/Trim (Windows) — Loggia/Balcony Glazing Measurement (most complex case)

- **The single most catastrophic, effectively unfixable mistake**: forgetting the loggia floor will be raised (screed buildup, e.g. 11cm in this example, needed to properly insulate the loggia). If glazing height is based on the OLD (pre-raise) parapet, the safety parapet ends up too low once the floor is raised. **Minimum safe parapet height from the future finished floor: 1100mm (1.10m)** — if raw glazing doesn't provide this, an insert/filler panel section must be added.
- **Measure glazing per mullion/section individually** — more measurement steps produce a more accurate overall result.
- **Tape-measure handling technique/safety**: never let the tape's free end flop loosely while measuring — always extend it, brace it against something stable, then read the value. Described as a professional-vs-amateur tell; "fire any measurer who does it the sloppy way."
- **Loggia glazing height, worked example**: measure raw height, subtract 30mm top joint and 15mm bottom joint.
- **Critical exterior-insulation-layer measurement trap**: if a loggia still has exterior insulation (batting/wool) installed at measurement time and the plan is to remove it (correct practice once the glazing itself is properly insulated — loose exterior batting becomes redundant once glazing + parapet/ceiling/floor insulation are all in place), the measurer must account for its thickness and subtract it from the glazing-width measurement, or the finished glazing will sit proud of the facade line once the insulation is removed. **Explicit scam/incompetence flag**: a measurer claiming "we'll account for the insulation and run the glass all the way to the wall, no filler strip needed" is wrong — removing insulation doesn't move the true facade plane, so oversized glass will visibly overhang past the real facade edge. A proper filler/spacer strip ("добор") is legitimate and necessary here, not something to avoid. Method to check true thickness: poke through with a long screwdriver (widening the hole if needed) until reaching the load-bearing wall, measure that depth, and size filler strips to it.
- **Critical positioning trap for an angled/corner loggia glazing run — the "worst" measurement mistake**: pulling the frame line inward to center it within the future insulated parapet buildup, which measurably shrinks the loggia's usable interior volume once occupied (a second, less severe mistake is making one leg too long so it protrudes past the parapet — self-correcting/immediately visible). **Correct approach: keep the glazing frame at the OLD/original parapet's outer line regardless of planned future insulation thickness** — let any insulation buildup simply create a wider finished windowsill afterward, rather than sacrificing interior floor space by recessing the frame inward.
- **A stretch ceiling atop a loggia glazing run needs a ~100mm-deep filler/spacer strip built in at the top**, giving the stretch-ceiling track (baguette) something to anchor into.
- **Bottom-of-glazing filler-strip sizing must account for both the future screed thickness AND the standard 30mm bottom foam-joint allowance together**, so the finished frame bottom aligns correctly with the future finished floor. **Common defect if under-sized**: the frame sits too low relative to the finished floor, so flooring crowds right up to (or under) the glass instead of meeting a proper frame reveal — looks bad from every angle, and critically, makes it impossible to later access/replace a broken glazing unit's retaining bead (штапик) if it's buried under the flooring. **Rule: the frame and its beads must always remain fully visible/accessible** — anything else is handled via filler strips, never by burying the frame itself.

## Planning Rules / Ergonomics

- **Proactively offer a lowered door-handle position for shorter household members** rather than installing at a "one size fits all" standard height and later blaming the client for needing a step-stool — record client height/preference at measurement time, framed as a client-facing standard, not just a technical option.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Loggia Safety Glazing

- **Once a removable/temporary parapet guard rail is taken down (commonly done well before final glazing installation), the lower glazing panels below the original parapet line must be either tempered or laminated/"triplex" safety glass — no other type is acceptable.** **Explicit preference: laminated/triplex over tempered** — tempered glass shatters into small pieces that won't cut you but still leaves an opening someone could fall through; laminated/triplex glass holds together when broken (like a car windshield), physically preventing a fall-through even after breakage — worth the extra cost specifically for a lower/safety-critical pane.
