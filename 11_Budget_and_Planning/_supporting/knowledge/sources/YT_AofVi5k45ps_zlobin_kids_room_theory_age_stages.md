## Rules / Heuristics — Preschool (3-6): Parent-Adjacency Structure (New)

**⚠️ A concrete room-adjacency layout, new to this store**: the ideal
structure for a preschooler's room is **direct adjacency to the parents'
own bedroom via a shared "wet zone"** — i.e. parents' bedroom → their own
bathroom → the kids' room, arranged so a parent reaches the child's room
by passing through (or alongside) the bathroom rather than via a general
hallway. Stated benefit: this is genuinely convenient for bathing/
caregiving tasks that span both rooms. A secondary option (when this
adjacency isn't achievable) is a dedicated connecting door directly
between the parents' room and the kids' room, bypassing the general
corridor. House-specific framing (assumes adjoining rooms/floor
adjacency), but the underlying "shortest real path for
caregiving-adjacent tasks" reasoning generalizes.

**Preschooler bed placement**: near the window, but shielded from
drafts. **South-facing room orientation is described as mandatory** for
a preschooler's room (also flagged as good, though not mandatory, for a
master bedroom). **Minimize sharp furniture corners** at this age
specifically — a genuinely active/newly-mobile child ("actively mastering
walking, i.e. running") is more likely to collide with furniture edges.
**A large central open play zone** is prioritized over furniture volume
at this age; small toy-scale furniture (little tables, toy shelving) is
sufficient. **Bright colors** recommended for room design at this age.

## Rules / Heuristics — Junior School (6-12): Furniture Needs and Layout Principle

- **New furniture requirement at this age**: a **work desk** becomes
  necessary (homework, and hobby activities involving writing/cutting/
  painting/modeling-clay tools), plus a **shelving unit positioned next
  to the desk** for books and toys.
- **Wardrobe sizing, kid-scaled**: since children own materially less
  clothing than adults, a compact wardrobe suffices — **1.2-1.5m width
  typical, 1.8m as the practical maximum** — explicitly smaller than an
  adult wardrobe by design, not a compromise.
- **Play zone shrinks but stays open-plan** at this age, and — new
  detail — **a Swedish wall/gymnastics ladder should ideally be sited
  near the play zone specifically** as a physical-development element
  (corroborates and extends this store's existing RemPlanner reference to
  a wall-mounted gym ladder at the 6-12 age band, adding the specific
  play-zone-adjacency placement detail RemPlanner's own entry didn't
  include).
- **⚠️ Core layout principle for this age band, new and genuinely
  reusable**: kids' rooms at this age should be laid out using
  **perimeter/built-in ("встраиваемая"/corpus) furniture hugging the
  walls**, reasoning stated directly: a child this age doesn't yet need
  large-volume furniture pieces (no need for big wardrobes/case pieces),
  so their whole furniture set fits comfortably along the walls, leaving
  the room's center open. Concretely: desk positioned at the window (for
  natural light), wardrobe and shelving along other walls, bed occupying
  whatever wall segment remains.
- **⚠️ A bed-placement conflict rule, new to this store**: the bed should
  be placed **neither near the entry door** (to avoid waking the child
  from hallway noise/light) **nor directly at the window** (since the
  window position is already claimed by the desk, per the natural-light
  rule) — these two furniture pieces shouldn't compete for the same wall
  segment.
- **Handedness-mirrored desk placement, corroborating this store's
  existing hand-shadow rule**: desk position/orientation should mirror
  depending on whether the child is left- or right-handed — stated
  directly as a factor to check per child, independently corroborating
  (from a third source/channel) this vault's existing
  hand-shadow-avoidance desk rule already on `Kids_Room_Guide.md`.
- **Compact-room worked example (9-10m²)**: even a small 9-10m² kids'
  room can fit four functional zones along its perimeter — a **bedside
  zone** (space to sit/stand/make the bed), a **study zone** (at the
  window), a **storage/shelving zone**, and a **wardrobe zone** (near the
  entry) — a concrete minimum-viable zoning template for a small kids'
  room, directly relevant to an apartment-scale room.
- **⚠️ A curtains-vs-blinds caution, new to this store, reasoning not
  stated explicitly**: for this age category, the architect recommends
  **blinds ("жалюзи") over traditional fabric curtains** — states the
  reasoning is "obvious" without elaborating (plausibly a
  safety/practicality concern — e.g. fabric-cord strangulation risk or
  durability against a child pulling on curtains — but this is inference,
  not stated). Recorded as `single-account`, reasoning `inferred`, not
  confirmed.
- **Built-in-furniture worked example**: a partition-created niche can
  host a sliding wardrobe, with open shelving mounted directly above the
  desk — walls used actively for storage capacity, described as solving
  "a lot of problems" this way, freeing floor-level space for the child's
  own activity.

## Rules / Heuristics — Two-Child Shared Room Layout Framework (Richest New Content)

**⚠️ A dedicated section on shared same-age-sibling rooms, not present in
this store's existing Kids Room content** — the architect frames this as
its own genuinely distinct design problem, driven by **family-dynamics
reasoning**: parents of same/similar-age children need to give "equal"
provision to avoid sibling equity conflicts ("who has what"), and any
architect/designer planning this room type must account for that
explicitly. **Core resulting rule: provide two identical beds and two
identical desks**, not asymmetric furniture, specifically to avoid this
friction.

- **Layout 1 — elongated room**: two desks placed with a **walkway of
  roughly 80-90cm to 1m between them** (not the tight clearance an office
  desk pairing would use — "it's not an office," access matters). A
  single **large shared wardrobe** (bigger than a single-child wardrobe,
  since it serves two people). Sleeping zone: two beds, either sharing
  one central nightstand or each with its own separate nightstand. A
  small shared play zone still fits.
- **Layout 2 — wide/near-square room**: beds arranged in a **Г-shape
  (L-shape)**, meeting at a shared corner nightstand; wardrobe placed
  elsewhere in the room. For two desks in a genuinely tight room, a
  **fallback (explicitly framed as a compromise, "not from a good life"
  but common in practice)**: **one long shared desk with two chairs**,
  rather than two separate desk units, when the room doesn't have space
  for both independently.
- **⚠️ Future-resale-value strategy, house-specific but instructive
  reasoning**: for two same-age children, deliberately build a **larger**
  combined kids' room (12-15m² range) rather than a minimal one — the
  stated reason is that once the children grow and eventually move out/
  separate, a room this size can convert into "a bonus full bedroom,"
  directly adding resale value to the house under this architect's
  broader future-resale design philosophy (see video 3's three design
  principles). A concrete example of "plan for the room's future
  identity, not just its current occupants," generalized from the
  guest-room framework in video 3 to the kids'-room context.
