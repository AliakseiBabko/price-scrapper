## Rules / Heuristics — The Repair Technique Itself (genuinely new)

1. **Drill-and-inspect diagnostic technique for an already-plastered
   wall**: drill a small hole through the plaster at a crack to confirm
   the underlying cause before committing to a repair method — in this
   case revealing masonry with insufficient adhesive at the vertical
   block joints underneath intact-looking plaster.
2. **⚠️ Real crack-repair technique, stated explicitly as a first-time
   application for this company (not a settled standard method)**: cut
   a 2cm-deep groove along the crack with an angle-grinder cutting disc
   (4 parallel cuts, vacuum-attached for near-dustless cutting), remove
   the loosened plaster, then set a **routed strip of sound-insulating
   drywall (12.5mm)** into the channel with 3mm adhesive (≈15mm total),
   leaving ≈3mm depth for a putty skim (≈18mm total) — the routed depth
   is worked out backward from the drywall+glue+putty stack-up so the
   patch finishes flush with the surrounding wall. **Sound-insulating
   (reinforced/heavier) drywall is chosen specifically for its strength
   — explicitly framed as "won't crack again"** — an unusual
   crack-repair-specific material choice (soundproof drywall used for
   structural crack patching, not for its acoustic property).
3. **Recessed paper-tape technique at the patch seams**: non-perforated
   armature paper tape is embedded/recessed *below* the putty surface
   level along both drywall-to-plaster seams (not applied flush or
   proud), so the combined paper+putty buildup doesn't create a raised
   ridge once finished — a specific technique for keeping a patched
   seam invisible.
4. **Same technique re-applied on a second, distinct crack in the same
   apartment**: diagnostic cutting revealed **two separate cement
   plaster layers with a gypsum-and-sawdust filler sandwiched between
   them**, which had itself begun cracking/separating — the same
   groove-cut-and-drywall-strip technique was reapplied there,
   supporting this as a general-purpose retrofit crack repair, not a
   one-off fix specific to the first crack's exact cause.
