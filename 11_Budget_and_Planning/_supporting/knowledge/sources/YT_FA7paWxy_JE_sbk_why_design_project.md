## Planning Rules — Four Renovation-Organization Models Compared

### Model 1: Renovation with no design project at all

- **One named advantage**: saves the design project's own fee.
- **⚠️ Eight named, concrete disadvantages, each with a real mechanism**:
  1. **Constant unanswerable field questions** — the client is asked, on the spot, dozens of specific technical questions (outlet count/height/location, switch count and pass-through wiring, tub vs. shower, toilet vs. wall-hung with installation frame, leak-protection system, radiator vs. convector, floor-vs-wall utility routing, ceiling type, wall finish, floor material) with no design document to answer from, and work cannot start until answered.
  2. **Client forced to develop the layout solution personally** — either drawn on paper or marked directly on the walls for the crew, with no professional check.
  3. **Verbal-only communication risk with the crew**, when no drawing exists — a real named failure mode: a verbally agreed outlet height (e.g. 300mm) installed differently (e.g. 350mm) becomes an unresolvable he-said/she-said dispute once discovered, since neither side documented the agreement.
  4. **⚠️ Constant later revisions/corrections, each one more expensive after the fact** — the video's own worked example: realizing mid/late-project that a kitchen hood outlet, or a TV's wired internet point (needed because the TV's own Wi-Fi doesn't support the streaming app in use), was never planned, forcing either a long cable run across the apartment or fresh wall-chasing after finishes are already in — plus real risk the original crew (especially if relations are already strained) refuses to do the rework, forcing a costly search for a new crew mid-project.
  5. **Client must personally source all materials** — tile, wallpaper, paint selection all fall to the client; genuinely time-consuming without a pre-decided palette/spec.
  6. **⚠️ Delivery-delay/crew-walkoff risk, a named real consequence of undirected material sourcing**: if the client hasn't sourced materials in time and a delivery delay stalls the crew, the crew may demand down-time payment, or simply leave for another paying job — with real risk they never return, forcing the client to find a replacement crew mid-project.
  7. **⚠️ Post-completion furniture/cabinetry conflict, a real named failure discovered only after finishes are done**: once tile/electrical work is complete and furniture makers arrive to install kitchen/built-in cabinetry, it's discovered that cabinet units block outlets/switches (behind a cabinet or refrigerator) or that cabinet doors can't fully open — because no layout solution coordinated furniture footprint against fixed-point placement in advance.
  8. **⚠️ Unclear final cost, with a named real overrun magnitude**: without a project, ongoing mid-project changes/second-guessing compound, and the final price can run **1.5-2x** the originally agreed figure — with a real named risk the renovation goes unfinished if the client's budget had no reserve for this.

### Model 2: Client has a design project, no author supervision

- **Three named advantages**: (1) a clear, documented expected end result the crew is held to (eliminates the "we never agreed to that" dispute pattern from Model 1); (2) a clear, computable cost basis (materials quantities and labor scope are known from the project, so both rough and finish material budgets can be calculated in advance); (3) fewer disruptive field questions, since technical decisions are already documented in the drawings.
- **Two named disadvantages**: (1) the designer/design bureau has already been paid at project handover and has no financial incentive to stay engaged during construction — a client can't easily get free ad-hoc consultation from the original designer once the project is delivered, unless author supervision was separately purchased; (2) the design project itself is an added cost on top of construction.

### Model 3: Client has a design project plus paid author supervision ("авторский надзор")

- **One named major advantage**: the designer stays actively engaged through construction, taking most material-procurement and layout-question decisions off the client's plate.
- **⚠️ Two named disadvantages**: (1) author supervision is a real added cost, typically billed as a fixed monthly fee on top of the design-project fee; (2) **a designer-and-crew coordination gap is a real named risk when the two haven't worked together before** — an independent designer and an independently-hired crew with no prior working relationship can fail to find common ground on technical decisions, generating conflicts the client then has to personally mediate, costing time and money and slowing the renovation.

### Model 4: Design and construction from the same company (the speaker's own stated preferred model)

- **⚠️ Four named advantages, self-servingly concluding in favor of the speaker's own business model — tier-steering, record the mechanism, not the endorsement**: (1) designer and crew already work together routinely, with technical solutions already worked out between them from prior projects, reducing conflict and speeding the renovation; (2) **the design project is actively checked against the client's real budget during design itself**, catching an unrealistic/overscoped designer proposal before construction starts rather than after — the video's own real cited case: a client with an externally-produced (already paid-for) design project specified a built-in track-lighting ceiling system; once the client priced it independently, the actual cost was far beyond expectation and the feature was simply dropped, meaning the money already paid for that specific design element was wasted — a concrete illustration of the disconnect this integrated model claims to prevent; (3) the client is substantially removed from day-to-day procurement/technical decision-making, engaged only when genuinely necessary; (4) the client retains standing, ongoing access to the designer throughout the project (not gated behind a separate supervision fee, as framed by the speaker — though this may itself simply be bundled into this model's overall price rather than genuinely free).
