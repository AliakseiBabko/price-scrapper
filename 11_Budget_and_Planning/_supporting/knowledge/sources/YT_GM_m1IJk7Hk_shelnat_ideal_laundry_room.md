## Genuinely New Content

- **Room-planning heuristic — organize the largest objects first, and think in wall dimensions/clearances, not square meters**: the source states a professional planning approach — place washer, dryer, and drying cabinet first (largest objects), then medium items (sink, baskets), then storage/small shelving last — and explicitly says designers/planners don't reason in raw square-meter targets, only in wall lengths and the clearances needed for doors/passages to open, because floor area alone doesn't determine whether the room will actually be comfortable to use. A general planning-sequence rule not yet on this page.
- **Swing (hinged) doors preferred over sliding doors for a laundry room, soundproofing reason stated explicitly, and this extends to a niche/cabinet format too**: a standard hinged door with ordinary latching is recommended over a sliding door because sliding doors reduce sound isolation — and the source explicitly extends the same reasoning to a niche/built-in-cabinet laundry format, not just a full room with a real door.
- **Smallest available utility sink size, a specific number**: roughly 15×25cm for the smallest sink models — described as small enough to fit into almost any interior. A concrete dimension not previously recorded on this page (existing content only described sink function, not minimum size).
- **Independent-channel corroboration of the separate-washer-and-dryer preference, from an informal poll**: the source ran an Instagram poll among her own audience and reports that most households who bought a combo washer-dryer unit were dissatisfied with the drying function specifically, while households with separate washer and dryer units were satisfied and often enthusiastic. This is a **different channel** than the source that first established this preference on this page ([[07_Bathroom/analysis/...|Kruglov/Ontario]] via [[YT_sd2XYBZY-K8_kruglov_bathroom_2026_top10]]) — genuine cross-channel corroboration, not a repeat from the same practitioner, though both are informal/anecdotal (audience poll, not a controlled study).
- **A recommended minimum "best combination" of exactly three devices for an apartment or smaller house**: washing machine + dryer + garment steamer, stated as the source's own subjective opinion but framed as sufficient to make "practically any average housewife happy" — a compact alternative to the fuller checklist (drying cabinet, ironing board, etc.) for space-constrained homes specifically.
- **Combining the laundry room with a walk-in closet, convenience reason stated**: some households prefer this specifically so clean laundry can be hung directly in its storage place immediately after washing/drying, without an intermediate carrying step.
- **Combined light+iron switch, additional mechanical-wear reason**: beyond the "did I turn off the iron" anxiety-reduction reason already recorded from this batch's Michael Moore source, this source adds a second, distinct reason for wiring the iron's outlet to the same switch as the ironing-area light rather than routine plug/unplug: repeatedly pulling the plug out of the wall outlet mechanically stresses both the in-wall receptacle and the plug itself over time.
- **Drying cabinets specifically suited to country houses/dachas with children, mechanism restated more specifically**: the source ties demand directly to children's contact with mud/snow/puddles outdoors, plus the cabinet's own large footprint, which the source says doesn't always fit in a city apartment — a slightly more specific version of this batch's existing "drying cabinets are a country-house feature" note (adds the specific outdoor-contact driver, not just floor space).
