## Rules / Heuristics — Fixture- and Surface-Specific Cleaning Technique

- **⚠️ Never use solvent or acid-based chemical cleaner on acrylic bathtubs
  or on organic glass ("оргстекло," e.g. plexiglass shower-door panels)**
  — acrylic and organic glass are explicitly named as chemically
  vulnerable: solvent applied to organic glass makes it visibly cloud/
  "melt" (loses transparency, becomes matte/frosted) almost immediately.
  Use a dedicated weak-acid plumbing-specific product ("Альфа," described
  as formulated for sanitary fixtures, distinct from and weaker than the
  general-purpose solvents used elsewhere) for acrylic surfaces instead.
  A second, independent instance of the "wrong chemical damages a
  finish" mechanism already flagged twice in this batch's other two
  sources (fireplace-door solvent damage, faucet acid damage). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]
- **⚠️ Post-renovation window cleaning is a distinct multi-pass process,
  not a single wipe-down**: (1) a first pass removes bulk construction
  grime with a soapy solution plus an acid cleaner ("Азелит") brushed
  into corners and the frame track with a small brush — deliberately
  accepting streaks at this stage since it's only removing primary dirt;
  (2) a steam generator is used to blow accumulated dust/grit out of the
  frame track ("межрамье") and hardware, since fine construction dust
  packs into every gap and gasket a wipe alone can't reach — the source
  states this steam-flush step is what makes post-renovation window
  cleaning meaningfully harder than routine window cleaning; (3) a final
  polish pass uses a squeegee/magnetic window-cleaning tool plus dry
  polishing cloths, done from **both** the interior and exterior side of
  the glass, since spots missed cleaning from one side become visible
  only once viewed from the other. **Concrete time benchmark**: one
  especially dirty post-renovation window took roughly **1.5 hours** to
  fully clean this way. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]
- **Polished/high-gloss porcelain tile ("керамогранит под глянец")
  requires a dry pre-wipe before any wet chemical is applied**: wiping a
  dusty glossy-tile surface while already wet (chemical or water)
  produces permanent-looking white streaks/haze that repeated wiping
  won't fully remove — the surface must first be wiped completely dry to
  remove loose construction dust, *then* a solvent (646 solvent,
  "646 растворитель," applied via a dampened viscose cloth) is used to
  dissolve grout/plaster smears, immediately followed by a dry polishing
  cloth before the solvent evaporates. **Also notes lower wall sections
  accumulate disproportionately more dust than higher ones (gravity
  settling)** — clean systematically zone by zone, not just top-to-bottom
  in one pass, and reserve one section of cloth as "dirty" once it's
  picked up visible dust rather than continuing to spread it. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]
- **Every plumbing fixture must be fully dry, not just clean, at
  handover**: kitchen sink, bathroom sink, toilet, faucet, shower hose —
  visible dryness on chrome/fixtures is what actually reads as "clean" to
  a client; a wet-but-clean fixture reads as unfinished work. Stated as a
  standard professional handover rule, not a one-off preference. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]
- **Mosquito/insect screens need their own multi-step process and seasonal
  storage rule**: remove screens, steam-clean, treat with soapy solution
  plus an acid cleaner, rinse with a shower head/hose. **Seasonal rule**:
  during winter months (roughly December-March), screens should be
  removed and stored indoors rather than left mounted outdoors — the
  synthetic mesh degrades (stretches, tears) faster under winter/outdoor
  exposure. Also: mount a screen at a slight angle if it would otherwise
  touch an adjacent large fixed pane, to avoid abrasion contact over
  time. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]
- **Cross-contamination caution between light and dark grout lines**:
  switch to a fresh cloth before moving from a section with white/light
  grout to one with darker grout (and vice versa) — reusing the same
  cloth transfers dark grout residue onto light grout lines, staining
  them. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]

## Professional Conduct / Scope Notes

- **Cleaners do not move furniture or appliances themselves** — a client
  request to relocate a washing machine, for example, is explicitly
  declined as outside a cleaning crew's scope; that requires movers.
  Stated as a standing professional-boundary rule, not specific to this
  job. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]
- **Warn occupants before re-entering a room mid-task** (e.g. announcing
  before starting bathroom work so no family member walks in
  unexpectedly) — a client-comfort courtesy the source treats as basic
  professional practice. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]

- **⚠️ Chemical/solvent fume exposure is framed as a genuine occupational
  health hazard, not a minor inconvenience** — the source states
  explicitly that people with allergies or asthma should reconsider
  working in professional cleaning specifically because of routine
  chemical exposure, not as a throwaway caution. `single-account`, first
  explicit occupational-health framing in this vault's cleaning content.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]
- **Cheap disposable gloves (~80 RUB, budget/"fix price" store tier)
  degrade quickly under the solvents/acids used in this work** — tear and
  dissolve faster than their price would suggest; a real durability/PPE-
  cost note for anyone doing this work themselves. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iu-GGC7hEKA_ilyonova_85m2_cleaning_secrets|iu-GGC7hEKA]]]
