## Rules / Heuristics — New Detail

1. **Converting a design-project's finished dimension into a raw
   masonry-layout dimension by adding a plaster allowance — a worked
   numeric example**: the design project specifies a finished dimension
   of 775mm for a chase/niche; the crew adds 10mm (1cm) to get 785mm as
   the actual raw block-layout target, since the design figure already
   assumes a finished (plastered) surface. A general technique for
   translating any design-project dimension into a masonry marking
   dimension, not specific to this one measurement.
2. **Precision utility-chase layout coordinated in advance with future
   kitchen-cabinet module placement**: a 600×600mm chase for hot/cold
   water and sewage lines is laid out and squared using a laser level
   specifically so that, once finished/plastered, built-in kitchen
   cabinet modules will sit flush against it with no gap — the chase
   position is derived directly from the design project's kitchen
   layout dimensions before any of the kitchen units exist on site.
3. **"Average-face" leveling method for compensating block
   dimensional imperfection, a distinct mechanism from the plumb-
   tolerance rule already on this page**: rather than aligning a course
   to one consistent edge/face (which lets small per-block errors
   accumulate and "run away" progressively down a wall), the crew
   levels to each block's *average* face position, tolerating up to
   ~2mm of individual block deviation to either side — imperceptible
   per block, and it prevents the cumulative drift that a single-edge
   reference method would produce over a long run.
4. **Rebar-groove cutting must wait for the adjacent block's adhesive to
   fully cure before cutting**: cutting a reinforcement groove with a
   reciprocating/sabre saw while a neighboring block's glue is still wet
   disturbs/dislodges the uncured bond — sequencing rule, cut only once
   adjacent adhesive has set.
5. **Working around existing embedded rebar tied into the building's
   structural risers, rather than cutting it**: when a block's placement
   would otherwise require removing rebar that's tied into the
   building's risers/structural framework, the crew explicitly states
   they "have no right" to remove it — the block itself is trimmed to
   fit around the existing rebar instead of disturbing the reinforcement.
6. **Reinforcement-groove technique detail**: grooves for embedding
   rebar at rows 3/6/9 are cut roughly 1.5cm offset from each block's
   center with a sabre saw, angled so the same rebar run can pass
   continuously; groove width/depth just needs to comfortably admit the
   rebar's full length, not a tight tolerance fit; loose dust is cleared
   by broom/brush and water before the rebar and adhesive go in, so the
   adhesive fully fills the groove without voids.
7. **Adhesive-application technique framed by analogy to wallpaper
   paste**: apply the block adhesive generously, then wipe/press it down
   to a thin, even layer with a single deliberate pass ("like wallpaper
   glue") — the adhesive layer is for adhesion, not for leveling out
   substrate unevenness; a too-thick adhesive bed is explicitly called
   out as weakening the bond, corroborating (with this specific analogy)
   this page's existing 3-4mm adhesive-thickness rule.
