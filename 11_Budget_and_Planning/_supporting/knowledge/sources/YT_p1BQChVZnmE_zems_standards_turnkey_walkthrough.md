## Durable Facts

- **Low-voltage panel needs a ventilated enclosure if it will house high-draw equipment** (e.g. an NVR/DVR video recorder or network storage) rather than the standard sealed low-voltage box — a standard enclosure will let such equipment overheat and fail prematurely.
- **Video intercom should be placed in the "clean zone"** (interior side, away from the entry/mudroom "dirty zone") wherever the floor plan allows, so residents can screen visitors without walking through/tracking dirt from the entry area to check who's at the door — explicitly framed as intentional design, not a planning mistake, if a viewer sees it placed this way.
- **Washing-machine and storage niches must be sized in the project accounting for finish-layer thickness** (wall leveling + tile), not raw wall-to-wall measurements — otherwise the machine or cabinet won't fit once tile is applied. Directly matches the general principle already in `07_Bathroom/Bathroom_Guide.md` §4 ("Correct Dimensions — Budget for Finish-Layer Thickness"), applied here specifically to a small-bathroom washing-machine niche and an adjacent storage cabinet.
- **Small-bathroom storage niches**: any unused space in a small bathroom should be converted into storage (the video shows a niche created via a raised water-inlet-node platform, used for toiletries/paper goods) — speaker states this specific standard doesn't apply to bathrooms over 15 m² (joked as rare).
- **Seating for elderly occupants in shower-only (no bathtub) bathrooms should be planned and built in at project stage**, not improvised later as a stool or hanging seat — improvised wet-area seating is called out as a fall/injury risk (can slip if sat on off-center). A properly built seat should be structurally solid, rated for load, cleanly finished with no exposed joints or odd cuts.
- **Electrical panel circuit granularity**: separate breaker per outlet group (not per whole room), separate breaker per room's lighting, and separate breakers for major appliances (AC, washing machine, fridge) — plus a mix of switchable and always-on circuits, RCDs (УЗО) and differential/AFDD breakers, all clearly labeled on the panel schedule so a non-electrician homeowner can read which breaker controls what. Explicitly contrasted with a bad-practice baseline of 3 breakers total (main/all-lighting/all-outlets), which forces a homeowner to kill all outlets in the apartment to fix one dead outlet, or change a single bulb in total darkness because there's no separate per-room lighting circuit.
- **Sconce ("бра") wiring practice**: strip old cable insulation before final connection (rather than leaving construction-stage-damaged insulation) and terminate with spring-type wire connectors (WAGO-style), described as standard practice the crew handles so the client doesn't have to.
- **Dedicated night/duty lighting ("дежурное освещение")** — low-level always-available lighting (e.g. for nighttime bathroom trips) so occupants don't have to turn on full room lighting (glare, sleep disruption) or navigate in total darkness (trip/injury risk, especially with pets or kids' toys underfoot).
- **Turnkey ("под ключ") scope definition** (useful as a checklist for what should be included before signing off): all interior doors installed; baseboards installed except where furniture will later sit (deliberately left for post-furniture trim-to-fit); bathrooms fully tiled with vanity, sink, mirror, towel warmer, shower cabin installed and functional; toilets with flush button installed and functional; all electrical wired and connected including switches/outlets; low-voltage panel prepped (equipment hookup only remaining); windows, sills, reveals, and radiator grilles installed; radiators installed and pressure-tested where called for in the project; only the stretch ceiling remains before handover. Furniture, kitchen, appliances, and interior design objects (art, etc.) are explicitly NOT included in "turnkey."
- **Building-common-area work (stairwell landing finishing, opening narrowing/relocation at the apartment's front door) is a separate cost the customer must pay**, not included in the apartment renovation price even for the most expensive tier — a common point of customer confusion flagged explicitly.
