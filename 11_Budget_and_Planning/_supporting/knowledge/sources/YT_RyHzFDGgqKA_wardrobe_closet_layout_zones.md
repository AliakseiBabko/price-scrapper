## Furniture / Built-ins — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Storage-zone tiering (single-sided)**: 1-tier (single rod ~170-200cm high — wastes space, only for temporary use before real wardrobe furniture arrives); 2-tier variant A (both tiers clothing — bad, forces same-length garments, bottom row nearly touches floor, top rod ~220-230cm, hard for short people) vs. variant B (bottom 50cm shoe tier + rod at ~2m for clothes — the space above ~210cm is reserved for ducted ventilation units in large/premium apartments, explaining why some rooms have unusually low usable ceiling); 4-tier (small shoe tier + 2 clothing rows + shelf — bad, awkward shoe access, top rod ~240-250cm, same-length-garment constraint); **3-tier is called the most practical**: bottom 50cm (shoes), middle rod at 2m (clothes), top shelf at 210cm. `confirmed`.
- **Shelf/zone depth rule**: clothing-zone depth 60cm; shoe-zone depth 35-40cm (max 45cm) — **never 60cm for shoes**, since shoe boxes placed two rows deep along a 60cm-deep zone block front-row access, forcing a search through the whole depth; shoes should sit perpendicular to the wall in a single row instead. `confirmed`, concrete rule.
- **Single-sided walk-in closet minimum width formula**: 135cm = 10cm door-trim standoff + 70cm rough opening (for a 60cm door) + 55cm storage-zone depth (becomes 60cm finished). Can be reduced to 125cm (drop the standoff if no adjacent wall trim conflict) or further to 120cm (drop standoff + shorten the storage zone by 45-50cm, using 30-40cm-deep shelving instead) with a 60cm walk aisle still functional. `confirmed`, precise numeric derivation.
- **Don't shrink a bedroom to carve out a single-sided walk-in closet** — a plain wardrobe in the same bedroom footprint holds the same volume of clothing without losing ~70cm just for walking-past clearance, plus avoids a narrower entry corridor and extra door/wall-finish cost. **Minimum room length to justify a single-sided walk-in**: 5m (135cm closet + 10cm dividing wall + 60cm wardrobe + 160cm bed-to-wardrobe aisle + double bed + 50cm aisle + 15cm curtain clearance + 15cm false-wall for pipes/radiators), assuming 3m room width — yields only ~10m² usable bedroom after ~15m² gross, "very little." `confirmed`, worked example.
- **Maximum single-sided walk-in width**: 145cm (at 150cm: 60cm storage + 60cm aisle + 30cm shelving opposite; at 175cm: upgrades to a double-sided layout — 55cm aisle + 2×60cm storage zones). Double-sided walk-in doors typically swing outward for access convenience, **except when opening into a narrow corridor, where inward-swing is chosen for safety over comfort**. `confirmed`.
- **Three-sided walk-in minimum leg length: 80cm** — shorter legs force overlapping storage zones (one zone must be pushed back behind another) that become impractically hard to reach (compressing 60cm of hanging clothes to reach another 60cm behind it). **Overlap direction rule**: always overlap counter-clockwise (right-side zone placed first, front zone overlaps it, left zone overlaps the front one) — reasoning: most people are right-handed, so compressing clothes with the stronger right hand while retrieving with the free left hand is more comfortable; the same handedness logic applies to reaching into far shoe-storage corners. `confirmed`, ergonomic handedness rule.
- **Four-sided walk-in minimum width**: 260cm (auxiliary zone on left) or 280cm (auxiliary zone on right) — from 300cm+ additional configurations become viable, more so above 360cm.
