## Design Concept / Furniture — Durable Facts & Rules

**Three mounting-method families for interior sliding partitions**, per
this source: (1) ceiling-mounted (upper guide rail fixed to the
structural ceiling); (2) wall-mounted (upper guide fixed to a wall
instead of the ceiling); (3) cassette-mounted (partition retracts into a
wall niche/pocket). All three share the same core hardware logic below.

**⚠️ Embedded-backing ("закладная") sizing formula for a ceiling-mounted
partition under a planned stretch ceiling**: backing thickness should
equal the gap between the structural ceiling and the stretch-ceiling
membrane (~5cm), so the membrane presses snugly against the backing
timber. Backing width: **15cm for a 3-leaf/3-rail partition, 10cm for a
classic 2-leaf/2-rail partition** (scales with rail count).

**⚠️ Don't sandwich the stretch-ceiling membrane between two backing
blocks**: installing one backing block under the structural ceiling, then
stretching the membrane, then adding a second matching block below the
membrane traps the fabric between them — a documented bad practice.
Better: install a single ~10cm-tall backing block *before* the stretch
ceiling goes in, accepting that the room now needs two independent
stretch-ceiling pieces (costs more) — but a damaged/flooded piece near
the partition can then be removed and reinstalled independently without
touching the rest of the room's ceiling.

**⚠️ Directly answers this project's own retrofit question — suspended-rod
technique for adding a ceiling-mounted partition to an ALREADY-installed
stretch ceiling with no embedded backing**: glue reinforced tape to the
membrane, cut a hole, insert threaded rods/studs ("шпильки") spaced
~0.5m apart through the hole into the structural ceiling above, and hang
a "floating" backing beam from these rods rather than a fixed embedded
block. Demonstrated stable in the company's own showroom for "several
years" at time of filming — a real, if `single-account`, alternative to
either (a) planning the backing before the ceiling goes in, or (b) the
floor-track fallback described in `YT_LB2oYxyYL3c`, or (c) the
cut-and-reweld-the-membrane approach in `YT_qcIz2--60Ts`.

**Third ceiling-mount option — hidden under drywall**: the upper rail can
instead be concealed entirely under a drywall ceiling section for a
minimalist look with no visible cornice/trim.

**Wall-mounted method's own limitation and gap-closing techniques**: only
supports two parallel-moving leaves (not 3+ as ceiling-mount allows).
Because the leaf doesn't terminate against a wall the way a
room-bisecting ceiling-mounted partition does, opening it leaves a visible
gap between the outer leaf and the wall. Two fixes: (a) build a box/return
the width of a cornice, creating a physical stop for the leaf to rest
against; (b) add a leaf-widening extension element so the gap closes when
retracted.

**⚠️ Partition-vs-hinged-door structural distinction, generalizable**: a
sliding partition's leaf frame is deliberately thinner/lighter than a
hinged interior door's (this source's own MDF partition: 6mm frame vs. an
ordinary interior door's ~11cm-quoted, evidently meant ~1.1cm, frame)
because a partition only ever hangs from the top track and never needs
the diagonal rigidity a hinged door does. **A sliding partition can never
be installed as a swinging hinged door; an ordinary hinged door CAN be
re-hung as a sliding partition** — asymmetric substitutability worth
knowing when comparing options.

**Cornice/trim covering the top track**: made from standard door-casing
stock, same finish as the leaves; minimum 150mm width for a harmonious
look (narrower looks visually thin/cheap). Telescopic (adjustable-depth)
casing is common but creates an unsightly visible step; a cleaner
technique is shaving down one slat of the telescopic casing for a thinner,
more elegant reveal.

**⚠️ Directly clarifies the household's core "no floor rail" question —
there is always exactly one floor-level fixation point, never a rail**:
per this source, sliding partitions never have a genuine bottom
*track/rail* — the actual guide channel is built into the bottom edge of
the leaf itself, and the floor only carries a single small pin/peg
("флажок"/"штырь") that this internal channel rides along. Regardless of
1, 2, or 5 leaves, there is exactly **one** floor contact point per system,
not a visible track — this is the mechanism behind the "no floor rail"
claim these systems are sold on, and matches the stopper/pin element
independently confirmed in `YT_sFE1EyKRHvE`'s customer testimonial for a
different installer's own product.

**Closed-leaf-to-wall abutment problem and four fixes** (relevant when a
ceiling-mounted partition bisects a room): a room's own baseboard/skirting
normally prevents the leaf's end from sealing flush against the wall.
Fixes, in this source's stated order of preference: (1) cut a gap/notch
in the baseboard where the leaf lands (simplest); (2) bevel/notch the
corner of the leaf itself to clear the baseboard profile (this source's
own least-recommended option, less practical); (3) mount a matching
strip/trim on the wall (from casing or architrave stock) for the leaf to
abut — also useful when the two zones have different wall colors, since
the strip doubles as a visual divider; (4) **this source's stated best
option — a concealed/flush baseboard system** with no protruding profile,
letting the partition (and furniture) sit fully flush against the wall.

**Leaf-height formula**: with no stretch ceiling, leaf height = floor-to-
ceiling distance **minus 12cm** (5cm embedded backing + 6-7cm rail/roller
hardware + ~1cm floor gap). Add another **5cm** if a stretch ceiling is
planned (total minus ~17cm).

**Leaf-width worked formula**: leaves overlap each other by one full
frame-width ("царга") at each junction so the closed partition reads as
one continuous surface from a distance. Worked example: 3m opening,
3-leaf partition, 6cm frame width → total width needed = 3m + (2 × 6cm) =
3.12m ÷ 3 leaves = **1.04m per leaf**. Same 3m opening with a 2-leaf
partition instead: **1.53m per leaf**.

**Hardware taxonomy and add-ons**: minimum kit = top rail, "bottom rail"
(in practice just the single guide pin described above), rollers, the
small pin/flag. Add-ons: (1) **synchronizer** — makes all leaves open
together when pulling just the first one, avoiding a jerky "micro-impact"
as one leaf drags the next into motion; described as both more comfortable
and better for long-term hardware durability (repeated small impacts/
vibration degrade the system faster over time); (2) **soft-close damper**
— cushions both opening and closing, and specifically helps achieve a
tight, gap-free seal against the wall/stop when closed; (3) **handle** —
any shape/color, but must have no protruding lip (it slides behind the
fixed leaf's edge when open); can incorporate a lock, but a lock needs a
mating strike plate/box built into the wall or an adjacent leaf.

**⚠️ Sound isolation — a blunt admission, corroborating this store's
existing caution**: sliding partitions have **minimal** sound isolation —
"по сути ширма" (essentially a screen/curtain-equivalent). Felt/brush
weatherstripping glued along the bottom and sides gives only a small
improvement. For anyone prioritizing real sound isolation, this source
recommends hanging thick curtains in addition to the partition — much
more effective than the glass partition alone. **Directly corroborates**
the existing no-soundproofing caution recorded from an independent source
in `11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/analysis/Bedroom_Design_Principles.md`
(`HGN_3WeL0Jk`, Kruglov/Ontario) — now a second, independent
practitioner (a manufacturer, not just an installer) confirming the same
mechanism.
