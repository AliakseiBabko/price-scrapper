## Planning Rules

- **Stated design-process order (1)**: (1) on-site survey — a 3D model
  built to within ~1 cm accuracy of the real apartment, plus a written
  client brief/questionnaire captured at the same visit; (2) concept
  development and client approval; (3) full 3D model development and
  approval; (4) schematics/drawings — explicitly deprioritized by the
  speaker as "a distant tenth-place concern" relative to getting the 3D
  model right first, since the speaker frames correct 3D modeling as the
  step everything else flows from smoothly once it's right.
- **Client-brief checklist — what a design questionnaire captures**,
  demonstrated on a real client's actual brief: intended ownership horizon
  (this client: "interested in this apartment for the next 10 years,"
  open to reselling later or not), family-planning status (no children
  yet, planning at least one), pet ownership (two cats — directly drove a
  flooring-material decision, see Flooring below), both occupants'
  height (174/170 cm, for ergonomic/reach planning), sleep-schedule
  compatibility ("biorhythms match"), allergies (dust only, no other
  allergies noted), vision (normal), handedness (both right-handed). The
  source states the real brief is "quite extensive" beyond what's shown.
  Reusable as a self-managed-owner checklist for what to think through
  and record before finalizing a layout, independent of whether a paid
  designer is involved.
- **Structural walls set the baseline geometry to design around** — the
  team explicitly starts a redesign from the load-bearing wall segments
  only, demolishing everything else (shown color-coded on the 3D model:
  red = demolished, teal = structural/kept) before laying out the new
  plan.
- **"Project within a project" — planning a room for a future
  furniture/use-case change without redoing electrical work later**: for
  a room expected to convert from one use to another later (here: an
  office/home-gym now, a children's room later), the design pre-plans
  BOTH layouts' electrical points during the single rough-in stage — the
  future layout's outlets are installed now, wallpapered over/hidden, and
  only uncovered when the room's actual furniture layout changes. In this
  project's case, two separate pull-up-bar wall reinforcements
  ("закладные") were also pre-installed for the same reason (one for the
  gym-era wall-mounted bar, one for the eventual different wall it would
  move to). `confirmed`, a genuinely reusable planning technique for
  anyone anticipating a room's use will change within the same electrical
  rough-in lifecycle (e.g. a nursery-to-kids-room or office-to-nursery
  conversion).
- **Matching wall thickness across two new parallel partitions purely for
  visual door-casing consistency** — even where one wall's required
  thickness is structurally driven by fitting a walk-in closet's minimum
  clearance (145 cm) and the other by other room-layout constraints
  (325 cm), the team deliberately built both new walls to the same
  thickness so that door casings installed into both openings would
  produce a matching-depth reveal on both doors, rather than two visibly
  different casing depths in wall thicknesses that didn't need to match
  structurally. `confirmed`, presented as the company's own standard
  reasoning, checkable design-consistency heuristic.

## Quantities / Measurements

- **Walk-in-closet minimum clearance figures (this project's specific
  case, not stated as a universal minimum)**: ~145 cm needed for a person
  to approach clothing and use a small seated grooming area; ~325 cm for
  an adjacent zone whose depth is driven by bed length + TV-zone
  thickness + a minimum walk-through clearance. Presented by the QC lead
  as values checked against the company's own internal "knowledge base"
  of minimum clearances — the specific 145/325 cm figures are this
  project's own dimensions, not asserted as fixed universal minimums, but
  the *practice* of maintaining and checking against a standardized
  minimum-clearance reference is the reusable part. `single-account`.
- **Toilet clearance zone: ~80 cm** — stated as generous ("quite a lot")
  space for the toilet itself plus a pet litter box, towel warmer, and
  sink/mirror in the same guest WC. Corroborates (does not newly
  establish) this store's existing WC-clearance figures from other
  sources — recorded here as a second same-figure data point from a
  different project.

## Flooring

- **Material-selection rule driven by pet ownership**: client's original
  preference was cork flooring throughout, but the designer talked them
  out of it specifically because cork wears out quickly under cat claws
  (2 cats in this household) — a concrete durability-vs-preference
  tradeoff resolved in favor of durability. **Resulting material split**:
  engineered wood board ("инженерная доска") throughout the apartment
  except wet rooms, quartz vinyl ("кварц винил," an LVT-type material) in
  the kitchen and entry hall specifically (explicitly stated as
  inappropriate to lay engineered board in those two rooms) — presented
  as visually near-identical to the engineered board despite being a
  different, more durable material category. The client separately
  rejected linoleum as looking "cheap" even though objectively it may not
  be, illustrating that perceived material prestige, not just technical
  performance, is a real factor in this kind of decision — the source
  states the designer did not push back hard on that preference.

## Family Requirements / Preferences

- **Laundry-sorting requirement**: client specifically wanted a large
  laundry hamper split into **four separate sub-baskets** for pre-sorting
  by category, located in a dedicated laundry-adjacent space, plus a
  separate closet for the household's cleaning staff. Illustrates a
  concrete, specific client requirement beyond generic "needs a laundry
  room."
- **His-and-hers closets** requested and delivered as two fully separate
  walk-in closets (one per occupant) rather than one shared closet — each
  with its own zone; the primary bathroom similarly got two full sinks
  ("something the client had wanted since childhood") plus a private
  entrance from the primary bedroom, a hygienic shower, and a separate
  toilet.
