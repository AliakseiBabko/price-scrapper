## Ceilings — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Zemskov's stated stretch-ceiling fabric width limit**: manufacturer-rated maximum 5.10m (5m + 10cm for mounting/installation allowance) — but he says to reliably plan against a flat 5m, since real usable seamless coverage tops out around 5.02-5.03m once installation is accounted for. **A room exceeding this in its longer dimension cannot get a single seamless (no-seam) stretch-ceiling piece over the whole span.**
- **Real cited case exceeding the limit**: a living room measuring 5.92m in one direction (window wall to opposite wall) and 8.5m in the other — both dimensions over the 5m seamless limit, in a room combining living room + kitchen ("гостиная и хом" — likely "гостиная и кухня" mis-transcribed/misheard) as one continuous space.
- **Zemskov's fix: build a false partition wall (пристенок) with a portal opening**, deliberately positioned to divide the oversized room into two zones each under 5m, letting a single seamless stretch-ceiling piece cover each zone independently rather than the whole space needing a visible seam. Construction specifics: 10cm-thick partition returns on each side of the portal opening, with the opening itself sized down 20cm from the ceiling; the opening's reveal finished with interior-door jamb-extension trim (доборы), even though the surrounding walls were already prepped for wallpaper — he states this rework was necessary despite that sunk prep cost.
- **Stated second benefit beyond avoiding the seam**: the new partition also protects an exposed exterior corner in the same location, referencing this store's already-recorded "emka"/corner-impact theme (corners at this location get hit repeatedly, especially when someone catches a foot on them) — the same underlying corner-protection logic already present elsewhere in this knowledge base, now applied to justify a ceiling-driven wall addition rather than the reverse.
- **Zemskov's own three-point summary, given directly at the end of the video**: (1) never forget the technical width limitation of stretch-ceiling fabric when planning a layout; (2) don't restrict nuance-catching and correction to the design/project stage — keep watching for issues and fixing them during construction too; (3) make corrective changes at the stage where they cost the client and the crew the least, not later.
