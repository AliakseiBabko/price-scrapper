- **Heated flooring in the entryway, stated independently by at least two
  of the eight featured owners as their own top recommendation** — reasons
  given: (a) speeds drying of wet/snowy shoes brought in from outside; (b)
  a pass-through entry corridor is otherwise noticeably cold underfoot. One
  owner explicitly regretted skipping it ("I made a big mistake — I didn't
  put in heated flooring here, and it was wrong, because our hallway is a
  pass-through and it's very cold"). `single-account` per speaker, but
  independently repeated by two of the eight unrelated households in this
  compilation — treated as a genuine repeated recommendation, not one
  source's opinion. [corroborates this store's existing entry-flooring
  durability heuristic from `yt_DsdLa87Acz4` (hard/scratch-resistant surface
  preference), extends it with the heated-floor reasoning specifically]
- **Level, threshold-free transition between two different entry floor
  materials (porcelain tile + engineered wood board) achieved with a
  liquid cork ("жидкая пробка") joint** rather than a conventional metal/
  plastic transition strip — avoids a raised threshold trip hazard entirely
  and keeps a flush, single-plane floor across the transition.
  `single-account`, real project.
- **Tall doors (2.70m, floor-to-ceiling) as a stated designer technique to
  make ceiling height read as taller than it is** — mentioned independently
  by two different featured apartments in this compilation as a deliberate
  visual-height trick, not just a stylistic preference.
- **Hidden electrical panel behind a removable decorative wall panel, with
  a ~10cm air gap built into the panel's own construction specifically for
  ventilation** — the stated reason is to prevent the panel (electrical
  breaker box) from overheating once enclosed; the gap also doubles as a
  small storage niche on each side. A real, checkable mechanism (not just

  finish material.
- **A large sliding/rolling wardrobe-and-laundry combo unit consolidated

  clothing rail, washer, dryer, vacuum, boiler, and long-term storage shelf
  all behind rolling doors in the entry zone specifically **to keep laundry
  functionally independent from the bathroom** — stated reason: so laundry
  can run while someone else is bathing, without noise/access conflict.
  `single-account`, real project — a concrete zoning rationale distinct
  from simply "put the washer somewhere else."
- **A small door-frame/wall recess ("ниша") sized so the built-in
  wardrobe's top edge lines up exactly with a wall color-break line** — used
  deliberately in a narrow, elongated (non-rectangular) entry to visually
  "push the walls apart," making the space read as wider and lower rather
  than narrow and tall. A specific, replicable proportioning technique for
  a small/awkward-shaped entry, not just a color choice.
- **In a small entryway, prefer unusual/dark/bold color choices plus

  stated explicitly as a counter to a common assumption ("many people think
