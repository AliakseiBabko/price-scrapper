## Rules / Heuristics — Coverage, Clearances, and Hardware Technique

- **Terminology/design-concept framing**: professional decorators avoid
  "шторы"/"тряпки" (curtains/rags) and use "текстильное оформление"
  ("textile treatment") — framed as a complete, finished visual concept
  for a window, not just a fabric panel. **Coverage-width decision**:
  extend the treatment only to the window's own width (+10-15cm), or run
  it wall-to-wall to also visually disguise/unify the flanking wall
  segments and complete the room's overall look — a real design choice
  with no single right answer, driven by whether the flanking walls need
  visual help.
- **⚠️ Radiator clearance rule**: where a radiator sits under a window and
  a two-layer treatment (sheer + drape) is used, allow **~15cm** of
  depth clearance so the hanging curtain doesn't contact/obstruct the
  radiator.
- **AC-unit concealment niche rule**: to hide a wall-mounted AC unit
  behind curtains (drapes) without a visible bulge, the concealment niche
  needs to be **≥30cm**.
- **⚠️ Ceiling curtain-track installation rule, a real failure-prevention
  detail**: avoid plastic tracks run in double/triple-row configurations;
  a slim-profile metal track is named as the best option (with a wide
  price range). **Regardless of track material, a wooden blocking strip
  ("закладная") must be embedded in the ceiling before finish work**, so
  the track has real anchoring — without it, the track can eventually
  fail and fall.
- **Motorized curtain-track ("электрокарниз") planning rule**: needs a
  wider niche (**≥20cm**) to fit the drive motor; the motor's mounting
  side needs to be decided in advance. Control options: a physical
  button, a phone app, or a bedside remote; some smart-home setups can
  open/close curtains on an automated schedule tied to the household's
  own wake/return routine ("biorhythm"-based scheduling).
- **⚠️ Real complex motorized-curtain project example**: a room with a
  raised terrace/step accessed by stairs, needing full blackout — one
  curtain track ran straight along the upper landing, a second track
  dropped down to the lower floor level, motor-driven from a point at the
  upper landing's edge, with the two curtains traveling unequal distances
  and opening in opposite directions. Cited explicitly as a genuinely
  hard-to-self-plan engineering problem, offered as the reason to involve
  a decorator for anything beyond a simple window. `single-account`,
  real project example.
- **Aesthetic-restraint principle**: an over-decorated result (cited
  cautionary example: a salon that layered every possible embellishment —
  "bells, flowers, everything that could be added") is contrasted with
  restrained, "dosed" use of texture/decoration — analogized to a
  well-dressed person needing no excess to read as elegant.
- **Two-sided blackout fabric exists as a single-layer alternative to a
  separate blackout lining** — one visually finished side faces the room,
  full blackout performance is built into the same fabric, for buyers who
  want blackout without paying for/managing a second lining layer.
- **⚠️ Fabric-selection technique — evaluate vertically, one swatch at a
  time, held to a real window**: don't judge fabric from a flat catalog
  spread; hold one swatch vertically against an actual window to see how
  light passes through and what color cast results. **"White" fabric
  varies meaningfully once backlit** — worked example given: several
  swatches that all look identically white laid flat on a table turn out
  cool-blue-tinted, warm-yellow-tinted, or pearl-toned once held to
  actual daylight — match the fabric's undertone against the room's own
  wall-color undertone (warm vs. cool) rather than trusting a
  flat/artificial-light comparison.
- **Fabric weight should match the room's existing visual "weight"**: a
  room already carrying heavy-textured wall treatments shouldn't also get
  a heavy fabric like velvet (overload); conversely, velvet can suit a
  visually light/minimal interior with oversized windows, used sparingly.
  Independently corroborates (from a different angle — fabric weight
  rather than color/contrast) `YT_ze87vOi2qrQ`'s existing "heavy-textured
  fabrics add visual depth but overuse causes clutter" principle. **Real
  durability/scale caution**: heavy fabrics like velvet aren't
  recommended for small rooms — visually heavy fabric can make a small
  space feel like it's closing in.
- **Fabric density as a room-proportion correction tool, distinct
  mechanism from `YT_ze87vOi2qrQ`'s color-based technique**: hanging a
  denser/heavier curtain fabric can be used deliberately to visually pull
  in/shorten a disproportionately long room dimension — the denser the
  hung textile, the more strongly it visually "presses" the space toward
  the viewer.
- **Window-reveal prep, condensation/mold prevention**: seal and properly
  finish the window reveal area to prevent condensation ("точка росы,"
  dew point) forming behind the frame, which causes mold around plastic
  window frames if left unaddressed — paint the reveals and install a
  proper windowsill. **Four named windowsill material categories**: stone,
  plastic, moisture-resistant LDSP (particleboard), and porcelain tile
  ("керамогранит").

## Mistakes / Warnings

- **⚠️ Real cautionary anecdote — fabric shrinkage after laundering**: an
  expensive fabric (~25,000 RUB, hung in a living room) shrank noticeably
  after being washed, ending up ~10cm short of the floor. Stated lesson:
  know a specific fabric's actual care requirements (some fabrics shrink)
  before committing to an expensive curtain — an aftercare-ignorance risk
  distinct from the installation-technique content above.
