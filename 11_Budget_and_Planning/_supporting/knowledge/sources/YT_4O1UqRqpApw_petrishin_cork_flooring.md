## Flooring — General Sub-Floor Prep (framed as applying to laminate, engineered board, and cork alike)

2. **Sub-floor flatness spec, a second numeric data point distinct from
   this page's existing Zemskov rule**: verified with a **3-meter
   straightedge**, maximum deviation **2-3mm** — this page's existing
   laminate/engineered-board rule (Zemskov) uses a shorter 2m straightedge
   with a stricter 2mm maximum. Not treated as a contradiction (different
   check span, similar order of tolerance), but recorded as a second,
   distinct real-world spec rather than blended into the existing one.
3. **Priming rationale, more specific than a generic "always prime"
   rule**: after thorough cleaning (construction vacuum preferred, broom
   as a fallback), primer is applied specifically because it seals in
   the micro-dust particles neither a vacuum nor a broom can fully
   remove — the primer layer itself is the actual dust-control step, not
   the cleaning pass alone.
4. **PVC vapor-barrier film step, genuinely new to this page**: after
   the primer cures, a PVC film is rolled out across the entire floor
   before the impact-noise underlayment — both because most laminate/
   parquet/cork manufacturers specify it in their own installation
   instructions, and because it lets any residual construction moisture
   in the screed escape toward the room's perimeter instead of being
   trapped directly under the flooring. **Cork-specific added benefit**:
   also isolates the cork from the rough surface texture of a cement-
   sand screed, extending the cork floor's service life beyond what
   installing it directly on the screed would achieve.
5. **Underlayment options named**: cork sold in rolls or in sheet/"carpet"
   form for use as the impact-noise underlayment layer beneath the
   finish flooring, alongside other isolator products on the market —
   a materials-category note.

## Flooring — Installation Technique

6. **Last-board-width planning rule, a genuinely new mechanism**: before
   starting, measure the room to determine how many whole boards fit and
   how wide the final board at the far wall will be. **If that last
   board would come out narrower than ~5cm, the first row is instead cut
   roughly in half** — a board narrower than ~5cm can't have its
   click-lock mechanism engaged without difficulty, so the fix is
   applied at the *start* of the layout (producing symmetric partial
   boards at both ends of the room) rather than accepted at the end.
7. **⚠️ Thermal expansion gap, a second numeric figure worth flagging
   against this page's existing baseboard-coordination note**: minimum
   **4-5mm** required around the entire room perimeter so the flooring
   can expand/contract with temperature and humidity — skipping it risks
   the floor visibly buckling/tenting within days to about 1.5 months.
   This page's existing Round 10 baseboard note cites a **typical
   executed gap of 8-12mm** (installer-to-installer variance, covered by
   baseboard width) — not treated as a contradiction (a stated
   *minimum* spec vs. a *typically-executed* range are different
   things), but recorded as a second data point rather than silently
   merged with the existing figure.
8. **Spacer-wedge technique for holding a consistent perimeter gap**:
   for straight, true walls, cut pieces of the same flooring material
   work as spacers; for walls with real deviation, tapered wedges of
   varying thickness are used instead to keep the gap consistent despite
   the wall's own unevenness.
9. **Joint sealant technique**: a sealant applied to board joints
   specifically to block moisture ingress between boards and extend
   service life; excess sealant is designed to cure in roughly 7-10
   minutes and then peel off cleanly like a film, leaving no residue on
   the flooring surface.
10. **⚠️ Seam-offset ("разбежка") rule, a concrete numeric minimum**:
    stagger between the end-joints of adjacent rows must be **at least
    15-20cm** — a smaller offset risks squeaking and gap development at
    that joint over time.

## Corroborated, Not Re-Counted
