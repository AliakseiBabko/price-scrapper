## Balcony / Loggia — Durable Facts & Rules (new content beyond the companion video)

- **Loggia finished-floor-height reference rule**: per this project's design, the loggia's finished floor level = the building stairwell/entry floor level **+1cm** — transferred from the stairwell to the loggia using a laser plane-builder, giving a concrete, checkable reference point independent of the loggia's own pre-existing (often inconsistent) floor level.
- **Fall-arrest harness anchor-point decision logic, explained in full**: the safety harness lanyard was tied off to a fixed point in the **ceiling** specifically, with the reasoning for rejecting every other option given explicitly — not the side monolithic concrete wall (inconvenient to reach/rig); not the gas-block wall (too soft/weak to trust); not the floor (would puncture the waterproofing membrane); not other walls (soft material or insulation behind them, unreliable). The ceiling was the only sufficiently solid, reliably anchorable point available on this specific site.
- **Добор-to-frame joint clamping rule**: use clamps (струбцины), never a hammer, when fitting добор profile to a window unit — avoids damaging either the добор or the unit itself.
- **Nagel (dowel-pin) добор-to-добор joint technique**: cut a notch/groove into one добор piece to receive the adjacent добор's own mounting "feet," then fasten with dowels at roughly 600mm spacing.
- **Erker (bay/oriel angled) connector technique**: a pipe/tube-shaped connector that joins glazing sections at variable angles, used specifically to follow the building facade's own existing multi-angle architecture rather than a simple straight run. **Alignment rule**: the connector's white side must face the unit's white side, and its colored/laminated side must face the unit's colored side. **Preserve the exact original angle pattern** for facade-architecture consistency — not an arbitrary angle choice. Fixed with dowels at ~600mm spacing, same as добор joints; erker adapters come with a **built-in gasket**, replacing the separate sealing-tape-plus-sealant step needed at other joint types.
- **Assembly-order rule, with explicit reasoning**: start assembling from the narrower, angled (turning) sections first, *not* the large front section — once the angled corners are set, the whole multi-section assembly is much easier to shift forward/back/left/right as a unit; starting with the large front section instead makes later repositioning far harder.
- **Intermediate-measurement discipline**: after joining the first two sections via the erker connector, deliberately leave the top temporarily loose and re-measure the straight section's dimensions before finalizing anything — an explicit checkpoint specifically to catch errors between assembly stages before they compound into a larger problem.
- **A second, distinct rigid connector type for straight-section joints (not angled), built for wind-load resistance**: three parts — a metal insert plate, an exterior-protruding plastic part, and an interior plastic part. **The exterior plastic part's top, and the metal insert, both need trimming** where they'd otherwise clash with an upper trim/casing box — a specific fitting detail easy to miss. The metal insert must be pinned/fixed so it can't shift during assembly. Unlike the erker adapter, this connector type still needs separate sealing tape and sealant, not a built-in gasket.
- **Anchor-plate spacing, this project's specific applied figure**: ≤600mm for this specific (colored/laminated) frame type — the maximum standard spacing rather than a tighter-than-code spacing, worth noting as one real applied data point alongside the "must be tighter than code" policy Zemskov states more generally in the companion video (for a different, uncolored frame project) — the two aren't necessarily contradictory (colored/laminated frames may have different structural allowances), but recorded as two distinct real spacing figures rather than blended into one rule. **Each individual anchor plate gets exactly 2 fixing points.**
- **Glazing-before-foaming sequencing rule, explicitly reversed from what might be assumed**: install the glass into fixed sections **before** foaming the perimeter joint, not after — Zemskov's/Konstantin's stated reason: on a large, oversized construction like this one, spray-foam expansion during curing can actually deform the frame if the glass isn't already in place to help resist that expansion force.
- **Glass-crack-prevention fitting technique**: always use rebate (фальцевые) inserts plus separate spacer/setting blocks under each glass unit, specifically so the glass never directly contacts internal metal connector hardware and cracks against it.
- **Glass-bead installation order, by rigidity — the reverse of what intuition might suggest**: install **short beads first** (they don't bend easily, so fit them while there's no flex to work with) and **long beads last** (they flex more, making them easier to spring into their final seated position after the short ones are already locked in).
- **Refined bead-hammering technique**: use a plastic/rubber-tipped dead-blow mallet, striking with a gliding/sliding motion along the glass rather than a direct hammer strike — protects both the plastic bead and the glass while simultaneously guiding the bead's own mounting foot into its correct seated position.
- **Real safety-glass specification for a floor-to-ceiling application**: the bottom section was specified in **triplex (laminated) safety glass**, explicitly to prevent a fall-through hazard at floor-to-ceiling height, and made from **frosted/matte glass**, explicitly for privacy (blocking a close outside view in) — a genuinely reusable spec combination for any floor-to-ceiling glazing application, not just this specific project.
- **Foam-joint prep detail**: dampen the joint surfaces before foaming, for better adhesion between the foam, the opening, and the window frame.
- **Insect-screen mounting lifehack**: install the screen's top fixing point on the **side**, not directly on top — prevents the screen being dislodged/blown off by wind.
- **Interior protective film — deliberately left on, contrasting with the exterior-film-removal rule in the companion video**: the frame's interior-facing protective film was left in place after installation, specifically because further loggia finishing work was still to come and the film protects the frame's surface from scuffing during that later work — a genuinely practical sequencing distinction (exterior film must come off before installation per the companion video; interior film can stay on until finishing work is fully done).
