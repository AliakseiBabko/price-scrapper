## Design Concept — Kitchen and Material Technique

1. **Same-material countertop-and-backsplash rationale**: the source
   explains that a tile backsplash will always look at least slightly
   different from a stone/composite countertop, however carefully
   matched — making both the countertop *and* the backsplash panel from
   the same material (rather than tile) gives a visually more coherent
   result. Framed as a general principle, not just this kitchen's own
   choice (tile backsplash-to-countertop is explicitly named as the
   inferior default).
2. **Push-to-open cabinet doors** (no handles, opens on a light push,
   closes by pushing back in) — a specific hardware choice noted in this
   kitchen.
3. **Deliberately non-built-in refrigerator as a visible design
   element**: rather than a panel-fronted, fully integrated fridge, this
   kitchen keeps an ordinary refrigerator with its own visible door,
   contrasted against the fully built-in range hood in the same run — an
   explicit design choice, not an oversight, worth noting against the
   more common all-built-in kitchen convention already documented
   elsewhere in this store.
4. **Quartz-vinyl flooring with a real wood-veneer top layer** — a
   distinct hybrid flooring product from engineered/parquet board: both
   use a real wood top layer, but engineered board's substrate is
   plywood, while this quartz-vinyl variant's substrate is not — a
   material-category distinction not previously recorded in this store's
   flooring content.

## Walls / Ceilings — Ceiling-Drop Mechanism Driven by MEP Routing

5. **Stretch-ceiling forced drop, driven by low-mounted risers and
   ventilation ducting, with a real client decision between two
   options**: hot/cold water riser shutoff valves are mounted
   unusually low, and the supply-ventilation duct is mounted even lower
   still — forcing the entire corridor-and-living-room ceiling to drop
   ~25cm to conceal them (a single continuous stretch-ceiling membrane
   can't span this large an area with an internal step, hence a full
   drop rather than a partial one). The foreman states the alternative
   — dropping *only* the corridor and creating a visible step/break at
   the living-room boundary, keeping the living room's ceiling higher —
   was offered to the client, who chose the full uniform drop instead. A
   concrete real example of a plumbing/HVAC-routing constraint forcing a
   ceiling-height tradeoff, with the actual client decision recorded.

## Rules / Heuristics — Sequencing, Serviceability, and Sensor Placement

6. **Delay installing finished fixtures specifically to avoid damage
   from a later trade**: a shower's built-in mixer valve top rain-head,
   hand-shower, and glass enclosure/door were deliberately left
   uninstalled during ceiling-finishing work, specifically so the
   ceiling crew wouldn't be obstructed by (or risk damaging) fixtures
   that were otherwise ready to mount — a general sequencing principle
   (delay install, don't just cover/protect in place) applicable beyond
   this one trade pairing.
7. **Centralize LED accent-lighting transformers on a single metal
   mounting plate**, both for heat dissipation and so any failed unit
   can be swapped without needing to access a hard-to-reach spot —
   extends this store's existing "LED transformer needs ventilated
   storage" rule (Round 5, `w6_e7nySEXI`) with a concrete implementation
   detail (a shared metal plate, chosen for both cooling and
   serviceability).
8. **Heated wall panel as an explicit alternative to a towel warmer**,
   controlled by its own dedicated thermostat — a bathroom wall is
   heated directly instead of installing a towel-warmer radiator, a real
   alternate technique choice not previously recorded in this store.
9. **Leak-detection sensor placement strategy**: wireless sensors are
   placed under individual fixtures (toilet, sink) at final finish
   stage; a single wired sensor included in the kit is instead
   positioned low, at floor level, specifically to catch and trigger a
   shutoff on any spill regardless of which fixture it came from — a
   concrete wired-vs-wireless sensor deployment strategy, extending this
   store's existing leak-protection-system content with a specific
   placement rationale.
