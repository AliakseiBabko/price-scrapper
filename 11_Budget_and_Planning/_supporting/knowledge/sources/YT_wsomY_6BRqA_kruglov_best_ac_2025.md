## HVAC — Indoor Unit Device Taxonomy for Split Systems (genuinely new)

- **A split system's indoor-unit role can be filled by four different device types**, all wired to
  the same outdoor unit: (1) standard **wall-mounted unit**; (2) **floor-ceiling/column unit**
  ("напольно-потолочный"/"колонный") — large freestanding column, mostly used in commercial spaces,
  large private houses, or assembly halls; (3) **cassette unit** — ceiling-mounted, blows in four
  directions simultaneously, common in restaurants/offices/concert halls; explicitly flagged as
  "the bane of every office worker" because a shared four-direction blow pattern in an open-plan
  space inevitably makes some occupants too cold and others too warm; (4) **ducted/channel unit**
  ("канальный") — concealed above the ceiling, feeding multiple rooms (e.g. three bedrooms) from
  one unit via ducting, with **individually-controllable electronic dampers per room** (manually
  toggled or smart-home-integrated, e.g. "open damper if temperature exceeds X"). Ducted units need
  ceiling depth, condensate routing, and access-panel/decorative-cover planning, and are described
  as most common in higher-end, high-ceiling renovations specifically because of this added
  design complexity. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_wsomY_6BRqA_kruglov_best_ac_2025|wsomY_6BRqA]]]

## HVAC — Multi-Split System: Hard Limit and Failure-Mode Downside (genuinely new)

- **⚠️ A multi-split system (multiple indoor units on one shared outdoor unit) has a hard limit of
  6 indoor units per outdoor unit.** Its key downside versus independent single split systems:
  **if the shared outdoor unit fails, every room it serves loses AC simultaneously** during repair,
  whereas with independent split systems (one outdoor unit each) a single failure only takes out
  one room. **Cost note**: a multi-split system serving 2 rooms can cost *more* than two fully
  independent split systems combined, despite serving the same room count — not always a cost-saving
  choice. The one scenario forcing multi-split regardless of this trade-off: when the building/
  facade only has physical space for one outdoor unit but AC is needed in more than one room.
  `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_wsomY_6BRqA_kruglov_best_ac_2025|wsomY_6BRqA]]]

## HVAC — Portable/Mobile AC: Quantified Ineffectiveness (genuinely new)

- **⚠️ A portable/mobile AC (no outdoor unit, hot air vented through a window duct/hose) is
  described as barely effective**: a small unit can take roughly **4 hours to drop room temperature
  by just 1°C**. Two compounding mechanisms cited: it's loud, and running it requires keeping a
  window cracked for the exhaust hose — but any open window already reduces any AC's efficiency,
  compounding the unit's already-weak output. The source frames real-world benefit as closer to
  a **"placebo effect,"** with the only meaningfully cool spot being directly in front of the unit's
  air outlet. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_wsomY_6BRqA_kruglov_best_ac_2025|wsomY_6BRqA]]]

## HVAC — Refrigerant Line Kink Threshold and Vibration-Resonance Mistake (genuinely new)

- **⚠️ A kink in the refrigerant line tubing that obstructs more than ~30% of the tube's flow
  cross-section will cause the unit to fail quickly.** A separate, less-obvious installer mistake
  (the source notes personally never having encountered it, but the company's own AC technicians
  have): **skipping vibration-isolation mounts/soundproofing when bolting the outdoor unit to a
  facade can cause the unit to resonate with the building's own wall panel**, producing an
  audible hum inside the apartment with an unclear-seeming cause unless the installer knows to
  check for missing vibration mounts specifically. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_wsomY_6BRqA_kruglov_best_ac_2025|wsomY_6BRqA]]]

## HVAC — Inverter vs. On/Off, Quantified Temperature Band (⚠️ nuances existing "inverter is the default" framing)

- **⚠️ Quantified temperature-holding comparison**: an inverter unit set to 22°C holds roughly

  default (per this store's existing FLATART-sourced claim), are described as more mechanically
  complex. **Practical recommendation: inverter for a bedroom (precise temperature holding matters
