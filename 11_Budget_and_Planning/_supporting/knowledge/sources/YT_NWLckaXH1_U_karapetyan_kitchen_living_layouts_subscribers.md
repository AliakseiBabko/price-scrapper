## Planning Rules — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Minimum area for a genuinely functional combined kitchen-living
  space with a full furniture set (kitchen + sofa + dining table) is
  ~25m²** — below that, plan to keep only 2 of the 3 (kitchen+sofa,
  kitchen+table, or sofa+table), not force all three into a smaller
  footprint.
- **⚠️ "Paper plan" furniture-size illusion caution**: a floor plan
  schematic routinely looks more spacious than the real furniture will
  be — a "normal" comfortable sofa needs roughly 1.0-1.1m of seat
  depth/width, and plans frequently show unrealistically compact
  furniture footprints. **Go select the actual sofa/table model you
  want and check its real dimensions against the plan directly**,
  rather than trusting a schematic block.
- **⚠️ Russian replanning-approval rule, kitchen expansion into a
  declared living room**: expanding a kitchen's footprint by absorbing
  space from an adjacent habitable ("жилая") room is not permitted
  under standard Russian replanning rules. **Genuine bureaucratic
  nuance**: if the kitchen niche itself keeps its original footprint
  and the additional absorbed space is instead declared as a
  non-habitable corridor/hall, the outcome depends on the discretionary
  judgment of the specific permitting-office specialist reviewing the
  case — not a fixed universal rule, so "this plan won't be approved"
  isn't always literally true, but is the base-case expectation.
- **⚠️ Russian replanning-approval rule, wet-zone expansion into a
  living space**: expanding a bathroom/WC (a "wet"/non-habitable zone)
  into an adjacent habitable kitchen-living space is essentially never
  approved — **except** for a ground-floor apartment, or one with no
  unit directly below it (i.e. no downstairs neighbor whose ceiling
  would sit under the expanded wet zone) — a floor-position-dependent
  code exception.
- **Minimizing internal partitions is generally the right response to
  an over-subdivided layout** — consolidate excess small enclosed
  spaces into one genuinely open combined zone, then use lightweight,
  reversible dividers (a curtain, a light partition) for occasional
  privacy needs instead of permanent interior walls.
- **⚠️ Flexible-partition guest-hosting design, for households with
  periodic overnight guests (weekly/monthly relatives)**: rather than
  keeping an awkward permanent galley-kitchen/corridor layout just to
  preserve a separate guest room, plan a genuinely open kitchen-living
  space with a lightweight closable divider (curtain or light
  partition) so the space can be isolated as sleeping quarters when
  guests actually stay, and reopened as normal living space the rest of
  the time.
- **⚠️ An isolated, visually-disconnected galley kitchen (no sightline
  to the living area) is flagged as a dated, less resale-marketable
  layout** compared to a combined kitchen-living plan — a resale-value
  framing, not purely an aesthetic preference.

## Kitchen-Living Combination Zoning — Durable Facts & Rules

- **⚠️ Avoid placing a refrigerator as a freestanding "column" at the
  dividing line between kitchen and living zones in a small combined
  space** — creates a maze-like, zigzagging circulation path around it.
  **Preferred alternative**: place the fridge in a hallway/corridor
  built-in cabinet accessible from the living side — itself imperfect,
  but still judged clearly better than a mid-room fridge column.
- **A kitchen built without upper wall cabinets or full side panels
  (a low, dresser-height/"обрезанная" kitchen run) lets more light and
  visual air into a small combined kitchen-living space**, both
  visually and physically enlarging the perceived room size, at the
  cost of losing that upper storage capacity.
- **"Cook facing the room, not facing the wall" kitchen-orientation
  trend, explicit social framing**: positioning the cook to face the
  combined room (rather than with their back to guests/family) is
  framed as reflecting a shift in household roles — the cook
  participates as an equal family member during meal prep rather than
  being isolated as a separate household function. Recorded as this
  practitioner's own stated design-philosophy framing, not a neutral
  ergonomic fact.
- **A protruding structural pylon/beam dividing a kitchen from a living
  zone in an already-small combined room reduces furniture-layout
  flexibility disproportionately** — flagged as a negative specifically
  because it removes options (where a sofa/table/rug could otherwise
  go), not purely for its visual appearance.
- **A dining table and a bar counter with stools in the same small
  zone are functionally redundant** — both serve the same eating
  function; keep one, not both, in a space too small to need the
  duplication.

## Furniture / Built-ins — Durable Facts & Rules

- **⚠️ Triangular-shaped built-in wardrobes consume disproportionate
  floor area for the storage they return**, and when adjacent to a
  shared kitchen-living space, distort the room's overall usable shape
  without a matching functional benefit. **Fix**: either remove the
  separating partition entirely (converting to a simple L-shaped open
  closet with no loss of storage function) or reshape the wardrobe into
  a proper rectangle with 3-sided (П-образный) shelving access — both
  recover usable combined-room floor area without reducing actual
  storage capacity.
