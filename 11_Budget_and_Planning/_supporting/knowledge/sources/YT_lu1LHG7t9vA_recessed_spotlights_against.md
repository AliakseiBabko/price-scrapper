## Mistakes / Warnings — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Argument 1 — constant bulb burnout, demonstrated with real data**: in the source's own rented apartment, every bulb was replaced roughly 1.5 years prior; within ~3 months, most had failed again. Over the full ~1.5-year period, the speaker estimates having replaced essentially every bulb multiple times. At time of filming: a group of spotlights (fully replaced ~2 months earlier) already had 5 out; another zone had 2 of 9 out; another 3 of 12; and in the entry hallway, one group had only 1 of several lit and a separate group of 3 was completely dead — demonstrated live by testing each switch individually to rule out a wiring fault. **Stated mechanism**: a recessed spotlight bulb is an ordinary bulb and fails on the same underlying schedule as any bulb (roughly every few months per the source's own experience) — the only difference is that with 40 installed, the *aggregate* failure rate is 40× a single fixture's. `confirmed`, extensively demonstrated with real counts, though acknowledged by the source as not universal (some viewers report longer-lived LED bulbs).
- **Argument 2 — uneven "cone" light pattern damages the perceived wall finish**: on textured/patterned wallpaper, the narrow light cones from spotlights create visibly uneven illumination that reads as a bumpy/uneven ("wavy") wall surface even when the wall itself is flat — worse on plain or subtly-patterned wallpaper than on heavily-patterned wallpaper (where the pattern already masks unevenness). `confirmed`, mechanism-specific.
- **Argument 3 — harsh, uncomfortable light when lying down/reclined**: lying in a bathtub or a reclined massage chair under spotlights (especially halogen) produces glare directly in the eyes; the source describes routinely getting up mid-relaxation specifically to walk over and turn the spotlights off because the light is intolerable even with eyes closed (bright spots still visible through closed eyelids). `confirmed`, real described behavior pattern.
- **Argument 4 — poor actual room illumination despite visual brightness**: spotlights direct a narrow beam straight down, typically onto a dark floor that reflects little light back into the room; walls receive only the edge of the light cone, so the room's ambient/fill light stays weak even with many fixtures lit, producing a persistent feeling of gloominess that drives occupants to keep adding more light sources (more spotlights, a chandelier, a table lamp) without actually resolving the underlying problem. `confirmed`, mechanism-specific, distinct from and complementary to arguments 2-3.
- **Argument 5 — higher total cost than a single premium fixture**: each spotlight needs its own wiring run; most are 12V and require a step-down power supply/transformer (itself an added cost and a second failure point) in addition to the mains-voltage circuit; installation drilling must avoid structural ceiling elements; ongoing maintenance costs accrue since both bulbs and the power supply itself can fail. Stated comparison: the total cost of an 18-spotlight installation shown in the video is described as several times more expensive than a single high-end, "premium" chandelier that would provide brighter, more even, lower-maintenance light in the same space. `confirmed` as the source's own cost comparison; specific total figures not given for either option.
- **Bonus/injury note**: spotlight glass diffuser lenses, after extended operation, can heat up enough that their retaining mechanism fails and the lens detaches and falls, shattering on the floor — a real incident is cited (the source recorded and sent proof-of-innocence footage to the apartment's owner after a lens fell and broke on its own). `confirmed`, real cited incident, a physical/injury-risk consideration distinct from the five main arguments.
