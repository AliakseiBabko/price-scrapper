## Design Concept / Lighting — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Give the dining-table zone a decorative accent fixture as a focal
  point**, distinct from functional lighting — a striking pendant/linear/
  round decorative form over the table draws attention and anchors the
  combined space's main "gathering point," regardless of the room's
  specific layout or ceiling height.
- **Functional light must be planned separately from decorative
  fixtures, with a stated target level**: **~200-300 lux** for genuine
  task use (reading, moving through the space comfortably) — many
  projects fail specifically because they rely on old-style pendant/
  chandelier fixtures alone, which don't deliver enough functional light
  on their own. Combine decorative fixtures with a dedicated functional
  system (a track/profile-mounted spot system on stretch ceiling or
  drywall, adjustable in fixture count/position).
- **Choose diffused vs. directional functional light deliberately, not
  by default**: diffused light (opal-diffuser fixtures/profiles) produces
  fewer/softer shadows; directional light (aimed spotlights on a track)
  produces more pronounced shadows and a more dramatic look — genuine
  personal preference either way; view both in a showroom before
  deciding.
- **Cove/perimeter backlighting ("закарнизная подсветка") is a distinct
  third light layer, not a substitute for the other two** — an LED strip
  recessed into a perimeter niche/cornice, workable under any ceiling
  type. Stated effect: visually expands the perceived size of the room
  and adds ambient warmth — the source states this is the single most
  universally well-received technique across every client project to
  date. **This corroborates the "primary / accent / decorative" 3-layer
  lighting structure already recorded in this store from
  `YT_TnYExlfWw9c`** (Diskill/Nadia Karmin) — an independent specialist
  channel converging on the same layered structure, though using
  different names for the layers (decorative-accent, functional, and
  perimeter-backlight vs. that source's primary/accent/decorative).
- **Centralized lighting-control systems are more affordable/simpler now
  than a few years ago** — a named control-app category (referred to as
  "Casambi"-style Bluetooth mesh control blocks wired per fixture or
  fixture group) lets scenes be pre-programmed (e.g. one button toggles
  both the decorative accent and the cove backlight together, a separate
  button/scene for functional light alone) — framed as a genuinely usable
  consumer-accessible option, not a premium-only luxury anymore.
- **Plan the lighting layout at least 1-2 months before installation, not
  day-of** — print the floor plan, mark fixture positions and types by
  hand, build a fixture specification table, and only then purchase.
  Stated failure mode: electricians/installers show up expecting a
  finished plan, and a same-day decision on lighting one will live with
  for ~10 years is a real, common planning mistake.
