## Planning Rules — Deliberately Shrinking the Bedroom to Enlarge the Walk-In Closet

- **The bedroom was intentionally made smaller than the developer's
  original layout, specifically to enlarge the adjoining walk-in
  closet** — stated reasoning: "a bedroom just needs to let you come sleep,
  that's it," i.e. the practitioner explicitly deprioritizes bedroom floor
  area relative to closet/storage capacity once the room's core sleep
  function is met. A concrete real-world data point on the
  bedroom-vs-closet floor-area tradeoff this project's own Small Bedroom
  page should weigh alongside the 12-item sequence's furniture-driven
  approach above (that sequence assumes the room's given footprint, not a
  choice of *how much* footprint the bedroom itself gets).
- **U-shaped ("П-образная") walk-in closet clearance formula, stated
  directly**: allow **~60cm for the central walkway**, plus **~60cm on
  each side** for the storage runs — the real closet built this way came
  out to 1.8m × 1.8m. A distinct, more specific formula than this page's
  existing walk-in-closet mirror-clearance note (that one assumes an
  internal mirror; this is the base U-shape clearance rule without one).
- **Furniture-panel (мебельные щиты) shelving chosen over metal shelving
  for the closet interior** — two reasons given: better appearance, and
  comparable cost to metal — a real practitioner cost/appearance
  comparison, not an assumption.
- **Closet doorway solution: a pocket/rolling door built into the drywall
  partition itself** (with a soft-close damper), chosen over a glass
  sliding-partition system quoted at ≈120,000 RUB — the drywall pocket-door
  build-out was substantially cheaper and, in the practitioner's own words,
  "you close it and nothing is visible" (the door disappears into the
  wall). A real cost-driven alternative to a glass-partition system for a
  closet doorway specifically (distinct from this page's Phase 2 bedroom-
  divider discussion, which is about a much larger room-dividing
  partition, not a closet door).

## Mistakes / Warnings — Soundproofing & Fixture Placement Near a Sleeping Child

- **⚠️ Door-sill soundproofing gasket/threshold, a technique not
  previously on this store's soundproofing content**: installed at both
  the bedroom and bathroom doors specifically to reduce sound transfer
  while the toddler sleeps. The practitioner directly tested it (played
  music, closed the door) and confirmed a real, noticeable sound-isolation
  improvement. **⚠️ Explicit tradeoff acknowledged**: the same seal that
  blocks sound also chokes off passive ventilation under the door — his
  own workaround is to prop the door slightly open, or rely on the room's
  own AC/ventilation instead of passive under-door airflow. Cross-reference
  [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Soundproofing|Soundproofing]] — a
  door-perimeter product, distinct from that page's existing wall/floor/
  ceiling techniques, worth adding there in a future round.
- **Avoided wall-mounted bedside fixtures specifically because the
  bedroom wall has frameless soundproofing installed and he didn't want to
  drill into it** — chose floor-standing nightstands instead of
  wall-mounted ones for this reason (a toddler "climbing like a monkey"
  everywhere was the stated secondary reason). A concrete real-world
  instance of this store's general "don't penetrate a soundproofing
  assembly" principle (previously recorded for screed beacons in
  [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Soundproofing|Soundproofing]]) now
  extended to bedside wall-fixture mounting hardware.
- **Baseboard/plinth installed only after furniture placement** — same
  sequencing rationale as this batch's other TimRemont source
  (`gWAOrislxFY`): avoids visible gaps around furniture legs/edges.
  `corroborates_existing`.
- **A crib/nursery bed occupies open floor space in the bedroom instead of
  a vanity/makeup table** — this practitioner's own household explicitly
  has no vanity table in the bedroom at all, one more real data point
  (this time in the direction of the original Kruglov mistakes-video
  caution, not the counter-example in `gWAOrislxFY`).

## Living & Dining Room — Relevant Zoning Content (checked per this batch's instructions)

This video's 14m² combined kitchen-living room is genuinely relevant to
`04_Living_and_Dining_Room` as well as Small Bedroom:

- **A fold-out ("accordion"-mechanism) sofa in the combined kitchen-living
  room deliberately replaces a conventional bed as the household's spare/
  guest sleeping surface** — stated reasoning: the room is narrow enough
  that a fixed bed would leave no floor clearance for a toddler to play,
  whereas the sofa folds away in one motion, restoring floor space by day.
  Same "aнатомический" (anatomical/orthopedic) mattress spec as a regular
  bed, not a cheap sofa-bed pad — a real functional substitute for a bed in
  a space-constrained combined room, worth a Living/Dining "Design &
  Zoning" page once that room accumulates enough sources (per this
  vault's per-room-integration threshold rule).
- **Continuous flooring material across the entryway, kitchen, and living
  zone** (quartz-vinyl, single contour) reinforced with matching wallpaper
  in the entryway and kitchen-living zone specifically — a visual-
  continuity technique for an open-plan space, distinct from (but
  compatible with) this store's existing flooring-transition-threshold
  notes.
- **Cooktop placed directly adjacent to the sofa** in this specific real
  layout, due to space constraints — the practitioner flags this himself
  as a real compromise (risk of oil/splatter reaching the sofa) and plans
  a small glass splash-guard partition as a fix — a named real-world
  layout compromise worth recording as a caution for a similarly tight
  combined kitchen-living space, not a recommended pattern.
