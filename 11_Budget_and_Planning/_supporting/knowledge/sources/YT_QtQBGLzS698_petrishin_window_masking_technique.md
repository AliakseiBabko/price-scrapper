## Rules / Heuristics — Masking Technique

1. **Materials and a specific film-width preference**: heavy-duty
   plastic film, tape, scissors, and a putty knife/spatula. Film width
   preference: **~1.5m economy-grade film**, not a narrower ~40cm roll —
   no need for a premium brand.
2. **Overlap-margin technique**: leave ~2cm of film unattached at each
   edge, specifically reserved as the bonding surface for the *next*
   strip's tape — lets consecutive film sections be joined cleanly
   rather than each being taped independently.
3. **A deliberate ~1mm gap at the top edge (against the wall/ceiling
   line)**: left open on purpose so the tape sealing that edge doesn't
   physically interfere with a later puttying/filling pass right at that
   junction.
4. **Static-cling assist and a tape-type nuance**: the film clings
   electrostatically to the glass/frame on its own, making positioning
   easier before final taping; **ordinary clear tape ("обычный скотч"),
   not blue painter's/masking tape, is used to seal the edges here** —
   worth flagging as a possible nuance against this project's existing
   Demolition.md rule to avoid "ordinary masking/painter's tape" for
   window protection (that rule concerns adhesive-residue risk from
   painter's/masking tape specifically; clear packaging tape is a
   different product, so this is not treated as a contradiction, but
   the distinction is worth keeping explicit rather than silently
   assumed).
5. **Trim technique to avoid scratching the window**: cut excess film
   flush with a putty knife/spatula held flat and pressed firmly against
   the frame/wall (not held at an angle), specifically to avoid
   scratching the glass or frame while trimming.
6. **Operable-sash masking, ventilation-compatible**: mask the movable
   sash as a separate flap along its own perimeter (rather than sealing
   the whole window as one fixed unit) so the window can still be opened
   for ventilation during the covering period — open the sash, apply
   film/tape cut to the sash's own size along its edge, then close and
   finish sealing the rest of the frame normally.
7. **Windowsill protection recommended too**, since tools/materials can
   be dropped on it during the dusty work stage.
8. **⚠️ Dark-frame-specific caution**: dark-colored window frames need
   extra care during masking/unmasking, since scratches show up far more
   visibly on a dark surface than a light one.

## Numeric Data — Real Time-Tradeoff Benchmark

- **A real, concrete time-cost argument for doing this prep step at all**:
  fully masking one window with this method takes about **5 minutes**;
  skipping it and later removing paint splatter/dust residue from an
  unprotected window instead costs **15-20 minutes** per window —
  roughly a 3-4x time cost for skipping the 5-minute prep step. Do this
  masking at the *start* of the dusty/painting work stage, not after.
