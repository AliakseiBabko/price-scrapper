## Durable Facts

- **Never accept a monotone/dark solid-color kitchen countertop imposed by a kitchen retailer against the client's preference** — real client case: a dark, uniform countertop shows every water spot/streak and is described as high-maintenance; the practitioner states it will be replaced. **Practical rule extending this store's existing flooring-pattern-hides-dirt logic to countertops**: prefer a patterned/speckled countertop finish, which visually hides spots/dust the way a patterned floor does. `confirmed` as stated design rationale, real case cited.
- **Heavy/visually-dense kitchen modules (an appliance column combining microwave and built-in oven) are placed deliberately on the side of a kitchen away from an intentionally unified, visually "light" countertop/backsplash run** — a compositional balance technique: put the visual weight in one zone so the rest of the run reads clean and light. `single-account`, design-composition heuristic.
- **Microwave-over-oven appliance column with lift-up (not hinged-down) cabinet doors above it**, specifically to avoid head-bumping when the upper cabinet doors are opened at that height. Concrete ergonomic mechanism, genuinely new for this store's appliance-column content.
- **Two separate, smaller (~260 L-class) refrigerators instead of one large unit** — stated as the more economical choice (implying lower unit cost than an equivalent-capacity single large fridge) and offering two operational advantages: food-category separation between the two units, and the ability to power off one unit entirely (e.g. while traveling, with little food in it) to save electricity — not possible with a single fridge holding everything. **Genuinely new appliance-provisioning heuristic** for this store.
- **Patterned/speckled quartz-vinyl flooring preferred over a uniform color for practicality** — corroborates and extends this store's existing flooring-pattern-hides-dirt principle: pattern/contrast conceals dust and footprints that a plain color reveals every mark of. Near-zero thermal/humidity expansion is cited as allowing continuous (single-loop, "единым контуром") laying with only millimeter-scale expansion gaps, without buckling risk — a specific material-property claim (distinct from, and more favorable than, this store's existing laminate-under-underfloor-heating 50% failure-risk heuristic from `yt_nFWxi3zbsNo`, which quartz-vinyl was already noted there to only partially mitigate).
- **Low-touch client-workflow model, worked example**: over roughly 3.5 months, the client made only a handful of site visits (roughly every 1-2 months) plus periodic phone check-ins with the foreman; the client's own time was spent selecting finish materials/colors/furniture rather than supervising execution. Presented as the intended, and (per the client's own on-camera statement) preferred, division of labor for a turnkey engagement. `single-account`, workflow description rather than a checkable technical rule — relevant context for comparing turnkey vs. self-managed time investment.
- **Cove/indirect ceiling lighting via a stretch-ceiling-integrated LED profile** (a shaped stretch-ceiling edge profile with a translucent insert concealing an LED strip) is presented as the practitioner's now-preferred alternative to an open dust-collecting light-strip channel/box, which he states he stopped recommending. The fixture is RGB/color-tunable, dimmable, and can run simple lighting effects (color cycling, etc.). **Cited cost: ≈30,000 RUB added to the ceiling cost for one room** — framed by the practitioner as trivial relative to total project cost. Genuinely new lighting-technique + price point for this store (distinct from this store's existing perimeter-LED-strip-in-baguette-channel content, which was already flagged as decorative/supplementary-only — this is a different, dust-sealed implementation of a similar idea).
- **Remote-control entrance lock trade-offs, discussed candidly**: no ordinary lock (electronic or mechanical) resists a determined, skilled break-in attempt for more than about 30 seconds except a **concealed lock**, which can only be defeated by mechanically grinding/cutting it out — slow and loud, a real deterrent. The practitioner's real prior-apartment experience (lock defeated via cylinder removal in ~10 minutes) is cited as the motivating case. **Stated drawback of remote/electronic locks**: batteries must be tracked and changed; many modern electronic locks fail open (auto-unlock) when the battery becomes critically low, as a stated safety default — meaning a depleted battery is itself a security exposure, not just an inconvenience. Corroborates and extends this store's existing "buy a plain door + separate smart lock" content with a distinct security/reliability trade-off analysis (lock-type security ranking + low-battery fail-open behavior).
- **Wardrobe/cabinet-box installer marking protocol to protect concealed wiring during later stretch-ceiling install**: after fixing a wardrobe/cabinet box before the stretch ceiling is installed, the installer must (1) photograph the installed box, (2) have the client photograph it on their own phone as independent evidence, and (3) attach a physical note to the box itself stating the exact distance from its underside to the concealed cable run, plus a maximum safe fastener length (35-40 mm) — so a later stretch-ceiling crew, who cannot see behind the finished box, doesn't drive a longer screw through a hidden cable. **Genuinely new, concrete cross-trade coordination/documentation protocol** for this store.
- **Guest/second WC provisioned deliberately as a real design decision, not a leftover space**: client explicitly requested two toilets in the apartment because "everyone's morning biorhythm aligns," creating a real bottleneck with only one — an ex-storage-closet space was converted to a small second WC. **Compact-footprint technical-design worked example**: even with the door left fully open, the space still comfortably fits a stacked washer/dryer, a toilet, sink use, and hygiene-shower use without clearance conflicts — fitting a toilet, ~50 cm-deep backlit sink, hygiene shower, toilet-paper holder, and an electric (temperature-adjustable) heated towel rail all into one small room via careful "technical design" measurement. Extends this store's existing technical-design-sizing content with a real dense-small-room worked case.
- **Tile-finish selection heuristic (bathroom)**: choose a tile surface that hides water/soap spots but still shows visible toothpaste splatter — i.e., not so glossy that every water droplet shows, but not so matte that grime disappears into the texture either; a specific, checkable practical selection criterion distinct from this store's existing tile-*layout* rules (which concern cut-piece sizing, not finish selection). Genuinely new tile-selection heuristic.
- **Underfloor-heating thermostat mounted outside the room it controls** — corroborates this store's existing `yt_uwXBHuWPUIo` visibility-first placement rule, with a restated rationale: a resident passing by in the hallway sees the lit display and can turn it off if it's running unnecessarily (e.g. overnight when not needed), without having to enter the room.
- **Hygienic-shower/bidet-sprayer plumbing without a visible wall shutoff valve, worked mechanism**: the sprayer head itself has no reliable shutoff hardware, so if left under constant line pressure with no valve anywhere, it will eventually burst/leak (a flooding risk); the practitioner's solution routes shutoff through **the sprayer's wall-mounted holder**, which contains a button that closes a "second circuit" cutting off flow when the sprayer is docked, plus a pre-set in-wall thermostatic mixing valve that blends hot/cold to a client-chosen fixed output temperature so no separate temperature-mixing control is needed at the sprayer itself. Stated trade-off: the holder's shutoff button makes docking/undocking the sprayer slightly less convenient than a plain holder, in exchange for eliminating the constant-pressure failure risk. **Genuinely new plumbing-mechanism detail** — distinct from, and more technical than, this store's existing hygienic-shower-handedness placement fact.
- **Careful bathroom fixture-clearance planning from known dimensions**: plan tub/sink/toilet clearances in advance from the actual selected fixtures' real dimensions (not generic assumptions) so a client can comfortably reach faucets from within the tub and sit comfortably on the toilet — corroborates this store's existing "technical design" measured-clearance-planning pattern, no new mechanism.
- **Joining a loggia increases the room's total heat-loss surface area versus an unjoined loggia (simple block only)**, so the resulting combined room runs somewhat cooler than if the loggia had not been joined — the practitioner states this is fully compensated by normal underfloor heating and the building's heating system, not a reason to avoid joining. **Extends** this store's existing loggia-join heat-loss content with an explicit "still fully compensable via normal heating" reassurance, relevant to this project's own loggia-join deliberation.
- **Renovation-budget spending-sequencing rule**: when budget is tight, fully fund and complete "fundamental"/concealed-systems work first — wiring, plumbing, tile, windows — because redoing any of these later (e.g. re-opening finished tile/reveals to replace windows or plumbing) is far more costly than doing it right the first time; defer non-structural, purchasable-later items instead (stretch ceilings can be added weeks/months after move-in, door casings and the doors themselves can wait until last, a kitchen can be delayed up to roughly six months). Framed as a normal, non-shameful budget-stretching strategy, not a sign of a failed project. **Genuinely new temporal-deferral prioritization heuristic**, distinct from this store's existing "don't cut cost on rough/concealed systems" heuristic (`yt_6Z7uH2_rXsw`) — this source adds the *sequencing/timing* dimension (what can wait, and roughly how long) rather than just what not to cheap out on.
- **Supply/fresh-air ventilation ("приточная вентиляция") is explicitly distinct from air conditioning** — flagged by the practitioner as a common client misunderstanding, with a dedicated future video promised; no technical mechanism content recoverable from this source beyond the flagged distinction itself.
