## Furniture / Built-ins — Upper Kitchen Module Assembly Mistakes, Genuinely New Content

- **Never cut factory box packaging open with a utility knife** — even careful cutting reliably scratches the module surface underneath; additionally, the cardboard itself is needed later in the assembly process (as a floor-protection surface), so cutting it up defeats a second purpose.
- **Never assemble modules directly on bare finished flooring** — reliably damages the floor covering and chips module corners; always lay out the retained packaging cardboard first and assemble on top of it.
- **Unpack and inspect every module for shipping damage *before* starting assembly on any of them** — a common mistake is to unpack-and-build one module at a time, only to later discover damage on a module that's already been through partial assembly; once assembly has begun, no damage claim against the manufacturer/shipper is possible. Photograph or video any damage found before starting assembly and contact the manufacturer immediately, not after work has started.
- **Sort all parts into per-module groups before starting, never work from one shared pile** — pulling parts from a mixed pile reliably causes at least two modules to be misassembled, discovered only once install is attempted, requiring full disassembly and rebuild to fix.
- **Confirmat-screw driver torque, restated with the specific failure modes for each direction**: too low a torque setting leaves modules loose/out of shape; too high strips the confirmat thread out of the particleboard, at which point the affected panel must be replaced entirely.
- **Use only the drawer-runner screws supplied with the runner hardware, never generic self-tapping screws** — the supplied screws are length-matched specifically to secure the runner without penetrating through the module; a generic substitute is very likely wrong-length either way (too short: runner doesn't hold; too long: penetrates the module and can mar the flooring surface it's resting on).
- **Range-hood duct cutout: oversize the hole by a minimum of 10mm over the corrugated duct's own diameter, not an exact match** — an exact-diameter hole makes the flexible corrugated tube difficult to feed through and reliably deforms it during installation; this is a specific numeric complement to `yt_19AUqOlliys`'s duct-cutting technique (that source covers the cutting sequence for a *rigid* duct hole; this source's oversizing rule is specifically for a flexible/corrugated duct fitted through the same kind of cutout).
- **Never fasten a module's back panel with self-tapping screws — use only staples or the supplied nails.** Explicit failure mode: screws split the module's side-panel material at the fastening point, forcing a full side-panel replacement and halting installation for weeks while a custom replacement part is manufactured.
- **Back-panel fastening spacing, explicit numeric rule**: nail/staple the *entire* perimeter of the back panel, spaced no more than **100mm apart** — not just the four corners (a commonly-seen shortcut). Mechanism: the back panel is what defines and locks in the module's overall squareness/rigidity; under-fastening it lets the module rack out of true over time.
- **Eurorail (евронавес) hook cutout on the back panel must be sized correctly, both failure directions explained**: too small and the hanger's hook simply won't fit into the opening; too large and the resulting gap around the hanger can't be concealed by its decorative cover cap.
- **Never skip the mounting strip (монтажная планка)** — explicitly flagged as a component even experienced installers sometimes dismiss as "unnecessary" and set aside. Its actual function: ties the two side panels together and locks the back panel in place; omitting it lets the back panel flex during normal use (particularly wall-mounted-module vibration from nearby activity) and eventually pop out of its retaining grooves — requiring the module to be pulled down, disassembled, the back panel and mounting strip properly reinstalled, then reassembled and rehung to fix.
- **Wire/cable pass-through hole placement for under-cabinet task lighting or a motion sensor**: drill the cable hole on the same vertical line as the sensor/switch/light fixture itself, positioned as close to the module's bottom edge as practical — keeps the bulk of the cable run tucked behind the back panel rather than needing a long, difficult router channel across the module's interior to reach a misaligned hole.
