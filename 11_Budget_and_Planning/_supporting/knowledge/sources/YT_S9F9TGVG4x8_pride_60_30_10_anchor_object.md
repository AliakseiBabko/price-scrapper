## Design Concept — Colour Hierarchy

- **⚠️ 60/30/10 stated as the single rule the video is built on — the third independent restatement now in this vault** (alongside Anuta Vlady's 60:30:10 in `LRtedmEn8mQ`, the Kruglov/Ontario 60-30-10 already on `Color_Palette_and_Material_Direction.md`, the curtains-batch sources, and ГК Авальремонт in this same batch). The framing added here is worth recording even though the ratio itself is not new: **"все цвета в комнате не должны быть равноправными — у них должна быть иерархия."** 60% = dominant background (walls, large surfaces, and explicitly *sometimes* a large sofa, rug, curtains, or part of a kitchen if it is open to the living room) and should be calm and non-tiring; 30% = support adding depth and contrast (wood in furniture, textiles, an armchair, an accent wall, a bed headboard); 10% = the accent that stops the eye (cushions, decor, a vase, a throw, frames, an art object).
- **⚠️ The rule applies inside a monochrome scheme too**, via saturation and depth rather than hue: light grey walls = 60%, mid-grey headboard/curtains/bedspread = 30%, graphite cushions and a dark throw = 10%. This is a direct answer to the monochrome-is-bad position taken by Artcolor.Online in this same batch.
- **⚠️ Neutrals don't count against the three-role budget — the answer to "three colours isn't enough."** White, black, grey, cream and natural wood "не всегда нами воспринимаются именно как отдельные активные цвета," so a room can carry a warm light background, a wood secondary, an olive accent, black lines, a white ceiling and grey textiles and still be a correct three-role scheme, because **the active colour hierarchy is still three components.** A genuinely useful clarification that this vault's existing 60/30/10 entries do not have.

## Design Concept — Where to Start

- **⚠️ The "якорный предмет" (anchor object) method — the strongest item in this source.** "Профессионалы почти никогда не начинают с выбора цвета стен. Они начинают именно с якорного предмета." Defined as **an object that already contains a beautiful combination of several colours** — most often a rug, a decorative cushion, patterned textile, a favourite armchair, or a painting you strongly like. The palette is then derived from it. Stated diagnosis of why Pinterest copying fails: people pick the wall colour first, "примерно как вы выбираете обувь ещё до момента, когда вы решили, куда вы по итогу пойдёте." **This independently corroborates the "прототип" method already recorded from Anuta Vlady (`LRtedmEn8mQ`) and the derive-colour-from-a-fixed-material technique on `Whole_Apartment_Coherence.md` — a third, unrelated channel arriving at the same starting move.**
- **⚠️ Retrofit application — the rule works without repainting.** If the renovation is already done, the existing walls simply *are* the 60%; assemble the 30% through textiles, furniture and wood, and the 10% through decor, lighting, cushions and throws. "Иногда в целом достаточно поменять просто несколько наволочек, добавить какой-нибудь один плед, заменить картину." A low-cost, checkable intervention path.
- **Self-training exercise offered**: open any saved interior photo and decompose it mentally by the formula to see how often it is actually being applied.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Four Named Failure Modes

- **Even distribution (50/50)** — "интерьер начинает просто спорить сам с собой," the eye can't tell what is primary and the room reads pretty but not whole.
- **Accent grown past its share** — at 10% it enlivens; **at roughly 20–30% it stops being an accent and starts competing with the base**, and the space reads unsettled. A usefully specific numeric threshold for a failure that other sources describe only qualitatively.
- **Ignoring lighting** — the same shade differs by orientation, time of day, lamp type and surrounding materials. Concrete instance given: **grey drifts bluish under cool light and toward beige or even a pinkish undertone under warm light.**
- **⚠️ Treating a materials/proportion problem as a colour problem** — "иногда люди думают, что интерьер немножечко скучноватый, и начинают срочно добавлять цветные вещи. Но на деле комнате может просто не хватать глубины, фактур, света, или более выразительных форм." A genuinely useful diagnostic caution: check depth, texture, light and form before reaching for colour.
- **Closing action plan**: pick one room (the one you spend most time in), find the anchor object, name three colours in the roles, then audit what you already have — often the walls and furniture already are the 60% and 30% and only the 10% is missing.
