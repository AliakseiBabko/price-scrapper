## Planning Rules / Design Process — Durable Facts & Rules

- **⚠️ Pinterest single-reference-image technique**: create a fresh,
  history-free account (so the recommendation algorithm isn't polluted
  by unrelated prior searches), follow a small number (4-6) of curated
  design-media accounts/boards, build one folder per room, and
  critically — **converge on exactly one reference photo per room and
  match it closely, rather than combining elements pulled from several
  different photos**. Stated mechanism: a non-professional can't
  reliably predict how elements from different sources will actually
  combine once assembled — picking one coherent "template" per room and
  sticking to it is described as reliably delivering "80% of the result
  for 20% of the effort" compared to freeform mixing.
- **"Live in it before you design it" exposure technique**: for someone
  with no design vocabulary/visual literacy yet, spend some off-days
  staying in different well-designed hotels/apartments (mid-to-premium
  tier) before starting a Pinterest search — builds a base of concrete
  reference details (fixtures, storage solutions) that's hard to
  originate from zero.
- **Target-demographic design technique for an undecided owner**: when
  someone genuinely has no personal aesthetic preference, treat the
  property as an asset and design for the likely renter/buyer
  demographic of that specific neighborhood instead of a personal
  preference that doesn't exist yet.
- **⚠️ Resale/rental liquidity vs. finish ornateness, an inverse
  relationship claim**: the more expensive/ornate a finish level (and
  higher price/m²), the *slower* it typically rents or resells, not
  faster — stated mechanism: people moving into a space generally prefer
  a neutral, adaptable backdrop they can furnish with their own life,
  not a heavily personalized/decorative interior they'd have to
  visually "fit into." Practical implication: a genuinely ornate,
  highly personalized renovation should be a deliberate tradeoff against
  resale/rental speed, not treated as a pure upgrade.
- **⚠️ Technical-architect-only cost ratio, stated approximate figure**:
  hiring a technical architect just to produce layout-change drawings
  (no full interior design service) costs roughly **1/10th** of a full
  interior-design service price — worth doing even when skipping a full
  designer, since proper drawings materially ease contractor
  communication (a clear scope-of-work reduces disputes, corroborating
  this store's existing written-scope-of-work practice).
- **⚠️ Contractor-market "negative selection" claim**: competent,
  well-organized crews are stated to exit the private-residential-
  interior renovation market relatively quickly, moving to either the
  premium segment or commercial-interior work (both described as easier
  or more profitable) — leaving weaker crews disproportionately
  represented in the mainstream private-apartment segment. Framed as an
  expectation-setting claim (be prepared that a first, second, even
  third crew may not work out), not a technique to fix the problem.
  `single-account`, `unverified`.
- **Payment-staging contract practice**: split a contractor payment
  schedule into small stage-based increments (even a "100% prepayment"
  structure is acceptable if it's 100% of a small stage, not a large
  lump sum) so either party can cleanly exit at any stage boundary
  without one side having pre-paid for undelivered future work.
  Complements this store's existing scope-of-work-in-writing practice —
  a second, independent contract-structuring defense.
- **⚠️ Change-request compounding effect during active construction,
  named mechanism**: any mid-project change (the given example: moving a
  partition 20cm after work has started) does two things at once — (1)
  it signals to the crew that the schedule has slack ("if there's time
  to move a partition, there's time to take the weekend off," in the
  practitioner's own framing), and (2) because construction tasks are
  interdependent (a chain of trades scheduled around each other), a
  single change cascades non-linearly through the schedule rather than
  adding a fixed, proportional delay — described in her own words as
  "not +2, but 2 squared." Practical implication: minimize the number of
  in-progress changes; decide as much as possible before construction
  starts rather than mid-project.
