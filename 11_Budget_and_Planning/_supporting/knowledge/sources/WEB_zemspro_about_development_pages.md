## Mistakes / Warnings & Design Rules — Durable Facts & Rules (from the verbatim-verified "Смысл жизни" tab)

Genuinely new content this pass — not present in the earlier WebFetch summary at all, discovered only via the tab-by-tab Playwright MCP re-check. Four concrete technique positions, each stated as this practitioner's own standing rule regardless of client preference:

- **Drywall (гипсокартон) ceilings with recessed spotlights are avoided by default**: drywall is framed as an outdated technique (30+ years old per the source's own framing) that cracks and yellows, needing repainting every 5-7 years; recessed spotlights are framed as harsh on the eyes, power-hungry, and prone to burning out weekly. `single-account`, strongly-worded practitioner opinion — the specific "every 5-7 years" and "burns out weekly" claims are `unverified` (no data shown), but worth recording as this practitioner's stated reasoning for the position (which is consistent with this store's existing stretch-ceiling-preference facts from other sources).
- **Frosted/opaque glass toilet doors are avoided regardless of client request**: even fully opaque (frosted) glass fails acoustically — "through it everything is audible, as if there's no door at all." Stated rationale ties this to a real household composition consideration (a family of 4+ occasional guests hearing everything). `confirmed` as the practitioner's stated reasoning (quoted in substance); a genuinely reusable acoustic-privacy caution independent of this specific company.
- **A child's homework desk should not be placed to the left of a window** (i.e., light source on the client's/designer's preferred side isn't automatically correct) — if the light source is to the right of a right-handed child's writing hand, the hand casts a shadow over the work surface, and the source claims this can measurably harm eyesight within 1-2 years from the child straining to see. `single-account`, the specific "1-2 years" eyesight-damage timeline is `unverified`, but the underlying ergonomic mechanism (hand-shadow from wrong-side lighting) is a concrete, checkable design rule.
- **Hygienic shower (bidet spray) placement should account for the user's handedness**: for a right-handed user, the hygienic shower/bidet spray should be positioned so the right hand does the washing and the left hand holds the sprayer — implying handedness should inform fixture-side placement rather than a single default configuration. `single-account`, concrete ergonomic rule not previously in this store.

**Framing note**: all four are presented by the source as reasons the company will actively override a client's stated preference — useful as technique content, but also a clear example of this company's own promotional "we know better than the client" positioning (see Advertising/Promotional filter). Extract the technique, not the "trust us over your own judgment" framing.

## Planning Rules — Durable Facts & Rules

- **An 11-stage design-project delivery process** is described end-to-end, from a 5,000 RUB date-reservation deposit through 3D modeling, technical drawings, and post-delivery support. Several stages explicitly exclude client participation (concept development, 3D model detailing, final drawings prep) — client involvement is concentrated at specific checkpoints (technical brief, concept approval, 3D model presentation/approval, final receipt), not continuous. `single-account` (company's own stated process), automated-summarization fidelity caveat applies.
- **A stated multi-stage quality-control sequence** (framed by the source as "five-stage" but the extracted description lists more than five named checkpoints): concept reviewed by a surveyor with the client, then a department head, then Zemskov personally; the 3D model is developed by a technologist and reviewed by a Quality Inspector; final drawings get a Project Manager inspection and a second department-head review before client delivery. **Rule worth extracting independent of this specific company**: layering review by role (surveyor → department head → principal → technologist → QA → PM → department head again) rather than a single reviewer is the underlying reusable planning idea, even though the specific role titles are this company's own structure. `single-account`.
- **Mid-process scope changes are explicitly bounded, not open-ended**: at the 3D-model-presentation stage, the client may request "additional electrical outlets, but not more" — i.e. a stated, narrow change-request window rather than unlimited revisions at that checkpoint. `confirmed` as a direct policy statement (quoted).
- **A 28-item design-project deliverable checklist** is described (see full itemized list in the evidence file) spanning: room specification, demolition scope, wall/partition plan, opening/portal formation, false-wall/niche specs, floor/ceiling plans, tile layout, door/portal schedule, materials calculation sheet, window/slope finishing, balcony/loggia glazing, water-supply/collector scheme, primary plumbing connection scheme, heating/radiator scheme, floor heating, ventilation/AC (including a stated **back-check-valve recommendation** — consistent with, and reinforcing, this store's existing check-valve/backflow fact already corroborated by two prior sources), kitchen module dimensions, electrical general recommendations (breaker config, circuit division, cable cross-sections), three-way-switch/emergency-lighting scheme, detailed electrical-point coordinates, furniture/accessory dimensions, plus supporting sections (notes sheet, work-sequence-by-stage, basic visualization). **Genuinely useful as a checklist template for what a complete design project should cover**, independent of whether this specific company is used — a self-managed renovation could use this list to audit whether its own plan/documentation has real gaps. `single-account`, self-promotional framing, but the checklist structure itself is a reusable planning artifact.
- **A stated project-management principle**: "Проект должен разрабатывать тот, кто может его воплотить" (the project should be developed by someone who can actually build it) — framed as the reasoning behind having builders/technologists directly involved in project development, not just architects/designers working in isolation from construction reality. `confirmed` (quoted).
