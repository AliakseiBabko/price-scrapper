## Genuinely New Content

- **⚠️ Ceiling-only soundproofing without wall soundproofing is
  substantially ineffective — a new explicit claim, extending this
  store's existing direct/indirect-transmission framing**: most
  perceived neighbor noise (assumed to come "from above," motivating a
  ceiling-only soundproofing job) actually transmits through the shared
  **walls**, not primarily through the floor slab above. Soundproofing
  the ceiling alone catches only a marginal percentage of total noise
  and isn't worth its own cost in isolation — do it in combination with
  wall soundproofing, or not at all.
- **Pocket/cassette sliding door drawbacks — a genuinely new door-type
  comparison for this store**: a door that retracts fully into a
  wall-mounted cassette ("пенал") system, distinct from an ordinary
  surface-mounted sliding door (which this source has no complaint
  about). Four stated drawbacks: (1) real cost premium — you pay for the
  cassette system itself, not just the door leaf; (2) no proper handle
  for comfortable repeated daily use, becoming a genuine annoyance over
  time; (3) meaningfully worse soundproofing than a hinged door; (4) a
  finite opening-cycle lifespan with real maintenance needs, unlike a
  hinged door's simple hinge mechanism.
- **Touch/capacitive wall switches, general-purpose impracticality — a
  new claim distinct from this store's existing under-cabinet-lighting-
  specific touch-sensor caution**: a touch switch requires visually
  locating the exact touch point and pressing precisely, and doesn't
  always register on the first attempt — contrasted with an ordinary
  toggle/rocker switch, which can be hit anywhere on its face by feel
  alone with no visual confirmation needed. Recommends touch switches
  only where already integrated into a smart-home voice-control setup
  (where the physical switch is rarely the primary control method
  anyway).
- **Floating-floor threshold sealing — a distinct cracking mechanism
  from this store's existing shadow-gap-baseboard damper content**: at a
  transition between a floating floor (e.g. laminate, which expands/
  contracts with temperature/humidity and is not glued down) and a
  different adjacent flooring (e.g. tile), filling the gap with cork
  compensator strip or sealant to avoid a visible threshold strip fails
  within a short time — the joint cracks/separates because the laminate
  side keeps moving. **Correct fix, stated directly**: use a small, flat
  transition threshold strip, which aesthetically covers the joint while
  leaving the laminate free to move underneath it.

## Restated / Extended Content (Not Fully New)

- **Stretch ceiling + baguette/molding framing cancels repairability —
  same mechanism as this round's own `kkE25HmFciU`, with two additional
  concrete details worth folding in as an extension rather than a
  separate new item**: (1) a bare stretch-ceiling membrane replacement
  (no baguette) takes about **3 hours** with zero mess/dust — the
  installer unclips the existing profile, tensions new material, and
  reclips; (2) where a baguette *is* present, removing it to access the
  membrane damages the paint/plaster at the wall-ceiling junction, so
  the repair cascades into repainting/re-finishing the walls too — a
  fuller "why it cascades" mechanism than `kkE25HmFciU` gave. Recorded
  as an extension on `Ceilings_Guide.md`'s existing Round 15 entry, not
  a separate new fact.
