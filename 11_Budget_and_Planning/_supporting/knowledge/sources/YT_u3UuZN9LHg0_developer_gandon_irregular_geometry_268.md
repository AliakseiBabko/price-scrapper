## Rules / Heuristics — Genuinely New Content

- **⚠️ Ventilation shaft vs. duct box distinction, reinforced with the underlying safety/legal mechanism spelled out**: a true ventilation shaft (шахта — a shared structural column running the full building height, serving every unit on that riser line) must never be chased/cut into (штробить), even along a thin section — **debris from chasing can fall down the shaft into other units below, and the responsible party is fined for it.** This is distinct from a decorative/simple duct casing (короб), which can be modified freely. Reinforces this store's existing zashivka-vs-venshakhta distinction with the explicit debris-fall/fine mechanism, not just "don't touch it."
- **⚠️ Wall-anchoring-to-structural-reference rule, cleanly demonstrated**: when building a new wall along an exterior wall, anchor to the load-bearing segment's own plane — not to whatever irregular, incidental structure (e.g. a protruding duct casing) happens to sit in front of it. If the incidental structure protrudes further than the load-bearing reference, build the wall at the structural reference anyway and cut a notch/recess around the protrusion, rather than shifting the whole wall's position to accommodate it.
- **⚠️ Deliberate exception to flush-wall-alignment, a second concrete instance**: rather than flush-aligning two wall segments per the usual best practice, a documented case deliberately left a step/protrusion between them because a specific piece of furniture (a closet) needed exactly that step as its own back wall. **Reinforces (with a second real example) this store's existing ~10%-exception rule** (from `Af8nNyn9a_c`) — flush-align by default, except when the resulting step is specifically needed to host a piece of furniture.
- **⚠️ Cross-traffic-through-a-seating-zone elimination technique**: when a door opening's position forces people walking to/from a room to cross directly in front of a TV-viewing seating area (a real "tripping over people watching TV" problem), relocate the door opening itself — not just the furniture — to route foot traffic around the seating zone entirely. Framed as a problem many designers treat as unsolvable, but straightforward once the opening (not just the furniture) is treated as the variable to move.
- **⚠️ Third documented instance of this store's "future-flexible planning" technique family**: pre-install reinforced wall backing/anchor points (закладные) for a wall-mounted gymnastic/Swedish ladder (шведская стенка) *together with* hidden electrical points for a TV zone, at the same wall location in each of two kids' rooms — letting the client choose (or later swap) between a Swedish ladder or a TV zone at that exact spot without redoing the wall. A third independent application of the future-flexible-planning principle already documented in this store (previously: a gas-code-compliant glass partition, a pre-wired future partition wall, pre-wired future electrical for a bed-configuration change) — this time, dual-provisioning one wall location for two *mutually exclusive* furniture/equipment choices rather than a single deferred future change.
- **Closet-depth-by-wall-offset-arithmetic technique, a clean worked example**: taking two walls of known thickness (150mm each) and offsetting one relative to the other by a chosen amount automatically produces a fixed, matching closet depth (60cm in this case) at the resulting recesses on both sides — useful as a demonstration that this kind of wall-offset math can guarantee a specific, mechanically-determined storage depth rather than needing to eyeball it.
