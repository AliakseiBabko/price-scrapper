## Rules / Heuristics — New Detail

1. **"Энергофлекс" ("Energoflex") pipe-insulation color-coded mechanism**:
   blue sleeve on the cold-water pipe prevents condensation from forming
   on the pipe (caused by the temperature differential between the cold
   pipe and the warmer cabinet air) — without it, condensation leads to
   dampness/rust and shortens the life of the cabinet's other components;
   red sleeve on the hot-water pipe retains the pipe's heat *inside* the
   insulation rather than radiating it into the cabinet, keeping the
   technical cabinet/bathroom from becoming uncomfortably hot. A concrete
   mechanism for a component not previously documented on the Water Inlet
   Node page.
2. **Central-system pressure and hydraulic-shock magnitude, a concrete
   numeric range**: normal central-system pressure runs 6-8 atm; a
   pressure regulator is set to a comfortable 3-4 atm for household use;
   during a hydraulic-shock event (tied to planned/unplanned water
   shutoffs and restorations) pressure in the central system can spike to
   **11 atm** — a specific figure for how far above the existing page's
   general "reducer protects against hydraulic shock" framing the surge
   can actually reach.
3. **Fine-filter omission stated to void fixture manufacturer warranty**:
   per Petrishin, expensive mixer/fixture manufacturers' own installation
   instructions and warranty documentation condition the warranty on a
   100-micron fine filter being installed upstream — framed as a concrete
   warranty-claim consequence, not just a performance/longevity argument.
   Tag as the company's own claim (unverified against an actual
   manufacturer document), but specific enough to record.
4. **Coarse/"косой" filter's building-side placement, tied to meter
   installation**: this filter is installed *outside* the apartment, on
   the shared building landing/staircase, without a meter attached to it
   directly — and is a precondition water-utility installers require
   before they will install the apartment's own water meter. A placement
   detail (building-common-area vs. in-cabinet) not previously recorded
   on the Water Inlet Node page, which otherwise treats the coarse filter
   as an in-cabinet, pre-meter component.
5. **Check-valve failure mode framed with an old-building-stock angle**:
   hot water pushing backward into the cold line (or vice versa) when
   line pressures differ enough to overwhelm a mixer's internal chamber
   is called "most common in older housing stock — Stalin-era and
   Khrushchev-era buildings" specifically, extending the existing page's
   general check-valve-backflow mechanism with an age-of-building risk
   signal.
6. **Dry-siphon consolidation of AC condensate and filter-flush drainage**:
   the AC unit's condensate line and the fine filter's periodic flush
   discharge are routed into a shared dry siphon before joining the
   sewage line — specifically to keep sewer gas/odor from venting back
   into the cabinet through either drain path. A routing detail not
   previously recorded.
