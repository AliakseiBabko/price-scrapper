## Genuinely New Content

- **Explicit three-letter design-check framework: "Д-П-У" (Доступность,
  Приватность, Удобство — Accessibility, Privacy, Convenience)**. The
  source states this explicitly as a personal rule he applies to every
  санузел layout before finalizing it: run through all three in order at
  the theory stage. This is a genuinely new *organizing framework* not
  previously captured on this vault's Bathroom/WC pages (which capture
  many individual accessibility/privacy/convenience-driven rules but not
  this named three-part checklist itself). Room-agnostic — applies to an
  apartment bathroom/WC exactly the same as a house's.
- **Fixture-access-order convention differs by architectural tradition,
  named explicitly as a Russian/domestic vs. Western distinction**: Russian
  ("отечественные") architectural schools teach that the *sink* should be
  the first fixture encountered on entry, toilet second; Western practice
  reportedly favors the opposite (toilet first, reasoning: faster access to
  the toilet itself, hand-washing treated as secondary). Presented as a
  genuine convention difference, not a right/wrong claim — worth recording
  as a named disagreement between traditions, `single-account` for which
  Western sources actually hold this view.
- **Ventilation duct sizing for a masonry (brick) vent shaft**: a
  140×140mm brick vent-duct opening is stated as sufficient for a
  санузел up to 10m² — sized to brick-coursing modularity, not an
  arbitrary round number. **Duct intake height convention**: for a
  standard 3m ceiling, the intake sits around 270cm (270 downstand into a
  dropped-ceiling void, explicitly reasoned as the height that leaves room
  for built-in downlights and other services above it) — can be raised
  further but only if the duct's own air-intake path through the ceiling
  void is separately worked out. In a non-masonry house, the duct is built
  from KUB (looks like a US-drywall-panel-derived shaft, "УЭСБИ" in the
  transcript — likely a mis-transcription of a panel product name) and
  must always vent to the roof, never terminate mid-building, specifically
  for odor removal. **An electric booster fan mounted in the duct is
  explicitly framed as odor-removal assistance only, not a substitute for
  forced ventilation** — the source is explicit it "probably can" work as
  forced ventilation but will be underpowered for that job.
- **Drain-line grouping principle, named and generalized**: the source
  explicitly recommends siting every wet fixture (toilet, sink, shower/tub)
  so their drain/supply connections land on a single straight line back to
  the technical chase/duct ("технический блок") — described as the most
  rational plumbing layout and applied consistently across every worked
  example in the video (guest WC, compact shower room, larger shower room,
  two bathtub layouts, floor-drain shower). This vault's existing content
  (Bathroom `Structure_and_Framing`) already covers routing pipe diameter
  vs. partition thickness, but not this specific "one straight service
  line" placement heuristic as its own named principle — genuinely new
  framing, though the underlying practice (group wet fixtures near the
  chase) is implicit elsewhere.
- **Finished floor level in any санузел is always dropped below the
  adjoining floor, by 3–5cm** — stated as a near-universal rule ("always
  and everywhere"), with the specific example of a floor-mounted shower
  (no pan/cabin, just a sloped floor and a floor drain) needing the lower
  end of that range. This **corroborates and adds a specific numeric range**
  to WC_Guide.md §4b's existing recessed-floor / leak-containment finding
  (which established the *direction* — never build the wet-room floor
  above the surrounding floor — but didn't carry a specific drop
  measurement). [source: this transcript]
- **Toilet clearance-from-obstruction rule, with a specific number the
  source calls out as commonly violated**: minimum 25cm clear from the
  toilet's centerline/edge to any obstruction (wall, fixture) on either
  side, 30cm recommended as more comfortable — explicitly criticized as a
  rule people skip ("I don't understand people who install a toilet flush
  against a wall and then insist it's fine because their apartment does
  the same"). Not previously captured on this vault's WC/Bathroom pages
  with this specific clearance figure.
- **Toilet length by installation type, with an explicit warning against a
  common false economy**: floor-mounted toilet (no in-wall frame) needs
  60cm length including the tank, measured honestly. A wall-hung toilet on
  an in-wall frame can shrink the *visible bowl* to 45cm — but the source
  explicitly warns this is not free room-space savings: an honestly-drawn
  in-wall installation frame itself needs a minimum 25cm of wall depth,
  which must still be accounted for in the room's footprint even though
  it's hidden behind tile. This nuance (frame depth still costs floor
  area, it's just hidden) isn't captured elsewhere on this vault's WC
  pages.
- **Wet-fixture service-run depth allowance, generalized beyond the
  drain**: leave 25cm of clearance on each side of hot/cold-water supply
  pipes for future access (repair, valve/fitting adjustment) — the same
  25cm figure the source recommends applying to the sewage line too,
  making a combined HVS/GVS/drain chase the simplest single-box solution
  rather than three separate chases.
- **Explicit prohibition, stated in the strongest terms in the transcript**:
  never chase a load-bearing wall to route a horizontal drain/supply
  line — reasoning given is structural load already carried from the roof
  and floors above, explicitly framed as "I do not permit this" rather
  than a soft preference. This corroborates Bathroom_Guide's existing
  "never chase a load-bearing wall" rule (sourced there to Zemstandart/
  zems_b160) from a second, independent named practitioner —
  cross-tradition corroboration worth noting even though the underlying
  rule was already on the page.
- **Guest-WC placement/access-sequencing logic, generalizable to an
  apartment entry despite the house framing**: the entrance to a guest WC
  should sit off the front hall (прихожая), not directly off the entry
  vestibule/mudroom (тамбур) itself — reasoning: the vestibule is still
  the "dirty" outdoor-shoe zone, and a guest WC is meant to be used in
  house slippers/socks after outerwear and shoes are already off. This is
  a sequencing principle (dirty zone → clean transition → WC), not a
  house-specific room count, and applies to an apartment entry hall
  layout too.
- **Two dedicated electrical outlets for a washer+dryer pairing, stated as
  the honest minimum** — one per appliance, not a single shared outlet;
  plus a **waterproof-rated outlet is mandatory near any sink**, called out
  as a non-negotiable code-adjacent minimum regardless of house type.
- **Compact shower-room worked dimensions** (below the guest-WC-with-shower
  size class): 1.6m clear × 2.0m clear as the source's own stated minimum
  for a shower + toilet + sink room, immediately followed with the same
  "clear" vs. "masonry-to-masonry" distinction used throughout — add ~10cm
  total (5cm/side) if measuring stud-to-stud rather than the finished
  clear dimension. **Shower cabin/pan sizing**: 1m × 1m stated as the
  source's own comfort default for a house project; 90×90cm and 80×80cm
  flagged explicitly as compromises ("you can buy a cramped Chinese shower
  cabin and learn to live with it, but better to plan for 1×1m from the
  start").
