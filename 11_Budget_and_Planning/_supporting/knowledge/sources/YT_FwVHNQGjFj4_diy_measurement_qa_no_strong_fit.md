## Durable Facts (methodology, not renovation execution)

- **Room measurement sequencing**: start from the room containing the entry door; each subsequent room to digitize should be the one adjacent to the largest number of already-measured rooms (maximizes cross-reference/anchor points, improving accuracy).
- **Contour-before-volume rule**: draw all room floor contours in 2D first; only after contours are verified should walls be raised to make the model 3D — and balconies/loggias should only be attached after the apartment is already 3D (using the balcony threshold as an anchor point).
- **Niche/opening digitizing**: a wall niche needs 5 measurements (offset from left or right edge, its width, offset from floor or ceiling, its height, and its depth); a door/window opening needs offset-from-edge, width, and height (plus sill height for windows).
- **What NOT to digitize**: removable/non-structural items like radiators (since they get removed) and baseboards are skipped; permanent structural elements — columns, pipe risers/stacks, structural ceiling beams — must be digitized to their exact real-world dimensions and position, since these can't be hidden (e.g. a stretch ceiling can't be dropped low enough to conceal a large structural beam).
- **Load-bearing walls must be flagged/colored separately in the model** and never altered in a redesign, as distinct from partition walls which can be freely reconfigured.
- **Rounding/scale convention**: measure to the millimeter, then round to the nearest 10mm using standard rounding (e.g. 1456mm → 1460mm, 1432mm → 1430mm); model at true 1:1 scale using an architectural-mm template.
- **Ceiling height measurement method**: measure at 5 points per room (four corners + center), across all rooms, and use the **single lowest measured value** as the design constraint — reasoning: screed is poured level to the highest point in the apartment, so actual final ceiling height will never exceed the lowest pre-existing measurement; planning around a higher number risks furniture/curtain-rod clearance problems later.
- **Structural columns encountered mid-room are described as valuable reference/anchor points** (since they're immovable), not obstacles — the surrounding geometry should be measured against them with extra care/redundant checks.
- **Cross-check methods**: after building a room, re-measure its diagonal (both physically and in-software) to confirm it matches; also use a "control" sum-check (e.g. left-of-opening + opening width + right-of-opening should sum to the room's total measured width).
