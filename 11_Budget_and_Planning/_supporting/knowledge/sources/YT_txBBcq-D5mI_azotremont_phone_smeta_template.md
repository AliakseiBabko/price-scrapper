## Durable, Reusable Content — Practitioner's Own Smeta-Building Method (Level 1)

- **Live, shareable Google Sheets smeta, not a static document**: the speaker builds every smeta directly in Google Drive/Sheets specifically because it gives the client a live online link — the client can view the estimate, watch it update in real time as line items/prices change, and optionally comment, entirely from a phone with no computer required either side. He explicitly frames this shareability (not just the itemization) as a competitive advantage over competitors handing over a static printed or verbal quote.
- **Two-speed workflow depending on scope**: for a simple, narrowly-scoped job with straightforward area/length math (e.g. flooring only), he fills in quantities and prices **on-site during the initial measurement visit**, in front of the client, in well under 10 minutes. For a **full apartment smeta**, he budgets roughly **2 hours in the evening** for a standard renovation with no unusual complexity; a job that also includes a full design project ("дизайн-проект") scope is "a whole day's work."
- **Spreadsheet column structure**: work-item name | unit of measurement | quantity | unit price | line total, with **formulas that auto-recalculate every line total and the grand total the instant a price or quantity changes** (demonstrated live: changing a rate from 600 to 20,000 RUB instantly re-totals the sheet) — the same self-recalculating-template shape as the Anatolich Group source (`YT_Y08zvnbxdM0`), but built by an individual practitioner in a generic spreadsheet rather than a company's dedicated tool.
- **Deep line-item granularity as an explicit trust/sales mechanism, not just bookkeeping**: the speaker's real smeta example runs to **as many as 65 distinct itemized work lines** for one apartment (worked example shown on camera: "демонтаж перегородок" (partition demolition) 29 m² × 600 RUB/m² = 17,400 RUB as one single line among many). His stated reasoning: when a client sees every individual work item priced and named, they understand *what* they're paying for and *why* the total is what it is — he contrasts this directly with a common competing practice of a crew pacing out an apartment by foot and giving a single round-number $/m² verbal guess with no breakdown ("с вас 25 тысяч квадрат" — vague, no itemization), or a same-day handwritten scrap-paper list. He states this granularity is a **direct competitive-advantage/close-rate factor**, not just paperwork.
- **Stage/payment-milestone tab, separate from the itemized work list**: the same workbook includes a dedicated stages ("этапность") tab that groups work into phases, most often **tied to payment milestones** rather than to completion of each individual line item — his own stated practice is to tie contract payment terms to stage completion (pay on completing stage 1, then stage 2, etc.) because it's simpler to write into a contract than requiring sign-off on every individual itemized work line. This is a concrete, checkable practice directly relevant to `Budgeting_Guide.md`'s existing "insist on a fully itemized smeta" guidance — it shows a real practitioner's own preferred way to *structure payments* against that itemization, not just the itemization itself.
- **Template distribution and reuse mechanics**: the speaker shares blank copies of his own smeta template via his Telegram channel; a recipient uses Google Drive's own "make a copy" function to clone it into their own Drive as an independently editable spreadsheet, rather than editing his master copy directly — a simple, generically-applicable distribution pattern for anyone wanting to reuse or adapt a spreadsheet-based smeta template.
- **Print-to-contract workflow**: once a smeta is filled in, the speaker copies its full contents from the spreadsheet and pastes it into a separate Google Docs contract-appendix document ("Приложение №1" / Appendix 1 to the signed contract), replacing whatever smeta was previously pasted there for a prior job. He explicitly checks **print preview before finalizing** — a pasted spreadsheet can silently get cut off at page edges/margins when printed, so he verifies fit and cost/quantity columns aren't clipped before sending the appendix to a print shop. A concrete printing/paperwork gotcha for anyone adapting this workflow.
- **Voice-to-text entry shortcut, with a real caveat**: to speed up typing work-item names on a phone, he uses voice input (e.g. dictating "укладка плитки по диагонали" — diagonal tile installation) rather than typing; the caveat is that Google's voice-to-text doesn't auto-capitalize the first letter of a dictated line, requiring a manual fix afterward if consistent capitalization matters to the user.
- **Stated business rationale for investing time in a detailed smeta**: even a modest lift in close rate (his own example: if a detailed smeta only improved conversion to 50%, from an unspecified lower baseline) is worth the extra time, given his own stated customer-acquisition cost of roughly **1,000 RUB per lead** — losing prospective clients to a competitor's clearer, itemized quote is framed as a real, quantifiable cost, not just a nicety. `single-account`, his own stated economics, not independently verified.
