## Budget Ranges / Cost Drivers — Durable Facts & Rules

- **RemProektMD states two headline labor-only price floors, split by building age, for a full design-driven turnkey renovation**: new-build ("новострой") ≈**100 EUR/m²** for labor only ("за работы без материала"), old-fund ("старый фонд", pre-Soviet/Soviet housing stock) ≈**150 EUR/m²** for labor only. Both are stated as **floors, not typical/average figures** — "the price practically never goes below this, but can go higher." **Explicitly excludes materials and any furniture cost.** `confirmed` (level 1, directly and repeatedly stated), but `single-account` (one company's own stated pricing, framed partly as a lead-generation pitch).
- **Why new-build costs less than old-fund, per RemProektMD's own reasoning** (a real "X vs Y, and here's why" comparison, not a bare assertion):
  1. **Electrical**: even in a new-build, a full design-driven renovation requires completely redoing the electrical (new panel with far more breakers than the developer's minimal default, dedicated circuits per major appliance with RCD/differential breakers for wet rooms) — this cost applies to both building types equally, so it's not the differentiator, but it establishes a shared cost floor.
  2. **Old-fund adds demolition scope not present in new-build**: full removal of old plaster down to the base structural material (concrete/aerated block/brick — "нельзя оставлять старую штукатурку," since new plaster won't adhere reliably or will eventually detach with the old layer), full removal of old screed down to the structural slab (not partial — "не на 5 см, не на 2 см... до плиты перекрытия"), full re-pour of the floor.
  3. **Old-fund screed must include insulation** (expanded clay/keramzit or extruded polystyrene/penoplex) for both thermal and sound insulation between floors — not just a bare cement-sand pour onto the slab.
  4. **Old-fund often requires replacing sewage risers and radiators**: old cast-iron radiators and old sewage stacks are frequently corroded/leaking and get replaced (with cast-iron or bimetal radiators) — ideally coordinated with the neighbor sharing the same stack. Central heating systems require this coordination; autonomous heating systems don't.
- **Small-apartment $/m² premium mechanism, a genuine cost-driver rule**: a small apartment (30-35 m²) has a **higher** $/m² cost than a large apartment (~200 m²) of the same building type — **not because of higher margin-seeking, but because every apartment regardless of size still needs one full bathroom** (the single most expensive room-type per m² in the apartment), so the bathroom's fixed cost is spread over fewer total m² in a small unit.
- **New-build pipe-replacement rationale (companion topic to Video 4 in this trial)**: developers commonly install the cheapest polypropylene pipe in new-builds, with enough concealed joints under the screed that a leak becomes a real risk over time — RemProektMD's own practice is to replace these with Rehau pipe when doing a full renovation, but explicitly **not always**: if the client says "don't replace them, just check them," they perform a pressure test ("опрессовка") and, if pressure holds, leave the existing pipes — with the caveat that pipes holding pressure today can still fail in year 3-4. RemProektMD describes at least one real case where a client declined replacement and a leak did occur later, requiring remediation.
- **Screed flatness QC rule**: verify with a 2-meter level/straightedge ("правило"); if deviation exceeds **2-3mm over 2 meters**, the screed must be leveled (self-leveling compound preferred over tile-adhesive buildup). New-build screed is marketed as "ready for laminate" but "very rarely" actually is.
- **Old screed replacement threshold**: if the building is **more than 20 years old**, existing screed should never be kept even if visually flat — it becomes friable/crumbly ("рыхлая") over time and will eventually delaminate under new flooring.
- **Ceiling QC finding**: a new-build's white-painted ceiling is not evidence of being level — check with a laser level; per RemProektMD, new-build ceilings (bare monolithic concrete slab, dry-mix-passed only, no leveling) are "most often" out of level once actually measured. Cost comparison: a drywall-boxed ceiling (plaster + paint) is described as roughly the same total cost as plastering-on-lath + double-skim + paint for the same slab — i.e. there's no cheap-and-easy "just repaint" option once the slab is genuinely out of level.
- **Contingency line item**: RemProektMD's own itemized quotes ("смета") build in **10-15% for unforeseen expenses** — costs that arise mid-project, not always the contractor's fault (sometimes client-initiated changes, sometimes genuinely unforeseen).
- **Design-style cost driver**: an itemized quote can only be produced once a design project exists, since a classical style (plaster molding, cornices) costs substantially more than a minimalist style for the same floor plan — the company provides the quote free, but only after (or alongside) the design project.
