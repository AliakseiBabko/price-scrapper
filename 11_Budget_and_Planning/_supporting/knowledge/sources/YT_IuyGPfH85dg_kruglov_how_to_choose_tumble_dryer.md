## New facts

1. **Why a dryer beats a washer's spin cycle alone**: spin removes only excess water, leaving
   at least 40-60% residual moisture in the fabric; a dryer additionally removes that
   remaining moisture, leaving laundry warm, soft, "hotel-like," and — with the right program
   and fabric match — sometimes wrinkle-free enough to skip ironing. Removes the need to hang
   laundry/bedding around the apartment (kitchen, loggia, etc.) to air-dry.
2. **Function taxonomy**: residual-moisture-sensing automatic drying; time/temperature-based
   manual drying; fabric-specific programs (cotton, synthetic, shirts, wool, bedding, etc.);
   steam function (on some models); anti-crease protection (a specific program); reversible
   drum rotation for more even drying.
3. **⚠️ "Dryers ruin clothes" myth, mechanism-level rebuttal**: this only happens from picking
   the wrong program or overheating fabric (e.g. running a cotton program on wool items) — with
   correct program selection, a dryer never damages clothes and actually *reduces* wear
   compared to the alternative (wet, heavy laundry stretching out of shape on a drying rack
   after a wash). Direct parallel drawn to washing machines: incorrect program choice or mixing
   colorfast/non-colorfast items ruins laundry in a washer too — a correct-program-selection
   principle that applies to appliances generally, not a dryer-specific flaw.
4. **⚠️ No sewer/drain plumbing connection required, in most cases**: nearly all dryers have a
   built-in condensate collection tray, and nearly all also have a direct drain-connection
   port for the central sewer line if you prefer not to manually empty the tray every 1-2
   cycles — connecting to the sewer removes that recurring manual-emptying task entirely.
5. **Standard vs. compact dimensions**: standard size is 60×60cm, 85cm tall; compact models
   also exist.
6. **⚠️ Three-tier drying-technology taxonomy, with real named tradeoffs**:
   - **Vent/exhaust drying** — the oldest, now largely unavailable on the market ("thankfully,"
     per the source). Mechanism: heats air, passes it through the drum, vents it outside.
     Requires a ventilation-duct connection (without one, indoor humidity rises noticeably);
     the highest electricity consumer of the three types; runs hottest, most likely to overheat
     and damage fabric over time. Only advantage: the cheapest, simplest construction.
   - **Condensation drying** — mechanism: heats air to dry laundry, with resulting moisture
     collected in a reservoir or drained away, while warm air recirculates inside the drum.
     **⚠️ An undisclosed drawback the source says sellers/manufacturers don't mention**: running
     this type measurably raises the *ambient room temperature* — a concrete example given: a
     bathroom/laundry room (already often warmer than other rooms) can rise **~6°C** during a
     cycle, a real comfort problem. **⚠️ Energy consumption: ≈3.5 kWh per cycle.** Also runs hot
     enough to damage fabric if the wrong program is chosen (mitigated by correct program
     selection). Advantages: the most affordable, most commonly stocked type; doesn't need a
     ventilation-duct connection (installable anywhere), simple hookup, ideally with a sewer
     drain connection.
   - **Heat-pump drying** — the source's recommended type for an apartment or house. Mechanism:
     a built-in heat pump recirculates air rather than heating it directly, extracting moisture
     with minimal heat — the gentlest of the three technologies on fabric. **⚠️ Price: a good
     model starts at ≈45,000-50,000 RUB (≈$560-$620) and reaches ≈100,000 RUB (≈$1,200) and
     above** — described as a comparatively rare, more expensive segment. Real tradeoff: a
     noticeably longer drying-cycle time than the other two types. **⚠️ Energy consumption:
     ≈0.8-1+ kWh per cycle** — a large efficiency gap vs. condensation drying's ~3.5 kWh.
     Additional stated advantages: noticeably quieter operation, and longer component
     lifespan/service life from reduced mechanical wear (all cited as reasons a heat-pump dryer
     specifically is worth its price premium).
7. **Stacking note**: any dryer type can be stacked on a washing machine using a dedicated
   stacking belt/kit designed for that purpose (a general mechanism, not tied to one brand).
8. **⚠️ Separate vs. combined unit, three real configurations with a named-brand caution**:
   (a) fully separate washer + separate dryer — maximum efficiency/quality with no
   compromises, each appliance does its one job at 100%; lets you wash and dry
   *simultaneously* (finish washing load 1, move it to the dryer, immediately start washing
   load 2) — the source states this roughly doubles effective throughput; separate units also
   last longer since a combo unit's internal components do double mechanical duty every
   "wash" cycle. (b) A combo unit with **one shared drum** for both washing and drying — saves
   space, but that's its only real advantage; everything else is a real downside (implicitly,
   per the source's blunt "that's it, one advantage, then a downside" framing).
   (c) **⚠️ A combo unit with two independent drums in one shared chassis** — named brands
   offering this configuration: **LG, Hyundai, Haier** ("Hire" in the transcript, likely
   Haier), explicitly flagged by the source as **not yet proven/well-established enough to
   fully trust** ("I'd still be cautious about these"). Real structural risk: if either drum
   fails, the entire combined unit stops working (unlike two fully separate machines, where one
   failure doesn't take down the other). These twin-drum luxury combo units also cost
   noticeably more than buying two fully separate machines.
9. **Price drivers, general list**: presence/absence of a heat pump; filter quality and drum
   material (stainless steel raises price); electronics/controls sophistication (some models
   offer Wi-Fi/app control with expanded program/feature sets); brand; and additional named
   features not present on every model — garment-refresh function, anti-allergen function,
   anti-crease protection, self-cleaning, a dedicated shoe-drying program, interior drum
   lighting, and reversible door-hinge side (mirroring the same feature on refrigerators).
10. **The source's own bottom-line recommendation**: if budget and space allow, buy two
    separate quality-brand machines (one washer, one dryer) rather than any combo
    configuration — corroborates, with additional named-brand caution around the
    twin-drum-combo variant specifically, this store's existing separate-units preference.
