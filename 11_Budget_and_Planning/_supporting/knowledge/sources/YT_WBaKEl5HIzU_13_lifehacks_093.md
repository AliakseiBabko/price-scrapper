## Doors / Trim

- **A remote-controlled (electromagnetic) entrance-door lock is a genuine security-vs-emergency-access tradeoff, not a strict upgrade** — its control electronics sit inside the door leaf itself; if that mechanism ever fails, the door **cannot** be forced open even by fully cutting through the hinge side, because passive deadbolt pins on the hinge side prevent that bypass. Explicit caution: "think ten times" before installing this hardware type. `single-account`, real installed example.
- **Door hinge-side placement rule, restated**: never place hinges on the side that forces occupants to squeeze past a narrowed passage, and never place hinges so the door strikes a neighboring door when opened — corroborates this store's existing hinge-placement content, illustrated by a real observed neighbor-door mistake.

## Furniture / Built-ins

- **Sliding-wardrobe (купе) top rail must be fastened into a dedicated backing block (brick/timber) or thick plywood — never into drywall alone or into the drywall's own metal profile framing** — repeated door-slam impact loosens a drywall-only fastener over time until it fails. Requires planning the backing ("закладные") before the drywall/finish stage.
- **Handover documentation practice for a wardrobe box/soffit built before a stretch ceiling is installed**: photograph the box for the client's records, have the client independently photograph it on their own phone too, and physically write a note directly on the box stating the distance from its lower plane to the (not-yet-installed) stretch-ceiling membrane plus a max-fastener-length caution (35-40mm here) — later installers working above the finished ceiling can't see what's hidden there and could otherwise puncture it with an overlong screw. `confirmed`, real cited failure mode this practice prevents.

## Materials / Tools

- **An electric tile cutter is mandatory whenever tile work includes 45° miter cuts** — a manual hand cutter or angle grinder produces frequently low-quality 45° cuts; doesn't need to be a specific brand, just electric.
- **Textured/relief ("рифлёная") tile is a practical WC choice**: splashes, toothpaste droplets, and hand-washing splatter don't show visibly on the texture, keeping it looking clean between cleanings despite actually getting dirty at the same rate as smooth tile.
- **A tile joint-gap tolerance of up to ~0.5mm between adjacent tiles becomes visually invisible after grouting when using beveled-edge ("с фаской") tile** — a real installation-tolerance note.

## Plumbing / Electrical / HVAC

- **Wet-room technical access opening (concealed valve/shutoff access) should be positioned out of the primary sightline on entering the room** (here: on the far/back wall rather than a more visible spot) — verify sufficient reach/clearance is still available (in this case via the platform/podium height) before finalizing the position.
- **Concealed-cistern access hatch spec: 3 tiles tall (=60cm) × 60cm wide**, in this real project.
- **Some building-management companies restrict independent riser-level plumbing adjustments** — a real cited complaint: the building management company refused to let the presenter's team raise/adjust water pressure at the riser themselves, instead performing a lower-quality fix ("goose-neck" pipe workaround) on their own. Illustrates that some plumbing riser work may be gated through building management rather than being freely executable by a self-managed project, even when the resulting quality is questionable. `single-account`.
- **Loggia radiator-relocation sequencing** (when merging a loggia): first build the recess/niche, route the lower plumbing supply into it, then install the radiator into the niche — described as simple, fast, and effective.

## Planning Rules / Project Management

- **Worksite hygiene practice**: a cheap disposable temporary shower stall (~2,000 RUB in materials — a budget mixer, basin, and offcut materials) is built fresh for each project and discarded afterward, specifically so on-site workers living at the jobsite have basic hygiene facilities.
- **Tool-rental negotiating tip**: when renting an occasional-use tool (here: a manual tile cutter, in lieu of buying a cordless kit) from a rental shop, let the vendor calculate/select the needed consumables themselves rather than estimating your own usage — if you run short mid-job, you can hold the vendor responsible for a free top-up instead of covering it yourself, since they mis-sized their own recommendation. Stated saving: roughly 1,500 RUB (the rental fee itself, avoided this way).

## Materials — Wallpaper (real pricing)

- **Paintable ("under-paint") wallpaper preferred over patterned wallpaper for 4 stated reasons**: very easy to repaint, very easy to patch/repair if damaged, doesn't crack over time the way some patterned paper can, and enables a full cheap interior color refresh later (move furniture, repaint — no re-papering).
- **Real project pricing, ~95.5 m² apartment**: standard-grade paintable wallpaper would have cost **~32,000 RUB** for the whole apartment; the premium-grade paintable wallpaper actually used cost **~130,000 RUB** — the presenter still recommends the premium option, reasoning that repaint/patch costs stay cheap regardless of grade while full patterned-wallpaper replacement after damage is expensive. `single-account`, real project figure, upload-date-confirmed 2019 — treat as a historical (2019 Moscow) reference point, not current pricing.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Radiator/Laminate Expansion-Gap Safety Rule

- **Radiators should always be recessed/concealed within a false wall by default** — even when this requires extra framing purely to route the lower plumbing supply pipes so they aren't left exposed above the finished floor.
- **Critical, safety-relevant expansion-gap rule for a rigid pipe penetrating laminate flooring**: laminate is a floating floor that expands/contracts with humidity and temperature. If the pipe-penetration cutout in the laminate is cut snug (as commonly shown in manufacturer installation diagrams, ~1.5cm), the floor's expansion has nowhere to go at that point and can push against the rigidly-fixed pipe/radiator assembly hard enough to shift or burst it — flooding the unit and potentially units below. **Fix: apply the same expansion gap used around the room's perimeter (typically 1.5cm) around each pipe penetration through the laminate too**, covered with a decorative cover flange rather than sealed tight to the pipe. Related caution: an oversized perimeter gap is also a defect — it exposes the underlayment/screed to view and dirt collection, so the gap must be sized correctly (matching a normal 1.5cm expansion allowance) and consistently covered by baseboard around the full room perimeter. `confirmed`, explicit mechanism, genuinely important safety-relevant rule not previously recorded this specifically in this store.
