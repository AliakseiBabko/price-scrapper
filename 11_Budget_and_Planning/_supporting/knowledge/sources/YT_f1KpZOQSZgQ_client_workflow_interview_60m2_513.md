## Rules / Heuristics — Project Workflow & Client Experience

- **Single-point-of-contact team structure**: on a real project involving up to ~14 different tradespeople (painters, plasterers, drywall installers, electricians, plumbers, tilers, a separate AC installer, a separate kitchen installer, a separate door installer), the client communicates with exactly one person — the on-site foreman/project manager ("мастер") — who coordinates every trade and handles all utility-company/management-company communication directly. The client never deals with individual tradespeople or the building management company. **A durable process design point for evaluating any turnkey provider**: ask specifically whether a true single point of contact exists, not just a sales/design contact who disappears once construction starts.
- **Rough-materials ("черновые материалы") procurement was 100% handled by the company** — the client had zero involvement in selecting or sourcing rough-stage materials, in contrast to finish materials ("чистовые материалы"), which the client selected themselves (mostly online) roughly 90-100% of the time, with the foreman then ordering, picking up, inspecting on delivery, paying, and retaining receipts (kept for warranty purposes) — a clean itemized-accountability practice worth replicating even in a self-managed project (keep every purchase receipt tied to warranty claims).
- **Client involvement naturally tapers over a project's lifecycle**: near-weekly site visits in roughly the first two months (client-initiated out of genuine curiosity, not distrust — "capital renovation doesn't happen every day"), dropping to minimal once the rough-works process is established and trusted, then picking back up during the finish-materials-selection phase, which needs more active client decision-making. Useful expectation-setting data point for a self-managed owner planning their own time commitment.
- **Post-handover warranty responsiveness**: reported defects found shortly after move-in were resolved within about a week of a single phone call to the foreman — framed by the client as unusually attentive relative to peer/friend renovation experiences.
- **A budget-conscious professional-class client profile, not a wealthy-elite one** — the client is a salaried software developer who saved for roughly a year and partially financed the renovation via a loan/credit, explicitly pushing back on an assumption that this channel's clients are all "oligarchs with money to burn." Corroborates a client-profile theme already suggested elsewhere in this store; worth tracking further if it recurs.
- **⚠️ Design caution: a protruding kitchen-countertop "bump-out" step/ledge added purely for visual interest, with no functional storage use (nothing fits under, behind, or on it usably), creates a genuine trip/collision hazard** when moving between adjacent kitchen work zones (e.g. stepping around it to reach a kettle or the stove) — the speaker's own client experienced repeated minor collisions with the feature's sharp edge corners. **If pursuing a purely decorative countertop step, round the edges/corners rather than leaving them sharp**, or avoid the feature entirely given its lack of functional payoff.
- **⚠️ Design caution: printed/patterned large-format porcelain tile (imitation-stone/marble-look) can show visible print-quality inconsistency across pieces** — the same printed pattern rendered inconsistently (uneven grey tinting, and on some cut pieces the print missing entirely, showing bare clear glaze underneath) once installed at scale. **Practical guidance stated**: keep bathroom tile selection simple/plain rather than heavily patterned when the room already has other visual-interest elements (e.g. a rain-shower fixture) — "if unsure, don't overcomplicate it," since simplicity reduces the risk of this kind of print-defect exposure and still reads as intentional.
- **Large-format porcelain tile in the ~3,000 RUB/m² range was specifically recommended over cheaper budget tile after an extensive comparison shopping process** (weeks spent across online retailers, building-material markets, and trade shows) — the client's stated experience was that every tile within their original lower budget was disappointing, while stepping into a higher price tier ("a segment we hadn't even considered") produced a tile they were fully satisfied with. `single-account`, one project's budget-vs-satisfaction tradeoff, not a universal price floor.
