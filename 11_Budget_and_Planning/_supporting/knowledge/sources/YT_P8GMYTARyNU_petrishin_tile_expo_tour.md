## Genuinely Reusable Buying/Design Heuristics

1. **Rectified tile and the "zero-joint" red flag**: rectified
   ("ректифицированная"/"ректиф") tile has precisely cut edges that
   allow a genuinely minimal grout joint (**1-2mm**), which some
   clients specifically request — but **a claim that tile can be
   installed with literally zero joint is a red flag, not a real
   feature**; a joint is still functionally necessary even at minimal
   width, and an installer/salesperson claiming otherwise should be
   treated with suspicion.
2. **Small-format tile suits a small bathroom** — offered as a direct
   design heuristic given how many clients' own санузлы are small; a
   large-format tile is not automatically the better choice regardless
   of room size.
3. **Contrast grout as a deliberate design lever for geometric tile
   shapes** (worked example: a hexagon tile pattern): choosing a grout
   color that contrasts with the tile (rather than blending in) makes
   the underlying geometric pattern read more clearly — demonstrated on
   a white tile with turquoise contrasting grout.
4. **Metallic/shimmer-insert tile — use sparingly, not on a full
   surface**: decorative metallic or glitter-finish inserts exist and
   catch light attractively, but the explicit recommendation is to use
   them as accents (walls, small feature areas) rather than a full
   floor/wall surface, paired with deliberate lighting to enhance the
   shimmer — the surrounding room's own color gets subtly reflected
   back by these inserts.
5. **⚠️ Large-format porcelain slab delivery/access logistics
   caution**: very large-format porcelain slabs (an example given:
   240×120cm) may not physically fit through a standard stairwell or
   into a small elevator — building access must be checked *before*
   ordering a large-format product, not discovered on delivery day.
   Where standard access won't work, delivery through a window via
   crane (the same workaround already used for oversized drywall
   sheets) is the practical fallback for a lower-floor unit.
6. **Design-trend snapshot (2018, this source's own stated year-over-
   year observation)**: the prior year's tile-decor trend leaned bright/
   saturated colors; this year's trend has shifted toward calm,
   monotone/monochromatic, pastel combinations — offered as a
   perishable trend note, not a durable design rule. Subway-style
   "kabanchik" ("кабанчик") tile is noted as originally a metro-station
   design element later adopted for residential use — background/
   provenance detail, not a buying rule.
