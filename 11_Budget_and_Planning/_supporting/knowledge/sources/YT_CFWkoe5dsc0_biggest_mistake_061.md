## Planning Rules / Design Concept — Never Use Curved/Radius Wall Corners

- **Never use curved/radius-corner wall sections in an interior**, for four stated reasons:
  - **Fabrication difficulty and cost**: achieving a genuinely smooth radius surface with plaster/skim compound across the full wall height is labor-intensive; poorly executed radii are common and require repeated redoing, and the client pays for that difficulty either way.
  - **Trim/junction incompatibility**: standard trim products don't handle curves well — an aluminum stretch-ceiling baguette can't follow a small radius cleanly; even a flexible, heat-formable plastic baseboard never lies perfectly flush on a radius corner (a real cited example needed repeated reheating/reforming and still didn't sit right); a premium solid-wood baseboard is essentially impossible to fit to a radius at all.
  - **"Graphic rhyme" principle**: a single one-off curved element with no other curved elements anywhere else in the space reads as visually arbitrary and unjustified. A curved-corner motif needs to be deliberately repeated multiple times throughout the interior (at varying sizes) to read as an intentional design language rather than a random insertion.
  - **Space inefficiency**: a radiused corner wastes usable floor area, since virtually all furniture, fixtures, and appliances (washing machines, cabinetry, sanitary fixtures) are rectilinear or at most 45°-chamfered — nothing fits flush against a curved corner, especially costly in an already-small room (real example: a small WC), where the client pays a premium for the curve while making the room functionally less usable.
