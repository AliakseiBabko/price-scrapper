## Design Concept — The Named Color Harmonies ("цветовые гармонии"), with Interior Verdicts

All schemes are defined on the **цветовой круг**, with primaries red/blue/yellow at three equidistant points and secondaries orange/violet/green between them.

- **Prerequisite step before choosing any scheme**: pick the work's **main color ("основной цвет")** first — the one that will definitely be present and will most likely dominate. Scheme selection comes after that, not before.
- **Monochrome ("монохромная")** — one hue varied by brightness and saturation. **Explicitly not recommended for interiors**: such rooms "плохо воспринимаются человеком и могут вызывать ощущение психологического дискомфорта." Works well for drawing/illustration and clothing. *(This is a direct disagreement with three other sources in the same batch — see routing.)*
- **Complementary / contrast ("комплементарная", also "полярная"/"дополнительная")** — two hues opposite on the wheel. Called one of the most common and hardest-to-lose schemes; bright, high-contrast, energetic. **Interior verdict: use where formality and energy should be emphasized — living rooms named as the example.**
- **Analogous ("аналоговая")** — several neighboring hues on the wheel. Deceptively easy; the real difficulty is finding balance between hues that sit close together. **Named failure mode: if the analogous hues also share similar lightness and saturation, the result is monotonous and boring** — so the scheme has to be worked by deliberately varying those two parameters. **Prescribed structure: one dominant hue, one supporting hue, and a third as accent, which may be an achromatic (white, black, gray).** **Interior verdict: rooms where calm and coziness should dominate — bedrooms named.**
- **Analogous-complementary ("аналогово-комплементарная")** — an analogous palette plus one complementary accent, complementary to exactly one of the analogous hues rather than to the group. **Rule given: the complementary accent should be the opposite temperature to the analogous base** (cool base → warm accent, and vice versa).
- **Triad ("триада")** — three hues equidistant on the wheel (an equilateral triangle). Strong visual contrast while staying balanced; calmer than a two-color complementary pair. One of the three is nominated primary, two supporting. Triads built on the primaries are the loudest; those on the secondaries are more even. **Warning: don't add further hues to a triad — extend it with achromatics instead, or you get "цветовой хаос."**
- **Split-complementary ("раздельно-комплементарная")** — a base hue plus the two hues flanking its complement. Described as **the most universal and hardest-to-spoil scheme of the set**, giving complementary-grade contrast with more composure; "применять такую гармонию можно практически в любой области."
- **Tetrad ("тетрада")** — four hues, found on the wheel as a **square, a rectangle, or a trapezoid**.
  - *Square*: contains no analogous pairs and is hard to balance; workable only by nominating one dominant and three supporting. **Interior verdict: "в интерьере, прямо скажем, на любителя"** — an acquired taste; mostly used in art and animation, redundant for makeup, rare in clothing.
  - *Rectangle*: more variations and easier to balance than the square precisely because it contains analogous near-neighbor pairs; still needs a nominated dominant *pair* and supporting *pair*, or it reads unbalanced and excessive.
  - *Trapezoid*: intermediate — one analogous pair plus two hues far from that pair and from each other.
- **Beginner recommendation**: don't attempt tetrads; stay with complementary or split-complementary. A six-color harmony exists but is dismissed as too complex to be worth covering.
