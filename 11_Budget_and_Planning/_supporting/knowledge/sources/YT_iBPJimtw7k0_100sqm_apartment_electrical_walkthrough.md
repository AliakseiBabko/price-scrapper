## Durable Facts (Placement Rules of Thumb, by Room)

- **Entry hall**: main light controllable from 4 separate switch points (entry, exiting bedroom, entering living room, entering kids' room) — a "4-way control" pattern for a hallway serving multiple rooms.
- **Comms/server point**: incoming data/TV/coax cabling should terminate at one dedicated "server" location with its own extra outlet and the electrical panel co-located there; all incoming trunk cabling arrives here and distributes to all rooms from this single point.
- **Bedroom**: main light switch at entry, PLUS a two-way (проходной) switch at the bed so the light can be turned off from either side of the bed regardless of which side is slept on; 2 outlets on each side of the bed (phone charging + reading lamp); AC placed above/behind the headboard wall (not directly over the bed) so cooled air doesn't blow directly onto the sleeper; a "makeup corner" (dresser+mirror) gets 2 outlets (hairdryer + curling iron/styler) plus a dedicated switch for mirror backlighting; TV zone over/near the bed gets 2 outlets + coax + Cat cable + DVI, for a receiver/game console; reserve (unused-by-default) outlets inside the closet, for future closet lighting or an electrical appliance.
- **Bathroom (совмещённый санузел, combined w/ WC in this layout)**: light switch near the entry; separately, a heated towel rail connection, washing machine outlet, an extra reserve outlet, and a dedicated switch for the extractor fan.
- **Living room**: TV zone gets standard outlet + coax + Cat cable; behind drywall (hidden/future-use) an additional 4 outlets are run for receiver/game console/other future AV gear; a line-out for a stereo/speaker system; **2 extra outlets on each side of the TV zone specifically sized for holiday decorations** — reasoning given: a corner Christmas tree placement risks being knocked over by guests, so outlets are duplicated on both sides to allow flexible tree placement.
- **Kitchen entry**: switch to control the living room light when leaving the kitchen (cross-room switch convenience pattern, same idea as the hallway 4-way switch above); extra outlets in the window-slope/reveal area (откосы) — the speaker notes he is a strong proponent of slope-mounted outlets generally (see companion video `P7_rUkk8clU` for the full rationale).
- **Loggia/balcony**: dedicated light switch for the loggia; separate switch for a floor-heating regulator; an extra outlet for a work desk/hobby area planned there.
- **Kids' room**: main light switch positioned so the door handle is opposite the switch hand (ergonomic mirroring — if the handle is on the right when entering, the switch sits to the left); a second switch reachable from the bed to turn off the main light without getting up; a dedicated outlet by the bed; a data (twisted-pair) drop by the bed; a closet-entry switch plus 2 reserve outlets inside the closet; TV zone opposite the sleeping area gets standard outlet+coax+data+extra outlet; a "study" desk zone gets outlets for a desktop PC and a laptop, plus window-slope outlets; AC outlet included.
- **Cross-room switch pattern (repeated across the layout)**: multiple rooms get switches for adjacent/downstream rooms so a resident leaving one room toward another can control lighting ahead of or behind them without walking back — explicitly called out by the speaker as a deliberate, repeated design pattern, not a one-off.
- **Bathroom/WC extractor fan switch placement**: positioned to be reachable both from a seated position on the toilet and while standing at the sink — a specific ergonomic placement rule.
- **Underfloor-heating regulator height**: placed at a "comfortable reach" height near the sink area (no numeric height given in transcript).
- **Sink/vanity electrical points**: 2 outlets near the sink for hairdryer/styling tools, plus a dedicated switch for mirror backlighting (independent of the main bathroom light switch).
