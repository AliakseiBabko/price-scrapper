## Materials / Rules Heuristics — New Facts

- **⚠️ Two-component waterproofing membrane degrades from application-
  time heat, not just service-life sun exposure**: applying two-
  component waterproofing during direct midday sun in a hot climate
  caused visible failure/crumbling on this project; switching application
  to evening/night hours (cooler surface temperature) fixed the issue.
  Practical rule for any hot-climate exterior waterproofing job: schedule
  the actual application for evening/night, not midday.
- **Reduce mechanical-support (opora) count before waterproofing exterior
  equipment mounts**: a previous, lower-skill crew had installed far more
  roof-mounted equipment support posts than structurally needed
  (including several clustered under one cable tray, "like pouring a
  foundation"), which also made it physically impossible to properly
  wrap waterproofing around each post near a wall. Removing all but the
  structurally necessary supports made proper waterproofing wrap-around
  physically possible again — a real "reduce clutter before you can even
  waterproof properly" sequencing lesson.
- **⚠️ Every joint/corner (примыкание) must be sealed — zero exceptions,
  no matter how minor-looking**: a single missed joint (a real large gap
  in a wall found on this project) let water pour directly into the
  building, defeating an otherwise complete waterproofing job. Reinforcing
  tape plus mesh must be used at every corner, tripled-up
  ("на три раза") specifically at corners, since corners are
  disproportionately the highest-failure-risk points.
- **⚠️ Water-ponding flood test methodology, with a specific verification
  trick**: after waterproofing, flood the entire roof surface with water
  and place a marked bottle/gauge with a visible water-level line in it
  as a reference, accounting for expected evaporation loss at the day's
  temperature (checkable via reference tables) — if the roof's water
  level matches the expected-evaporation-only line after a multi-day hold
  (3 days on this project) with no unexplained drop, there's no leak.
  **Explicitly rejects "no visible leak inside the house" as a reliable
  test** — water can be leaking behind a piece of furniture undetected
  for a year before black mold appears; only the flood/gauge test is
  treated as reliable evidence.
- **Cable/conduit routing through a rain-exposed tray is a common,
  serious failure point**: open corrugated conduit inside a rain-exposed
  cable tray let water flow directly into the electrical panel on a
  previous installation (already-failed once before this project); the
  prior "fix" was crudely breaking and foam-sealing the conduits, called
  out explicitly as a bad practice ("никогда так не делайте"). **Real
  fix applied**: fully silicone/waterproof-seal the tray joints per
  standard technique (stated as no more inherently dangerous than any
  ordinary household electrical routing, if done correctly), plus drill
  drainage weep-holes in the bottom of every cable tray as a backup so
  any water that does get in drains back out rather than pooling, plus
  swap standard bolts for waterproof bolts with pressure/compression
  washers throughout.
- **Roof drain (воронка) sizing is easy to under-provision**: a roof with
  only one small drain funnel for its entire catchment area was flagged
  as clearly insufficient capacity, though the source notes uncertainty
  about being permitted to add more drains after the fact in this
  specific jurisdiction/building.
- **⚠️ Termination-to-wall convention named as a standard Russian
  practice, apparently not followed by default in this Dubai market**:
  waterproofing membrane should always be run up and terminated against
  the adjoining wall, not stopped at the floor plane — stated as always
  done this way across the source's Russian projects, but reportedly
  routinely skipped in the observed Dubai market. This upward termination
  is also what makes a flood/ponding test (above) physically possible
  at all, since it lets water be held at a raised level (10cm on this
  project, held 3 days) without simply running off the roof edge.
- **Real material/cost data point (non-comparable currency, recorded as
  context only per this project's location/currency rules)**: decorative
  ballast layer over roof waterproofing — two-tier gravel (coarse layer
  first, then a fine decorative layer on top) imported from Vietnam,
  ≈200 AED (≈500 RUB equivalent stated directly by the source, exact
  conversion basis/date not given) per bag. **Not comparable to this
  project's Russia/Belarus market** — recorded as a data point only, per
  this project's location+year comparability rule.
- **Parapet/facade-edge overhang detail to prevent water ingress behind
  insulated stucco**: a flush (no-overhang) roof-edge/parapet profile
  with a stuccoed-over mineral-wool insulation layer lets rainwater run
  directly down the facade face and seep behind the stucco into the wool
  insulation, visibly cracking/detaching the stucco over time (shown via
  two neighboring buildings with this exact failure already visible).
  **Fix applied on this project**: add a small overhanging tile/edge
  detail so water sheds directly off the roof edge onto the ground
  instead of running down the face, plus remove a problematic recessed
  reveal/profile at the roofline that was independently found to trap
  water with "no chance" of it draining.
- **⚠️ "Waterproofing is a systemic, all-or-nothing task" — explicit
  practitioner framing with a real consequence**: a single missed
  penetration point (a flower-bed drainage/electrical conduit
  penetration under a kitchen area, in this case) caused a full-height
  leak reported by the client as "still leaking everywhere" despite an
  otherwise complete waterproofing job elsewhere on the same structure —
  and a small missed leak point can be effectively impossible to locate
  after the fact, sometimes requiring redoing the entire roof rather than
  patch-finding.
