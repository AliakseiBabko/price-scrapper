## Genuinely New Content

**⚠️ Tempered vs. laminated ("triplex") safety glass — new topic, no
prior coverage on this store despite substantial existing shower-glass/
partition content**: tempered glass shatters into small, relatively
blunt fragments when it breaks but can still cause injury; laminated/
triplex glass (two glass panes bonded around a protective interlayer
film, the same construction as automotive windshield glass) shatters
*within* the film layer without dispersing fragments at all, the safer
option. **Application scope named**: shower-enclosure glass, glass
partitions, and glass railings/guards — recommends triplex over
tempered wherever the glass is in a genuinely high-contact or fall-risk
location, acknowledging the real cost premium as worth it.

**⚠️ Furniture anti-tip wall-anchoring for dressers/shelving units — new
safety topic, no prior coverage on this store's kids/pets or furniture
content**: any freestanding dresser, bookshelf, or cabinet-type unit
should be securely fastened to the wall, since tipping such a unit over
is described as easy for a child, a pet, or an adult leaning on it —
framed as a real injury-prevention measure, not just furniture-damage
prevention.

**Named upholstery fabric durability ranking — a concrete, named-
material complement to this store's existing Martindale-abrasion-index
rule**: for durability specifically, the source ranks **flock (флок),
microfiber, micro-suede (faux suede), and furniture velour** as the
most wear-resistant upholstery options — a concrete named-fabric-type
list this store's existing sofa-durability content (built around the
abstract Martindale index and colorfastness properties) doesn't itself
provide. Also restates (not new): avoid genuine eco-leather entirely,
since it cracks and peels with normal use over time, especially with
pets in the household; avoid solid-wood furniture legs specifically
because pets chew them; prefer furniture with removable, washable
slipcovers on both the main piece and cushions; prefer simple
rectangular furniture shapes over complex curved ones for cheaper
future reupholstering.

**⚠️ Genuine stainless-steel requirement for bathroom metal storage
systems — extends this round's own `oDHSbp6QRRE` finding (ready-made
metal shelf units as a bathroom-shelving alternative) with a real
material-quality caution**: two visually-identical metal shelving
product lines are commonly sold on marketplaces — one made of plain
steel (cheaper), one of genuine stainless steel (more expensive). In a
wet zone specifically, the plain-steel version will rust, especially at
welds/joints, **even with a quality powder-coat or primer finish** — the
coating doesn't reliably prevent this. Only genuine stainless steel is
safe to use in a bathroom's wet zone; either type is fine in a dry
storage room or walk-in closet.

**Mirror placement caution in narrow corridors — new topic**: avoid
hanging a mirror in a narrow hallway/corridor specifically, since it's
easily struck and broken while carrying furniture, boxes, or other bulky
items through — recommends mounting the mirror on a door instead (e.g.
the entry door) as a safer placement that avoids this specific
carrying-collision risk.

**Heavy glass-globe pendant light fixtures — new topic, no prior
coverage on this store's lighting-safety content**: glass-orb pendant
fixtures (commonly hung over a nightstand in a bedroom or over a sink in
a bathroom) are flagged as a real injury/breakage risk — easily
knocked and broken by an arm reaching for a phone at night, and some
models are notably heavy (a real project example cited), compounding
both the breakage risk and the injury risk from a falling fixture.
Recommends avoiding pendant glass-globe fixtures in these two specific
locations.
