## Doors / Trim

- **Protect an entrance door's hinges, latch/lock mechanism, and handle mechanism from dust ingress during rough construction work** — mask/cover the mechanism gaps, not just the door face.
- **Door hinge-side placement rule, restated for completeness across videos**: never place hinges on the side forcing occupants to squeeze past a narrowed passage, and never place hinges so the door strikes a neighbor's door when opened.

## Entrance / Hallway / Furniture

- **Splitting one large developer entry hall into two separate smaller zones has two benefits**: (1) lets flooring material differ per zone (e.g. continuous quartz-vinyl/laminate through most of the apartment, with a separately-tiled ceramic zone where wanted); (2) creates room for a large double-door built-in wardrobe (all outerwear/shoes) plus a fast-access changing area with a makeup-mirror zone.
- **Widen a corridor adjacent to a bathroom/WC pair to eliminate a door-swing collision risk** — a narrow corridor means someone walking through can be struck by a bathroom/WC door swinging outward; widening removes the risk and typically adds bonus storage capacity (a niche/shelf).
- **Renovation debris volume is roughly comparable between a new-build and an existing (secondary) unit** — the common client assumption that new-builds generate dramatically less trash is inaccurate; the real difference is limited to demolition-debris volume specifically, not overall job-site waste. `single-account`, practitioner observation.

## Kitchen / Living Room

- **A moderate (not maximum-width) opening between a combined kitchen and living area is sufficient** — reject the assumption that the widest possible opening is needed; only enough width is required for comfortable passage. Recommends leaving a durable, structurally solid pier/partition section at the opening specifically because occupants inevitably improvise it into an informal bar-counter/leaning surface over time — a flimsy partition there won't hold up under that repeated informal load.

## Materials / Walls / Ceilings

- **Radiators should generally be recessed/concealed, but this doesn't always require a full false wall** — sometimes recessing directly into the existing wall with only minor trimming of the lower/side plumbing supply routing is sufficient, cheaper and simpler than always building out a false wall. Stated as "no downside" when structurally feasible.
- **Reinforce (frame in) the wall section above any planned curtain-cornice mounting point in advance**, even if the cornice isn't installed immediately — otherwise the ceiling drywall risks sagging/failing later under the hardware's weight if a client requests one installed after the fact with no pre-planned backing.
- **Size a decorative niche's depth to closely match its actual intended contents** (e.g. ~2-3cm clearance) rather than building it needlessly deep — a real example of a rounded/curved-profile niche built with excess depth consumed 5-6 bags of plaster (paid by the client) beyond what was functionally needed.

## Loggia / Balcony

- **Split a long, awkward L-shaped/corner loggia into two zones with a dividing partition** so furniture can be placed against walls on both sides — reliably useful whenever the loggia has two separate exterior access doors (make one small room per access point); with only one access point, still worth sectioning off a portion as a dedicated small storage/utility nook rather than leaving the whole space uselessly long and thin.

## Quality Control / Technique

- **Verify a corner is genuinely 90° with a laser square before proceeding with a built-in kitchen install** — catching an out-of-square corner during rough work is cheap to fix; discovering it only after kitchen cabinetry installation means a full, expensive redo.
- **Plastering precision spec, with cost rationale**: acceptable deviation from a straightedge should be at most ~5mm, ideally 1-3mm. Plaster costs roughly twice as much as base filler/skim compound because reaching that tolerance often needs two full passes — the presenter flags a common bad practice of using a heavy layer of finishing filler to paper over sloppy plastering rather than plastering to the correct tolerance in the first place. `confirmed`, explicit numeric tolerance and cost rationale.

## Planning Rules / Project Management

- **Provide (or contractually require) proper hot-water shower facilities for on-site crew living at the jobsite** — corroborates/extends the temporary-shower-cabin practice already noted in this batch's companion source (`yt_WBaKEl5HIzU`); presenter frames good worker hygiene facilities as a baseline responsibility, not optional.
