## Rules / Heuristics — Genuinely New Content

- **Furniture-facade (мебельные фасады) plumbing-box access, a rising trend replacing the tiled hidden hatch**: pros — visually seamless (no visible hatch seam), matches other cabinetry material in the apartment, and can be built much larger (wider/taller) than a tiled hatch, which is deliberately size-limited (an oversized tiled hatch can sag/loosen and jam) — explicitly recommended when a storage-tank water heater sits behind it and needs generous access. Con — the failure point is the panel's edge-banding: once it delaminates even slightly, water/humidity reaches the panel core and it swells/disfigures at that seam; the same happens if the panel face itself is mechanically damaged down to the core. Routed to [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Structure_and_Framing]].
- **Concealed curtain mount recessed into a stretch ceiling** — a rising trend hiding the curtain rod/hooks inside a built niche in the ceiling directly above the tub, for a cleaner visual junction. The source's own explicit opinion: he doesn't like the look, calling it a real downgrade versus a glass partition. Routed to [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Bathtub_and_Shower]].
- **"Warm wall" towel-drying substitute — a second lost function beyond drying speed, not previously recorded**: separately from the existing "towel doesn't fully dry" finding, a warm wall also fails to remove excess ambient humidity from the room's air, unlike an electric towel warmer — the wall's heat output is too weak to meaningfully condition the surrounding air. Routed to [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Bathtub_and_Shower]] as an addition to the existing warm-wall entry.
- **Hidden-shelf material choice has a real installation-timing consequence, not previously recorded**: a tiled niche/shelf must be planned during design/tiling — can't be retrofitted. Glass and metal hidden-mount shelving can both be added at any point after the renovation is finished, including 5-7+ years later. Metal specifically is a newly-rising third option (alongside tile and glass) due to marketplace/local-manufacturer growth. Routed to [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Shelving_and_Furniture]].
- **Rounded corners on a tiled access hatch — a small, low-cost trend**: purely aesthetic (a "livelier"/cozier feel); added labor cost is explicitly minor, just more careful tile-cutting — distinct from the page's existing, much costlier full injury-safe rounded-corner grouting technique. Routed to [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Structure_and_Framing]].
- **Rounded/curved walls, bathroom-adjacent trend**: poor fit for small bathrooms, rising in large private-house/free-plan bathrooms; removes corner harshness at a corner-facing entry but meaningfully complicates plastering/painting/finishing, and rules out tile entirely (mosaic or roller/brush-applied finish only). Routed to [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Decor_and_Finish_Selection_Technique]] (general design/finish technique, not this project's own decision).
- **Microcement (микроцемент) as a wall/floor finish — new material, new to the store**: fully monolithic/seamless (no grout lines), water-resistant contingent on the correct topcoat, minimalist look, wide texture/color range; requires a highly skilled specialist (base coat or topcoat error both defeat the claimed properties), expensive in material and labor, less impact-resistant than porcelain tile. Routed to [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Decor_and_Finish_Selection_Technique]].
- **Large-format + small-format tile combination — a 2025-2026 trend**: pairing a large/medium-large tile (e.g. 80×120cm) with a small-format tile or mosaic in the same room for a deliberate contrast — cited rationale: adds volume/dynamism and visual zoning even in a small room. No stated technical downside, only ordinary taste. Routed to [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Tile_Selection_and_Layout]].
