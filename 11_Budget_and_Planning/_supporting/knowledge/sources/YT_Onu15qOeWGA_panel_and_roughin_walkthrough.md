## Durable Facts

- **Route electrical above plumbing (on the ceiling, not the floor)**: plumbing conventionally runs along the floor; if electrical conduit also runs along the floor, the two conduit "sleeves" (each ~6 cm thick) stack where they cross, forcing extra screed thickness. Near an entry-door threshold that is already tight (~7 cm in the example), stacked conduits can leave only ~1 cm of screed above them — thin enough to crack underfoot. `unverified`, single-account, but a coherent physical mechanism, not just an opinion.
- **Voltage-control relay (РКН/реле контроля напряжения) recommended as a standard component in every distribution panel**, regardless of apartment size — presented as a baseline safety element, not an upsell. `unverified`, single-account.
- **Group utility cabinets (electrical + plumbing) so they share one technical-cabinet section** and minimize unused space between them — a layout/planning heuristic. `unverified`, single-account.
- **Conduit (gofra) clearance from the wall**: keep the nearest conduit run at least 15 cm from the wall, ideally 20–25 cm. Reasoning given: conduit routed too close to a corner is harder to install (especially with direct-mount tools like gas-powered nail guns), and it interferes with mounting the stretch-ceiling perimeter baguette. `unverified`, single-account.
- **Conduit color-coding for readability**: this speaker's own practice is black conduit for power cables, red for lighting and low-voltage/data cable, to keep the finished rough-in visually legible. `unverified`, stated as the speaker's personal/company convention, not a code requirement — and the on-camera master (Nikiforov) uses plain grey instead and gives his own reason (avoiding customer arguments about conduit flammability), so this is explicitly **not a universal rule even within this one channel**.
- **PND (polyethylene) conduit flammability claim**: speaker argues the corrugated conduit is less flammable than wallpaper, doors, or laminate flooring, and dismisses "the conduit melts and drips on you" as fear-based sales talk. `unverified`, anecdotal opinion, no test data cited — preserve as a claim, not a fact.
- **Paint bare concrete ceiling (black or another opaque/dark color) before installing a stretch ceiling**: purpose is so that any dust, debris, or loose concrete fragments that later detach from the raw ceiling don't visibly show through against the underside of the stretch-ceiling membrane. `unverified`, single-account, but a plausible mechanism.
- **Socket-box (podrozetnik) cutouts should be sized precisely to the box** so only a thin skim of alabaster/gypsum plaster is needed to seat it, rather than a loose oversized hole packed with plaster. Achieved with a dust-extracting wall saw for a clean round cut; an angle grinder can achieve comparable results only with a skilled operator and quality blade. `unverified`, single-account, presented as a personal/school-of-practice preference (transcript notes other masters hold the opposite view).
- **Run a cable channel between stacked outlets behind a wall-mounted TV location** at rough-in stage, so an HDMI (or similar) cable can later be routed from the TV down to an AV device on a stand below without hanging visibly. `unverified`, single-account — consistent with the more detailed treatment in a separate video from this channel (`_wWteDpfIso`) on the same topic.
- **Where a wall will later receive added soundproofing panel buildup, socket boxes must be recessed deeper into the wall by the future soundproofing thickness at rough-in time**, so that once the sound-insulation layer and panel are applied, the boxes finish flush with the new surface. This must be decided at the design stage — not something that can be corrected after the fact without cutting into finished work. `unverified`, single-account.
- **Wireless leak sensors preferred over wired**: cited advantage is that a sensor can be relocated (mounted via double-sided tape) if its initial placement produces false positives or turns out not to be where a leak would actually accumulate — a wired sensor's position can't be changed without redoing work. `unverified`, single-account.
- **Add lighting inside the utility/plumbing cabinet** so leaks can be found and worked on without relying on a phone flashlight. `unverified`, single-account.
- **Install a pressure reducer at the entry point in every new-build apartment**, adjusted via a hex key and checked against a gauge — described as something the speaker "never" skips. `unverified`, single-account; this is a plumbing-adjacent fact included here because it was raised in the same utility-cabinet walkthrough.
