## Walls / Ceilings

- **Full drywall-joint-to-wallpaper-ready preparation sequence** (multi-day process — explicitly stated that prepping even a single wall for wallpaper cannot be finished in one day, since each stage requires full material drying time before the next):
  1. **Prime the floor along the wall base first**, specifically so dust doesn't kick up from the floor onto the wall during the joint-filling step — floor dust landing on wet filler leaves a long visible groove/streak. Floor is then protected with film to catch putty drips before wall work starts.
  2. **Prime every drywall-to-drywall joint and every screw head individually** before filling — stated purpose: (a) improves adhesion between drywall and filler, (b) protects screws from moisture/corrosion, since an unprimed screw will eventually show through the finished filler as a rust stain. Also prime the area around each outlet box (подрозетник) and all joints.
  3. **Foam-plug (пеноплекс) insert placed inside each outlet box before filling**, specifically to keep the box interior clean/unfilled — `confirmed`, single-account, a specific reusable technique for keeping outlet boxes clean through multiple wet trades.
  4. **Gypsum-based joint filler mix ratio: 2 parts dry mix to 1 part water**, water poured first then mix added, mix left briefly to absorb the water before stirring.
  5. **Mix only in small batches (quarter-bucket at a time, not a full bucket)** — the stated reason is the mix's working/open time before it starts losing workability; with two workers filling in parallel, batches need to be even smaller/more frequent than with one worker.
  6. Fill drywall joints and screw depressions with filler in two passes ("in two movements"), smoothing with a putty knife; fill joint-to-outlet-box gaps too.
  7. **Clean putty knives before every subsequent stage** — stated as "a matter of principle": dried filler crumbs left on a knife will scratch the wall if not removed.
  8. **Joints must be primed again before taping** (a second priming pass, after the first fill has dried and been cleaned/dust-removed) — explicit stated failure mode if skipped: tape applied over dust rather than a primed surface **will eventually come loose, later, together with the filler layer over it — requiring the entire area to be redone**. `confirmed`, explicit stated consequence, genuinely reusable QC rule.
  9. **Paper reinforcing tape technique for joints**: cut vertical tape lengths first (measured to ceiling height, cut with margin), mix a thinner/wetter filler batch specifically for the tape layer (more water than the joint-fill batch, so the layer stays thin and the tape fully saturates), apply filler along the full joint height, then draw a **notched/toothed trowel** through the wet filler to leave exactly enough material to bond the tape without squeezing out excess from underneath once pressed.
  10. **Soak each cut length of tape in a bucket of clean water for about one minute before applying** — stated reason: a wet tape absorbs the underlying filler better than a dry one, improving bond.
  11. Apply the soaked tape, press and smooth with a putty knife until it becomes visually integrated with the wall ("becomes one with the wall").
  12. **If a bubble/fold forms in the applied tape, never try to flatten it in place** — the corrective technique is to lift the tape at that point and re-lay it, not press through the fold.
  13. Horizontal joints and joints around outlet boxes are taped after all vertical joints — dimensions/lengths are pre-measured and marked on the floor, tape pre-cut for the needed pieces to speed the work; horizontal tape is applied **overlapping onto the vertical tape (not the reverse)**, deliberately, so that once the filler sets, the small overlap excess can be trimmed cleanly later.
  14. After the taping layer sets, **trim tape overlaps with a utility knife using a putty knife as a straightedge guide**, deliberately not pressing hard with the blade so as not to cut through the drywall's paper face.
  15. **Sand only the taped-joint areas at this stage** (not the whole wall) using a sanding block/pad, protective mask and goggles — stated to go roughly 3x faster with three people working the same wall.
  16. **Vacuum the wall and the electrical cable specifically** after every dust-generating stage (sanding, in this case) — dust on a cable is called out explicitly as a contamination risk for the next wet trade.
  17. **Prime the whole wall again before the first full skim coat** — stated rationale: "we don't skimp on a couple of glasses of primer" because it ensures dust is fully bound down and the filler will grip the wall and not eventually fall off.
  18. **Mechanized skim-coat application via a dedicated putty/plaster spraying station ("шпаклёвочная станция")**, described as the company's increasingly standard method for fast, even, thin-layer application versus hand-troweling — before use, the mixed filler is thoroughly stirred, the pump hose is fully purged of air ("so it doesn't sputter/hiccup and apply unevenly"), then the station is loaded and spraying begins. **Two-person technique**: one person sprays while a second continuously tops up the hopper so the pump doesn't run dry and start drawing air ("choking/sneezing").
  19. **First skim coat applied and leveled in horizontal thirds of the wall (top, middle, bottom)**, each third leveled with a straightedge/wide knife immediately after spraying, working horizontally then vertically to a fully flat surface, with special care to blend/erase the seam between adjacent thirds so no visible line remains; outlet boxes are skipped over (protected by the earlier foam plug).
  20. **Grazing-light inspection lamp ("лампа Лосева" — a raking/side light on a low stand)** used throughout sanding to reveal even the smallest surface defects — during quality checks, holding a straightedge against the wall under this light will show visible light rays leaking under the straightedge wherever sanding is inadequate — a direct, checkable QC method.
  21. **Sand only with fine-grit paper at low RPM for the finish coat**, explicitly warned against using coarse grit (would strip the whole thin finish layer) or high speed; change the sanding disc as soon as it clogs — a clogged/worn disc both fails to cut and can tear a groove into the surface that then needs re-filling, re-leveling, re-drying, and re-priming — described as a real time/labor cost if skipped.
  22. **Second (finish) skim coat applied with a softer, more flexible trowel** specifically suited to thin-layer leveling, aiming for a near-glass-smooth surface; each newly worked section is blended into the previously finished section as work proceeds.
  23. **Final priming pass before wallpapering, with an explicit stated purpose distinct from the earlier priming steps**: without this final primer, wallpaper paste would be absorbed too quickly into the filler layer, leaving no time to adjust/align seams while hanging paper — the final primer is specifically to slow paste absorption and preserve working time for seam alignment. `confirmed`, explicit stated mechanism, genuinely new and specific technique for this store's wallpaper-prep content.
