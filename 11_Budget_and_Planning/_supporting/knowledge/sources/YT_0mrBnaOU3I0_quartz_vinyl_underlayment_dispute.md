## Flooring — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Real cited defect mechanism**: the design recommended quartz-vinyl flooring; the client bought a different product recommended in-store by the retailer's salesperson (something between quartz-vinyl and PVC tile), plus a matching underlayment also recommended by the same salesperson. Within ~1 month of occupancy, the client observed end-joint separation across the flooring — worsening over roughly 3 months, eventually affecting an estimated 60-70% of joints, concentrated in a hallway (10 of 13 joints counted there specifically separated).
- **The retailer's and distributor's first-line explanation, per the client — flagged as false**: they initially claimed the defect was caused by installing the flooring as one continuous unbroken run ("единым контуром") without transition thresholds, calling this a hard rule (must always break a continuous run with thresholds). The client states this directly contradicts what he was told by Zemskov's own design technologists *before* purchase — that a continuous run without thresholds is fine specifically for quartz-vinyl up to a stated coverage limit. **Zemskov frames the retailer's threshold explanation as a diversionary claim, not the real cause.**
- **The actual root cause, per an independent manufacturer inspection**: after the client escalated in writing to the retailer, the distributor, and finally the flooring manufacturer directly, a manufacturer representative visited the apartment, inspected the installation, and found the underlayment was the real defect — the batch used was too soft (3mm) and should have been a firmer, thinner product (≤2mm) for this specific flooring type. The client describes being able to feel the floor "give"/cushion underfoot in a way it shouldn't ("должен лежать мертвым, как на бетоне" — should sit dead, like on concrete), which he treats as a tactile confirmation of the underlayment defect independent of the manufacturer's own finding.
- **Practical rule this case supports**: an underlayment product must be matched to the specific flooring type's own firmness/thickness spec, not just "a compatible-sounding underlayment" recommended informally by a retail salesperson at time of purchase — an overly soft/thick underlayment can cause visible joint separation over time, not just a comfort difference.
