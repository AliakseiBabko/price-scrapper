### Walls / Ceilings

- **Drywall-profile positioning-drift problem and hot-glue tack fix, a genuinely reusable precision technique**: when screwing a metal drywall track profile to a wall/ceiling along a marked reference line, a hammer-drill bit can wander sideways on encountering aggregate/debris in the substrate, nudging the profile off the line by a few millimeters before the fastener is even driven — a bigger problem for an inexperienced/home installer than an experienced tradesperson with a practiced, steady hand. **Fix**: after positioning the profile exactly on the marked line (using shims/wedges if needed), tack it in place first with a dab of hot-melt glue through the fastener hole (or against the profile's back face on the side with no drywall/dowel hole) — this holds the profile perfectly still while drilling the actual fastener holes, after which it can no longer drift. `confirmed`, checkable technique, directly generalizable to any profile/track installation where precision matters more than the installer's own steadiness.
- **Wall-hung-toilet frame ("инсталляция") leveling-nut position-memory trick**: after leveling and setting an installation frame's adjustment nut to the correct height, fix that nut's position with a small dab of hot-melt glue — if the frame is later removed and reinstalled (e.g. during other work), the nut stays at its already-correct setting rather than needing to be releveled from scratch; to intentionally readjust later, simply peel off the glue and turn the nut. `confirmed`, technique detail, minor but genuinely reusable for any threaded height-adjustment mechanism revisited multiple times during a renovation.
