## Lighting — Current Trend: Track Lighting as the Lead Fixture Type

Nikita Kuznetsov (designer) reports: **track lighting is currently the most on-trend fixture type**, credited to a clean/minimal aesthetic — black, geometric forms (square, rectangular, L-shaped) with no curves — combined with a wide range of attachable fixture heads (different beam shapes, different appearances) that can be freely added/swapped on the same rail. Expected to remain a leading trend for a while yet. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]

- **⚠️ Wall-bounce technique, genuinely new placement idea**: track lighting is often mounted on the ceiling and aimed at a wall, or mounted on a wall and aimed upward/outward, so the light reaches the room indirectly by reflecting off the wall surface rather than shining directly downward — a deliberate indirect-lighting technique using track fixtures specifically (distinct from the general "background/ambient" category in the taxonomy video). Nikita Kuznetsov (designer). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Chandeliers haven't disappeared but have shifted role from main light source to decor/accent** — either as a large statement decorative piece, or as a smaller zonal pendant lighting a specific spot (a table, a piece of furniture) rather than the whole room. Nikita Kuznetsov (designer). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Point/spot fixture trend**: surface-mounted (накладные) point fixtures are now generally preferred over recessed for design projects; where recessed fixtures are still used, they trend toward larger units with adjustable beam direction rather than small fixed downlights. Nikita Kuznetsov (designer). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]

## Lighting — Current Trend: Linear Light Lines as a Track-Lighting Alternative

Nikita Kuznetsov (designer) reports: **linear "light line" fixtures (световые линии) are a current trend alternative to track lighting**, laid out flexibly — parallel runs, crossing patterns, or freeform arrangements. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]

- **⚠️ Overuse caution with a stated mechanism**: using too many light lines, or too powerful/bright a strip, over-illuminates the space and makes the room read as visually flat, defeating the sense of depth/volume a well-lit interior is supposed to achieve — the opposite of the intended effect. **General principle: fewer, deliberately-placed light elements preserve a room's perceived depth/volume better than many.** Nikita Kuznetsov (designer). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Where a light line transitions from ceiling onto a wall, balance its brightness so the wall-mounted segment doesn't glare into the eyes** — a specific installation-quality caution for this fixture type. Nikita Kuznetsov (designer). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]

## Lighting — Perimeter/Accent Lighting Guidance

- **Perimeter cove lighting (ceiling and floor level), shelf backlighting, and countertop backlighting are all good practice and should be used** — explicitly excludes colored LED accent lighting, which is called out as never having been stylish and still isn't. Nikita Kuznetsov (designer). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **⚠️ Wall-wash/perimeter lighting reveals every micro-imperfection in the wall surface — a real construction-sequencing implication.** If perimeter or wall-directed lighting is planned, the wall must be finished to a genuinely flat/even standard beforehand; this technique will visibly expose ordinary plastering imperfections that would otherwise go unnoticed under conventional lighting. Nikita Kuznetsov (designer). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]

## Lighting — Sconce (Bra) Nuance

- **⚠️ Bedside sconces are often positioned as "intimate"/mood lighting rather than genuine reading light, despite the reading-light framing** — because they're commonly mounted over the nightstand rather than directly over where a book/phone would actually be held while reading, they end up functionally decorative rather than task-effective for reading. This nuances (doesn't contradict) the existing directional-bedside-reading-light guidance: the fixture needs to be positioned and aimed at the actual reading zone, not just labeled a "reading sconce" by product category. **A sconce aimed at the wall itself (rather than outward into the room) adds visual depth/interest** and is called out as a good look distinct from the reading-function question. Nikita Kuznetsov (designer). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]

## Lighting — Color Temperature and Scenario-Count Nuances

- **Main/base light should be neutral; accent/decor lighting (LED strips behind 3D panels, cove lighting, etc.) can deliberately be warm-toned for coziness** — a specific pairing rule for combining color temperatures within one room, rather than the "everything must match" same-scene rule in the existing page content (this applies specifically to primary-vs-accent roles, not simultaneous same-function fixtures). Nikita Kuznetsov (designer). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Dim/muted light in select spots can meaningfully elevate an otherwise mediocre design** — achieved correctly not by dimming an overhead fixture to near-nothing, but by using an indirect/wall-reflected light source (see wall-bounce technique above) instead of direct overhead light. Nikita Kuznetsov (designer). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **⚠️ Explicit upper bound on scenario count, complementing the existing "minimum three scenarios" rule**: don't exceed roughly 3-4 lighting scenarios per zone — beyond that, the extra scenarios genuinely stop getting used day-to-day and only get set up for guests, defeating the purpose of scene-based lighting. Nikita Kuznetsov (designer). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
