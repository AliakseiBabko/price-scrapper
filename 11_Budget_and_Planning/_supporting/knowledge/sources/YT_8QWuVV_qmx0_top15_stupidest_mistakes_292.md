## Numeric / Ergonomic Rules

- **Electrical panel (щит) height standard: ~140cm from floor** — stated so even a shorter-than-average person can operate it in an emergency (presenter cites his own 178cm as "average" height for context).
- **Entry light-switch ergonomic placement: ~90cm from floor, ~15cm from the opening edge** (or 95cm if the household is notably tall) — positioned so it's found "blind" (without looking) on entering/leaving a room. Stated as this practitioner's own standard convention.
- **Windowsill overhang: correct depth is 40mm** — too little wastes usable width; too much (real example ~120mm) risks the sill cracking/bending under load, visibly worse on a glossy finish.
- **Entrance-door/interior-door trim material-matching rule**: entrance-door slopes/casing should be made from the same material family as the interior doors' own casing/jamb-extensions, even though entrance and interior doors are different classes — keeps the two visually coordinated.

## Mistakes / Warnings

- **Glossy windowsill finish on a loggia is a poor choice**: street dirt (loggias face outside air) shows worse on gloss, minor installation waviness reads more visibly under a loggia's typically strong light, and small scratches are highly visible — loggia windowsills should be matte.
- **Never use drywall boxing to fake a load-bearing element's geometry or to avoid straightening an irregular wall/ceiling** — real example: an entire fake "structural column" and a fake "beam" (with a visibly misaligned smoke detector poking through it) were built purely from drywall to avoid properly straightening the room.
- **Bedroom/dense-lighting-zone switch-to-fixture mapping should be intuitive and tested from the user's actual entry path** — a real example with 5 separate lighting circuits and mismatched switches took extensive trial-and-error to map; none of the 4 entry switches controlled the room's own niche backlighting, the single fixture most likely to be wanted.
- **Baseboard style must be consistent within a single room** — real example mixing four different baseboard treatments (tile-offcut-built, door-casing-offcut-built, thin tapered "designer" profile, thick casing-offcut profile) in one room.
- **A loggia countertop intended as a real workspace (not just decoration) must be built at proper desk height** — building it directly atop the developer's low windowsill height (rather than raising it to merge with a properly-heightened sill) leaves it too low to use comfortably.
- **Decorative niches are dust/dirt traps that are hard to clean**, worse when finished with real wood trim (harder to clean corners) — compounded in a real example by narrowing an already-tight doorway opening so two people can't pass.
- **Entrance-door casing must have a minimum ~3cm standoff/reveal from the wall on both sides** — a real defect had the casing on one side butted flush against the wall with zero reveal.
- **"Dirty zone" (грязная зона) near an entrance must be a genuine physically-distinct zone (different flooring material/color), not merely implied by a decorative element** (e.g. a mismatched-color floor tile plus a large mirror) — a decorative cue alone doesn't functionally contain dirt/mess from spreading further into the apartment.
- **Attached/merged-loggia flooring needs a raised podium** because insulating the developer's cold slab requires more buildup than a bare screed — a real example built the podium's threshold/step incorrectly (should extend flush to the vent-grille plane, but was left as an awkward two-step ledge instead).
- **Wall-hung ("подвесной") toilets are preferred over floor-mounted, especially in a small WC**: less floor footprint, easier to clean around/under (vs. floor-mounted, awkward to clean around), and height is adjustable at install time (floor-mounted height is fixed).
- **Decorative faux ceiling beams/battens (декоративные брусья) in an entryway are criticized on multiple counts**: outdated trend, expensive despite being non-structural, hard to clean in corners (explicitly bad for robot-vacuum reachability), and they block furniture placement against that wall.
- **A "massage"-type shower head (scalp-contact circulation function) must be mounted low enough to actually reach the head** — mounted too high (as if a standard overhead rain shower), it defeats its own stated purpose.
- **Avoid unnecessary new exterior/convex wall corners in a layout** — they reliably take impact/chip damage over normal use ("bump/wear") that flat runs and interior corners don't.
