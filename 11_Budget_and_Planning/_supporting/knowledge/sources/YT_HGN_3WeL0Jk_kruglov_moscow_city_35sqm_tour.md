## Rooms / Zones — Sliding-Partition Bedroom Carve-Out (the genuinely new content)

- **A sliding partition/door system is used to divide one open studio-style
  space into a separate visual bedroom zone**, in this real 35m² unit — the
  practitioner's own framing: putting a bed directly in the open living
  room "would look strange," so a sliding partition closes off a proper-
  looking bedroom on one side and leaves an "adequate" bed-free living room
  on the other, functioning both ways depending on which side the door is
  drawn to.
- **⚠️ Explicit acoustic caveat, stated directly and unprompted**: "no
  sliding partition/door will give you any soundproofing" — the practitioner
  is clear this is a purely visual/privacy division, not an acoustic one.
  **Directly relevant to this project's own Phase 2 glass-partition plan**
  (see this page's Household Plan section above) — confirms from an
  independent real case that a sliding divider of this type should not be
  assumed to isolate bedroom noise from the adjoining living/kitchen zone,
  reinforcing why this store's existing soundproofing/damper cautions for
  that divider (see [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Soundproofing|Soundproofing]])
  matter even though the door itself looks like a room boundary.
- **Minimalist bedroom-zone furnishing in this case**: nothing but the bed
  itself, a soft fabric wall panel used as a headboard substitute (no
  separate freestanding headboard piece), and the window view treated as
  the room's main remaining "feature" — thin but consistent with this
  page's existing textile-headboard note; not counted as a new fact on its
  own.
