## Doors / Trim — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Three interior door style categories**: Modern (contemporary but not sharp/futuristic), Hi-Tech (sharp straight lines, heavy glass use, futuristic), Classic (traditional appearance, modern construction). **Doors and furniture should be from "the same era" stylistically** — Hi-Tech doors clash with most furnished (non-minimalist) interiors, since Hi-Tech's sparse, sharp aesthetic only reads correctly in genuinely minimalist spaces. Classic is reserved for apartments with ceilings above ~3m; in typical apartments under ~200m² with standard ceiling heights, "imperial"-scale classic doors look visually out of place. **Modern is the practitioner's stated default.** `confirmed`, stated design principle.
- **Avoid raised door-panel moldings/protrusions** — described as an outdated design element that adds thickness for no functional benefit. `confirmed`.
- **Prefer rounded (not sharp) door-panel edge profiles** — sharp panel corners visibly collect dust over time. `confirmed`, concrete maintenance-driven rule.
- **Minimize glass area in interior door leaves** — glass materially worsens soundproofing; the source gives a blunt real-world example (a WC conversation/activity being fully audible through a glazed door). `confirmed`, corroborates this store's existing glass-door acoustic-leakage facts from other sources with a specific room example (WC).
- **Door surface material should be solid wood or better — avoid cheap laminated-paper-wrap ("присобанная бумага с ламинацией") finishes**, which peel/delaminate cheaply, especially where debris works its way under the laminate at edges. `confirmed`.
- **Light/white-colored doors should use a mottled or subtly textured finish, not a flat glossy white** — a flat light finish shows every fingerprint/handprint and dirt mark; a textured or patterned light finish hides marks far better. `confirmed`, concrete buying criterion.
- **Casing (наличник) miter-joint convention**: classic 45°-mitered corner joints are outdated for a modern-style door; modern doors should use straight (butt-style, non-mitered) casing joints, matching how the door leaf's own panel elements are framed. **Best-practice detail**: make the top casing piece slightly thinner than the two side pieces so it sits slightly recessed relative to them — this hides the top piece's cut end grain from view, considered the highest-quality finish detail. `confirmed`, a specific, checkable installation-quality signal.
- **Handle selection — grip test**: grab the handle firmly and quickly ~10 times in a row; if it's uncomfortable or causes pain/soreness at any point, reject that handle model — a practical, hands-on ergonomics screening method. Avoid gold/shiny-plated handles — the plating wears through at contact points, leaving visible bare-metal patches ("proplešiny") over time; prefer a silver-toned finish instead, which doesn't show this wear pattern. Avoid overly large/ornate handles that dominate visual attention; prefer simpler designs. `confirmed`, three distinct concrete selection criteria.
- **Hinge type by door style**: Hi-Tech doors should use only concealed hinges (visible hinges look wrong against the style); Classic doors should use only visible/traditional hinges (concealed hinges look incongruous against ornate classic hardware); Modern-style doors can use either. **Concealed-hinge caveat**: not all concealed-hinge models open a full 180° — if a door needs a wider-than-90° swing (the source's own example: a bathroom door that needs to open past 90°), this must be specified explicitly to the supplier/order manager in advance, since concealed hinges cost more and standard models may not support the needed swing angle. `confirmed`, a specific, easy-to-miss ordering requirement.
- **Bathroom door lock type should differ from a study/office door lock**: a bathroom door needs a lock that can be secured from inside but opened from outside in an emergency (e.g. with a coin or simple tool) — so someone who collapses inside can still be reached; a study/office door instead needs a proper keyed lock, since its purpose is to secure the room (and documents inside it) when the occupant leaves. `confirmed`, corroborates this store's existing bathroom-door-emergency-access theme from a different context, now specifically contrasted against office-door lock requirements.
- **Prefer "silent" magnetic door latches over standard spring-loaded tongue latches** — a magnetic latch has no protruding tongue mechanism; it engages only on closing (via magnetic attraction) and is described as more durable, with nothing to wear out mechanically over time. `confirmed`, specific hardware recommendation.
- **Entrance-door sequencing rule — buy the entrance door first, but only after interior door style is already decided.** The entrance door is purchased at the very start of a renovation, while interior doors are installed at the very end — yet the entrance door's interior-facing decorative trim panel, and the wall-reveal finishing around it (откосы, since the wall becomes thicker once the door frame is installed), must be chosen to match the interior doors', baseboard, and casing style. **Correct order**: choose the interior doors you want first → select matching baseboard and casing profile → only then choose the entrance door's interior trim panel to match all of it. `confirmed`, a specific, non-obvious project-sequencing rule — genuinely useful and easy to get backwards, since the entrance door is naturally the first door purchased chronologically.

## Regulations / Security — Entrance Door Hardware, Durable Facts & Rules

- **Entrance-door exterior finish should be vandal-resistant, not a wood-look/wood-veneer finish** — wood-look surfaces show permanent damage (cigarette burns, dents, scratches from bumps) with no way to repair it invisibly; a plain vandal-resistant surface avoids this risk entirely. `confirmed`.
- **Hinge count**: 2 or 3 acceptable; 3 preferred over 2 for a heavier entrance door.
- **A quality entrance door should have two locks**: the one operated by a small flat key must have an armored strike-plate cover (бронакладка) protecting it from cylinder-pulling/drilling attacks; the other lock should be a **re-pinnable type** — if keys are lost (e.g. in a building stairwell/entryway), a new key set (~2,000 RUB) can be bought, the door opened with the still-working old key, the cylinder's pins reset to match the new keys, and the lost keys immediately stop working — without replacing the lock body or damaging the door. `confirmed`, a specific, actionable security/cost rule with a real price figure.
- **Standard entrance-door kit**: peephole, two locks (per above), and a silver-toned handle (gold avoided for the same wear-pattern reason as interior door handles above).
- **Passive deadbolt pins (пассивные ригели)** — fixed pins built into the door leaf's hinge-side edge that engage into the frame when the door closes, always present (not activated by the lock). Purpose: prevent someone from cutting through the door on the hinge side (e.g. with an angle grinder) and swinging it open from that side even with the main lock secured. `confirmed`, security mechanism explanation.
- **Active deadbolt pins (активные ригели) — explicitly NOT recommended by this practitioner.** These are pins that extend into the frame's corners specifically when the lock is turned, intended to reinforce corner security. **Reasoning against them**: door-frame corners are already difficult to pry open regardless (not the actual weak point they're marketed to protect), and the mechanism has a documented failure mode — the linkage rod (тяга) connecting the lock to the pin can wear through friction against the door edge over repeated use and snap, causing the pin to drop and jam permanently in the extended position, locking the door shut and preventing normal opening. `confirmed`, real cited failure mechanism, a specific rationale for avoiding a feature that's often marketed as a security upgrade.
