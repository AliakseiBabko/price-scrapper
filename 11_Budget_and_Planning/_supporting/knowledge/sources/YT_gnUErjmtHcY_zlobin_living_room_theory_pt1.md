## Rooms / Zones — Living and Dining Room

- **Historical origin**: the modern living room descends from the
  European fireplace-hall tradition (two elements only — a fireplace as
  the attraction center, and soft seating/side tables around it); the
  fireplace is replaced today by a media/AV zone as the functional
  equivalent attraction point. Regional variants noted (French salon,
  German formal "cold" reception room, English hybrid living room
  combining reception + relaxation + music) — background context, not
  independently actionable.
- **⚠️ Dual-function default for a typical (non-palatial) house**: a
  living room almost always serves both guest reception *and* household
  relaxation in the same room — separating these into two dedicated
  rooms is realistically only affordable in very large houses (~1000m²
  cited). Directly relevant to sizing/zoning any moderate-size living
  room for both uses at once.
- **⚠️ Adjacency rule: route a study/office toward the living room, but
  route stairs and guest-bedroom doors *away* from it, into a
  distribution hall instead** — doors should face the hall, not open
  directly into the living space. Explicit caveat: for a genuinely small
  home (~150-170m²), this often isn't fully achievable and some
  compromise is expected.
- **⚠️ Pass-through/circulation living-room design, a named regional
  contrast**: American-style layouts are credited as the model for
  circular circulation — walkable all the way around a central soft-
  seating zone via multiple doorways to reach the kitchen-dining, hall,
  or entry — versus a traditional "dead-end" living room (source
  attributes the dead-end convention to Georgian/Victorian-era English
  design). Modern trend favors the pass-through model even in compact
  homes.
- **Bay window (эркер/"bow window") as a room-expansion technique**:
  historically a Victorian feature (English "bow window" term cited) —
  projects a rectangular room outward for extra space/light; the
  windowsill can double as a seating bench. Only relevant where the
  architectural style genuinely supports it.
- **⚠️ Floor-to-ceiling glazing ("windows to the floor"), a full design/
  engineering technique with real constraints, not just an aesthetic
  choice**: removing the sill/kneewall raises the natural-light
  coefficient and reads as a strong modern facade feature — **but if you
  do this, you must also carry the window nearly to the ceiling, not
  stop at a conventional ~1.8-2m height** — an incomplete-height "floor
  window" against a ≥3m ceiling looks visibly wrong. **Leave a ~40-50cm
  gap at the top** for ceiling finish, curtain-track hardware, and any
  supply/exhaust ducting routed under the slab — the window's actual
  final size follows from these two constraints, not from picking a
  number first.
- **⚠️ Heating consequence of floor-to-ceiling glazing**: full-height
  glazing carries genuinely severe heat loss and eliminates the
  sill-wall space where a standard radiator would normally sit — plan
  underfloor heating or in-screed convectors as the room's actual heat
  source in this case, not a wall radiator. Corroborates this vault's
  existing panoramic-glazing/bidirectional-radiator content
  ([[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Radiators_and_Convectors|Radiators and Convectors]])
  from an independent source and angle.
- **⚠️ Room-size minimums and proportions**: living room area **≥25-30m²**
  for a smaller private house, **≥40-50m²** for a larger one. **Ceiling
  height ≥3m minimum**; for a larger (40-50m²) living room, **≥3.5m clear
  height recommended** — with engineering/curtain allowances stacked on
  top, total structural floor-to-ceiling can reach **~4m**. **Avoid both
  extremes of proportion**: an overly elongated/narrow room (looks like a
  train car, forces furniture into one awkward long row, hard to zone
  well) and an overly *square* room (looks fine on paper but is
  genuinely hard to zone functionally despite looking "ideal").
- **⚠️ Seven-zone taxonomy for a living room's functional program** (plan
  for as many as apply, not necessarily all): (1) soft-seating zone, (2)
  media/AV zone, (3) fireplace zone (see the fireplace-specific content
  below), (4) library zone (mainly an older-generation preference today),
  (5) **workspace/desk zone — recommended almost always, even in a small
  house without a dedicated study**, specifically for quick incidental
  tasks (opening mail/parcels) that are awkward to do on a coffee table
  or by carrying items to a bedroom, (6) musical-instrument zone (larger
  homes, large decorative acoustic instruments valued as much for
  appearance as sound), (7) a **"contemplation zone"** — a deliberate
  visual focal feature distinct from the seating/media zones: a
  Soviet-era potted-plant corner as the traditional version, with modern
  equivalents named as a built-in wall aquarium, a lit antique statue,
  an art/collectible display case, a small indoor water feature/fountain,
  or a prominently lit piece of art.
- **⚠️ Double-height ("second light") caution**: can look dramatic but
  risks turning a genuinely small living room into a claustrophobic
  "well" — apply cautiously and check proportions first. Separately,
  tall second-light windows facing south or west create a real summer
  overheating risk.
- **⚠️ Doorway/opening sizing for a living room, a full tiered
  guideline**: a standard interior door is wrong for a spacious, bright
  living room. **Minimum 1m single-leaf opening** (bare floor for the
  most compact plans only); **average living room: a 1.2-1.3m
  double-leaf door**, one leaf (~30-40cm) permanently latched shut and
  the other (~90cm) the working leaf — sized to move large furniture
  through; **where the plan allows: a full double-leaf door at 1.5-1.6m**;
  **for a genuinely large living room, skip doors entirely and use a wide
  arch (1.5-2m)** instead, for better spatial flow — solid doors aren't
  always the right choice for a large room.

## Furniture / Built-ins — Arrangement Patterns and Real Sizing

- **⚠️ Five named furniture-arrangement patterns around a central
  attraction point (fireplace or media zone)**: (1) **one-sided** — a
  single sofa facing the point, coffee table in front; (2) **parallel
  two-sided** — two facing sofas (seats 6-7 people), or a sofa facing two
  armchairs across the coffee table; (3) **L-shaped (corner)** — two
  sofas meeting at a corner around the table, optionally with 1-2
  armchairs; (4) **U-shaped** — sofa + sofa/armchairs forming three sides
  around the table; (5) **curved/free-form** — non-rectilinear curved
  sofas around the table, a less standard configuration.
- **⚠️ Sizing figures for the arrangement patterns above**: standard large
  living-room sofa length **2.0-2.2m**; adding an armchair (L-shaped
  layout) grows the overall footprint to **~2.8-3m**; opposite-corner
  distance for a U-shaped layout ≈ **3m**; full U-shaped seating zone
  footprint ≈ **3×3m (~9m²)**. Adding circulation space and any secondary
  zones easily brings a genuinely comfortable living room to **30-35m²**
  total.
- **⚠️ Guest-reception seating clearances (parallel two-sided layout
  specifically)**: sofa depth **60-80cm**; **coffee-table-to-sofa gap
  20-30cm** (20cm for shorter people, 30cm for taller — leg-length
  variance is the stated reason); coffee table depth **≥40cm**; distance
  between two facing armchairs **2-3m**; armchairs pulled back **~0.5m**
  from the coffee table — enough for someone to walk in/serve without
  turning sideways.
- **⚠️ Wall/circulation clearance rules**: circulation path behind soft
  seating — **1.2m ideal, 1.1m acceptable, 1m absolute floor, never
  less**. Where the seating's back sits against a wall with a window,
  leave **minimum 20cm** from the wall (radiator + curtain clearance);
  where there's no window, leave **at least 10cm** (baseboard clearance
  so furniture isn't jammed flush against the wall).
- **⚠️ TV viewing-distance rule of thumb: 4× the screen's own diagonal** —
  the source explicitly prefers this self-scaling formula over a fixed
  meter figure (some sources cite 3-3.5m flat), since it automatically
  adjusts to whatever screen size is actually chosen.
- **⚠️ TV mounting-height caution**: don't wall-mount a large TV
  unnecessarily high just to "save floor space" — forces the viewer to
  crane their neck upward; keep the TV at eye level on a low console/
  stand instead for comfortable viewing.

## Regulations / Planning Rules — Fireplace Zone

- **⚠️ Large wood-burning fireplaces are increasingly rare in modern
  living rooms, for two independent, mechanism-explained reasons**: (1)
  a genuinely dirty zone forms within roughly a **1-1.5m radius** — rugs
  and soft furniture can't be placed close; (2) intense localized heat
  output conflicts with good whole-house central heating (e.g. gas),
  making the immediate area uncomfortably hot when both run together.
- **⚠️ Emerging alternative technique — relocate the fireplace to an
  adjoining glazed veranda/terrace rather than the living room itself**
  (house-specific mechanism, doesn't transfer directly to an apartment,
  but the underlying zoning-isolation logic below does): keeps
  mess/heat outside the main living space; extends the veranda's usable
  season via the fireplace's own heat output (sliding glazing makes it a
  real year-round-adjacent space rather than a summer-only "aquarium");
  and provides **sound isolation** — a closable door lets a lively
  gathering avoid disturbing sleeping children elsewhere in the house.
  **The transferable underlying principle: isolate a genuinely
  mess-generating or loud/hot activity zone into a semi-enclosed adjunct
  space with its own closable door, rather than forcing it into the main
  shared living space** — relevant to any renovation weighing where to
  put a similarly disruptive activity zone, even without a literal
  veranda.
