## Durable Facts — Cost-Saving Contracting Strategy

## Durable Facts — Rough-Stage Acceptance Checklist

- **Test every outlet/switch before accepting rough electrical** (with a test lamp or tester) — protects against a later ceiling-installation crew accidentally puncturing a cable and the customer wrongly blaming the original electrician.
- **Leak-protection sensor test**: wet each sensor near a plumbing fixture with water, confirm the water valve closes; then dry the sensor and confirm the valve re-opens/allows flow again.
- **Instant water heater (проточник) plumbing rule**: connect it to the COLD-water line split (not the hot line) at the collector, because when a building's central hot water is shut off (routine maintenance event), the hot line carries only air — the heater should instead take a branch of the cold supply and split it into "still cold" and "heater-warmed" outputs, so hot water remains available during a central outage. `confirmed` as the speaker's stated correct practice; frames "beautiful Instagram water-inlet-node photos with the heater wired wrong" as a common contractor mistake.
- **Every electric point should have documented coordinates** in the project drawing, in a specific format: circuit group, height from floor, distance from nearest reference point, then a description of the point — recommended as a format to require even from a different designer.
- **Dry siphon (сухой сифон) required at the water-inlet node even in the most basic build** — lets filter-flush water drain automatically instead of requiring a bucket; without one, infrequent manual flushing plus standing water in a regular siphon causes sewer odor. Extends existing Plumbing page content on dry-trap siphons.
- **Cable gauge for high-draw appliances** (e.g. cooktop) must be visibly thicker than standard outlet cable (~2.5 mm²/conductor) — if the same thin gauge is used for a cooktop circuit, demand it be redone; undersized gauge will burn out on first use.
- **Water/heating collector node**: demand pressure testing ("опрессовка") of cold water, hot water, AND heating circuits together, performed in front of you before acceptance — otherwise a leak surfaces as water in your screed and the downstairs neighbor's ceiling instead.
- **Door-opening height must be re-verified at rough stage, before trim/wallpaper**: standard interior door leaf height is 2.00 m, requiring a 2.07 m opening. A mismatch discovered later (after flooring/wallpaper/paint/stretch ceiling are done) requires redoing the lintel and structural reinforcement above the opening — described as enormously more expensive than catching it now.
- **Hygienic shower/bidet spray must have hot water routed to it, not only cold.**
- **Bathtub installation footprint** should be sized only a few millimeters larger than the tub itself (not "tile the whole wall, then slide the tub in") — so the tile edge overlaps the tub rim and water drains back into the tub rather than behind it, which otherwise causes leaking/odor behind the tub apron.
- **Central electrical panel — three checkable "cornerstones"**: (1) every eventual load is accounted for and split into separate circuits per room/function (so you can isolate outlets, AC, loggia, etc. independently); (2) every breaker is labeled/marked; (3) "selectivity" — breakers sized/coordinated so a fault trips only the smallest relevant downstream breaker, not the main incomer or the building's own riser breaker.
- **Deliberately unclamped rough-stage plumbing fittings are not a defect**: crews are told to leave supply fittings (e.g. at a mixer connection) resting loosely with pipe slack and crimp-sleeve fittings pre-installed but not yet crimped, until tile is laid and the exact grout-line position is known — so the fitting can then be centered on a tile joint for a clean look. Rough-stage handover should show pipe stubs with sleeves already on and fittings resting on a mounting plate, not yet compressed.
- **Waterproofing must always be bright blue, never gray**, specifically because gray waterproofing is visually indistinguishable from surrounding gray plaster, making coverage gaps invisible to a non-expert inspecting the work; bright blue makes any missed patch obvious even to an untrained eye.
- **AC refrigerant line routing rules**: lines must run at correct slope, with no kinks/sharp bends, and the bend where lines exit the wall to the outdoor unit must match that specific unit's connection-point spec (a straight cut-and-exit is wrong for most units — a formed bend is required). Damage during plastering/electrical work forces tearing out an entire false wall to repair. For remote/split-team renovations, recommends a video call with the AC installer to review this specific interface before it gets covered by other trades. Reinforces existing HVAC-page condensate/line-routing content.
- **Utility-cabinet (water-inlet node) lighting**: a dedicated light fixture inside the cabinet enclosure is recommended as a standard rough-stage item.
- **Loggia insulation-board fastening density**: insulation boards need enough mechanical fasteners ("mushroom" anchors) per board — visually demonstrated as roughly one per board minimum — to hold both the insulation and the later plaster/decorative-plaster coat; insufficient fastener density causes the plaster layer to delaminate/fall off later. Insulation must also be continuous and of adequate, correct thickness.
