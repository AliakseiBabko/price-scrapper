## Durable Facts

- **Install 3 temperature sensors per electric underfloor-heating mat, not 1** — the sensor is stated to fail more often than the mat itself; wiring 3 lets an installer swap to a spare in-place if one fails, without opening the floor, extending practical system lifespan (framed loosely as "3x", not a rigorous claim).
- **Protect finish-stage cables (HDMI, audio) from construction debris** — during wall skim-coating and wallpapering, falling compound/adhesive fouls exposed cable ends and connectors; cleanup is time-consuming and, if debris reaches inside a connector/plug itself, can permanently damage the cable. Team's practice: physically shield these cables during that work stage.
- **Separate power and low-voltage cabling by at least 15 cm within an outlet group**, routing power cable to one side (stated as the left edge) and low-voltage cable to the other (right edge) — reasoning given: 50 Hz mains cabling induces electrical noise onto adjacent low-voltage lines at close range.
- **Window protection should separate the opening sash from the fixed frame** when taping/covering for construction — protecting the whole window as one unit prevents the sash from being opened at all, which becomes a problem in high-humidity construction phases; protecting the sash separately keeps ventilation available throughout.
- **Recessed junction box behind towel-warmer and mirror-light cable runs, installed under tile before tiling** (rather than leaving a bare cable stub) — after tiling, the cable can be extended, shortened, or tucked flush through the box at final fixture installation, rather than being a fixed-length stub that has to work exactly as left.
- **Entry door needs full-coverage rigid protection (e.g. cardboard/corrugated board) inside and out, plus handle protection**, not just plastic film — film alone doesn't resist mechanical damage from bags, tool corners, or protruding fasteners on moving equipment; the entry door absorbs the heaviest abuse of any surface during a renovation (movers, delivery staff, multiple subcontractor crews).
- **Reinforce windowsills with rebar (a threaded rod, M10 in this case) when clearance above a radiator beneath the sill is too shallow** for the sill to safely bear a person's weight (sitting/leaning/standing) — framed as a durability fix for sills that will otherwise be undersized for that load.
- **Protect installed sanitary fixtures with plastic sheeting for the remainder of the renovation** to avoid a lengthy final dust/debris cleanup pass before handover.
- **Only drill/core into fully cured (dry) plaster, never wet plaster** — wet plaster clogs a core bit with paste rather than producing dust, which can jam the bit inside the wall; when that happens the drive shaft can spin freely inside the housing instead of the bit turning, a real hand/wrist injury risk if the tool binds unexpectedly. Framed as a basic but frequently-skipped safety/quality rule.
- **Recess a low-voltage panel enclosure into the wall depth where wall thickness allows**, even if the enclosure was purchased as a surface-mount unit and the customer wasn't aware recessing was possible — saves floor-area/wall-projection and avoids a protruding ledge that collects dust.
- **Non-sagging access hatch clearance on the hinge side must account for the mitered (45°) tile edge that will swing on the hatch door** — insufficient clearance means the tile hits a trim profile before the hatch can open, limiting it to roughly 25% of its full swing.
- **Shower/wet-area podiums built without a raised curb/lip where the waterproofing and partition design allow it** — reduces trip hazard and simplifies tile/mosaic installation on the podium surface; framed as the team's default whenever site conditions permit, made possible by modern partition/waterproofing systems that don't require a physical curb to contain water.
- **Breather ("бризер") wall niches sized per-model from a manufacturer template, with a size margin (not an exact fit)** — margin is especially important at the top of the niche, since most breather models exhaust air upward and need clearance there; niche size varies by breather model/size class (a full-size unit vs. a "nano" compact unit use different templates). Where possible, recess the breather fully into the niche.
- **Building-wide (общедомовая) ventilation ducts should be soundproofed, using non-perforated wrap/straps instead of traditional perforated metal strapping** — perforated metal tape is reported to damage the soundproofing material during installation; the smoother alternative avoids that damage while still being easy to install.
