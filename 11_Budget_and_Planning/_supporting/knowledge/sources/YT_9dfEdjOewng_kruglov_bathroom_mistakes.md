## Rules / Heuristics — Genuinely New Content

- **Hydronic towel-warmer failure mechanism, a new specific reason**: modern units include a riser bypass loop, which very slightly reduces the flow reaching the towel warmer itself — a small pressure drop that can cause the unit to stop heating over time, with no real fix once it happens. Extends this store's existing 3-source "prefer electric" corroboration with a distinct, previously-unrecorded failure mechanism (vs. the leak/corrosion mechanisms already recorded).
- **⚠️ "Warm wall" (heated wall, same mechanism as underfloor heating but wall-mounted) is a poor towel-drying substitute, mechanism explained**: wall/tile surface temperature is deliberately capped near ~27°C for barefoot comfort (matching underfloor-heating comfort limits) — genuinely warm enough to stand on, but not warm enough to dry a towel resting against it, since so little heat differential remains to transfer. An electric towel warmer, by contrast, delivers all its heat directly to the towel with none lost heating tile/adhesive/wall mass.
- **⚠️ Freestanding tub enclosed on 3 sides as the *sole* bathing/shower method is a specific bad combination, not a freestanding-tub problem generally**: normal bathing splash wets the surrounding floor regardless of tub type, but a 3-sided enclosure blocks the reach needed to clean behind/beside the tub properly, and drainage for a freestanding tub already typically forces a raised podium or whole-room floor build-up. The freestanding-tub form itself isn't the issue — using it as the household's only way to shower, boxed in on three sides, is.
- **⚠️ Minimum built shower-cabin size: 80×90cm** — below this, comfortable only for children/adolescents, not an average adult. No stated upper limit.
- **⚠️ Vanity/counter sink mounting-angle rule**: the angle between a top-mounted (накладная) sink's outer edge and the countertop surface must be ≥90° — anything narrower cannot be cleaned properly even with a small hand and a toothbrush-scale tool, and re-soils quickly even if painstakingly cleaned once.
- **⚠️ Tiled/mosaic countertop surfaces accumulate dirt at every grout seam and are effectively impossible to keep clean** — cement-grouted seams in this location can also grow mold over time. General rule stated: any horizontal tiled surface should minimize the number of seams it has.
- **⚠️ Floor-standing vanity cabinet legs are a real, recurring injury hazard** — the source states repeated finger injuries (including at least one fracture) from stubbing against exposed cabinet legs; recommends wall-hung (floating) vanity cabinets instead, which the source states handle any realistic load and are also easier to clean around (no legs to clean around/under).
- **⚠️ Over-concealment caution, two specific examples**: (1) flush, tile-embedded hooks look sleek but are harder for guests to identify/use than an ordinary visible hook that reads clearly as a hook; (2) a fully concealed access hatch/panel for toilet-paper storage is disorienting for a guest who doesn't know the room's own hidden logic — prefer an open niche or an obviously visible storage solution over hiding routine-use storage behind unmarked panels.
- **⚠️ Don't force full seam continuity across floor→wall→bathtub-apron transitions as a design goal** — chasing a single continuous grout line across a change of plane and material (floor tile into wall tile into a bathtub screen) drives cost up sharply (requires an unusually skilled tile setter) for a detail the source calls not worth it. Distinct from this page's existing apron-tile-must-match-adjacent-wall-tile *pattern* rule (which is about the apron matching the wall's own layout, not about forcing one unbroken seam across a full room's plane changes) — not in tension with it, a narrower/different claim.
- **Exhaust-fan independent-switch rule, corroborating and reinforcing this page's existing 3-pattern rule**: never wire the fan to only ever come on together with the light on one single switch/button with no separate control — a dedicated fan switch (inside the room, any position) is required regardless of which of the page's existing 3 wiring patterns is used.
- **Second/ambient light source, corroborating and reinforcing existing multi-source lighting guidance**: relying on one single (often harsh, "headlight"-like) light source in a bathroom is uncomfortable at night and unsuitable for a relaxed bath — plan a second, dimmable/decorative source for exactly those situations, framed here specifically as a cheap, easy-to-plan-ahead addition (a candle substitute) rather than a luxury upgrade.
