## Furniture / Built-ins — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Built-in vs. free-standing ("corpus") wardrobes**: a built-in wardrobe's usable space is bounded by the room's own walls (only sliding door panels are purchased); a free-standing unit's dimensions are fixed by its manufacturer. This note covers built-in sizing only.
- **Height**: built-in wardrobe height = room ceiling height, floor to ceiling, by default. **Three internal tiers**: bottom ~50cm (shoes), middle 50cm-to-2m (clothes), above 2m (mezzanine/антресоль for bulky items — luggage cited as the most common). **If room height exceeds 280cm**, split the wardrobe into two independently-doored sections (main + auxiliary) rather than one tall section — a single very tall door panel becomes heavy, hard to operate, and wears out its roller track faster. Main section height typically 230-250cm. `confirmed`.
- **Length**: unconstrained by a fixed rule — built wall-to-wall or wall-to-nearest-opening, closed at the open end with a false wall or dedicated end panel. **The real constraint is door-panel width**: optimal range 60-90cm per panel (narrower panels are prone to warping/binding; wider panels become heavy and unwieldy). **Height-to-width aspect-ratio limit**: panel height must be no less than 2× its width and no more than 4× its width (e.g. a 2m-tall panel must be between 50cm and 100cm wide). `confirmed`, precise numeric rule.
- **Depth — two commonly-misjudged figures**: everyday clothing (shirts, jackets on hangers) needs 50-53cm of hanging depth; outerwear (coats, fur, heavy jackets on hangers) needs 60-63cm. **Common mistake**: assuming the wardrobe itself only needs to be 55cm/65cm deep to match — wrong, because (a) clothing needs ~2-3cm of air clearance on each side (shouldn't be pressed against the back wall or constantly brushing the sliding doors), and (b) the sliding-door track/rail system itself consumes ~10cm (each panel ~35-40mm thick + ~10mm clearance between panels, summed to ~100mm). **Correct minimum depths**: everyday-clothing wardrobe 65-70cm; outerwear wardrobe minimum 75cm, **80cm preferred**. `confirmed`, the single most load-bearing numeric correction in this source — explicitly flagged by the speaker as commonly miscalculated.
- **Entry-zone wardrobe width floor**: the zone from door-opening trim to the nearest wall is often too shallow in modern apartments for a proper-depth entry wardrobe. **Rather than accept an undersized ("castrated") wardrobe, relocate the entrance-door opening** to create adequate width. Minimum width for an 80cm-deep entry wardrobe: 90cm (80cm wardrobe + 10cm door-opening trim allowance) — **or 1m if a light switch also sits on that same wall segment** (switch itself needs ~10cm clearance too). `confirmed`, concrete planning-stage checklist item.

## Numeric Data

- Shelf tier heights: 50cm shoes / rod to 2m / mezzanine above.
- Split-section threshold: room height > 280cm.
- Door-panel width: 60-90cm optimal. Aspect ratio: panel height between 2× and 4× its width.
- Hanging-garment depth needed: everyday 50-53cm, outerwear 60-63cm.
- Finished wardrobe depth (incl. air gap + rail): everyday 65-70cm, outerwear 75-80cm (80cm preferred).
- Entry-zone wardrobe minimum width: 90cm (no switch) / 100cm (switch present on same wall).
