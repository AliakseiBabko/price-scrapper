## Durable Facts

- **Multi-floor-covering screed differential must be pre-planned, not patched with self-leveling compound afterward** — differences between porcelain tile and engineered board (with plywood underlayment) can reach 3–5 cm; pouring self-leveling compound to make up that much height will crack, is expensive at that thickness, and raises the finished floor enough to interfere with door clearances. Same recurring theme as `Uhqtnv3lsuo` and `UYLIMck97Jo`.
- **Even the same flooring material can need different screed heights in different rooms** — e.g. porcelain tile over an underfloor-heating mat vs. porcelain tile with no mat plus a tile-adhesive layer — both must be leveled to match, planned in advance.
- **Door-opening and outlet heights must be recalculated whenever screed height is deliberately varied** to compensate for flooring-thickness differences — a corollary the speaker flags as commonly missed even by teams that get the screed differential itself right.
- **Minimize visible cut/trimmed pieces when laying one flooring material through irregularly-shaped connected rooms** — plan cuts to land inside closets or other hidden areas rather than in visible traffic areas.
- **Small-bathroom storage**: raise the water-inlet node ("узел приёма воды") on a platform to create usable storage space underneath for toiletries/paper goods, addressed at project stage — same technique as in `p1BQChVZnmE`.
- **Shower podium can sometimes be skipped entirely**: with a sufficiently thick screed (14 cm in this case), the drain/trap can be recessed without raising the floor at all — if site conditions allow, skip the podium. Where a podium/curb is still needed, keep it low (~5 cm) — cited as tall enough to register visually and prevent stubbed toes, contrasted with a taller podium that's a worse trip/injury risk.
- **Shower valve/spout height should be set per-occupant, not a fixed standard height** — the speaker states no "standard height" is used; instead the family's heights are measured and the specific shower-wand hose length (customer picks the shower set in advance) is factored in so the height works for every household member.
- **Cable-splicing capsules for extending high-load power cables**: when a new full-length incoming cable run isn't possible (e.g. can't run new cable all the way from the building's electrical room), extend the existing cable using a rated splicing capsule rather than any ad-hoc splice — described as fire-safety-rated, reliable, and easy to install; an incorrect splice on a high-load cable risks overheating, and in the worst case, fire.
- **Low-voltage panel should aggregate all data/AV cabling** (TVs, workstations, a redundant internet feed from the building's own low-voltage room, Wi-Fi router, network switch) behind one cover, for reliable Wi-Fi/wired internet to every outlet, without visible clutter.
- **Dry-trap ("сухой") siphon under dishwashers** to prevent sewer-gas odor — standard practice on this channel's objects, framed as a defect most homeowners don't know to ask about. This is the same class of fixture referenced generically in `00_Master/wiki_page_format.md`'s own content-routing example ("install a dry-trap siphon").
- **Under-sink cabinet electrical provisioning**: 3 outlets provisioned at the kitchen-sink cabinet at project stage — one for a garbage disposal, one for an active water-filtration unit, one spare — planned ahead so retrofits don't require new wiring later.
- **Breather ("бризер") venting routed through a window reveal/embrasure** as a workaround when facade penetrations are banned by the building — the intake/exhaust opening is cut into the window's reveal rather than the exterior wall.
- **Outlet/junction boxes must themselves be sound-rated wherever the surrounding wall has soundproofing** — standard (non-rated) boxes let essentially all sound through the wall at that point, defeating an otherwise well-executed soundproofed wall.
