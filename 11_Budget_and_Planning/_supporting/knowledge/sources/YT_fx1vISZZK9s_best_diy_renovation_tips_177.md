## Durable Facts

- **Two-component ("двухкомпонентные") radiator grilles are a genuine upgrade over standard fixed grilles**: standard grilles make it very difficult to reach the radiator behind them (for bleeding air or wiping down); a two-component grille splits into an outer frame plus a magnetically-attached center insert, so the center panel alone can be popped off for access without removing the whole grille. Made to order by the same custom-fabrication shops that make standard bespoke grilles — just requires specifying "two-component" in the fabrication brief. **Genuinely new grille-mechanism detail** for this store's radiator-concealment content. Designer radiators (left exposed, never concealed) are noted as an exception, at roughly 3x the cost of a standard radiator.
- **Stretch-ceiling light lines ("световые линии") can now fully replace a room's main/central light fixture** (chandelier or ceiling-mount lamp) — every room needs a main light source bright enough for both comfortable use and cleaning, and modern stretch-ceiling light-line technology is stated as capable of meeting that bar alone. **Two caveats explicitly flagged**: (1) light lines cost significantly more than a standard light fixture; (2) if a light line fails, repair is difficult and can require replacing the stretch ceiling itself — a durable trade-off worth recording alongside the store's existing stretch-ceiling content.
- **Paintable wallpaper ("обои под покраску") is preferred by this company over painting walls directly ("стены под покраску"), and is visually indistinguishable once finished** — stated advantages: doesn't crack, harder to damage, easier to repair when damaged, and cheaper overall than a directly-painted wall finish. Additional mechanism given here not previously in this store: **because wallpaper absorbs some paint, the finished surface comes out slightly matte, which hides fingerprint/grease marks that would show clearly on a directly-painted wall.** This corroborates and adds a specific visual/mechanism reason to the store's existing paintable-wallpaper-preference theme (previously supported mainly by the door-height rework-cost comparison in `yt_ps_6QOFsmAY`).
- **Wall-finish assignment (which covering goes on which wall) should be spelled out explicitly, wall-by-wall, in the design project documentation** — stated as this company's own design-project practice. Company's own **design-project fee stated as 3,000 RUB/m²**, sold via the company's own website (ems.pro). **Price/location/year note**: this is a same-channel, same-currency data point in this store's existing design-fee price-history series — this store already has 3,000 RUB/m² dated 2022-12-20 (`yt_ChXZrzgDrgs`) and ~4,000/5,000 RUB/m² at later dates. This video's upload date (2022-10-09, metadata-confirmed) is **about 2.5 months earlier** than the existing 2022-12-20 data point at the same 3,000 RUB/m² figure — consistent, not conflicting: the rate appears to have held steady at 3,000 RUB/m² from at least October 2022 through December 2022 before later rising. Strengthens (does not contradict) the existing price-history series; region is Moscow-area company practice (level 2, channel branding only, not independently re-confirmed at level 1 in this transcript).
- **Laminated-finish window sills and slopes should match the window's laminate color, not default to white** — practical sourcing problem flagged: window manufacturers generally don't sell matching sill/slope panels, and matching-color panels are described as very difficult to find at typical building-materials markets. The stated fix: don't shop at markets for this — matching-color panels do exist but are sourced through specialized companies (not named), not general retail. Repeatedly observed failure pattern cited: premium wood-laminate windows paired with ugly mismatched white sills/slopes because installers gave up after checking only markets.
- **Curtain cornices ("карнизы") are now installed flush with, or slightly above, the ceiling plane** rather than the older style of a cornice hanging visibly below the ceiling with visible curtain hooks. A dedicated ceiling profile now exists to form this raised/flush transition directly (previously this junction had to be hand-built from drywall by finish crews). Curtains hung from this cornice type have their top edge sit above the ceiling plane, hiding the mounting hardware. Genuinely new technique/product detail for this store's window-treatment content.
- **Adding a second, upper electrical outlet inside a window slope (in addition to a lower one) is now a frequently-requested feature** at this company, driven by a specific stated use case: seasonal window/holiday light garlands. Plugging a garland into a low slope-mounted outlet leaves visibly dangling cords running down the slope for weeks at a time; an upper outlet, positioned so it's normally hidden behind closed curtains and unused most of the year, avoids this. Extends this store's existing window-slope-outlet content (previously covering the rationale for slope outlets generally) with a specific second-outlet placement use case.
- **Quartz-vinyl (SPC/rigid-core vinyl) flooring is used by this company on nearly every project**, including higher-budget ones, specifically because it can be installed as one continuous field across room boundaries without transition strips ("porozhki") at doorways — described as looking good, being water-resistant, and holding up even in non-premium price tiers of the product category (a cheaper quartz-vinyl product is still stated as water-resistant and seamless-installable, not just the expensive tiers). Reinforces flooring-selection content already in this store with an explicit "even the cheap tier still delivers the core benefit" caveat.
