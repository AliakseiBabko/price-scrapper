## Doors / Trim — Durable Facts & Rules (door jamb / откос plastering)

- **A door jamb (дверной откос) left uneven by the installers who fitted
  the door** ("сказали что вот эти косяки неровные, не так их отделали")
  is a real, apparently common handoff problem between a door installer
  and whoever finishes the surrounding wall — the jamb finishing is not
  automatically included in door installation and needs separate
  planning/budgeting even for a "just install the door" job.
- **A rigid corner guide-profile (уголок/profile), meant to be screwed to
  the wall as a straightedge to plaster against, could not be mounted
  because the source lacked the tools/ability to drill into the concrete
  wall herself** (self-described as a non-professional, working alone) —
  she attempted to substitute **double-sided tape** to hold the profile in
  place instead of screws. **This failed**: the taped profile repeatedly
  came loose/fell off under any contact, and she abandoned the
  guide-profile approach entirely partway through, finishing the corner
  free-hand by feel instead. **Concrete negative data point for DIYers
  without power-tool access**: double-sided tape is not a viable
  substitute for mechanical fastening (screws/anchors into concrete) when
  mounting a rigid plastering guide-profile — plan for actual drilling
  capability (or a helper who has it) before relying on this technique.
- **Working free-hand without a guide profile produced a visibly less even
  result, particularly at the top of the jamb, which was left with large
  gaps/holes** the source planned to fill once dry — an honest, undressed
  account of what happens when the recommended tool/technique isn't
  available and a workaround is required instead.
- **A genuine mitigating factor specific to this project**: since the wall
  is going to be finished with a faux-brick texture afterward (this
  batch's other episodes), the jamb-plastering precision bar is lower than
  it would be for a smooth/painted final finish — the brick texture's own
  irregularity conceals moderate unevenness underneath. **Generalizable
  planning point**: match the required precision of a substrate-prep step
  to the actual final finish it will sit under, rather than defaulting to
  "as smooth/even as possible" for every substrate.
- **Real time cost, stated directly**: roughly 2 hours on one corner of the
  jamb, and "a whole day" (her words) for the jamb overall — a genuinely
  slow, first-attempt pace for an inexperienced DIYer working solo.

## Mistakes / Warnings

- See above: double-sided tape as a profile-mounting substitute for screws
  is a documented failure, not a viable technique.
- Installer handoff gap: a door install did not include making the jamb
  reveal itself even/finish-ready — verify this scope explicitly when
  hiring installation-only labor for a door.
