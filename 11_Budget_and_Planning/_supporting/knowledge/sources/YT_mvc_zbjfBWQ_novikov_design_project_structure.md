## Planning Rules — Durable Facts & Rules (Design-Process Methodology)

- **⚠️ Three-component structure of a design project, stated as the direct
  answer to "what do you pay a designer for"**: (1) the album itself (the
  instruction handed to builders); (2) calculation and estimate
  ("калькуляция и смета") — rough-material quantities and utility routing
  worked out before finishes are chosen; (3) author's supervision
  ("авторский надзор") during construction. Explicitly rebuts the "you pay
  for pretty pictures" framing — visualization is presented as impossible
  without a correctly developed concept and drawings first.
- **Album build order, 4 stages**: (1) concept; (2) drawing development;
  (3) visualization + technical drawings; (4) album assembly. **Concept
  alone is stated as ~30% of the finished project.**
- **Concept-stage sub-sequence**: gather reference/analog images (client
  or designer-sourced) → designer's own interpretation integrating client
  wishes into the specific apartment → layout variants (may include
  replanning) → layout approval → furniture placement → preliminary then
  approved drawings → sketches.
- **⚠️ DIY "copy a picture from the internet" pitfall, speaker's own
  sales argument for why "you can't do this yourself" — extract the
  mechanism, not the conclusion**: the stated failure mode is real and
  checkable (a reference image may simply not fit a specific room's
  dimensions without adaptation), and the general point that a mistake
  made at the concept stage cascades into costly rework later
  corroborates this store's existing staged-approval-gate logic (Kodolov's
  concept-collage staging, Archwood's revision-payment policy) with an
  independent source. But this is the speaker's own pitch for his paid
  service, not a neutral finding — a self-managed builder with time and
  attention to dimensions can adapt a reference image themselves; the
  reusable takeaway is "check real dimensions before committing to a
  reference," not "you need to hire a designer."
- **⚠️ Furniture-before-electrical-points sequencing rationale, with a
  concrete team-size mechanism**: outlet/switch placement cannot be
  finalized before furniture placement — a socket can end up hidden behind
  a sofa and become unusable. At the drawing-development stage the project
  team grows from one person to three, framed by the speaker (a studio
  owner) as a direct additional cost driver, not just a sequencing nicety.
- **⚠️ Staged-approval gating, explicit cost rationale**: each stage
  requires client sign-off before the next begins; a client-requested
  change discovered only at the visualization stage forces rework of
  every completed upstream stage, at cost — the whole staged/gated
  structure exists specifically to control this rework cost, not just to
  manage expectations. Independently corroborates the existing revision-
  payment and staged-workflow entries in `Durable_Facts.md`'s Design
  Process section from a fourth independent source.
- **⚠️ Material price-tier example, single apartment, 3 brand tiers
  (2020, Russia, level 2 region)**: tiling a 100 m² apartment's bathroom +
  WC + kitchen + entrance costs roughly **100,000–120,000 RUB (≈$1,300–
  $1,600) for a mid-tier brand (Kerama Marazzi, named)**, versus roughly
  **≈$6,700 (500,000 RUB, "half a million rubles") for Spanish/Italian
  premium tile** on the identical footprint/picture. A third figure for
  "Italon" tile was spoken but the ASR transcript garbles the number
  (heard as "примерно наверное 1250 300") — **not usable as a data point,
  flagged `ASR-uncertain`, do not treat as a real figure.** Point of the
  example: the same visual result spans a very wide price range depending
  on brand tier, which a layperson has no practical way to navigate alone.
  Conversion: trailing-6-month mean before the confirmed 2020-12-01
  upload date, 74.1231 RUB/USD (`tools/pricing/currency_converter.py
  --pair USD/RUB --trailing-months 6 --before 2020-12-01`).
- **Designer-as-market-guide framing, generalized beyond tile**: same
  logic extended to AC unit selection and door selection — a door-size
  change requires a technical spec ("техническое задание") from the
  supplier that the designer must ensure reaches the builders, and every
  material must arrive on-site neither too early nor too late. Designer
  is framed as already knowing which supplier fits a stated budget,
  saving the client sourcing time.
- **Two site-visit models, explicit tradeoff**: paid designer-accompanied
  visits to the client's own preferred stores, **or** sourcing from the
  designer's own vetted supplier network (for which the designer states
  they take responsibility) — paid via commission on the client's actual
  purchase through that channel rather than a flat visit fee.
- **⚠️ Self-reported transparency/anti-kickback stance**: the speaker
  states a preference for disclosing all costs/arrangements to the client
  before contract signing, contrasted with (unnamed) designers who
  allegedly conceal a kickback arrangement with contractors/suppliers.
  Framed as the speaker's own ethical practice, not a verifiable industry
  fact — `single-account`, `unverified`, self-serving framing noted.
- **Cross-material compatibility check named as a real material-selection
  step**: e.g. tile-to-laminate/parquet floor-height mismatch requires an
  additional floor build-up ("подливка") at the transition — an extra
  cost that must be disclosed before work starts, not discovered during
  installation.
- **⚠️ Kitchen-cabinetry-against-window curtain-clearance rule**: when
  kitchen cabinetry runs up to a window wall, a technological gap must be
  left for curtains to hang — otherwise there is nowhere for the curtain
  to sit once the window has curtains at all. A concrete, checkable
  clearance rule not previously in this store.
- **⚠️ Large-format tile vs. passenger-elevator delivery constraint,
  named as a genuine planning failure mode**: a design using large-format
  tile (spoken example ≈120×40cm) can be fully approved and drawn, yet
  never actually reach the unit if the building's passenger elevator
  cannot carry a slab that size — requiring an external hoist/crane
  delivery via the facade or roof if the elevator can't. Framed as a
  logistics check the designer must catch before finalizing large-format
  material choices, distinct from (but complementary to) this store's
  existing tile-quantity-undercounting caution from ARCHWOOD
  (`YT_LOw05Uk3rUk`).
- **Bare-shell ("коробка") object requires separate dedicated engineering
  albums per utility**: water supply/sewage and electrical each need
  their own dedicated project and specialist, distinct from the interior
  design album itself — though the design album still carries schematic
  (not fully engineered) nodes for items like ceiling-to-wall junctions
  or bulkhead boxes. Corroborates this store's existing "each communication
  requires its own album and specialist" framing from other sources in
  this topic area.
- **Author's-supervision phase covers furniture/decor selection with the
  same deliverability logic as materials**: sofa dimensions/clearance
  checked for real walkaround space (especially for a sofa-bed that must
  fold out), textile coordination (curtains matched to cushions etc.), and
  an explicit caution against over-accessorizing ("too many small details
  ruin the whole picture"). A "good designer" is framed as staying
  involved through this phase rather than handing off after drawings are
  approved.
