## Design Concept / Furniture — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Real client reasoning for choosing a wall-bed over a sofa-bed**: a
  proper bed with a real orthopedic mattress needs a minimum footprint
  of roughly 2×1.4m to 2×1.6m (~3.2m²) plus a nightstand — in a room that
  also needs to work as a living room (seating for guests, watching TV
  together), that floor-area cost was judged too high; hence the
  wall-bed solution.
- **⚠️ Real planning-sequence mistake and its lesson**: the client had
  already finalized electrical wiring/outlet placement BEFORE the
  furniture maker was brought in — a real sequencing error, since the
  outlet layout should follow the furniture plan, not precede it. This
  left as little as **10mm** of clearance margin for the whole cabinet
  against a wall/radiator obstruction. Resolved through careful
  measurement (no rework needed here), but the maker states this
  directly as a mistake to avoid: **finalize wall-bed/wardrobe unit
  dimensions before the electrical rough-in stage, not after.**
- **Interior wardrobe-zone design details**: an ~800mm-wide open shelf
  zone subdivided by a vertical center divider specifically to avoid
  excessive sag under unknown future loading; two stacked hanging rods
  for ~1600mm of combined hanging length within a compact footprint;
  decorative display shelves included specifically to give the "living
  room" identity a place for photos/décor, not just closed storage.
- **⚠️ Monolithic/seamless custom-build technique, a real
  differentiator vs. modular furniture**: deliberately built as one
  continuous facade with no visible module-width seams (avoiding a look
  where a viewer can spot "this is a 1200mm module next to a 1600mm
  module") — this reads as bespoke/high-end rather than flat-pack, a
  real distinguishing choice worth asking about when comparing a custom
  maker vs. a modular product.
- **Electrical retrofit workaround for the pre-placed-outlet mistake**:
  outlets/antenna/cable routing hidden in a compartment behind the TV
  console section with a cable channel through the countertop, trimmed
  in aluminum edging for a clean finish.
- **Triple-track sliding door as a clearance-driven compromise**: the
  same 10mm clearance constraint forced a 3-track sliding storage door
  instead of the client's preferred 4-leaf (2-and-2) swing-door
  configuration — a real example of hardware choice being dictated by a
  tight wall clearance, with the tradeoff (triple-track is less common/
  convenient than standard double-track) stated explicitly.
- **⚠️ Lift-bed safety mechanism, detailed**: a pusher/actuator + magnet
  + lock/latch holds the bed closed — necessary since a single mattress
  alone weighs ~100kg. The closed unit does not release from merely
  leaning on it; release requires a deliberate, sharp press, and the
  release force is tunable by the installer per client preference (some
  clients want it easier to open, some want it harder to release
  accidentally). Gas-lift struts hold the panel stable even partway open
  — demonstrated holding one-fingered.
- **⚠️ Cross-bar/center-support structural detail, a real quality
  differentiator to check when selecting a mechanism**: this maker flags
  that SOME competing mechanisms on the market only support the mattress
  frame at the two side edges, leaving the middle unsupported. Their own
  design adds a center support in addition to both side supports —
  stated as important for even mattress support and reduced sag.
- **⚠️ Thick-mattress workaround, a real dimension/margin lesson**: the
  mechanism's nominal rated depth handles mattresses up to ~190-200mm
  thick, but this client's mattress was 230-240mm — solved by shifting
  the entire mechanism assembly slightly deeper into the cabinet (only
  possible because the cabinet had spare depth to allow it), leaving a
  further margin to spare. A real consideration for anyone specifying a
  thick mattress against a wall-bed mechanism's rated maximum.
- **Above-headboard storage shelf with retention straps**: integrated
  into the mechanism for pillows/bedding when the bed is folded up, WITH
  retention straps ("ремешочки") — a different design position from
  `YT_wfmIOYsE2Hs`'s no-strap approach; this maker still cautions against
  relying on the strap to hold significant weight when the bed is raised.
- **⚠️ Frame-material durability claim — steel tube vs. particleboard,
  directly extends `YT_9jDv0Iibbfw`'s reliability finding**: most
  competing mechanisms use a 25mm particleboard (ДСП) panel as the
  structural frame back — heavy (a single sheet ≈80kg, awkward enough
  to require two installers to lift it vertically into the track) and,
  per `YT_9jDv0Iibbfw`'s independent finding, prone to cracking at the
  mounting-plate attachment point over time. This maker's own mechanism
  instead uses a **2mm-thick profiled steel tube frame**, described as
  much stronger, with mattress slats mounting directly onto the steel
  frame as an integrated system rather than a separate loose orthopedic
  base — presented as a structural solution to exactly the weak point
  `YT_9jDv0Iibbfw` documented failing in ДСП-panel construction.
- **Real usage account**: client had used the bed daily for ~3 months at
  time of filming (raised/lowered daily), self-reported as working very
  well — genuine, if short-term, daily-use data directly from the client
  (not just the maker's own claim).
- **Materials**: LDSP (Egger brand), 2mm edge banding on all
  visible-facing panels specifically for impact resistance (maker's own
  claim: can be struck with a hammer without visible damage), 25mm panel
  thickness on visible faces, thinner/cheaper material used for
  non-visible internal shelving to control cost. Estimated total load on
  the mechanism in this build: mattress + frame + side panel ≈65kg+.
- **Aesthetic-functional finish choice**: a light, mirrored, glossy
  ("champagne") finish was chosen over an initially-considered dark
  cabinet specifically because a light/mirrored finish visually opens up
  the room, whereas a dark, heavy cabinet finish visually shrinks it —
  mirror inserts add perceived depth and reflect light into the space.
