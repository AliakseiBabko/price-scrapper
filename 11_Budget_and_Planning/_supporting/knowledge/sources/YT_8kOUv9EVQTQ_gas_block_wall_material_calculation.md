## Walls — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Zemskov's project-documentation requirement**: a usable design project must show step-by-step wall/partition construction with explicit reference-point dimensioning for each wall (a project showing only overall room dimensions is, per Zemskov, unusable and needs to be redone). Separately, a good project must include a **wall-marking sheet**: a unique label for every individual wall plus that wall's own area — **not a single total-wall-area figure**, which Zemskov calls essentially useless on its own. His reasoning: a real apartment (unlike his own atypical single-thickness clinic site) mixes block thicknesses (his examples: 100mm, 150mm, 200mm walls, plus 50-70mm boxing around utilities) and mixes finish materials by wall (tile on some, wallpaper on others) — a per-wall labeled area breakdown is what actually lets a buyer or contractor calculate exact material quantities by thickness and finish type; a lump total area is only useful for one narrow purpose (total plastering area) and doesn't support any other material calculation.
- **Zemskov's block-quantity formula**: 7 blocks per 1m² of wall, regardless of block thickness — sum all wall areas from the labeled-wall sheet above, multiply by 7. **Never buy the full calculated quantity in one purchase** — precise final quantity always differs once door openings and on-site cutting/trimming are accounted for, so a second purchase is inevitable regardless of care taken. Zemskov's own practice: buy roughly half the calculated total in the first order — enough to work without stacked blocks blocking site movement, without wasting significant time versus ordering the exact number up front (since some second order is unavoidable anyway).
- **Zemskov's site-logistics sequencing**: mark out room contours on the floor (spray paint recommended) *before* the block delivery/placement, and stage delivered blocks so they don't sit on the future wall footprint itself. **Store/stage blocks horizontally, never stacked on end/vertically** (Zemskov notes he only stood a few on end briefly for the camera to illustrate a later adhesive-calculation segment, not as a real storage recommendation) — actual laying is always done with blocks horizontal.
- **Zemskov's hard material-substitution rule**: gas-concrete block masonry is a **thin-joint system** and must never be laid with ordinary tile adhesive or cement-sand mortar — it requires a dedicated block-specific adhesive formulated for that thin joint, which is what gives the assembly its actual bonding strength; substituting a generic adhesive is presented as a straightforward defect, not a debatable choice.
- **Zemskov's adhesive-quantity formula, by volume not surface area**: one 25kg bag of block adhesive covers 1 cubic meter of masonry, **regardless of block thickness** (100mm, 200mm, 500mm all use the same 1-bag-per-m³ figure) — his stated reasoning: thinner blocks need proportionally more individual units to fill the same cubic volume, but each gets a proportionally thinner adhesive layer, so the adhesive consumed per cubic meter stays constant; calculating by volume avoids needing a separate formula per block width. **Apply a real-world correction of +20-25% over the manufacturer's nominal stated coverage figure** — Zemskov's own worked example: a nominal calculation of 17 bags became an actual purchase of 22.
- **Zemskov's first-row exception**: the very first course of blocks is always laid on ordinary cement-sand mortar, not the thin-joint block adhesive — because the first row sits on an inherently uneven base (the raw floor/screed) that the thin-joint adhesive system isn't designed to level, while every subsequent row (on an already-flat block surface) uses the thin, minimal-consumption block adhesive proper. **Cement-sand mortar quantity has no fixed formula** — it depends entirely on the specific floor's own flatness/unevenness at each location; Zemskov gives a typical range of 1.5-3cm layer thickness, with the specific amount left to the mason's on-site judgment (his own clinic-site example used just 1cm, which he flags as an exception, not the norm).
- **Zemskov's minimum tool/material checklist for a real (non-trivial) block-laying job**: at least 2 mixing buckets (since a batch of adhesive needs to rest briefly after initial mixing before use — a single-bucket workflow can't accommodate this), a notched masonry trowel (кельма) — worthwhile starting at roughly 20m² of masonry, below which a plain putty knife/spatula suffices — a rubber mallet, mixing paddle/mixer attachment, cordless drill/driver, angle grinder, rotary hammer, hand or reciprocating saw rated for block cutting, laser level, string/twine and construction pencils for course alignment without extra measuring tools, foam-gun sealant, 3m levels (1-2), a mason's square, a bubble level, a tape measure, PPE (gloves, safety glasses, and — per Zemskov, often overlooked — hearing protection), 6mm concrete drill bits and matching dowels, driver bits, screws, and marking chalk/paint for discs. **Reinforcing mesh should be embedded every 2-3 courses (every 2 preferred)**, plus wall-tie hangers for securing new block walls to existing structural walls.
