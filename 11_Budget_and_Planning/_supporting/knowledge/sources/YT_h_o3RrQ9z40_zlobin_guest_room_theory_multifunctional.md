## Planning Rules — Three Principles for a "Sellable"/Future-Resale-Oriented House Design

The architect opens with an explicit design philosophy, framed as why
roughly half of a private house's rooms end up needing multifunctional
design — new context for this store, not previously recorded:

1. **Functionality as a whole, not just isolated details** — design the
   coherent overall floor plan first; individual room decisions should
   never be evaluated apart from the whole plan.
2. **Clarity = simplicity** — avoid overly complex layouts (curved
   partitions, room clusters/bay excesses, disconnected pass-through
   halls); a simpler, more legible floor plan reads better to any viewer.
3. **Follow established world-standard comfort conventions** — luxury
   hotels already embody solved comfort standards; don't reinvent
   planning conventions that are already well-established.

**Practical consequence stated directly**: because a house needs to
remain attractive to a *future* buyer 5-15 years out, not just its
current owner, roughly half its rooms should be planned with
multifunctional future use in mind — guest rooms and kids' rooms are
named as the two clearest examples of this principle in practice.

## Mistakes / Warnings — A Common Architect Mistake in Guest-Room Sizing

**⚠️ A specific, named mistake**: architects frequently draw a guest room
with only a single bed (larger than a child's crib, but still single) yet
give it a disproportionately large room footprint — the architect calls
these two choices directly contradictory: a small-capacity bed paired
with generous floor area wastes space that isn't actually functionally
justified. **Named fix**: use a sofa-bed instead of a fixed single/double
bed (see furniture rules below), and keep the room's area modest,
matching what a sofa-bed genuinely needs rather than over-sizing "just in
case."

## Rules / Heuristics — Guest-Room Location Rules

- **Always ground floor** — placing a guest room on an upper floor or
  (worse) a basement level is called out directly as "absurd" and
  unacceptable.
- **As close as possible to a guest-dedicated bathroom/WC**, which must
  itself include a **shower** — a guest should be able to use full
  bathing facilities without needing to walk through the house (in a
  towel) to reach the owners' own upstairs bathroom.
- **Ideally near the house's own exit/entry**, not requiring a guest to
  route through the living room or (worse) the kitchen/dining area to
  reach their room — flagged as a common real mistake seen in actual
  projects.
- **Avoid north-facing orientation where practical** — secondary to the
  scenario-specific use the room is actually being planned for.

## Quantities / Measurements — Guest-Room Sizing

- **Maximum useful guest-room area: 12m²** — sizing beyond this is called
  out as pointless for a room of this function. **Typical sufficient
  size: 10-11m²** in most real projects.
- **Compact office/workshop-conversion variant**: roughly **11-13m²**
  (see multifunctional scenarios below).
- **Full future-bedroom variant** (scenario 3 below): explicitly sized
  larger than the compact 10-13m² range — "the small compromise room
  won't satisfy anyone" once the room's real future use is a full
  bedroom; plan the larger footprint from the start rather than
  retrofitting later.

## Rules / Heuristics — Guest-Room Furniture Defaults

- **A fold-out sofa-bed, not a fixed single or double bed, is the
  recommended default guest-room furniture piece** — a single explicit
  reasoning chain: (a) it converts between single- and double-occupancy
  sleeping capacity as needed, avoiding the wasted-space mismatch flagged
  above; (b) since a guest room sees only occasional/episodic use, a
  sofa's upholstery wears far less over time than a permanently-made
  double bed would in the same low-use pattern; (c) it eliminates the
  need for a bulky permanent double-bed frame in a room that doesn't
  justify one.
- **A small dedicated wardrobe/closet is recommended even for a guest
  room** — the architect calls this his own personal addition (not a
  universal convention) but justifies it directly against the
  multifunctional-use scenarios below, where a wardrobe's storage
  function repurposes cleanly (document storage, a small safe room) if
  the room later converts to an office.
- **Keep furnishing genuinely minimal ("ascetic")** for a guest room
  that's staying a guest room — explicitly warns against over-furnishing
  with poufs, extra soft seating, or other filler furniture unless a
  specific multifunctional need justifies it.

## Rules / Heuristics — Four Future-Use Scenarios for a Guest Room (Core Framework)

The central reusable framework of this video — plan guest-room
infrastructure (utility rough-ins, wardrobe placement, room sizing)
against these four named future-conversion scenarios, not just the
room's current use:

1. **Guest room → stays guest room** (most common scenario). Minimal
   furniture set: sofa-bed, a wall storage/shelving unit ("Т-zone"), and
   a small wardrobe. No soft lounge zone needed for this scenario
   specifically.
2. **Guest room ↔ home office or workshop** — described as a very
   common real scenario; clients frequently state upfront that the room
   will alternate between guest and office/workshop use over time.
   **⚠️ Concrete infrastructure implication**: if this scenario is
   anticipated, provision the relevant utility rough-ins *at design
   time* — water and/or ventilation for a workshop use case, or extra
   electrical capacity for an office. **Furniture conversion detail**:
   remove the wall-unit/soft-zone furniture, keep the sofa (it can
   relocate to a different wall) so the room still functions for
   receiving guests/visitors; position a desk near the window
   specifically so daylight falls from the side onto the work surface;
   swap open shelving for bookshelves or document storage; the guest
   room's own small wardrobe (see furniture rules above) converts
   cleanly into document storage shelving or even a small dedicated safe
   room. **This is directly relevant to this project's own guest/study
   room concept.**
3. **Guest room ↔ a future elderly-parents' bedroom, or the current
   owners' own future downstairs bedroom** — an aging-in-place scenario:
   as owners age, climbing stairs to a second-floor bedroom becomes
   harder, and the only ground-floor bedroom-capable room in many house
   plans is the guest room. **⚠️ Concrete infrastructure implication**:
   if this scenario is genuinely anticipated, plan a proper double bed
   (not a sofa-bed) and the room's full furniture complement (wall
   storage, soft/lounge zone, own wardrobe) from the start, and — if the
   nearest guest WC isn't genuinely close by — build in a **full private
   ensuite bathroom** from the outset rather than relying on the shared
   guest WC once this becomes someone's permanent bedroom.
4. **Guest room ↔ staff quarters** (live-in cleaner, security, etc.) —
   explicitly flagged as the least common scenario, relevant only to
   larger houses with grounds/staff, not applicable to a typical/smaller
   house. Furniture-wise, treated similarly to scenario 2/3's compact
   range (sofa-bed or a double bed, or even two separate single beds for
   staff quarters specifically).

**Design-time consequence, stated directly**: scenarios 1, 2, and 4 can
share broadly the same compact ~11-13m² footprint and furniture set
(sofa-bed-centric, convertible); scenario 3 (a genuine future full
bedroom) is the one case that must be planned larger and more completely
from the very start, since it can't be retrofitted cheaply later — a
real, concrete instance of this store's existing "future-flexible
planning" principle (already recorded for this project's own Murphy-bed
plan on `Bedroom_Design_Principles.md`), now generalized to guest-room
planning specifically.

## Rules / Heuristics — Overall Guest-Room Priority Framing

Closing summary, stated directly: a guest room is "не так уж важен" (not
that critical) on its own, since guests typically visit infrequently —
**but** if a specific long-staying visit is anticipated in advance (a
named example: a distant relative staying for an extended period), the
room should be deliberately upgraded ahead of that visit: add/confirm a
small wardrobe, consider a dedicated ensuite if budget allows, or at
minimum ensure the room doesn't cross other public/private traffic flows,
has good natural lighting, and comfortable furnishing. A budget-conscious
prioritization heuristic, not a rule to under-invest in guest space by
default.
