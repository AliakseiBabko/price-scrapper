## Genuinely New Content

**⚠️ 60-30-10 color-proportion rule, plus a repeat-of-three accent rule
— new topic, no prior coverage on this store**: 60% of a room's color
should be the base fill (walls, floor), 30% mid-weight elements
(furniture, curtains), and only 10% genuinely accent colors (decor,
light fixtures, chairs). **The accent color must repeat at least 3
times** across distinct objects (e.g. a painting, a cushion, a vase all
sharing one accent tone) — a single accent instance doesn't read as a
deliberate choice.

**⚠️ Three-criterion contrast rule for accents, plus named warm/cool
palette pairings — new topic**: for an accent color to work, it must
differ from the base along at least one of three axes: **hue** (a
different color outright), **temperature** (warmer or cooler), or
**material/texture** (wood vs. metal vs. stone, etc.) — if unsure about
color contrast specifically, temperature or material contrast is framed
as the safer default. **Named palette pairings**: a warm base (beige,
sand, cream, terracotta, warm gray) pairs best with deep blue, olive or
forest green, black, brass, or gold; a cool base (concrete gray, cold
white, graphite) pairs best with terracotta, caramel, cognac tones,
warm wood, bronze, or warm green.

**⚠️ Rug-sizing rule tied to furniture placement, and a bedroom-specific
walking-path variant — new topic**: every major furniture piece in a
grouping (sofa, armchair, coffee table) should have at least its two
front legs resting on the rug, so the rug visually binds the grouping
into one composition rather than reading as a separate floating
element. **Bedroom-specific version**: the rug should be large enough
that stepping out of bed lands on it and the walk to the door stays on
it — a rug so small that only part of a footstep lands on it is worse
than no rug at all, since it highlights the mismatch rather than
solving it.

**Interior scent as a deliberate decor layer, with an explicit "don't
do it badly" caution — new topic, no prior coverage on this store**:
recommends a deliberately chosen ambient scent (tabletop diffuser,
scented candle, or a track-mounted automatic dispenser for a larger
budget) as a real, distinct layer of an "expensive" interior alongside
visual decor — but with a strong caution that matching scent to space
requires real care, since an ill-matched or overpowering scent can make
a whole apartment read as a public restroom; the source's own framing:
better to skip this layer entirely than execute it carelessly.

**⚠️ A four-layer decor framework — new organizing structure for this
store's existing decor-technique content**: (1) base — textiles
(curtains, throws, rugs, bedspreads); (2) texture — hard-surface
materials (wood, stone, porcelain, glass, metal, other decorative
finishes); (3) architecture — built decorative structure (GKL niches,
false walls, decorative panels, glass blocks, textile-wrapped panels);
(4) light — every lighting layer together (curtain backlighting, floor
lamps, decorative LED, point accent lighting on art). Framed as the
sequence that produces visual "depth," i.e. the mechanism behind why a
room reads as expensive rather than flat — a categorization lens over
content this store mostly already has piece by piece, not a source of
new individual facts on its own.

**⚠️ Small-object composition rules (four sub-rules, no prior coverage
on this store) — grouped as one item since none stands alone as
substantial**: (1) **rule of thirds for object groupings**: each
successive object in a grouped composition should be roughly 1/3
taller or shorter than the previous one (e.g. 10cm/20cm/30cm
figurines), not evenly or randomly sized. (2) **odd-number preference**:
groupings of 3, 5, or 7 objects read better than 2, 4, or 6 — stated as
an empirical design convention, not explained mechanistically. (3)
**triangle-shaped composition**: a grouping's overall silhouette, or
its key accent points, should trace a triangle the eye can follow.
(4) **visual-balance/"scale" rule**: one large object on one side of a
composition should be counterbalanced by a combination of smaller
objects on the other side with roughly equivalent visual weight, not
left to feel lopsided.

**⚠️ Artwork/poster placement rules — new topic, no prior coverage on
this store**: prefer one large-format piece over two or three smaller
ones in the same spot — a single bold piece reads as more deliberate.
**Sizing rule**: a piece's width should be roughly **2/3 the width of
the furniture it's paired with** (a sofa or bed) for visual cohesion.
**Placement rule**: hang the piece low enough to visually anchor to the
adjacent furniture (sofa, dresser) rather than floating in otherwise-
empty wall space, which reads as an afterthought filling space rather
than a deliberate composition.
