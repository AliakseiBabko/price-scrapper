## Durable, Broadly-Reusable Conceptual Facts (Level 1)

- **"Price per m²" without context is close to meaningless** — the same visual result can vary 2–5×, sometimes up to 10×, in real cost depending on material/build quality, decision complexity, and specification level. Stated directly and illustrated with concrete examples throughout (see Numeric Data below). This is one of the most broadly useful framing statements found in any source for this topic.
- **Quality and durability are related but distinct concepts** — worked example: budget and expensive porcelain tile in a wet zone can both be equally *durable* (waterproof, functionally fine over time), but differ sharply in *quality* (geometry, flatness, dimensional stability, print precision) — and quality is what actually determines whether a tile can be laid flat and even at all; a geometrically inconsistent tile can't be saved by a skilled installer.
- **A "rational purchase" framing doesn't apply to a comfort-tier personal interior** — the speaker distinguishes "buying housing as a tool" (where resale value, liquidity, and rational cost-benefit apply, analogous to an economy car) from "buying comfort" (a personal-taste interior, analogous to choosing between premium car brands or a fine-dining meal — not chosen on cost-efficiency grounds). Two direct conclusions drawn: (1) "investment design that resells for more" is described as rarely true — a good interior almost never pays for itself at resale; (2) tying interior budget to apartment price as a percentage is called a myth, since material/labor costs don't change based on the apartment's own price or location. **This independently corroborates a theme already recorded twice in this store from other practitioners** (`yt_DsdLa87Acz4` Prolife Invest, `yt_OrKB6uSKRyk` Бородатый Прораб) — a third independent source now makes the same core point, strengthening it as a cross-source pattern rather than one practitioner's opinion.
- **"Wishes" vs. "expectations" as a distinct, non-segment cost driver** — a *wish* is a specific named brand/product target (e.g. "I want this exact $2-3M-RUB sofa" or "I want a specific named oven brand") with enormous cost variance by brand choice alone (a stated example: a budget oven ≈100,000 RUB vs. a specifically-named premium oven brand ≈1,000,000 RUB — a single line item, 10× difference). An *expectation* is a described functional requirement without naming a brand (e.g. "I roast a duck once a year" vs. "I need to reliably roast meat, bake pizza, bread, and pastry") — also varies cost by up to ~10× depending on the implied capability level. Both are described as operating independently of which market segment (mid/premium/lux) a project sits in.
- **"Nuances" (small execution/detail decisions) accumulate into large budget swings** — worked example: laminate + tile vs. engineered-wood flooring + tile look similar from a few meters away, but differ ~5× in material cost (~3,000 RUB/m² laminate vs. ~15,000–17,000 RUB/m² engineered wood) and in installation detail (floating laminate needs a visible metal T/P transition profile at material junctions; glued engineered wood allows a nearly invisible cork expansion joint instead) — a single such decision can add 10,000–15,000 RUB/m², and dozens of comparable decisions across a project can compound to 200,000–400,000 RUB/m² in total budget difference. **Directly extends this store's existing "vague smeta entry hides scope" theme** with a materials/detailing-level example rather than a labor-pricing one.
- **Stated budget-allocation philosophy** (the speaker's own, explicitly presented as one approach among possible others): never cut cost on skilled labor (execution quality matters more than pretty materials); never cut cost on rough materials and concealed systems (heating, plumbing, pipes, junctions) since replacement later is either impossible or an expensive, disruptive operation.
- **Concrete build-quality tolerance standard**: cites GOST/SP building-code wall-verticality tolerance as ~3 mm per meter (worked example: a 3 m ceiling height could see up to ~9 mm of deviation at the top, causing visible gaps where built-in furniture meets a wall) — contrasted with the speaker's own studio working standard of ±0.5 mm per 2 linear meters (referred to as "Q4" tier, out of a Q1–Q4 quality-tolerance classification the speaker references as an existing standard, not self-invented). At a 3 m ceiling height, Q4 tolerance keeps deviation under ~1 mm. `confirmed` as the speaker's stated standard and framework; the Q1-Q4 classification's existence as an external standard is not independently verified here.
- **Economy segment explicitly excluded** — the speaker states directly he doesn't have reliable material/furniture/budget reference points for the economy segment and skips it, covering only mid/premium/luxury. This is an honest scope limitation stated by the source itself, not a gap this project introduced.
