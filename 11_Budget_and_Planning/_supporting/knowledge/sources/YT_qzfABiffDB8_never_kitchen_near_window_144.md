## Kitchen / Planning Rules — Why Not to Place a Kitchen Directly at a Window

- **1. Sink drainage routing conflict**: a kitchen sink's sewage drain must reach the stack, and stub-out groups are always positioned as far from the window as practical (typically the opposite wall) — placing the sink at the window forces either a very long, slow-draining horizontal run or a sump/macerator pump. **Sump-pump downside, direct client account**: audible motor hum that triggers unpredictably (whenever the unit senses accumulated water, not tied to active use), frequent clogging from food debris causing repeat failures, and — during outages — a multi-day sewage odor in the kitchen that forced the household to wash produce in a nearby bathroom instead.
- **2. Cooktop/hood incompatibility with a window wall**: a range hood needs either wall or ceiling mounting near the cooktop; wall-mounting isn't available at a window wall, and ceiling-mounting there blocks natural light and looks visually poor. **The bigger stated problem**: opening the window while cooking, expecting it to vent cooking smells outward, instead does the opposite — outdoor air blows inward toward the cook, pushing kitchen odors *away* from the window and out through the apartment's front door instead, the long way around through every intervening room.
- **3. Loss of upper-cabinet storage**: a window-adjacent kitchen run forfeits upper cabinets on that wall entirely, which matters disproportionately because a window-wall kitchen is already narrow (typically one wall's worth of run). **Client account of a workaround failure**: open shelving used instead of upper cabinets collects dust constantly, and proved usable only for a few decorative items — bulkier kitchen equipment (a food processor, a meat grinder) didn't fit and had to be stored elsewhere (a closet, a different room), requiring a trip to retrieve them whenever needed.
- **4. Fridge placement/door-swing conflict**: a fridge can't sit centered on the window itself, so it's forced into a side corner — where a wall can block the door from opening a full 90°, in turn blocking drawers behind that door from opening at all. **Client account of the resulting rework**: had to rehang the fridge door to the opposite hinge side (requiring a new door panel purchase), still has to walk around and press against the wall to open it, and the open door itself blocks the countertop, forcing repeated round trips between the fridge and any free counter space to unload multiple items.
- **5. Eye pupil-adaptation/glare mechanism, a genuine physiological argument, not just preference**: standing at a bright window for an extended period causes pupil constriction; moving away to a dimmer part of the kitchen then requires roughly a minute of readaptation, during which cutting or handling hot items carries a real elevated injury risk (cut fingers, burns cited as real client-reported incidents). The source adds (own framing, not attributed to a client quote) that repeated pupil constriction/dilation cycling is also a meaningful ongoing strain on eyesight.
- **6. Window-operation accessibility**: a countertop run placed directly under a window blocks normal reach to open/close the window or adjust blinds, forcing the occupant to climb onto the countertop with their feet to operate either. **Client account**: an east-facing window with strong morning glare forced the household to keep blinds permanently closed (to avoid repeatedly climbing onto the counter), and even then still had to climb up roughly twice a year to clean them, given how quickly kitchen grease/soot accumulates on window-adjacent blinds specifically.
- **7. Task lighting conflict at night**: daylight covers only part of the day, and a window-adjacent cooking zone still needs mandatory task lighting for evening use (matches this store's existing mandatory-task-lighting rule from an independent source). **Client account of a real failed retrofit**: no fixture point existed near the window for task lighting when originally planned; a later attempt to add ceiling pendant fixtures made things *worse* at night, since the fixtures reflected directly in the dark window glass, creating glare directly in the cook's eyes — the opposite of the intended fix. A further client quote frames the original "cook while looking out the window" motivation as illusory in practice: attention stays on the food being prepared, not the view, and at night the window becomes a mirror reflecting the room (and the cook) rather than showing anything outside, forcing an explicit choice between having the light on to see or off to see through the window — never both.
- **8. Radiator/condensation interaction, mechanism-explained**: a kitchen has meaningfully higher ambient humidity than most rooms (cooking steam), making its window more prone to condensation than elsewhere in the apartment — but the radiator that would normally offset this (by warming the glass) can't do its job through ~60cm of kitchen cabinetry blocking it from the window. **Client account, worked before/after**: the same window never condensed during the renovation (when humidity was arguably higher, from wet trades), but began streaming condensation once kitchen furniture was installed in front of the radiator, warping the countertop from the moisture; a later fix added a drainage grille to the affected countertop section, which stopped the visible streaming but the room stayed noticeably cold, since even the most powerful radiator can't project heat effectively through a wall of kitchen furniture.
