## Planning Rules — Real Project Layout Optimization, 3 Worked Decisions

- **Solution 1 — oversized-hallway-to-closet-niche conversion**: an original 2m-wide entrance hallway looked generous but had no usable wall run long enough for a coat closet (living-room and kitchen door openings consumed the available wall). Client's actual requirement was a kitchen-living combo (freeing the original kitchen doorway location) and a full-height coat closet. Fix: shift the living-room wall to reduce the hallway to 1.5m (explicitly stated as "still a very wide hallway" even at 1.5m — 1.5m is called the threshold for "very wide"), gaining living-room floor area *and* creating a recessed niche in the freed wall run for a full closet plus the household electrical panel and network equipment. **Reusable rule embedded in the example**: don't treat "wide hallway" as inherently good — width without a usable unbroken wall run to place furniture against is wasted area; a narrower hallway with one buildable wall segment beats a wider one with none.
- **Solution 2 — eliminating a protruding external corner at a high-traffic junction**: two walls originally met at a jog that left both a protruding exterior corner and a small, functionally useless recessed niche, right at the convergence point of traffic from two bedrooms, two bathrooms, and the kitchen-living-hallway zone — a corridor pinch-point only 80cm wide at that junction. Fix: realigned both walls (moving one 50cm one direction, the other 50cm the opposite direction) to straighten the junction, which both widened the pinch-point to a full double-width corridor and eliminated the exposed corner, freeing enough depth to fit a proper two-door sliding wardrobe at that location. **Explicit safety/wear framing for the "why fix a protruding corner" question, corroborating this store's existing curved/protruding-corner caution content from an independent source and project** (`yt_CFWkoe5dsc0`/`yt_DQfiBquc-L4`) — a protruding corner at a high-traffic convergence point gets scuffed/bumped repeatedly and complicates furniture-moving, not just an aesthetic nitpick.
- **Solution 3 — the "designer's gambit," a deliberate trade-off of one room's dimension for another's**: an adjoining kitchen (243cm wide) and living room (325cm wide) shared a wall; the kitchen was too narrow to fit three full-depth 60cm cabinet modules with proper >90° door/drawer swing clearance (a drawer, e.g. a freezer drawer, cannot be pulled out if the door in front of it can't open past ~90°), and 60cm depth doesn't fit within 243cm cleanly. Fix: shifted the shared wall 10cm into the living room (living room 325cm → 315cm, kitchen 243cm → 253cm) — explicitly framed as "losing" 10cm from the living room to "win" a genuinely usable kitchen. **Reusable rule embedded in the example**: a wide room's spare width has diminishing marginal value (315cm vs. 325cm living-room width is imperceptible in daily use — sofa-to-TV distance stays ~270cm either way), while the same 10cm is decisive for a narrow room crossing a hard functional threshold (three full 60cm modules with clear door/drawer swing vs. not) — deliberately trade width from the room where it's cheap to the room where it's expensive, rather than defaulting to "don't touch either room's footprint."
