## Materials / Rules — Genuinely New Content

- **What decorative panels are and why used**: prefabricated modules/elements mounted to walls — hide substrate unevenness, add volume/character/style, create an accent zone (e.g. behind a bed, TV zone, entryway), and protect walls from mechanical damage (chairs, bags, clothing) in high-traffic areas.
- **Panel classification axes (beyond material)**:
  - **Texture**: smooth; relief/textured ("рельефные" — wavy, cube-shaped, slat/rail types count here); natural-texture imitation (wood, marble, other stone); soft/upholstered panels.
  - **Rigidity**: rigid vs. flexible. Flexible panels can follow a wall's curvature (used for semicircular walls, transitions, wavy elements) — either a genuinely flexible base material, or an MDF-core panel faced with real veneer ("шпон") where the MDF core is scored/cut so the veneer can wrap the angle.
  - **Mounting method**: metal brackets/special fixings (typically for heavy panels); glue or "liquid nails" (жидкие гвозди), sometimes reinforced with studs/pins ("шпильки"); self-tapping screws or dowel-nails.
  - **Joint style**: butt joint with no visible seam; butt joint with a deliberately visible seam; or a gap left between panels — which can be painted, finished with a spline/fillet ("шпонка"), or left unfinished (e.g. acoustic panels, where the visible gap exposes the acoustic-felt backing).
- **Material-by-material comparison (13 distinct types)**:
  - **MDF/LDF** (medium-/low-density fiberboard) — most popular option; face finish can be lamination, real veneer, acrylic, film, or enamel — near-unlimited décor variety. Moderately moisture-resistant, durable. Easy to install, no complex substrate prep required.
  - **Polyurethane, thermoplastic polymer ("тюрополимер"), and polystyrene** (grouped together) — very lightweight, strong; common in moldings and 3D wall constructions. Practically moisture-proof, paints well; factory-pre-painted versions exist. Easy install, minimal substrate prep. Ready-made panels mimicking a classic painted-baguette/molding wall treatment exist as a pre-fabricated product.
  - **Gypsum (plaster) panels** — bought or custom/made-to-order. Heavy and fragile — installation needs high installer skill/experience, easy to break during mounting. Maximum surface detail, takes paint excellently, but afraid of moisture — **not recommended in humid or wet rooms**. Requires a perfectly flat substrate.
  - **Plastic/PVC** — budget option, maximally moisture-resistant; common in bathrooms, toilets, loggias, and other high-humidity spaces. Visually the weakest option of the set and not notably durable.
  - **Wood + veneer** — plywood or solid-wood base with real veneer glued on. Very natural/premium look, but needs stable ambient humidity and temperature over time; this base "plays" (warps) most when humidity swings in either direction (both increases and decreases). **Least suitable material for bathrooms.**
  - **Textile / acoustic panels** — not automatically the same thing (textile ≠ always acoustic, acoustic ≠ always textile) but often combined in one product. Typical structure: a soundproofing layer plus either soft upholstery or wood slats facing. Good for bedroom, home office, and TV zone — versatile. Not needed in bathrooms/loggias, since fabric variants have no moisture resistance. Requires a very flat substrate so the soundproofing layer presses evenly against the whole wall area.
  - **Cork** — made only from eco materials: agglomerated cork, cork veneer, natural wax or lacquer finish. Reasonably stable in humid rooms and adds extra sound insulation, but the material itself is soft, so it needs an ideally-prepped flat substrate.
  - **LDSP / laminated chipboard** — rarely used, but the source has encountered it both on its own jobs and on estimates it has reviewed for others. A standard-quality LDSP sheet is cut to size and glued directly to the wall, with edges pre-finished at the factory (even a normal 2mm edge band achievable). Maximally budget, and can yield a genuinely moisture-resistant panel — but the source's own recommendation is that **MDF is meaningfully more stable**. Shared main weakness with MDF: if the lamination layer is damaged, moisture penetrates quickly, especially in wet rooms (same risk profile as LDSP furniture used in bathrooms). Only smooth panels are achievable with this material — any texture has to come from the LDSP sheet's own surface pattern.
  - **Bamboo panels** — a newly popularizing option, still not widely available even in Moscow (level-1 location mention). Modern manufacturing technology; can imitate wood, metal, leather, marble, or fabric facings. Durability/performance track record is still thin, since it only recently entered the market — the source explicitly compares this to how quartz-vinyl flooring entered the market before it.
  - **Concrete / cement-composite panels** — used very rarely (the source states they've personally encountered this only a handful of times); correspondingly exclusive and expensive, with performance characteristics still not well understood in practice.

## Planning Rules — Five Stated Selection Tips

1. **Decide the panel's function first**: pure accent wall → any material the client likes; impact/mechanical protection in a hallway → MDF, preferably with a dense decorative face layer; budget option that still projects durability/security → economy LDSP panel.
2. **In humid zones, avoid wood, gypsum, and other moisture-sensitive materials** — plastic or cork are the source's actual best picks, though the source notes that "given Russian consumer taste," MDF still ends up used there in practice anyway.
3. **Account for humidity and temperature swings** — e.g. installing panels on an unheated loggia: avoid anything sensitive to either moisture or temperature; **PVC is named as the only fully suitable option** for that specific case.
4. **Match color/texture to the interior's overall style** — veneer/veneered panels for modern-style interiors; gypsum or polyurethane/thermoplastic/polystyrene for classical-style interiors, especially where the panel is meant to be painted.
5. **Plan decorative lighting for accent walls deliberately** — linear/directional light fixtures, or backlit false-wall perimeters — since 3D-textured panels won't deliver their intended "wow effect" under the wrong lighting.
6. **(Closing general advice, not numbered with the five above) Don't cut costs on installation labor for complex decorative-panel work** — installation quality/skill matters as much as material choice, especially for heavier/fragile materials (gypsum) and precise flexible-panel curve work.
