## Rules / Heuristics — Appliance Tower Slot Assignment by Usage Pattern, Not Just Ergonomic Height

- **Microwave optimal standalone height reconfirmed: ~1.10m from the floor** — at this height you can see what you're reheating and remove a full/hot dish (e.g. hot soup) more safely than from either a low countertop-level or a high upper-cabinet-level placement. A built-in oven+microwave tower column (600mm wide × 600mm deep, height matched to the top of the upper cabinet run) is described as this channel's long-standing best-practice default.
- **⚠️ Standout finding — appliance tower-slot assignment should be driven by usage *frequency* and the specific physical *maintenance/refill* action required, not just each appliance's own "ideal ergonomic height" considered in isolation.** Documented real case, told as a genuine self-correction: this channel's long-standing default places the microwave in the ergonomically-ideal ~1.10m slot and the coffee machine above it — but for a client who personally handles her own coffee-machine bean refills daily (no household help) and uses the microwave only rarely, this defaults produces a real daily annoyance: the coffee machine's bean-hopper opening ends up at roughly **1.75m** when mounted above the microwave, requiring a reach of about 60cm above the machine's own body — described as "impossible at arm's length," forcing a step stool for a task performed potentially several times a day (coffee needs ~20g of beans per cup, so a frequent coffee drinker refills often). **Fix**: swap the two — coffee machine in the lower, easily-reached slot; microwave (used rarely) in the less-convenient upper slot instead, even though this is the opposite of the ergonomically "ideal" placement for the microwave considered alone.
- **⚠️ General planning principle, explicitly derived from this case**: when assigning kitchen appliances to fixed tower-module slots, check not just how an appliance is *used* in a single interaction, but how it's *serviced/maintained* and how *often* — the appliance requiring more frequent hands-on maintenance (refilling, restocking, cleaning) should generally win the easily-reached slot, even if a different appliance's single-use ergonomics would otherwise suggest it. **A household's staffing situation matters to this decision**: the source notes that other clients with the same coffee-machine-above-microwave layout never noticed this problem because household help (a domestic worker) handled the daily bean refill for them — this specific client did the refills herself, which is what surfaced the issue.
