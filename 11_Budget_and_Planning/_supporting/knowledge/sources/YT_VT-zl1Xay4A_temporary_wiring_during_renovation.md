## Durable Facts

- **Sequencing rule**: temporary wiring should be installed only **after** the design project (layout/electrical plan) exists, but **before** interior walls are built. Reasoning: without a plan, temp lighting is placed "evenly" across the open floor area and ends up off-center relative to future rooms (or directly where a future wall will land) once walls go up. `unverified`, single-account, but logically sound and a checkable planning-order rule.
- **Routing**: temporary cable runs along the ceiling (not the floor), fixed to future-room centerlines marked on the floor from the plan — walls in an open-plan space are described as "just getting in the way" for temp wiring at this stage. Consistent with permanent-wiring ceiling-routing convention mentioned in a companion video (`XmeoZUiGwBM`).
- **Mounting-tape technique**: fasten cable to the ceiling with mounting tape (монтажная лента) folded into a single loop with **one fastening point per loop**, not stretched flat and nailed at two points. Reasoning given: two-point flat mounting doubles the fastener count needed and makes de-installation much harder (cable trapped along its length vs. a single loop you can just cut/pull down).
- **Alternative fastening without a nail gun**: drill a 6 mm hole with a hammer drill, insert the cable, then push in a dowel-clamp (дюбель-хомут) as both the fixation and the fastener — described as cheap (~100–200 RUB per pack) and adequate for temporary wiring duty.
- **Cable types for temp wiring**: soft/flexible cable types (ПВС, ПУНП/ПВВП family) — flexible types typically for lighting drops; **ПВС specifically recommended for temporary extension cords/outlet runs** because its jacket is durable enough to be walked on/kicked without damage.
- **Continuous-run principle**: on a single temporary circuit branch feeding both lights and outlets, run the cable as one unbroken loop rather than cutting/splicing at each drop point — drops for a lamp holder or outlet block are made by stripping the running cable at that point, not by cutting and rejoining it. Stated as "critically important" for reliability of the temporary circuit.
- **Temporary socket outlet spacing**: drop points for lamp holders spaced roughly **1 meter apart minimum** — closer spacing risks knocking one lamp when plugging/unplugging a cord at an adjacent drop.
- **Temporary outlet height**: the speaker criticizes the common practice of hanging temporary drop-cord outlets loosely near the ceiling and only pulling them down to working height when needed (then re-coiling them back up) — recommends instead permanently hanging them at working height (roughly torso/reach height, not specified numerically) from the start.
- **Warning about off-the-shelf commercial extension cords**: retail extension cords are called out as commonly undersized — conductor cross-section often **0.75 mm²**, occasionally up to 1.5 mm² at a premium price — and the speaker recommends builders make their own extension leads from **2.5 mm² ПВС cable** plus a separately-purchased socket, cut to the needed length, rather than buying a pre-made retail extension cord. `unverified`, single-account opinion, but a concrete/checkable spec (cross-section) worth flagging for anyone assembling site power extensions.
- **Strain-relief technique at the temporary panel**: run the incoming cable into and back out of the panel enclosure before terminating it on the terminal block, so that if someone yanks the trailing cable, the pulling force is absorbed by friction/loop slack at the enclosure entry rather than transmitted directly to the terminal connection.
- **Dedicated charging-station circuit**: recommends putting a small dedicated outlet block on its own 10 A breaker specifically for charging phones/power-tool batteries during the renovation — called a small, cheap (~100–200 RUB item), frequently-forgotten addition that avoids conflicts over shared outlets.
- **Lighting color temperature note (tangential/production-context, not a general renovation rule)**: the speaker mentions choosing ~5000-6200K daylight-white LED lamps for temporary lighting specifically because the footage is used for video production, at a stated cost of ~6,000 RUB for the lamps — this is a video-production choice, not a general homeowner recommendation, and is noted here only for completeness/context, not as a durable fact to promote.
