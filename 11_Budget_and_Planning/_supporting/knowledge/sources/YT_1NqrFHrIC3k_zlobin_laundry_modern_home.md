## Genuinely New Content

- **Utility sink philosophy extended beyond the laundry room itself**: the source argues a small sink belongs not only in the laundry/utility room but also in a garage and any technical/mechanical room — reasoning explicitly stated: a washing machine handles bulk loads efficiently, but doesn't help when you need to spot-treat one small stain, dampen a small item, wash a small garment, or just wash your hands. This is a broader placement philosophy than this page's existing utility-sink content (which is laundry-room-specific).
- **A separate hot/cold tap with a floor drain pan (поддон), distinct from the sink itself, called out as "very important"**: specifically for filling a bucket with hot or cold water, or hosing off very dirty boots after garden work — use cases the source explicitly says shouldn't be pushed onto the sink, and especially not onto a bathroom. Also covers washing a dog's paws or one's own feet after unexpected messes in a country-house setting. This is a distinct fixture (open floor-level tap + drain pan) from the raised utility sink/pet-shower-trap already recorded from earlier sources in this batch — a third, lower-tech option for the same general "wash something dirty without using the sink or bathroom" need.
- **Natural light is recommended for the utility/laundry room specifically because sorting and spot-checking laundry is visually demanding work** — the source explicitly acknowledges that in practice (including in his own architectural studio's own floor plans) this room is very often placed in the center of a floor plan with no exterior wall, calling that "acceptable but not ideal," and recommends a small window where the floor plan allows it, specifically to make visual laundry tasks easier on the eyes.
- **A dedicated flat, smooth work counter is needed specifically for inspecting/spot-checking garments** (laying out shirts and blouses to look them over and clean-check them before folding or hanging) — distinct rationale from a general folding-surface: the stated purpose is visual inspection, not just staging. The source recommends this counter can be integrated over the washer/dryer column rather than requiring separate floor footprint, and recommends siting it near the window (ties back to the natural-light point above) since inspection work benefits from good light specifically.
- **Non-mechanical drying space must be reserved regardless of whether a dryer exists, underwear-specific damage claim**: the source states plainly that experienced homemakers confirm certain laundry categories — especially underwear — are damaged by tumble-drying, so floor space for a foldable metal drying rack (or equivalent) must be planned into the room even when a dryer is also present, not treated as optional once a dryer is installed.
- **Chemical storage kept in the utility room itself rather than a separate pantry, logistics reason stated**: while chemicals *could* be stored in a pantry instead, the source recommends keeping all laundry-related chemicals (detergent, softener, etc.) in the utility room specifically to avoid repeatedly carrying bottles/canisters back and forth between rooms.
- **A dedicated "transfer closet" (разгрузочный шкаф) between the utility room and the bedrooms**: a plain closet with shelves and hanging rods, planned explicitly as a hand-off point for folded/ironed clean laundry — the source frames this as solving a real logistics problem (carrying items to bedrooms one piece at a time, or piling clean laundry on the floor before it's distributed) that becomes "a headache" if not planned for during design. Distinct from this page's existing "clean-laundry folding zone" idea (from [[YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials]]) — that's a surface inside the laundry room itself; this is a separate storage closet positioned as an intermediate stop before items reach bedrooms.
