## Plumbing

- **Three reasons to demolish standard brick boxing around utility risers
  (1)**:
  1. **Hidden defects/leaks can't be found or fixed if the boxing stays
     up.** The speaker shows a leaking pipe coupling behind a riser box
     with visible staining — demolishing the box is what let him find it;
     left in place, the leak would surface only much later (after
     finishes are done), by which point the only mitigation is living
     with the smell rather than fixing the cause.
  2. **Soundproofing a noisy riser requires removing the boxing first** —
     the box itself can't be treated for sound while still up; the
     speaker specifically cites a riser near a planned staff break room
     as a case where flush/drain noise would otherwise startle anyone
     resting there.
  3. **Space reclaim** — a standard brick box adds roughly **15 cm** to a
     wall's footprint compared to building the wall directly at the
     riser's own inspection-valve ("ревизия") plane, or bending the wall
     line in tighter still around the pipe itself; removing the box lets
     the finished wall sit that much closer to the pipe, reclaiming the
     15 cm as usable floor area the owner already paid for.
- **Commercial-code caveat (not directly residential)**: the corridor-

  коридоры уже метра-двадцати") is stated as a rule for **medical

## Mistakes / Warnings

- Leaving riser boxing in place through the rest of a renovation means
  any leak or defect behind it stays invisible until finishes are
  complete, at which point remediation options collapse to "live with the
  smell" rather than an actual repair — the source frames this as the
  primary reason boxing should always come down early, before other
  finishing work proceeds.
