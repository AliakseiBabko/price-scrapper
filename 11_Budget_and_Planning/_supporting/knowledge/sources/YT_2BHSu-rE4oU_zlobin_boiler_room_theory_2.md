## Genuinely New Content

- **⚠️ Current code (SP 402.1325800.2018 §5.6) prohibits gas equipment
  inside a bathroom or WC (any wet room), but does NOT prohibit it in a
  kitchen or a laundry/utility room** — explicitly contrasted against the
  Soviet-era norm many people grew up with (a gas water heater installed
  directly in the bathroom), which the source states the government is
  now actively phasing out for safety reasons. **The mechanism explained
  for the wet-room prohibition specifically**: installing a toilet,
  shower pan, or sink inside what was meant to be a boiler/heat-generator
  room automatically reclassifies that room as a "sanitary" wet room under
  the code, which then makes gas equipment in it non-compliant — the
  prohibition follows from the room's *fixture content*, not a separate
  standalone gas rule. **Directly relevant to this household's own
  apartment if it currently has (or its building stock commonly has) a
  gas water heater in the bathroom** — this would need relocating to a
  kitchen or laundry/utility space to be current-code-compliant, not
  simply replaced in place. `Russia level 1` — not independently confirmed
  to apply to Belarus, but a strong prompt to check the Belarus equivalent
  given how directly this touches a real, common apartment condition.
  [source: this transcript]
- **A manufacturer-recommendation-vs-code-classification "gotcha" for
  closed-combustion-chamber gas boilers ("тип C" per European
  classification)**: cited to direct correspondence with Buderus
  (equipment manufacturer, explicitly flagged by the source as not an ad),
  the manufacturer itself recommends a "B22"-type connection — drawing
  combustion air from *inside* the building rather than outside — for cold
  climates, since it improves equipment performance. **The catch**: when a
  gas-utility inspector sees an indoor combustion-air intake on a
  nominally closed-chamber unit, they reclassify the installation as an
  *open*-combustion-chamber system for code-compliance purposes, which
  then triggers the stricter minimum-room-volume and ventilation
  requirements the manufacturer's own recommendation was trying to avoid
  needing. A real installation-planning trap worth knowing before
  following a manufacturer's efficiency recommendation at face value for
  any gas combustion appliance, not just a house-scale boiler.
- **Terminology correction, minor but worth recording for future source
  reading**: the technically correct term per SP 41-108-2004 for a
  ≤100kW-capacity gas equipment room is "теплогенераторная" (heat-generator
  room), not "котельная" (boiler room) — the latter is colloquial/common
  usage the source continues using deliberately for accessibility. Useful
  to know both terms appear interchangeably across this channel's videos
  and other Russian-language sources on this general topic.
