## Rules / Heuristics — Positive Findings (praised, not flagged as mistakes)

- **A wide entry hall with a deep (80cm) closet is achievable without feeling cramped when width is deliberately allocated at the design stage** — praised as rare among developer-default layouts.
- **Milled/routed cabinet and closet door handles (no protruding pulls) are both safer (no snag/impact hazard) and cleaner-looking than traditional protruding handles** — reiterated as this channel's standing convention, consistently applied.
- **A dedicated, deliberately-planned open floor zone plus its own outlet for a robot vacuum avoids two problems at once**: the vacuum has full unobstructed territory, and the alternative (trimming a closet short to make room reactively) would expose an unfinished closet end-panel. Corroborates this vault's general "plan the fixture's footprint before, not after, finalizing adjacent furniture" principle, applied here to a robot vacuum specifically.
- **Every room should have at minimum two independent lighting circuits (a daytime/general circuit and a night/accent circuit)**, consolidated onto smart switches so occupants aren't managing many physical switches for circuits they rarely use independently — the clients' own retrospective admission: the original as-wired granular multi-circuit lighting (several independent zones per room) went almost entirely unused in practice once installed, and smart-switch reprogramming was needed afterward to consolidate them onto fewer physical controls. **General cost-driver caution: highly granular lighting-circuit wiring is easy to over-spec at design time and commonly goes unused — plan for 2 real circuits per room (day/night) as the practical default, not more, unless a specific documented need exists.**
- **⚠️ Smart-switch relay click noise near a bedroom is a real, checkable nuisance, not a hypothetical one** — the source flags this directly: some smart-switch relay types click audibly on activation, and this is disruptive specifically in a quiet nighttime bedroom setting. **Verify a smart switch's relay noise level before installing one near a sleeping area**, independent of its other features.
- **A decorative light strip with a deliberate visual break/gap (rather than one continuous unbroken run) reads as more intentional design** — the source's own aesthetic reasoning: an unbroken light line running the length of a hallway starts to resemble an aircraft emergency-exit path marker rather than a design feature.
- **Diagonal single-contour parquet flooring, while expensive and difficult to install correctly, visually enlarges the perceived size of small rooms** — praised as effective even in genuinely small rooms in this apartment.
- **Decorative (textured) wall plaster is a durable, high-impact-resistant finish well-suited to a household with active children** — praised specifically as chip/scratch-resistant in practice (the source could not find any damage despite two active kids), contrasted against paint-ready wallpaper as the cheaper/more practical default recommendation for most other cases.
- **A kids'-bathroom sink serving two children benefits from either two separate basins or one wide basin with two faucets** — specifically to prevent morning conflict over sink access between siblings; a genuinely new, checkable fixture-planning rule for a multi-child household.
- **Concealed/hidden interior doors are justified specifically when positioned within or adjacent to a main TV-viewing sightline** — a direct nuance to this vault's existing "concealed doors carry real hidden costs, often oversold" caution (see `13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/analysis/Concealed_Door_Considerations.md`): here, the source explicitly endorses the choice because a visible door casing in that specific sightline would have been a distracting visual element during TV viewing — "in another renovation I wouldn't understand this choice, but here it's justified."
- **A visible interior-fixture switch bank (e.g. bathroom light switches near a shared living space) can be an acceptable placement specifically when it avoids protruding into or interrupting a TV-facing wall** — another sightline-driven exception, same reasoning as the concealed-door point above.
- **Door swing direction can legitimately be decided by where a functional door-stop can physically be mounted, not a fixed universal rule** — the source explicitly praises a case where one bathroom door swings inward (no room for an outward stop) and the adjacent bathroom's door swings outward (an inward swing would hit the toilet) as both being the objectively correct call for their respective constraints, rather than picking one direction as a blanket default.
