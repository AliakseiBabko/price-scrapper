## Durable, Broadly-Reusable Facts & Rules (Level 1)

- **Labor-cost economics worked example**: a 100 m² design-project renovation takes ~10 months with 2 craftsmen; each craftsman is paid a minimum ~150,000 RUB/month as an independent contractor (no pay during illness/vacation, justifying the rate) — 2 × 150,000 × 10 months ≈ 3,000,000 RUB in wages alone, before foreman, taxes, and company margin. Offered as the underlying reason a managed company's rate exceeds direct-hire pricing. `single-account`, but a specific, checkable line of reasoning.
- **Segment-matching philosophy, stated plainly**: don't commission a premium designer renovation in a low-value secondary-market unit (it won't "fit" the property or resell well); don't do bare cosmetic work in an expensive new-build (wastes the property's potential). `confirmed`, general advice, consistent with a similar segment-matching theme already in this store from `yt_DsdLa87Acz4`.
- **Budget-constrained buyers advised to prefer a developer-finished unit over a bare-shell unit they might not be able to fully renovate**: running out of money mid-renovation (e.g. stalling at the tiling stage) leaves a worse outcome than buying an already-finished, if unremarkable, developer unit for the same total money. `single-account`, but a specific, plausible risk-avoidance argument.
- **Resale/personalization mismatch — corroborates a theme already in this store from a different practitioner**: a highly personalized, high-quality renovation (concealed systems, individual taste) will typically **not** be valued at full cost by the resale market, which credits renovations closer to a generic ~100,000 RUB/m² regardless of actual build quality — because buyer taste varies and personalized choices don't universally appeal. This is offered as the reason flipping-focused renovations deliberately use cheaper, more generic finishes. `single-account`, but directly corroborates (from an independent practitioner) the segment/resale-matching logic already recorded from `yt_DsdLa87Acz4` (Prolife Invest).
- **Plumbing brand substitution under cost pressure**: the company has substituted premium Italian/German plumbing brands (filters, collectors, leak-protection systems, pressure reducers) for Chinese/Russian equivalents to control rough-material cost inflation, while **keeping the same equipment set** (filter + collector + leak protection + reducer) — framed as a brand downgrade, not a scope reduction. For self-managed/budget-constrained buyers, the speaker explicitly says it's reasonable to *reduce the equipment set itself* (fewer valves/reducers = fewer failure points) as a legitimate cost-saving move, distinct from just downgrading brands within a fixed set.
- **Concrete cautionary anecdote on flush pre-filters**: a client skipped installing a 100-micron flush pre-filter to save money; sediment buildup caused a wall-hung toilet's cistern float mechanism to jam after the water was shut off during a month-long vacation, requiring an emergency repair callout. The company now installs flush filters as a standard, non-negotiable line item on every project as a direct result. `confirmed`, specific and technical, not vague.
- **Inflation-vs-savings timing advice**: the speaker advises against incrementally saving toward a "quality" renovation, since renovation costs (materials, furniture, labor) tend to inflate faster than typical personal savings accumulate — recommends committing to a simplified scope within current means now, or deferring the purchase/renovation decision entirely, rather than saving toward a moving target. `single-account`, opinion, not independently verified.
