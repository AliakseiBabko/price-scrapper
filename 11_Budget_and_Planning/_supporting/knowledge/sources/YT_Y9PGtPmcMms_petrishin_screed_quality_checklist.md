## Screed — Acceptance/QC Checklist (all items new for this store)

1. **Staged-payment negotiation heuristic**: agree to pay in two
   installments — 50% at the start, the remaining 50% only after all work
   is finished and the final result is visible — framed as fair to both
   client and crew. Must be agreed before work starts, not renegotiated
   mid-job.
2. **A zero-measurement quote is a red flag for an incompetent crew**: a
   contractor who offers to show up and pour screed with no on-site
   measurement first is a warning sign — a real measurement is needed to
   check whether pipe/conduit clearances, door-opening height, or a
   too-thin/too-thick target thickness will actually work at this
   specific site.
3. **Screed strength depends on four factors, and cutting cement is a
   named contractor-dishonesty motive**: sand:cement ratio (some crews
   deliberately under-dose cement to pocket the savings, producing
   cracks), compaction/tamping, target thickness, and the drying/curing
   regimen (below). Skipped tamping is called "the single biggest
   mistake" — it leaves micro-voids that crack later.
4. **Two field compaction-QC tests, both new to this store**: (1) walk
   across the poured screed the next day in rubber "concrete boots" — it
   should not sink or leave footprints if properly tamped; (2) pour water
   onto the surface — on a well-compacted screed, the water sits on top
   and absorbs in gradually, rather than rapidly disappearing into the
   body of the screed.
5. **Minimum-thickness recommendation stronger than the nominal
   standard, with a stated real-world failure case**: the generally
   accepted minimum is 4cm, but the company recommends **6cm+** as a
   value that "won't crack" — they state they've had to demolish a
   4cm screed that was poured in full technical compliance and still
   cracked. Where full thickness isn't achievable over water/electrical
   conduits, reinforce that section with a metal mesh laid over the
   conduits.
6. **Detailed curing/drying protocol, a fourth distinct protocol variant
   for this store (not merged with existing screed-curing numbers per
   this project's non-blending convention)**: cover the poured screed
   with damp rags plus heavy watering, then plastic film, for **1 week**;
   remove the film and check whether the rags have dried out — if dry,
   the screed has fully absorbed that moisture and needs re-wetting;
   repeat the water+cover cycle every **3-5 days** as needed, then let
   the screed dry undisturbed for a further **21 days** to reach full
   design strength. (Round 1's semi-mechanized-screed video from this
   same channel gave a different cycle — 3-4-day film/water cycles at
   ≈1-1.5 L/m², 14-day full film removal, 10-day load-avoidance window —
   recorded as a distinct protocol variant, not averaged with this one.)
7. **A specific written-vs-verbal tolerance bait-and-switch warning, a
   new contractor-honesty red flag**: some crews verbally quote a
   generous tolerance (e.g. "2mm per 2m") to win the job, then present
   different, looser written tolerances once work begins and the client
   has no leverage — get the tolerance figure in writing before work
   starts, not just a verbal quote.
8. **Final acceptance tooling and numeric tolerances**: 2m straightedge
   + laser level. Straightedge-to-screed gap must not exceed **2mm**
   anywhere, checked in multiple locations/directions; a 5-ruble coin
   pressed into any gap must not sink in (same coin-test technique as
   this channel's plastering-acceptance video, now applied to floor
   screed); a bubble level placed on the straightedge must read centered
   everywhere (confirms no unintended slope); a laser level cross-check
   should show no more than **2mm** deviation from true horizontal at
   any point.
9. **Corners are the most common screed defect location, with a stated
   mechanism**: the power trowel ("вертолёт"/helicopter finishing
   machine) physically can't reach into corners, so corners must be
   hand-finished separately — crews that skip or under-do this leave a
   detectable level "jump" right at the corner.
10. **Deformation-joint layout detail, extending this store's existing
    screed-expansion-joint content with a specific corner technique**:
    besides perimeter damper tape along every wall-floor junction and
    joints between rooms, cut a **diagonal T-shaped relief cut from every
    external/outside corner of the room** — outside corners concentrate
    the highest stress in a screed slab, and this cut relieves it
    deliberately rather than letting a crack form there on its own.
11. **QC-timing rule with a stated cost mechanism**: inspect the screed
    while the mix is still fresh/wet ("living"), not after it's cured —
    a defect caught while the material is still workable can be
    corrected on the spot; the same defect caught the next day (once
    cured) can only be fixed via a self-leveling top-up layer or
    mechanical grinding, both slower and more expensive. Also explicitly
    agree with the contractor in advance who is responsible for watering/
    covering the screed during the curing window — someone must actively
    own that process, not assume it happens automatically.
