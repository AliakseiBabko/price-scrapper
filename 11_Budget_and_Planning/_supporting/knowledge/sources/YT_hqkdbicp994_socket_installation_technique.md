## Durable Facts

- **Remove factory mounting screws from socket boxes (подрозетники) before rough finishing work (plastering/puttying/painting), not during final installation** — reasoning: if removed only at final-fit time, the screw holes will already be clogged with plaster/putty/paint residue, making them harder to use.
- **Cable jacket must be stripped with a dedicated cable-stripping knife, never a regular utility knife** — reasoning: a regular knife makes it easy to nick the conductor's insulation underneath the jacket, creating a latent defect; a proper cable-stripping knife is designed to cut only the outer jacket without reaching the insulation.
- **Conductor insulation must be stripped with a dedicated wire-stripping tool, not a knife** — same reasoning (risk of nicking the copper strand itself). Framed as a checkable red flag: if you see an installer stripping insulation with a knife, the speaker says to stop them.
- **Standard terminal color/position convention** (as demonstrated): phase (live) on the left terminal, neutral on the right, ground in the middle — presented as the convention used in this installation, though whether this maps to a formal universal code standard is not stated in the transcript. `unverified` — treat as this installer's stated convention, not confirmed as a universal wiring code rule.
- **Modern finish outlets use self-clamping (screwless) terminal connectors** — cable insertion alone completes the clamp; consistent with the same claim made independently in the companion tooling video (`XmeoZUiGwBM`).
- **Leveling technique**: use a small magnetic bubble level that clips onto the metal part of the outlet body, allowing hands-free leveling while the installer's hands are free to also handle the mounting screws — the leveler is left in place until final tightening of the mounting "ears"/expansion tabs, specifically so the outlet doesn't shift out of level during that last step.
