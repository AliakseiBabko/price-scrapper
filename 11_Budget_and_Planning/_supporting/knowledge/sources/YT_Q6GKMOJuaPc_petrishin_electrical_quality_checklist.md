## Electrical — Rough-In Coordination & QC (new items only)

1. **Furniture-maker coordination timing rule, new planning mechanism**:
   before rough-electrical work starts, have the furniture makers
   (`мебельщики`) come measure and propose their own outlet/switch
   placement preferences (for built-in cabinetry/appliances) *first*, and
   fold any changes into the rough-electrical-stage layout — the company's
   stated experience is that furniture installers routinely show up after
   rough-in is done and ask for relocated outlets anyway, so front-loading
   this avoids the later rework.
2. **Kitchen built-in-appliance socket placement rule**: don't place
   outlets directly behind a built-in appliance (blocks flush installation)
   — put them to the left/right of the appliance, or lower in the (usually
   removable) plinth/kickplate area instead, so the appliance can sit flush
   against the wall.
3. **Cooktop feed-cable length rule**: leave at least 1.5m of cable at the
   cooktop (`варочная панель`) location, since cooktops are usually
   hard-wired directly rather than plugged in — insufficient length forces
   a splice/re-solder later.
4. **Leak-sensor shutoff valve ("аквасторож") power feed reminder**: if
   the apartment will have an automatic water-shutoff/leak-sensor unit,
   its power feed must be run during the rough-electrical stage — flagged
   as commonly forgotten.
5. **Outlet-box flush-fit QC technique**: after plastering, every outlet
   box must sit flush with the wall surface — check with a wide putty
   knife/spatula held against the box: it should show no rocking, wobble,
   or gap. A visible gap under a socket/switch frame reads as a defect
   especially on light-colored walls. **One stated exception**: on a wall
   that will get sound-insulation panels, boxes should intentionally
   protrude past the raw wall by exactly the future soundproofing
   thickness, ideally using sound-absorbing-rated boxes for that wall.
6. **Ceiling priming rationale specific to stretch ceilings**: prime the
   ceiling (paint can serve as the primer) before a stretch ceiling goes
   up, so loose dust/debris from an unfinished slab doesn't keep falling
   onto the membrane afterward — stated as a low-cost line item relative
   to the rest of the budget.
7. **Floor-routed wiring over sound/waterproofing membrane — no clips,
   use tie-wrapped mesh instead**: when electrical runs are laid on top of
   an already-installed sound/waterproofing membrane, secure the cable to
   a metal mesh (3mm wire, 50×50mm cell) laid over the membrane using
   plastic zip ties — never puncture the membrane with clips/staples, which
   degrades its function.
8. **Underfloor-heating sensor conduit rule**: route the heated-floor
   sensor in a 16mm-diameter Rehau (PE-Xa) or metal-plastic pipe, not the
   thin corrugated tube some sensor manufacturers bundle — the sensor must
   be able to slide freely to the pipe's far end so it can be pulled and
   replaced later if it fails; the manufacturer-supplied corrugated sleeve
   is described as too thin for reliable future extraction.
9. **Low-voltage transformer ceiling-avoidance rule**: never mount
   step-down transformers for low-voltage lighting circuits inside the
   ceiling — they fail more often there. Keep transformer access open,
   and if a lot of LED strip lighting is planned (correspondingly many
   transformers), consider routing them to a separate dedicated
   distribution point instead of scattering them through the ceiling void.
