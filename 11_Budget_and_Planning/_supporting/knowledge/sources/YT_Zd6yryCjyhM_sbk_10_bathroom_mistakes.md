## Bathroom / Plumbing — 10-Point Mistakes List

1. **Tub-in-three-walls corroborated a fourth time in this store**: for business/premium segment work, a non-freestanding tub is always installed against three walls — building a decorative "presentation" arrangement with shelving around a tub not fully enclosed is called an unavoidable mold-risk/craftsmanship compromise, not a viable design alternative. Consistent with this round's own video 1 and this store's existing tub-installation content.
2. **⚠️ Leak-protection sensor system — always installed, described mechanism matches this store's existing content closely**: floor sensors detect water reaching the floor (e.g. from an unattended overflowing tub) and signal a control unit that automatically shuts the water supply, regardless of whether anyone is present to notice. Variants exist with Wi-Fi remote control and app-based monitoring. **Genuinely new detail**: sensors can be installed flush/recessed into the floor (not just surface-laid) for a cleaner look — this speaker's own stated practice on all of the company's projects.
3. **Pressure testing ("опрессовка") must be performed above normal working pressure, or leak risk is real** — thin restatement of this store's existing, more detailed pressure-testing protocol (`12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Pressure_Testing.md`). **New documentation-transparency practice**: this company records the entire pressure-test process on video and shares it into the client's own project chat, as a trust/verification measure.
4. **⚠️ Gypsum plaster requires two coats of acrylic waterproofing before tiling, or the tile adhesive reacts with the gypsum and destabilizes**: skipping this causes the tile-adhesive-to-plaster interface to degrade ("turn to mush") and tile eventually detaches. Cement-based plaster doesn't strictly require this waterproofing layer, but the speaker recommends applying it anyway as a low-cost extra safeguard. Corroborates and adds a specific coat-count (two) to this store's existing, extensive gypsum-vs-cement-plaster-in-wet-rooms content (`12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Waterproofing_and_Plastering.md`).
5. **⚠️ Avoid drywall (GKL) ceilings in a bathroom specifically because of flood risk from the unit above, cited from direct repeated experience**: the speaker states his own company has had 3-4 objects flooded multiple times each within a single year during construction — stretch ceilings are far cheaper/faster to remove and reinstall after a flood than fully redoing painted drywall. Consistent with, and a more emphatic restatement of, this round's video 1 and this store's existing stretch-ceiling flood-argument content.
6. **⚠️ New mechanism: never buy clearance/close-out tile from limited remaining stock**: tile develops internal stress during cutting and a normal fraction will crack regardless of installer skill — a real, expected process requiring spare stock, not a sign of poor workmanship. Buying a discontinued or near-sold-out tile line at exactly the quantity needed leaves no margin for this normal breakage, and if the specific collection is truly gone, matching replacement tile may be unobtainable at any price, or require expensive cross-city shipping for just 1-2 tiles.
7. **⚠️ Related new mechanism: never buy tile by the exact piece count shown in a store's layout diagram**: a diagram showing "45 tiles needed" and purchasing exactly 45 ignores the same cutting-breakage margin above — the installer will run short mid-job, need to special-order more, lose time and pay extra shipping, and (compounding point 6) may be unable to get a matching batch if the collection has since sold out or been discontinued.
8. **⚠️ Tile adhesive selection by class letter/number code (S1/S2/C1/C2, etc.), not by price**: these letter/number codes on the bag denote adhesives formulated for different tile weights, formats, substrates, and room types — buying by price alone (an implicit ≈300 RUB vs. ≈1,000 RUB per-bag price contrast is given as illustrative, not converted/benchmarked) risks a genuine mismatch between adhesive and tile/substrate, not just a quality difference. Genuinely new, checkable buying criterion for this store.
9. **Horizontal sewer-pipe routing must use two 45° elbows joined together, never a single 90° elbow, to avoid clogging** — direct corroboration of this store's existing sewage-elbow-routing rule (already recorded, `yt_` sourced separately) with an independent source and consistent reasoning (smoother flow, easier to clear).
10. **⚠️ New mechanism: pipe thermal-insulation sleeves prevent physical damage from thermal expansion, not just condensation/energy loss**: a bare (uninsulated) pipe embedded directly in plaster/tile adhesive has nowhere to expand into as it heats — the expansion force can crack the surrounding plaster/adhesive or even puncture the pipe itself, and any resulting hole becomes a real leak (compounding directly with point 2 above if no leak-protection system exists). This is a distinct mechanism from this store's existing pipe-insulation-sleeve-durability content (18mm ID / 6mm wall spec, tear-resistance framing) — this source adds the thermal-expansion-clearance rationale specifically.
