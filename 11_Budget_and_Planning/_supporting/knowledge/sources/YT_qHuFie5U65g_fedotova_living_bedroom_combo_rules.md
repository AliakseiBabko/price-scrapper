## Design Concept — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Two fundamental structural approaches to combining a living room and
  bedroom in one room, with stated trade-offs for each**:
  1. **Sofa-only** (a fold-out/sleeper sofa, no separate bed) —
     **advantages**: significantly more open floor space for movement
     (particularly valuable for frequent guests/gatherings), and frees
     room for additional furniture (armchair, desk, a large wardrobe).
     **disadvantages**: must be folded/unfolded daily, a real recurring
     chore (bedding has to be put away every morning before work) — the
     source notes many people leave it unfolded for a week at a time,
     which becomes impractical the moment guests visit; and genuinely
     good sleeper sofas force a three-way trade-off between (a) real
     orthopedic-quality mattress/base, (b) attractive/stylish design when
     folded, and (c) affordability — realistically only two of the three
     at once (an "accordion"-mechanism sofa gives a flat, orthopedic-
     quality sleeping surface but a bulky, unattractive folded profile; a
     genuinely well-designed French-fold sleeper with a real orthopedic
     mattress costs considerably more).
  2. **Bed + separate sofa** (two distinct zones) — **advantages**: a
     real orthopedic mattress with zero daily fold/unfold effort, the bed
     simply stays made. **disadvantages**: eats real floor area — **a
     full double bed plus adequate access on both sides needs a minimum
     of ~9m² dedicated to the sleep zone alone**, which in a typical
     16-22m² combined room (the source's own stated common range) leaves
     only **~9-12m² for the living/sofa zone** — a concrete, checkable
     room-sizing rule for this exact layout decision.
- **Decision heuristic, stated explicitly**: choose based on actual
  lifestyle, not a default preference — guest frequency/size (a household
  that rarely hosts more than a couple of guests can comfortably run a
  bed+sofa combo even with only 9-12m² of living-zone space; frequent
  larger gatherings favor the sofa-only approach for its extra floor
  area) and personal tolerance for daily folding/unfolding. **If storage
  need is the dominant priority**, consider dropping a dedicated bed zone
  entirely in favor of large wardrobes or even a walk-in closet instead.
- **Bed-vs-sofa placement relative to the entry — explicitly "no
  universally right or wrong answer, it depends on the room's own
  geometry"**: if the room has a recessed niche/alcove near the entry,
  place the bed in that niche and put the living/sofa zone nearer the
  window; if the room is a straight walk-through with no recess, it's
  better to place the living/sofa zone in the entry/walkway path and put
  the bed further in, near the window, away from entry traffic. A
  concrete, geometry-conditioned placement rule rather than a fixed
  default.
- **Physical zone-dividers for separating a sleep zone from a living
  zone, ranked with stated trade-offs**:
  - Heavy curtains/drapes matching the window treatment — cheapest
    ("econom") option; can be opened for one shared space or closed to
    section off the sleep zone at will.
  - A glass partition — recommended specifically in glass so daylight
    still reaches the far zone, with some incidental sound-dampening
    benefit.
  - **Glass partition + curtains combined** — described as the more
    complete solution: glass blocks some sound, curtains add visual
    privacy on top.
  - A raised platform/podium — mentioned only briefly as another option,
    not elaborated with reasoning.
  - Furniture (e.g. a bookshelf) used as a divider — explicitly flagged
    as the designer's own less-favored option, since it blocks a large
    amount of light from reaching the far part of the room.
- **Wall-mounted transformer/foldaway furniture system — the designer's
  own explicitly stated top recommendation for this room type**: a
  chipboard-based wall-mounted mechanism that a sofa attaches to; by day
  it presents as a full living-room sofa (with room left over for e.g. an
  armchair and clear floor for walking), and in the evening the unit
  folds down to reveal a complete bed with its own mattress underneath —
  no daily sheet-stripping/bed-making required, unlike a conventional
  sleeper sofa. The source notes **multiple Russian-market manufacturers
  now produce this type of unit** as of filming, and suggests approaching
  a local custom furniture maker if a ready-made unit isn't available in
  a given market.
