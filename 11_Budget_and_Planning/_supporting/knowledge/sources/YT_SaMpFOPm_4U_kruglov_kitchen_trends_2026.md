## Genuinely New Content

1. **No-upper-cabinets design trend, with an explicit storage-relocation caveat**: increasingly popular for 2026, but the source is explicit that removing upper cabinets from one wall *requires* relocating that lost storage elsewhere (an island, or another vertical storage module on a different wall) — not simply dropping the storage capacity. Framed as stylish/modern but not universally suitable.
2. **⚠️ Quartz-agglomerate countertop with an undermount sink made of the same quartz-agglomerate material — a newer manufacturing capability, not universal**: previously only a glued-on (surface-mount) sink was possible with this material; some manufacturers can now produce an integrated undermount sink from the same quartz agglomerate, though a visible seam remains and not every manufacturer offers it. **Properties**: premium appearance, does not absorb moisture (so staining liquids don't penetrate), very high strength, high resistance to household chemicals, a tactile feel close to natural stone. **Cons**: high cost, and the seam can never be fully eliminated even with this integrated-sink capability.
3. **⚠️ Compact-laminate ("compact-plate") countertop with an integrated same-material undermount sink, positioned as a "Comfort+" tier below quartz agglomerate on price but above plastic countertops on performance**: thin panel profile (a current trend in its own right), high impact resistance, good chemical resistance, heat resistance lower than quartz agglomerate but substantially higher than any plastic countertop, wide range of textures/colors, comparatively simple installation. **Cons**: scratches slightly more readily than quartz agglomerate; noticeably harder to repair chips than quartz agglomerate.
4. **⚠️ Dish-drying rack relocated into a lower drawer instead of an upper cabinet — a real ergonomic/practical trend**: benefits a shorter-statured cook (everything within reach without stretching up), benefits anyone who cooks frequently (no need to keep an upper cabinet door open mid-cook, especially relevant if that upper cabinet is hinged rather than lift-mechanism), reduces the risk of dropping/breaking dishware while lifting it up to a high shelf, and a lower-drawer rack typically holds more capacity and is easier to use day-to-day than an equivalent upper-cabinet rack.
5. **⚠️ Steam-function oven, a growing trend, described as a scaled-down home version of a professional combi-steamer** (the type used in high-end restaurant kitchens) — available at both premium and mid-price tiers (mid-tier is framed as a real but compromised option; premium tier has "no compromises"). **Named benefits**: food comes out juicier/softer, prevents over-drying, speeds up cooking, improves baking quality, and is specifically excellent for *reheating* — worked example: pizza reheated in a steam oven reportedly tastes/feels like freshly baked, versus visibly worse texture/taste from microwave reheating.
6. **⚠️ Integrated-hood cooktop (venting built into the cooktop itself), a premium-segment answer to the no-upper-cabinet trend**: solves the "where does the hood go" problem once upper cabinets (and their hood-mounting wall) are removed — requires the ventilation duct route to be planned well in advance, most often routed under the screed, and these units are physically larger than a standard cooktop, so unsuitable for a small kitchen. **Named manufacturers**: Bora, Bosch, Miele, Smeg (heard as "Smack"), Siemens, and Elica.
7. **Pull-out step/drawer built into the cabinet plinth (toe-kick), two use-cases**: under the oven, holds baking trays/pans and other oversized cookware; a step-equipped version doubles as a literal step-up aid for a shorter person to reach higher shelves without needing a kitchen step-stool.
8. **⚠️ Monochrome/minimalist facade aesthetic named as the dominant 2026 trend, with a named technique list**: integrated or entirely absent handles, calm/muted tones, and matching the countertop and backsplash in the *same material* (not just a coordinating color). For clients who still prefer a classical (non-minimalist) facade profile but want calmer tones/textures, "neoclassic milling" (a routed facade profile) is offered as the compromise option.
