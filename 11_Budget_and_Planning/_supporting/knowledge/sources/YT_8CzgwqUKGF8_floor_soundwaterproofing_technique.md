## Durable Facts (Level 1, `single-account`/`unverified` throughout)

- **Heating disconnect safety sequence before floor demo/prep**: developer-installed temporary heating is typically a throwaway "времянка." Correct sequence: (1) close the shared riser shutoff valve in the building's stairwell/utility closet, (2) open the radiators' bleed ("Maevsky") valves and confirm no pressure remains, (3) only then cut the main heating loop with an angle grinder. Cutting pipes before confirming depressurization is flagged as a common, dangerous shortcut.
- **Two-pass cleaning rule before priming/leveling**: knock down construction dust with a garden pump sprayer, sweep with a long-handled broom, bag it — then do this a *second* time after grinding down protrusions/bumps with a rotary hammer + chisel bit (especially at wall perimeters and around ventilation shafts), because leftover bumps under the waterproofing membrane cause the first course of interior-wall blocks to sit at an angle, requiring ~10x more correction time later. Skipping the first cleaning pass is invisible to a customer (the waterproofing hides it) but is still avoided as standard practice.
- **Priming is mandatory before floor leveling and before applying waterproofing to walls**: without a primed base, cement-sand leveling compound won't bond to the substrate, and waterproofing membrane won't adhere to unprimed walls. Priming is called out as specifically required in high-humidity rooms.
- **Mixing order**: always pour water into the bucket first, then add the dry mix, then mix with a drill paddle (not the reverse).
- **Membrane orientation matters**: the membrane roll must be laid so the *adhesive* edge (not the felt edge) faces the wall; at seams, the second roll must be tucked *under* the first, not overlapped on top of it, otherwise the joint has no felt underneath and produces poor sound isolation at that seam — identifiable later by a visible longitudinal dip in the finished floor.
- **Wall overlap height rule**: before laying membrane, the crew determines the planned screed level and instructs installers to run the membrane up the wall at least 3 cm above that level, so the excess can be trimmed after the screed is poured and the joint then sealed with acoustic sealant.
- **Save membrane offcuts, don't discard them during or after installation** — offcuts are used to reinforce/tape all penetrations at load-bearing columns and ventilation shafts, and are kept even after the main installation to patch punctures caused later by building maintenance crews replacing central heating risers.
- **Unified (combined) sound+waterproofing membrane preferred over separate sound and waterproofing layers**, despite being harder to install, because it's stated to cut installation time roughly in half and allows the whole floor to be completed in a single work shift.
- **Torch-welding (heat-sealing) seams — safety and technique**: uses cheap spatulas, standard gas canisters, and a garden-type piezo igniter (no specialized tool). Always bring at least 2 spare piezo igniters and 2 spare gas canisters to a job, since both fail/have defect rates high enough to stall work for hours if there's no backup. **A fire extinguisher must be present on-site any time open-flame torch work is being done** — stated as a rule frequently forgotten by both crews and customers.
- Torch-welding at wall junctions is done only in high-humidity rooms (elsewhere acoustic sealant alone is used); torch-welding around load-bearing columns is always done because the membrane is installed there in pieced/cut sections that need fusing. Top seams are torch-welded *in addition to* standard adhesive bonding as a deliberate redundancy measure to eliminate leak risk, described as going "200% instead of the standard 100%."
- Additional gluing at ventilation-shaft transitions is only performed on sites where the screed is poured as one continuous pour before interior walls are built up — a sequencing-dependent detail, not universal practice.
- **Never fully seal off the area around sewage stack risers with the waterproofing membrane** — a deliberate gap must be left there so that, in the event of a flood from an upper floor, water has a path to drain through to the floor below rather than pooling inside the apartment; fully sealing this area is described as actively harmful to the owner.
