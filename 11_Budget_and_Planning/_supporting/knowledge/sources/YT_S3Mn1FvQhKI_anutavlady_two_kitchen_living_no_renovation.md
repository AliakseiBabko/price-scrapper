## Design Concept / Furniture — Durable Facts & Rules (genuinely new items only)

- **Surface-mounted track-light retrofit for an off-center ceiling
  light, no wall/ceiling work required**: install a track rail (a 3m
  length, or shorter segments joined with connectors) and mount
  multiple fixture types along it wherever light is actually needed —
  a real no-renovation fix for a light source positioned off to one
  side of a room instead of centered.
- **Choose a sofa with a fully finished back on both sides specifically
  so it can be placed with its back toward a functional work zone** (in
  this case a child's desk) — lets the desk read as visually "tucked
  away" behind the sofa within an otherwise open room, without a
  physical partition.
- **Custom shallow (60cm-depth) built storage unit for utility items
  behind a curtain**: a made-to-order laminated-particleboard (ЛДСП)
  shelving unit specifically sized to house a drying rack (on open side
  shelves) and vacuum-cleaner storage (bottom), with a horizontal
  stiffening rib for shelf stability, topped by a single-row curtain
  track and concealed behind a curtain — **deliberately hemmed shorter
  than floor length so a robot vacuum can pass underneath**. Framed as
  a low-cost fix (little material, made-to-order but simple) for
  keeping several utilitarian household items out of sight without a
  full closet build.
- **Furniture should generally be bought as individual pieces from
  different collections, not as matched sets** (bedroom sets, sofa+
  chair sets, table+chair sets) — reads as more current/stylish than a
  uniform set.
- **When deliberately mixing classic and contemporary styles, blend at
  the individual-piece level, not the whole-zone level** — e.g. pair a
  classic table with contemporary chairs (or vice versa), rather than
  placing an untouched contemporary kitchen block directly beside an
  untouched classic dining-furniture block, which reads as two
  disconnected style zones rather than one coherently mixed room.
- **Use an existing rug's full color palette (including secondary
  accent colors, not just the dominant tone) as the source for
  selecting new upholstery/chair colors** — a concrete worked example
  of deriving a new furniture color from an already-present textile.
- **Vertical/venetian blinds are flagged as a maintenance burden (dust
  collection, hard to clean) vs. a Roman shade**, which is fully
  removable and washable and reads more cohesive alongside classic-
  style decor elements.
- **Combine two small matching decor pieces (here: embroidery) into one
  shared mat/frame rather than framing them separately** — reduces
  visual clutter and reads as one unified design element instead of two
  competing small pieces; frame material (wood matched to floor/
  countertop, or gold) is a further style-cohesion lever.
