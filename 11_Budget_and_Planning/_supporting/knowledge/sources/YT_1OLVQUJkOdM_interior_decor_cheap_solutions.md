## Walls / Ceilings — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Slat/rail (рейки) wall cladding**: low-cost, high-visual-impact. Backlit slats ("рейки с подсветкой") look even better. **Rule**: slat spacing must equal the light-channel box (короб) module — mismatched spacing makes the backlighting impossible to install after the slats are up. `confirmed`.
- **Three slat-to-baseboard (плинтус) junction methods, worst to best-for-this-project**: (1) run slats to the baseboard and cut flush — looks bad, called injury-risk ("traumaopasnyy," mechanism not explained); (2) install baseboard first, cut its back out to fit the slats (used on this project) — looks fairly good, simple, cheap; (3) install slats to the floor before the baseboard (no cut), then fit small baseboard pieces between slats left/right — hardest to execute correctly, most expensive. `confirmed`.
- **Wall panels (стеновые панели)**: smooth vs. milled/routed (фрезерованные) finish tradeoff — milled looks more dramatic but is much harder to keep clean (especially in the recesses); company defaults to smooth, solid panels for comfort-class projects for this reason. `confirmed` as company's stated practice.
- **Single most-flagged beginner mistake**: assuming a wall doesn't need leveling before wall panels go on. Panels mount onto guide rails fixed directly to the wall — an uneven wall produces a visibly warped panel. **Rule**: always level the wall first, regardless of the final finish going over it. `confirmed`, called the single biggest, practically unfixable mistake beginners make.
- **Velour wall/ceiling/floor inserts — emphatic warning, never use anywhere**: shows visible marks from the slightest touch, doesn't fully groom out even with repeated brushing. Speaker anecdote (nearly an hour grooming a sample before filming, still not fully fixed) offered as corroborating, not statistical, evidence. `confirmed` as speaker's strong recommendation.

## Furniture / Built-ins — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Floating furniture (парящая мебель)** (e.g. cabinets that appear to float above the floor or near the ceiling): a common mistake by both installers *and* designers is failing to specify in the design documentation that the wall behind floating furniture needs special preparation. Even on walls already built from dense/solid material, mounting requires **hardware rated for increased/higher loads**, not a standard fixing. `confirmed`.
