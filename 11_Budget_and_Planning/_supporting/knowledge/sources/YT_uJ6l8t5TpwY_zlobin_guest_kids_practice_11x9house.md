## Rules / Heuristics — "Traffic-Flow Crossing" as a Named Planning Concept

**⚠️ A named professional planning concept, new to this store**: the
architect explicitly names "пересечение потоков" (traffic-flow
crossing) — the undesirable situation where two groups of people who
shouldn't unexpectedly encounter each other end up sharing a path
(worked examples given: a home-office client crossing paths with the
household's own family members; a staircase exit dumping directly into
the living/dining room where guests are present). **Stated planning
response**: route circulation (stairs, guest-room access, home-office
access) through neutral zones specifically to avoid these crossings,
rather than through a space where the two groups would naturally
overlap. A reusable planning vocabulary/heuristic applicable to any home
office, guest space, or mixed-use room — including this project's own
guest/study-room concept.

## Rules / Heuristics — Pantry Entry-Point Placement (Directly Relevant to Video 6 of This Batch)

**⚠️ A concrete pantry-placement rule, directly relevant to this batch's
video 6 (`t9XmjCGO1Tw`, pantry theory)**: locate a pantry near the
house's entrance (since items are typically carried in/out through the
entry), but **the entry point into the pantry itself matters as much as
its general location**. A worked comparison: entering a small
under-stair pantry from its **narrow/cornered end** (e.g. off a stair
landing) leaves only a genuinely cramped ~1-1.2m of usable depth — most
of the pantry's nominal footprint becomes unusable due to the access
geometry. Entering from a **centered point** instead splits the same
footprint into **two independently usable storage zones**. A concrete,
checkable rule: when planning a pantry (or any awkward-shaped storage
room), evaluate the entry point's effect on usable area, not just the
room's gross square footage.
