## Materials / Regulations-Adjacent (Facade Rules)

- **Tilt-and-turn hardware is stated as unambiguously the better default
  choice over turn-only, all else equal** — the cost premium is small:
  **~1-2% of total window cost**, framed as negligible given the
  functional gains.
- **Three stated advantages of tilt-and-turn over turn-only**:
  1. A turn-only sash needs a hard mechanical fall-limiter/restrictor
     (child-safety stop) installed, since it swings fully open with no
     other restraint — an added part and failure point turn-and-tilt
     avoids by allowing a safe tilt-only position.
  2. Turn-only sashes have no trickle/slot ventilation mode — only fully
     open (losing heat/security) or fully closed; tilt-and-turn's tilt
     position gives a small continuous perimeter gap for fresh air
     without opening the sash and without losing heat/security to the
     same degree.
  3. **Durability/self-weight mechanism for large sashes (the source's
     main point)**: a large, heavy sash operated by full swing-open
     turning bears its own weight on the side hinges every time it's
     opened, and gradually deforms/sags — adjustment screws can
     compensate for a while but not indefinitely, eventually requiring
     hardware replacement. **A large sash (worked example: ~2m×2m,
     near-square) cannot be split into a narrower vertical pair** for
     facade-appearance/code reasons (the source states splitting or
     modifying the sash's shape would violate the building facade
     design) — so a heavy near-square sash operated in full-open
     position is claimed to visibly deform within about a week of
     regular use to the point it can no longer close properly, even with
     the installer's own temporary shim wedges meant to help support it.
     **The fix: restrict such a large/heavy sash to tilt-only operation**
     (used only occasionally, e.g. for cleaning) — tilting transfers the
     sash's full weight onto the bottom frame/horizontal transom instead
     of the side hinges, avoiding the deformation mechanism entirely and
     yielding what the source calls an effectively permanent sash.
