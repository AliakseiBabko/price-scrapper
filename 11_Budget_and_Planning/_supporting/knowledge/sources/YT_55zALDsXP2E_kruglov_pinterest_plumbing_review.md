## Plumbing

Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario says (level 1, spoken directly):

- **⚠️ Mixer shape (round vs. flat) affects visible-mark accumulation independently of color/finish** — a flat-profile mixer shows fingerprints and dried-water marks more readily than a round one of the same finish, because a flat surface presents a larger uninterrupted plane for marks to register on; this applies across every finish discussed (glossy black, chrome, satin). **This nuances (not contradicts) this store's existing chrome-color guidance**: the existing store entry says chrome is the most forgiving finish for *water-spot* marks specifically; this source adds that chrome (especially flat/glossy chrome) is markedly *bad* for showing fingerprints — the two claims describe different residue types, not a real disagreement.
- **⚠️ A specific 2024-2025 trending mixer type, praised explicitly as inspiration-worthy**: a pull-out kitchen mixer with a silicone (not metal-braid) hose, which doesn't show water-mark residue at all and functions as a flexible hand-held sprayer when pulled from its docking bracket; paired with a separate, dedicated small tap for filtered drinking water (fed from an under-sink filtration system) mounted alongside the main mixer.
- **⚠️ Satin ("сатинированный"), specifically an aluminum-toned satin finish, is named as one of the best color/finish choices overall** — hides both fingerprints and dried-water marks better than chrome, black, or white. Matte finishes generally show the least residue of any category discussed.
- **⚠️ Glossy sink surfaces accumulate visibly more residue than a glossy mixer of the same finish**, purely due to the sink's much larger surface area — the same glossy-finish maintenance problem already recorded for mixers scales up meaningfully on a sink.
- **⚠️ A round, dark, low-glare micro-sink paired with a round dark mixer is specifically recommended for a small guest WC** with only a wall-hung toilet, sink, and hygienic shower — the small round shapes minimize visible fingerprints (little flat surface to register them) and minimize standing-water pooling.
- **⚠️ Integrated one-piece stone sink+countertop in white specifically stains/marks badly and may periodically need professional polishing to restore its factory-new appearance** — a specific caution beyond this store's existing general "colored/textured finish hides marks better than white" guidance, naming the polishing-maintenance cost of choosing white for this particular fixture type.
- **⚠️ A rounded integrated sink+countertop with a raised lip/edge on both sides creates two hard-to-clean interior corners** — flagged as a real cleaning drawback of this design unless the raised edge serves a genuine functional purpose (e.g. spill containment on a work surface).
- **⚠️ A deep sink in a small bathroom/WC is flagged as impractical, with a stacking-depth reason given**: a deep sink plus its required siphon can add up to roughly 30cm of total under-counter depth — a concrete reason to prefer a shallower basin in a space-constrained room, beyond pure aesthetic preference.
- **⚠️ Drain-overflow (слив-перелив) mechanism: cleaning access and replacement access are two distinct problems, and solving the first doesn't solve the second.** An externally-accessible cleanout design (mentioned as already covered in an earlier video) solves routine cleaning, but **replacing the mechanism entirely still requires disassembling the tub/apron/vanity regardless of cleanout access** — and if the wall behind the tub backs onto a neighboring unit (no access from that side), a failure (which can occur anywhere from a month to 5-10 years after installation, e.g. from a bad gasket) forces tearing out much of the bathroom to reach it. Framed explicitly as a "delayed-action mine," independent of how attractive the installation looks.

## Design Concept

- **A critique of a popular "fake freestanding" tub trend**: a tub styled and marketed as freestanding but actually pushed flush against a wall on one side (visually reading as a single gap on the far side) is functionally impossible to clean or service behind, unlike a genuinely semi-freestanding tub with real access from 3 sides. Origin noted as an Italian design trend now widely and more cheaply copied by Chinese manufacturers (material speculated as a resin/epoxy composite, not confirmed). Framed as a real design-vs-maintainability tradeoff worth knowing before choosing this style for its looks alone.
- Floor-mounted or wall/frame-mounted external mixer options specifically for a freestanding tub in an arbitrary location (e.g. mounted on a T/C-profile frame for placement away from any wall, such as a terrace) — a technique note, not a new planning rule.
