## Quantities / Measurements

- **Minimum comfortable corridor width target: 110cm**, stated as the
  figure to memorize and default to whenever floor area is tight and a
  corridor needs to be squeezed as narrow as reasonably possible without
  becoming uncomfortable — explicitly flagged as not always achievable
  given real constraints, but the target to aim for. **Directly derived
  math**: 110cm corridor width allows two correctly-built walls with a
  10cm return/reveal on each side, yielding a 90cm rough opening — which
  fits an 80cm door leaf, **the door width the source recommends as
  standard for a residential space**. Corroborates and generalizes this
  store's existing per-door rough-opening math (already recorded for
  other door widths) with the specific standard applied to a
  minimum-width corridor scenario.
- **Real worked case**: a 3-room apartment's corridor was originally only
  100cm wide (a load-bearing wall segment on one side; a ventilation
  shaft plus a 10cm partition wall stacked in front of it on the other,
  with an unintentional ~2cm gap between the shaft and that partition).
  The fix: demolish the 10cm partition and rebuild it flush against the
  ventilation shaft's *outer* plane (eliminating the wasted 2cm gap)
  instead of offset in front of it — recovering the corridor to exactly
  112cm raw (110cm after plastering), hitting the target standard.
  Concrete demonstration that a wasted gap between an existing fixed
  obstruction (a shaft) and an added partition, not just raw material
  thickness, can be the actual cause of a too-narrow corridor.
