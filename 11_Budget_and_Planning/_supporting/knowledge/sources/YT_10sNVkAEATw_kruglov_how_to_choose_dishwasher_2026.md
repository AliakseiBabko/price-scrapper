## New facts

1. **⚠️ Width standard, with a real named-brand market-structure claim**: 60cm is the
   worldwide standard; 45cm is a shortened version made specifically for CIS-market buyers.
   Named example: a brand the source calls "AS" doesn't make 45cm units at all; SMEG's own
   top Italian lines are 60cm-only, and SMEG's 45cm units sold to CIS buyers are actually
   ordered under the SMEG brand name from Electrolux's factory. **New technology and
   innovations always debut in 60cm units first**, only reaching 45cm units later, if at all.
   45cm is only justified for a single occupant, someone who rarely cooks, or a short-term
   rental studio for 1-2 guests — the source's own stated experience is that people buy 45cm
   hoping to save incrementally across a renovation budget, but this specific economy is a
   real false economy for anyone with regular multi-person use.
2. **⚠️ Wash-quality driver #1 — internal working water pressure, an undisclosed spec with a
   concrete named-brand comparison**: not the input water-supply pressure (which
   manufacturers do publish), but the *internal* operating pressure the unit washes with —
   a spec manufacturers don't publish at all; only specialist consultants who've researched it
   directly know it. **Named example**: MEA-brand base models (and derivative brands) wash at
   0.8 bar; SMEG's top lines wash at 1.2 bar — ~40% higher pressure, which the source states
   directly translates to meaningfully better wash quality. Source's own research method: a
   3-hour consultation with a named industry contact (Alexey, head of a company/channel called
   "Oven" — appliance-review specialists) who had himself only learned this obscure fact from
   a colleague (Oleg) after years in the industry.
3. **Wash-quality driver #2 — max water heating temperature**: 70°C and above is adequate;
   65°C is called insufficient. A stated paradox: even a lower-pressure unit can wash well if
   its max temperature is 70°C+ — temperature can partly compensate for pressure.
4. **Wash-quality driver #3 — detergent chemistry** (tablets/powder/gel, rinse aid, salt) —
   covered in more detail in items 9 below.
5. **Secondary/indirect wash factors, explicitly de-emphasized**: spray-arm shape and jet
   trajectory variation, dedicated glass/stemware sprayers — real but minor factors; the
   source's own advice is to focus purchase decisions on the three drivers above, not these.
6. **⚠️ Drying-type taxonomy, with a genuinely important 4th category**: condensation drying
   (cheapest, most basic — leaves visible water-spot residue on dishes) — cheap, reliable,
   "medium uselessness" in the source's own words; turbo-drying (adds a fan, faster and
   somewhat more effective, slightly noisier); zeolite/mineral drying (a named "ZIT"-style
   system, terminology varies by brand — expensive, found mostly in premium models; **the
   source's own opinion: not worth buying a dishwasher specifically for this feature**).
   **The genuinely important 4th type: auto-door-opening at cycle end** — vents residual
   steam/moisture automatically, replaces the need to check an end-of-cycle indicator light,
   and removes the risk of forgetting a finished load inside. Caveat: steam venting isn't
   harmful to surrounding cabinetry/appliances as long as the countertop is undamaged and
   properly sealed.
7. **Programs**: only 4 core genuinely useful programs — eco, quick, intensive, and automatic;
   everything else marketed as additional programs is mostly marketing, not worth weighing in
   a purchase decision.
8. **Noise level, context-dependent importance**: a less significant factor for most enclosed
   kitchens, but a real, significant one for an open-plan kitchen-living room or studio
   apartment. Baseline acceptable range: 42-44 dB; under 40 dB is meaningfully better.
   **Caveat**: actual real-world noise also depends heavily on installation/cabinetry-fit
   quality, not just the spec-sheet rating — recommends checking granular product reviews
   (not just store reviews) that specifically mention noise for the exact model, since a
   stated 40dB rating isn't guaranteed to hold in practice.
9. **⚠️ Detergent/chemistry taxonomy, with an explicit Moscow-specific salt rule**: tablets
   (all-in-one, may bundle detergent + rinse aid + salt) — most expensive, most convenient/
   effective; powder — washes well but needs correct manual dosing, may need separately-added
   rinse aid if spotting occurs, and needs separately-added salt in hard-water areas; gel —
   the weakest performer of the three, a compromise choice. Rinse aid reduces spotting and
   improves drying (often built into tablets). **⚠️ Salt necessity is regional — water
   hardness varies significantly across Russia, and the source states explicitly that for
   Moscow specifically, salt is mandatory** — with tablets, salt is often unnecessary since
   it's frequently already built in.
10. **⚠️ Price/brand tiers, 60cm-only comparison (45cm explicitly excluded from this
    comparison per the source's own stated subjective view that it's the least practical
    width)**: **Economy: ≈30,000-50,000 RUB (≈$390-$650)** — the source names a single
    dominant OEM factory (MEA/Midea) supplying 50+ rebadged brands off the same internal
    hardware, naming Samsung, Gorenje, Hansa, and Coopersberg among them — with the specific,
    counterintuitive claim that a non-MDEA-branded unit is sometimes cheaper than the
    identical MDEA-branded model. **Comfort: from ≈75,000 RUB (≈$980)** — a clear named
    market leader, SMEG, with a specific recommended model (transcribed as "Smac STL362 DQ,"
    likely a SMEG model number — flagged for verification given auto-caption transcription
    quality) called a genuinely good price/quality/feature balance; comfort-tier SMEG units
    are Polish-assembled base-spec models. **Premium: from ≈100,000 RUB (≈$1,300)** — SMEG's
    Italian-assembled flagship lines, plus a named second premium brand (transcribed as
    "ASCA," likely ASKO — flagged for verification). **What premium buys**: internal
    build/component quality, higher internal working pressure (better wash quality per item
    2), rack customization flexibility (tines reconfigurable for large/small items without
    structural modification), and refinement in controls/temperature management.
11. **⚠️ Mandatory-vs-nice-to-have checklist**: mandatory — 60cm width; water heating
    temperature 70°C+; internal pressure ≥0.8 bar at the economy tier, ≥1 bar at
    comfort/premium; a third rack/tray specifically for cutlery. Nice-to-have: auto-door-
    opening, disassemblable racks (ideally with a height-adjustable top rack too), varied
    spray-arm trajectories.
12. **⚠️ Warranty structure as an explicit, personally-weighted selection criterion**: a real
    tradeoff the source frames as having no universally correct answer — a Chinese-branded
    unit often carries a 5-year official manufacturer warranty, while a European-branded unit
    often carries only a 1-year warranty from the *retailer* (not the manufacturer) — real
    risk if that specific retailer later closes, since there's then no manufacturer recourse.
    Recommends checking reviews for the exact model across multiple independent sites/
    marketplaces, not just one retailer's own product page, since retailer-page reviews can be
    bought/inflated.
13. **⚠️ Named appliance-matching consulting service, a real price point**: a personal-shopper-
    style paid service (analogous to a car-buying concierge) that recommends specific units
    matched to stated budget/needs — **≈1,000 RUB (≈$10) per consultation**, called
    negligible relative to a renovation or appliance budget.
