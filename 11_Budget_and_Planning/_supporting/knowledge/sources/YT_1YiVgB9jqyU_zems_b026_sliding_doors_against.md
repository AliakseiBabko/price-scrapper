## Doors / Trim — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Sliding door soundproofing is materially worse than a hinged interior door**, even when the sliding door hides behind a false wall (фальш-стена) — a hinged door has a rebate/stop on 3 of its 4 sides, providing real acoustic sealing; a basic wall-mounted sliding door (rollers along the wall face) has essentially no seal at all ("just a formal partition"). Speaker states ~80% of sliding-door owners in his own client base complain about poor soundproofing, **including** those with the false-wall pocket version; switching them to a hinged door eliminates the complaints. `single-account`, but a specific, checkable practitioner claim, not vague.
- **Floor-space rationale for sliding doors is frequently overstated**: sliding doors are chosen to avoid an opening "eating into" room space, but (a) modern door stoppers/limiters already protect furniture from a hinged door's swing, and (b) well-designed openings are rarely centered in a room anyway — they're offset toward one wall specifically so a wardrobe can sit along the same wall, which also squares off the room's proportions (a squarer room reads brighter/more spacious than a long, corridor-like one, since light falloff increases with distance from the window). The zone right beside a properly-offset hinged door (where the open leaf rests) is typically dead space anyway (no furniture placed there), so a hinged door "costs" little functional floor area in a well-planned layout. `confirmed` as speaker's stated design logic.
- **Sliding-door pocket has a real floor-space cost of its own**, contrary to the marketing framing: even the thinnest false-wall pocket costs roughly 10 cm of usable room length (5 cm for the leaf, 5 cm minimum false-wall thickness) — and unlike a hinged door's swing zone, that strip is unusable for furniture/console/mirror placement even when the door is open, since a shelf/cabinet in that path would block the leaf's travel. `confirmed`, direct comparison made by the speaker.
- **Sliding doors sit noticeably taller than hinged doors of the same nominal 2 m leaf height** — the sliding hardware (rail + rollers) sits above the leaf, adding roughly **5 cm** to the overall installed height versus a hinged door (frame sits directly atop the leaf). This creates a visible height mismatch when sliding and hinged doors appear in the same sightline (a common situation, since not every door in an apartment can practically be sliding — wet rooms in particular are excluded on soundproofing grounds). `confirmed`, concrete number.
- **Sliding doors reduce usable clear opening width more than hinged doors of the same rough opening**, because the sliding leaf needs an integrated pull-handle recess that must stay clear of the opening — speaker's worked example: an 80 cm rough opening yields ~79 cm clear width with a hinged door but only **~71 cm (≈8 cm less)** with a sliding door. In tight corridors "where every centimeter counts," speaker states this can push effective openings down to **as low as 60 cm**, which he calls unacceptable in a quality renovation. `confirmed` (specific numeric worked example, single source).
- **Sliding doors cost more than hinged doors of comparable quality** — a good rail+roller hardware set alone costs roughly as much as a good leaf; premium (e.g. Italian) soft-close rail systems add further cost on top. `confirmed` as speaker's stated market observation, no specific currency/figure given (kept qualitative, not treated as a numeric data point).
- **Dust accumulation inside the wall cavity** behind a pocket sliding door is cited as a real, recurring homeowner complaint — dust collects in the cavity between the structural wall and false wall over 3-4 years and cannot be removed without disassembling the door/wall; owners with dust allergies reported coughing/sneezing/poor sleep tied to this. `single-account`, but a specific mechanism.
- **Reliability/repairability failure mode (the speaker's central argument)**: sliding-door mechanisms (rollers, stoppers, soft-close dampers) commonly begin failing en masse roughly **3-4 years** after installation, and are easy to break even with moderate force/carelessness. Repair requires disassembling substantial finished work to reach the mechanism — speaker describes needing to remove furniture, ceiling, flooring, and part of the wall to service a hidden roller/stopper mechanism. Framed as a major warranty-liability driver from an installer's own business perspective (he personally warranties his own completed projects). `single-account`, but concrete and specific — this is the speaker's stated primary reason for abandoning sliding doors in his own practice.
