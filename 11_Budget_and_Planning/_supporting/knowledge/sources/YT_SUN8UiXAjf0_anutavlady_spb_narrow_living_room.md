## Case — 22m² Long/Narrow Living Room, St. Petersburg (Minimal-Change Pass)

- **⚠️ Accent-wall-on-the-longest-wall mistake, general rule**: putting
  an accent wallpaper/finish on the *longest* wall of an already long/
  narrow room emphasizes the room's poor proportions further. In a
  narrow room, accent only a **segment/portion** of that long wall, not
  its full length — this both creates zoning and helps correct
  proportion at the same time. States the general principle behind this
  batch's earlier off-center-accent-wall composition rule
  (`DvQTBiItLpY`) explicitly, as a stated do/don't rather than only a
  composition preference.
- **⚠️ Genuine-plain vs. "technically neutral" companion-material
  distinction**: a solid-color wallpaper chosen to accompany a bold
  patterned accent can still function as *another* pattern if it has
  visible texture/sheen (here: embossed veining/stripes with shine) —
  the source explicitly calls this "not quite solid" despite being a
  single color. For an already-finished room, simply painting over such
  a wallpaper flattens its sheen/texture into genuinely plain, cheaper
  than replacing it outright.
- **Cumulative-texture-load principle**: several individually
  "technically neutral" surfaces (a wood-grain floor, a patterned rug, a
  velour sofa with light-shifting sheen) can still add up to a visually
  overloaded room even though none of them is a single bold pattern on
  its own — a distinct concern from any single surface's own pattern
  status.
- **⚠️ Furniture-height-contrast technique for ceiling perception**:
  removing a wall unit's horizontal open shelving (leaving the tall
  wardrobe sections and a separate low console with a stark height
  difference between them) visually raises the ceiling — a third,
  distinct mechanism in this batch for the same low-ceiling-perception
  goal (alongside vertical stripes/moulding and oversized art), this one
  driven by height *contrast between adjacent furniture pieces* rather
  than a single element's own verticality.
- **Adhesive/plastic cover discs for shelf-removal mounting scars** —
  a cosmetic fix (various colors/diameters, sold on marketplaces) for
  visible screw-hole marks left after removing shelving from furniture.
- **⚠️ Override-the-literal-request methodology, worked example**: the
  client asked for a *larger* rug to cover the whole room; the source
  explicitly declines, reasoning a full-room rug would emphasize the
  room's long/narrow proportions even further — instead **repositions
  the existing, already well-color-matched rug** to sit squarely in
  front of one specific zone (sofa + wall unit), calling this more
  logical and harmonious than buying new. A concrete instance of
  diagnosing the *underlying* proportion problem behind a client's
  literal request rather than executing it as stated.
- **Radiator-and-plant composition technique**: fully conceal an
  unattractive radiator behind sheer curtains, then place a floor plant
  directly in front for a deliberate visual composition, combined with
  2 more plants of varying height for a layered grouping.
- **Round coffee table retained/repositioned specifically for its
  no-sharp-corners shape** in a tight-clearance living-room zone —
  **corroborates** this batch's existing no-sharp-corners-in-a-tight-space
  theme (previously recorded for a child's play zone, `fkoUNvB9LV8`),
  now applied to a general adult living-space furniture choice — same
  channel, not independent corroboration, but a second within-batch
  application of the same underlying principle.
- **Table-lamp height tied to adjacent armrest height**: when a side
  table sits lower than a sofa's armrest, choose a lamp tall enough
  (here: >80cm) that its shade still rises to a visually harmonious
  height relative to the armrest — a lamp-height-to-adjacent-furniture
  coordination rule.
- **Two-tone narrow curtain panels as a third ceiling-height technique**:
  at a wide window pier, two narrower two-color curtain panels (colors
  pulled directly from the room's existing wallpaper pattern) were used
  instead of one wide panel — the extra vertical seam lines add further
  height-stretching effect beyond what a single wide panel would give,
  while also adding visual color variety.
- **Small-scale gallery-grid as an explicit large-format-art fallback**:
  when no ready-made large painting/print matches a room's palette,
  size, and theme, a grid of 6-8 smaller framed pieces gives comparable
  visual impact and is far easier to source — stated as a general
  substitution rule, not a one-off workaround.
- **⚠️ Decorative-pillow anti-uniformity rule (two parts)**: (a) restates
  this batch's 50×50cm minimum pillow-size floor; (b) **don't make all
  pillows on a sofa the same size** — deliberately combine large pillows
  with smaller/differently-shaped ones, rather than a matching uniform
  set, for a better-looking mix.
- **Furniture-relocation-over-replacement as an explicit project-level
  default**: most of this project's "fixes" were simply repositioning
  existing furniture (a round table, a dresser, decor objects) to better
  spots in the same room rather than buying replacements — the source
  explicitly frames this as "why replace something that isn't actually
  the problem," a philosophy that shaped the whole project's low total
  spend.
- **Round/soft-form armchair as a corner-softening counterbalance**:
  a soft, round armchair was chosen specifically to counterbalance
  several nearby hard geometric edges (a sharp coffee-table corner, a
  rectangular dresser, a rectangular painting) accumulating in the same
  part of the room — extends this batch's round-rug-for-sharp-geometry
  rule (`ZlkBfwlhYFU`) to a furniture-piece choice instead of a rug.
