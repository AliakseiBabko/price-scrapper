## Entrance / Hallway

- **Flooring — moisture-resistant vinyl (kварцвинил) or porcelain
  stoneware only, not laminate/parquet/engineered board**: reasoning
  stated directly — an entryway seasonally tracks in snow/de-icing
  chemical residue (winter), dust (summer), or mud (spring/autumn), and
  wood-based flooring can't tolerate that moisture cycle. Specifically
  calls out that not all quartz-vinyl products are moisture-resistant —
  the resistant type is the one intended here, not the category as a
  whole.
- **Electric heated floor recommended for the entryway specifically** —
  reasoning: actively dries residual moisture/de-icing chemical residue
  rather than letting it sit, reducing cleaning frequency.
- **Rigid (not soft/flexible) baseboard recommended** — reasoning: a soft
  baseboard leaves a gap dirt/slush can migrate under, a hard baseboard
  seals that gap.
- **Lighting — deliberately over-light, not under-light, the entryway**:
  stated as a zone that should never be lit by a single fixture; err
  toward more light than seems necessary.
- **Full-height mirror treated as a near-mandatory element**: can be
  wall-mounted, recessed into the wall, mounted on a wardrobe door leaf,
  or mounted on the entrance door's interior face — flexible placement,
  but a full-body view is the stated requirement (framed by the speaker as
  primarily valued by female household members, his own phrasing, not
  independently verified as a universal preference).
- **A seat (pouf, or a built-in seated spot inside a wardrobe) for
  putting on/removing shoes**: ergonomic reasoning — useful when tired or
  unwell, avoids needing to balance while standing.
- **Closed storage strongly preferred over open shelving/open hooks**,
  with an explicit tradeoff acknowledged: open shelves/hooks inevitably
  accumulate small clutter (keys, fobs, mail) no matter what, whereas
  closed storage costs "three extra actions" (open door, take/put item,
  close door) but preserves a sense of order and reduces cleaning
  friction. Framed as worth the extra step.
- **Furniture design should accommodate robot-vacuum cleaning**:
  cabinetry should either seal fully to the floor (no gap at all) or be
  raised clear of the floor (legs, or wall-mounted/floating), avoiding a
  mid-height obstruction that blocks a robot vacuum and forces manual
  shoe removal to clean underneath. An open floor plane under/around
  storage furniture is called out as both a cleaning convenience and a
  visual-order aid.
- **Security — two device recommendations**: (1) a video door
  intercom/peephole camera, so who's at/rang the door is always visible;
  (2) a door-open sensor integrated with a smart-home hub (named:
  Yandex Station cited as one option) — described as needing no
  additional wiring, installable in ~10 minutes, and useful for a
  concrete family scenario (getting an open/close notification when a
  child leaves for or returns from school).
- **Smart locks — a stated change of opinion**: initially skeptical,
  became favorable after reviews/comparisons, especially with smart-home
  integration providing a fallback (camera + notification) if the door is
  accidentally left unlocked. A named product (Aqara) is cited for
  multiple unlock methods (fingerprint, code, rotating temporary codes
  e.g. for a cleaner), open/close time logging, and multiple low-battery
  warnings before failure — commercial mention, not adopted as neutral
  brand fact, but the underlying feature set (temporary codes,
  event logging, battery-life warning redundancy) is a reusable
  selection-criteria list independent of the named brand.
- **"Master light switch" (single button kills all lights in the
  apartment) — a conditional recommendation, not a universal one**:
  works only when household members share synchronized schedules (a
  single occupant, or a couple who leave/return together); explicitly
  **not suitable for a larger family with staggered schedules**, because
  the mechanism only works one-directionally from the entry point — a
  household member returning later still has to walk to the entryway and
  turn everything back on before turning on just the room they need. If
  installed anyway (e.g. only for a vacation-departure routine), recommends
  mounting it out of normal reach (a closet/wardrobe interior) specifically
  to prevent a guest or resident accidentally triggering it.
- **Practical near-zero-cost tip**: add an electrical outlet near the
  in-closet shoe-storage area to allow a boot/shoe dryer to be used exactly
  where footwear is stored, avoiding drying wet footwear on a balcony or in
  a living space.
- **Wall wear-resistance, entrance-door reveals/jambs called out as the
  single most vulnerable wall surface** in the entryway (repeated
  bag/parcel scuffing): recommends decorative plaster (stated as the most
  durable non-tile wall finish) for entryway walls generally, and either
  decorative plaster or a protective wood panel/trim specifically at the
  door reveal — reasoning given is that the small area involved (5-6 m² of
  wall) makes the cost negligible against a full renovation budget, so
  there's little reason not to upgrade it.
