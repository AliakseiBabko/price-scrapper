## Durable Facts

- **Client site-visit standard: the client visits the site exactly twice** — once to sign the renovation contract, once at the end to formally accept the completed work. Stated explicitly as not unique to this company (other companies do this too), but contrasted with self-managed/unsupervised renovations, where **the practitioner claims material costs typically run roughly 1.5–2x higher when the client does not personally supervise the renovation** than when they do. `single-account`, `unverified` as an industry-wide statistic, but a direct, quotable claim about the value of owner oversight — relevant to this project's own self-managed approach (as an argument *for* active client supervision, independent of whether a company or a self-assembled crew executes the work).
- **Entrance-door ergonomic-clearance standard**: the entry door and adjoining hallway should be laid out so a person can enter without squeezing past the opening door or the door striking them from behind — demonstrated against a real neighboring apartment's bad example (~10cm clearance, forces squeezing through, door hits the person's back) vs. this company's design (enough clearance to walk a stroller or bicycle straight in). Concrete, checkable ergonomic-planning rule.
- **Full-length entrance-hall mirror on the entry door itself, only as a fallback when wall space is under 40cm wide** (e.g. because a built-in wardrobe reaching close to the wall leaves insufficient width for a wall-mounted mirror) — explicitly framed as a compromise/downgrade solution, not a first choice: a door-mounted mirror gets smudged near the door handle over time from repeated contact, and should only be used once wall-mounting options are genuinely exhausted.
- **Separate, dedicated-lighting makeup zone recommended in the entryway/hallway whenever space allows**, distinct from a full-length mirror (which doesn't reveal fine grooming details under overhead light). Requires 2 electrical outlets and a dedicated switch tied to that zone's lighting — corroborates the store's existing full-length-mirror-plus-makeup-zone content from an earlier same-channel source, with the specific outlet count now stated.
- **Long-distance stretch-ceiling-to-wardrobe attachment boxes are being phased out by this company** — the stated reason is a cross-trade tolerance mismatch: the company's own boxes are built to precise dimensions, but furniture installers (a separate trade the company doesn't control) can't consistently match millimeter-level tolerances to them, leaving a visible gap/color-mismatch strip that perfectionist clients notice and dislike. **New approach**: bring the furniture flush to the ceiling, leaving a dedicated top module, then attach the stretch ceiling to that module instead of a separate pre-built box. Genuinely new cross-trade-coordination lesson-learned for this store's existing wardrobe-ceiling-box content.
- **In-wardrobe "home server" cabinets are being phased out in favor of a separate dedicated low-voltage equipment cabinet** — reasoning: dust accumulation inside a wardrobe-integrated server nook is a recurring client pain point, and cleaning staff sometimes accidentally tear cables while dusting around the exposed wiring tangle. A standalone low-voltage cabinet is smaller and holds less equipment, but is judged the net-better trade-off, especially on larger/longer-distance projects. Extends this batch's existing server-closet content with a design-evolution note (moving away from wardrobe integration).
- **Standard electrical-panel spec** (restated as firm company standard): a voltage-protection relay always present, a separate breaker per circuit group always used, every breaker clearly labeled, and a detailed panel-operation guide provided to the client. Corroborates this store's existing panel-standards content.
- **Duty/night lighting is described as a universal, non-optional standard regardless of its exact position** (ceiling, low, or even integrated into a baseboard on a higher-budget project) — two concrete rationales given: (1) lets an occupant navigate to the entrance without waking someone sleeping in an adjacent room behind an open door; (2) lets an occupant use the bathroom at night without triggering the full bright main light. Corroborates and generalizes this store's existing duty-light content.
- **Quartz-vinyl flooring direction-matching across rooms rotated 45° to each other**: even where one room's plan is rotated relative to an adjoining one, flooring should still be laid in the direction of movement in each room, and the visual plank lines carried through consistently at the rotated boundary rather than left visually disconnected. A specific technique detail for irregular/non-standard layouts.
- **Concealed door hinges recommended in every style except genuinely classical interiors** — in a classical-style renovation, visible hinges paired with a matching visible handle are described as a deliberate, cohesive part of the classical aesthetic; in every other style (including Scandinavian and modern), hinges should always be concealed. **Concrete price point: a standard visible hinge costs about 700 RUB vs. roughly 3,000 RUB for a concealed hinge (~4x)** — framed as a negligible difference against a renovation's total budget, with explicit advice to delay door installation and save up rather than cheap out on hinge type.
- **Paintable textured wallpaper spot-repair mechanism, restated with a concrete client-facing detail**: every client is left a small labeled bucket of matching touch-up paint; a scuff/mark can be dabbed out invisibly with a small sponge and a drop of that paint, because the wallpaper's absorbency makes the touch-up blend in — a directly painted wall can't be spot-repaired the same way (patch absorbency mismatch shows as a visible blotch), requiring a full repaint of the wall instead. Different wall colors within the same room (for TV-wall or art-wall accents, for example) are also easier to execute on paintable wallpaper than on painted walls.
- **Tier-specific wall-finish compatibility rule, genuinely new for this store**: comfort-class renovations use **only** paintable wallpaper; business-class renovations explicitly **avoid** paintable wallpaper, because business-class pairs with concealed door frames and concealed baseboards, and a wallpaper edge meeting a concealed door frame will visibly fray/catch over time at that junction — concealed-frame/baseboard systems are stated to pair correctly only with painted walls, not paintable wallpaper. A concrete, checkable finish-selection rule tied to which other tier-specific features are chosen.
- **Recessed (false-wall) radiators paired with ceiling-recessed curtain cornices are argued to cost no usable floor space**, contrary to client intuition — reasoning given: curtains occupy a fixed plane regardless of whether the radiator behind that plane is recessed or not, since curtains can never overlap directly into the radiator's own zone either way; recessing the radiator behind that same plane is therefore "free" relative to the curtain's footprint, while also eliminating a dust-collecting niche and any protruding visible pipework. A specific, load-bearing spatial-reasoning argument not previously captured this explicitly in this store.
- **Two-component magnetic radiator grille** (corroborates the same mechanism already recorded in this batch from `yt_fx1vISZZK9s`): the magnetic center panel gives emergency/quick access to the radiator (e.g. to dry mittens or boots on it) without removing the whole grille.
- **Air conditioning in a kitchen-living combined room, or any large kitchen where significant time is spent, is treated as a strict requirement, not optional** — the practitioner states that in the company's own experience, **every single project without kitchen-living AC eventually retrofits it later**, always at much higher cost (wall-channel cutting, full wallpaper replacement around the disturbed area) than installing it during the original renovation. **If budget genuinely doesn't allow immediate installation, the recommended fallback is leaving pre-installed, capped conduit stubs** ready for a future unit, decoratively covered (e.g. a taped-over plastic bag) rather than leaving nothing prepared at all. A firm, financially-justified planning rule.
- **Switch-to-fixture intuitive-mapping rule, with a stated internal validation claim**: even with several switches controlling several light sources in one open room (a kitchen-living example with 4 switches / 2 fixture groups given), the switch **physically nearest** a given fixture should control that nearest fixture, not a farther/diagonal one — the company states it tested this specifically ("on several hundred people") and found people instinctively reach for the nearest switch when leaving a zone (e.g. turning off a TV-reflected glare while seated on a sofa). **Avoid large unlabeled multi-gang ("piano") switch panels (a 12-switch example given) in comfort-class work** — reserved, if used at all, for business-class-and-above projects (200,000–500,000+ RUB/m² total project cost cited as the tier where such a panel becomes acceptable/expected) where a dedicated smart-home or dimming control system typically accompanies it.
- **Kitchen work-zone task lighting must always use a simple, direct dedicated cable and dedicated switch — never a sensor, gesture, or motion-based control**, corroborating this batch's existing automatic-fixture-avoidance philosophy (`yt_ZX_J9uAXfeQ`) with a kitchen-specific application. **Bar-zone lighting similarly needs its own dedicated switch, physically located in the bar zone itself** near the fixtures it controls, not folded into a distant multi-gang panel elsewhere in the room.
- **Split/divided galley-kitchen layout technique for an awkward corner-constrained space**: rather than a small, functionally-useless corner run or one overly long single run that would consume the entire adjoining living-room footprint, split the kitchen into two connected runs (e.g. a straight run with a bar counter, plus a shorter two-column auxiliary run) joined by a single step between them — preserves an ergonomic, non-cramped kitchen while still leaving room for a large sofa/seating area. A concrete worked layout solution for irregular-shell kitchens.
- **Modern high-power cooktops typically ship without a plug** (a bare hardwired terminal block instead) — the supply cable run must therefore be planned with sufficient length to reach the cooktop's terminal block directly, and this exact routing/length is one of the itemized electrical points recorded in the company's design documentation (each point specified with floor-height and reference-point coordinates) — restates and reinforces this store's existing design-documentation-completeness theme (points specified precisely enough that any qualified electrician, or a self-managed client doing partial DIY work, can execute the plan without the original design firm).
- **"Zems wish" (explicitly flagged by the practitioner as a preference, not a firm standard): door-casing (наличник) thickness should sometimes deliberately differ between two adjoining trim pieces meeting at a corner**, so the thinner piece's cut edge doesn't show against the thicker one's rounded/finished edge — if a specific door product doesn't offer differing-thickness casing options, the fallback is to paint mismatched-thickness casings the same color to visually unify them.
- **Bathroom floor built flush (same level) with the rest of the apartment floor is now standard practice for this company**, made legally/code-compliant by full apartment-wide waterproofing rather than relying on a raised curb/threshold to contain a leak — the company also always adds floor soundproofing regardless, and states that current combined soundproofing+waterproofing material systems let the whole apartment floor act as a single leak-containing "basin" without needing a threshold step.
- **Leak-detection sensor system (wired or wireless) is standard on every project**, paired with the flush-waterproofed-floor approach above.
- **Concealed/wall-mounted mixer and thermostatic-valve bathroom systems carry a large, explicit price gap versus a standard exposed fixture set: roughly 70,000 RUB (standard) vs. roughly 280,000 RUB (concealed) — about a 4x difference.** Explicitly **not recommended for comfort-class** renovations given that price gap, but recommended for business-class. A concrete, directly comparable price point for this store's fixture-tier content.
- **Concealed-thermostat hygienic-shower/bidet-sprayer systems require a specific pressure-relief-capable holder, not a standard holder** — using a holder designed for a normal mixer with a concealed-thermostat sprayer is stated to reliably cause a leak within roughly one to two weeks, since a standard holder doesn't relieve residual line pressure on the hose the way the correct holder type does. Extends this batch's existing hygienic-shower holder-shutoff plumbing content (`yt_FO9mQWUeSuA`) with an explicit incompatible-holder failure warning.
- **Siphon/trap revision-access technique when no direct access point is possible at the fixture itself**: two named alternatives — (1) a drain assembly accessed from above, or (2) an access hatch routed through an adjacent room (demonstrated: a hatch placed inside a neighboring closet, reaching the siphon from behind the wall). A concrete worked solution for this store's existing plumbing-access-hatch content.
- **Firm, repeated standard: only electric towel warmers, never hydronic/water-fed ones, across every project tier** (comfort, business, and even "super-elite") — corroborates this store's existing towel-warmer content with the strongest, most absolute statement of the rule yet recorded from this channel.
- **Furniture-position gaps built specifically to hide a sagging access hatch are being phased out**, now that non-sagging hatch products exist — previously a common workaround for a standard 60×120cm sagging hatch.
