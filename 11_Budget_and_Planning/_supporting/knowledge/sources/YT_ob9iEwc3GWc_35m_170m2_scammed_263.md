## Mistakes / Warnings — Genuinely New Mechanisms

- **⚠️ A door without a proper rebate/stop ("прихлоп") against its frame — leaving a visible perimeter gap all around — has near-zero soundproofing, regardless of the door material.** A documented WC door defect: a flush door sitting ~5mm short of the opening's edge on all sides transmitted essentially all sound through the room (including paper-related noise). **General rule: the frame rebate/stop is what creates a door's acoustic seal — a door installed without one is acoustically almost equivalent to no door at all**, independent of the leaf's own material/thickness.
- **⚠️ A fully-concealable folding/sliding kitchen-front door system (a "disappearing kitchen") is flagged as a real reliability liability, not just a durability nice-to-have.** The documented defect: the mechanism worked well initially but the source describes it breaking down roughly every 2 weeks, each repair cycle taking about 2 months, in a repeating pattern — and because of this the doors end up left permanently open in practice anyway, defeating the concealment purpose while still obstructing the workspace and sacrificing storage capacity to the mechanism itself. **General caution: weigh a "disappearing kitchen" system's real long-term reliability record against its aesthetic appeal before committing**, distinct from this vault's existing masonry/soundproofing content.
- **⚠️ A podium/platform step height in the ambiguous 3-4cm range is a genuine trip hazard because the brain doesn't reliably register it as a step — a specific, checkable ergonomic-safety threshold.** By contrast, a documented case using a **10-11cm platform height** was explicitly praised as safe and intuitive ("I can see it's higher, I lift my foot"). **General rule: a floor-level change should either be negligible/flush or clearly perceptible (≈10cm+) — avoid the ambiguous middle range that people routinely stub their toes on.**
- **⚠️ Modern "incombustible" (non-burnout) underfloor-heating cable exists as a mainstream product category (the source dates its emergence to roughly 2-3 years before this video, i.e. ~2021-2022) and should be the default choice over older burn-prone cable types.** The documented defect: an installed heating cable had already failed/burned out. **General buying guidance: specifically request a non-burnout-rated heating cable product line when buying underfloor heating**, not just any heating cable.
- **A rain/tropical showerhead's actual water delivery depends on nozzle density relative to head diameter, not just the head's size — a real buying-guidance mechanism.** The documented defect: a shower head's *diameter* was stretched larger for a more dramatic "tropical" look, but its *nozzle count* stayed the same as a smaller head — diluting water delivery per unit area so severely that washing long hair took an extended, impractical amount of time. **General rule: verify a rain/tropical shower head's nozzle density (not just diameter) before buying — a wide head with sparse nozzles functions as a water-saving fixture, not a genuine high-volume tropical shower experience**, and pair it with a normal handheld head as a practical backup.
- **A full-perimeter in-floor convector loop is specifically recommended for rooms with large panoramic windows, with an explicit physics-based mechanism**: cold air at a large glazed surface sinks and flows along the floor into the room (cold air is denser, sinks; warm air rises), producing a perceptible cold draft at floor/ankle level even with otherwise-adequate room heating. A documented defect placed convectors only on one side of a seating area, leaving the other side under-heated near the glazing while the heated side ran uncomfortably warm. **General rule: a room with substantial panoramic glazing needs heating routed along the *entire* window perimeter, not concentrated at one point, specifically to counteract this convection-driven cold-air-fall mechanism.**
- **A large-format tile/flooring transition seam between two different rooms/materials should never be a thin (~1cm) grout-only gap** — a documented defect used exactly this, and the seam was already visibly cracking. Reinforces this vault's existing wall-shifting-vs-tile-shifting principle (shift the material boundary, not the grout width, to avoid a fragile thin seam) — applied here to a floor-material transition specifically, not a wall corner.
- **A structural/load-bearing column integrated into one unified plane that also routes ventilation grilles on both sides is a genuinely effective technique, praised explicitly** — turns a necessary structural obstruction into functional ductwork housing and a clean kitchen/living-room zoning boundary at the same time, rather than treating the column and the ventilation as separate design problems.
