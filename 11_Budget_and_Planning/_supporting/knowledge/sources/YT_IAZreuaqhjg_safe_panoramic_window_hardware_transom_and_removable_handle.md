## Windows — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Standard safety-code parapet height (~1.1m, stated as "не менее метра десять") conflicts directly with a panoramic-window design goal** — at that height, someone seated (e.g. on a sofa) sees mostly the opaque parapet wall and only a strip of sky, not the view a floor-to-ceiling glazed section is meant to provide. Filmed demonstration on a 31st-floor unit makes the height comparison directly (marked against the narrator's own body). `confirmed`, level 1, directly demonstrated on camera. [transcript text]
- **Standard resolution: split the window into two zones — a fixed panoramic (non-opening) section below the code-required parapet height, and a small opening transom (фрамуга) above it**, rather than making the whole panoramic window openable (which would either violate the height rule if opened low, or be unreachable if positioned high). `confirmed`, level 1, stated as the standard solution this hardware supports.
- **Device 1 — remote transom-opening mechanism**: a handle-plus-linkage-plus-angled-hinge assembly that lets a high transom (positioned anywhere above the low parapet, at any height) be opened from a handle mounted at normal reachable height, without climbing on furniture. Framed by the narrator as solving two failure modes of the naive alternative: (a) a low handle positioned to be reachable, but which then lets driving rain blow straight in through the low opening gap and onto the floor; (b) a handle mounted high enough to avoid the rain problem, which then becomes unreachable — described as requiring standing on tiptoes/a stool, with a real risk of falling and breaking the handle off in the attempt. `confirmed`, level 1, mechanism described with a stated before/after rationale for why the naive alternatives fail.
- **Device 1 includes an integrated anti-break-in lock/blocker** (a push-button) specifically because a small transom section, per the narrator, is very easy to force open from outside by simply pushing on it. Pressing the button locks the whole window against external opening; from inside, one motion releases it. `confirmed`, level 1, mechanism and stated rationale (break-in vulnerability of transom-style openings) both given directly.
- **Device 1's safety framing vs. standard child-safety window locks**: the narrator states standard child-safety locks on openable windows are unreliable in practice because parents forget to re-engage them after use — positioned as a reason to prefer a design (low fixed panoramic pane + high transom, reachable only via the device) that removes the child-accessible opening entirely rather than relying on a lock a parent must remember to use every time. `single-account`, narrator's own stated reasoning, not independently benchmarked against child-safety-incident data.
- **Device 2 — removable/detachable technical handle with decorative snap-in caps ("заглушки")**, used for a panoramic (non-transom) window section that must default to non-openable for child safety, but occasionally needs to open — the narrator's own stated example is periodic servicing access to an external AC condenser unit mounted behind the glazing. Mechanism: normally, a decorative cap sits flush in the handle spindle position (window reads as a plain fixed pane); a small arrow/indicator shows how to slide the cap aside, exposing the spindle, where a separately-stored ordinary PVC-window technical handle is fitted; the window then opens/closes normally with that handle; the handle is removed and the decorative cap snapped back afterward. `confirmed`, level 1, mechanism described step by step.
- **Device 2's stated child-safety property**: the narrator claims this is "practically impossible" for a child to defeat without the stored handle — specifically ruling out a child using a pencil or other found household object to turn the exposed spindle, which he describes as "quite difficult" ("делается достаточно сложно") to accomplish that way. `single-account`, narrator's own claim, not independently tested or benchmarked — tag as an asserted-but-unverified difficulty claim, not a proven security property.
