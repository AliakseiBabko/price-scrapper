## Planning Rules — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Core stated methodology**: design the floor plan/program around the specific client profile first, then apply aesthetic/finish design afterward — stated as the opposite of designing for looks first and fitting the client to the result, which the speaker says commonly produces something beautiful but impractical to live in. `confirmed` as speaker's stated process; `single-account`, self-serving framing.
- **Kitchen/living-room combination should be driven by guest frequency and overnight-guest duration**, not treated as a default choice — contrasting worked examples: a young couple with a mother-in-law who sometimes stays a month kept the living room **separate** (closable, doubles as guest bedroom); an elderly couple who rarely have overnight guests **combined** kitchen+living room to free space for a large bookcase. `confirmed`.
- **Windowsill-height desk features must be verified per-apartment**, not copied from a "measure from the bottom of the window" rule of thumb — windowsill height varies unit to unit and a blind copy can produce a seating position too low or too high. **Must be paired with blackout/opaque blinds** — prolonged facing of bright/direct light is described as a near-certain eyesight risk, worse on a sun-facing exposure. `confirmed`.
- **Concealed dual-position electrical rough-ins for a room expected to be reconfigured later**: pre-install concealed outlet points ("скрытые электроточки") compatible with both a current and a future furniture arrangement (e.g. a kids' room ahead of a second child) at build/rough-in time. Reconfiguring later needs no rewiring — only cutting the wallpaper over the previously-hidden point and installing the visible outlet mechanism/cover plate; the old point doesn't need capping because it was planned in advance to be compatible with both layouts (underlying electrical-safety mechanism for why capping isn't needed is not explained in the source — flagged as under-explained, not independently verified). `confirmed` as technique; safety rationale `unclear/needs confirmation`.
- **Washing-machine placement should allow access without entering the bathroom/WC** when household laundry frequency is expected to be high (e.g. two-child household). `confirmed`.
- **Bathroom shower-vs-tub choice should be driven by an explicit client-preference conversation, prioritizing the primary occupants over guests** — worked example: a couple who liked showering together vs. a visiting mother-in-law who preferred baths; the team chose the large shared shower, reasoning the apartment is designed for the primary occupants, not guests. A second example: an elderly couple's stated difficulty climbing over a tub rim led directly to a spacious shower stall with a fold-down seat. `confirmed`, real client-conversation-driven decisions in both cases.

## Furniture / Built-ins — Durable Facts
