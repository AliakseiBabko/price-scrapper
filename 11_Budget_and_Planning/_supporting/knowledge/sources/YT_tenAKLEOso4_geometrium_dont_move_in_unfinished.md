## Planning Rules — ⚠️ Directly On-Topic Finding: Never Move In Before the Renovation Is 100% Complete

- **⚠️ Explicit, named "common mistake": moving into the apartment before renovation is fully finished down to the smallest details causes incomplete work to remain unfinished indefinitely** — the speaker's own stated real-world pattern: people living for **years** without a functioning kitchen simply because they moved in before ordering/installing it, since once you're already living there, an unfinished item stops feeling urgent ("it's no longer on fire"). **Explicit recommended sequencing rule**: complete absolutely everything — including minor decor/final touches — before moving in, both so day-one living quality is genuinely finished and, counterintuitively, because finishing before move-in is **faster** than finishing after (unfinished work has no urgency once occupied). This is a directly relevant, concrete heuristic for Topic 3's core question (whether/when to occupy an apartment during or before full completion), complementary to (not overlapping with) the existing `YT_2ZNSYED46LQ` (Zaharchuk) source's cost-based rent-vs-live-in-place analysis — this source adds a *behavioral/completion-risk* argument against partial occupancy, distinct from Zaharchuk's cost and hygiene/safety arguments.

## Budget Ranges — One Adjacent Heuristic (minor addition)

- **20% budget buffer explicitly recommended for time-driven price drift**: because a full design-and-build project on a complex apartment can span from the design phase (2-6 months) plus construction (a year or more for a complex, design-led renovation), the source explicitly recommends budgeting a 20% contingency specifically because material/labor prices can shift meaningfully over that elapsed time, separate from any budget overrun caused by scope changes.
