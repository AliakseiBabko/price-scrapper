## Walls / Ceilings

- **Plaster-thickness compensation rule for offsetting from a developer's (uneven) wall, stated with a worked example**: when measuring a design offset from a wall the developer built (typically much less flat than a wall the crew builds itself), do **not** manually pad the nominal design dimension to "pre-compensate" for expected plaster thickness (e.g. adding 2–3 cm to a 400 mm offset in anticipation of finish-layer buildup) — measure and set the exact projected/design dimension from the real as-built condition (after screed/demolition, before finishing) instead. Reasoning given: plaster thickness on a developer's crooked wall vs. a self-built straight wall is asymmetric and unpredictable in advance — a worked example is given where a crew offsetting exactly 400 mm from a developer wall, without padding, ended up with 415 mm actual finished dimension purely from uneven plaster buildup on that side; deliberately padding the offset in anticipation would have compounded rather than corrected the error. `confirmed` as stated methodology with a specific numeric worked example (400mm nominal → 415mm actual after plastering), `single-account`.
- **Companion rule for the reverse case — offsetting between two self-built (crew-built) walls**: when both reference planes are walls the crew itself builds (not a developer wall), plaster thickness on both sides is consistently even, so the exact design/projected dimension can be used directly with no compensation needed — worked example given where a 600 mm design dimension between two self-built wall segments measured exactly 600 mm after plastering and wallpapering. `confirmed` as stated, `single-account`. Together these two rules form one coherent compensation framework: pad/compensate only when one side of a dimension is a developer's uneven wall and the other is a flat reference or self-built wall with materially different expected plaster buildup; use the nominal design dimension unchanged when both sides are self-built (evenly plastered).
- **Interior partition wall-thickness figures used in this project**: 100 mm partition between bathroom and adjacent spaces (used repeatedly through the layout), 150 mm partition specifically between the bedroom and the kitchen/living space — explicitly stated reason given this time (unlike a prior source's unexplained 150 mm figure): the thicker wall is used here specifically to fit a sound-insulation layer between the bedroom and kitchen/living space, and separately in another spot to fit the water manifold (коллектор) and electrical panel (электрощит) inside the wall cavity. `confirmed` as stated, corroborates and adds explicit reasoning to this store's existing 100/150 mm partition-thickness data point from `YT_m5TalhbzESA`.
- **False wall built specifically to correct/absorb a developer wall protrusion** ("сраный выступ" — a non-flat jog in the developer's wall that can't be knocked down or leveled) — a short compensating wall segment is built out from the true reference plane to square off the room before other offsets are measured from it. `single-account`, consistent with this store's existing false-wall/protrusion-concealment pattern from other Zemstandart sources, applied here specifically to a wall-flatness (not utility-routing) problem.
