## Balcony / Loggia — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Zemskov's material update on wall cladding**: his own long-standing PVC-panel recommendation for loggia cladding is no longer strictly necessary — modern decorative plaster now also holds up well in a humidity/temperature-swing space, **with two caveats he states explicitly**: (1) the space must be genuinely well insulated for plaster to work there, and (2) regardless of which finish material is chosen, **cracking along the frame's edge over time is never fully avoidable** — a durable, honest limitation rather than a defect specific to either material.
- **Zemskov's sill-width rule for a non-standard/beveled-corner loggia shape**: never install a narrow windowsill matching a narrow skewed/angled corner section — instead, make the sill **maximally wide, spanning the full angled area**, so only a single sash needs fitting there. His reasoning: a narrow sill in that skewed corner leaves the space permanently unusable for normal furniture, or forces an expensive custom-furniture order to fill it.
- **Corollary sill rule — never economize with a visible joint**: if widening the sill per the rule above, don't save ~2,000-3,000 RUB by using a long-narrow sill with a visible splice/joint partway along — the seam will be a permanent visual irritant. **Correct approach**: buy one continuous slab sized for the full window run, cut away the unused excess between the two needed pieces, and keep only a single seamless sill with no visible joint — worth the extra material cost.
- **Zemskov's lighting rules, his most emphasized point, split into three parts**: (1) a loggia should always have its own dedicated light fixture — light spilling in from the adjoining room is never sufficient, under any circumstance. (2) **Loggia lighting should never be ceiling-mounted — put it on the "warm" (back) wall instead** — a ceiling fixture physically blocks the sashes from opening, since they strike it. (3) **If a ceiling fixture is used anyway, it must be the flattest model available, and even then a minimum 100mm jamb-extension/добор is required at the top of the frame** — the frame itself is only ~30mm, and once cladding + insulation adds another ~30mm, an opening sash swings nearly flush against the ceiling; no fixture, however flat, clears it without that minimum 100mm extension. **The same minimum 100mm extension is needed if a stretch ceiling will be installed there**, independent of the lighting question.
