- **Vertical stripe/moulding technique for a spacious-but-low-ceiling
  room**: painted vertical stripes or self-adhesive metal moulding strips
  added to a feature wall specifically counteract a room that feels
  spacious in floor area but visually squat due to low ceilings. **This
  corroborates** this store's existing vertical-emphasis low-ceiling rule

  small Russian khrushchevka bedroom, suggesting the underlying
  proportion principle is context-general, not room- or size-specific.
- **Don't add window treatment where it adds no value**: small flanking
  windows beside a TV feature wall, already sealed from outside and not
  otherwise contributing to the room's look, were deliberately left
  untreated rather than curtained "just because" — an explicit
  restraint/anti-over-decoration call.
- **Rug-size mismatch triage rule**: when an existing rug's color/style
  is right but its size is too small (requiring a second layered rug
  nearby to compensate), replace it with one correctly-sized rug rather
  than continuing to layer multiple mismatched rugs — stated size
  target here: **4m×3m** for the room in question.
- **Wall-clock functional-placement rule**: place a wall clock somewhere
  visible from every point in the room, not at an arbitrary decorative
  spot — moved here from beside a window (poor sightline coverage) to
  above the TV console (visible room-wide).
- **⚠️ Chaise-attached armchair as a market-driven substitute for a
  hard-to-find reclining chair**: in a market (here, France) where
  standalone reclining/lounge chairs are hard to source, an armchair
  with an attached ottoman/chaise extension serves the same "watch TV
  while your legs are up" function and is easier to find/move.
- **⚠️ Furniture-consolidation rule for a child's play zone inside an
  open living space**: swapping individual existing pieces one-for-one
  doesn't reduce visual clutter — consolidating several small pieces
  (two side tables + an open shelving unit) into **one larger,
  purpose-fit piece** (a round coffee table with a built-in lower shelf,
  serving as both play surface and toy-adjacent storage) cuts "visual
  noise" in a shared family room more effectively than a like-for-like
  furniture swap.
- **No-sharp-corners furniture selection for a child-use zone** —
  explicit safety-driven design constraint (the round play-table's lack
  of corners is called out as "a big plus").
- **Floor cushions as a low-cost comfort mitigation for a window-adjacent
  play zone** — added specifically because sitting/playing directly by a
  window "might feel cool."
- **⚠️ Storage relegation-with-upgrade-path sequencing rule**: temporarily
  relocate a child's toy-storage unit to a less-visible spot in the room
  (out of the main sightline but still close to the play zone), with an
  explicit plan to replace it with a proper dresser/console **once the
  child outgrows toy storage** — a concrete example of sequencing a
  furniture decision around a child's growth stage rather than solving
  for "forever" today.
- **⚠️ Regional sofa-mechanism market distinction**: in France/Western
  Europe, the "eurobook"-style fold-out sofa mechanism common in Russia/
  Eastern Europe is uncommon; fold-out mechanisms available there are
  typically full sleeper-sofa mechanisms meant for overnight guest sleep,
  not a simple recline-to-watch-TV mechanism — a real, checkable
  regional furniture-market difference relevant to sourcing decisions in
  either market.
- **Modular/sectional seating as an explicit alternative to a fold-out
  sofa for reclined TV viewing**: a bench-loveseat-bench modular
  configuration (e.g. IKEA Kivik-style) avoids the daily friction of
  clearing toys and unfolding/refolding a sofa mechanism just to recline
  — the source's own stated preference over her first-proposed
  transforming-sofa option, specifically because of that daily-use
  friction.
- **Dining-pendant-light diameter proportioned to table size**: a
  50cm-diameter pendant shade chosen to suit a 2m-long dining table — a
  proportion-matching rule for pendant fixtures over a large table.
- **Kitchen-adjacent wall paint durability technique**: a painted wall
  near a cooktop exposed to grease/splatter should get a matte protective
  varnish/sealant top-coat over the paint, specifically to make routine
  cleaning easier.
- **DIY "faux wall panel" technique using oversized art**: hang two
  identical large-format art canvases (here, 2m), with one deliberately
  flipped/inverted so the pattern doesn't read as an obviously mirrored
