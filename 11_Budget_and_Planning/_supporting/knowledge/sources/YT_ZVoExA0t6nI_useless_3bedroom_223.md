## Rules / Heuristics — Genuinely New Content

- **⚠️ Rounded/curved wall corners are a real, well-reasoned developer defect — full mechanism explained**: (1) plastering a curved surface costs multiple times more than a flat wall of the same area; (2) no matter how well-executed, a curved plastered wall shows persistent waviness, and wallpaper reliably bubbles on it; (3) a curved wall forces buying a curved curtain rod/track, which reliably jams and sticks in use; (4) "fixing" this with a straight rod spanning the curve (a chord instead of following the arc) wastes significant floor area, since the standard curtain-zone depth allowance (200mm) then applies across the full straight-line width rather than hugging the actual wall. **Rule: convert a rounded/curved wall segment into two straight, angled ("faceted") segments instead of leaving it curved** — cheaper to finish, doesn't degrade over time, and avoids the curtain-rod problem entirely. A genuinely new, fully mechanism-explained developer-defect + fix pair.
- **Fridge-placement dead-space caution**: a fridge needs a minimum ~60cm of clearance on its access side, and its placement must be checked to ensure nothing ends up permanently inaccessible ("dead space") directly behind it — a specific practical check distinct from this store's existing general appliance-clearance content.
- **⚠️ Detailed "derive wall position from functional clearances, not the nearest visual corner" technique, fully worked**: when a new wall's natural position (from a fixed load-bearing reference point, adding up a door-opening's own footprint plus an adjacent fixture's clearance requirement) doesn't land exactly on a room's corner, **don't snap the wall to the corner for tidiness** — leave the resulting small gap (documented case: ~19mm) and finish it with a small triangular filler instead. Snapping to the corner instead would force the fixture (here, a bathtub) to creep into the interior door's own reveal zone, requiring an ugly on-site notch cut into the tub surround later. **General technique: always derive a wall's position by summing the real functional clearances needed from a fixed reference point — accept a small "wasted" residual gap rather than forcing a downstream fixture/door conflict for the sake of visual tidiness.**
- **⚠️ Combined justification for a small "wasteful-looking" entrance-zone divider wall**: a small partition near the entrance that could technically be omitted for a wider, more open passage was kept for two combined reasons — (1) storage/makeup-zone furniture placed in open space with no defined boundary reads as visually incoherent ("hanging" awkwardly); (2) omitting the divider would force an oddly-shaped, complex stretch-ceiling cut with excessive material waste (offcuts). **Planning heuristic: when evaluating whether a small dividing wall segment is "worth" the floor space it costs, weigh both furniture boundary-definition and finish-material-waste implications together, not floor-space loss alone.**
- **A straight/linear kitchen run narrower than ~2.30m is functionally too narrow regardless of layout configuration attempts** — a documented real too-narrow example (the source's own stated reasoning: no reconfiguration within that width made the kitchen anything but cramped and awkward, ultimately solved only by combining it with the adjacent living room into an open plan instead).
