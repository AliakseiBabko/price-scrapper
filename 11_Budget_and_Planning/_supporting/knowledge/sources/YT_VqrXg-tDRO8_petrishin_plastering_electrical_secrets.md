## Electrical — Corrugated-Conduit Fire Behavior and a Self-Conducted Cable Burn Test

1. **Corrugated-conduit ("гофра") ignition risk clarified as low in
   practice**: theoretically a short circuit could ignite the conduit,
   but in practice the circuit breaker trips well before that — actually
   igniting the conduit requires a sustained overload of roughly 5
   minutes *without* any breaker tripping, a scenario the speaker
   considers unlikely. Once genuinely burning, though, fire spreads
   faster through the conduit than through open cable runs. **Black
   gofra supports combustion somewhat more than gray gofra**, but the
   speaker's own view is that once an apartment is already on fire, this
   color-related difference doesn't materially change the outcome —
   flagged as the speaker's own opinion, not a lab-tested claim.
2. **Self-conducted comparison burn test, standard cable vs. ВВГнг-LS**
   (this page's already-standard cable spec per
   `Cable_Circuits_and_Panel_Design.md`): the crew held an open flame to
   ~30cm cut segments of both cable types. Standard cable's insulation
   failed and slid off the conductor after exposure; ВВГнг-LS kept its
   per-core insulation intact and the cable remained apparently
   functional afterward. Cited as the practical, hands-on reason (beyond
   the existing GOST-certification buying rule already on this page) for
   the company's move toward ВВГнг-LS specifically, not just
   ГОСТ-certified cable in general.

## Walls / Ceilings — Conditional-Value "Quick Flatten" Trowel Technique

3. **⚠️ A long/oversized flat trowel used as a budget alternative to
   full beacon-based plastering — explicitly conditional, not a
   universal shortcut**: when a client has no budget for proper
   beacon-plastered walls and just wants them "roughly evened out," a
   long rigid trowel can visibly improve an uneven wall's flatness
   without a full beacon setup. **Explicit warning**: applying the same
   technique to a wall that's already properly flat (correctly
   beacon-plastered, or a precisely-built drywall wall) can make the
   result *worse*, not better — the technique only has value on a
   genuinely uneven substrate.
4. **Beacon-position marking via a magnetic long tape measure**
   (named product: Fatmax) referenced against a self-tapping screw,
   rather than notching a straightedge — used for spacing beacon
   positions up to ~1.5m apart. (Lower confidence than most extracted

## Planning Rules / Cost Drivers — Post-Plastering Drying-Time Bottleneck

5. **Humidity after plastering is a real, named trade-sequencing
   bottleneck**: high residual humidity in freshly-plastered walls
   blocks the electrician from starting the next stage, and normal
   air-drying takes about 3 weeks. The company's stated mitigation is
   investing in industrial dehumidifier/moisture-absorber units
   specifically to compress that window to roughly 1.5-2 weeks so the
   electrical and painting trades can start sooner — framed as an active
   business decision to reduce dead time between trades, with a further
   stated intention to invest in (or rent) mechanized-plastering-plus-
   drying equipment/"stations" to shrink the window further still.
