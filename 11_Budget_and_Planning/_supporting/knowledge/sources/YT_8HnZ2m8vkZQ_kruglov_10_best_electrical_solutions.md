## Electrical

Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario says (level 1, spoken directly):

- **⚠️ Three governing principles stated up front for every recommendation in this video**: (1) compliance with ПУЭ (Russian electrical-installation code); (2) convenience/functionality; (3) "ремонтопригодность" — repairability, i.e. any vulnerable connection point should be easy to access and fix later. This explicit 3-principle framing is new; each individual recommendation below is this source's application of it.
- **⚠️ Ceiling routing is specifically forbidden (not just discouraged) when the ceiling is a plastered/monolithic finish rather than a suspended one** — chasing into the actual floor slab above is not permitted, a sharper version of this batch's existing false-ceiling-vs-plastered-ceiling distinction.
- **⚠️ Ceiling-height-driven routing cost mechanism, explained precisely**: most sockets sit ~30cm above the finished floor; running a cable ~30cm up from the floor to reach that height is cheaper than running the same connection ~2.7m down from a 3m ceiling — so at standard/low ceiling heights (example given: 2.65m) floor routing is preferred partly just to avoid dropping the ceiling further, and cost scales with cable run length in either direction.
- **⚠️ A concrete "renovate without disturbing existing MEP" scenario**: buying a "whitebox" apartment (liked as-is except electrical/wall finishes) and wanting to avoid demolishing the existing screed or heating risers — the stated solution is to build a false ceiling (drop ~10cm) specifically so all-new electrical wiring can be routed through the ceiling instead of cutting into the existing floor buildup.
- **⚠️ Panel "modes" extended with a second named mode beyond vacation/away**: a **"winter" mode** that disconnects air-conditioner circuits specifically to prevent an accidental cold-weather AC startup, which the source states can damage the unit; distinct from (and in addition to) the vacation-mode exception list already in this store (fridge, router, leak-protection, alarm stay live).
- **⚠️ Explicit "where NOT to cut cost" rule for panel components**: never use non-original/counterfeit breakers or RCDs to save money — the correct way to reduce panel cost is by optimizing circuit grouping (the source's own example: combining WC + bathroom lighting onto one shared breaker), not by downgrading device authenticity.
- **⚠️ Electrical-point density benchmark, based on the company's own design-project sample**: analyzed 30 of their own design projects and derived an average optimal density of **0.85 electrical points per m²** — a "point" counts any outlet, switch, dimmer, underfloor-heating thermostat, HDMI outlet, or low-voltage/network outlet. Worked example: a 50 m² apartment needs at least 42 points; more is fine, less is not recommended. This is a new, checkable planning benchmark not previously in this store.
- **⚠️ Junction boxes are obsolete in modern rough-in practice** — splicing now happens inside a deep back-box at the outlet/switch location itself rather than in separate mid-run junction boxes set into walls/ceilings; among reliable connector types, **Wago spring-clamp connectors are specifically named as the only genuinely serviceable/repairable option** (ties to the video's stated "ремонтопригодность" principle).
- **⚠️ LED-strip transformer placement: two valid options, with the deciding factor being ease of future access, not aesthetics** — mount each transformer near its own LED strip, or centralize all transformers in one location (e.g. a closet ceiling near the panel). Explicit rationale for centralizing: transformers fail relatively often, and a centralized bank near the panel makes locating and replacing a failed unit fast; either approach is acceptable as long as whichever is chosen stays easy to reach.
- **⚠️ Smart-home setup sequencing, stated as a 4-step algorithm**: (1) decide which specific smart devices you actually want (sockets, switches, smoke detectors, motion sensors, door-open sensors are named as the most common); (2) choose the hub/"brain" ecosystem before buying more devices; (3) install/wire/configure; (4) build automation scenarios last, once the base system works. Two connectivity methods named: Wi-Fi and wired network cable.
