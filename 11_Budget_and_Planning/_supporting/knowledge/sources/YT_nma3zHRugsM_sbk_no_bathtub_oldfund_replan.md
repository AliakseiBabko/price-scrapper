- **⚠️ Real found legacy layout defect, used to justify commissioning a design project even for a small (35m²) apartment**: the original layout placed the bathtub and kitchen in the same combined space directly inside the entry, with no functional separation — described by the speaker as absurd (walk in, take off shoes, get straight into a hot bath while simultaneously being positioned to cook dinner). This concrete, checkable dysfunction is used to make the broader point that a design project isn't just "pretty pictures" for a small apartment — it's the tool needed to produce a layout that is both genuinely ergonomic and legally approvable, especially in an old-fund building where any structural/plumbing change must go through official replanning approval.
- **⚠️ Named approval-driven fixture-location constraint, overriding the client's own stated preference**: the client wanted to keep half of an original masonry stove ("печка") in place, on the room side, specifically to use as a small heated nook to sit by in the evenings. - **Partition removal between two original rooms cited for two concrete, checkable benefits**: (1) combining the rooms brought the apartment's large existing windows into the merged space, meaningfully increasing natural light and making the apartment read as larger than its actual footprint; (2) the resulting new partition layout freed a specific wall segment sized for a large television, which the original room division did not allow.
