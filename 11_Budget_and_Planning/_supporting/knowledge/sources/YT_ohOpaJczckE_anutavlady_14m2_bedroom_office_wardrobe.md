## Case — 14m² Bedroom/Wardrobe/Home-Office, England (subscriber "Irina")

- **Space-recovery-by-relocation technique**: removing a small, awkward
  built-in closet (narrow door, wide interior, hard to access) frees a
  ~1m niche in that wall. A full wardrobe (60cm depth) is relocated into
  that niche, leaving clearance for the wardrobe door to open freely —
  net effect: the same storage volume, but a full worktable (120cm width)
  becomes possible in the space the wardrobe used to occupy. (Numbers as
  stated in the ASR transcript; the exact niche/clearance arithmetic
  doesn't fully reconcile on a plain read — flagged `ASR-uncertain` at the
  line-item level, the overall relocation *technique* is not in doubt.)
- **⚠️ Bedside-table sizing constraint under real space pressure**: when
  floor space beside the bed is genuinely tight, standard nightstands
  don't fit — target **max width ~30-35cm, height ~70cm** to match a
  tall mattress-top height. The source explicitly struggled to find
  stock furniture this narrow and this tall together.
- **Wall-mounted rotating/height-adjustable "tray on a bracket" nightstand
  alternative**: when no stock nightstand satisfies the narrow-width +
  tall-height combination above, a swiveling tray-on-a-wall-bracket
  fixture (adjustable height and rotation) is presented as the best
  found substitute — a real product-category workaround for this
  specific narrow-nightstand problem, not a custom-build.
- **Reading-sconce switch-reach test before installation**: when
  wall-sconces have their own built-in switch, mount height should be
  set only after the occupant physically tests reach-from-lying-in-bed
  comfort — not assumed from a standard height figure.
- **⚠️ Door-count visual-fragmentation fix for a small bedroom**: a room
  with 3 doors (down to 2 after a room reconfiguration) is still "a lot"
  for a small bedroom — painting every door the same color as the walls
  (rather than a contrasting door color) is the stated fix to keep the
  room from reading as visually chopped-up/fragmented.
- **Saturated/dark accent color explicitly recommended for small rooms**
  — the source states saturated color (here, a deep green feature wall)
  is "just the ideal solution" for small rooms specifically, choosing it
  over a safer neutral/beige alternative the subscriber had also
  proposed. **⚠️ Flagged contradiction with this store's existing
  bedroom-color rule**: Kruglov/Ontario's "10 Common Bedroom Mistakes"
  source (already on `Bedroom_Design_Principles.md`) states the opposite
  — avoid bright/saturated colors in a bedroom specifically, reasoning
  saturated color is psychologically stimulating and works against sleep.
  Genuine same-topic disagreement between two independent channels, not
  a corroboration either way — see Perspectives note below.
- **Reverse-image-search technique for matching a Pinterest/reference
  photo wallpaper**: search the reference photo via Google Images to find
  the exact original wallpaper (available through multiple print
  catalogs), or bring the same reference photo directly to a custom
  photo-wallpaper printing company, who can locate/print it themselves.
- **Two-tier desk stand for a monitor, framed as an ergonomic/posture
  choice**, not just a styling choice — raises the monitor to a
  comfortable eye height, explicitly linked to better posture during
  desk work.
- **Decorative bed-pillow minimum length rule**: **80cm minimum**,
  longer where the room/bed size allows — stated as a general sizing
  floor, not specific to this one case.
- **Low-ceiling-appropriate chandelier selection**: at a stated **238cm**
  ceiling height, choose a compact-profile but wide fixture with a soft
  fabric shade (for light diffusion) rather than a tall/deep pendant —
  explicitly to avoid the fixture hanging too low or projecting harsh
  light into a sleeping face (the prior fixture's stated problem).
- **Avoid wall-mounted overhead storage directly above a window-adjacent
  desk** — even when storage is tight, overhead shelving above a desk
  positioned near the window is deliberately skipped specifically to
  avoid blocking window light; left as a future addition only if
  storage need becomes acute.
- **Carpet-patch-from-salvaged-material technique**: when removing a
  piece of furniture (here, a closet) exposes a previously-covered floor
  area but the client wants to keep existing wall-to-wall carpet rather
  than replace it, a patch cut from carpet salvaged out of a *second*
  piece of furniture being removed (which will itself be fully covered by
  new furniture afterward) can cover the gap — a no-new-material,
  cost-avoiding partial-fix technique specific to a redesign that
  preserves existing flooring.
