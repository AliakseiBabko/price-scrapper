- **⚠️ Key distinction driving the soundproofing material choice**: ordinary sound-absorbing material (foamed polyethylene wrap, mineral wool wrap) removes *airborne* noise from a pipe but does **not** address the *vibration* component transmitted through the pipe wall itself — a stack specifically needs **vibro-acoustic** soundproofing (a material engineered to damp vibration, not just absorb air-transmitted sound) to be effectively quieted. `confirmed`, mechanism distinction, generalizable and checkable against any similar riser-noise problem.

### HVAC — Separate-Zone Bathroom/Toilet Exhaust Ventilation

- **Developer-default shared exhaust duct is explicitly rejected as inadequate for two separate rooms**: the practitioner's apartment has one shared exhaust ventilation channel physically located in the toilet room, while the bathroom is a separate room with no channel of its own; the developer's own default fix (a flat duct with holes cut mid-run to let both rooms feed into it) is stated to not work "at all" — when a connected fan is off, air simply reverses through the mid-run holes and pushes the other room's humid/odorous air back out into the room instead of extracting it, defeating the whole purpose. `confirmed`, mechanism explanation, checkable reasoning.
- **⚠️ Rejected simpler alternative and the specific reason**: running two independent room-mounted fans (one per room) into the shared duct via a T-junction would still let air currents mix between the two rooms whenever only one fan runs — humid bathroom air would still migrate into the toilet room through the inactive branch. **A ducted/channel fan serving both rooms through fully separated, individually dedicated duct runs (not a shared T-junction) is required instead**, to keep the two rooms' air streams genuinely isolated from each other while still exhausting through the one available shared building channel. `confirmed`, mechanism reasoning, directly generalizable design rule for any apartment with one exhaust channel serving two wet rooms.
- **Named product: S&P TD-EVO-VAR channel/duct fan** — low-profile, compact; quiet operation attributed to rubber vibration-isolating mounts ("сайлентблоки") that prevent vibration transfer to the housing; built-in electronics with multiple operating modes including a continuous-run mode at an adjustable minimum speed, a timer, and support for external air-quality sensors via a 0-10V analog signal input. His chosen configuration: continuous low-speed running with a step-up to higher speed on manual button/switch activation, and a timed delay before dropping back to minimum speed after the switch is turned off (rather than an instant drop). `confirmed`, product-spec claim.
- **Air-distribution box, a required system component**: a custom sheet-metal box (commissioned from a metalworker/"tinsmith") splits/combines the airflow between the two separate room ducts and the single shared building exhaust channel; the practitioner explicitly notes this could instead be self-built from flat plastic duct sections using a heat gun (thermal glue gun) at a much lower cost than commissioning a custom metal box, for anyone on a tighter budget. `confirmed`, buying-alternative heuristic.
- **Ducting materials and joining technique**: rigid plastic flat/round duct sections (125mm size cited for the main run) joined with hot-melt/thermal glue at every gap-prone seam (a recurring technique across this whole channel's series — this is the source's *third* Round-4 sighting of hot-melt glue used for a construction-adjacent purpose, after episodes #16 and #18); flexible, multi-layer sound-insulated ducting used for the final room-to-distribution-box connections, secured with plastic cable ties and reinforced aluminum tape; a custom 90°-bend duct section fabricated by the practitioner himself from flat ducting stock using an angle grinder and heat gun, rather than bought pre-formed. `confirmed`, technique detail.
- **Design outcome stated plainly**: this configuration allows the bathroom and toilet's air streams to be exhausted through the single shared building channel while remaining fully separated from each other, preventing sound, humidity, and odor transfer between the two rooms — directly satisfying the stated goal from the video's own opening line. `confirmed`.

### Walls / Ceilings

- **Wet-wall furniture-vs-tile concealment rule, a genuinely reusable planning heuristic**: for a wall carrying plumbing fixtures/equipment (in this case: a wall-hung-toilet frame/"installation," manifold plumbing distribution, a water heater, and ventilation equipment), the practitioner explicitly recommends concealing it behind a **removable furniture-style construction rather than permanently tiling over it** — so any of those mechanisms can be accessed again later without demolition. `confirmed`, technique/heuristic, directly generalizable and consistent with (though not identical to) general "leave an access point" guidance already present elsewhere in this project's stores. `single-account`.
- **Sequencing rationale for finishing a wall before mounting equipment on it**: once plumbing/HVAC/electrical equipment is mounted to a wall, working on that wall's own finish becomes far more difficult — so the wall itself should be brought to a finished state (in this case, a two-coat cement-based finishing putty over a primed substrate) *before* any equipment is hung on it, not after. `confirmed`, sequencing rule, generalizable.
