## Materials — Шумопласт (granular impact-noise underlayment), a distinct product from this store's existing Шумонет

1. **Шумопласт (ASTic Group)**: a granular expanded-polystyrene-bead
   material bound with an acrylic-based binder plus a rubber-crumb
   compensating additive, sold in ~13.8kg bags (15.8kg with packaging),
   one bag covering ~10 m² at 2cm thickness. **Manufacturer-stated
   reduction: -28dB impact noise, -7-9dB airborne noise.** This is a
   **distinct product from this store's already-documented "Шумонет"**
   underlayment (a different material category — see comparison below),
   not a duplicate entry.
2. **Шумопласт vs. Шумонет, explicit product-selection comparison**:
   Шумопласт achieves a **higher soundproofing level** than Шумонет and
   handles a badly uneven old-building slab (Stalin-era, Khrushchev-era)
   better, since the loose granulate self-conforms to an existing
   uneven surface rather than needing to be cut/trimmed/joined the way
   a sheet material (Шумонет) does on an out-of-level slab — Шумонет
   would tear under those conditions. A concrete criterion for choosing
   between the two by substrate condition, not just by soundproofing
   spec alone.

## Rules / Heuristics — Floating-Screed Application Technique

3. **Floating screed principle with a named perimeter tape**: the
   screed must have no rigid connection to the slab or walls — a
   damper/isolation tape (Vibrock M150), applied in **two layers**
   around the full room perimeter and stapled to the wall, is the
   mechanism that stops noise transmitting into the walls at the
   floor-wall junction; skipping it lets noise reflect back off the
   wall itself.
4. **Шумопласт application sequence and QC signals**: prime the slab
   first (removes fine dust for adhesion) → spread granulate to ~2cm
   using rail-guide battens as a level reference (or floor-leveling-
   compound pins/stakes on a badly uneven old-building slab) → compact
   with a screed float, **listening for a denser sound as confirmation
   of proper compaction** (a real practitioner-taught QC signal, not
   just a visual check) → periodically rinse the float with water to
   prevent bead buildup and improve compaction. **Don't extend an
   already-compacted section after 10-15 minutes** — beads won't bond
   properly onto material that's already begun setting; complete a
   section in one continuous pass.
5. **Cure time, manufacturer spec vs. real practice**: the manufacturer
   states 24 hours before pouring screed over Шумопласт, but the
   practitioner's own experience recommends waiting **48 hours** for a
   denser, more reliable result — cure speed varies with season/room
   temperature/drafts, so 48h is the safer default.
6. **Cover the cured granulate layer with plastic film before screed
   crews arrive** — without it, foot traffic from 3-4 screed workers
   plus heavy material bags can cause the granulate to detach from the
   substrate.
7. **Real screed sand-quality caution**: sand must be washed river sand
   of a consistent grade, and explicitly **not frozen** — a real winter
   problem when a crew's sand delivery arrives partially frozen in
   clumps and gets used anyway under schedule/cost pressure. Cement
   dosage must also be kept within a correct range — too much or too
   little both cause problems.
8. **Fiber additive ("фибра")** mixed into the screed to create
   micro-fiber reinforcement that helps prevent cracking.
9. **Reinforcement mesh must be physically lifted mid-pour** so it ends
   up embedded within the screed body rather than resting uselessly at
   the bottom — if it settles at the bottom, "it doesn't work at all,"
   and the money spent on it is wasted.
10. **Screed curing protocol, explicit 2-3 day sequence**: cover with
    plastic film immediately after pouring, then for 2-3 days
    periodically uncover and thoroughly water the screed (a 5-6cm+ pour
    absorbs this without issue), then re-cover — done to slow moisture
    loss so the concrete/cement reaches its rated strength; skipping
    this is common and causes cracking, lifting, and warping even on an
    otherwise well-executed pour.

## Cost Drivers — Screed-Level Planning Lifehack, Quantified on a Real 120 m² Object

A concrete, quantified restatement of this store's existing screed-
buildup cost-trap mechanism (previously recorded from Round 3/4 as a
general 150,000-300,000 RUB range): on this real 120 m² apartment, if
the finish-flooring plan (e.g. porcelain tile at the entry vs.
quartz-vinyl elsewhere) isn't known and communicated to the foreman
*before* the screed pour, a later self-leveling compound layer to
equalize the height difference across the whole apartment costs a
**minimum additional ≈300,000 RUB (≈$3,800)** — avoidable entirely by
deciding finish materials early and having the foreman pour the screed
at pre-calculated differential heights instead.
