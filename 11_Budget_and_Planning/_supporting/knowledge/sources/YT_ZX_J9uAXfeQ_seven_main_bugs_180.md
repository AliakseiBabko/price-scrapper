## Durable Facts

- **Kitchen curtain necessity depends on the whole apartment's window-covering consistency, not the kitchen in isolation**: if every other room (living room, bedroom) uses roller blinds rather than curtains on the exterior glazing, a kitchen with no curtain "zone" designed into the layout doesn't need one either — presented as a design-consistency rule, not a universal "never curtain a kitchen" rule.
- **Large fixed panoramic window panes (stated example: ~2×2m) that cannot be manually washed should be cleaned with a magnetic window-cleaning gadget (manual or motorized/self-driving), rather than three explicitly discouraged alternatives**: (1) hiring industrial climbers — expensive and not always high-quality, often requiring repeat visits; (2) attempting to reach the outside from indoors with a mop, a stated developer-blame tactic that doesn't work; (3) **ordering the sash split into smaller opening sections** — the practitioner states this fails on multiple counts: the added weight of an opening sash eventually tears its hinges and causes visible sagging, it creates a cold thermal bridge that ices over the first winter, and — the most consequential point — **splitting a large fixed facade pane into operable sections is described as a facade-code violation carrying a formal restoration order plus fines**, forcing the owner to revert the change at their own cost. **Genuinely new panoramic-glazing maintenance + facade-modification-risk warning** for this store. Developer-installed large fixed panes are explicitly defended as a deliberate, reasonable design choice (unobstructed exterior sightlines/building aesthetics), not a developer failing.
- **Always size a radiator recess/niche for a concealment grille even for a client who currently wants an open, unconcealed radiator** — real observed pattern: clients who initially insist on open radiators (for drying items, easy access/cleaning) commonly change their mind after living with the exposed radiator, but retrofitting a niche after the fact is often no longer possible once walls are finished. Pre-sizing the niche and leaving it in a rough/unfinished state costs little extra at design time; the client pays a modest surcharge for the visible rough surface if the grille is never installed later, versus a niche retrofit being effectively impossible. **Genuinely new pre-emptive design-flexibility heuristic**, distinct from this store's existing radiator-concealment content.
- **Standard multi-hinge revision/access hatches sag measurably over time**: a real example shown — a hatch installed roughly one week prior with an even ~2mm perimeter gap had already opened up to a 4mm gap on one edge and 0mm (binding) on the opposite edge, with the practitioner stating this sagging only worsens and cannot be corrected once it starts. **Flagged as tier-steering/self-promotional**: the same passage previews an upcoming company product (an anti-sag hatch developed with a manufacturer, plus a planned dedicated online store) not yet available at time of filming — the sagging problem itself is a genuine, useful defect-mechanism observation, but the proposed fix is an unreleased proprietary product, not a neutral market alternative; record the mechanism, not the product pitch.
- **Avoid automatic/sensor-triggered fixtures (motion-activated exhaust fans, automatic lighting) in favor of simple manual on/off switching** — stated design philosophy: automatic triggers are "uncontrollable," turning on/off according to their own sensor logic rather than user intent. Illustrative anecdote cited (a person on a step-ladder repeatedly triggering a light on/off cycle via a falling/settling book, worsening a fall) as a cautionary tale against over-automating a home. `single-account` design opinion, not a cited safety standard.
- **Study/homework desk placement rule, distinct from a general work desk**: a desk specifically for handwriting/schoolwork should sit strictly to the right of the window so daylight falls from the left onto the writing hand (explicitly modeled on standard classroom layout — light from the left, board at the front). A general (e.g. computer) work desk has more placement freedom (either side of a window) but should **never face directly into the window** — cited eye-strain mechanism: repeated pupil adjustment from looking up/down between a bright window and a screen accelerates vision deterioration; keeping curtains closed to compensate is impractical long-term.
- **Desk width comfort rule, refined with an on-camera comparison**: a genuinely comfortable desk width is 70–80cm, not 50/40/30/20cm — a 60cm-wide desk is workable only for a small laptop (demonstrated: a 13" and 16" laptop both fit reasonably on a 60cm desk), but becomes unworkable once a monitor (demonstrated at 21") is added, since the required viewing distance pushes the keyboard/elbows into an awkward, back-fatiguing position regardless of how close the desk is pushed to the wall.
- **Long-bedroom wardrobe placement hack**: in an unusually long bedroom, the doorway opening can be shifted up to ~80cm from its "classic" position (normally: door opens flat against the entry wall, ~10cm hinge-side clearance for a ~95° swing, then a 15–20cm gap before a ~60cm-deep wardrobe begins) to free space for a deeper wardrobe (60 or even 70cm) positioned so the door swings directly onto the wardrobe face. The usual floor-mounted door-stop is replaced with a small adhesive door-stop sticker mounted directly on the wardrobe front — demonstrated as durable even under repeated hard door contact. Only applicable in a bedroom with enough length to spare; **genuinely new space-recovery layout technique** for this store.
- **Telescoping wall-mounted drying racks as a space-efficient alternative to a fixed ceiling-mounted drying rack** — folds to a minimal footprint but extends to hang a full washing-machine load. **Multiple units (2–3) can be installed in the same small room at different heights** when space is tight, all foldable out of the way when not in use — a detail the practitioner notes most people overlook (assuming only one unit is possible).
- **TV electrical-point placement, two-part rule**: (1) the TV's own main power connection should be routed to an outlet positioned directly behind the TV itself, not confused with the secondary outlets typically found lower/under a TV stand (those serve peripherals like a set-top box); (2) **for wall-mounted TVs on a bracket, the outlet must be placed clear of the bracket's own footprint** — designers/planners should check typical bracket dimensions for the specified TV size and position the outlet in a free zone that doesn't sit under the bracket mechanism, and does not extend beyond the TV's own visible frame either. Extends this store's existing TV-cable-provisioning content with a bracket-clearance dimension check.
