## Furniture / Built-ins — Countertop On-Site Trim & Reinstall Technique

- **Root-cause framing, worth recording as a supply-chain caution, not a technique**: an exact-to-the-millimeter project and kitchen-manufacturer cut order does not guarantee an exact-to-the-millimeter countertop if the actual slab is separately sourced from a building-materials market rather than supplied by the kitchen manufacturer itself — the intermediate supplier in this case treated a 6mm overage as within tolerance and proposed trimming a thin decorative edge cap instead of the slab itself, which the source states is not actually feasible (the cap material chips/can't be evenly trimmed by that margin with a standard mitre saw).
- **Dishwasher clearance standard, explicit and reused as a live check during the repair**: 450mm clear width under the countertop for a built-in dishwasher — the source treats this as a fixed project figure to preserve exactly, not something to compromise for the sake of accommodating the oversized slab.
- **On-site countertop-length trim procedure, full sequence**:
  1. Unscrew and remove the countertop; unscrew the joining plate (already sealant-coated from the original install) and trim/scrape old sealant off one side, then peel it fully away.
  2. Apply painter's tape along the countertop's visible front (laminated) edge before cutting, to prevent chipping/splintering at the cut line.
  3. Flip the countertop face-down — cutting with a fine-tooth mitre/chop saw against a laminated countertop must be done from the *back* face, not the front, to keep any chip-out on the hidden underside.
  4. Set the saw's stop/fence to remove exactly the needed amount (6mm in this case) from the cut end.
  5. Re-set the dishwasher clearance to the fixed project figure (450mm) as the reference for where the cut line falls, confirmed with a tape measure before cutting.
  6. Cut with a shop-vac connected to the saw's dust port throughout.
  7. Flip the countertop back over; **do a dry test-fit for edge/reveal clearance before doing any edge finishing work** — explicit sequencing rule, framed as a step people skip at their peril.
- **Sealant/joint reassembly sequence, explicit and time-gated**:
  1. Re-tape the visible front edge with painter's tape (protects it during the joint-sealing steps, removed once sealant is applied).
  2. Apply silicone sealant to the cut countertop edge, smooth with a rubber spatula, then remove the tape while the sealant is still wet (not after it skins over).
  3. Apply sealant to the joining plate itself, aiming for a fully closed/unbroken bead around its perimeter (matches this store's existing ≥3mm continuous-bead joint rule from the companion countertop-assembly source already routed to `03_Kitchen/Furniture/Kitchen_Furniture.md`).
  4. Seat the joining plate, press until visible sealant squeeze-out appears around its edges, fix with 12-16mm-long self-tapping screws, then immediately wipe excess sealant with a rubber spatula and finish cleanup with paper towels so no residue is left on the visible countertop surface.
  5. Apply sealant to the joining plate's second (front-facing) side.
  6. **Wait 5-7 minutes for the sealant to begin curing before reinstalling the countertop** — an explicit, numeric working-time rule not previously recorded this precisely in this store's countertop-joint content.
  7. Reinstall the countertop: raise and seat the *rear* edge against the wall first, then lower the front edge into final position (not the reverse) — explicit installation-order rule to avoid dragging/smearing the fresh sealant joint.
  8. After reinstalling, re-check the full countertop perimeter for a tight fit against the wall, re-check the joint for a sealed/consistent appearance, and re-verify the module dimensions (dishwasher clearance) one final time before considering the repair complete.
