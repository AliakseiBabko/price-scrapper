## Rules / Heuristics — Genuinely New Techniques

- **⚠️ "Truncated closets" (усечённые шкафы) — a named, deliberate design pattern**: a closet built to look like a full-depth unit from outside, where only part of its width has genuinely usable depth. **The correct use of the shallow/unusable section is for wall-mounted utility equipment (an electrical panel, a water-supply manifold), never for clothing/general storage** — reserve the genuinely deep section(s) for real storage and let the shallow section absorb an otherwise-wasted room irregularity while still doing useful work. Distinct from simply criticizing a shallow closet as a defect (as in other Category 5 sources) — this is the constructive version of that same geometry.
- **A single enclosed/glazed loggia can be split into two separate functional zones (e.g. two home offices, each paired with its own adjacent storage closet) by building a partition wall within it** — a genuinely creative space-multiplication technique: build the partition, then treat each half as its own mini-room with its own heating/insulation contour, rather than treating an enclosed loggia as one large undifferentiated space by default.
- **A furniture-maker's fabrication tolerance for a tapering/wedge-shaped corner cabinet section: at least 4-5cm of material at the narrowest point, never a true zero-width taper** — cabinet makers can't fasten a drawer front or door panel to a knife-edge. **Practical design consequence**: when designing a corner filler/wedge unit, reference the guide line from the unit's *depth* dimension to guarantee this minimum, not from its width — the width itself can be almost anything, but the depth at the narrow end must clear this floor.
- **A kitchen enclosed behind its own door (not open-plan) needs its doorway opening sized to include full clearance for the door's own swing, separate from the standard "cabinet run + 100mm reveal" wall allowance** — a specific worked clarification: even though a kitchen cabinet run itself needs only 600mm depth + 100mm reveal (700mm total), an enclosed kitchen's actual doorway zone needs 900mm — the extra 200mm is for the door leaf itself to open without striking the cabinet run. **General rule: don't confuse a cabinet-run wall allowance with a doorway zone's true footprint when the door swings into the same area.**
- **Before allowing a designer to proceed to rendered visuals, require a stated justification for every wall thickness, every opening reveal, and every irregular corner — a concrete, actionable vetting heuristic.** The source's own framing: a designer who can't (or won't) explain the reasoning behind a specific dimension, or dismisses the question as a trust issue, is a red flag regardless of how polished the eventual renders look — renders can make a fundamentally broken layout look appealing, but layout defects don't show up until you look at the plan dimensions themselves. **Practical advice: demand dimensional justification first, approve aesthetic direction only after the layout itself is validated.**
