## Walls / Ceilings

- **⚠️ Decision driver for false-wall material choice: whether the underlying wall is load-bearing/monolithic, and building-type-specific chasing rules**: a monolithic reinforced-concrete load-bearing wall **cannot be chased/cut at all** to route plumbing — any pipe run against such a wall must be concealed by an added false-wall structure instead, never by cutting into the wall itself. Distinct rule for a **panel-building apartment**: shallow vertical chases are permitted there, but panel-building bathrooms typically have non-load-bearing walls surrounding them anyway (unlike a monolithic-frame building, where the same wall position may well be load-bearing) — so the panel-building chasing allowance is somewhat moot in practice for a typical bathroom, since those walls are usually cuttable regardless. `confirmed`, checkable rule, directly extends this store's existing "route the widest pipe along the thickest bearing wall, bridge the gap with a false wall" principle (already on `Structure_and_Framing.md` from an unrelated Sidorik episode, #4) with a clearer explanation of *why* a false wall becomes necessary in the first place.
- **⚠️ Full-bodied brick false wall as a bathroom-specific alternative to sheet-material (drywall/GVL) framing, with explicit reasoning for the switch**: the practitioner initially planned a sheet-material (GVL/drywall) false wall — and had already plastered the substrate wall in preparation for that approach — but switched to solid/full-bodied brick (250×120mm units) laid on-edge and glued flat against the wall, specifically **"to make the structure massive/robust,"** i.e. for extra rigidity/mass at a location carrying bathtub plumbing, water outlets, sink supply, and a washing-machine drain. `confirmed`, `single-account` design decision with stated (if brief) reasoning — a genuinely new false-wall-material option for this store's bathroom framing content, which previously covered only sheet-material and block/silicate-block options.
- **Brick-laying technique for this false wall**: full-bodied brick, 250×120mm, laid both on-edge (main structural courses) and flat/plashmya (bonded fully to the substrate wall as an anchoring course) — glued with Holcim block adhesive (the same adhesive already used for this practitioner's partition walls in episode #4), noted as suited to strongly-absorbent surfaces. First course leveled with wedge shims to correct an uneven floor; second course onward laid by eye/without a level once the first course establishes the reference plane, set with adhesive and lightly hammer-tapped. Whole wall completed in one working day. `confirmed`, checkable technique + timeline.
- **⚠️ Sub-millimeter marking precision required, with a concrete worked example** — a genuinely new precision-planning detail: because tile-joint tolerance is measured in fractions of a millimeter, the false wall's own layout must account for the *finished* tile surface position, not the raw substrate. Worked example: a 75cm-wide bathtub recess was marked at 72.5cm on the raw substrate — the missing 2.5cm is reserved for tile thickness, adhesive layer, and the plaster coat that will go over the brick — with the laser-level reference line itself offset by that same 2.5cm below the intended finished height for the same reason. A horizontal reference line for a future tiled shelf/ledge above a countertop (where electrical outlets will be recessed) is marked the same offset way. `confirmed`, checkable dimensioned technique, directly generalizable to any false-wall or masonry layout that will later be tiled.
- **Brick-cutting dust-control technique, a reused DIY trick from elsewhere in this channel's own repertoire**: angle grinder fitted with a section of sewer pipe as an improvised dust-extraction shroud (his own words: "an old, proven scheme") — cut partway through from both faces, then snap the brick rather than cutting all the way through. `confirmed`, minor technique, `single-account`.
- **Plastering-adhesion-layer habit, explicitly flagged by the practitioner as a personal reflex rather than a necessity**: even though the cement-sand plaster (Holcim) is stated to bond well to brick directly, he applies a combed tile-adhesive bonding layer first anyway "because it certainly won't hurt" — applied one day ahead so it's dry before the plaster coat goes on. Substrate given two coats of deep-penetrating primer beforehand. Plaster sets enough to trim to beacons in roughly **3-5 hours** depending on conditions (a checkable, if `single-account` and condition-dependent, cure-timing figure). `confirmed`, technique + timing detail.
- **⚠️ Thin OSB + GVL false wall as a distinct, much-lower-profile technique for a kitchen utility-stack cluster** — a second, genuinely different false-wall construction method in the same video, worth treating as its own reusable technique rather than a variant of the brick method above: the developer's original kitchen false wall (concealing the vent-channel/riser cluster, "узел со стояками," specifically so nobody drills into ventilation ducts or needs outlet boxes cut into it) was solid block, **16cm thick**. The practitioner demolished it and rebuilt a much thinner version: metal-stud drywall profile frame (standard Knauf-style track + studs, screwed to a pre-plastered, plumbed, 90°-squared substrate wall) clad in two layers — **18mm OSB board** as the first/inner layer (painted white, two coats, on the utility-facing side for a clean appearance if ever reopened; pre-drilled/cut for the numerous cables emerging from the wall; bonded to the plaster substrate with polyurethane adhesive plus screws for solid contact; small gaps deliberately left between OSB sheets) and **12.5mm GVL (gypsum fiber board)** as the second/outer, tile-ready layer (screwed to the OSB with GVL-specific fasteners) — total finished thickness including the ~3cm plaster layer on the substrate: **only 38mm**. Extra blocking/reinforcement profiles were added at the top of the frame specifically to support a hanging kitchen cabinet's load. GVL was chosen over plain drywall specifically because this surface will be tiled. A vent opening was cut into the finished assembly with a jigsaw. `confirmed`, checkable dimensioned technique — this OSB+GVL combination and the 38mm total thickness are new, specific numbers for this store's false-wall content.
