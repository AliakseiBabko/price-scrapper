## Planning Rules — Full-Apartment Replan, Corner-Damage, and Storage Techniques

- **"Wardrobe-split-into-two/three" storage technique, a reusable concept**: an S-curved (or, for three-way, a more complex) partition wall splits what would otherwise be one large storage volume into 2 (or 3) separately-accessed wardrobes — e.g. one entered from a hallway, another from an adjoining bedroom, sharing the same overall footprint. **Failure mode observed in the critiqued project**: the same technique taken too far produced a "seven-wardrobe" outcome — seven small, individually near-useless storage nooks from over-subdividing one storage zone, illustrating that the technique has a practical subdivision limit.
- **Exterior-corner placement should route around, not into, actual foot traffic and door-swing arcs** — a corner that must exist somewhere (not eliminable without other tradeoffs) should be positioned as far as possible both from where people would physically strike it while walking and from where a swinging door or opened drawer/cabinet face would repeatedly rub against it (the two are treated as distinct failure mechanisms — impact injury/damage vs. gradual wear from repeated brushing contact). A real worked example shows deliberately choosing a slightly awkward wall angle specifically to push an unavoidable corner into the lowest-traffic path available, at the cost of a less "clean" floor plan.
- **Sofa-to-TV minimum distance for a large-format television: 2.7m.** Stated as the practical minimum to avoid feeling like the screen is "in your face"; the same distance can be shorter for a smaller TV, but 2.7m is the target once a large-format screen is the given fixture. `confirmed`, explicit numeric target not previously recorded this precisely in this store.
- **A bed-that-folds-into-a-storage-wall unit ("Murphy bed" style) can convert a small guest bedroom into flexible-use space (home gym / yoga studio) on demand** — a real worked example: a client's wishlist included a home yoga space they assumed was impossible to fit anywhere in the apartment; combining the guest bed with a fold-away storage-wall mechanism let the same room serve as a normal guest bedroom by default and open into enough clear floor space for yoga (even for two people plus an instructor) when the bed is stowed.
- **Water-inlet-node (collector) height and reach, a checkable installer-quality point**: mount the node at a normal standing-reach height in an open location (e.g. above a wall-hung toilet's installation frame), never low behind a shower stall where accessing it requires kneeling and reaching around/behind other fixtures — a real defect found placed the node behind a shower, reachable only by kneeling inside the shower stall itself and reaching into a cramped gap.
- **A washing machine should never be placed directly under a sink specifically to save floor space if it forces the sink itself to an ergonomically wrong height** — a real defect found: fitting the machine under the sink pushed sink height up to an impractical level, and the layout still didn't accommodate the client's actual requirement (both a washer and a separate dryer).
- **Exterior wall insulation can be selectively removed to widen a loggia's usable floor area, if — and only if — the resulting glazing itself provides adequate thermal performance without that extra insulation layer.** A real worked example: removing an existing insulation layer plus its windowsill portion widened a loggia by a full 20cm, explicitly framed as safe specifically because the planned replacement glazing is rated for that exposure without the removed insulation.
- **Sound isolation between a bedroom and adjoining shared spaces (hallway/lift lobby) should be planned as a first-priority layer, before furniture layout** — a real defect found: a sliding door directly connecting a bedroom to the building's own elevator lobby left occupants able to hear the lift doors opening/closing at all hours, described as a serious, avoidable sleep-quality defect that should have been caught at the earliest planning stage.
- **Door casing reveal/return (отбортовка) must be applied consistently on both sides of an opening, not just one** — a real defect found: the critiqued project applied a matching return on one side of an opening (correctly copying this channel's own technique) but omitted it on the opposite side of the same opening, defeating the purpose of the consistency the technique is meant to achieve.
