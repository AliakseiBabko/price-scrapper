## Materials — Named Pipe System and a Durability Claim

- **Kan Therma Ultraline — axial-press metal-plastic pipe, no internal
  rubber O-rings**: unlike classic metal-plastic pipe (which has two
  internal rubber rings that can become a weak point over time), this
  system press-fits using a tension sleeve with no rubber seal at all.
  Specs: max temperature 95°C, max pressure 10 bar, wall thickness
  2.2mm. Fittings available in brass or PPSU; **different pipe types can
  be joined to each other** (e.g. metal-plastic to PEX/"сшитый
  полиэтилен").
- **A visual/mechanical advantage over classic metal-plastic at a
  corner fitting**: classic metal-plastic elbows show visible diameter
  necking/narrowing at the joint; this axial system's elbow avoids that
  narrowing.
- **A seminar-verified durability data point** (the speaker attended a
  manufacturer seminar): quality pipe doesn't delaminate under 1 tonne of
  tensile force. A worked stress test: a 16mm pipe was first expanded
  with a 16mm expander, then re-expanded with a 20mm expander — the pipe
  stretched but remained intact (didn't crack or delaminate).

## Rough Plumbing — Installation Tooling and Technique

- **Same press tool required as for PEX ("сшитый полиэтилен") pipe** —
  normally a rental item; the speaker obtained a battery-powered
  (cordless) version instead of the usual corded rental tool.
- **Installation process, identical to PEX**: slide the tension sleeve/
  ring onto the pipe, expand the pipe end in two passes with a 30°
  rotation between passes, then compress/seat the fitting with the press
  tool.
- **Additional parts needed**: water outlets with internal threads
  ("водорозетки" — can mount to a wall or a dedicated mounting plate),
  universal connectors for attaching pipe to the manifold, tension
  sleeves, floor pipe clamps, and color-coded insulation sleeves (red for
  hot, blue for cold).
- **The core selling point of this whole approach: zero joints inside the
  screed** — a single continuous pipe (in conduit/insulation sleeve) runs
  from the water outlet fitting straight to the manifold connection, with
  no intermediate joint buried in the screed at all. **Directly reinforces
  this store's existing manifold/radial-distribution rationale** (fewer
  embedded fittings in the screed) with a concrete illustration of what
  "zero embedded joints" looks like in practice for the supply side.

## Rough Plumbing — Mounting Workaround, Spacing, and Chase Confirmation

- **Real workaround for a genuinely new product's missing accessory**: no
  mounting plates existed yet for this specific new pipe system at the
  time, so the speaker improvised — cut the mounting tabs off plates from
  a *different* (PEX) system and instead glued the water outlets in
  place with hot glue. Explicitly described as "having to improvise a
  bit" (колхозить).
- **Mixer-fitting outlet spacing: 150mm center-to-center**, glued
  precisely to this spacing since a mixer will attach there later —
  **independently confirms this store's existing 150mm shower-mixer
  fitting-spacing convention** on
  `12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Fixture_Stubout_Coordinates.md`
  from a different practitioner/project.
- **Chase (штроба) sizing reaffirmed on a different work stage**:
  chases were pre-cut earlier (during the sewage-install stage, already
  shown in episode #24) sized to the *outlet/fitting's* depth and width,
  not the thinner pipe itself — an independent same-channel confirmation
  of the chase-sizing-to-fitting rule already extracted from episode #24
  this round.
- **Sequencing tip**: mount and level the water-outlet fittings first,
  *then* run pipe to them — doing it the other way (pipe first) makes it
  harder to get the fittings level since the pipe is in the way.
- **Where the 150mm mixer spacing doesn't apply** (a single supply
  point, e.g. a washing machine), skip the dedicated mounting plate
  entirely — use plain ГВЛ (gypsum fiber board) as a simple mounting
  platform, or for a genuinely single point (the washer), just embed the
  outlet directly into the plaster with no plate at all.
- **Insulate exposed pipe/outlet ends before plastering** to keep plaster
  out of the fitting during the wall-finishing pass.
- **Pipe-routing sequencing logic**: lay pipe runs simply so they don't
  cross each other — side by side in a row, in whatever order avoids
  intersections. Full pipe layout for the entire apartment took **1
  day**, described as feeling "like assembling a toy construction set"
  given how fast the press-fit process is once outlets are mounted.
- **Pressure-testing (опрессовка) performed after full layout**, before
  final wall patching.
- **A specific PPSU-fitting warning**: spray foam was used to fill deep
  recesses before plastering, but **PPSU fittings must never be sprayed
  with foam** — foam wasn't applied at press-sleeve locations either.
  `single-account`, a specific material-compatibility warning not
  previously in this store.
