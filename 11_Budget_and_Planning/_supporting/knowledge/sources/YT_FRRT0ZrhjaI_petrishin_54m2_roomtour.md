## Rules / Heuristics — New Mechanisms and Real Mistake/Fix Examples

2. **Manufacturing-defect rework absorbed at no extra client cost**: the
   wardrobe's factory production didn't match spec — roughly half the
   unit was remade from scratch at company expense, with no additional
   charge to the client. A self-promotional anecdote (flag per the
   advertising filter: a company describing its own warranty behavior),
   but a concrete, checkable claim rather than a vague "great service"
   testimonial.
3. **Sofa-sizing heuristic — usable seat width vs. stated width**: when
   choosing a sofa, subtract the width of both side armrests (cited
   example: ~20cm each, ~40cm combined) from the stated overall width to
   get real usable seating width — a sofa that looks wide enough on
   paper can end up cramped once armrest width is accounted for.
   **Firmness/comfort should be tested in person** (the client visited
   showrooms and sat on multiple options before choosing a firmer,
   customized cushion spec) rather than chosen from a catalog photo
   alone.
4. **TV-viewing-distance structural constraint, and the workaround**:
   distance between a sofa and a TV wall was fixed by two load-bearing
   columns and couldn't be increased — the practical fix is choosing a
   **smaller TV diagonal** instead of a bigger one, specifically to
   avoid an uncomfortably close "front-row cinema" viewing effect,
   rather than trying to change the fixed distance.
5. **Wood-panel toilet-cabinet access door, functional + aesthetic
   rationale**: the wall behind a wall-hung toilet was built as a
   furniture panel with two wood doors (not tiled) — reasoning given
   directly: wood reads as more comfortable/aesthetic than cold tile,
   while still giving full serviceable access (the toilet tank/flush
   mechanism unscrews on two bolts, the panel and upper doors are
   removable) to all the concealed plumbing behind it. A concrete
   aesthetic-vs-tile alternative for a toilet's rear access panel.
6. **Range-hood type-change mid-project causing a real hole-relocation
   redo**: the design originally planned a hanging/freestanding hood
   centered on a pre-cut duct opening; realizing a built-in hood gave
   more usable storage, the team switched hood types — which shifted
   where the duct opening needed to be, requiring a technician to
   relocate the hole (done in a few hours, no major disruption). A real
   example of a mid-project design-preference change cascading into a
   rough-work rework, caught and fixed cheaply rather than left as an
   unresolved mismatch.
7. **3D wall-panel integration precision caution**: when built-in
   furniture (e.g. a shelf) butts directly against a 3D-textured wall
   panel, the panel's cut edge must be essentially perfectly straight —
   even a 1mm deviation at that junction is immediately visually
   obvious. A real construction-precision requirement specific to
   combining 3D panels with adjoining furniture, not just a general "be
   careful" caution.
8. **AC indoor-unit placement precision caution, a real installed
   error**: the bedroom AC indoor unit ended up offset ~1-2cm left of
   the doorway's own centerline after installation — the client caught
   it and had it corrected. **Recommendation given directly**: model the
   AC unit's exact placement in the 3D design model beforehand,
   especially in a tight installation space, since even a 1-2cm error
   is visually noticeable there.
