## Durable Facts (Level 1, `single-account`/`unverified` throughout)

- **A small glazed loggia left as "cold" (i.e., the building's insulated/heated envelope stops at the interior wall, not the balcony's exterior glazing) is described as functionally near-useless space**, even when nominally merged visually with an adjoining room: the only heat source available (a radiator positioned in the balcony corner) cannot offset the heat loss of a large glazed area, leading to chronic condensation on the glazing and moisture reaching the floor below. The problem is worsened when the space adjoins a kitchen (steam/humidity from cooking) and a high-traffic living room.
- **A cold glazed enclosure adjoining a living space cannot be furnished properly**: reasons given — no solid wall to place shelving/TV against, glazing directly at floor level is a safety hazard for furniture edges and (if it were a kids' room) for children given the ordinary (non-laminated/non-tempered) glazing typically installed, and any clutter/storage placed in the space remains visible from the adjoining room through the glass, making it read as a visual "junk zone" rather than usable storage.
- **Fix applied: move the insulated glazing contour out to the balcony/loggia's own exterior wall**, converting the space into fully interior, heated square footage — then subdivide the newly-interior space with a partition wall (built with an L-shaped/niche-shaped opening in this case) rather than leaving it as one undivided open extension of the living room. Reasoning for subdividing rather than opening it up fully: (1) a home-office/study function doesn't belong visually merged into a living room — it accumulates its own clutter (cables, papers, "creative mess") that shouldn't be visible from the main living space; (2) a partition gives each side a solid wall to anchor furniture against (e.g., a corner sofa needs a backing wall — without the partition it would sit exposed in open space with an awkward transition to the office furniture behind it); (3) a partition creates clear zoning so the boundary between "living room" and "office" reads immediately rather than blending ambiguously.
- **Radiator/permitting workaround** (recorded as the speaker's stated practice, not independently verified against any specific jurisdiction's code): rather than formally relocating the radiator onto the balcony/loggia footprint (which the speaker implies triggers a materially harder approval process), the crew keeps the radiator in its original pre-existing location and instead builds the new partition with a niche/recess around it, and points to the relocated glazing contour itself (described as "the cheapest balcony window block available") as the documented evidence that the insulated envelope, not the radiator, defines the compliance boundary. The radiator niche is finished with decorative trim/paneling and a ventilation grille to conceal the radiator while still allowing heat to circulate. `unverified`, single account, and presented by the speaker somewhat glibly ("show the inspection commission the glazing contour and tell them where to go") — recorded here as the speaker's stated approach, not as generally-applicable legal guidance.
