## Plumbing — Rough-In Coordination & QC (new items only)

1. **Utility-cabinet (`тех.шкаф`) rough-in rules, three distinct items**:
   placement must match the design project's stated location exactly, or
   the whole manifold/collector assembly ends up offset behind the
   access hatch; a light fixture should be installed inside the cabinet
   (repairing equipment one-handed while holding a phone flashlight in
   the other is explicitly called out as the alternative to avoid);
   waterproofing the cabinet interior is recommended since it's a
   frequent minor-leak location — protects the surrounding wall finish
   from small leaks rather than preventing them outright.
2. **Equipment labeling for owner self-service**: every piece of
   equipment at the collector/manifold node should get a labeled sticker
   naming its function, so the owner can perform basic plumbing actions
   (isolating a branch, etc.) without calling a professional for routine
   tasks.
3. **Radiator alignment relative to the future window sill, not just a
   level check**: radiators must be checked level and square with a
   level and square tool (not by eye) — but also specifically parallel to
   where the future window sill will sit once windows go in, since a
   radiator that's level to the floor but visibly skewed relative to the
   sill above it reads as a defect to some occupants even though it
   passed a basic level check.
4. **Radiator supply-pipe fixation detail**: pipes feeding into/out of a
   radiator must be anchored in their wall chase with plaster mix at the
   wall entry/exit points specifically (not just anywhere along the
   chase), so the pipe doesn't move against the finished radiator
   connection.
5. **Pre-closure pipe-documentation technique, with a named failure mode
   it prevents**: before sealing plumbing chases, photograph the exact
   pipe routing and tape-measure the vulnerable/hidden runs. Stated
   real incident: an installer later cutting a hidden access hatch
   (`люк-невидимка`) under a bathtub accidentally punctured a pipe
   because no one on-site knew exactly where it ran — this company's own
   practice is to always produce these measured photos so a tiler later
   knows exactly where the routing sits under the finished floor.
6. **Towel-warmer (`полотенцесушитель`) sustained-run test — a specific
   false-negative test-timing failure mode**: don't just confirm the unit
   heats at initial startup — let it run a full 1-3 hours before judging
   it working. A unit can heat normally when the system first pressurizes
   but cool back down within 1-3 hours if there's an underlying
   insufficient-pressure, poor-circulation, or placement problem — a
   short initial check alone will miss this and pass a defective
   installation.
7. **Tile/adhesive-thickness mixer-centering mistake, a specific
   mechanism**: a common plumber mistake is not accounting for the
   finish tile plus tile-adhesive thickness when planning the bathtub
   mixer's position relative to the tub — the result is a mixer that
   can't be centered on the tub's spout/drain once tiling is done, and
   the water stream runs off-center from the tub opening instead of
   straight down the middle.
8. **Shower-valve-height design mistake, with a stated root cause**: a
   frequent designer mistake is setting the shower mixer/valve height
   without accounting for the future shower podium height plus the
   specific shower-arm/showerhead reach — an average-height adult ends up
   hitting their head on the showerhead. Root cause: height wasn't
   checked against the *combination* of mixer height, podium height, and
   showerhead arm length at design time. **Fixing it after the fact
   requires demolishing the finished tile** — this must be caught and
   corrected at the rough-plumbing stage, before tiling.
9. **Same-crew rough-and-finish plumbing recommendation, with a stated
   incentive mechanism**: ideally the same crew performs both the rough
   and finish plumbing stages, since they're then personally motivated to
   get the rough stage right (they'll be the ones dealing with any
   consequence at finish stage) — must be explicitly agreed with the crew
   *before* work starts; raising it mid-project is described as poor
   practice.
