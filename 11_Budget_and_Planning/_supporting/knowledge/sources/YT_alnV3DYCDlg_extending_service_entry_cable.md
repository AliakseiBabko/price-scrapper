- **When relocating a panel within a new-build**, code/permitting realities (multiple inspection commissions) typically prohibit simply extending the entry cable along interior walls in surface-mounted trunking the way you could in an older building under renovation — the existing embedded entry cable segment must instead be spliced/extended using a proper method, not rerouted freely.
- **Determine the entry cable's cross-section before buying splice materials**: either ask the building management company directly, or (with the apartment de-energized) cut a small sample from the **ground (yellow-green) conductor only** — explicitly do **not** cut into any other conductor for this purpose (safety warning: only the ground conductor is safe to sample from a de-energized-but-uncertain cable) — and bring that sample to the supply shop to match cross-section.
- **Splice-kit shopping list for extending a cable**: matching-size cable, a cross-section-matched splice/junction sleeve/capsule kit, crimp sleeves ("gilzy") and insulated-flange lugs/ferrules, and a hand crimping tool if the installer doesn't already own one (implies a professional electrician would already have this tool — its absence is a red flag about the installer's experience level).
- **Stranded-to-solid conversion via crimp sleeve (gilza)**: most modern building entry cables are soft/stranded (multi-strand), and stranded wire clamped directly under a screw terminal will loosen over time — the fix is to crimp a ferrule/sleeve onto the stranded wire end first, converting it into an effectively solid, screw-terminal-safe connection.
- **Splice-capsule procedure** (gel-filled cold-splice kit, described as rated for permanent underwater/submerged cable splicing): insert both cable ends into the connector body and clamp; place the joined connector inside the splice housing/capsule and seal the cable entry points with the included sealing tape; mix the supplied two-part epoxy/gel adhesive (a sealed pouch split by an internal clip — do **not** cut the pouch open to mix, remove the clip instead, then knead for about a minute until the pouch visibly warms); pour the mixed compound into the capsule through its fill port once the housing is firmly secured in place (never held loosely by a second person — cable and capsule can jerk unexpectedly and spray adhesive, which the speaker warns is dangerous if it contacts skin/eyes); remove the fill funnel, cap the port, and tilt the assembly to distribute the compound evenly.
- **Standard safety practice cited**: always fully de-energize the apartment before starting cable work, and prevent any possibility of accidental re-energization during the work.
