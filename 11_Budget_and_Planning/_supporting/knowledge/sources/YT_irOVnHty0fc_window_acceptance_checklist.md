## Balcony / Loggia / Windows — Durable Facts & Rules

### Installer-process checklist (what a crew should do, in order) — standard window

1. **Check window and opening dimensions before demolition** — Konstantin's stated consequence of skipping this: if a measurer's error surfaces only after demolition, the opening stays fully open for a **minimum of 3 days**, even with rush manufacturing.
2. Unpack, prepare a staging area, disassemble the new unit (sashes + fixed-section glass out) to ease handling.
3. Prep the frame: attach anchor plates at correct spacing, or pre-drill through-fastener holes at correct spacing.
4. Disassemble the old window the same way; **free the old anchor plates from their fixing points before cutting the old foam joint** — cutting foam first means the saw keeps hitting the still-attached plates.
5. Remove old window; clean the opening of old foam/debris, with intermediate cleanup as needed.
6. Set new window in the prepared opening; **remove the exterior protective film at this point** — Konstantin's explicit warning: if the crew doesn't do this now, the customer has to do it later, which he describes as difficult and genuinely dangerous to attempt personally.
7. Level horizontally and vertically with a laser plane-builder; fix the window — anchor plates need **minimum 2 fixing points each**; through-fasteners need **≥50mm penetration** into the wall material.
8. Prepare external waterproofing for the foam joint (tape or a vapor-permeable membrane) before setting the window, not after.
9. Seal the exterior window-to-building joint with sealant; install exterior cover strips if the design calls for them.
10. **Foam the joint in two passes minimum** — skipping this risks through-voids that grow over time and eventually cause interior drafts around the window's perimeter.
11. Apply vapor-barrier tape over the joint (protects the foam from interior room humidity).
12. Install sashes and fixed-section glass; install handles; final hardware adjustment; check locking-hardware position; install decorative hinge cover caps.

### Customer acceptance checklist — standard window

**Tools needed** (most homeowners won't own these — Konstantin's explicit advice: ask the crew to lend you their own tools to check their own work): laser plane-builder, tape measure, a glass coating (low-E) detector, an elevation tripod, digital calipers, two different types of squares, a hand bubble level.

- **Horizontal/vertical level check**: project a laser's vertical beam onto the frame's edge, checking left/right alignment top and bottom. **Tolerance: 1.5mm per meter of height, maximum 3mm total over the full unit height.**
- **"Street-to-house" plane check** (the frame's perpendicularity to the wall depth-wise): laser parallel to the window, square to measure the beam-to-window distance top and bottom, both sides.
- **Horizontal level check**, same laser/tripod method. Explicit consequence of skipping any of these: reduced service life and malfunctioning hardware.
- **Foam-joint check**: even fill, no voids — if found, insist the crew fix them *before* final acceptance/payment.
- **Exterior joint finish check**: foam must never be visible from outside — must be covered with sealant or tape; unprotected foam degrades quickly and leads to freezing at that joint.
- Confirm exterior protective film was actually removed.
- Check handle-mounting screws are fully tightened.
- **Check every single locking/hardware point on a sash, not just whether it closes** — Konstantin's worked example: 7 points total (3 hinge-side, 4 handle-side); his explicit mechanism: a sash with only one functioning point will still visually appear to close normally, but with the rest disengaged there will be real drafts. Check each point individually.
- **Cross-check profile brand against the design brief**: legitimate profile systems carry a branded protective film, duplicate marking on the window's interior part, *and* marking inside the bead's own groove; cross-check hardware brand against spec the same way (hardware also carries maker markings).
- **Verify low-E/energy-efficient glass if specified, three methods**: (1) a dedicated coating detector tool; (2) the glass unit's own edge marking/label; (3) a lighter-flame reflection test — a low-E coated pane reflects the flame in a visibly different color than plain glass; **bonus**: counting the number of flame reflections reveals how many glass layers the unit actually has.
- **Advanced check**: digital calipers to measure the "flush-under-the-rebate" (наплав под притвором) dimension — the gap between frame plane and sash plane, which every profile manufacturer specifies exactly in their own documentation; an out-of-spec value risks drafts around the sash perimeter.
- **Drip-cap sound-insulation check**: tap/knock it — should sound dull/deadened if properly sound-dampened; a ringing sound means no dampening material was used, and rain noise will be audible.

### Loggia glazing on a parapet — nuances beyond the standard-window process

- Verify presence *and* correct sizing of добор profiles, connectors, exterior cover strips, drip caps, and canopy/cap pieces — loggia glazing typically arrives as separate individual units, each prepped like a standard window but with добор profiles fitted around its own perimeter beforehand.
- **Critical sequencing rule**: every unit must be fully assembled indoors *before* the old glazing is demolished — Konstantin's stated reason: weather conditions can seriously complicate installation otherwise.
- Remove any adjoining structure (old railing) and any cold-bridge elements around the opening's perimeter as part of demolition — skipping cold-bridge removal causes freezing specifically at those points.
- Install units progressively (left-to-right or the reverse), temporarily fixed on all sides first, level horizontal/vertical, *then* permanently fix the perimeter — same foam-in-two-passes and vapor-tape rules as a standard window.

### Loggia glazing acceptance checklist — additional to the standard-window checklist

- **Additional check specific to loggia glazing: verify добор profile size against spec on the left, right, and top.** Konstantin's stated consequence: an undersized добор lets you see the building's own facade through the gap around the glass (a real, checkable visual defect even if the foam joint itself is technically within spec); an oversized добор instead throws off the foam-joint's own dimension. **Specific foam-joint-width spec cited for this project: 15-60mm.**

### Floor-to-ceiling panoramic glazing — additional nuances

- Same basic process as parapet glazing (unpack, disassemble, добор + anchor plates per unit, demolish, clean, install), plus: remove any existing railing in front of the glazing; may need to remove wall insulation adjoining the glazing to allow proper fixing of the new construction; **a fall-arrest harness is explicitly required for this height of work.**
- **Erker (bay/angled) connector nuance**: where the loggia's footprint bends/angles rather than running in a straight line, install this specific connector type at the angle points — a plastic profile housing an internal metal tube, which is what actually provides the rigid structural connection between the two angled units; not interchangeable with an ordinary straight joint.
- **Design-brief-specific requirement**: the bottom section's set height must leave the exact clearance dimension the project specifies for future floor insulation and finish buildup — the glazing's bottom reference must account for the eventual finished floor stack-up, not just today's raw slab.

### Floor-to-ceiling panoramic glazing acceptance checklist — additional to the parapet-glazing checklist

- Confirm the erker connector is actually used at any angled joint, not a plain connector substituted in.
- **Verify correct glass placement at the bottom section**: impact-resistant (triplex) glass must be on the **room-facing side** — checkable by ear, since striking impact-resistant glass produces a noticeably duller/deadened sound than plain glass.
- If the design brief specifies frosted/tinted glass, verify its actual presence.
- Same добор presence/sizing check on sides and top as parapet glazing.
- **A genuinely new check versus parapet glazing**: verify the actual clearance dimension reserved under the glazing (for insulation + finished flooring buildup) matches the project's specified value — a parapet's own structure already fixes this dimension, but a floor-to-ceiling unit's bottom placement is a construction choice that must be explicitly checked against spec, since nothing structurally forces it to be correct.
