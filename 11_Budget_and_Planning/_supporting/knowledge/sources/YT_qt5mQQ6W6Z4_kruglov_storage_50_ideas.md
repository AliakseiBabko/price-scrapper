## Kitchen Storage (→ `03_Kitchen/Kitchen_Furniture.md`)

- **Telescoping drawer dividers**: wooden (beech-type) dividers without
  connecting crossbar inserts hold up best long-term; models with
  crossbar inserts between dividers are less reliable/stable.
- **Dish-drying rack order**: store mugs on the lower shelf, plates on
  the upper shelf (opposite of the common default) — mugs are used more
  often and this keeps them at an easier reach height for shorter/average
  users; plates' greater diameter needs the lower rack's larger vertical
  clearance anyway.
- **Shelf-mounted stick-on pull-out drawers**: adhesive-mounted pull-out
  drawers can be added above and/or below a single fixed shelf inside a
  budget modular kitchen that has mostly hinged (not pull-out) fronts —
  a low-cost way to add drawer-style storage without replacing the
  cabinet box.
- **Bulk dry-goods containers**: wide product choice for
  grains/sugar/salt.
- **Shelf organizer ("second-tier" shelf)**: a stacking unit that adds a
  usable storage layer above an existing shelf item (e.g. above a pot).
- **Pull-out trash bin**: cheaper marketplace alternatives exist to
  dedicated multi-bin sorting systems that can cost **20,000-30,000
  RUB** — not meaningfully worse at the basic function.
- **Trash-bag dispensing technique**: keep the bag roll at the bottom of
  the bin itself; pull a fresh bag from the roll each time the full one
  is discarded, so a spare is always in a known place.
- **Concealed sponge holder**: mount on the back of the sink cabinet
  face or under-sink, out of sight (avoids a visibly bright/colored
  sponge as visual clutter).
- **Integrated dish-soap dispenser**: built into the counter/faucet run
  (matching-brand or third-party), more aesthetic than a free-standing
  dispenser bottle.
- **Door-back containers near the trash-bin cabinet**: for dishwasher
  tablets or cleaning gloves specifically — **not** for cleaning rags,
  which need to air-dry and should be stored elsewhere; dry (already-
  dried) rags are fine there.
- **Paper towel storage**: under-upper-cabinet mount (flagged by the
  presenter as visual clutter, not recommended) vs. an in-drawer
  dispenser insert (preferred).
- **Tall pantry cabinet (~35cm wide) upgrade**: replacing the standard
  narrow pull-out basket/carousel (called only "questionably
  convenient") with a custom rear-of-door shallow-but-wide shelf system
  ordered from the kitchen company — a real cost add (~30,000 RUB for
  the full interior) but a genuine usability improvement over the
  narrow pull-out.
- **Cutlery tray selection criteria**: maximize drawer-footprint
  coverage; choose a tactilely pleasant, durable material so it doesn't
  degrade within ~6 months.
- **Home medicine storage**: dedicated sized boxes for pill
  organizers/medicine, kept in the kitchen.
- **Lid storage**: door-back clip-style mounts for pot/pan lids.
- **"Magic corner" cost/usability alternative**: rather than an
  expensive rotating "magic corner" corner-cabinet mechanism (called
  "cool but expensive"), narrow wheeled bins that roll in and out of the
  same dead corner zone are a much cheaper ("kopeck-priced")
  near-equivalent for usability, holding cleaning supplies, rags, or
  even potatoes.
- **Bulk-food clip dispenser with pour spout**: an airtight sealed
  container/clip combining sealed storage and a pour spout, replacing
  plain plastic bag clips for cereal/grain-type dry goods.

## Bathroom Storage (→ `07_Bathroom/analysis/Shelving_and_Furniture.md`)

- **Cosmetic organizer boxes**: off-the-shelf plastic organizers in
  various shapes/colors, recommended specifically as the fallback once a
  renovation is already finished and a built-in tiled niche or concealed
  storage can no longer be added.
- **Cotton-pad/cotton-swab dispensers**: near-unlimited product variety;
  sealed-enough to keep out excess humidity, avoiding rummaging in an
  open bag each time.
- **Detergent/cleaning-chemical boxes**: dedicated bins for laundry
  powder and household chemicals, stored near the washing machine zone
  or elsewhere in the storage system.
- **Uniform-container labeling for liquids**: use matching containers
  (same shape/style, different labels) for shampoo/conditioner/gel
  products, for visual unity and minimum clutter.
- **Adhesive (no-drill) hooks**: pre-applied adhesive backing lets hooks
  mount directly to tile without drilling; available in cheap plastic or
  pricier styled-metal finishes.
- **Wall-mounted mop/broom holder**: a wall bracket to hang a mop
  vertically rather than leaning it loose.

## Entrance/Hallway Storage (→ `01_Entrance/analysis/Storage.md`)

- **Restates this channel's own existing claim** (already recorded on
  this page from `cM0AndkKdVk`): closed shoe storage is preferred over
  open shelving, given accumulated street dust from shoe soles — **not
  re-recorded as new**, flagged here only as a same-channel repeat
  across two videos.
- **New — robot-vacuum-compatible open shoe storage**: if you use a
  robot vacuum, store shoes on an *open* shelf raised on a plinth/podium
  specifically so the vacuum can pass underneath — this avoids having to
  clear the shoe-storage niche by hand before every cleaning cycle (a
  cheaper robot vacuum otherwise disturbs shoes trying to enter the
  niche and everything needs re-placing afterward).
- **Coat/outerwear storage**: prefer closed storage for the bulk of
  outerwear; at most 2-3 open hooks for frequently-worn items.
- **Key-tray critique**: a dedicated small "keys only" shelf/tray
  reliably ends up holding miscellaneous clutter in practice (not
  actually just keys) — instead use a small closed console table or a
  pull-out drawer specifically sized for keys, tissues, a shoe-cleaning
  sponge, etc., to avoid open-surface spillover, and add a raised edge
  ("бортик") to any open catch-all surface if one is used, or loose
  items will slide/fall off it.

## Dresser & General Closet/Wardrobe Storage (→ `14_Furniture/Wardrobes_and_Storage.md` / `analysis/Wardrobe_Vocabulary_and_Sizing.md`)

- **Drawer organizers for underwear/socks**: pre-made insert options, or
  the same telescoping-divider hardware described for kitchen drawers —
  same reliability caveat applies (the divider itself holds well; the
  connecting crossbar/insert between two dividers is the weak point and
  can loosen or detach).
- **Watch storage box**: recommended once a household has 2+ analog
  watches (smartwatches that live on a charger don't need this) —
  otherwise watches accumulate scratches and dust loose in a drawer.
- **Bag storage — dividers work well specifically for bags, unlike
  clothing stacks**: vertical bag-compartment dividers (the same
  hardware sometimes marketed for stacking folded shirts) perform poorly
  for shirt stacks (top-heavy load bows the thin divider) but work well
  for bags specifically, since a bag's center of mass sits low and
  doesn't load the divider the same way.
- **Multi-level closet rods**: install upper and lower rod levels, plus
  a dedicated long-garment zone (dresses, trousers) sized taller than
  the rest.
- **Pantograph (pull-down) rod lift**: optional accessory for reaching a
  high rod, especially useful for shorter users — worth skipping if it
  doesn't suit the household or is too expensive, not a mandatory
  fixture.
- **Storage box/bin selection for a walk-in/wardrobe interior**: choose
  a box line first (plastic vs. textile; clear/windowed vs. solid), then
  size the internal shelving/cubbies to that specific box's footprint —
  lets every cm² of the closet interior be used efficiently. Textile
  boxes with a clear window make contents visible without opening;
  solid (non-windowed) boxes can be labeled instead using adhesive label
  tags.
- **Telescoping basket/drawer rails**: same top-of-shelf or
  bottom-of-shelf mounting options described for kitchen cabinets, reused
  for wardrobe/walk-in shelving.
- **Dedicated trouser hangers**: multiple mechanism types available,
  pick by personal preference.
- **Small-accessory organizers**: dedicated compartments for belts,
  glasses, jewelry, cufflinks, etc., planned in alongside the
  large-item (hanging clothes) storage rather than as an afterthought.
- **Fabric/textile shoe-storage bags**: a distinctly higher-end
  alternative to cardboard shoe boxes or generic plastic storage bags —
  no cardboard bulk, doesn't read as "cheap," used for the portion of a
  shoe collection that doesn't fit the main wardrobe/entryway zone
  (seasonal off-rotation pairs).
- **Wheeled low bins for hard-to-reach zones**: narrow bins on casters
  for the floor of a walk-in closet or for under-bed storage, so the
  container itself rolls out rather than needing to be dragged/lifted.
