## Doors / Trim — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Concealed/hidden doors ("двери как на Pinterest") are trend-driven**, popularized by designers/bloggers/high-end interiors, creating pressure that a renovation "looks dated" without them. `confirmed`, speaker's stated market observation.
- **Cost multiplier**: a concealed door is described as a full system (special frame, special hardware, opening prep, wall prep, special installation), not just a door — typically **2–3x the price of an equivalent classic door**; across 5–6 doors in an apartment this compounds into "hundreds of thousands of RUB" difference. Design renders/visualizations typically do not disclose this cost delta to the client up front. `confirmed`, corroborates and quantifies the multiplier already implied qualitatively by other sources in this store (yt_yHmEQTqduDk's ~2x install-cost finding, from a different, unrelated company).
- **Wall-quality prerequisite**: classic doors and their casings forgive minor wall unevenness (casing hides small deviations); concealed doors forgive nothing — any waviness/deviation becomes visible, so concealed doors require higher-spec wall prep as a prerequisite, itself an added cost. Speaker's analogy: "sport wheels on a car with a broken suspension" — installing concealed doors in an otherwise economy-tier renovation is described as a mismatch. `confirmed`, a genuinely new mechanism not previously in this store (existing sources cover cost/soundproofing/mechanism-durability, not this wall-prep-quality precondition).
- **Cracking at the seam over time**: building settling (especially in new-construction buildings) can produce a fine crack at the door-to-wall material junction months/years after installation — described as a cosmetic/structural-motion artifact, not a defect, but visually alarming to clients who paid a premium expecting a permanently flawless flush surface. `confirmed`, speaker's stated experience; new mechanism for this store (distinct from the frame-registration mismatch defect already recorded from another source).
- **Daily-use wear is more visible on concealed doors**: because the door reads as "part of the wall" rather than a separate object, fingerprints, scuffs, and impact marks (worse with dark paint colors, worse with kids/pets, ~20 opens/day) stand out more than on a classic door with a casing "frame" that visually contains such marks. `confirmed`, corroborates and generalizes the specific dark-paint-fingerprint-marking mechanism from another source in this batch (yt_-HKwxCBa40k).
- **Poor maintainability/repairability**: because the door and wall function as one integrated surface, a later cosmetic change (repaint, re-finish) that would be trivial with a classic door becomes a more complex, multi-trade "quest" with a concealed door. `confirmed`, corroborates existing store content on concealed-door renovation complexity from a second, independent source.
- **Expectation-mismatch warning**: clients sometimes install a concealed door expecting it alone to deliver a "magazine-cover" interior result, then are disappointed because premium-interior perception is driven by many compounding factors (lighting, furniture, proportions, materials, ceilings, textiles, details) — not achievable via a single component swap. `confirmed`, speaker's stated framing; a genuinely new caution not previously in this store (distinct from, but thematically related to, the store's existing "price per m² without context is meaningless" framing fact).
- **Where concealed doors are still recommended (speaker's own balanced conclusion)**: minimalist interiors, where visual space-expansion and reduced "visual noise" genuinely matter to the design intent — but the decision should be driven by fit to interior/budget/lifestyle, not trend-following alone. `confirmed` as the speaker's stated view — this is the source's own conclusion, kept distinct from the tier-steering caveat above since it is presented with real caveats rather than as a blanket sell.
