## Furniture / Built-ins — Durable Facts & Rules

- **⚠️ The defining distinction between a "sliding partition" and a
  "sliding door" (wardrobe/closet door), stated directly and used as a
  practical field-identification rule**: a genuine interior sliding
  partition is custom-sized to the specific opening (no standard sizes,
  unlike a sliding door) and its mechanism/track sits **only at the top**
  — the floor is always completely clean underneath. **If you see a
  bottom-mounted mechanism/rail, that is a wardrobe/closet ("шкаф-купе")
  door, not a partition.** This is a sharper, more actionable version of
  the top-track-only convention already recorded on
  [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Sliding_Partition_Mechanisms|Sliding Partition Mechanisms]]
  from three other independent sources (dver1.ru, Kupe-dsgn, and the
  13DS/Olesya + Sergey Gusev pair) — now a **fourth** independent source
  confirming the same top-track-only convention, and the first to frame it
  as a direct partition-vs-door identification test rather than just a
  design/installation preference.
- **Three mounting-type taxonomy, matching but adding detail to the
  existing dver1.ru/Holzberg framework**:
  1. **Visible ceiling rail, in-opening** — the classic option, used when
     the opening is already finished/no false ceiling is possible (e.g. an
     unmodifiable concrete ceiling that can't be routed/recessed); rail
     concealed by a decorative trim casing.
  2. **Hidden ceiling rail, in-opening** — requires roughly a 6cm niche
     recessed into the ceiling to fully conceal the rail; only the leaf
     itself is visible, no exposed hardware.
  3. **Wall-mounted rail, leaves sliding along the wall** — used when the
     full opening needs to be simultaneously and completely clear; leaves
     retract along the wall face rather than staying within the opening.
- **Leaf-count taxonomy tied to opening width, with real limits per
  configuration** — genuinely new sizing/selection guidance not yet
  recorded on this page:
  - **2-leaf, hidden-ceiling-rail**: the single most popular configuration
    — minimal mechanism footprint — but only one leaf's worth of the
    opening can ever be open at a time (part of the opening stays covered
    regardless of which leaf is drawn back).
  - **3-leaf, in-opening**: leaves nest against each other on the same
    minimal-footprint rail; two of the three sections can be opened, but
    part of the opening still stays covered.
  - **Wall-mounted (leaves slide along the wall)**: fully clears the
    entire opening, but is **limited to 2 leaves only**, each leaf up to
    **125cm wide** (so a maximum ~2.5m total opening), and needs enough
    clear wall surface beside the opening for the leaves to retract into.
  - **⚠️ "Паровозик"/telescopic pocket system — framed as the most
    advanced solution**: leaves recede one behind another into a
    pre-planned wall niche/pocket, planned at the design stage. Fully
    clears the entire opening AND uses a minimal rail footprint
    simultaneously — the source frames this as resolving the trade-off the
    other configurations above can't avoid (full clearance vs. minimal
    hardware footprint). Consistent with (and a second, independent
    confirmation of) the cassette/pocket-mounting family already recorded
    from Holzberg on this page, though this source doesn't give a specific
    added-wall-thickness figure the way Holzberg's ~190mm example does.
  - **4-leaf and 6-leaf configurations**: used for openings beyond what
    2-3 leaf systems can span — roughly 1.5-3.5m covered by 2-3 leaf
    systems per this source, 4.5-6m by 4- or 6-leaf systems.
  - **Stationary (non-sliding) partitions**: permanently fixed panels with
    no moving rail at all — used purely to divide space while preserving
    borrowed light, e.g. two fixed glass panels flanking a hinged door for
    an office cutout, or the same idea applied to carving out a kitchen or
    walk-in-closet zone from an open space.
  - **⚠️ Custom "book"/transformable folding partition** — this company's
    own developed solution for a large open space needing occasional
    partial division (real cited client requests: a meeting-room cutout,
    and a car-dealership VIP-delivery bay): small panels fold together
    like a book, either extending to close the opening or collapsing into
    a small stack against a wall when not needed. A distinct mechanism
    from the fixed-leaf-count systems above, and from the "book"-style
    telescopic *leaf* variant already recorded from Holzberg (that one is
    a leading-leaf-pulls-trailing-leaf sliding system, not a folding
    accordion-style structure).
  - **Single-leaf sliding systems for narrow openings**: for an opening
    too small for a 2+-leaf sliding partition but where a hinged door's
    ~1m swing clearance isn't wanted — either a single leaf sliding along
    the wall, or a single leaf retracting into a pocket/niche. Stated to
    be common in Italian residential design but rare in this source's own
    market.
  - **"Magic"-mechanism sliding door (explicitly classified as a door, not
    a partition)**: constrained to standard door widths (60/70/80/90cm)
    by the rail itself; the sliding mechanism is fully concealed behind
    the door leaf for a minimalist "clean opening, no visible hardware"
    look — distinguished from a true partition by both its standard sizing
    and its behind-the-door mechanism concealment (vs. a partition's
    always-custom sizing).
- **Material taxonomy — aluminum+glass vs. wood veneer, each with a
  stated trade-off**:
  - **Aluminum + glass**: single-glass leaf = lightest weight, maximum
    light transmission; double-glass leaf (two glass panes set on either
    face of the same profile) = framed as giving materially better sound
    isolation than a single pane — one of the two panes can be a mirror
    instead of glass. Glass finish options: clear, frosted/matte, various
    tints, a textured "streaming rain" visual-effect glass, and a glass
    with an internal filling (e.g. metal mesh) for a volumetric light
    effect. **A distinct semi-transparent/one-way-mirror-effect glass** is
    also available — reads as a mirror on the unlit side when only the lit
    side has its lights on, and as transparent when the lighting reverses
    — a real functional detail not yet recorded on this page. Aluminum
    moldings (single vertical/horizontal bars, a Japanese-lattice-style
    grid, or other geometric patterns) can further divide a leaf visually,
    independent of the glass-type choice; matching a partition's finish to
    an adjoining hinged door's finish is recommended for overall design
    unity.
  - **Wood veneer** (second-most-popular material per this source):
    described as adding warmth and slightly better sound isolation than
    aluminum+glass by default; wide veneer species/tone range (light,
    medium walnut, oak, smoked oak, dark), with vertical, horizontal, or
    C-shaped grain-orientation options. Finish can also be paint-ready,
    wallpaper-ready, or factory-pre-painted.
- **⚠️ Quality-check checklist for evaluating a physical sample before
  buying** — a concrete, checkable procedure not yet recorded on this
  page in this form:
  1. Prefer proven European manufacturers.
  2. Physically test a sample leaf's open/close motion — should be smooth
     with no audible noise, vibration, or creaking.
  3. Confirm soft-close dampers are present — explicitly framed as
     protecting both the finish and fingers, given a partition leaf's real
     weight.
  4. Inspect the profile coating (typically powder-coated) for evenness —
     no visible unevenness or "orange-peel" texture, and it should feel
     smooth to the touch.
  5. Confirm the glass is genuine safety glass and ask the manufacturer to
     specify/verify it directly.
  6. Check overall proportions and detailing (e.g. flush/integrated
     handles with no protruding elements) for design coherence, and
     confirm the partition's finish can be matched across doors, closets,
     and other furniture in the same space for a unified interior style.
- **Installation advice**: prefer a company offering full service (sales +
  installation + warranty) over buying hardware alone; if installing
  independently, a well-engineered system is designed to assemble simply
  (a "like Lego" framing) — the top rail mounts first, then the sliding
  mechanism/leaf assembly inserts into it.
- **A concise 5-step buyer's decision sequence, restated as this source's
  own summary**: (1) determine the opening's size and desired opening
  behavior (how much of it needs to clear); (2) choose the mounting type;
  (3) determine the primary purpose — maximize light transmission vs.
  prioritize sound isolation — and let that drive the material choice;
  (4) research the specific manufacturer's nuances/reputation; (5) don't
  overlook installation quality — use a company offering a real warranty.
