## New facts

1. **Fridge-type taxonomy, three main types**: freestanding (typically 60cm wide, the most
   popular, fits any interior style, widest color/style range); built-in (integrates into
   kitchen cabinetry, hidden behind a matching furniture facade — chosen for aesthetic/style
   unity; downsides: costs more, involves real compromises [detailed below], and is far more
   demanding of correct installation quality and ventilation); side-by-side (two vertically
   split doors opening outward). **⚠️ Side-by-side sizing "trick" flagged**: a side-by-side
   looks narrower on paper (~90cm) than two separate 60cm units (120cm combined), but
   manufacturers require a 10-15cm clearance gap on *each* side for the doors to open to their
   needed angle — once both clearances are added, total footprint is ≈120cm, identical to two
   standard 60cm units. Side-by-side is mainly chosen by people with large apartments/houses
   with no space-saving pressure, largely for its styling and built-in ice-generator tray;
   two independent single-door fridges are called more practical/efficient in actual use.
2. **Dimension ranges by type**: freestanding — height 83-216cm, width 45-91cm; built-in —
   height 82-212cm, width 54-70cm; side-by-side — height 170-193cm, width 78-122cm
   (before adding the door-swing clearance above).
3. **Freezer placement flexibility**: freezer compartment can be positioned above, below, or
   omitted entirely from a freestanding/built-in unit (all one continuous fridge interior in
   that case), or you can buy a same-family freezer-only column paired with a fridge-only
   column — yielding meaningfully more combined volume than an equivalent side-by-side unit.
4. **⚠️ Cooling-system taxonomy, with a real budget-tier trap flagged**: drip/capillary
   cooling — the simplest, cheapest, most "basic-sounding" system, but in practice the best
   choice specifically for the fridge (not freezer) compartment: keeps interior humidity high,
   which benefits herbs, raw meat/sausage, etc.; downside is uneven cooling top-to-bottom and
   front-to-back (warmer at the door, colder at the back wall). **No Frost** — a fan
   circulating air inside the chamber; not essential for the fridge side but a hard
   requirement for the freezer side (eliminates the need to periodically defrost thick ice
   buildup, and cools more evenly). **Full No Frost / "multi airflow"** (marketing names vary
   by brand for the same concept) — No Frost applied to *both* fridge and freezer
   compartments. **This is a real advantage only in expensive/premium models and a real
   negative in budget models**: a drip-cooled fridge compartment holds ~70% humidity
   (beneficial for herbs/cheese/sausage); a *budget* implementation of Full No Frost pulls
   that humid air out via the fan, dropping humidity below ~30% — harmful for
   humidity-sensitive foods. Expensive models avoid this because their humidity-controlled
   crisper zones are genuinely engineered to re-humidify (see item 5) — **explicit recommendation:
   don't buy an economy-tier model advertised as "Full [No Frost]" or similar; prefer a plain
   drip-cooled fridge compartment paired with a properly-implemented No Frost freezer.**
5. **⚠️ Humidity-controlled crisper-drawer mechanism, extends item 4 with the underlying
   cause**: fridges typically have two drawer types — a "zero-zone" drawer (near-0°C) and a
   "high-humidity" drawer. With plain drip cooling and no air circulation, the lower part of
   the chamber is naturally more humid than the top (basic physics) — real humidity, no active
   mechanism needed. Add a fan for Full No Frost on a **budget** model, and that natural
   humidity gradient is destroyed with no compensating humidification — the drawer is still
   *labeled* "high-humidity" but doesn't function as one. **Expensive** Full No Frost models
   instead actively pump humidified air into these drawers, so the labeled function genuinely
   works as advertised. Confirms: only buy Full No Frost at the premium price point; stick to
   plain drip-cooling + freezer-only No Frost at the budget/mid tier.
6. **⚠️ Compressor economics — inverter payback period specific to Russian electricity
   pricing**: compressor type is called the single most important factor for service life,
   noise level, and time-to-first-repair. Inverter compressors are quieter, start up more
   smoothly, and are more energy-efficient than standard compressors — but the price premium
   for an inverter compressor (from ≈10,000 RUB/≈$130 and up) takes roughly **5-7 years** to
   pay back via electricity savings in Russia's low-electricity-price context (unlike Europe,
   where high per-kWh rates make the same efficiency gain pay back much faster). **Compressor
   quality depends on the compressor manufacturer, not the fridge brand** — two units of the
   same model sitting side-by-side in a store can have different compressor suppliers, and
   there's no way for a buyer to control for this at purchase time.
7. **⚠️ Manufacturer-recommended annual power-cycling rule, an obscure real recommendation**:
   virtually every manufacturer states you should unplug the fridge from power for at least
   ~2 hours once a year — the source states it doesn't fully understand the underlying
   mechanism but confirms this is a universal manufacturer recommendation, not folklore.
   Practical tip: combine this annual power-off window with the fridge's periodic
   cleaning/washing.
8. **⚠️ Energy-efficiency class is explicitly called irrelevant in Russia**: unlike markets
   with very high electricity prices (the source cites $600-$1,000/month electricity bills as
   the kind of context where it matters), Russia's electricity pricing makes chasing an energy
   class rating for a fridge (or household appliances generally) pointless — recommends
   ignoring this spec entirely when shopping in this market.
9. **Interior ergonomics and a stated cultural/design-philosophy contrast**: shelf count,
   height-adjustability, drawer count, telescopic drawer rails, and other interior extras
   affect comfort but not build quality. **Chinese-made models are said to invest
   disproportionately in this area** (more visual polish, lighting, flourishes that create a
   "wow" effect) while **European manufacturers focus primarily on core build quality**,
   sometimes at the expense of these interior comfort extras — the source frames this as a
   genuine design-philosophy difference between manufacturing traditions, not a quality
   hierarchy, and recommends choosing based on personal preference for this dimension.
10. **Controls**: mechanical, electronic/touch, hybrid, or Wi-Fi/app-connected (including a
