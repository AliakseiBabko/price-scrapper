## Durable Facts

- **Wall-flatness check technique**: hold a straightedge vertically against the wall and check for light gaps, keep the level's bubble centered along the full height. Named beginner mistake: drifting the straightedge slightly off-plane as it approaches the ceiling, which produces a bump right at the top — check specifically for this. Repeat horizontally and diagonally per wall; also check passage/doorway reveal ("отбортовка") consistency across a wall, and near windows for light gaps (a gap there is very visible against daylight).
- **90° corner-squareness rule**: corners only need to be verified/forced truly square in wet zones (shower/tub — needed because tile there is 45°-mitered and other angles can't be executed cleanly) and wherever square corner furniture will sit (e.g. a corner kitchen). Everywhere else — e.g. a wall that will just have a TV on it — an 88° or 87° corner is imperceptible in use; forcing it square there wastes excess plaster, labor hours, and up to ~10 cm of usable floor area. Outer corners matter for a different reason: a leaning outer corner makes an adjacent cabinet door visibly stick out past the corner edge. `confirmed`, speaker's stated standing rule (repeats/corroborates the same rule given in the `_cz4hL-fZIY` source from this channel — same-channel repetition, not independent corroboration).
- **Room-dimension check after plastering**: measure and compare to project dimensions; some deviation from spec is expected and acceptable because the plaster layer itself has thickness.
- **Waterproofing fold-up-height rule**: floor waterproofing's vertical overlap up the wall must be at least as tall as the entrance-door threshold height — cited examples from one apartment: 10 cm, 11.5 cm, 10 cm, 12 cm across different rooms, all compliant. The overlap must be continuous with no contour breaks; heat-welded (not just glued) seams verified primarily by visual inspection, with a putty knife used to physically test that seams don't lift.
- **Riser/stack zones must not be sealed over by waterproofing** — deliberate gaps there let a flood from upstairs neighbors drain straight down through the building rather than pooling inside the unit; sealing them defeats this and traps the water inside instead. (Corroborates the `_cz4hL-fZIY` source's identical rule — same channel, not independent.)
- **Heating-loop/radiator rough-in check**: verify supply and return are connected per the radiator's technical spec at each unit, and that the collector has a shutoff valve and an air-bleed valve on both supply and return lines; do a visual leak check, then await the building management company's pressure test.
- **Heating pipe routing standard**: all runs must be securely fixed in place, bends must be smooth (no kinks), and the Energoflex pipe-insulation sleeve must form one continuous, unbroken cover with no bare pipe sections exposed (exposed bare sections are more vulnerable to physical damage).
