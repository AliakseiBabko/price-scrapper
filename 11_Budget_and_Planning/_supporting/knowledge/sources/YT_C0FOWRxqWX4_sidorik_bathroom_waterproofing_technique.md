## Regulations / Permits / Approvals & Materials — Waterproofing Product Comparison

This store's existing `12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Waterproofing_and_Plastering.md`
page covers roll/membrane (rolled bitumen/felt) waterproofing in detail
but has comparatively thin coverage of **brush-on/cementitious**
waterproofing product selection specifics — this source fills that gap
with two **named** products and a direct comparison:

- **Ceresit CR 65 (dry cement-based waterproofing)**: for non-deforming
  substrates only (reinforced-concrete structures, cement plaster/screed
  surfaces); usable inside or outside a building; **cannot be applied to
  gypsum-based substrates**. Cheaper than the elastic mastic alternative
  below. Requires the substrate to be pre-wetted (not soaked, just damp)
  before application — see mistake note below. Tile can be laid **no
  sooner than 7 days** after application.
- **Ceresit CL 51 (ready-mixed elastic waterproofing mastic)**: usable on
  **any substrate type** in wet rooms under tile, interior use only (not
  rated for pools or constant-water-contact areas); stays elastic enough
  to tolerate small substrate cracks without losing its seal. More
  expensive than the dry cement product. Tile can be laid after only
  **16 hours** — the speaker's own preferred product specifically because
  it's faster to apply (ready-mixed, no cement mixing) and unlocks tiling
  far sooner.
- **Elastic waterproofing tape**: must be thin, specifically so it
  doesn't measurably add to the overall waterproofing-layer thickness
  once bonded.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Waterproofing Application

- **Never apply cement-based waterproofing (e.g. CR 65) to an
  unwetted/dry substrate** — pre-plastered walls must be lightly
  sprayed with water (a garden pump sprayer is the speaker's
  recommended tool) and given time to absorb the moisture before
  application; applying to a dry substrate is named as one of the two
  most common mistakes with this product type. The correctly-wetted
  substrate is also visibly easier to apply the material to.
- **Never apply cement-based waterproofing to a gypsum-based substrate**
  — the second of the two named common mistakes; cement waterproofing is
  only rated for cement-based/concrete substrates.
- **Waterproof the floor last, not first** — a genuinely useful sequencing
  rule: waterproofing the floor before the walls forces the installer to
  walk on the fresh floor waterproofing to reach the remaining walls,
  damaging it; walls (and their corner tape/mastic) should be fully done
  first, floor last.
- **A cost-cutting client request that reintroduces real risk**: the
  speaker notes that per the correct technique, waterproofing tape should
  be applied at **every** wall corner (including vertical corners), since
  cracks can form there — but this particular client asked him to skip
  taping the vertical corners to save cost, applying tape only at the
  floor-wall junction. Flagged explicitly as **not fully to spec**: in a
  **panel building specifically, the vertical joints between wall panels
  are the single most vulnerable crack-prone point**, so skipping tape
  there is a real (if client-chosen and cost-driven) compromise, not a
  neutral simplification. Recorded as a real, named tradeoff rather than
  silently treated as acceptable practice.
- **Mix the mastic/mix material thoroughly before use**, even a
  ready-mixed product like CL 51 — improvised mixing tools (the speaker
  uses a scrap length of PP pipe) are acceptable, though a proper mixer
  attachment is preferred.

## Planning Rules — Application Technique and Sequencing

- **Substrate prep sequence before any waterproofing**: scrape all loose
  debris/plaster defects off the wall with an old, disposable putty
  knife (paying particular attention to corners), vacuum the scraped
  debris (including sand that collects in corners), then apply a
  deep-penetrating primer (named: ST 17) by roller — allow **at least
  4 hours** to dry in an enclosed room before waterproofing.
- **Corner-taping technique**: brush mastic into the corner first, apply
  mastic to the tape itself as well (an extra-adhesion step beyond
  instructions, the speaker's own practice, not required by the
  product's own documentation), press the tape in, trim excess with
  scissors, then coat the entire tape (not just its edges, again beyond
  the manufacturer's minimum instruction) with mastic.
- **Two-coat, cross-hatched application for mastic-type waterproofing**:
  apply the first coat in one direction (e.g. horizontal), wait ~1.5
  hours, then apply the second coat perpendicular to the first (e.g.
  vertical) — same cross-hatch logic used for tile adhesive/plaster,
  applied here to waterproofing coats specifically.
- **Scope decision**: a full bathroom waterproofing (every wall,
  floor-to-ceiling) is the speaker's own default, but a cost-saving
  partial-scope alternative exists — waterproof only the floor and the
  walls directly in a fixture's water-contact splash zone, rather than
  every wall in the room.
- **Working around wet application**: to avoid having to clean rollers
  between waterproofing coats, wrap them in plastic film during the
  ~1.5 hour wait between coats rather than washing and re-loading them.
- **Cement-waterproofing product includes a red pigment** specifically
  so the applied layer is visually distinguishable against the grey wall
  substrate, making coverage/gaps easier to verify visually.
