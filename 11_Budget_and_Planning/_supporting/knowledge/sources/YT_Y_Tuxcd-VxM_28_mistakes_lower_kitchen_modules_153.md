## Furniture / Built-ins — Kitchen Module Assembly Checklist

- **Unpack modules by hand, never with a utility knife** — a blade reliably cuts through the cardboard into the panel surface underneath; keep the box afterward, it's reused in later steps (as a floor-protection sheet, below).
- **Inspect every panel for shipping damage on unpacking, especially corners** (most vulnerable in transit) — stop assembly immediately and contact the manufacturer if damage is found; once assembly starts, damage claims are no longer accepted.
- **Never drill your own confirmat pilot holes** — panels always ship pre-drilled with factory-placed hardware holes; if a delivered module lacks them, it's from an unreliable manufacturer and should be returned outright, since matching factory hole placement by hand is not realistically achievable.
- **Assemble and install lower modules fully (leveled and joined together) before starting upper modules** — upper modules, once built, physically obstruct lower-module assembly work.
- **Sort and group all hardware/panels by type before starting assembly** — working from one mixed pile reliably causes mismatched parts; a wrongly-assembled module usually has to be fully disassembled and rebuilt to fix.
- **Set a cordless driver's clutch to a medium torque setting for confirmat screws** — too low leaves parts loosely joined; too high strips the screw thread out of the particleboard (МДФ/ДСП) entirely, at which point the panel itself must be replaced (pre-drilled holes can't be re-drilled accurately by hand).
- **Insert and hand-start every confirmat screw for a joint loosely before fully tightening any one of them** — fully tightening the first screw before the others are in place shifts that panel relative to its neighbor, misaligning the remaining pre-drilled holes; correcting this risks damaging the very first hole, again forcing a panel replacement.
- **Always assemble modules on a cardboard-protected floor** (reuse the unpacking cardboard) — assembling directly on flooring damages both the finish flooring and the module's own corners.
- **Mark a consistent offset from each module's edge before installing front legs** — installing legs "by eye" leaves them out of line, which then prevents the lower plinth/kickboard from fitting; the offset must be identical across every module.
- **Always flip/stand a module upright onto its legs with two people, never alone** — solo handling reliably breaks the legs and can puncture the cardboard/damage the finish flooring underneath.
- **Leave a 50mm margin from the module's edge when installing the metal countertop-fixing brackets** — brackets placed too close to the edge make it very difficult (sometimes impossible) to drive the countertop-fixing screw into them.
- **Always lift a module to its installation spot and set it down — never slide it across the floor**, even for a shift of just a couple centimeters — sliding scratches the floor and can snap the legs; even 1cm of sliding is enough to break a leg.
- **Check drawer-runner (направляющая) hardware orientation before screwing it to a side panel**: the latch/catch mechanism must face the module's front — an error is usually only discovered after the module is already installed, forcing an awkward in-place removal and refit.
- **Use only the screws supplied with drawer-runner hardware, never generic screws** — the correct length is manufacturer-matched; a too-short screw won't hold the runner, a too-long one can punch through the panel (sometimes into the floor beneath).
- **Never swap a module's left/right side panels** — the panels aren't symmetric (runners must sit on the inside), and once assembled this can't be fixed by simply relocating hardware (no pilot holes exist on the wrong face) — the whole module must be disassembled. Always check the manufacturer's L/R marking labels before assembly.
- **Never swap the top and middle connecting rail/stretcher pieces** — the top rail is shaped to stay within the module's footprint (so a countertop can sit flush on it); installed in the middle position, it protrudes and blocks countertop installation. Marking labels distinguish them.
- **Fasten the back panel with a stapler or the manufacturer-supplied nails only, never regular screws** — screws frequently split the side-panel material, forcing a panel replacement and a stall of several weeks (typical custom-panel manufacturing lead time cited).
- **Check a module's squareness before fully fastening the back panel**: fasten only the first two corners, measure both diagonals with a tape to confirm they're equal (a standard squareness check), then fasten the remaining two corners — skipping this risks a permanently racked (non-square) module that can't be installed.
- **Fasten the entire perimeter of the back panel, with nails/staples spaced no more than 100mm apart** — the back panel is what gives the module its structural rigidity and holds its squareness; a sparsely-fastened back panel (real observed bad practice: one nail per corner only) doesn't hold geometry.
- **Insert corner-joint eccentric fasteners (эксцентрик) before final assembly of end/corner modules** — once the mating panels are joined, the fastener slot becomes inaccessible without disassembling and pulling the wooden locating dowels back out.
- **Tall modules (vs. the ~90cm standard height) need legs installed before the carcass is fully assembled**, not after — installing legs on a low module after assembly is a convenient way to avoid kneeling on the floor, but is impractical/risky on a tall module.
- **Never install a back panel on a built-in-refrigerator module** — that module typically ships without one, and a proper refrigerator install requires no back panel at all for correct appliance function/ventilation. A common beginner mistake reuses another module's spare back panel there.
- **Pre-drill a "горизонт" (countertop-fixing rail) panel's screw holes before installing it into the module**, not after — drilling after installation with the countertop already resting on top risks drilling straight through the countertop itself, and any hole drilled through settled sawdust/debris inside an already-assembled module won't hold the fixing screw properly.
- **Clear completed modules out of the active assembly area as they're finished** — a growing pile of finished modules in the working area reliably gets damaged during assembly of the next ones; move each finished module to its final wall position (or at minimum against a clear wall) as soon as it's done, ideally starting with the corner module.
- **Correct kitchen-module assembly/installation order: corner module first, then the remaining standard modules, then the two end modules that bound the run left and right** — assembling standard modules before the corner module and placing them along the walls first can leave no room for the corner module to be fitted in afterward.
- **Level all lower modules to the same height (using a laser level, to the project's specified target height) before tying them together** — un-leveled modules won't present one flat plane for the countertop; in the worst case the countertop-fixing rails end up at different heights, requiring a full teardown to separate, re-level, re-drill, and rejoin the modules.
- **Level modules to the same depth (front-face plane and distance from the wall) before tying them together**, not just the same height — otherwise installed fronts/doors end up in visibly different planes, again requiring a full teardown to fix.
- **Install all drawer/door hardware (slides, hinges, fronts) only after the sink and cooktop cutouts have been cut into the countertop** — cutting the countertop in place showers installed mechanisms with sawdust/debris, jamming them; fixing this means removing, disassembling, cleaning, re-lubricating, and reinstalling every affected mechanism.
