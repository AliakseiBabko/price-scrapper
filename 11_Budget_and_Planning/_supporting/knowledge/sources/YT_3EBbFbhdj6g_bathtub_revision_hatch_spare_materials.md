## Bathroom / Plumbing — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Never make a bathtub siphon revision-access hatch quick-release/magnetic — always tile it in and seal the perimeter with silicone.** A magnetic hatch is not watertight; bathwater that splashes past the tub rim (common when filling/entering a full tub) seeps into the unsealed gap, and — with no airflow/ventilation under the tub — sits and rots, producing a persistent sewage-like odor with no obvious source (mistaken for a dried-out trap). **Second failure mode**: tile adhesive is hygroscopic; water reaching it via an unsealed hatch causes it to swell and detach, eventually dropping tiles. `confirmed`, two concrete cited failure mechanisms, real cautionary framing (cited as a common practice among installers trying to be "helpful").
- **Correct hatch construction**: position the tile precisely (spacers/wedges), then seal the full perimeter with silicone — this both secures the tile and waterproofs the joint in one step. `confirmed`.
- **A leak-protection sensor should be placed specifically at the potential-leak point under the bathtub** (part of the whole-apartment leak-protection system) — if the siphon does eventually leak, the sensor trips and shuts off water before real damage occurs, which is why a permanently-sealed (non-quick-access) hatch is safe: emergency access is needed rarely, and the sensor provides early warning regardless. `confirmed`, ties into this store's existing leak-protection-system facts.
- **Store spare tile inside the same sealed bathtub hatch space, not in a separate storage area (loggia/storage room/garage).** Real-world observation: spare tile stored "elsewhere" consistently doesn't survive until it's actually needed (used for other purposes, lost, discarded). Reasoning for storing it under the tub specifically: tile collections are frequently discontinued, and even same-collection replacement tile from a different production batch will visibly differ in color — keeping the original leftover tile on hand is the only reliable way to get an exact match later. If both dark and light tile were used in the original job, store spares of both. `confirmed`, practitioner's stated repeated real-world experience.
- **Store spare grout and an unopened tube of silicone alongside the spare tile.** Grout for a repair is just as hard to source/match later as tile (especially two-component grout, which is difficult to clean out and redo if mismatched) — and an already-opened silicone tube will have dried out and become unusable by the time it's needed years later, so the spare must be a sealed, never-opened tube. `confirmed`, directly reasoned rule.
- **Retrieving a repair from a sealed silicone-jointed hatch is explicitly framed as easier than the alternative it's protecting against**: cutting the silicone seal with a utility knife and re-sealing afterward is trivial compared to the real damage scenario the spares exist for — a cracked tile requiring jackhammer demolition, adhesive removal, and full retiling. `confirmed`, comparative reasoning given directly by the source.
