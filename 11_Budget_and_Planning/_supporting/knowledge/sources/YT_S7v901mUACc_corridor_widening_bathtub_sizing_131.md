## Planning Rules — Corridor Widening by Reallocating Adjacent-Room Wall Position

- **Core technique**: shift the two walls forming a corridor's boundary, taking width from an oversized WC/bathroom and giving it to the corridor and adjoining living rooms — worked numbers: living spaces gained 300mm, bathrooms lost 120mm, net result a 1200mm-wide corridor (up from a narrower starting point) while gaining 3m² of total living-room floor area across three rooms.
- **Corridor-width perception caution**: a 1200mm corridor can look narrow on a 2D floor-plan drawing, but is stated as a genuinely comfortable width for two people to pass each other — the source explicitly frames sub-1000mm as cramped and 1200mm as generous, not marginal. **Independently corroborates and exceeds this store's existing minimum-corridor-width target** (110cm, `yt_0GEBViPVBbo`) with a real worked project at 120cm.
- **WC/bathroom shrinkage without functional loss, the video's central worked example**: reducing a bathroom's width by 120mm did not require dropping any fixture from the client's full wishlist (toilet, washing machine, sink, shower, bathtub) — achieved by careful footprint reallocation within the smaller envelope, not by cutting scope. Result: a sink counter area large enough for a double-basin vanity, and a full-size bathtub that "lost nothing" from the reduction.
- **Bathtub-length sizing rule — same-practitioner repeat, not a new fact for this store**: **tub length should approximately equal the user's own height, or up to ~10cm less — not longer.** Worked example: the source (178cm tall) demonstrates comfortably fitting in a 180cm tub with feet reaching the end wall; a 190cm tub for the same person would be *worse*, not better — the extra ~10-12cm removes the foot-bracing contact point, risking sliding down/submersion. **This is the identical figure and identical 178cm→180cm/190cm worked example already recorded in this store from a different, later Zemskov video** (`yt_SBzDJk_yp8w`, "#145," 2021-08-22, in `07_Bathroom/analysis/Planning_and_Layout.md`) — this source (#131, 2020-08-30) is roughly a year *earlier*, making it the earliest-dated telling of this rule in this store so far, not independent corroboration. Per this vault's own corroboration rule (same channel ≠ independent source), do **not** treat this as a second confirming source; it's the same practitioner restating his own standard formula. Recorded here only to flag the earlier date, not as new content.
- **Second WC (utility-focused) layout, adjacent to the main bathroom**: houses washing machine, dryer, a small toilet (explicitly there to relieve morning traffic on the main bathroom, not as the primary toilet), a small sink, and a large clear zone in front of the plumbing-riser access panel — sized at 2.2m², explicitly justified as needing no additional width toward the corridor since every fixture already has comfortable access within that footprint.
- **Main bathroom final size**: 4.5m², achieved via the wall-shift technique above.
- **Reusable planning principle, stated as the video's own summary point**: don't treat a bathroom's existing footprint as untouchable just because bathrooms are "supposed" to be generously sized — a corridor or living space starved for width can legitimately take space from an oversized-relative-to-need bathroom, provided the fixture layout within the reduced footprint is planned carefully enough to avoid actually losing function.
