## Root Cause and the Liability Dispute

1. **⚠️ Two-pipe, non-radial ("двухтрубная не лучевая") heating-system
   design flaw**: when one radiator's fitting fails on this system
   topology, the entire apartment's heating must be shut off/de-energized
   to stop the leak — every radiator in the unit, not just the failed
   one. A genuine system-topology liability distinct from any prior
   flood mechanism on this channel.
2. **Failure point**: a multi-connector ("американка"-style) push-fit
   fitting's rubber gasket seal burst at the **lower** connection point —
   the shutoff/pressure-test valve location — not the upper connection
   point where a radiator is disconnected for routine renovation work
   (painting, repositioning).
3. **⚠️ Liability-determination mechanism, an explicit dispute**: the
   management company blamed the contractor, asserting radiators "must
   never be touched" and attributing the failure to the contractor
   having removed the radiator during renovation. The plumbing specialist
   rebuts this directly: only the **upper** connection was disconnected
   (standard practice, done everywhere), while the **lower** connection —
   where the actual failure occurred — was never touched. Whether the
   failure is upstream or downstream of the point a contractor actually
   worked on is the deciding fact in this kind of dispute, not just
   "was the radiator touched at all."
4. **Material-traceability red flag**: the specific pipe/fitting that
   failed carried no visible manufacturer stamp/branding at all
   (contrasted directly with known reputable brands the specialist names,
   Uponor and "TC") — an unbranded/unverifiable developer-installed
   material is flagged as a real risk factor independent of the specific
   failure mode.
5. **Confirmed pattern within the same development, not an isolated
   incident**: the plumbers who came to re-seal the failed connection
   independently confirmed multiple similar leak incidents had already
   occurred elsewhere in this same residential complex the same day —
   a real corroborating base-rate signal for developer-piping-defect risk
   at the scale of one development, not just this one unit.
6. **Practitioner's structured 4-factor root-cause theory** (not a single
   guess): (a) developer materials often technically meet code but are of
   "murky," non-durability-focused quality; (b) developer installation
   crews are paid/managed for throughput, not attention to detail, and
   may physically stress fittings during original construction (stepped
   on, pulled); (c) too many multi-piece connector joints instead of
   continuous whole-pipe runs with a minimum number of connections —
   fewer joints, lower total failure-point count; (d) an unverified/
   unbranded pipe supplier is itself a risk signal versus a known
   reputable brand.

## Preventive Recommendations, Given Directly by the Crew

7. **Maintain access to the entrance-level heating shutoff/riser valves**
   during any renovation work touching the heating system, and never
   leave the system re-pressurized/restarted unattended — specifically,
   don't let heating come back on without the foreman or another
   responsible person present who can shut it off immediately if needed.
8. **Request advance notice from the management company before any
   system restart** (heating, water) following an interruption, so a
   responsible person can be on-site when it happens.
9. **Handle any exposed original developer piping gently during
   renovation work** (don't step on it, don't pull on it) — treat it as
   inherently less reliable than replacement material.
10. **Hire an independent handover/acceptance inspector** (someone who
    checks electrical, plumbing, heating, glazing, etc.) before accepting
    a newly renovated or new-build apartment, rather than relying on a
    visual walkthrough alone.
11. **Two further recommendations, given as a package**: let a new-build
    apartment pass through **at least one full heating season** before
    finalizing renovation work where possible (more confidence the
    developer's systems are sound); create as-built documentation of the
    developer's existing plumbing/heating layout **before** pouring a
    screed that would bury and hide it; and — framed as the single most
    decisive recommendation, budget permitting — **fully replace all
    developer-installed materials/equipment** rather than reusing them.
