## Materials / Cost Drivers — Large-Format Porcelain Tile: the Hidden Delivery Cost

- **Large-format porcelain slab sizes named directly**: 1.2×2.4m or 1.2×3m depending on manufacturer/collection — large enough to tile an entire wall with only two side seams and no visible joint interruption to the printed pattern, the aesthetic reason designers favor it.

- **2021 St. Petersburg material/labor price points (explicitly period-specific, not current)**: large-format tile material **from ≈30,000 RUB/sheet (≈$400/sheet)**; installation labor **from ≈5,000 RUB/m² (≈$70/m²)**, both stated as 2021 St. Petersburg market-entry ("starting from") prices, not averages.
- **⚠️ Design-vs-budget mismatch case, the video's central warning**: a design project quoting a client roughly **8,000-9,000 RUB/m² design fee** for a full 100 m² apartment renovation (full electrical + plumbing replacement, ceiling work, etc.) at a stated total client budget of **≈850,000 RUB (≈$11,300)** is arithmetically incompatible with including large-format porcelain tile in that same budget: 14 sheets alone, at ≈30,000 RUB material each plus the per-floor lift cost, already consumes a large share of that total before any of the rest of the 100 m² gut renovation (electrical, plumbing, ceilings) is paid for. The speaker's explicit point: designers frequently draw visually appealing renders using trend-driven materials (like large-format porcelain) without checking whether the resulting real-world cost is compatible with the client's actual stated budget, leaving the client unable to physically realize their own approved design.
- **General warning, stated as the video's takeaway**: don't assume a design project's specified materials are automatically budget-compatible just because a designer proposed and priced the project — verify hidden/derived costs (delivery/access constraints in particular) independently before committing to a material choice at scale.
