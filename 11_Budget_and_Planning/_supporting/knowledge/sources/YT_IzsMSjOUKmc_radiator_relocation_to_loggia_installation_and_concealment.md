## Balcony / Loggia

- **The core installation is for a radiator relocated onto a joined loggia** — confirms and extends the existing store fact (`yt_aoKerAFdZwg`) that moving a central-heating radiator onto a loggia is illegal in this practitioner's stated regulatory framework. `confirmed`, `single-account`.
- **Explicit statement that this is illegal and enforcement is real**: Zemskov states directly that if an inspection commission ("комиссия") finds the radiator installed/plumbed to heat in the direction of the loggia, they will say the loggia now "belongs to" the heating system and order it removed/redone ("убирайте всё это... и переделываете"). `confirmed` (source's own direct statement, not merely a store-level rule about it).
- **The company's stated workaround to evade inspection, described as standard practice for clients who want a joined+heated loggia without registering it**: the connector piece between the radiator's piping and the through-wall pipes is deliberately installed with its mounting screws facing the room side (not the loggia side), specifically because a technician needs to be able to service/disconnect it from the room side. When an inspection is expected, the client calls the company; the company temporarily swaps in the cheapest available plastic balcony-door/window unit (номинально/nominally, i.e. purely to look present) into the opening and caps the radiator connection with a blank plug instead of the decorative grille, satisfying a surface-level inspection. After the inspectors leave, the company removes the temporary balcony unit and the cap, and reinstalls the decorative grille — described as the company's standard service ("вот именно так мы и делаем для своих заказчиков, которые хотят присоединённые лоджии, но не хотят, чтобы их заставляли что-то переделывать"). `confirmed` (source's own direct, detailed description), `single-account`. **This is presented as routine practice, not a one-off anecdote** — worth treating as a real, current (2023) описание of how this specific company operates for clients who want the joined/heated result without registering it, not just a historical or hypothetical scenario.
- **Supply/return plumbing orientation rule**: the pre-installed connector's supply and return ports are marked (factory-fitted red and blue plugs) and must be matched to the manufacturer's required orientation — supply to the end-most outlet, return to the second-from-end outlet — because reversing them causes a thermostatic radiator valve (TRV) to malfunction (radiator either runs constantly too hot or too cold, TRV effectively non-functional) even though the radiator will still "heat" in a basic sense. Installers additionally tag the two ports with colored tape/stickers as a secondary reminder once the factory plugs are removed, since the two bare pipe ends look identical afterward. `confirmed` mechanism, technique detail.
- **Bracket/mounting technique ("туфли" — literally "shoes," the floor-standing bracket type used here for aluminum/bimetal radiators)**: positioned and cut to length *in situ*, never pre-calculated and marked before physical test-fitting — Zemskov states this explicitly as the one correct method, contrasting it with a "never do this" approach of mathematically calculating bracket position from wall measurements alone (routinely off by a small amount, requiring rework). Brackets attach to the radiator's second-from-edge section on each side, not the first or third. `confirmed` technique/heuristic.
- **Radiator clearance requirement for a convector-style radiator**: 150–200mm total clearance above and below combined is needed for the unit to convect properly; this directly constrains niche/countertop height in a combined loggia-office or loggia-kitchen build (in this job, the client wanted a desk-height countertop at 75cm, which forced choosing a radiator with 350mm center-to-center spacing rather than a taller unit). `confirmed`, dimensioned.
- **Never fasten radiator brackets directly into aerated concrete (газобетон) even for what looks like a one-off/temporary fixing** — screws pull out over time from normal radiator servicing (removal for painting, accidental knocks); always pre-drill and use the specific anchors supplied with the bracket. `confirmed` rule.
- **A "decorative cap" (декоративная чашка) is a frequently-forgotten finishing element**: the rough bracket/fastener assembly is meant to be plastered over as part of the wall finish, then the radiator is temporarily removed a second time during the finish stage specifically to fit these caps over the now-flush fasteners before final reassembly — skipping this step is common among less experienced installers and leaves visible screw heads. `confirmed` technique detail.
- **Pipe-cutting tool rule**: piping must be cut with a dedicated pipe cutter, never an angle grinder ("болгарка") — a grinder-cut pipe end develops a burr/lip that is not acceptable for a compression/press-fit plumbing connection and will leak; a proper pipe-cutter tool also chamfers the outer edge and requires a separate manual deburring tool for the inner edge (both edges must be checked by touch — "nothing should catch, inside or outside"). Zemskov states this company has a dedicated quality-control department that can fine an installer's full pay for the job if the internal chamfer step was skipped. `confirmed`, specific and checkable.
- **Assembly sequencing rule for cutting connecting pipe lengths**: cut with margin first, test-fit against the actual radiator position, then trim the final small amount — rather than trying to calculate and cut to final length in one pass — because pipe can be shortened easily but never lengthened back. `confirmed` technique.
- **Foam-fixing caution repeated from elsewhere in this store's Balcony/Loggia section (consistent, not new)**: apply foam sparingly around fixing points — excess foam is rigid enough to deform properly-positioned pipes/fittings as it cures. `confirmed`, consistent with existing store guidance on foam use elsewhere in this project's notes.
- **Acceptance/QC criteria stated for this specific work** (useful as a checklist item if this project ever needed radiator-relocation QC, though not applicable given this project's own no-relocation plan — see Relevance below): structure must hold to design dimensions and be strong enough to sit or lean on; radiator must sit exactly centered on the niche's axis with equal clearance above/below (accounting for finish-layer thickness); supply/return orientation must match manufacturer spec for that radiator model.
