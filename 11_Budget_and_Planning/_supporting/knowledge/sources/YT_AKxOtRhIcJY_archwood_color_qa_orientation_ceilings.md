## Design Concept — Choosing and Distributing Colour

- **⚠️ The ready-made-palette shortcut, stated as a deliberate recommendation against her own profession's instinct** ("сейчас дизайнеры будут, конечно, меня ругать"): if you need to pick colors **once**, for your own current renovation, take a ready-made palette off Pinterest or a palette service rather than learning colouristics. Corroborated verbatim by the same presenter in `_Vw-eaRekDk` three years later. Named mechanism: services exist that ingest **any** interior photo, painting or image and extract its colors, which can then be converted to real tinting codes (**RAL** named) — "не один клик, но задача простая."
- **⚠️ Structural observation about ready-made palettes** — a genuinely checkable claim, not just advice: **almost all of them are built on opposing wheel positions** (forming a square or a triangle), typically pairing a nominally cool hue (blue, green, or derivatives) against an opposing warm/dark family, **plus intermediate tones diluted with white and one neutral (white, gray or beige)**. This connects the ready-made-palette shortcut back to the formal schemes without requiring the user to know them.
- **⚠️ The same palette produces opposite interiors — the distribution is the real decision**: worked with a blue+terracotta palette. For a **calm, light** room, the neutral intermediates (white, gray, light beige) take **60–70% of the wall and floor volume** and the saturated members appear only as accents. For a **bold, maximally contrasting** room, the saturated opposites become the base (terracotta object against a dark wall) and the neutral is **minimised to the floor alone**. Both stay harmonious because neither leaves the palette. Independently corroborates the same "palette selection is the easy half, distribution is the hard half" framing from `_Vw-eaRekDk`.
- **⚠️ Выкрасы are mandatory even when you already have the color code**: "даже если вы знаете номер цвета, обязательно нужно делать выкрасы" — small paint test patches on the wall, **compared against the reference only after drying**. Stated reason from her own repeat experience across sites: even a color already proven on other objects can distort, from something as ordinary as trees or a brightly-coloured neighbouring building outside the window.

## Design Concept — Wood as a Palette Member

- **⚠️ A named, explicitly non-scientific but workable technique**: mentally (or in Photoshop) **strip the texture from the wood** — the rings, the fibres, the grain that make you recognise it as wood — and treat the flat residual tone as an ordinary color. Then either (a) look at your chosen palette, decide which member the wood *would* be (usually the light or mid beige), and substitute a wood of that tone; or (b) take the tone of wood you already own, lay it against the palette, and if it clearly doesn't belong, change the palette. To fit a darker species (тёмный дуб, орех), add a brown shade on the warm side and widen the palette accordingly.

## Design Concept — Perceived Space and Ceilings

- **⚠️ Warm advances, cool recedes, with the mechanism stated**: warm colors (yellow, orange, warm beige, red) slightly **reduce** perceived space; cool ones (blue, cool green) slightly **increase** it, because an intense warm color reads as nearer to the eye and a cool one as further. She is explicit that the difference is a real but small illusion — "разница будет не такой заметной, но эффект есть" — and **not worth tolerating cool shades you dislike for**. Practical form: prefer neutral (white, gray) or cool (серо-голубой) in small rooms, then add **warm contrasting details** so the room doesn't read cold overall while the walls still visually step back.
- **⚠️ Dark / coloured ceilings — a full rule set:**
  - A ceiling darker or brighter than the walls **lowers** the perceived height, **and the effect scales with the ceiling-to-wall contrast**.
  - A *warm* dark ceiling lowers it more than a cool one (same advance/recede mechanism).
  - **The case where this is useful**: ceilings that are genuinely too high — **above ~3.2–3.5 m** — where the height itself has become a defect and the room reads uninviting.
  - **Execution rule when doing it**: paint **the ceiling plus the upper band of the wall** (or, in a classical scheme, the ceiling plus the cornice) rather than the ceiling alone — the transition then reads softer, whereas a bare contrast breaking exactly on the wall/ceiling angle "часто смотрится грубо."
  - **Standard-height ceilings, no intent to lower**: paint walls and ceiling **the same color** — you enter "такую коробочку" where only the floor stands out, and the floor should be lighter than the chosen color; the ceiling then reads at roughly its true height.
  - **Low ceilings, ~2.5 m or below**: keep light, or use one light color for walls and ceiling together (white with a hint of tint), or keep the ceiling lighter than the walls — in which case there is no visual distortion even if it isn't white.
  - The ceiling is "тоже участник общей выбранной палитры," and need not be delivered with color at all — concrete, wood or a combination qualifies. Own project cited: ceiling partly clad in parquet board with white fragments.

## Design Concept — Wall Colour by Window Orientation

Stated with the assumption that nothing outside the window is reflecting into the room:

- **North** — receives "нечто вроде серого фильтра"; any color including white reads greyer or dirtier. **Choose a shade slightly warmer than the one you actually want**; for white walls use a warm or milky white; замените серый на серо-бежевый or terracotta. Either a warm or a cool gamut is workable — it's a taste question. Notably, **north rooms distort colors the least overall**.
- **East** — morning light gives a pink cast, midday a slight green one; **the largest distortion of any orientation, because the light changes so much across the day**. Blues and greens distort least. **The riskiest choices are complex gray-beiges**, which can shift substantially. If that worries you, use clearer, simpler colors — white, gray, серо-голубой.
- **West** — evening sun gives an orange cast; warm shades (beige, terracotta) distort least; cool colors and cool grays shift somewhat.
- **South** — complex deep shades, mostly cool ones, work well. When picking a complex neutral, **choose it slightly cooler than the one you want**, because a warm yellow filter will lay over it; a cool gray will read warmer than its sample.

## Design Concept — "Complex" Colours and Budget Rescue

- **⚠️ Two conditions for rescuing a budget interior with colour**: (1) the colors must be harmonious with each other (back to the ready-made palette); (2) **use "сложные" colors** — and this is the substantive half.
- **⚠️ Definition of a complex colour, with a food analogy used as the mechanism**: a dish reads as sophisticated when it engages several receptors at once — sweet *and* salty *and* sour *and* slightly bitter, in some proportion; even an apple is simultaneously sweet, sour and faintly bitter. Only sweet reads cloying; too weak a signal reads bland. **By analogy, a complex color contains at minimum two, often three, of the primaries (red, blue, yellow) in differing proportions, diluted with grey or white**, while still reading obviously as "a blue" or "a grey-beige."
- **⚠️ Why you cannot make one yourself — the actionable part**: "такие оттенки не получаются путём равномерного смешивания простых цветов — получится просто грязь." Producing them is skilled work, which is why **paint and tinting manufacturers maintain dedicated collections of complex shades**, worked on by whole teams. Named families: cool dusty shades, greys with sea-green in them, mint, wine/marsala shading into grey. On such backgrounds even gold elements read elegant and plausible. **Practical instruction: choose a ready-made complex base color from a manufacturer's own collection rather than tinting to a code out of a simplified catalogue.**

## Design Concept — Curtains and Composition

- **⚠️ Ask the composition question before the colour question**: curtains (specifically full-length portières) are a **vertical element comparable to a column**, so the first question is not "what colour" but "**do I need a contrasting vertical element on this side of the room at all?**" — for instance to balance a massive element or dominant on the opposite side. Only if the answer is yes does contrast follow, and **contrast can be lighter than the wall as well as darker**; against a darker wall, light curtains are the contrasting element.
- **Curtains join the already-chosen palette**; if the palette lacks a suitable member, extend it with a neighbouring darker or lighter shade rather than importing an unrelated color.
- **⚠️ Two composition self-check techniques, applicable to any element (kitchen, curtains, sofa)**: (1) mentally **simplify the whole interior to plain rectangles and squares**, then look at whether everything sits where it should and try moving elements; (2) **convert the view to black-and-white**, because visual weight and the sense of colour read differently without hue. She notes composition is what most of an architectural education is actually spent on, and that it is the correct starting point.
