## Design Concept / Furniture-Built-ins — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Wood slat/batten partitions**: visually divide a space while letting
  sunlight pass through — genuinely no full acoustic or visual privacy.
  Technical mounting note: requires a **ceiling mounting point
  ("закладная")** especially with a stretch ceiling (the floor side needs
  no special prep). Sold either as individually-fixed slats or slats
  pre-joined by a connecting timber piece. **Cost-saving mistake flagged
  explicitly**: cheap dimensional lumber battens (e.g. from a general
  hardware retailer like Leroy Merlin) are not dimensionally stable and
  will likely warp/twist over time — acceptable for pure wall decor, but
  for a genuine structural partition, **custom-ordered MDF slats** are
  recommended instead: pricier, but arrive pre-finished/painted and are
  far stronger/more reliable long-term.
- **Curtain zoning**: cheapest/fastest option; blackout fabric gives full
  light-blocking; flexible (open/close at will), fully separable or fully
  open; works well paired with a corner or U-shaped sofa/bed layout.
  **Downside**: multiple curtains sharing a wall with window curtains can
  read as "a pile of fabric" — mitigation: pair with a Roman blind on the
  window itself to visually lighten the load. Curtains are dust magnets
  and can't be simply wiped clean — require periodic removal and
  washing. **Mounting technique for a cleaner look**: a plain curtain
  track/rod looks unfinished on its own — either drop the ceiling on both
  sides to form a recessed niche for the track, or (if a ceiling
  perimeter cornice already exists) build two short drywall stub walls
  at the cornice's height and mount the track between them, so the
  curtain glides through the middle of that niche.
- **Japanese/panel curtains**: fabric panels with a small weight sewn
  into the bottom hem to keep them straight; collapse down to the width
  of a single panel when opened. Suits minimalist or Japanese-influenced
  ("japandi") interiors specifically.
- **Shelving-unit (стеллаж) as a divider**: popular, affordable, and
  mobile — can be disassembled quickly if the zoning need goes away;
  doubles as storage for books/décor. **Real downside stated
  explicitly**: without genuine household discipline about keeping
  displayed items organized/color-coordinated, an open shelving divider
  becomes "a vertical junk pile" rather than a clean design element.
  **Mitigation**: use a mix of open and closed cabinet sections rather
  than a fully open shelf — still lets light through, but reduces visible
  clutter and cleaning burden. Off-the-shelf (e.g. IKEA) is the easy
  route; a custom-ordered unit can run full floor-to-ceiling with
  integrated lighting.
- **Sliding partitions (glass or plastic)**: glass looks better but costs
  more and is heavier; glass can be clear, frosted/matte, tinted/colored,
  combined with metal inserts, ribbed/corrugated panels, or a fully
  opaque section — flexible visual effect by material choice. **Real
  project price data point (this company's own "Жаклин" project, no city
  stated, ~2020, RUB currency inferred from context, no exact date)**: a
  glass sliding partition separating a bedroom from a kitchen-living zone
  cost **~100,000 RUB**. Recommend pairing a sliding partition with
  curtains for a fully-closed option layered on top of the glass. **Key
  mounting caution, stated explicitly and generalizable to any sliding
  partition/wardrobe-style door system**: a partition mounted on
  **both** a top and bottom track (like a standard wardrobe/closet door)
  is **not recommended** — the bottom track creates a threshold people
  trip over and visually cheapens the interior. **Prefer a top-track-only
  mounting system** instead — but this is a heavier mechanism requiring a
  genuinely solid structural mounting point in the ceiling; if the
  ceiling will be dropped, **confirm this requirement with the partition
  manufacturer in advance** so the ceiling crew installs the correct
  backing/blocking during rough construction, not after.
- **"Rope zoning"**: decorative panels made of wood slats with taut
  ropes/cords strung between them — pattern density and visual openness
  can be varied by adjusting the spacing between cords. Suits modern
  interiors with wood elements, or Eastern-motif styles. **Can be
  genuinely low-budget and DIY-buildable** — jute cord plus a few wood
  strips.
- **Decorative milled/routed MDF partition panels**: any shape, pattern,
  texture, or color; usually custom-ordered, sometimes combined with a
  shelving unit to reinforce the structure. Also requires a ceiling
  mounting point ("закладная").
- **Screen/room-divider ("ширма")**: a standalone furniture piece,
  maximally mobile — can be placed or removed from any spot quickly;
  available in fabric, wood, or metal. **Downside stated candidly**: some
  people find living with one uncomfortably flimsy/unstable — choose a
  different method if a more solid-feeling separation is wanted.
- **A permanent wall**: the most obvious option but "intimidatingly
  final" given its fixed nature — if the household is confident the
  zoning need is permanent, plan a partial partition wall at the
  rough-construction stage. Can be combined with curtains or a glass
  partition; specifically, adding short stub walls on both sides of a
  glass partition **reduces how much glass partition width is actually
  needed, lowering its cost**.
- **Furniture-as-divider**: placing a sofa mid-room marks the start of a
  new zone (reinforceable with a wall-color change or lighting). A
  wardrobe/cabinet used as a divider adds closed storage the same way a
  shelving unit does, but its unattractive rear panel is a real practical
  downside — **mitigation**: combine it with slats or the rope technique
  behind the wardrobe so it visually "rests" against a finished-looking
  backdrop rather than showing a bare cabinet back. **Build a
  divider-wardrobe to full ceiling height rather than partial height** —
  since it's freestanding (not against a wall), a low unit looks
  incomplete/awkward; this usually requires a custom order rather than
  an off-the-shelf unit.
