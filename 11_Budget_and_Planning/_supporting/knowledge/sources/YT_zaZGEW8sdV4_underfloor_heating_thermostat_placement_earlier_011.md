## Electrical / Switches — Underfloor-Heating Thermostat Placement

- **Thermostat placement, restated and corroborated**: mount outside the
  wet room (visibility of on/off state without entering), on the same
  vertical axis as the light switch below it (15cm from the rough
  opening edge), at **165cm height exactly** in this practitioner's own
  projects (a specific figure sitting inside this store's existing
  160-170cm range from the later source — corroboration, not new
  information on its own).
- **A genuine cross-source reasoning tension worth flagging explicitly**:
  this source states child-safety **is** a real reason for eye-level
  thermostat mounting — its stated mechanism: children lose interest in
  tampering with a simple light switch quickly, but stay engaged with an
  "electronic gadget"-like thermostat display for much longer (the
  source's own estimate: roughly ages 2 to 7). This store's existing
  entry from the same channel's later (2022) video (`yt_uwXBHuWPUIo`)
  **explicitly states the opposite** — that LCD legibility, not
  child-safety, is the real justification for eye-level height, and
  argues child-safety is *not* the real reason. **Both are the same
  practitioner, four years apart** — recorded as an unresolved,
  genuine change/inconsistency in stated reasoning over time, not
  silently reconciled in favor of either account.
- **Long-sensor-wire purchase warning, a genuinely new practical detail
  not in the existing entry**: the floor-temperature sensor's factory
  lead wire is typically short, sized for a waist/baseboard-level
  thermostat mount — **if planning an eye-level mount, buy a thermostat/
  sensor kit with a long sensor wire from the start**, rather than
  discovering the wire doesn't reach once installation is underway. This
  store's existing entry instead recommends *soldering a wire extension*
  at rough-in — a different (and, on its face, more robust/universal)
  solution to the same underlying short-wire problem; both are recorded
  as alternative techniques for the same issue, not treated as
  contradictory.
- **Multi-gang switch/thermostat mapping rule, worked example**: for a
  3-gang switch plate controlling two rooms' lighting plus an overhead
  light, and two adjacent underfloor-heating thermostats for those same
  two rooms, position each thermostat directly above its corresponding
  room's switch (not stacked arbitrarily) — so the spatial mapping
  between switch and thermostat is immediately intuitive rather than
  requiring memorization of which controls which. Genuinely new specific
  multi-gang layout rule.
