## Durable Facts / Numeric Data (Level 1, `single-account`/`unverified` throughout)

- **Staged debris-volume rule of thumb, cited for an ~85-100 m² apartment**: stripping old wallpaper and removing old doors alone already fills a full light-truck ("Gazelle"-class) load; disassembling old built-in furniture and removing old flooring adds roughly another 1-2 heavy-truck ("KAMAZ"-class) loads; demolishing old stud/timber-and-plaster interior partitions, removing an old prefab plumbing cabin (santekhkabina), and removing existing flooring as part of a thorough renovation adds further volume on top of that.
- **Bag capacity limit**: roughly 25 kg is the practical maximum per debris bag before it tears — used as the basis for the bag-count estimate below.
- **Rule of thumb: a typical 100 m² apartment demolition alone produces on the order of 300+ debris bags**, not counting bulkier debris (large offcuts, old cabinetry) simply stacked along walls rather than bagged.
- **During rough-in/demo work, debris is allowed to accumulate on-site to the point of being difficult to walk through** — described as an acceptable, tolerated condition during the demolition/rough phase specifically, but explicitly **not** acceptable once finishing (chistovaya) work begins, at which point removal must become more frequent and in smaller batches to keep the site workable and the finish work clean.
- **A comparable additional volume of debris accumulates during the renovation itself** (material offcuts, packaging) on top of the initial demolition volume — combined, the speaker estimates roughly 2-3 more heavy-truck loads across the remainder of the project.
- **Overall rule of thumb for a 100 m² apartment: approximately 18-20 separate debris removal trips across the full renovation** — stated as a real, verified figure from the crew's own experience, offered specifically because customers are typically surprised by it and assume far fewer trips are needed.
