## Durable Facts

- **Multi-floor-covering-at-one-level planning must happen at project stage, not during construction/procurement** — quartz-vinyl (hallway/kitchen/living room), engineered board (two other rooms), and porcelain tile (two bathrooms) all have different thicknesses; a technologist must calculate and document the required screed-height offsets in the project before work starts, or the team discovers mid-renovation that engineered board is thicker than quartz-vinyl and has to redo screed work. Same theme as `Uhqtnv3lsuo` and `wQwuSsTtM1k` — recurring across this playlist, no dedicated page currently owns it.
- **Underfloor heating mats top out at ~12 m² per mat** (stated as true "for any manufacturer") — larger rooms need either a center-only or perimeter-only mat, or two separate mat loops with separate thermostats. To avoid forcing occupants to walk between multiple thermostats, the speaker's team installs one aggregating app/controller that lets multiple room thermostats be operated as a group from one interface. Concrete example: living room = two 12.6 m² mats, kitchen = two 12–14 m² mats, each room with two thermostats.
- **Wall-prep standard should be a named, verifiable spec in the contract (Q1–Q4 grading scale)** — business-class work typically specifies Q3 or Q4 (the two tiers suited to paint or decorative plaster finishes), and this should be independently verified by a third-party technical inspector before the paint/plaster stage, since an unaided customer generally can't assess wall flatness themselves. Speaker frames the inspector's fee as small insurance against a full redo.
- **In-apartment sauna wiring/venting has specialist-only requirements, distinct from standard electrical work**: (1) the heater's power feed cable must be fire-resistant and rated to the specific heater's declared load — sizing must come from the sauna contractor, not assumed by the general electrician (anecdote: a crew that sized a cable only for the general contractor's own assumption of load, without checking with the sauna installer, would have had to replace an expensive cable); (2) run two spare empty conduits ("гофры") for the sauna's control-panel cabling, per the sauna contractor's own spec; (3) sauna extraction hood must be all-metal — non-metal ducting can't survive sauna heat loads.
- **Shower curb height should account for handheld-shower/bucket use**: where a household member dumps water over themselves rather than only using an overhead fixed shower, plan for the resulting splash volume (worked example: ~20 L) with a correspondingly higher curb/threshold (~6–7 cm in this case) rather than a standard low curb, to contain the water inside the shower area.
- **Wall soundproofing in living room + bedroom framed as effectively mandatory for business-class**, both to protect the occupant from neighbor noise and — the speaker's specific anecdote — to protect neighbors from the occupant's own noise (a client who initially refused soundproofing, claiming he slept fine regardless of noise, later added it post-renovation specifically because his neighbor, described as a prosecutor's aide, complained). Framed as a stronger, business-class-specific version of soundproofing guidance already seen in prior sources; type used here described as frame-based ("каркасная") soundproofing.
