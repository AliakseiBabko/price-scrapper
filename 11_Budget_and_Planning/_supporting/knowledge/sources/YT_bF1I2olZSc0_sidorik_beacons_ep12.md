## Walls / Ceilings — Beacon (Lighthouse) Installation Technique

- **Laser-level beacon-setting method, full sequence**: use a 360°-beam
  laser + a long straightedge (правило) cut to the room's ceiling height
  (speaker keeps a 3m straightedge stock and trims per-room). (1) **Find
  the wall's true plane** by moving the laser toward the wall until the
  beam just starts to disappear/clip — this identifies the flattest
  achievable plaster plane while revealing every bump/dip on the existing
  wall. (2) **Target minimum plaster thickness ≈1.5cm including the
  beacon itself.** (3) **Offset-marking formula**: mark 11cm out from the
  laser-plane line at each end of a wall — 10cm is the straightedge's own
  thickness, +1cm allowance for the beacon-plus-plaster layer — then
  re-position the laser onto these new marks. (4) Apply plaster dabs
  (same material as the plaster coat) at intervals along the beacon line,
  sized to the intended layer thickness to avoid wasted trim-off later;
  press the beacon in, align to the laser (half the beam visible on the
  beacon, half showing through to the wall behind).
- **Install end beacons first, then check middle beacons against a
  straightedge spanning both ends** (not against the laser individually) —
  the laser itself carries real margin of error, so cross-checking against
  a physical straightedge between two already-set beacons catches drift a
  laser alone would miss. A plain bubble level is used as an additional
  cross-check.
- **Working-condition tip**: install beacons with room lights off and
  windows covered with cardboard when not filming — makes the laser beam
  (especially a red beam, harder to see than a green one) much easier to
  read precisely.
- **Straightedge-tilt self-check via the laser beam**: holding the
  straightedge perpendicular to the wall keeps the beam visible/centered
  on it; tilting it off-perpendicular makes the beam visibly disappear
  from the straightedge — a built-in visual check that doesn't require a
  separate square, as long as the installer is actively watching for it.
- **No-laser fallback**: stretch a string line between the two
  already-set end beacons and align middle beacons to the string instead.
- **Beacon spacing (strip width) guidance**: up to ~1.2m spacing
  recommended for a beginner; an experienced installer can go up to ~1.5x
  wider, but a wider gap risks a trapezoidal-profile screed straightedge
  flexing/bowing between beacons and scooping out too much plaster.
- **90°-corner-alignment technique using the same laser**: use a carpenter's
  square to mark a matching offset distance (e.g. 10cm, arbitrary) on both
  walls meeting at the corner; place the laser at those two marks so one of
  its planes extends off the *longer* wall while the other plane sits
  flush against the perpendicular wall — this projects the long wall's own
  plane onto the corner, immediately revealing (via a measured plaster-
  thickness difference, e.g. observed 2.5cm vs. 6mm sides in this episode)
  whether the second wall deviates from a true 90° angle, which the beacon
  placement then corrects for. **The longer of the two walls is used as
  the reference plane by default** (needs less added plaster/material to
  build out to a flat plane), though either wall could technically serve
  as the base if preferred. Named locations in this project needing a
  verified 90° corner: everywhere a built-in wardrobe/cabinet will stand,
  plus bathroom walls, and this project's own kitchen-corridor corner.
- **Tie-in technique where new plaster meets retained developer plaster**:
  where an existing flat developer-plastered wall section is kept as-is,
  one beacon is set resting on a straightedge spanning from that existing
  flat plane, so the new plaster ties in flush with the retained old
  plaster rather than creating a step.
- **Re-patch beacons after final trim to prevent sagging under straightedge
  pressure**, ideally once the beacon-setting plaster itself has fully
  dried/hardened.
