## Case 1 — Pentagonal (5-Sided) Bedroom, St. Petersburg

- **⚠️ Flooring-direction distraction technique for an irregular room**:
  lay new flooring in the standard/classical direction relative to the
  window (not following the room's odd wall angle), and position the
  main furniture (bed, wardrobe) as if the room were regular-shaped —
  this deliberately draws visual attention to the "regular" portion of
  the room and away from the extra angled corners, rather than trying to
  visually resolve the irregular geometry directly.
- **Floor-thickness-matching rule for a partial flooring replacement**:
  before buying new flooring material for a room-only replacement (not
  whole-apartment), demolish/lift a small section of the existing floor
  elsewhere (e.g. the hallway) to check its thickness, and choose the
  new material's thickness to match — avoids a height differential
  (a trip hazard / visible step) at the room-to-hallway transition.
- **Faux suede reupholstery as a scratch-resistant (not scratch-proof)
  alternative to velour** for a pet-owning household's existing bed —
  explicitly caveated that no fabric is fully damage-proof against a
  cat's claws, just more resistant than velour.
- **Dark-accent-anchoring coherence rule**: when a fixed dark element in
  a room can't be changed (here, existing dark doors), add a small
  amount of dark color elsewhere in the room (here, dark nightstands) to
  visually "justify" that dark note rather than leaving it isolated —
  a small-amount-is-enough version of intentional color echo.
- **Low-pile/flat-weave rug for a pet-owning household** — easier to
  keep pet hair from becoming visible/embedded compared to a plush pile.
- **⚠️ TV-height-for-reclined-viewing rule**: when a TV will primarily be
  watched while reclining/lying on the bed (not sitting upright), mount
  it higher via a taller console/dresser rather than a standard low TV
  cabinet — a comfortable reclined viewing angle needs a higher screen
  position than seated viewing does.
- **Two full parallel design variants (neutral vs. saturated palette)
  prepared side by side for a genuinely undecided client** — the same
  "prepare two real options rather than force an early choice"
  methodology already recorded for a layout decision (ARCHWOOD,
  `gNN7G7vNyqY`), applied here to a color/palette decision instead.
- **Structured/quilted bedspread for easy bed-making**: a stiff,
  shape-holding quilted bedspread that can simply be thrown over the bed
  and still look tidy (vs. a soft, easily-wrinkled draping fabric) is
  recommended whenever the household doesn't want to carefully smooth
  fabric daily — corroborated independently in Case 2 below for a
  different household member's habits.
