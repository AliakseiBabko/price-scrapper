## Walls / Ceilings — Genuinely New Plastering Content Not in Prior Sources

1. **Minimum temperature for applying plaster: above +5°C** — a specific
   numeric threshold not previously captured from this channel's other
   plastering sources. Framed as a "banal but expensive" mistake some
   plasterers still make in unheated buildings.
2. **Vapor-barrier-to-wall junction technique — first vapor-barrier/
   roofing-adjacent content in this store**: where a roof's vapor barrier
   membrane ("пароизоляция") extends down onto a wall, plastering
   directly over the membrane fails because the membrane surface is too
   smooth for plaster to adhere to. **Mechanism stated**: the vapor
   barrier's main job is blocking warm, humid indoor air from reaching
   the attic in winter (otherwise the roof ices over/forms icicles); at
   any gap in the membrane-to-wall junction, that same escaping air
   condenses. **Fix**: trim the membrane back ~2cm from the wall, and
   bridge the gap with a purpose-made two-sided tape — one face rough
   enough for plaster to key into, the other face highly adhesive to
   bond to the wall/membrane. **Attribution note**: the source frames
   this junction as the roofer's responsibility to get right, not the
   plasterer's, but says a plasterer/owner should still verify it before
   work proceeds.
3. **Ventilation protocol around plastering work**: no drafts are
   permitted *during* plastering (windows must be installed, even if
   covered/protected from dust/splatter during the work), but the room
   must be well-ventilated *after* plastering — either by cracking
   windows or using industrial moisture-absorber units — otherwise mold
   and fungus are described as inevitable.
4. **Localized forced-drying with an electric heat gun is a real
   documented failure mode, not just a theoretical caution** — a real
   case from the speaker's own experience: under deadline pressure, a
   corner that wasn't drying was force-dried with an electric heater
   ("пушка"); this dried only the surface of an unusually thick plaster
   layer, trapping moisture inside. Wallpaper was hung over it, and mold
   appeared roughly 6-7 weeks later, requiring a full redo at the
   client's insistence. **Rule distilled**: never use localized electric
   heaters to force-dry a thick plaster layer.
5. **Real wall-geometry defect example with a specific measurement
   discrepancy**: in a closet/wardrobe room, a laser line sat flush with
   the beacon thickness (~6mm) at the top of a wall but showed a 6cm gap
   at the bottom of the same corner — evidence of a genuine
   out-of-square wall inherited from the original wall-construction
   stage (not the plastering stage), requiring a thick corrective
   plaster layer to true it up. **Rule distilled**: correcting this kind
   of geometry defect avoids downstream problems with flooring
   (avoiding an ugly tapered/wedge-shaped floor line) and furniture
   installation (cabinetmakers otherwise have to custom-trim furniture to
   fit a crooked wall) — the source states correct 90° geometry should be
   treated as essential in bathrooms, hallways, and kitchens specifically
   (fixture/countertop/cabinet fit), and ideally throughout the whole
   apartment.
