## Wallpaper & Paint — Acceptance/QC Checklist (all new for this store)

1. **Wallpaper seam and corner requirements**: seams must be butt joints
   (no overlap, no gaps); internal and external corners must be sharp,
   not rounded, ideally reinforced with paper tape or a metal corner
   bead. **Named bad practice**: applying wallpaper as one continuous
   sheet across an internal corner instead of cutting and taping it —
   traps air bubbles behind the paper, which leads to peeling later.
2. **Pattern-alignment QC, with a source-of-blame distinction**: pattern
   drift at seams isn't always the installer's fault — a manufacturing
   defect in the wallpaper roll itself is a real possibility. **QC
   technique**: dry-lay rolls flat on the floor before hanging to spot a
   factory pattern-alignment defect ahead of time. A skilled installer
   can sometimes still correct minor pattern misalignment while the
   paste-wetted sheet is still pliable enough to be nudged into place.
3. **Full visual wallpaper-defect checklist**: no bubbles, stains, gaps
   in glue coverage, peeling, skew, or wrinkles ("морщины") anywhere —
   the whole surface should read flat under inspection.
4. **Debris-under-wallpaper defect mechanism**: unremoved dust/debris
   under wallpaper becomes visible, especially under thin paper
   wallpaper and specifically once wall sconces are switched on
   (raking light reveals the bump) — root cause is inadequate wall prep
   (dedusting + priming) before hanging, not a wallpapering-technique
   failure itself.
5. **Adhesive-residue and windowsill-scratch acceptance checks**: some
   glue residue is acceptable mid-work but must be fully cleaned off by
   handover; windowsills must show no utility-knife scratches from
   wallpaper trimming.
6. **⚠️ Inspection/"reveal" lamp technique — the video's own headline
   point, a genuinely valuable acceptance technique with cost
   implications**: use a special raking/grazing-angle inspection lamp
   (not an ordinary work floodlight) to reveal painted-wall surface
   defects invisible under normal lighting — demonstrated with a direct
   photo comparison of the identical wall under each light type.
   **Practical negotiating tip**: ask a prospective painter what
   inspection tools/method they use before hiring — a painter who works
   to an inspection-lamp standard signals meticulous work, and this
   justifies a correspondingly higher price than a painter who doesn't.
7. **Painted-wall visual-defect checklist**: no stains, streaks, or
   drips ("подтёки") anywhere, checked from multiple viewing angles and
   under multiple light sources/angles, not just one head-on look under
   ordinary light.
8. **Two-color paint junction and paint-to-wallpaper junction QC**: the
   line where two paint colors meet must be crisp and straight, no
   waviness; a paint-to-wallpaper junction must be clean, with no glue
   smears, surface damage, or lifted wallpaper edges at the seam.
9. **Dark-wallpaper installer lifehack, a competence signal**: near a
   dark-paint-to-dark-wallpaper junction, paint a 5-10cm strip of the
   wall under the future wallpaper edge in the same dark color before
   hanging — this means any small gap (up to ≈0.5mm) that later appears
   at the seam isn't visually noticeable, since the wall behind it
   already matches. Framed as a sign of a knowledgeable, competent
   installer when observed.
10. **Ceiling-molding/cornice ("потолочный плинтус") acceptance
    checklist**: install without visible gaps or cracks; miter seams
    invisible; painted evenly along the full length; requires careful,
    timely removal of squeezed-out specialty adhesive; a dedicated
    joint-filler adhesive should be used specifically at seams, not the
    same adhesive used for mounting.
11. **General finish-protection rule, with named real failure modes**:
    keep all finished surfaces (walls, floors) clean, undamaged, and
    covered from dust throughout the remainder of the project — named
    failures this guards against: spilled adhesive, or a dropped paint
    roller, ruining an already-finished surface. Extend the same check
    to electrical (panel, outlets, switches) and plumbing fixtures —
    paint/adhesive splatter left on them by other trades is very hard
    to remove once dried.
