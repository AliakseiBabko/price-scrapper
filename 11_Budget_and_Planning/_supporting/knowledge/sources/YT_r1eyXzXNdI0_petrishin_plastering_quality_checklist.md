## Plastering — QC & Technique (new items only)

1. **Beacon rust-bleed-through mechanism, new and genuinely useful — a
   root-cause explanation for why beacons must always be pulled**: even
   galvanized beacons get scratched/scored by the straightedge
   ("правило") tool as it's dragged across them during plastering,
   exposing bare steel under the galvanizing. That exposed steel then
   rusts, and the rust eventually bleeds through the finish layers above
   it (putty, fiberglass mesh/wallpaper backing, or paint) months later.
   **Fix**: always remove every beacon after plastering and patch the
   channel flush with the same plaster material, in the same plane as
   the rest of the wall — never leave a beacon embedded, galvanized or
   not.
2. **Corner-reinforcement mesh, mid-depth placement detail**: internal
   corners of exterior walls should be pre-scored/cut before plastering
   and reinforced with mesh laid specifically *inside the plaster body*
   (mid-thickness), not just anywhere in the layer — extends this page's
   existing exterior-corner mesh-reinforcement rule with the actual
   burial-depth detail.
3. **90° corner requirement is deliberately scoped, not whole-apartment**:
   strict 90° corners throughout the apartment aren't required — only the
   kitchen and bathroom need them, specifically so built-in furniture and
   fixtures can sit flush. Elsewhere, rounded/imperfect corners are
   acceptable as long as they don't interfere with wallpaper seams (see
   the existing door-opening-defect note on this page).
4. **Coin-press QC technique, a new field test distinct from the
   existing straightedge/gap check**: press a coin against the plastered
   surface — if the coin sinks in, the plaster is under-hard/defective;
   a properly cured, adequately hard surface resists it. The same test is
   also applied at door-opening reveals specifically, to check for
   gaps/soft spots there.
5. **Plaster-thickness photo-documentation as a stated client-transparency
   practice**: whenever plaster thickness exceeds 3cm, the site foreman
   photographs it — framed as this company's standard practice so the
   client can see exactly how much extra material/labor a thicker-than-
   normal plaster layer actually required, rather than just being told a
   number. (Consistent with, but a distinct QC-documentation framing
   from, the over-norm-thickness *billing* mechanism already recorded
   for this channel's 2022 cost-breakdown case in `Budgeting_Guide.md`.)
