## Plumbing / Materials — Floor-Standing Radiator Risks & Mitigation

- **Tile-grout cracking mechanism**: the supply-pipe stub-outs at a floor-standing radiator's base sit inside a tile cutout sealed with grout; grout has near-zero elasticity, and the pipes expand/contract with heating cycles — repeated thermal cycling reliably cracks the grout at that joint over time.
- **Laminate expansion-gap violation, independently corroborating and extending this store's existing rule** (`yt_WBaKEl5HIzU`): laminate flooring must be able to expand/contract freely; a rigid pipe penetration fixed directly against the laminate blocks that movement at the penetration point, and as the laminate expands it can push against the radiator/pipe assembly hard enough to shift or crack it — an installation defect serious enough to void the laminate manufacturer's own warranty independent of any flood risk. **Genuinely new detail not in the existing source**: radiator floor-mount brackets often have elongated (not round) foot holes, apparently intended to let the laminate expand — but this only accounts for expansion in one direction (perpendicular to the window); the source states laminate actually expands in all directions, and expansion parallel to the window (unaccounted for by the elongated-hole design) can wedge the laminate solidly between adjacent radiator legs, defeating the mitigation entirely.
- **Cleaning difficulty**: dirt/dust accumulates around radiator feet and pipe stub-outs in a way that's awkward to clean given the tight clearances.
- **Physical fragility and real injury/damage anecdotes**: floor-standing radiators are described as structurally delicate — a hard mop/broom strike against the supply-pipe joints can start a leak. Two concrete real-client examples: a radiator positioned at a loggia doorway was repeatedly knee-bumped by guests passing through, eventually causing recurring leaks at the joints; radiators generally present sharp/aggressive edge geometry that poses a real impact-injury risk to a small child running through a room (source references this with a dark joke about child safety, not literal advice — flagged as rhetorical framing, not a data point).
- **Baseline recommendation**: avoid floor-standing radiators wherever avoidable — for a standard renovation with a normal windowsill/wall configuration, use a wall-mounted radiator behind a false wall instead (matches this store's existing wall-mounted-radiator-preference content).
- **Mitigation technique 1 — windowsill recess/false wall, when a windowsill ledge exists**: recess the radiator into the windowsill depth or box it behind a false wall, so the supply pipes never contact the finished floor covering at all and the radiator body is protected from foot/mop/cart impact.
- **Mitigation technique 2 — raised podium, when there's no windowsill (floor-to-ceiling glazing with the radiator directly under it)**: build a 10-12cm podium under the window, route the supply pipes up through the podium (not through the finished floor), and set the windowsill on top of the podium; the section under the podium is closed off with baseboard. Net effect matches technique 1: pipes never contact the finished floor covering, eliminating both the grout-cracking and laminate-expansion failure modes at their source (no pipe-to-flooring penetration point exists at all).
