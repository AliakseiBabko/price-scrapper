## Design Concept

- **Reusable-design mechanism**: the company built 10 complete interior designs (5 styles: modern, Scandinavian, neoclassical, eco-steppe/"эко степь", minimalism — each in a light and a dark variant) on one 160 m² fully-featured virtual reference apartment (kitchen, living room, bedroom, kids room, bathroom, shower room, attached loggia, separate/isolated loggia, entry hall, spacious hallway, walk-in closet/dressing room). The designer states these designs are explicitly built to be **scale-independent** — the same material/color palette and layout logic is stated to transfer to any real apartment's actual footprint without redesign, because the chosen materials/finishes are deliberately selected to harmonize with each other regardless of room proportions. Demonstrated once: the same style was applied unmodified to a real 74 m² two-room apartment and is described (by the same speaker, no independent verification) as looking equally polished. `single-account`, unverified beyond the speaker's own claim, but the underlying design principle (choosing a materials/color palette specifically for cross-scale harmony rather than bespoke-fitting) is a genuine, reusable idea distinct from the promotional wrapper around it.
- **"Modern dark" style — described technique detail** (the specific style Panfilov selected for his own current project, presented as representative of the overall zems-design approach):
  - Deliberately kept **detail-minimal relative to typical "loaded" modern styles** — described as avoiding both classic minimalism's "empty/cold" feel and typical modern style's clutter, by using a small number of textures/colors/details consistently repeated.
  - **Three lighting layers used together**: primary (general/functional), decorative, and "duty/night" (дежурный) lighting — stated purpose: lets the occupant build different lighting "scenarios" for different needs (e.g. dim diffused light for watching a film vs. bright/sparkling light for a celebration) rather than a single fixed lighting setup.
  - **Slat/batten wall cladding ("рейки")** called out explicitly as "one of the most effective, accessible techniques" for a modern style at relatively low cost — stated to visually make a renovation look more expensive than its actual cost.
  - **Mirrors** used as a second low-cost technique for the same "makes it look pricier" effect, doubling as visual space-expansion, sometimes combined with the slat technique in the same room.
  - **Consistent porcelain tile ("керамогранит") without inserts** used throughout wet rooms, paired with colored furniture/backlighting rather than tile-pattern variation, to keep the material palette minimal.
  - **Floor-and-furniture texture matching**: natural-look flooring texture chosen first, then furniture textures selected to closely match it, as a deliberate cohesion technique.
  - **Wall color**: neutral dark tone with a warm undertone, stated as the mechanism for achieving a "cozy minimalism" feel without visual clutter.
  - **Track/magnetic-rail lighting system**: primary lighting built on embedded magnetic-track wiring, allowing fixtures to be repositioned, added, removed, or swapped in type after installation without rewiring — described as flexible over the life of the space, not fixed at install time.
  - **Stated color temperatures**: primary lighting ≈ 4500 K ("теплый дневной свет" / warm daylight-equivalent); decorative/accent lighting ≈ 3800 K (warmer still). Presented as this specific style's chosen values, not a universal rule.
  - **Profile LED strip added along the slats** with a diffuser, described as adding two more lighting-scenario options per room at "practically no cost increase."
  - **Kids-room furniture**: a custom furniture model described as scalable to any room size while keeping consistent seating comfort/usability for a child doing schoolwork, regardless of the room's actual dimensions.
  - **Two named finishing accents** closing out the style: an abstract-pattern canvas/artwork ("холл с абстрактным рисунком" — likely "полотно"/canvas, transcript ambiguity noted below) placed in the living room, and a chalkboard placed in the kitchen.
- **"Design as scale-independent product" framing** is the video's central design-philosophy claim — worth recording as a general concept (a materials/color palette *can* be designed for cross-scale reuse rather than bespoke per-apartment) even though the specific execution here is this one company's own product, not a general industry technique.
