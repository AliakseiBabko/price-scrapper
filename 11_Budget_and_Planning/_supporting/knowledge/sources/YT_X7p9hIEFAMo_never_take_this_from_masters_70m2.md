## Durable Facts

- **Leave an adjustable opening/formwork pocket for the manifold ("коллекторный") cabinet's heating-loop trace pipes before pouring screed**, so pipes can still be shifted left/right after the screed is down, instead of having to jackhammer the cured screed to reposition fixed pipe runs. **Install the manifold cabinet itself only after wall plastering is finished**, not before — installing early means hours of cleanup/protection during plastering, or outright damage requiring cabinet replacement.
- **Same adjustable-opening technique applies to radiator trace-pipe runs when the customer hasn't yet chosen a radiator model** — designer radiators can have left- or right-side connections, so the team leaves a pipe-routing pocket sized for the largest plausible radiator footprint rather than committing to a fixed left/right run before the fixture is selected.
- **Interior partition walls built using adhesive foam ("клей-пена") bonding** (rather than traditional mechanical framing/blocking) — stated as the team's standard method for roughly the last 6 months across nearly all objects; cited example of 2 workers completing multiple room's walls in 2 days, described as easier logistically (lighter material handling/delivery) than prior methods. `single-account`, no comparison data on long-term durability given.
- **Storm-drain pipes ("ливнёвка") routed through an apartment must be soundproofed** — an un-soundproofed storm drain was blamed (humorously, but as a real complaint pattern) for making occupants feel a persistent urge to urinate from the sound of running water; team's stated practice is to soundproof these before closing walls.
- **Dedicated non-switchable circuit for router + security camera**: wired through one always-on outlet (with a splitter for router/camera/accessories) that stays powered even when every breaker in the main panel is switched off — lets the team/property manager remotely check camera footage overnight (e.g. responding to a flood complaint from neighbors/management) without a truck roll, and prevents anyone on-site from accidentally or deliberately disabling surveillance by killing power at the panel.
- **Interfloor ventilation duct/riser integrity check before cosmetic work**: verify duct and riser condition and clamp security first; if only the surrounding soundproofing (not the duct itself) is damaged, still restore it properly rather than leaving it as found, since the appearance matters to customers inspecting progress. **Developer-installed square sheet-metal exhaust ducts are replaced with round plastic ducting** to reclaim airflow/cross-section the square ducts otherwise waste.
- **Inspect and retighten every hose clamp on water-supply risers/stacks** inherited from the developer — cited as commonly under-secured, a real safety issue given riser water is under significant pressure; also flagged a case of a water meter installed at an unusually low height where a wall-hung-toilet installation frame belongs instead, requiring rerouting.
- **Windows can be safely left unprotected during rough work if their full replacement is already planned for later in the schedule**, ideally after the dirtiest work (e.g. after painting) — avoids paying for protection material that will be discarded anyway when the window is swapped.
- **Expansion/control joints ("деформационные швы") required at every wall-to-floor junction and every corner, but only for walls built after the screed was poured** — walls that predate the screed pour don't need them at their base. Absence of these joints allows the screed to crack over time; verify their presence before/during screed work, not after.
- **Minimum screed-height differential of 1.5 cm planned between quartz-vinyl and tile zones** even when not strictly required by material thickness, specifically to preserve flexibility — the alternative (flush level) forces full demolition of the tiled-zone screed if the flooring plan changes later, since removing only 1–1.5 cm of screed isn't practical. A shallow (~1 cm) threshold strip is presented as a worse alternative: height differences under ~5 cm aren't reliably perceived visually, so a small lip becomes a stubbed-toe/trip hazard rather than a visible step.
- **Two-layer floor soundproofing** (a first branded underlayment layer plus an additional "ТЗИ" thermal/sound-insulation felt layer on top) applied continuously across the whole floor, including areas later reopened for radiator-niche work (removed and precisely reinstalled after niche work, to avoid gaps in coverage). The material's overlapped/lapped edges, later trimmed off, double as an expansion buffer that absorbs the screed's thermal expansion/contraction — described as a detail that shouldn't be economized away.
- **Anti-fungal admixture added to ceiling paint applied above a stretch ceiling**, because rough-stage work (screed curing, plastering, even plain painting) generates significant condensate, and a ceiling surface can accumulate enough moisture to grow mold that's invisible once the stretch-ceiling membrane is installed over it — framed as a preventable, easily-skipped step.
- **Temporary construction-stage electrical**: pre-run a flat two-conductor cable (ШВВП 2×0.75 mm², rated for most power tools except heavy-draw ones) from the panel to a single outlet, mounted via adhesive-tape cable clips, rather than relying on extension cords + work lights strung room to room — reduces trip hazards and avoids having to re-route dangling cords during screed pours.
- **Pre-plastering QC pass using only an angle gauge and a tape measure**: verify all 90° corners are true (specifically flagged as mandatory for the kitchen corner and every bathroom corner) before plastering, so only a thin plaster coat is needed rather than thick correction layers (saves material and interior floor-area loss); also verify every rough-opening height against the project spec — **minimum stated opening height 2.06 m** in this project — always measuring from the floor up with the tape resting on the floor, not held aloft by hand (called out as an unreliable/untrustworthy measuring habit to watch for in a crew).
