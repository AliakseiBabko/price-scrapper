## Durable Facts

- **Ceiling soundproofing is low-value; upstairs-neighbor floor soundproofing is far more effective** — adds 200,000–500,000 RUB to budget on this apartment size, but every ceiling penetration (vent/electrical mounts) degrades it, and a leak from above is far worse under a stretch ceiling with hidden ceiling soundproofing (traps water/mold) than a bare stretch ceiling (just drop/dry/re-hang). Speaker's stated preference: pay to soundproof the floor of the unit above instead. In premium-class work he says both are still done. `single-account`.
- **Supply-air ventilation ("приточка") design must be done only by a specialized ventilation contractor**, never a general contractor, architect, or interior designer — sequence: full project done first (marked only "supply" or "supply+exhaust" or "ducted system"), then ventilation contractor visits, measures, and produces the actual vent design, then the general project is updated to add the required electrical feed/breakers, then boxing/drywall to conceal ducts is finalized last. `single-account`, stated as a firm rule.
- **Round-section ducts recommended over flat/rectangular** for lower noise; flat ducts are used only to minimize ceiling drop, with the actual concealment technique being to run supply ducts above kitchen cabinets and box them behind a floor-to-ceiling kitchen facade (rather than dropping the whole room's ceiling to the duct's lowest point) — cited as recovering roughly 12–15 cm of ceiling height.
- **Interior door height 2.30 m** recommended over standard 2.0 m for premium-class projects with ceilings above 2.80 m — costs roughly 2–4x a standard 2.0 m door but framed as expected at this segment; **"strongly recommend, and mandatory for wealthy clients"** once ceiling exceeds 2.80 m. `single-account`.
- **Electrical panel capacity (15 kW total) can't support two tankless water heaters running simultaneously** plus a cooktop/oven — a **recirculation pump with a thermal sensor** (not a plain recirculation pump) is used instead to deliver fast hot water to a second bathroom from one tankless unit ~15 m of pipe away, activating only when the loop temperature drops rather than running continuously. Plain (sensorless) recirculation pumps are called out as a costly mistake (constant run, wasted energy).
- **Flooring transition sequencing (quartz-vinyl vs. engineered board vs. porcelain tile)**: engineered board is thicker than quartz-vinyl; screed height differentials must be planned before installation, not compensated later with self-leveling compound (too expensive at that thickness, and cracks) or tile adhesive layer. **Plywood underlay for engineered board must never be glued onto self-leveling compound** — only onto sub-screed; reputable installers will refuse or void warranty otherwise. **Engineered board must acclimate on-site** (temperature/humidity) before installation, same as laminate but for longer — installing it fresh off a truck is a defect risk. This flooring-transition/sequencing theme recurs across this playlist (also in `p1BQChVZnmE` and `wQwuSsTtM1k`) — no existing wiki page owns it cleanly (see Target Pages below).
- **Leak-protection system**: sensor under every radiator (100% radiator reliability doesn't exist); wireless/radio sensors preferred over wired to avoid visible cabling (caveat: robot vacuums reportedly "eat" wired sensor cables). **The leak-protection control unit ("brain") must be mounted behind a plastic panel/door, not inside a metal manifold cabinet** — radio signal can't penetrate the metal door, silently disabling the whole system.
- **Porcelain tile standard size 60×20 cm** (or 60×20/120×60 as size variants) declared as the channel's current fixed default for bathroom walls/floors — minimizes grout lines, ages well over 10–15 years, priced acceptably at this format. Any tradesperson tiling at this size must own both a manual tile cutter capable of cutting up to ~120 cm and an electric wet saw; lacking either disqualifies them from the job (stated humorously but as a real vetting criterion).
- **Non-sagging access hatches** preferred over cut-and-imitate-factory-edge tile hatches for large-format tile over plumbing risers/water-inlet nodes.
- **Shower podium in mismatched format**: cut podium risers from the same porcelain tile as the floor (small wedge-shaped cuts for slope/drain) rather than substituting mosaic, because mosaic never matches the main tile's exact look — alternative shown: cut the same porcelain tile into small squares and mesh-mount it as custom-matched "mosaic" for the podium.
