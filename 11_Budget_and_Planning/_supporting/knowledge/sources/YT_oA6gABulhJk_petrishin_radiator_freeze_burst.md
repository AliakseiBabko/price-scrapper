## Explicit cross-check against this channel's other flood incidents (Round 4 towel-warmer, Round 9 ventilation-defect, Round 10 radiator-fitting flood)

**Confirmed a genuinely distinct fourth mechanism, not a restatement of
any prior incident.** Round 4's incidents (`Z1IuJFudcPY`,
`qFM8NIDIRro`-adjacent content) are **demolition-crew physical damage**
to an existing towel-warmer pipe. Round 9's (`nhJI_yGjfRU`) is a
**years-later concealed shared-ventilation-valve defect** surfacing only
after a new downstairs neighbor's complaint. Round 10's (`GLU9nJYrtbQ`)
is a **developer-installed heating-fitting/gasket failure at a specific
connection point**, with liability turning on which connection point
was touched. **This video's mechanism is different from all three**:
a **freeze-burst caused by an interrupted heating supply combined with
an open window left for post-plaster drying** — cold air entered
through the cracked window, the radiator's water column froze because
heating had been externally cut off, and the resulting ice expansion
tore the rubber gasket at both the top and bottom radiator connections
simultaneously (not a single fitting failure point). No demolition
damage, no years-later concealment, no simple installation-fitting
defect — the proximate cause is environmental (cold + no heat), not
mechanical/installation.

- **Root-cause chain, structured**: (1) the building's management
  company/developer had a **separate, unrelated radiator leak
  elsewhere** in the same new-build (many units still vacant); (2)
  rather than diagnosing which specific unit was actually leaking, the
  management company **shut off heating supply to multiple apartments
  at once**, including this one, as a blunt precaution; (3) this crew
  had left a window cracked open in this apartment specifically to
  speed moisture evaporation after plastering/screed work — a
  reasonable, common practice, but one that became dangerous once
  heating was unexpectedly cut; (4) with no heat and a cold-air path in,
  the water remaining in the radiator loop **froze**, and ice expansion
  **ruptured the rubber gasket at both the upper and lower connection
  points** of one specific radiator.
- **⚠️ Management-company diagnostic-failure pattern, a reusable
  caution**: a management company responding to *one* unit's leak by
  cutting heating to *several* units, without first identifying which
  unit is actually affected, is itself a real risk factor during
  renovation — it can convert an unrelated problem into a freeze-burst
  in an apartment that otherwise had no leak at all.
- **Real incident scale, quantified**: roughly **1-1.5 liters** of water
  total leaked out before the client caught it and shut the floor's
  water-supply valves — explicitly framed as lucky/minor ("мы никого не
  успели затопить") compared to a multi-apartment flooding event.
- **Diagnostic sequence used by the client**: noticed unusually low room
  temperature → checked the apartment's own heating manifold/collector
  node ("коллекторный узел") and found all valves open (i.e. nothing
  wrong on the apartment's own side) → went to the floor/landing's
  shared water-supply shutoff and found the developer had closed it →
  reopened those valves, which is what revealed the actual leak (water
  then flowed to the now-frozen/burst radiator gasket).
- **⚠️ Radial ("лучевая") piping topology — positive corroboration of
  Round 10's implied recommendation, made explicit here**: because this
  apartment's heating was piped radially (each radiator on its own
  dedicated supply/return line and valve, run in cross-linked
  polyethylene — "сшитый полиэтилен"/PEX), the crew could **isolate and
  shut off only the single burst radiator**, leaving the other three
  radiators/rooms fully functional while the repair (gasket
  replacement) was made. Explicitly generalized by the speaker as a
  direct recommendation: **if replacing heating pipes/radiators at all,
  choose radial wiring specifically for this isolation capability** — the
  same system-topology tradeoff Round 10's non-radial ("не лучевая")
  incident flagged as a liability from the opposite (negative) direction.
- **Named product**: radiators are brand "Royal Termo," client-purchased
