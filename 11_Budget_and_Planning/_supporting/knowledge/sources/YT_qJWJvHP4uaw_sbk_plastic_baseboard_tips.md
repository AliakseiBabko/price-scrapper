## Materials / Mistakes / Warnings — Cutting Plastic Trim and Baseboard

- **⚠️ Named tool-choice technique: metal shears (ножницы по металлу), not wire cutters or a utility knife, for cutting plastic corner trim/baseboard**: wire cutters ("кусачки") cause the plastic to chip/snap at an uncontrolled point rather than the intended cut line; a utility knife is both slow and unsafe (blade can slip or fly off, risking injury) and requires real skill to cut cleanly — explicit safety note: wear protective gloves if using a utility knife regardless. **Metal shears cut quickly, cleanly, at any angle/direction, without chipping or snapping the plastic** — any remaining small burrs can be cleaned up afterward with a utility knife.
- **Angle grinder as a faster but noise-constrained alternative**: also usable for this cut, but loud enough that it's a poor choice during quiet hours/a lunch break in a shared building — an explicit neighbor-noise consideration for this specific tool choice.

## Materials — Plastic vs. MDF Baseboard, a Wall-Flatness Planning Trap

- **⚠️ Plastic baseboard's real functional reason for use in budget renovations, not just a cost-cutting default**: plastic baseboard is flexible enough to conform to and visually hide uneven developer-standard (unplastered) walls — a genuine functional justification for choosing it on a budget renovation, not merely "the cheap option."
- **⚠️ Named real mid-project trap: switching from plastic to MDF baseboard late in the project, once walls are already built to accommodate plastic**: MDF baseboard is rigid and does not conform to an uneven wall — installed against a wall with real waviness, it leaves a visible gap at points where the wall isn't flat, which then must either be filled/caulked or left visible depending on the installer's skill and the gap size. **Real trigger scenario described**: a client on a budget renovation (walls not fully plastered flat, plastic baseboard originally planned) sees nicer-looking MDF doors/baseboard at the same store while selecting interior doors near project completion, and decides to upgrade the baseboard too — without realizing the wall prep assumption behind the original plastic-baseboard plan no longer holds. **Explicit mitigation stated directly**: if you're doing a budget renovation with imperfectly flattened walls, don't swap plastic for MDF baseboard at the end; if you do want MDF baseboard, tell your installers *up front* so they can either talk you out of it or prepare the walls accordingly — avoiding a late-stage problem for both installer and client.

## Site Practice / Life-Hacks

- **Improvised temporary mixer/faucet from on-hand materials when no real mixer is installed on-site yet**: a vacuum-cleaner hose attachment plus two flexible supply hoses and tape (electrical/insulating tape) can be assembled into a working temporary faucet; alternatively, a standard 50mm sewer tee fitting plus an elbow can substitute for the vacuum attachment if one isn't available.
- **⚠️ Coarse-strainer ("фильтр грубой очистки") orientation depends on water-flow direction, a real installation-error source**: the standard angled coarse strainer is designed to be installed with its mesh/screen facing downward, correct only when water flows into it from above. **Where the water supply instead comes from below, installing this same angled filter with the mesh facing up is described directly as incorrect** — the fix is to use a straight/universal-pattern filter instead of forcing the angled one into an orientation it wasn't designed for.
