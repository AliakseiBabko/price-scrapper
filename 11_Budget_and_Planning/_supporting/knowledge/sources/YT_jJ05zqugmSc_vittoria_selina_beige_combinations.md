## Design Concept — What Beige Is

- **Definitions given**: "бежевый" is the French term for undyed natural wool fabric; **"экрю" (écru)**, one step lighter, is the French term for unbleached linen or silk. Both are named as fundamentally natural, unprocessed colours; natural referents cited are wheat, stone surfaces and sand.
- **⚠️ Definition of a *neutral / base* beige, the video's most reusable idea** — the shade that will accept any companion colour and can be redecorated around seasonally:
  - **Not the lightest beige and not the darkest** — those are already committed to a specific story (lighter/airier or deeper/more characterful). The base beige is **the mid-point between them**.
  - **Temperature-neutral.** A beige that leans yellow ("оттенок сливочного масла") is beautiful but has predispositions — it will *not* take everything, and specifically won't take a cool light blue, though it will take a warmer one. A genuinely neutral beige "и не тёплый, и не холодный" is what makes "к нему и синий, и зелёный, и красные, и жёлтый — всё должно подходить."
  - **Named real products she says she has used repeatedly on real objects**: Little Greene **Clay 39**, **Rolling Fog 143**, and **Rolling Fog Dark 160** — the last described as a beige-grey that is her most-used neutral. Recorded as concrete, checkable product references, `single-account`.
- **⚠️ Beige/grey walls flatter objects better than white does — with a stated mechanism**: against pure white a coloured object is just "предмет и предмет," whereas "именно вот такие оттенки серого и бежевого создают тени для этих предметов," so the same object reads richer and more deliberate. She frames white walls as a photography backdrop rather than an interior decision. Applies equally to grey.

## Design Concept — Path 1: A Fully Beige Interior (No Colour At All)

Three stated rules, presented as a set:

- **⚠️ Rule 1 — shades must share a temperature, and you must *stretch* the beige across several distinct tones.** Explicitly warns against the opposite: carrying a sofa fabric swatch around the shops to match chairs exactly — "так делать не нужно." Her worked breakdown: neutral beige walls (largest area), lighter curtains (less area), a slightly darker practical sofa, and wooden chairs in another tone again. Different lightnesses of one colour is what makes the room read three-dimensional instead of the flat, impersonal monochrome that gives beige its bad reputation. Adjacent warm relatives are also admissible inside this — khaki as the green, earthy terracotta (defined as brown plus red, from *terra* = earth) rather than a bright red clay.
- **⚠️ Rule 2 — texture difference is mandatory, and is the actual source of volume.** Named combination: painted wall + linen curtains + velvet sofa = three materials, three tones, three textures. Explicit instruction not to place velvet chairs next to a velvet sofa — use рогожка, бархат, букле, хлопок differently across pieces. "Множество фактур в интерьере — это всегда выигрышный вариант."
- **⚠️ Rule 3 — no coloured decor in this path.** Permitted decor: natural-toned ceramics, white clay pots, wooden trays/stands/salad bowls, and **clear uncoloured glass and crystal**. Refinement she stresses: use glass with *varied* relief and ribbing and pattern, not just plain clear, in quantity — the same "several different shades" logic applied to the glass. **Brass** is named as the single addition that most raises the perceived level, with wood second.

## Design Concept — Path 2: Beige as a Base for Colour

Named pairings, each with the specific handling:

- **Beige + pink (+ wood)** — light pink that is deep rather than pale; adding wood is what she calls the strongest version. A more saturated pink object also works, because the beige wall emphasises it.
- **⚠️ Beige + light blue, with black in the *details only*** — she demonstrates the intended quantity of black by holding up a single pencil. Concrete permitted forms: **black skirting, black cabinet handles, black door handles, black doors, a black table underframe, black chairs, one pendant lamp over the table** — "не обязательно, что вводится такая вот чёрная стена. Да, это именно про деталь." A rare, genuinely quantified statement of how much of an accent metal/black is intended.
- **Beige + yellow + greenery** — read explicitly as Italian; add clay and terracotta; keep the walls beige and the yellow in details (curtain trim, beading, decor); "очень много растений."
- **Beige + violet (краплак) + warm green** — violet velvet sofa, rogozhka chairs in краплак, a green pendant on a black cord. Stated mechanism for why it stays warm despite violet being a cool colour: the green chosen has yellow in it, and the beige is the neutral one, so "фиолетовый приобретает немного даже нейтральность."

## Design Concept — Two Contrarian Claims Worth Flagging

- **⚠️ Contrarian claim on dark rooms — a direct contradiction of the standard advice and of another source in this batch.** The conventional rule that dark rooms should be painted light "чтобы расширить" is rejected: "ничего подобного, в практике всё на самом деле по-другому." Her mechanism — in a room with genuinely blocked daylight (her example: a third-floor room with a poplar outside the window) **a light beige "превращается просто моментально в серый тон,"** losing exactly the softness and refinement it was chosen for. Her conclusion: **"тёмное помещение лучше красить в тёмные цвета"** — a small dark room painted a genuinely dark colour becomes deeper and, she claims, reads as expanded. She explicitly leaves the choice with the reader (if you'll be viewing it under warm table-lamp light and don't mind the grey shift by day, do it anyway). **Route as a Perspectives block** — this contradicts ГК Авальремонт (`2__5Y0sLAxo`, light walls for small rooms) in this same batch and sits in tension with the existing ARCHWOOD dark-room section on `Color_Palette_and_Material_Direction.md` (which prescribes *more saturated*, and the brightest available white, for a low-light room).
- **⚠️ Don't default a coloured or dark-walled room to a pure white ceiling.** With bright or dark walls, the hard white/colour contrast makes the ceiling jump forward, the central chandelier reads "как паучок на этом белом потолке," and the room's exact height becomes explicit — bad at ~2.50 m. Her fix short of painting the ceiling in the wall colour: **buy a warm white / экрю ceiling paint instead of a pure white one.** Stated mechanism: against coloured walls it is still *perceived* as white, but it softens the transition and works as a correcting tone. She also names the cornice-carried-onto-the-ceiling technique as a separate way to raise perceived height. Partially corroborates ARCHWOOD's `AKxOtRhIcJY` ceiling rules from the opposite direction.

## Design Concept — Testing and Judgement

- **⚠️ Never judge wall paint before the interior is assembled — with a real client case.** A client visited during painting, saw the first or second coat of a deep pink on bare walls, found it too bright, and had it changed to a paler pink. Her position: "никогда не оценивайте краску на стене, когда ещё нету готового интерьера у вас — так нельзя, это могут делать только профессионалы," because the professional is extrapolating to how it will sit with the furniture, wallpaper and decor. A concrete, costly, avoidable failure mode, and the client's decision is reported as final and respected.
- **Sampling technique**: tape a sheet of **ватман** to the wall with masking tape, paint the ватман, then look at it **morning, midday and evening and in different weather**. A cheap variant of the выкрас that avoids painting the wall itself.
- **General position on theory**: she deliberately teaches without the colour wheel or complementary theory — "зачем вам нужно знать эту теорию, когда вам нужно просто выбрать какой-то оттенок и покрасить им стены." Recorded as a stated pedagogical stance, and as a real counterweight to the two theory-first sources in this batch.
