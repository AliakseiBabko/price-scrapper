## Doors / Trim

- **Pre-installation measurement protocol**: always call a measurer even if
  you could measure yourself (free service) — a common mistake is measuring
  the opening including door casing/liners still attached, producing a
  wrongly-sized door order. Existing developer casing must be removed
  before measuring so the true rough-opening size is exposed. Width is
  measured at 3 points along the install line to ±0.5cm precision; height
  is measured at 2 points from the outer (opening-direction) plane, to
  preserve floor clearance for the door swing.
- **Real named service price breakdown, Minsk, 2020-12-23** (each rounded
  per this project's convention, trailing-6-month USD/BYN average): door
  installation **90 BYN (≈$40)**; old-frame removal while preserving the
  frame for resale **15 BYN (≈$10)**; supplemental mounting-plate
  installation **15 BYN (≈$10)**; cutting a notch in stairwell tile
  skirting without removing the tile **15 BYN (≈$10)**. The removed old
  door/frame itself can be resold via classifieds for **≈45 BYN (≈$20)**,
  partially offsetting install cost.
- **Mounting-plate technique for monolithic-frame openings**: a monolithic
  concrete door opening often has embedded rebar directly behind planned
  anchor-bolt locations; supplemental steel mounting plates let the
  installer shift an anchor's exact position sideways to avoid hitting
  rebar, at a small added service cost (above).
- **Door weight and handling**: a 100-series steel entrance door weighs
  ≈130 kg; two installers carry it with shoulder-slung lifting straps, or
  four people without straps.
- **Anchor count and pattern**: 12 anchors total for this installation — 6
  through the door frame directly, 6 through supplemental mounting plates.
  Frame is leveled with plastic shims in two planes before any anchor is
  driven; **level is stated as the single most important acceptance
  criterion** for a newly installed entrance door.
- **Foaming technique**: gap between frame and rough opening is foamed in
  two passes to ensure full fill; frame is masked with painter's tape
  beforehand to keep foam off the finish; excess foam is trimmed the day
  after installation (allows full cure).
- **Post-install maintenance routine**: periodically lubricate the metal
  latch/deadbolt and the door's exposed hinge-metal moving parts (a
  lubrication port exists in the hinges — lift the door slightly to inject
  lubricant); always fully press the door shut after checking whether it
  latched, so the latch bolt fully seats in the strike plate — a stated
  common habit (repeatedly nudging a door to test the latch) that can leave
  it not fully seated if not followed by a firm press.
- **Protective wrap-during-renovation technique**: wrap the whole frame in
  PVC tape (named product: Blue Dolphin) and cover the door face with a
  cut sheet of the door's own cardboard shipping packaging (preferred over
  the bubble-wrap the door also ships in) — cardboard resists impacts (e.g.
  an accidental drywall-profile jab) better than plastic film alone. One
  prior project's tape residue-free removal after ~1 year on-site is cited
  as the reason for that specific brand recommendation. `single-account`.
- **Door-tier selection criteria, in the speaker's own stated priority
  order**: (1) soundproofing — door "series" number (60/70/80/90/100)
  denotes leaf thickness; thicker leaf = more mineral-wool fill = better
  soundproofing; his chosen 100-series leaf also has 4 sealing contours (3
  on frame, 1 on door) using German Schlegel weatherstripping, plus a
  16mm MDF exterior-face panel — all cited as contributing to sound/smell
  isolation from the corridor; (2) appearance/material match — matching
  the door's interior face finish to the apartment's own interior-door
  line (Porte Richi) as a deliberate "complex solution" continuity choice,
  especially relevant when interior and entrance doors sit near each other;
  (3) security — steel thickness 1.5mm, break-in resistance class 4 (of a
  13-class scale, 5-13 being the classed range, with class 13 reserved for
  bank-vault-grade doors), anti-removal bolts (ригели) specifically
  preventing hinge-side forced removal even if hinges are cut from outside.
- **Lock-cylinder strategy, a real practitioner habit worth noting**:
  installed cheap temporary cylinders (Vella) during the renovation itself
  because many different tradespeople need a key, planning to swap to
  higher-security abus cylinders after renovation completes — abus'
  named advantage cited is a "master key" system letting one key open
  multiple different abus-cylinder locks (e.g. apartment + office + house),
  with cylinder recoding available if a key is lost (owner-card required to
  cut a duplicate). This is a genuine security/practicality tradeoff
  pattern (cheap-during-construction, upgrade-after) independent of the
  specific brand named.
- **Keyless/smart-lock note, unresolved for this speaker**: acknowledges a
  market trend toward phone-controlled keyless locks (named: Danalock) but
  states he personally hasn't adopted one yet, calling premium versions
  expensive with more budget options emerging — his own stated
  undecided/future-maybe position, not a recommendation either way.
