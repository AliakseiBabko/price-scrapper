## Doors / Trim — Durable Facts & Rules

- **A doorless, wide, casing-trimmed opening between rooms needs a small "при стенок" (a short stub of wall, ~10 cm) preserved at each outer corner** where casing (наличник) will terminate on *both* faces of the wall (i.e. both rooms' sides of the opening) — without it, one side of the opening cannot be cleanly trimmed: the casing has nothing to butt/return against, forcing either a crude straight cut (looks unfinished) or a mitred (45°) return into thin air with no adjoining casing on the other face to anchor it visually. `confirmed`, concrete and specific — a real jobsite failure mode, not hypothetical.
- **Root cause described**: a site foreman/master judged the stub "too narrow" and removed it without checking with the design technologist (project author) — the crew only discovered the resulting trim problem when framing the casing on the opposite face. Speaker's explicit standing instruction to his crew: **never deviate from the technologist's project without approval**, even when a detail looks locally awkward or oversized on site — the wider ramifications may not be visible from one side alone. `confirmed`, a process/QA rule rather than a technical dimension rule.
- **Fix applied**: reopen the finished drywall/wallpaper on the affected side, frame in a small drywall stub (~10 cm) to recreate the missing при stenok, then re-finish (replaster/re-wallpaper) — described as intentionally quick/invisible rework so the client wouldn't notice a defect occurred. `confirmed`, real remediation example.
