## Rules / Heuristics — Genuinely New Content

- **⚠️ Door casing/baseboard color-and-material-consistency rule**: door casings (наличники) and baseboards should be the same color and, ideally, the same manufacturer/material — reading as one continuous trim element flowing through a space, not two visually distinct product lines. Applies universally (own-use or rental).
- **⚠️ Never try to paint a door to match an adjacent wood-textured surface's exact color** — the colors will never truly match under real lighting; use a deliberately contrasting color for a painted door near wood-textured elements instead of attempting a subtle match.
- **⚠️ Flooring-to-door-frame transition rule**: flooring should meet the door frame directly, with only a small gap filled by color-matched sealant — never a separate, visible transition threshold strip, especially not one with exposed fasteners, a mismatched color, or an angular/asymmetric shape (all three flagged together as a real documented failure case).
- **⚠️ Master-switch differentiation rule**: when a switch bank includes one "master" switch that cuts power/lighting to the entire unit, it must be physically set apart from the regular switch group — a different height/position, and visually distinct in appearance — specifically to prevent it being pressed by accident. Corroborates and sharpens this store's existing switch-panel guidance.
- **⚠️ "Appliance parking zone" (паркинг техники) correctly defined, with an explicit negative example of what it is NOT**: a parking zone is an *extension of the main countertop at the same height/level*, used for small appliances used *infrequently* (a juicer, a bread maker) — kept plugged in and ready but out of the main workflow. **It is not any recessed/sunken countertop nook** — a documented misuse in this project sunk a nook lower than the main countertop and mislabeled it a "parking zone," when it functioned as neither a real parking zone nor a usable prep surface, and additionally created an unnecessary sharp exterior corner. This sharpens an existing store concept with a clear "what it is vs. isn't" distinction.
- **⚠️ Kitchen cook-zone continuity rule**: when a fixed obstruction (e.g. a ventilation shaft) sits within a kitchen run, don't split the cook zone (between sink and cooktop) around it into two disconnected halves — push the entire run outward past the obstruction instead, keeping the zone continuous. Splitting doesn't meaningfully "gain" space; it breaks the zone's actual usability, creates an unnecessary sharp corner, and can leave an unusably narrow gap (documented case: a dish-drying gap too narrow to fit a single plate).
- **Sink/mixer handedness preference**: this practitioner prefers washing dishes with the mixer/faucet positioned to the *left* of the sink basin, not the right — `single-account` stated preference, not a universal ergonomic law, but worth recording as a documented practitioner opinion.
- **⚠️ Drawer-vs-flip-up-cabinet-door preference, with reasoning**: prefer full-extension, straight pull-out drawers over hinged "flip-up" (upward-swinging) cabinet doors that don't open to their full extent — the flip-up type is functionally inconvenient (can't fully open, effectively wasting the storage space behind it).
- **⚠️ Nested double-drawer durability caution**: a single drawer-front fascia containing two smaller drawers nested inside looks sleek, but has a poor leverage ratio — the entire large drawer front must be pulled out (a long lever arm) just to access a small item stored in the inner drawer, and repeated high-leverage opening wears the mechanism out faster than a normal single-purpose drawer would. A real durability/mechanism-longevity caution against this specific "designer" drawer configuration.
- **⚠️ Bedroom door-reveal correction: 10cm, not 30cm.** An oversized reveal (30cm) on the side a bedroom door pushes toward wastes space that should instead go to an adjacent closet — the correct reveal on that side is only 10cm, maximizing the wall run available for storage. A specific, corrected numeric figure distinct from this store's more general reveal-minimum guidance.
- **⚠️ Baseboard-integrated electrical distribution caution**: routing outlets, TV coax, and network/twisted-pair cabling through a baseboard channel system (rather than to fixed wall-mounted points at furniture height) is explicitly criticized — the resulting problem is a furniture-placement conflict: there's no way to know in advance exactly where a TV or other furniture piece should sit if its power/data connections are scattered along a low baseboard run rather than fixed at the wall location the furniture will actually occupy. **General caution: plan electrical/data points at their intended furniture-height locations, not as a length of baseboard-channel capacity to be tapped anywhere convenient.**
- **⚠️ Combined wall-thickening technique for tub-ledge removal + mixer-reach improvement**: thickening a wall by ~10cm to shift a bathtub's outer edge forward simultaneously eliminates an awkward horizontal ledge/shelf next to the tub (a hard-to-clean, bacteria-collecting surface) **and** makes a wall-mounted mixer easier to reach from within the tub — a single ~10cm wall adjustment solving two unrelated problems at once.
- **Extreme documented multi-switch QC failure case**: a real bedroom had **5 switches at its entry plus 6 more elsewhere (11 total across two adjoining rooms)**, none clearly labeled by function — a striking, citable real-world instance of this store's existing single-switch-per-room-exit principle being violated to an extreme degree.
- **Water-supply-connection-point red flag**: an exposed, undressed cluster of shutoff valves (rather than a proper concealed/finished connection node) is flagged as substandard finish quality for a premium-tier project — a checkable red flag (visible bare valves rather than a dressed/concealed installation) worth looking for during any handover inspection, even though this source doesn't spell out the full positive specification for what a "proper" installation looks like.
- **Decorative accent-lighting-over-headboard segment-tier caution**: strong decorative backlighting directly above a bed's headboard looks striking in photos (useful for rental-listing marketing appeal) but is genuinely uncomfortable for actual daily/nightly use and reliably goes unused once lived with. **Independently corroborates this store's existing segment-tier-dependent design-choice theme** (from `D8t1ADisUE8`'s premium-tier furniture-centering exception) — here, the same "looks great for marketing, bad for daily life" tradeoff applies to accent lighting specifically, and the source explicitly frames it as an own-use-vs-rental distinction rather than a universal rule.
