## Genuinely New Content

1. **⚠️ Heated-floor-under-tile repairability technique, genuinely new mechanism for this store**: if an electric heated-floor cable/mat sits directly under tile, removing a cracked or damaged tile for replacement later risks cutting/damaging the heating cable during demolition. **Fix**: after installing the electric heated-floor element, pour a protective cement/self-leveling layer *over* the cable/mat before tiling — this buries and protects the heating element, so individual tiles can later be removed and replaced without risking damage to it.
2. **⚠️ T-molding-threshold mechanism at a laminate-to-rigid-material junction, extends this page's existing T-molding construction content with the underlying "why"**: at a laminate-to-tile (or any laminate-to-rigid-material) junction, neither gluing the laminate rigidly to the tile nor sealing the joint with sealant alone works — laminate is a floating floor that keeps moving with temperature/humidity, so a rigid glue bond at that junction will eventually separate, and a sealant-only joint will crack from the same ongoing movement. **A T-shaped threshold strip is stated as the only viable solution for this specific material pairing**, not simply an aesthetic default.
3. **Laminate expansion-gap minimum, a second data point for this store**: minimum **5mm** perimeter damper gap for laminate specifically — a distinct figure from this page's existing cork-flooring 4-5mm minimum and the typically-executed 8-12mm baseboard-covered range, recorded as a separate material-specific spec, not merged with either.
4. **⚠️ Real glue-staining-under-finish defect mechanism and its mitigation, genuinely new for this store**: when installing glue-down parquet/engineered board/parquet-plank flooring, adhesive that gets onto the board's face during installation and is wiped off can still etch/stain the underlying lacquer or oil finish invisibly at first — the defect only becomes visible later, viewed at a raking angle against light, and requires a full re-lacquer of the entire floor to fix. The source states this happened to the company itself at some point. **Stated mitigation**: only let dedicated parquet specialists ("паркетчики"), not general flooring installers, handle glue-down parquet/engineered-board/parquet installation specifically to avoid this defect.
5. **Oil-finish vs. lacquer-finish distinction for parquet/parquet board, a genuinely distinct finish category from this page's existing Kodolov lacquer-subtype content**: a true oil finish feels noticeably better tactilely underfoot than any lacquer finish, but requires ongoing maintenance (periodic oil reapplication) — the source states this is why oil finish is comparatively rarely used in practice despite the superior feel, distinct from Kodolov's existing four lacquer-subtype taxonomy (which doesn't cover a raw oil finish as its own category).
6. **Discrete-plywood-pad leveling technique specifically for parquet/solid-wood (массив) installation**: solid wood flooring has no plywood backing of its own (unlike engineered/parquet board, which already includes it) — plywood pads are laid down discretely (cut into pieces, with damper gaps between each piece) directly on the screed first, ground flat, and only then is the solid-wood board installed on top of that leveled plywood base.
7. **Quartz-vinyl thickness-as-damper tactile-detection nuance**: thicker quartz-vinyl provides real extra cushioning/softness underfoot, but this isn't detectable by pressing on it with a finger — it's only perceptible when actually walking on it. Thicker quartz-vinyl is also cited as improving heat retention, more relevant to a private house than an apartment.
