## Mistakes / Warnings — Three Named Materials-Purchase Fraud Schemes

- **⚠️ Scheme 1 — foreman ("прораб") return-skimming, named as the most common variant among budget-segment ("эконом") foremen**: the client gives the foreman an advance to purchase materials; the foreman orders a full truckload from a real supplier (the source names "Петрович," a real Russian building-materials chain, as the reference example). When the truck arrives and unloading begins, the foreman has the driver return part of the load — **if the foreman paid for the materials on his own card, the refund for the returned portion goes back to his own card, not the client's**, and the client — who wasn't present at receiving/checking-in the delivery — has no way to detect the discrepancy, since the foreman retains invoices only for the portion that was actually delivered to the site. **Stated mitigation implied directly**: the client must personally be present at material receiving/check-in to catch this.
- **⚠️ Scheme 2 — fake below-market wholesale reseller found via an open classifieds site (Avito)**: a "seller" advertises a specific material at a price meaningfully below the named retail chain's own price (the video's own illustrative example: plaster ~300 RUB vs. ~450 RUB at "Петрович" — an illustrative comparison only, no unit/quantity given, not extracted as a real data point), claiming wholesale/insider pricing and explicitly offering "pay only on delivery, no risk to you" to defeat the buyer's own stranger-danger instinct. **Mechanism as described (transcript is ambiguous on one link in the chain — see Assumptions)**: the scammer arranges for a real delivery truck from the legitimate supplier to actually arrive at the buyer's address (using his own contact number as the order contact, so the driver calls him, not the buyer, on arrival); once the buyer visually confirms a real, full truck is present, the scammer calls the buyer to request the payment transfer; the buyer — reassured by seeing a genuine delivery — transfers the money by card directly to the scammer, who then vanishes (discarded SIM card, deleted ad) before the buyer can determine whether the material was actually released to them. **Explicit real-world corroboration cited**: the speaker references a real, recent arrest of two people running this exact scheme in St. Petersburg, while noting that two arrests are "a drop in the ocean" relative to how many victims never recover their money or find the perpetrator.
- **⚠️ Scheme 3 — buying real, stolen materials, with a named criminal-liability risk to the buyer, not just a financial one**: same setup as Scheme 2 (classifieds ad, below-market price, "pay on delivery") but here the delivered materials are genuinely real and are actually handed over to the buyer — the catch is that the materials were **stolen from a construction site by an organized group and resold through classifieds**. **Two named outcomes depending on payment method, stated as a direct causal branch**: (1) if the buyer paid by traceable card transfer, investigators recovering the stolen goods can generally establish the buyer purchased in good faith without knowledge the goods were stolen — the buyer becomes a witness, loses the money paid, but avoids criminal exposure, at the cost of time/stress dealing with the legal process; (2) **if the buyer paid in cash with no receipt/proof of purchase, proving good-faith purchase becomes very difficult, creating real risk of criminal liability** (potential prison time) for receiving/handling stolen property, on top of losing the money paid and needing to fund a legal defense.
- **⚠️ General stated warning, framed directly by the speaker as the video's own thesis**: classifieds-site below-market-price material offers are consistently framed as bait for gullible buyers ("замануха для доверчивых граждан") — trying to "outsmart the market" by buying significantly under retail price on an unverified open listing carries risk disproportionate to the savings, escalating from simple financial loss (Scheme 1/2) to potential criminal exposure (Scheme 3).
- **Practical mitigation implied across all three schemes (not stated as a single unified rule, but consistent across them)**: pay traceably (card, not cash) whenever the counterparty/goods provenance can't be independently verified, and be physically present for material delivery/check-in rather than delegating it entirely to a foreman or an unverified third party.
