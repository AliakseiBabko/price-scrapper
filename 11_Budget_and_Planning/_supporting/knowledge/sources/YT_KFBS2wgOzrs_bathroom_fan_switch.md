## Durable Facts

- **Rule 1**: the exhaust-fan switch for a toilet/WC (same logic extended to a bathroom) should always be located **inside** the room it serves, not in the hallway outside. Reasoning: most people don't want the fan running every single use, so an inside switch makes it optional at the point of decision; a hallway-mounted switch forces an "open door, flip switch, close door" motion every time it's needed. `unverified`, plausible UX reasoning, single-account.
- **Anti-pattern — door-open sensor + delayed auto-off timer** (e.g., fan triggers on door-open, auto-shuts-off ~5 minutes after door closes): argued against because (a) the sensor is impractical to install (wiring under tile) and prone to failure from humidity, and (b) it removes manual control — no way to turn the fan on early if needed immediately, and no way to turn it off early either. `unverified`, single anecdote (source: "someone told me about this today").
- **Anti-pattern — humidity-sensor-only control**: argued against because a fan that triggers purely on ambient humidity can activate unexpectedly during activities where noise/draft is undesirable (e.g., a hot bath, bathing a sick child where a draft risks a chill) and may fail to turn on/off exactly when wanted. General principle stated: automation should only ever be used for the **OFF/timeout** function of a fan, never for the **ON** trigger — unexpected automatic activation (noise, sudden draft) is framed as a comfort/trust problem. The speaker generalizes this same caution to other auto-triggered home devices, citing motion-activated lighting as another example of automation that can startle occupants (e.g. sudden light while lost in thought, or light triggered late at night while trying not to wake others). `unverified`, opinion generalized from anecdote.

- **Three recommended fan-switching configurations** (stated as the only ones recommended):
  1. Simple manual switch inside the room, independent of the light switch.
  2. Fan wired to activate together with the light switch, but with its **own separate manual switch** as well — so fan can run without light, or light without fan.
  3. Manual push-button start with a **~60-minute** run timer that auto-shuts the fan off (automation only on the OFF side, never auto-triggered ON).
