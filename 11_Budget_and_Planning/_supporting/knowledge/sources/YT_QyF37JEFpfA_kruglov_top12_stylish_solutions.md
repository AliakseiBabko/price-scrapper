## Genuinely New Content

**Wood slat/rail panels — vertical-vs-horizontal contrast, new specific
mechanism**: vertical slats visually stretch a room (taller-feeling
ceiling, larger-feeling volume) — the standard reason for choosing them
in a small room; **horizontal slats lose that stretching effect
entirely and also become a genuine dust collector**, since dust settles
on each individual horizontal rail's top face. **New concealment
benefit**: decorative slat/rail panels are an effective way to hide
revision/access hatches (plumbing or electrical) almost seamlessly —
the hatch's own seams disappear into the repeating rail pattern.

**Slat/rail room-dividing partitions — a distinct new failure mode from
this store's existing wall-panel dust content**: a rail partition (e.g.
separating a bedroom sleeping zone from a living/sofa zone) is normally
fixed permanently to both floor and ceiling — **committing the
household to living with a purely decorative divider for years**, since
removing or replacing it later isn't simple. The presenter's own stated
preference is a glass sliding partition instead, specifically because it
can later be closed off with an actual door in the same style — not
possible with a fixed rail structure. **Dust mechanism, distinct from
the wall-panel case above**: dust accumulates between the individual
vertical rails at floor level, and a robot vacuum physically cannot fit
between them to clean it — only manual cleaning (by the household or
domestic help) can.

**⚠️ Floor-standing bathtub mixer fragility, new to this store's
freestanding-tub content**: a floor-mounted mixer for a freestanding tub
is typically **~80cm tall** and anchored only at the floor — its long
lever arm can be **snapped off by a single hard lean or bump**,
especially with children or large pets in the household. **Fix**: build
a sturdy TC-profile-framed box around the mixer's base and internal
plumbing — fully solves the fragility problem, but is an added
construction step worth planning for, not something to discover after
the fact.

**⚠️ Concealed-door finishing-cost figure and concealed-baseboard price
multiplier — new concrete figures, extending this store's existing
Concealed_Door_Considerations cost content**: the full finish-work
package for one concealed door (painting both faces, sealing the
partition-side joint, prepping and painting the opposite-side reveal
with fiberglass mesh etc.) can cost **≈20,000 RUB (≈$210, trailing
6-month USD/RUB mean before 2025-02-28, 97.0314 RUB/USD)** per door — a real, easily
underbudgeted line item distinct from the door hardware/leaf cost
itself. **Concealed-mount baseboard, the type this store's existing
compatibility list already pairs with concealed doors, costs
4-5× a standard rigid MDF baseboard** — at a real linear footage (the
source's example: 200 running meters across an apartment), this
multiplier meaningfully inflates the total renovation budget beyond
what a per-meter sticker-price comparison alone would suggest.

**Soft/upholstered decorative wall panels — new durability/repairability
mechanism distinct from this store's existing rigid-panel content**:
soft panels cannot be spot-repaired — any damage requires replacing the
entire affected panel, and even a color-matched replacement panel can
visibly mismatch an aged/faded original due to gradual UV/wear fading
over time, forcing a full-wall panel replacement rather than a patch.
**Headboard-specific wear case**: a full upholstered-panel headboard
wall wears/discolors specifically at the head-contact point for anyone
who reads or reclines against it regularly — a slow, predictable failure
point unique to that usage pattern. Pets and children in the household
substantially accelerate general damage risk on soft panels.

**Rigid decorative panel calibration/relief nuances — new to this
store**: some rigid panel products (MDF, gypsum, duropolymer) arrive
poorly factory-calibrated, so adjoining panels don't align perfectly and
require extra on-site sanding/fitting labor, priced roughly in
proportion to the painter's actual time spent correcting it. **Relief-
orientation dust rule**: choose a vertical relief pattern over a
horizontal "wave" relief specifically to reduce dust accumulation — a
horizontal wave profile visibly re-accumulates dust within about 2 hours
of a thorough cleaning, a real day-to-day livability cost distinct from
the panel material choice itself.

**⚠️ Floor-level shadow profile ("теневой плинтус"/"напольный теневой
профиль") — extends this store's existing "doesn't perform the
baseboard function" finding with new mechanism and staging detail**:
beyond not protecting walls, this profile is a genuine dust/dirt trap —
a robot vacuum can't clean it at all except with a narrow brush-nozzle
accessory some (not most) manufacturers offer. **Floating-flooring-
compatible spring-loaded variant exists but is unproven**: no long-term
track record yet on reliability. **⚠️ Renovation-sequencing violation, a
new mechanism**: this profile's internal hardware is installed at the
rough-electrical/plumbing stage, but the finish flooring only goes down
at the very end of the project — meaning the chase concealing this
profile must be cut into the wall *after* finish flooring is laid,
forcing painters to return and refinish **80-90% of the apartment's
walls** a second time. The presenter's own stated personal opinion:
would never use this product for exactly this reason.

**⚠️ Linear/recessed lighting wall-to-ceiling seam cost — a new figure,
complementing this round's own transformer-centralization finding**:
achieving a genuinely clean, invisible seam where a linear light channel
transitions from a wall into the ceiling requires a skilled plasterer's
work and costs **10,000-15,000 RUB (≈$100-$150) per seam** — trivial for
one or two seams, but scales into a real budget line at 15-30 seams
across a whole apartment.

**Loggia-as-functional-office requirements, extending this store's
existing general "loggia as functional space" content with concrete
criteria**: a loggia genuinely functions as a usable home office only
with blackout curtains, a 3-4-chamber (thick-profile) double-glazed
window unit, and full thermal insulation — **just placing a desk and
chair on an insulated loggia does not make it a usable office**: without
those specific conditions, direct sun glare, street noise, and outdoor
distractions (the source's own example: children playing football under
the window) undermine actual working comfort.

**Water-vapor "fireplace" humidifier — a new device category for this
store**: a decorative unit that produces a realistic flame-like visual
effect using only water (no real combustion, distinct from a bio-fuel
fireplace), can be built into furniture, and can be integrated into a
smart-home system — offered as a natural humidifier alternative with a
stronger aesthetic payoff than a plain humidifier appliance.

**⚠️ Yandex Alice smart-home ecosystem scale figure — a new number
complementing this round's own smart-home pair**: as of this video, the
Yandex Smart Home app lists **260 registered device-manufacturer
companies** compatible with Alice — offered as evidence of the
ecosystem's practical maturity for a first-time adopter.
