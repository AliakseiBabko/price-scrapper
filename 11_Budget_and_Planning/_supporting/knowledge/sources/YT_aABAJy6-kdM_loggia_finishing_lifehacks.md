## Balcony / Loggia — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Zemskov's seamless-corner technique for PVC panel cladding (inner and outer corners), avoiding a separate corner trim profile**: cut a groove in the panel's back rib with a utility knife where it needs to bend, heat the bend a few times and wipe with a clean glove, then fold and install. **For outer corners specifically**: the same back-rib-cutting approach, but more cuts (grooves) produce a smoother bend, and — per Zemskov — no heating is needed even in cold weather, and the panel won't crack. His stated payoff: faster, cheaper, and better-looking than using a separate corner joint profile.
- **Zemskov's technique for hanging a heavy item on PVC panel cladding at a point with no pre-planned backing block**: drill into the wall behind the panel for a thick-walled dowel/wall anchor; **insert it but don't drive it fully flush — leave it protruding ~2mm past the panel's finished face**; then mount the item with a screw and washer through the panel into the dowel. The dowel's thick wall acts as a rigid sleeve/bushing that stops the panel from flexing or crushing as the screw is tightened. Per Zemskov, this lets even a wall-mounted TV or a mini-fridge be safely hung on the cladding after the fact, without disassembling the whole finished wall to add a proper backing block.
- **Zemskov's technique for mounting an outlet directly into PVC panel cladding where there's no room for a standard back box**: before installing the panel, screw the outlet to small blocks of thick plastic at its mounting points, driving the screws through from the panel's back side — then install the panel with the outlet already attached. Result, per Zemskov: an outlet secure enough that normal plug insertion/removal won't loosen or dislodge it, versus mounting an outlet directly to bare panel material (his stated failure mode: one careless motion and the whole outlet pulls free with the plug).
- **Zemskov's fast cosmetic-repair technique for a scratched or punctured PVC cladding panel**: rather than the multi-day full-panel-replacement repair (disassemble the whole run back to the damaged panel, replace it, reassemble), use a hole saw sized to match the cladding's own round ventilation/convection grille holes (~4cm diameter) to cut out the damaged area, then glue a matching grille over the hole with universal adhesive, pressing and holding ~10 seconds. Per Zemskov this both hides the damage in minutes and, as a side benefit, slightly improves under-panel ventilation.
