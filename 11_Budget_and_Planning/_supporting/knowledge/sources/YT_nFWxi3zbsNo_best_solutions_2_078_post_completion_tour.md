## Durable Facts

- **Server/network closet convention**: a dedicated small closet/panel area receives all incoming cabling — twisted pair (Ethernet, typically both ends run in), fiber optic if used, and the TV-distribution signal cable — and redistributes from there to the whole apartment. Houses the TV signal patch panel, router, and network-attached storage (NAS) together. `confirmed`, standing company convention.
- **Voltage-protection relay (реле напряжения) now recommended on every electrical panel**: one single-phase relay per phase — a single relay for single-phase service, three relays (one per phase) for three-phase service. Speaker notes a dedicated video on this topic was planned. `confirmed`, stated as a recent (as of this 2019 video) standing practice change.
- **Diffuse LED ceiling light panels** ("световые панели") are presented as giving dense, bright, glare-free light output, contrasted later with a living-room application (see below).
- **Single-loop ("единым контуром") underfloor heating under laminate flooring is a real, roughly 50/50 failure risk** — the speaker states this explicitly as a known coinflip based on recent-years practice: laminate over a single-loop heated-floor zone has "50% odds" of buckling/warping. Using quartz-vinyl tile instead of laminate mitigates but does not eliminate this risk, especially in irregularly-shaped rooms. **A genuinely new, safety/defect-relevant heuristic for this store** — no prior source states an explicit failure-rate estimate for laminate-over-underfloor-heating. `confirmed` as the practitioner's own stated risk assessment, `single-account`, `unverified` as an industry-wide statistic (no source cited for the 50% figure).
- **Sockets mounted in window reveals/slopes must be anchored as securely as a normal wall-mounted socket** — the box must not pull loose from the reveal material when a plug is removed; described as a specific quality-control check (a loose insert base moving with the plug when pulled out is a defect sign).
- **Furniture-layout-before-electrical-points sequencing, worked example**: determine the single correct furniture layout first (driven by structural constraints like a door/entry zone that fixes where a bed can and can't go), only then measure the specific customer (height, weight, habits) to size nightstands and their height, and only then place electric points precisely centered above/beside the nightstand at a defined offset — explicit rejection of a "sprinkle outlets everywhere, hope they land right after furniture placement" approach. Result stated as: outlets are never hidden behind a moved nightstand, switches are never trapped behind a headboard. Corroborates and extends this store's existing centerline-referencing electric-point technique with the "furniture layout must be locked first" prerequisite step made explicit.
- **Correctly installed windows + adequately-sized convector heaters prevent window condensation even at outdoor temperatures around -15 to -20°C** — demonstrated on camera with a dry convector grille at those temperatures. Practitioner attributes the *primary* cause of window condensation to window installation quality, and states that convector grille covers only mildly reduce convection (not the meaningful factor some clients worry about) as long as radiators give full heat output — helped by an insulated duct/enclosure behind a false wall that limits heat loss to the void. Removable grilles allow radiator maintenance and easier cleaning while looking better than an exposed radiator. `confirmed`, directly demonstrated on camera, `single-account`.
- **Provide independent bedside lighting with a separate switch on each side of the bed, even when not initially requested** — rationale given: watching TV in total darkness under only a bright screen is uncomfortable/eye-straining, but a single centered ceiling fixture used as a "night light" compromise reflects visibly in the TV screen and interferes with viewing; independent per-side bedside lamps solve both problems (comfortable ambient light, no screen reflection) and let one person read/watch while the other sleeps. Speaker explicitly calls this one of the best solutions in this project even though the actual client declined bedside fixtures/switches here.
- **"Technical design" for appliance niches (worked example: washing machine)**: know the exact appliance dimensions in advance so the door-swing clearance, wall setback, and casing/trim fit are all planned around the real unit — casing must not hit the wall while the appliance still has enough clearance to open/close and be accessed.
- **Never use water-fed (hydronic) heated towel rails "without genuine necessity" — use electric towel warmers instead.** Reasoning given: electricity cost for an electric unit is trivial compared to water-damage repair cost (to the unit's own apartment and the neighbor below) from an eventual hydronic-line leak, which the speaker states as a near-certainty over time ("they always leak eventually, it's only a matter of time"), not merely a risk. `confirmed` as stated practitioner opinion/practice; framed with a stated (not measured) cost-comparison mechanism.
- **Always include a small sink even in a tight bathroom that also has a bidet/hygiene shower ("гигиенический душ")** — reasoning: after using the hygiene shower a person needs to wash their hands, and having to touch a dirty door handle or walk to another room's sink with wet/unclean hands to rinse is worse than a cramped but present sink. A concrete, checkable planning rule with a stated mechanism — genuinely new to this store (no prior source addresses hygiene-shower + sink pairing).
- **Living-room ceiling light panels as a chandelier alternative**: barely visible in daylight/bright ambient light but capable of near-full-room flood lighting when dark — pitched specifically at clients who don't care about a chandelier as a decorative/social focal point (contrasted with clients who do value chandelier sparkle/social ambiance, for whom this solution is explicitly *not* recommended).
- **Cable-channel provisioning for AV**: run a dedicated conduit/channel for the HDMI cable between a receiver/set-top box and the TV, plus a second horizontal channel for a subwoofer cable when a subwoofer is part of the system, so no visible cable runs between components.
- **Avoid installed audio wall-outlet plates for speaker wiring — leave bare capped wire stubs behind the wall instead.** Reasoning given: if the customer later removes the speakers, an installed audio outlet plate becomes a permanent visible eyesore ("stands out like a sore thumb") stuck in the middle of a wall with nothing to plug into it, whereas a bare wire stub can simply be cut short, tucked, and the wallpaper patched invisibly. Additional connector pairs (as an outlet plate requires) also mildly degrade audio signal quality. `confirmed`, stated company default practice with two distinct reasons given.
- **Non-textured "paintable wallpaper" ("обои под покраску") recommended over painted plaster for repair-prone/high-traffic wall zones** — real example: a wall section damaged during furniture move-in. Reasoning: thick, absorbent, texture-free wallpaper can be locally spot-repainted without leaving a visible patch/blotch (because of even paint absorption across old and new paint), unlike a painted plaster wall, which shows patch marks after a partial repaint. Extends to a general product-selection rule: if the client wants a Venetian-plaster look, use paintable wallpaper that imitates the Venetian-plaster texture rather than real plaster (or a painted-wall imitation), for the same spot-repair benefit — but explicitly *only* wallpaper genuinely designated "for painting," nothing else.
- **In-unit TV can display building security-camera feeds** (4 cameras in the example: building entrance, parking area, elevator lobby, front door) with one click — framed as a practical convenience (checking a parked car, or watching a child walk from the elevator lobby to the apartment door) rather than a luxury gadget.
- **Deliberately multiplying switch points at different logical locations is a feature, not laziness** — direct response to a recurring viewer criticism ("won't this many switches make you lazy") — the practitioner explicitly distinguishes deliberate physical/fitness inconvenience (exercise) from *unnecessary domestic friction* (fumbling for the right switch out of many undifferentiated ones), arguing the two should never be conflated.
- **Never clad an opening/archway/portal edge in natural stone/brick veneer if it will meet a standard baseboard** — real client-requested case where the practitioner refused a natural-stone-brick request specifically because natural stone doesn't join cleanly with a baseboard at that transition. **Standard practice instead: trim all openings, portals, and arches with interior-door casing extensions ("доборы межкомнатных дверей")** — including the loggia-opening reveal casing after joining a loggia, extending this store's existing loggia-join content. Cited cost: roughly 5x a plain PVC corner-trim baseline (~2,000 RUB), still framed as "not that expensive" relative to the visual-quality improvement, and stated to visually pair much better with a built-in kitchen/countertop than PVC corner trim. `confirmed`, one concrete price data point (RUB, 2019).
- **Workstation electrical provisioning checklist**: quick-access phone-charging outlets, underfloor-heating thermostat, dedicated task-lighting switch, and multiple outlets sized for a stationary desktop computer, plus general cable-routing provision — a compact, reusable checklist for any home-office/desk zone.
- **A kitchen niche will almost always end up used as an appliance "parking spot" (multicooker, kettle, coffee maker), not as general storage** — regardless of the niche's originally intended use, the practical outcome is consistent enough that the practitioner recommends always adding a couple of outlets to any kitchen niche as a default, independent of the client's stated storage plan.
- **Vacuum-cleaner outlet mounting height: ~1.1 m** — two stated reasons: (1) avoids bending down to plug in, (2) at that height the cord tends to release cleanly ("flick out") rather than yank the plug hard when the vacuum is dragged across the floor away from a floor-level outlet, reducing repeated-plug-stress wear. A specific, checkable ergonomic figure — genuinely new precise number for this store's vacuum-outlet content (a prior source in this store discussed entry-zone vacuum-charging outlets without a height figure).
- **Tile layout rule restated (bathroom)**: plan tile layout so no narrow sliver/offcut pieces occur anywhere in a room — all cut pieces should be more than half a full tile — corroborates this store's existing tile-layout-planning content, no new mechanism.
- **Kids-room bedside switch reachability**: a light switch reachable both at room entry and from the bed (lying down), so the occupant never has to get up to turn the light off — corroborates this store's existing bedside/two-way switch content, no new mechanism.
- **Ethernet wiring to every TV location is standard regardless of stated Wi-Fi sufficiency**, because modern Wi-Fi is described as not reliably delivering consistently high-quality video streaming compared to a wired connection — a wired Ethernet drop is run to every TV location as a default, independent of whether the client explicitly asked for it.
- **Real mid-project rework example — added a second switch group after initial single-switch design proved inconvenient**: the original design used one switch controlling one light loop logically (in via entry, out via the same path) but didn't account for a resident sometimes continuing to a different room (living room) instead of exiting — the team cut into already-plastered/wallpaper-ready walls to add a second switch point mid-project once this was noticed, rather than leave the inconvenient original design in place. Presented candidly as "even we make mistakes and fix them mid-build," not just as a client-facing polish story.
- **Switch must be positioned on the same side of a doorway as the door handle** — stated as a firm, non-negotiable rule ("once and for all") with a stated ergonomic reason: entering while holding the handle in one hand, the switch should be reachable by the same hand without needing to switch hands or reach across the doorway. A real on-camera correction example is given where an incorrectly-placed switch was identified and the practitioner says he "held everyone responsible accountable" for the error.
- **Socket-to-sink minimum-distance concern is presented as a largely outdated myth for modern sockets** — the practitioner attributes the original concern to 1960s-era aluminum-contact socket construction with genuinely poor water ingress protection, and argues modern IP-rated sockets are meaningfully better protected such that ordinary hand/face-washing splash near a sink-adjacent socket does not realistically pose a shock/short risk, demonstrated with an on-camera (comedic) test. `single-account`, `unverified` as a general electrical-safety claim — presented with a stated construction-era rationale but no cited standard/rating number, and demonstrated with a non-rigorous camera bit rather than a real test. Do not treat as a verified code-compliance claim; record only as this practitioner's stated professional opinion.
- **Furniture assembly is explicitly outside the renovation contractor's standard contracted scope** — in this project the client purchased and delivered furniture themselves; the crew only assisted informally. **Baseboard/trim installation and fitting around built-in kitchen and furniture IS explicitly part of standard contracted scope**, performed after furniture placement — the practitioner draws a clear scope boundary here: if furniture/kitchen installation is delayed past the crew's departure, the furniture/kitchen installer is then responsible for their own baseboard fitting, since that's no longer the renovation crew's job once they've left the site. A clear scope-of-work delineation useful for a self-managed project assembling its own trade sequence.
