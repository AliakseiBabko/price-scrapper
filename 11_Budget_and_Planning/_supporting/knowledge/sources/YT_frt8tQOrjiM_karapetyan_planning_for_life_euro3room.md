## Planning Rules — Durable Facts & Rules

- **⚠️ "Wet zone" (мокрая зона) constraint mechanism, stated generally**:
  bathrooms/WCs and kitchens are "wet zones." **If a non-residential
  space sits directly below your apartment**, wet-zone placement
  constraints can be ignored. **If a residential apartment sits below
  you** (as in this case), a wet zone can only be *expanded* by
  absorbing adjacent non-habitable space (corridor, home office/study,
  entry hall) — never by absorbing adjacent habitable space (living
  room, bedroom). This generalizes/corroborates the narrower "kitchen
  expansion into a habitable room" rule already recorded from this same
  channel's other video (`YT_NWLckaXH1_U`) — same underlying mechanism,
  stated here as the general wet-zone principle rather than one specific
  case of it.
- **Partition-movement decision method, concrete worked example**: before
  redesigning, map every partition into "can move" vs. "cannot move"
  using the wet-zone rule above — in this project: could NOT move the
  partition between guest WC and kitchen (both wet-zone-adjacent, no
  non-habitable buffer), could NOT move the partition between
  kitchen-living and the two bedrooms (wet-to-habitable boundary), could
  NOT move the L-shaped corridor partition (structural/fixed); COULD
  move partitions between guest WC and entry hall, kitchen-living and
  entry hall, entry hall and bedroom, walk-through wardrobe and
  bedroom(s), WC and walk-through wardrobe (wardrobe is non-habitable),
  walk-through wardrobe and entry hall, and the partition between the
  two bedrooms.
- **Entry hall must not be a walkthrough/thoroughfare space** — stated as
  a near-inviolable rule ("незыблемое правило"): residents circulating
  between rooms should not track dirty footwear across the entry zone;
  design a genuinely separate, self-contained entry hall with its own
  shoe/outerwear storage rather than letting it double as a pass-through
  corridor.
- **A walk-through (проходная) wardrobe/closet room is often a false
  economy**: in the original developer layout, a walk-through wardrobe
  room could fit only one ~2.8m run of closet — no better functionally
  than just putting an ordinary closet in the hallway/corridor it
  replaces, while the extra partition consumes floor area for corridor
  space with no storage benefit. Verdict: either remove the separating
  partition (converts to open corridor closet, same storage) or redesign
  so the space earns its keep with real additional storage capacity.
- **Quantified payoff of a full storage-focused replanning pass**: same
  77.1m² apartment — developer's original layout could fit a maximum of
  ~8.5–9 running meters of closet/wardrobe storage; the redesigned plan
  (minimized corridors/appendix-shaped dead space, corrected room
  geometry) achieved ~13.8 running meters — roughly +50-60% more linear
  storage from layout optimization alone, no added footprint.
- **Two full bathrooms (not one full bath + one WC-only) is argued as the
  correct default even in a modest apartment**, specifically because
  bathing/showering is the single most time-consuming daily routine —
  with only one washing location, other household members queue; a
  second full shower avoids this even if smaller than the primary
  bathroom. Practical application here: guest WC was expanded to include
  a proper shower stall rather than staying toilet+small-sink-only.
- **Correcting an L-shaped/irregular bedroom into a clean rectangle by
  absorbing a sliver of the neighboring room, worked example**: an
  L-shaped bedroom (with a small "appendix" corridor nook eating usable
  area) was straightened by aligning its far wall to the adjacent
  bathroom partition line and taking a shallow strip from the
  neighboring bedroom — result: a proper rectangular room fitting a
  3.9m-long wardrobe run and a bed that would not have physically fit in
  the original L-shaped footprint (the original closet position blocked
  it).
- **Home-office/nursery door placement decision, forward-planning
  rationale**: chose to place the office/future-nursery door opening off
  the kitchen-living room rather than the entry hall, specifically so
  parents could supervise a child more easily once the room converts to
  a nursery — a family-lifecycle-driven circulation decision, not purely
  spatial efficiency.
- **Open kitchen-living space, dining-table-against-wall caution
  (corroborates `YT_NWLckaXH1_U`)**: pushing a dining table flush against
  a wall in an open kitchen-living space is called out as producing a
  cramped "khrushchev-era small kitchen" ("хрущевка") look regardless of
  actual room size; if the kitchen is small, the practitioner's stated
  default is to remove the dividing wall entirely for a combined space
  rather than accept a wall-hugging table.

## Plumbing / Engineering coordination — Durable Facts & Rules

- **⚠️ Fixture-to-riser proximity rule, stated rationale**: place toilets
  as close as physically possible to the existing sewage riser
  ("канализационный стояк") to minimize horizontal run length of the
  110mm waste pipe — explicit reasoning given: longer horizontal runs on
  a 110mm drain line risk future waste-evacuation/clogging problems, not
  just cost/material savings. Applied to both bathrooms in this project;
  also applied to the kitchen sink (positioned close to the same stack)
  specifically to avoid drainage design problems.
- **Wall-thickening technique to conceal supply/drain lines**: where a
  sink or bathtub needs concealed water supply/drain routing that
  doesn't align with the existing riser, the practitioner built out
  ("нарастили") a thicker false wall section to hide the piping and
  concealed mixer valves, creating an incidental flat shelf on top
  useful for shampoo/toiletries.
- **⚠️ Equipment-access-via-cabinet-back technique, worked example**:
  where a hallway built-in cabinet's back panel backs onto a wall
  carrying the electrical panel, heating manifold ("коллектор
  отопления"), and water manifold, the practitioner (a) left a 150mm
  clearance gap between the wall and the cabinet's back panel
  specifically to fit the panel plus its DIN-rail-mounted breakers/power
  supplies (bulkier when mounted crosswise on a DIN rail), and (b) made
  the cabinet's back panel/facade removable from inside the cabinet, so
  all that equipment stays serviceable by opening the cabinet's back
  rather than needing a separate wall access panel or hatch. Framed
  explicitly as a "function alongside beauty" design principle, not
  incidental convenience.
- **Placing the laundry machine, dryer, and water heater in a hallway
  cabinet module directly adjacent to a bathroom** was chosen
  specifically because it let plumbing/drain lines route directly from
  the adjoining bathroom's existing wet-zone infrastructure, rather than
  running new lines further.
