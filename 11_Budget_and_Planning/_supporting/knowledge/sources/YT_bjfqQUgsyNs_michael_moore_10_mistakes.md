## Room Types and Sizing — Genuinely New Content

- **Two laundry formats, each with a distinct use case**: a **dedicated room** (best for a house/large apartment; recommended size **3.5–7 m²**; can absorb washing, drying, ironing, chemical storage, dirty-laundry storage, and even a shoe/dog-paw shower) vs. a **closet/niche format** (≤ ~2 m² footprint; washer, dryer, chemicals, and a small utility block fit inside a niche; the only format usable in a genuinely small apartment). The dedicated-room format is explicitly recommended over the closet format when there are children in the household, specifically to physically limit their access to stored chemicals — a distinct child-safety rationale not framed just as space efficiency. For a dog-owning household, the source recommends locating a dedicated laundry room on the ground/basement floor specifically for after-walk paw-washing convenience.

## Materials — Genuinely New Content

- **Moisture-resistant wall paint, mechanism stated**: ordinary paint (paired with e.g. laminate flooring) can deform over time from repeated water contact; moisture-resistant paint can be wiped with a damp cloth without absorbing water splashed on it.
- **Quartz-laminate (кварцевый ламинат) preferred over both tile and ordinary laminate for laundry floors and walls, three stated reasons**: (1) warmer underfoot than tile; (2) less brittle — the source's own example: if a mug is dropped, quartz-laminate is more likely to survive undamaged than tile; (3) fully water-resistant unlike ordinary laminate. Composition stated as ~75% ground stone, which the source also credits for high geometric/dimensional stability — cited as the reason it also uses it on **walls**, not just floors, in wet-adjacent rooms.

## Placement / Installation Rules — Genuinely New Content, Including a Direct Conflict With an Earlier Source

- **A washing machine must sit directly on the floor on a rigid, level base — never elevated on a plinth/socle** — stated as a hard installation rule (per the appliance's own instructions, per the source), because the base must be solid and level to handle the machine's spin-cycle vibration load. The source gives a real cautionary case: one project needed plinth-level lighting under a cabinet and had to mount the washer on a raised socle base to achieve it; they reinforced the plinth structure with a rebar frame to make it strong enough, but explicitly says **"I don't recommend doing this yourself"** and states every other project of theirs installs the machine directly on the floor. **This directly conflicts with this batch's [[YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials|Diskill/Liza Robizon source]], which recommends raising washer/dryer to chest height for elderly-accessibility reasons** — flagged as a genuine Perspectives disagreement (see routing note below), not resolved by this note.
- **Stacked washer-dryer sequencing rule, with a stated mechanical reason**: the washing machine always goes on the bottom, dryer on top — because the washer is heavier and bears higher structural load (it must extract water from a fully soaked load), while a dryer only handles an already-spun (much lighter) load.
- **A pull-out shelf between stacked washer and dryer is a load-transfer necessity, not a convenience add-on**: needed specifically so wet clothes can be pulled from the washer and set down immediately before loading into the dryer above, without a wasted extra trip.
- **Laundry-basket clearance below a front-loading dryer door**: the source flags forgetting to check this as its own distinct mistake — a tall laundry basket parked underneath a stacked unit can physically block the dryer's door from opening if the basket's height wasn't checked in advance.
- **Quality 2-in-1 combo units are acceptable only above a stated quality bar**: the source allows combo washer-dryer units for space-constrained cases but only "high-quality models that can actually do the job each machine does separately" — a de facto minimum-quality gate not previously recorded on this page.
- **A buffer/staging counter surface is called out as a specific missing-item mistake**: entering the room with an armful of laundry or chemicals with nowhere to set it down before opening the washer, or needing a surface to sort dried laundry and hunt for a matching sock — the source frames this as solvable with a dedicated island, table, or simply a pull-out shelf, not requiring a full counter run.

## Drying and Ironing — Genuinely New Content

- **Drying-cabinet mechanism detail, refrigerator-sized, beyond this batch's earlier description**: internal hangers allow bedding and bedspreads to be dried vertically (not just outerwear); explicitly suitable for delicate items the source says shouldn't go in a tumble dryer at all; and it dries wet winter jackets and boots after a walk, in addition to laundry.
- **Retractable/pull-out drying racks built into the cabinet itself** — a distinct sub-type from the floor-standing/wall-mounted racks already recorded from this batch's first source: these tuck back into the cabinet once laundry is dry, so they don't obstruct the room when not in use.
- **Heated floor recommended as a supplementary room-warming measure specifically to prevent the laundry room from accumulating ambient moisture**, in addition to (not instead of) a heated towel rail — a distinct rationale (moisture control, not comfort) from this page's other heated-floor mentions.
- **Ironing press (гладильный пресс) as a distinct appliance from a hand iron**: faster steaming/pressing than an iron, has multiple temperature settings, but costs substantially more — the source's explicit advice is to reserve its footprint in the room plan early, before finalizing shelving, precisely because it's easy to forget until the layout is already fixed.
- **Task lighting for ironing/sorting must be planned separately from general room lighting, at a specific stated color temperature**: 4000–5000K (daylight range) specifically because it makes stains on dirty laundry easier to spot — general ceiling light alone is explicitly called insufficient for this task.

## Storage — Genuinely New Content

- **Color-coded floor-mounted flip-top laundry baskets let you skip presorting entirely** — load directly into the color-matched basket at the point of use, then carry the whole basket to the machine at wash time, rather than sorting a mixed pile later.
- **Pull-out storage shelves cost more than fixed shelves but avoid reaching into a niche** — a stated cost/convenience tradeoff for laundry-room shelving specifically.
- **A laundry chute (белья провод)** — a large vertical shaft with an intake hatch on an upper floor and a collection basket at the bottom, used in multi-story houses where the laundry room sits in the basement — recorded as a real (if niche) architectural solution for a multi-floor home.

## Electrical — Genuinely New Content

- **A "master switch" for the whole apartment's lighting plus the iron's specific outlet must be wired through a dedicated contactor in the breaker panel, never through an ordinary wall switch** — the source states this explicitly as a wiring-topology requirement, not just a convenience feature; framed as a safeguard against leaving an iron plugged in and energized after leaving the house.
- **Outlet planning should be counted per planned appliance before finalizing the room**, listing drying cabinet, ironing board/press, vacuum charging dock, garment steamer station, and shoe dryer as the specific appliance types to count outlets for.

## Fixtures — Genuinely New Content

- **A sink in a dedicated laundry room serves pre-treating stains before a wash cycle, filling a bucket, and washing shoes** — the source frames a mini or deep sink as most valuable specifically for households with children (immediate stain pre-treatment) and states this directly as a parent-relevant feature.
- **A raised platform for a pet paw-washing shower/trap, ergonomic reason stated**: raising the trap avoids the user having to bend all the way down, protecting the back — not purely a drainage/plumbing consideration.
- **Combining the laundry room with the boiler/mechanical room is a real design pattern** the source calls out for a house project: plan the laundry room and boiler-room utilities together from the start of design so shared mechanical connections (water, drainage) serve both without duplicated runs; boiler equipment on one side, washer/dryer on the other.

## Closet-Format Laundry — Genuinely New Content

- **A location priority order for a closet-format laundry when a full room isn't available**: (1) a walk-in pantry/storage closet first choice — converting it yields something close to a real dedicated room; (2) a hallway or a second WC next; (3) a bathroom last, specifically because final placement still depends on where drainage/plumbing can actually be run, not preference alone.
- **Do not convert an ordinary wardrobe/clothing cabinet into a laundry niche — four distinct stated reasons**: (1) a washer needs clearance gaps on every side inside the cabinet so its vibration isn't transmitted into the cabinet structure, and door hinges can physically block the machine from sliding fully into the module if depth isn't calculated including hinge protrusion; (2) the module needs cut openings for the water supply, drain, and electrical connections, ideally routed via an adjacent module for serviceability; (3) the base under the machine must be a rigid floor connection — a standard wardrobe's own plinth/socle base is not installed under the machine's own footprint; (4) the enclosure needs planned top or bottom ventilation so humidity doesn't accumulate inside a sealed cabinet box.
- **Door-swing direction must match between the cabinet door and the appliance door, both ways**: the source calls a mismatched combination — where the cabinet door and machine door hinge from opposite sides, or the cabinet door blocks the machine's own detergent-drawer opening — "the most frustrating mistake," and separately flags that some appliance models don't support re-hanging the door to the other side at all, so this must be checked before ordering, not assumed fixable after installation.
- **A kitchen countertop must not overhang far enough to block a washer's detergent-drawer travel** when a washer is installed under a kitchen counter — a specific interference case distinct from the general depth-budgeting formula in this batch's second source.
