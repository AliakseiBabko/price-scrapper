## Design Concept — Why No Designer Can Give You "The Perfect White"

- **⚠️ The core claim: their studio has never reused the same paint article on two objects. Not once.** "Нет ни одного объекта, где бы мы использовали тот же самый артикул краски, как и на предыдущих объектах." The finished results read as the same beautiful warm white; **the articles behind them are entirely different.**
- **⚠️ The demonstration that makes it concrete**: take the paint used on object A and the paint used on object B, make two выкрасы, and view them side by side under identical conditions at home — **they will differ visibly in lightness, in saturation, and in pigment.** One may read "поросячье-розовая" and distinctly saturated, the other greyish, muted, even greenish. Neither is what you saw in the designer's photo.
- **⚠️ A named real failure case, which is the most checkable item here**: she found a warm white she loved on one project — **Little Greene "Flint"** — and carried it to the next project, where it came out "настолько отвратительно грязно-серовато-зеленоватым, что никаким теплом и уютом там и не пахло."
  - **This is a direct, useful tension with the 2026-08-30 colour batch**, where Vittoria Selina named three specific Little Greene shades (Clay 39, Rolling Fog 143, Rolling Fog Dark 160) as reliable go-to neutrals she reuses across objects. Same manufacturer, opposite methodological position. Route as a Perspectives block, not as a correction of either.
- **⚠️ The factors she lists as making a shade unrepeatable**: floor area; room configuration (an elongated room with one window vs. an elongated room with five windows along a wall); the storey; the glazing itself (facade glass differs); the view; the buildings opposite; window size and count; and the other shades already present elsewhere in the flat. She notes this is not even a complete list.
- **⚠️ A colour also differs between showroom, object, and phone** — the camera distorts it and then the viewer's screen distorts it again. Her live example is not even about walls: their crew photographed **the same floor covering from different angles** and it reads as two different colours, because light reflects off it into the camera differently. Independently corroborates the screen-distortion point from PRIDE (`S9F9TGVG4x8`) in the colour batch.
- **⚠️ The method: выкрасы, and expect two iterations, sometimes three.** Buy a first batch of sample pots, paint them, assess, **correct on the basis of what you actually see**, and buy a second batch. She says the second or third iteration is where people land on the right white for *their* flat, and that **correcting intelligently requires understanding pigment, saturation and lightness** — which is why she considers white genuinely difficult rather than a safe default. Explicit reassurance: designers go through the same iterations, and not because they are inexperienced.

## Design Concept — Fan Decks: RAL Is the Wrong Tool for Walls

- **⚠️ "Это самый ужасный, самый маленький веер, который только может быть"** — RAL is for painting plastic and metal constructions (her example: the aluminium profile of a partition), not walls. She notes essentially every question she receives is phrased as "what RAL is on the walls," and that **designers rarely pick up a RAL deck for wall colour at all.**
- **In ~99% of cases the reference is a paint manufacturer's own fan deck** — shades devised by the paint-makers' own designers. She has used an NCS deck once, to find a specific grey. **This independently corroborates ARCHWOOD's "сложные цвета" argument** from the colour batch: choose a ready-made complex shade from a manufacturer's own collection rather than tinting to a code out of a simplified catalogue.
- **⚠️ And the reason the RAL shortcut is attractive is itself a mistake**: people pick a RAL wall colour specifically so they can order cabinet fronts to the same code later. **She states flatly that this does not work and will produce разнотон with 100% certainty** — the same claim, from the same practitioner, as the on-site NCS experiment in `avRNMkNdOBs`. She reports repeatedly receiving messages from people who painted walls and fronts to one code and got an obvious mismatch — "заляпанные, им уже 15 лет" is how she describes the fronts reading — visible to anyone, not just a perfectionist.
