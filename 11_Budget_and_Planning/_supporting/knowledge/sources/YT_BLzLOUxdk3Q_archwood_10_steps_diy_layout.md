## Planning Rules / Design Process — Durable Facts & Rules

- **⚠️ 10-step DIY layout-planning method, full sequence**: (1) list every
  piece of furniture/fixture wanted plus its typical dimensions before
  laying out anything; (2) identify constraints (load-bearing
  walls/columns/pylons, ducts, risers, wet zones) vs. resources
  (removable partitions — corridors, halls, storage rooms — that can be
  reallocated toward a bathroom or kitchen); (3) determine cardinal
  orientation (affects natural light and view, used to decide where the
  main living zone goes); (4) legality-check every idea against local
  building/sanitary code before committing (see below); (5) strip the
  plan down to only truly immovable elements and re-evaluate freed
  space; (6) walk/visit the real apartment and decide zone placement
  from there; (7) color-code zones and place major furniture at true
  scale; (8) apply a "coziness" gut-check per zone (see below); (9)
  check circulation/traffic flow for every household member and pet,
  reconsider door placement/type; (10) apply horizontal-ergonomics
  clearance norms to remaining furniture/fixtures; (11) full-scale
  mockup validation on-site (see disagreement below).
- **⚠️ Constraint-vs-resource framing, explicit practitioner claim**: "I
  have never, in years of practice, seen an apartment that can't be
  improved at least somewhat" — resource space (removable partitions:
  corridors, halls, storage rooms) can always be reallocated toward a
  wet zone (bathroom/kitchen) if approached as "clay," not a fixed
  layout. A stated professional-mindset framing more than a checkable
  fact, but consistent with this channel's own worked 90m² case already
  on file.
- **Legality check corroborates existing wet-zone rule**: sanitary norms
  generally prohibit expanding a kitchen via a bathroom (or vice versa)
  and prohibit expanding habitable rooms via either wet zone —
  corroborates the wet-zone-expansion restriction already on file from
  Karen Badalyan (`YT_HpCg-xIrOlg`) and Karen Karapetyan
  (`YT_frt8tQOrjiM`, processed earlier this same session). Load-bearing
  walls: at most, a code-approved, engineered/reinforced opening can be
  cut — never full removal.
- **⚠️ "Coziness" psychological framing, a distinct sub-method**: coziness
  is defined as a felt sense of protection ("felt through the back and
  shoulders" — a primal instinct to have a wall/shelter behind oneself,
  near a hearth). Practical test: sit/lie in each planned position
  (bed, sofa, dining chair) and check what's visible around and *behind*
  you, and whether you're visible/exposed from unexpected sightlines
  (e.g., is the entry door or the bathroom door visible from the dining
  spot). A genuinely distinct heuristic from this vault's existing
  sightline/privacy content — worth cross-checking against
  `17_Design_and_Ergonomics` zoning content.
- **Door-placement rule**: a door should sit directly on a household's
  natural path between two zones, not simply open into a hallway by
  default — door type (swing vs. sliding) chosen per task/space
  constraint, not a fixed preference.
- **Light-transmitting door/partition inserts, or ribbon skylights, as
  the fix for an interior dark zone** created by an internal replan (a
  windowless zone resulting from subdividing a naturally-lit room).
- **⚠️ Explicit disagreement with an existing store entry — full-scale
  floor mockup validation**: this source's step 10 explicitly
  *recommends* taping/chalking partition and furniture outlines directly
  on the real floor (or drawing to true scale with tape-measure
  spot-checks if the space isn't accessible yet) and physically walking
  the mockup before finalizing a plan. This is the **opposite**
  conclusion from Sergey Kodolov (`YT_PJKk1BwtgVY`, already on file, same
  Durable Facts section), who explicitly *rejects* floor-taping as
  giving no genuine spatial sense and argues standardized
  clearance/opening-width norms are the reliable substitute instead. A
  real, checkable disagreement between two independent practitioners on
  the same specific technique — flagged, not resolved.
