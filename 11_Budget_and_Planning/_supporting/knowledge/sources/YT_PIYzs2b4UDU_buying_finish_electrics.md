## Durable Facts

- Buy finish electrics (outlets, switches, frames) roughly **1 week before installation** — i.e. at the wallpaper-hanging / finish-flooring / door-installation stage — not months in advance. Reasoning given: (a) items stored on-site for months risk damage, scratching, loss, or theft; (b) electric point counts/locations are reported to change constantly during a renovation (owners almost always *add* points, rarely remove them), so early purchases usually need re-buying anyway; by the wallpaper stage even designers no longer change the electrical plan, so a purchase then is described as reliably final. `single-account`
- Recommends counting electrics **after all rough-in stages are complete**, by physically walking each room and recording actual points into a table — rather than counting from the project drawing. Reasoning: most non-custom project drawings show point locations only symbolically, without full component specification, and kitchen electric point layout in particular is said to depend on the final kitchen furniture order, which is usually decided independently of the original drawing. `unverified`
- Three switch mechanism types exist, visually identical but mechanically different: (1) **standard switch** — one wire in, one wire out; cheapest; use where a light has only one control point. (2) **two-way/проходной switch** — one wire in, two wires out; used in pairs when a light needs to be controlled from 2 locations. (3) **intermediate/crossover/перекрестный switch** — two wires in, two wires out; required for every control point beyond two, when a light is controlled from more than 2 locations (the two end switches stay two-way, everything in between must be the crossover type). `unverified`, standard technical claim, consistent with the wiring explanation in a separate video in this batch (VXfpDJruyVs).
- Recommends buying **grounded outlets everywhere**, even in spots where the anticipated appliance (e.g. a floor lamp) has a non-grounded plug — reasoning: price difference between grounded and non-grounded outlets is small, and any outlet may later be used for a grounded-plug appliance (iron, other metal-body device). `unverified`
- Common buying mistake flagged: recording outlets and switches in a list without noting which frame (1-gang/2-gang/3-gang/etc.) each point needs, then guessing frame quantities at the store — frames are described as comparatively expensive, so should be counted with full precision in advance, point by point. `unverified`
- Recommends buying a **spare stock** of finish electrics beyond the exact count needed, because components fail or get scratched over time; the speaker states customers commonly return 3–5 years later wanting to buy a replacement outlet, switch, or frame, and if the exact original item has been discontinued, the whole room's points sometimes have to be replaced to keep them visually matching. `single-account`

## Numeric Data

Also framed as insurance against missing 1–2 points during the walkthrough count (e.g. hidden behind construction debris). `single-account`, specific to this speaker's stated practice; not cross-checked.
