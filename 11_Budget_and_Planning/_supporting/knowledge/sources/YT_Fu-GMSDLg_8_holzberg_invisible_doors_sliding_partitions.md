## Design Concept / Furniture — Durable Facts & Rules

**Sliding-partition/door mounting taxonomy** (this manufacturer's own
system, applies to both "invisible" hinged doors and sliding partitions):
track mounted directly to the ceiling with a visible baguette/trim; track
mounted to a wall with a baguette; or **track fully embedded/hidden
within the ceiling** — the cleanest, most minimalist look, with only
small decorative cover plates visible.

**⚠️ Pre-mount box ("предмонтажный короб") technique for the
fully-hidden-in-ceiling version — a distinct alternative to an embedded
backing board**: for this version, a pre-fabricated box/carcass is
installed during the rough-construction stage (on the bare structural
ceiling), suspended from threaded rods/studs ("шпилька") rather than
requiring any embedded backing plate at all. Box profile depth: **67mm
minimum**; if a deeper drop is needed (e.g. to integrate acoustic
treatment, embedded lighting), the box can be lowered via the threaded
rod to any depth — up to ~1m demonstrated in the company's own showroom
— **with no effect on structural stability**, and independent of whether
the finish ceiling surface itself is level/true.

**Two-stage installation sequence for the hidden-track version**: (1)
rough-construction stage — install only the pre-mount box (the most
critical/complex step, directly analogous to this manufacturer's
"invisible" hinged-door frame installation); (2) finishing stage — after
all flooring/wall/ceiling finish work is complete, install the actual
partition leaves, track, and decorative cover plates — a clean,
dust-free, no-cutting finish-stage step.

**Leaf size limits and play/wobble ("люфт") minimization**: up to 1.5m
leaf width, 3-3.2m height. **Ideal width for minimal mechanical play:
1200-1300mm** — wider leaves have measurably more play in the mechanism,
and can also be harder to deliver to a site; the source's own
recommendation is to divide an opening into leaves within this range
where possible.

**⚠️ Structural child-safety detail — impact load routed to the bottom,
not the rollers**: this system is engineered so that any impact or
rocking force (e.g. a child colliding with the partition on a scooter)
is absorbed at the *bottom* of the leaf, with the top roller mount
specifically isolated from that load. Worst case, the leaf can dislodge
from its lower guide pin but remains hanging from the top track — it
does not fall, even under a hard impact or a child's full weight applied
by "riding" the dislodged leaf.

**Telescopic ("book"-style two-leaf) system**: a "leading" leaf pulls a
"trailing" leaf when opened. Compared to the standard sliding
arrangement (leaves split the opening roughly 50/50 when open), this
telescopic version leaves **2/3 of the opening clear** in the fully-open
position — more compact stacking, same hidden-track installation method.

**Pocket/"cassette" wall-buildout sizing formula**: for a partition that
fully retracts into a newly-built stud wall (rather than remaining
visible when open), the added wall's minimum thickness is **~70mm**.
Sizing formula demonstrated: existing opening + ~50mm (leaf clearance/
gaps) + ~70mm (added stud wall) ≈ **190mm total buildup thickness** in
the company's own minimal reference example.

**⚠️ Flush-fit, no-visible-gap technique**: a U-shaped pre-mount box lets
the new stud wall be built flush over/around it with only a ~6mm
reveal visible; internally, a recessed pocket ("ниша") has its own dust
seal/wiper strip that reduces play so the leaf sits flush without a
visible gap when fully closed — omitting this recess leaves the leaf
slightly short of the wall with a visible gap. The tighter dust-seal fit
of this pocket version is also stated to give **slightly better sound
isolation** than the standard sliding version — described as only a
minor improvement, not real soundproofing.

**Floor-stopper hardware taxonomy** (relevant even for a "no floor
rail" partition, since a stopper is a separate single-point element, not
a track): (1) classic visible surface-mounted barrel/block stopper; (2)
**adhesive magnetic stopper**, used specifically where floor drilling is
not allowed — no drilling required at all; (3) **recessed/drilled
magnetic stopper**, this practitioner's own preferred/most-reliable
option — a metal rod on the leaf's underside snaps to a below-floor
magnet as the leaf reaches its closed position; minimal floor profile.
**⚠️ Recessed-stopper depth caution**: the stopper body recesses **42mm**
into the floor — a real risk of drilling into embedded floor-heating or
plumbing lines running through the screed at that depth; check before
drilling.

**Concealed baseboard system with integrated task lighting**: a
channel-and-insert profile (insert can be painted MDF or any material)
with no protruding element, letting a door/partition leaf swing or slide
a full 180° without being blocked by a baseboard — optionally integrates
an LED strip as wayfinding/night lighting along the floor line.

**Glass and profile finish options** (commercial/brand-tagged): frosted,
clear, mirror-coated, engraved, ribbed/textured glass, and a
metallized-fabric-inlay glass (same category used by Rimadesio/Lualdi
factories, per this source). Profile: black anodized aluminum standard
(matte), custom-paintable to any RAL/NCS color including gray and gold/
brass tones.

**⚠️ Real pricing (EUR, this company, region unresolved — no location
spoken at level 1)**: a pocket/cassette hidden partition without an
elaborate portal trim, leaf recessing fully into the pocket wall, wall
built flush to the leaf edge — **≈€750 turnkey**. A more elaborate finish
(visible reveal trim, per the showroom demo unit) adds **≈€300** more
(≈€1,050), varying by size/leaf thickness/casing choice. Structural
wall-prep and materials are billed separately. No USD conversion applied
— this project's currency-conversion tooling supports USD/RUB and
USD/BYN only, not EUR, and region is unresolved regardless, so a forced
conversion would add false comparative precision without resolving the
underlying region gap.

**Finish-technique tradeoff**: factory-painted (97% matte paint + matte
lacquer, notably scratch-resistant) vs. primed-only for on-site painting
to exactly match the surrounding wall color — this practitioner's own
preference is primed/on-site painting for the most seamless minimalist
look, despite factory paint being marketed as more durable; flags that
factory-paint texture will visibly differ from a painted wall's texture,
a real aesthetic-continuity consideration when choosing.

**Warranty**: 2 years (contractual); no claims reported yet on the
sliding-partition hardware/hinges specifically, per this practitioner.
