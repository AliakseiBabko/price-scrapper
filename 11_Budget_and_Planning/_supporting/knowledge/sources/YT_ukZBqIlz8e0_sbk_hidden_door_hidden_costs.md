## Doors / Trim — Concealed-Mount Door: Real Schedule and Cost Consequences

- **⚠️ Concealed-mount doors change the construction *sequence*, not just the door itself**: an ordinary door is installed after finish flooring/walls are complete. A concealed-mount door instead requires: (1) rough-only wall prep (base plaster/putty, no finish) *before* flooring goes down; (2) flooring installed and carefully protected; (3) **only then** can the aluminum door-frame-to-wall joint be finished — plastered/drywalled and reinforced against cracking, then fiberglass-wallpaper-reinforced — **meaning final wall paint/finish happens in two separate visits by the painting crew**, not one continuous pass.
- **⚠️ Real scheduling risk from this two-visit requirement**: the second (finish) painting visit may be delayed for weeks if the original crew is already committed to another job, and **not every painter is competent with this specific joint detail or willing to do "finishing touches" on someone else's partially-done work** — a real gap between "the base is ready" and "a qualified crew is actually available to finish it."
- **⚠️ Concrete cost breakdown, St. Petersburg pricing (level 1, spoken)**: painter's finish-joint work around one door averages **≈1,500 RUB per running meter**, roughly 10 running meters of joint per door (5m each side) → **≈15,000 RUB in joint-finishing labor per door alone**, before the door unit's own (higher-than-standard) price or the crew's extra site-visit costs. **Worked example**: a house with 10 such doors → **≥150,000 RUB in joint-finishing labor alone**, on top of the doors themselves.
- **⚠️ Wet-zone (bathroom) tile-reveal cost, same mechanism**: where a concealed-mount door meets a tiled wet-zone reveal, the corner must be tile-cut at a 45° miter — **≈4,000 RUB per running meter minimum**, roughly **≈20,000 RUB per side** in the source's worked example.
- **⚠️ Genuine mechanical limitation, not just a cost issue — door swing is physically capped below 90°+ by its own trim**: decorative moldings applied to a concealed door must themselves be mitered at 45° at the door's opening edge just for the door to open at all; even correctly mitered, **the door then only opens to roughly 90° (±a couple degrees)** — pushing it further **breaks the molding**. This must be accounted for at the design stage (furniture/traffic clearance planning), not discovered after installation.
- **⚠️ A second, independent swing-limiting mechanism: protruding (non-flush) baseboard**: any baseboard proud of the wall plane (including a slim 3-step profile, not just a thick 16mm one) will physically stop a concealed door's swing before it reaches its full intended arc — repeatedly forcing the door into the baseboard **breaks both** (chips paint on the door edge and the baseboard) and requires redoing both finishes. **The only fix that restores full 180° swing is a flush/hidden-mount baseboard** — itself an added cost (St. Petersburg pricing: **≈2,000 RUB per running meter installed with prep**, ≈**120,000 RUB** on a typical ~60-running-meter apartment).
- **⚠️ Practical takeaway, stated as the point of the video**: none of this makes a concealed-mount door a mistake in itself — but every one of these consequences (schedule, cost, and the real swing-angle limitation affecting furniture/clearance planning) should be disclosed and agreed with the client **at the design-project stage**, not discovered mid-construction or after handover, when it reads as designer or contractor incompetence instead of an inherent property of the door type itself.
