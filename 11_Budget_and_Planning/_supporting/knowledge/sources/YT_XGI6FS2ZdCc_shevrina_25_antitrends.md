## Design Concept — The Items With Real Reasoning

Twenty-five items are listed; most are bare taste verdicts and are not extracted. These carry a stated mechanism or a checkable specification:

- **⚠️ Gloss, two myths dismantled at once.** (a) **Fingerprints show *more* on gloss, not less** — matte hides them, and coatings like Fenix or soft-touch hide them entirely. (b) **Gloss does not enlarge a room.** Her mechanism: a glossy surface reflects the whole cluttered room back at you, so **you get visual clutter rather than expansion.** Recommends matte fronts, matte porcelain (floors and bathrooms) and matte ceilings.
- **⚠️ And the same qualification applied to mirrors — a genuine refinement of a rule this vault already holds.** "Даже зеркало не всегда работает на увеличение и расширение помещения, потому что зависит от того, что отражается в этом зеркале." If the mirror reflects a heavy zone — a hallway full of shoes and clutter — the clutter is doubled. **A mirror enlarges only when what it reflects is itself light and minimal.** This adds a second condition to the mirror-placement rule already on `Color_Palette_and_Material_Direction.md` from ARCHWOOD (which conditions placement on where the *light* is); Shevrina conditions it on what is in the *reflection*.
- **⚠️ Marble-look tile: avoid the middle formats.** Named as unacceptable: **20×40 and 30×60** with a marble decor. Go either genuinely small, or **larger than 120×60 — she names 150×80, or wide-format ceramic at 3 m × 1.5 m** with a proper stone figure running floor to ceiling. Restates the identical rule from `QES02ExtmAg` (within-channel, not independent).
- **⚠️ MDF slat panels are technologically superseded by shadow profiles.** She is explicit that the slat/rhythm *effect* is fine — the objection is to the delivery method. **Profiles installed during wall finishing create the same groove effect, and are much cheaper than buying paint-ready MDF panels and then colour-matching enamel to the wall paint.** Note this is not a contradiction of her defence of рейки in `QES02ExtmAg`: there she defends the rhythm principle, here she objects to the panel product.
- **⚠️ Yellow walls, and where they come from — a direct engagement with the 2026-08-30 colour batch.** Her diagnosis: people want a beige or warm-white interior and overshoot into yellow, and **the reason is the long-standing belief that a north-facing, low-sun room should be painted a warmer, yellower colour** — advice people then apply too heavily. **This is exactly the rule ARCHWOOD gives on `Color_Selection_Process_and_Testing.md`** ("choose a shade slightly warmer than the one you actually want" for north rooms) — so this is a real-world caution about that rule's failure mode, not a contradiction of it. Her mitigation: gather Pinterest references, compare your выкрас on your own wall against them side by side, and judge the match.
- **⚠️ "White, warm-white, beige and greyish are the *hardest* colours in an interior."** "Проще простого сделать более такой цветной интерьер, нежели чем вот такой вот просто базово-бежевато-белый." **This independently corroborates Надежда Кузина's core premise on `Neutrals_and_Earth_Tone_Palettes.md`** — that calm palettes are harder to combine, not easier — from an unrelated channel. A genuine cross-source confirmation of the single most counter-intuitive claim on that page.
- **⚠️ Plastic and metal trim elements as a category** — windowsills, reveals, corner beads, edge profiles on porcelain and panels. Her replacements: **paint; or microcement including on the sill and reveals; or acrylic stone set flush with the wall in the wall colour.** For porcelain edges, a 45° mitre rather than a profile; if the mistake is already made, plaster the exposed edge and paint it the porcelain's colour, or plan a recess in advance so it sits flush. Consistent with `avRNMkNdOBs` and `0WXiKNXPD_0`.
- **⚠️ Multi-level and floating ceilings.** Stated principle: **a ceiling, like a window, should not draw attention to itself.** Extra levels plus emphasising strip-light create "эффект тяжёлой плиты, нависающей над вами" and visually reduce the height — worst with already-low ceilings. She notes a floating ceiling only works in a properly rectangular room.
- **Coloured (RGB) strip lighting cheapens an interior** except in a deliberately themed room (she names a gaming/computer room as legitimate). For a normal interior use **4000 K neutral, or 3000 K warm** — she recommends warm specifically because a strip is usually part of the evening scenario. Consistent with the colour-temperature reasoning in `NfHyCfo1J4w`.
- **Sofa beds**: most on the market are uncomfortable, and **a 10 cm thin mattress that rolls up into a cupboard is more comfortable for a guest, takes less space, and frees you to choose a slimmer, better sofa.** Framed as part of a broader shift toward designing for the residents rather than for guests.
- **Splayed legs ("ножки враскоряку")** on scandi furniture — technologically unjustified, purely stylistic, and you kick them; prefer straight legs.
- **Shelves with visible fixings** — same concealed technique as `z4G-ocStu9o`: an acrylic or metal plate clamped between porcelain courses, appearing to run wall to wall.
- **Grey mottled tile paired with heavily-figured knotty wood in a bathroom** — texture overload that reads as dirt; worst in small bathrooms. Corroborates her grey-tile point in `avRNMkNdOBs`.
- **A houseplant as a universal filler** — offered self-deprecatingly as a designer's trick, especially for people renovating unaided: an empty-feeling corner, a room lacking warmth despite textiles, a gap that needs something — "поставь растение."
