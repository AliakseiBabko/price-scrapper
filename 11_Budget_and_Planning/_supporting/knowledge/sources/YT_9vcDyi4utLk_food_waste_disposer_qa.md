## Kitchen Appliances — Food Waste Disposer (Измельчитель пищевых отходов)

- **Purpose**: keeps the trash bin comparatively clean and eliminates its usual food-waste odor; convenience for disposing of bones/peelings that would otherwise sit in the bin. `single-account`/client testimony.
- **What must NOT go into a disposer** (client emphasizes this was counter to her own assumption — she expected hard items like bones to be the danger, not soft ones): **all fibrous produce** — she specifically damaged/jammed hers with **banana peel** and **vegetable trimmings**, which wind around the cutting mechanism ("измельчающие") and stop it working. The unit's own instructions (checked after the failure) also list **pumpkin, asparagus, and artichoke leaves** as prohibited, alongside the general rule of **no plastic items**. This is the single most concrete, checkable warning in the source — a real jam-failure case, not a generic caution.
- **What CAN go into a disposer** (client's own answer): chicken and rabbit bones, grape seeds, bread, cheese, soup, coffee grounds (which she says she'd previously always poured into the trash/drain in a jar), and even a fish head. Presented as a genuinely counter-intuitive answer (hard bones are fine; soft fibrous produce is not).
- **Hand-safety rule**: never put hands into the disposer's opening — a dedicated pusher tool exists for pushing food waste in, specifically because the unit's blades ("ножи измельчители") are active inside. `single-account`, safety-relevant, concrete mechanism (cutting blades) given as the reason.
- **Electrical safety**: speaker states the water + electricity combination inside a disposer is "completely safe" and cannot deliver a shock; client corroborates that she and her husband looked into this concern specifically before use and found no issue. `unverified`/single-account — no technical explanation of the safety mechanism (e.g. sealed motor housing) was given, just an assurance.
- **Operating noise**: the unit does make noise (electric motor grinding bones), but it is not very loud, is partially masked by the running water used during operation, and only runs briefly (well under a minute per use). `single-account`.
- **No unexpected self-activation**: clarifies a common confusion — the disposer is not the same device as a "solo lift" (сололифт, a sewage macerator/lift pump that can activate automatically via its own float/level sensor); a disposer only activates when its control button is pressed by the user, never automatically. Useful disambiguation between two different under-sink/utility devices that share a similar "grinds things" association.
- **Maintenance**: unit is described as effectively self-cleaning — running water while grinding flushes and cleans the internal components; client states no separate user maintenance routine is required.
- **Physical footprint under the sink**: size compared to roughly a 5-liter water bottle; shapes vary by model (some slimmer units hang lower toward the floor, some are shorter/wider), but described as generally compact enough to still leave room for a separate water-filter unit under the same sink. `single-account`, not measured/specified in exact dimensions.
