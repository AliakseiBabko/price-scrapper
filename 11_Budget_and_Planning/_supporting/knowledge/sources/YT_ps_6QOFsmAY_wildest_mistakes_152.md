## Durable Facts

- **Custom wall fresco/mural must be proofread in full before printing and before installation** — real case (likely the original telling of this store's existing fresco anecdote): a world-map mural had geographic-label errors (incorrect capitalization/spelling of ocean/sea names) not caught before printing; costs ≈50,000 RUB and the piece has to be fully re-hung once an error is found, since it installs as one continuous panel unrolled corner-to-corner with no room to reposition mid-application. **Rule: proofread at the design-mockup stage, before printing, not after installation** — corroborates and slightly refines the store's existing fresco cost/rework entry (same figure, same failure mode, earlier telling).
- **Bathtub protection during rough/dirty work should use plywood over the tub, not just plastic sheeting** — reasoning: sand/grit trapped on the rim under a thin sheet can scratch or shift the tub, and careless on-site accidents (a dropped tool, a master's phone falling into the tub) are common enough in the practitioner's own experience ("I could name a hundred cases") to justify real protection. **Extended technique**: mask the tub rim with several layers of painter's tape before laying the plywood, since plywood itself gets dirty during the project and crews will sometimes just flip the dirty plywood over to look clean for a client/inspector visit rather than replace it — taping the rim underneath protects the tub even when the visible plywood surface has been "gamed" this way. **Genuinely new protective technique + a candid warning about a real quality-control workaround workers use.**
- **Floor drain/duct outlet grilles must be installed strictly level (horizontal)**, not tilted — an uneven grille conflicts with flooring installed around it (can force cutting a laminate board to fit, or simply look and feel wrong underfoot). Concrete installation-quality check.
- **Bathtub rim must sit recessed relative to the adjacent tile/wall surface by at least 1.5cm, ideally 2-3cm — never flush/level with it.** Reasoning: a flush installation causes the grout/sealant line at that joint to degrade faster over time. **Genuinely new, specific numeric installation rule** for this store's bathtub-installation content.
- **Relief/textured tile forces a trade-off with perfectly mitered 45° inside corners** — the practitioner states no installer, however skilled, achieves a truly clean inside corner with a relief-textured tile pattern (vivid anecdote: "even if they do the splits, even if they sit on a bottle, they'll never nail those corners" — tried redoing corners repeatedly on a past project, still imperfect, though the client accepted it). **Practical rule: choose either perfect 45° corners or relief-textured tile, not both.** Genuinely new material-selection trade-off.
- **Compact-footprint bathtub worked example**: where a doorway or fixed wall prevents the room from being lengthened enough for an ideally-sized tub, build a tiled niche closed with a furniture-hinge cabinet door at the tub's foot end to recover usable depth — doubling as broom/vacuum/appliance storage — while the tile-overhangs-tub-on-3-sides rule (already in this store) is still maintained. Concrete space-recovery worked case.
- **The structural/finish ceiling behind a stretch-ceiling fabric must always be painted** (color is not the point — any coat is sufficient) so nothing sheds or dusts onto the underside of the stretch-ceiling fabric over time. Genuinely new maintenance-relevant rule for this store's stretch-ceiling content.
- **On-site CCTV cameras are installed specifically to supervise the crew's own behavior, not as a client-facing feature** — stated rationale: crews work more carefully, professionally, and "culturally" when filmed, and footage also protects workers themselves (e.g. from later disputed blame). `single-account`, stated company practice.
- **Plumbing access-hatch ("сантехнический люк") design principle**: the point of a well-designed hatch is not decorative appearance but full "revision"/technical access — sized and positioned so any single fitting/valve inside can be removed and replaced individually without disassembling the whole assembly around it. Reusable functional-design principle, distinct from (and adds mechanism to) this store's existing general utility-concealment-box sizing heuristic.
- **A full supply+exhaust ("приточно-вытяжная") ventilation unit can consume a large share of a room's ceiling height** — demonstrated on camera as reaching close to head height even for the practitioner. **Two concrete consequences flagged**: (1) it doesn't just eat height in its own room — adjacent rooms often need a partial ceiling drop too, so a client wanting this system should budget for meaningful lost ceiling height and cost in more than one room; (2) **sequence duct-routing subcontractors before finalizing/showcasing planned electrical wiring layouts** — duct installers reportedly route disorganized, ad hoc ducting wherever needed regardless of an already-planned, neatly-parallel electrical layout, effectively overriding it; a clean electrical-layout "portfolio" result requires waiting until after ventilation ducting is finished, not photographing it beforehand. **Genuinely new cost/space trade-off warning plus a concrete cross-trade sequencing rule.**
- **A genuine "tropical"/rain shower head must either protrude directly from the wall on a proper riser arm, or be ceiling-mounted** — a shower head connected via a horizontal hand-held-style hose fitting is explicitly *not* a real tropical shower installation, regardless of appearance. Rule stated plainly; deeper mechanism explanation deferred to a promised dedicated video (not covered further in this transcript).
- **To-the-ceiling kitchen cabinetry combined with a stretch ceiling requires a purpose-built corner box**, matching the existing wardrobe-ceiling-box coordination principle already in this store: since a floor-to-ceiling kitchen run leaves no room to attach a stretch-ceiling baguette directly to cabinet doors, build a box sized exactly to the kitchen cabinetry's footprint (sometimes with integrated lighting for the upper cabinets) so the stretch-ceiling baguette attaches to the box's edge while the kitchen mounts to/near the same box from below. **Extends** the existing wardrobe-box coordination principle to a second furniture type (to-ceiling kitchens).
- **Pre-wire a dedicated electrical outlet for a heated-seat/smart toilet before finish work**, rather than improvising a connection from a nearby sink outlet afterward — described as looking unprofessional and something that must be planned into the design/project in advance, not solved on the fly.
- **Never change a door/opening height mid-project once construction has started** — real cost/schedule comparison given: switching from a taller modern door height (210cm) down to fit lower-than-expected ceilings, once already underway, forces reworking wall openings apartment-wide. With this company's standard paintable wallpaper finish, the rework costs only ≈2 extra days; with painted walls or standard (non-paintable) wallpaper, the same rework costs ≈2 extra *weeks*. **Genuinely new, concrete rework-cost comparison tied specifically to wall-finish type** — a strong practical argument reinforcing this store's existing paintable-wallpaper preference from a different angle (rework cost, not just spot-repair).
