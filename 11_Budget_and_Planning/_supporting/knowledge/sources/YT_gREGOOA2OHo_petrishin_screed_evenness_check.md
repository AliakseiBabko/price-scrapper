## Flooring — Real Semi-Dry Screed QC Check, One Month Post-Pour

1. **Real-object QC finding, semi-dry mechanized screed poured by a
   third-party subcontractor**: examined more than a month after pour.
   Curing protocol followed: entire apartment floor covered in plastic
   film for 10 days after the pour, then uncovered. Result assessed as
   generally good-plane, with minor acceptable deviations.
2. **The one real defect found, and where it occurs**: a ~3mm bump
   specifically at a room-to-room threshold/doorway transition — flagged
   as the most common evenness mistake across builders generally (a
   difference in plane between one room and the adjacent one, most often
   right at the doorway junction), not a defect specific to plane
   flatness within a single room. Fix: local grinding at that spot.
3. **Semi-dry screed framed as a legitimate cost-saving choice, with an
   explicit strength caveat**: if minimizing cost is the priority,
   semi-dry screed is a reasonable choice — walked and tap-tested
   ("стучали") across the whole floor with no hollow sound detected
   anywhere, though some minor surface cracks appeared, assessed as
   within normal tolerance. Framed as measurably less robust than a
   mechanized/reinforced alternative, but acceptable for the cost
   tradeoff.

## Bathroom / WC — Custom Shower Base, Waterproofing, and Fixture Detail

4. **Custom-built shower base ("подиум") construction**: built from
   foam-block ("газосиликат"), floor filled with concrete, with a floor
   drain ("трап") installed inside the platform — a full masonry-plus-
   concrete shower-pan build rather than a factory pre-formed pan.
5. **Cement-based wall waterproofing** applied before tiling in the wet
   zone (named product name ASR-garbled, not confidently transcribable —
   excluded rather than guessed).
6. **Real tile dimensions for this shower**: wall tile 15cm × 7.5cm;
   floor mosaic notably thin, estimated ~3-4mm thick.
7. **Client-driven flush-floor design decision**: at client request, the
   hallway floor level was built to match the adjoining bathroom floor
   level exactly — flagged as a deliberate choice (not the more typical
   stepped-down wet-room floor), a real design/family-preference data
   point.
8. **Leak-protection system, named product and component list**:
   "Нептун" (Neptun) leak-protection system with 2 electromechanical
   shutoff valves that automatically close the water supply on a leak
   event; surrounding utility-cabinet components: a check valve, a
   fine-particle filter with backwash capability, a water-pressure
   regulator, a second filter, a distribution manifold ("гребёнка"/
   коллектор) box, an instantaneous water heater (named brand:
   Electrolux), and the heating-system manifolds, all co-located in the
   same utility niche.
9. **Bathroom tile-height convention for this job**: tile runs up to
   roughly 1.10-1.15m, transitioning to paint above that line, with the
   transition finished using a bullnose/pencil trim strip.
10. **Wall-buildup technique to eliminate a tile-to-paint plane step**: a
    painted wall section adjoining tile was built out with drywall so
    both surfaces sit flush in the same plane — avoiding the visible
    step/reveal a tile's own thickness would normally create against an
    adjacent painted wall.

## Kitchen / Living / Flooring — Site-Protection and Finish Detail

11. **Cost-saving tile-scope decision**: the wall area behind a
    built-in refrigerator was deliberately left untiled (no herringbone
    backsplash tile there) since it would be fully hidden once the
    fridge and an overhead cabinet were installed — a direct
    material-cost-saving choice made possible by known furniture
    placement.
12. **Site-protection practice**: finished kitchen floor tile was kept
    covered with protective film during ongoing work specifically to
    avoid scratches from foot traffic/tools before the project's later
    stages are complete.
13. **Pre-installed structural backing for an undecided finish detail**:
    in a room where the client hasn't yet finalized which wardrobe/
    closet style will be installed, the crew still pre-installed a
    blocking/backer strip ("закладной") so a ceiling crown molding can
    later be mounted flush along the top of whichever wardrobe is
    eventually chosen — a sequencing lesson: install structural backing
    for a finish detail ahead of a pending design decision, rather than
    waiting for that decision to be finalized first.
14. **Flooring installation detail**: engineered wood board glued
    directly to the screed with two-component adhesive, no plywood
    underlayment used; each box contains ~4-5 different plank lengths
    specifically to produce a varied, non-repeating pattern once laid.
    Porcelain tile elsewhere on the same object was separately noted as
    very difficult to drill/cut (hole-saw, notching).
