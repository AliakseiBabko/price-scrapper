## Mistakes / Warnings — What "Quality Cosmetic Renovation" Actually Requires

1. **Company scoping policy — corroborates and extends this channel's
   own already-captured principle from Round 5 video `s27qG_Eg3SY`
   ("cosmetic renovation is only genuinely cosmetic if a full capital
   renovation was already done")**: this source frames it as an active
   company policy, not just a caution — they only accept cosmetic-scope
   jobs on units that already had a genuinely good capital renovation
   with a modern base, explicitly declining Soviet-era buildings with
   old-style capital work ("не в хрущёвках с советским ремонтом").
2. **Full furniture/floor/door wrap-protection, with an explicit wood-
   floor mechanism**: declutter everything first (remove items not
   fitting the new concept), then wrap all remaining furniture and cover
   floors/doors in plastic sheeting/bubble wrap — even careful
   demolition, filling, or sanding produces microscopic airborne dust
   that a wood floor absorbs permanently into its gaps if unprotected,
   ruining nearby furniture. Framed as "spend on plastic wrap now, or
   spend on dry cleaning/breathe the dust later."
3. **Ceiling light fixture: full removal preferred over tape-and-bag when
   painting the ceiling** — taping around a fixture is slow per fixture
   and still risks paint bleeding under the tape, leaving a visible
   unpainted halo/patch once removed; fully removing (or disconnecting
   and lowering, wrapped in cellophane) avoids this defect entirely.
4. **Outlet/switch faceplate removal technique for wall painting, without
   touching the wiring itself**: remove only the faceplate/cover, tape
   over the exposed mechanism, paint the wall, then reinstall the clean
   faceplate — avoids painting around a fixed cover (uneven edges) without
   requiring any electrical rework.
5. **"Cosmetic" redefined as point-repair, not blanket recoating**: the
   source explicitly distinguishes real cosmetic renovation from "just
   rolling new paint over old paint" — genuine cosmetic work means
   locating and repairing specific damaged spots (chipped/scratched
   corners, dents, furniture-contact marks) via fill → sand → true the
   edge → prime → paint, room by room, spot by spot, without touching
   wiring or screed.
6. **Low-cost, high-visual-impact swap heuristic for cosmetic scope**:
   even when an old wooden baseboard (15 years old, gapped/worn), a
   sun-faded/cracked windowsill, or a worn/loose door could theoretically
   be kept, replacing them is framed as a cheap operation with a
   disproportionately large visual payoff — leaving them in place against
   freshly redone walls creates an "ugly contrast" that undercuts the
   whole cosmetic job's value.
7. **Protective wall panels as a scenario-driven fix for a recurring
   family wear pattern**: a dining-area wall at chair-back height showing
   repeated scratch damage (previous occupants had covered it with
   pictures) is treated as a design problem, not just a paint problem —
   repainting alone would show the same damage again within 1-2 weeks;
   recommended fix is a protective wall panel material (flexible
   "marble," or slat/rail paneling) specifically sized to the at-risk
   zone, framed as a deliberate life-scenario decision, not a decorative
   afterthought.
8. **Parquet-gap sealant as a genuine budget alternative to full
   refloor refinishing, with real cost figures**: full refinishing
   (sanding + lacquer or oil recoat) costs **150,000-200,000 RUB minimum
   (≈$1,900-$2,500, trailing-six-month USD/RUB average 80.0023 ending
   2026-02-08)** plus several days of dust and noise; a flexible,
   wood-tone-matched parquet gap sealant/caulk fills board gaps (stopping
   dust accumulation) without sanding the boards themselves, at a
   fraction of the cost. **Heuristic stated**: "80% of the result for
   20% of the price" for gap/squeak complaints specifically, not a
   substitute for genuinely worn-out flooring finish.
9. **Cosmetic-scope window handling, and a new child-safety detail**: the
   glass units themselves aren't replaced (a capital-scope item), but
   existing windows get adjustment/re-sealing (new weatherstripping,
   hardware service) — corroborating this channel's existing
   aging-hardware finding — **plus lockable handles specifically in
   children's rooms**, for safety and draft/noise reduction, a detail not
   previously captured from this channel.
