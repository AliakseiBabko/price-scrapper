## Mistakes / Warnings — Construction QC Defects (mechanism-explained)

- **All switches in a run must be mounted at one consistent height (this channel's standard: 90cm from floor, 15cm from the opening edge)** — a defect found: one switch correctly placed, the very next one at a visibly different height, with no attempt made to match it.
- **A single-layer drywall partition needs a plywood backing block behind any wall-hook/hanging-hardware location, with screws driven through the drywall into that backing** — without it, any hook/hanger pulls out of the drywall under normal use. A general partition-planning rule: identify hanging-load locations before closing the wall, not after.
- **An exposed (non-recessed) electrical panel is a real defect when its size doesn't match a legitimate circuit count** — the defect found was not just an unconcealed panel but one sized far beyond what a small apartment's circuit count would need, effectively decorative rather than functional; the general rule (established elsewhere in this vault) is that a real panel with multiple independent circuits should be planned for and concealed via a false wall, not left exposed as a wall feature.
- **Bare, unisolated plumbing and sewer stacks — including a kitchen-visible sewer stack — are a severe QC failure at any renovation tier**, described as something the source had "never seen" attempted as a design choice this openly; corroborates this vault's existing false-wall/plumbing-concealment guidance.
- **⚠️ A bathroom floor must never be raised above the surrounding apartment floor level — a genuinely important waterproofing/leak-containment mechanism, not just an accessibility preference.** The defect found: the bathroom floor sat 3cm *higher* than the rest of the apartment (achieved by pouring extra screed inside the bathroom specifically) — the source calls this backwards: **the correct direction, if not building flush with full-apartment waterproofing, is a shallow recessed basin (lower, not higher) so a leak stays contained and can be manually bailed toward the drain/toilet rather than flowing out into the rest of the unit.** Two valid methods only, never mixed: (1) a raised-threshold system with an angled corner molding positioned directly under the door leaf, or (2) a flush, single-level transition (requiring whole-apartment waterproofing, per this vault's existing rule) with a plain straight seam, also under the door leaf. **Two corner moldings never coexist at the same transition** — a defect found exactly this (both types present together), an explicit "never do this" per the source.
- **A tile-based baseboard, cut without a matching molding, produces visible raw-cut edges — never conceal this by taping wallpaper over the top edge of a tile baseboard.** A defect found used exactly this fix; called out as categorically unacceptable regardless of baseboard material.
- **A dining countertop/bar surface needs enough width for two people to eat facing each other without their elbows/plates colliding** — corroborates this vault's existing dining-clearance planning principle from an independent source in this batch.
- **A kitchen module found to be oversized for its opening during installation should be remade to the correct size, not disguised with an add-on trim piece** — the defect found added a decorative filler strip along the countertop edge specifically to hide a module that didn't fit, rather than correcting the module itself.
- **A sink/vanity design that trades away usable basin depth/width for a narrow, elaborately-shaped cutout serves no practical hygiene purpose** — the source's pointed framing: a basin should span the available width at a normal, functional depth; an oddly narrow, deep, gapped design (as found here) has no real use case over a straightforward full-width basin.
- **A ceiling light fixture ending up mounted over a mirror rather than on the ceiling is a rough-in sequencing failure**, not a design choice — the mechanism: no ceiling electrical circuit was run to the fixture's intended location before the ceiling was closed, so installers improvised by tapping into the only nearby circuit (a mirror-backlight line) instead. **General rule: confirm every fixture's electrical rough-in is actually run to its intended location before ceiling/wall closure, not just planned on paper.**
- **A wall-mounted TV bracket designed at a deliberate viewing angle (off-center relative to the seating position) must use a swivel/articulating mount, not a fixed one** — a fixed mount at an angled position makes the picture genuinely hard to view (LCD color/contrast degrades sharply off-axis), defeating the point of the angled placement in the first place.
- **A sauna is a real overreach in a small (single-bedroom, two-level) apartment** — the source's direct assessment: a single well-executed full bathroom would have served the unit better than splitting scarce square footage between a cramped sauna and a compromised bathroom.
- **A glass shower/toilet-room door provides effectively zero acoustic isolation — a real concern when that room sits just a couple of meters from a bed.** Framed as a basic privacy/acoustic-planning oversight, not primarily a construction-quality issue.
- **Decorative loft-style elements (exposed "brick" veneer panels, wood accent trim) must be installed with the same rigor as structural finishes** — defects found: a wood accent panel installed loose enough to visibly wobble under hand pressure, and a brick-veneer application installed crooked/uneven.
- **A wood accent surface meant to read as natural material should be finished with clear/tinted wood stain to preserve grain texture, not painted over with cheap opaque oil paint** — the defect found used a low-grade, knot-heavy wood painted flat white, defeating the intended natural-material look entirely.
- **Never permanently glue removable trim elements (corner moldings, casing) with an adhesive so strong the trim can't be removed without destroying the surrounding finish** — a defect found used exactly this, making any future correction destructive by necessity.
- **A door's transitional trim (доборы/jamb extensions) at a door-to-adjacent-surface junction must be made from the same material as the interior doors' own jamb extensions**, not an unrelated cheap silver corner profile — corroborates this vault's existing door-material-matching convention.
- **Floor-covering transition moldings (thresholds) must be color-matched to the specific flooring product, never a generic silver/aluminum profile** — reiterated as a basic, checkable standard.
- **LVT/quartz-vinyl flooring must terminate against a wall with a proper baseboard, never left as a raw bent edge against the wall with no trim at all.**
- **Creaking stairs, even faintly, are a defect requiring rework**, not a tolerable cosmetic issue — flagged explicitly as something to reject outright rather than accept "since it's minor."
- **A thin, low-cost decorative wall panel (~300 RUB market-grade) installed in a high-traffic transition area (a stairway landing) that can be pulled off by hand is a real durability defect**, independent of its low material cost — placement/durability mismatch, not a pure cost issue.
- **Electrical outlets/switches added after wall finishing (because the designer only planned circuit locations once seeing the completed space) get run in visible surface cable channels, not concealed in-wall** — a visible, avoidable consequence of finalizing electrical layout too late in the design sequence; reinforces this vault's existing "plan electrical points before, not after, finish work" guidance.
- **A single, unexplained entry-hall outlet placed with no clear purpose is flagged as evidence of un-planned, ad hoc electrical layout** rather than a deliberate design decision.
- **Double (not triple) gang plates were used where three separate electrical points existed at one location** — a minor but real detail: matching gang-plate count to actual point count is a basic finishing-quality check.
- **Wallpaper hung with plain water rather than proper wallpaper adhesive is a severe, checkable installation-quality failure** — visible seam gaps and poor adhesion throughout were attributed directly to this substitution.
- **Sealant/caulk application quality at a countertop-to-backsplash joint should leave a clean, consistent bead — a visibly messy, uneven application (in this case, described as applied "with a toe") is a legitimate rejection criterion**, not merely cosmetic nitpicking.
- **A countertop edge-banding strip damaged/chipped during use or installation should be replaced, not left visibly compromised** — flagged as a basic material-integrity check at handover.
