## Planning Rules / Mistakes / Warnings — White Box, Real Defect Evidence

- **⚠️ Paint over White-Box putty: real chip-through defect, not a lighting/opinion issue**: a White-Box wall painted and lit only by a single ~60W bulb from the developer looks acceptable, but hanging even a simple wall sconce on the same wall reveals the paint job is poor under proper lighting. **⚠️ Real mechanical defect, corroborating and extending the existing "walls aren't genuinely paint-ready" caution with a specific failure mode**: scraping the developer's paint off to redo it reliably also pulls the underlying putty/skim-coat off with it — **no method has yet been found to strip the paint cleanly without damaging the putty layer beneath it** — meaning a full re-putty-and-repaint is the realistic remediation, not just a repaint.
- **⚠️ Named worse-case pattern: façade-facing walls specifically prone to hidden delamination requiring demolition**: on one real apartment, an entire façade-facing wall's putty/plaster needed opening up and inspecting for detachment ("сколько там отлёт") — plaster from the developer can be found hollow-sounding ("штукатурка звенит") over a large area, not just localized spots; in the worst observed cases, the **entire wall's plaster required full demolition and redo**, not a local patch. **⚠️ Named safety risk stated directly, not just cost**: the speaker gives a real scenario — a child sitting at a desk near such a wall, doing homework, with a piece of the wall coming loose and falling on them — framing this as a genuine safety hazard, not merely a cosmetic defect.
- **⚠️ Window-reveal ("откос") vs. sash ("створка") diagnostic distinction, a thermal-imaging technique**: where a draft/heat-loss issue is found near a window using a thermal camera, distinguish whether the problem source is the sash itself (adjustable, simple fix) or the window reveal/jamb plaster (откос) — if the latter, the откос must be fully removed and redone, a materially bigger job than a sash adjustment.
- **⚠️ Developer-installed screed heating pipe: named leak-risk defect from excess soldered joints inside the screed**: on one real object, the underfloor heating pipe embedded in the developer's screed was built using tee-fittings with an unusually high number of soldered/welded joints inside the finished screed — framed directly as a safety concern **not just for the current owner but for everyone below them** (the example given: roughly 15 apartments below a single unit) — **any leak inside a buried, joint-heavy screed pipe run risks flooding all 15 floors beneath**, a materially larger liability than a normal single-run screed heating pipe.
- **⚠️ Developer electrical panel: a real, photographed unsafe-twist-connection example, explicitly named as a fire-safety issue**: the developer's own distribution panel in one real 150m² apartment was found using bare twisted-wire splices ("скрутка") — not proper terminal blocks or approved connectors — with visible remaining plaster residue in the splice and exposed bare conductor. **This single splice fed the apartment's entire outlet-circuit group.** **Explicit consequence stated directly**: once occupants moved in and began running normal household electrical load through this connection, the speaker states plainly this would very likely (his own words: "100%") result in a fire.
- **⚠️ Named market-segment correlation, stated as the speaker's own generalization**: White-Box units are typically offered in the economy/comfort ("эконом/комфорт") market segment specifically, and the speaker frames the quality level shown as consistent with what should be expected at that segment — not a random one-off, but the norm for this handover standard at this price tier. **Explicit fairness caveat**: some developers do White Box well (the speaker states his company has encountered such units with no complaints), but characterizes this as a small minority (~10%) rather than the norm.
- **⚠️ Real-cost framing: "economical" White-Box purchase does not net out cheaper once redo costs are included**: the buyer pays the developer for the unit, for the developer's own pre-finish materials/labor, and then — if genuinely wanting a durable, safe result — pays again for demolition, debris removal, re-plastering, re-puttying, and repainting on top of that; the speaker's explicit conclusion is that this is **not real savings** in most cases, framed directly as the developer's own margin motive (fast, cheap labor and cheap materials at scale across a large multi-unit building) rather than a genuine cost-saving handover for the buyer.
- **Practical decision rule stated directly**: White Box is a reasonable choice only for a rental/flip unit, or for an owner with genuinely low finish-quality requirements who can tolerate wallpaper-only finish and budget flooring — it is explicitly *not* recommended for someone who wants durability, safety, and long-term "won't leak/won't catch fire/won't fall off" peace of mind in their own residence.
