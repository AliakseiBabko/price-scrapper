## Structural/Substrate Technique — Wood Floor Joist Support Extension, a Real Cost Figure

- **⚠️ Wood-joist-on-steel-channel ("швеллер") support gap requires a welder, a named cost driver**: joists ("брус") normally span between two existing steel support channels with two solid bearing points. Where an added/extended joist section falls short of the second channel, a welder must be called to weld on an additional channel section to create a third bearing point before flooring can proceed — **stated cost: an additional ~15,000-20,000 RUB** (≈$210-$280 USD, trailing-6-month average before 2020-09-03, per this project's conversion convention) purely for this one fix, on top of the base floor budget.
- **Floor-height-gain tradeoff on old-fund floor replacement, two named options**: removing old wood floors typically drops the sub-floor level by ~15-20cm, which must be built back up to reach a comfortable door/threshold height. Two named approaches: (1) **wet screed** — heavy, physically demanding to mix/pour especially by hand, long cure/dry time; (2) **dry build-up** — plywood/OSB ("фанера", "ЛСУ" sheet layers) — lighter and faster, but requires "quite a lot" of sheet material, which is not itself cheap. **Either path costs a materially significant sum** — framed as a real either-way cost, not a way to avoid the expense.
- **⚠️ Demolition-stage risk to the downstairs neighbor's ceiling, a real liability concern distinct from the noise-through-riser issue already on file**: once old floorboards are removed, the crew is standing directly on what is, from the unit below, essentially the neighbor's lath-and-plaster ("дранка") ceiling — jumping, dropping tools, or hammering on these exposed boards can visibly crack/shed the neighbor's ceiling plaster below. Explicit site practice: don't jump, don't drop hammers, don't strike with a sledgehammer on the exposed sub-floor boards during this stage.
- **Wall lath-and-plaster substrate, an added mechanical detail beyond the existing "дранка is a weak point" red flag**: stripping old plaster from a dранка-and-lath wall in this apartment revealed, underneath the lath, a bituminous/roofing-felt-like layer ("рубероид") plus the original wood boards. **Consequence stated directly**: fresh plaster will not adequately bond to this substrate — the wall must first be sheathed in sheet material (drywall/GVL) before any leveling ("под маяк") or skim-coat finishing can proceed, which is itself additional material and labor cost beyond a simple re-plaster.
- **Existing plaster over lath is described as audibly hollow/loose ("вся звенит")** even before demolition — priming and re-wallpapering over it carries a real risk that some patches will crack/detach during that process, though not guaranteed in every spot. (Corroborates, with an added acoustic diagnostic detail — tapping/listening for a hollow ring — the existing "бухтит"/hollow-tap red flag from `f8lzn1KEIww`.)
- **Unstably-bonded original brick masonry, a distinct in-wall defect from the лath/plaster issue**: some original brick coursing is only loosely/poorly bonded ("мягко скажем не совсем хорошо закреплены") — remediation requires either reinforcing the existing masonry in place or knocking out and replacing the affected bricks, both adding real time and cost.

## Systems Workarounds — Heritage Fixtures and Chimney/Flue Serviceability

- **⚠️ Heritage elements (fireplaces/stoves, period fixtures) legally/practically cannot be demolished or altered, only restored**: framed as a real constraint distinct from the "sink must go where the stove was" approval case already on file (`nma3zHRugsM`) — here, a still-present decorative masonry stove ("печь") can only be cleaned, insulated, and cosmetically restored (e.g. repainting/whitewashing), not removed or rebuilt, if the owner wants to preserve it as a heritage feature.
- **⚠️ Chimney/flue inspection is a real prerequisite before using or connecting anything to an old-fund flue, not an optional step**: to actually use a stove (or connect a modern gas water heater, "газовая колонка", to an existing flue), the full flue/chimney system must first be inspected/cleaned by a chimney sweep ("трубочист") — old-fund flues are described as routinely clogged by default. **⚠️ Named failure mode when this is skipped**: connecting a modern gas water heater to a clogged/underperforming flue causes insufficient draft; when the draft reverses, the water heater cuts out and the hot-water supply reverts to cold — described as a recurring real problem in old-fund apartments specifically in winter. Calling a chimney sweep for one flue also typically obligates inspecting all the building's other flues at the same time (they're usually all in similar condition), adding to the cost.

## Electrical — Three Named Structural Problems, With a Real Capacity-Upgrade Example

- **⚠️ Three named electrical problems stated as near-universal for old-fund apartments**: (1) old aluminum wiring already near/past the end of its safe service life (corroborates the existing buy-time red flag from this store); (2) wiring routed by an unknown installer in an undocumented layout, making it difficult to trace/diagnose without specialist help; (3) **very low allocated electrical capacity from the building** — this apartment's real allocated capacity was **only 3 kW**, insufficient for a modern water heater plus a 4-burner electric cooktop together.
- **⚠️ Real capacity-upgrade case with a concrete before/after figure**: to get enough capacity for modern appliances, the owner had to make several calls/applications to have the building's allocation increased — this apartment's allocation was raised to **5 kW across three phases**, at an added cost (the approval calls/paperwork plus new breakers sized for the increased capacity) beyond the base electrical rewire budget.
- **Aluminum wiring replacement is a fire-safety mandate, not a discretionary upgrade** — stated directly as required regardless of budget, since degrading aluminum wiring (especially behind wood-frame walls/ceilings, as in this building type) is a genuine fire risk, not merely inconvenient.

## Plumbing — Neighbor-Coordination Constraint on Sewer Riser Replacement

- **⚠️ A distinct, harder version of the "neighbor coordination" constraint than previously recorded**: replacing a cast-iron sewer riser ("канализационный стояк") properly requires also replacing its cross-fittings ("крестовины") and tees ("тройники"), which are physically shared with the unit(s) above/below on the same stack — requiring the neighbor's cooperation and access. **Named real failure case**: where a neighbor has already completed their own renovation and won't grant access, only the accessible section of riser can be replaced; the old cast-iron tee at the inaccessible boundary must be left in place — a real, un-resolvable risk of a future crack/leak at that joint that the current owner cannot control or predict.
- **General plumbing framing**: full re-piping of all old-fund supply/waste lines is described as worthwhile ("к счастью") because it buys ~30 years of reliability, but is unavoidably costly ("к сожалению") — both framings stated directly by the speaker as two sides of the same fact.
