## New facts

1. **Loading-type comparison (front vs. top)**: front-load is the standard/optimal type (~90%
   of models sold) — needs floor-space clearance for the door swing, and is required if a
   dryer is to be stacked on top in a column. Top-load only makes sense in width-constrained
   spaces where a front door swing won't fit, or is sometimes chosen for elderly users (less
   bending to load/unload) — but with a real counterpoint: pulling out wet bedding (a duvet
   cover specifically) from a top-loader requires noticeably more physical effort/strength
   than lifting it out of a front-loader or tipping it into a basin, which can be harder for
   an elderly user than the bending top-loaders are meant to avoid.
2. **Full-size vs. narrow washer dimensions and tradeoffs**: full-size ≈60cm wide, 55-60cm
   deep, 7-10kg capacity — better balance, less vibration, higher motor longevity, quieter.
   Narrow washers: 33-45cm deep, 5-7kg capacity — worse stability, shorter service life,
   worse spin-cycle balancing, stronger vibration.
3. **Motor type: only inverter motors worth considering in 2026** at any normal price point —
   quieter, more efficient, longer service life. Non-inverter motors are only a reasonable
   choice at the absolute bottom of the market.
4. **⚠️ Drum-capacity labeling caveat, extends this store's existing capacity guidance with a
   real measurement-reliability warning**: baseline bands are 5-6kg (too small), 7-8kg
   (optimal), 9kg+ (needed for a family) — but there's no single industry standard for
   measuring capacity, and a stated "9kg" figure usually reflects a specific fabric type
   (typically cotton); other fabrics fit less by weight. In practice, a machine labeled 9kg
   sometimes only actually holds 6-7kg — don't take the spec-sheet number at face value.
5. **Machine weight as an imperfect proxy for drum size**: heavier machines usually have
   larger/more capacious drums, but real named exceptions exist — one model reaches a 63L
   drum at only 65kg total weight, while a Candy model at the same 65kg weight has only a 48L
   drum. Weight-to-capacity correlation is directional, not reliable in every case.
6. **Spin-speed guidance, with a motor-longevity nuance**: minimum viable spin speed is
   1,000rpm; optimal is 1,200rpm. Chasing higher rated speeds (1,400rpm+) doesn't meaningfully
   improve dryness — spin-quality plateaus around the 1,000rpm mark. However, running a
   machine at its own true maximum rated speed maximizes motor stress and shortens its
   service life — a machine rated for 1,400rpm but routinely run at 800-1,000rpm will
   outlast a machine whose max rating is only 1,000rpm and is always run at that ceiling.
7. **Balancing mechanics and a weight-band guide**: balancing (even laundry distribution during
   spin) is driven by drum size, total machine weight, and control-algorithm quality; poor
   balancing causes louder operation, faster mechanical wear, and in extreme cases the machine
   can physically "hop" and damage nearby structures. Weight bands: 55-60kg = light,
   mediocre balancing; 65-75kg = normal; 75kg+ = best — with a stated named exception
   (a lighter Samsung model that balances well anyway due to superior algorithm tuning).
8. **⚠️ Noise/vibration root-cause ranking — installation quality dominates price tier**: noise
   and vibration are driven by weight, size, motor type, and — most importantly —
   installation quality. Even the most expensive premium machine will vibrate and wear
   prematurely if installed on an uneven, unlevelled base or pressed flush against adjacent
   cabinetry; correctly installed, the noise gap between economy and premium tiers is modest
   (not 5-10x), not the dramatic difference price alone might suggest.
9. **Control-type comparison and a wet-hands caveat**: touch panel, mechanical buttons/dials,
   hybrid, and smartphone/app (Wi-Fi) control. App control is explicitly called irrelevant
   for ~99% of users by this source's own stated opinion, though one real named use case
   (a viewer remotely configuring a washing machine for an elderly relative in another city)
   is acknowledged as a genuine exception. Touch controls on very budget machines can work
   poorly, especially with wet hands.
10. **Program-count is a weak selection criterion**: only ~6 core programs (cotton, mixed
    fabric, delicate, quick wash, spin, rinse) are used by ~99% of people; the other 20-30
    "extra" programs marketed on some machines are just recombinations of the same underlying
    parameters (wash time, water volume, spin count/speed, temperature) — algorithm/tuning
    quality matters far more than program count.
11. **⚠️ Steam-function taxonomy — two distinct implementations, one that doesn't actually
    work**: cheap heating-element-based steam (water never reaches the element on a dry-laundry
    refresh cycle, so it doesn't actually work for that use case) vs. a real steam-generator
    mechanism (works well). Named example: LG's own two lines — "Steam" (element-based, poor,
    low adoption) vs. "True Steam" (generator-based, performs well). Real benefit where it
    works: freshens/de-odorizes laundry and lightly reduces wrinkles without a full wash cycle
    — does not replace real ironing, and meaningfully raises the machine's price.
12. **Auto-dose (detergent) feature**: fill a reservoir once, and the machine doses the correct
    amount automatically per cycle — most commonly implemented for gel/liquid detergent, rarely
    for powder. Downsides: added mechanical complexity and a new potential failure point, and
    the dosing system needs periodic flushing/cleaning.
13. **⚠️ Washer+dryer combo unit caution — extends this store's existing separate-vs-combo
    preference (already documented from `sd2XYBZY-K8`) with new mechanism detail**: a combo
    unit doubles wear on a single shared motor/mechanism versus two dedicated separate
    machines, and there's a real capacity mismatch — a machine that washes 9kg in one load
    may only be able to dry 5-6kg of that same load in one cycle. Recommends buying two
    separate dedicated machines whenever budget/space allow — corroborates, with new
    reasoning, this channel's own existing stated "ideal" preference for separate units.
14. **Economy/mid/premium tier comparison**: economy — if it breaks, cheaper and more logical
    to replace outright than repair; low motor durability, unsophisticated electronics/no real
    algorithm tuning, light chassis (raises noise), high failure risk right after the warranty
    period ends. Mid-tier: solid construction, longer service life, better balance tuning,
    quieter, a predictable 5+ year service life under normal use. Premium: higher-quality
    build materials, more sophisticated control algorithms, styling/design focus, and
    typically premium service/support from the maker or retailer. **Wash quality itself is
    roughly equal across all three tiers** — correct program selection and correct
    installation matter far more than price tier.
15. **Micro-compact wall-mounted washers, a stated niche category**: maximum 1-2kg load,
    typically wall-mounted, used in short-term rental apartments or guest houses needing only
    minimal incidental laundry — not intended for regular household use.
16. **The presenter's own "ideal" washing-machine spec checklist**: front-load; full-size body
    (55cm+ depth); 7kg+ drum capacity; inverter motor; 1,200rpm+ spin speed; from a proven,
    reputable manufacturer and reputable seller.
