## Rooms / Zones & Planning Rules — Master-Bedroom Repartitioning

- **10m² bedroom, 3.10m ceiling height**, developer-finish apartment
  (existing plaster/skim coat on walls, poured screed, roughed-in
  electrical present but not trusted — see Electrical below).
- **"Master bedroom" concept applied to a modest 10m² room**: repartitioned
  to add a private en-suite WC and a walk-in closet accessed from the
  bedroom, by demolishing part of the developer's own block wall (>200mm
  thick, described as unnecessarily thick for an interior partition) and
  rebuilding with a 50mm-profile double-sided 2-layer drywall frame with
  mineral-wool infill.
- **⚠️ Real layout-revision mistake, directly relevant to bedroom/closet/
  WC zoning decisions**: the practitioner's first pass put **both** the WC
  and the closet doorway directly off the bedroom. Mid-project he decided
  this was wrong and reworked it so the **WC is entered through the
  walk-in closet, not directly from the bedroom** — his own stated
  reasoning: "more convenient and somehow more correct" for the sequence
  to be bedroom → closet → WC. `corroborates_existing`-adjacent: this
  store's existing walk-in-closet-access rule (enter a closet from a
  shared hallway, not the bedroom, to preserve bedroom wall space) is
  about the closet's own entrance; this is a distinct, additional rule
  about *routing the WC through the closet* rather than giving it its own
  separate bedroom-facing doorway. Both point the same direction: minimize
  the number of separate openings punched directly into the bedroom's own
  walls.
- **⚠️ Real cost/effort lesson — reworking a drywall partition's openings
  is much more labor-intensive than reworking a block-wall partition's**:
  moving door openings in an already-built drywall frame took roughly two
  days (vs. an expected "couple of hours") because the entire frame at
  that opening had to be unsheathed, reframed, and re-sheathed, whereas a
  block-wall opening can just be re-cut. A concrete argument for finalizing
  a drywall partition's door/opening layout before framing, not after.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Finishing Technique

- **⚠️ Never paint a color test swatch directly on bare skim-coat/putty**:
  the practitioner painted his test swatches straight onto the finished
  wall putty to choose a color, then had to apply **4 full coats** of the
  final paint just to fully cover the swatch patches (paint reliably looks
  lighter in-store than once applied to a real wall, so a swatch test is
  still worth doing — just not directly on the surface that will be the
  final finish). Correct method: tape/glue a piece of cardboard to the
  wall and test swatches on that instead.
- **Interior (concave) corner treatment before painting**: caulk the
  corner with acrylic sealant first, producing a deliberately slightly
  rounded corner profile — stated as looking noticeably better than a
  crisp/sharp painted corner.
- **Decorative wall-panel sizing mistake (дюрополимер/polyurethane-type
  panels)**: bought 2m-length panels instead of 3m, despite 3m being
  available — created unnecessary horizontal seams that then needed
  repeated putty/sand cycles to blend into the surrounding texture,
  costing much more finishing time than planned. **Vertical seams between
  these panels are explicitly not recommended** (very difficult to finish
  cleanly); horizontal seams are acceptable if unavoidable. Routed to
  [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/analysis/Decorative_Wall_Panels|Decorative Wall Panels]].
- **Panel installation sequence**: laser level for placement + construction
  adhesive ("жидкие гвозди") to mount, a separate specialized joint
  adhesive to bond panel-to-panel seams, then putty + repeated sand/putty
  cycles at every seam to blend the joint texture into the panel's own
  surface finish before painting. Same product/technique used for a
  matching durapolymer baseboard (painted before installation, then
  joint-glued and puttied the same way).

## Electrical

- **Never keep a developer's own electrical wiring "as is" when it's
  aluminum** — stated as a hard rule in this source specifically because
  the apartment's roughed-in wiring was aluminum; full rewire done
  (marking outlet boxes, cutting new chases, running new cable through
  ceiling/chases).
- **Bedside crossover ("перекрёстные"/two-way) switches at both sides of
  the bed, plus 1-2 outlets per side** — same pattern already recorded on
  [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/analysis/Bedroom_Design_Principles|Bedroom Design Principles]]
  from other sources; corroborating, not new.

## Plumbing

- **Radiator stub-outs relocated from floor to wall** even though the
  developer's own radiator unit was kept as-is (technically fine, just
  visually dated) — stated reasoning: a wall stub-out looks better and is
  easier to clean around than a floor stub-out. Supply tee fittings buried
  under the screed were also replaced while accessible, followed by a
  pressure test (опрессовка) before closing everything up.

## Flooring

- **Quartz-vinyl flooring laid in a herringbone pattern, run as one
  continuous surface from the adjoining kitchen into the bedroom** — a
  self-leveling compound pour (after screed sanding, priming, and
  perimeter damper-strip installation) was used specifically to bring the
  quartz-vinyl surface level flush with the adjoining room's tile, not just
  to flatten the substrate generally.
- **Floor-material transition (quartz-vinyl to an adjoining floor type)
  finished with an aluminum T-profile threshold**, glued with liquid nails
  into a shallow pre-cut groove — routine transition-strip technique, not
  previously recorded in this level of installation detail.

## Furniture, Trim & Layout

- **Custom wardrobe furniture installed floor-to-ceiling before the
  stretch ceiling was installed** — sequencing note: furniture first, then
  stretch-ceiling membrane, then 2 recessed spotlights added into the
  ceiling over/near the wardrobe afterward.
- **Interior doors: white, non-standard 220cm-tall openings** — white
  called out as a "universal" color that works with any interior style, a
  stated reason rather than just an aesthetic choice.
- **Bedside zone asymmetry, a real example**: left side of the bed built
  as a small vanity/makeup zone for the wife (pouf, small table, lit
  mirror) instead of a second matching nightstand; right side kept as a
  conventional floor-standing nightstand. A concrete counter-example to
  this store's existing "vanity table = friction point" caution (see
  Kruglov's mistakes video above) — here the vanity is deliberately placed
  *in* the bedroom by choice, working fine for this specific couple, tag
  as one more single-account data point on a genuinely debated question
  rather than resolving it either way.
