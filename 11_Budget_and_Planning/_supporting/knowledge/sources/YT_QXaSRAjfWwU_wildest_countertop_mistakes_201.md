## Furniture / Built-ins — Countertop Installation Checklist

- **Level lower modules with a laser level (self-leveling laser on a tripod, horizontal plane) before laying the countertop**, checking every module's top edge aligns to that plane — faster, easier, and more accurate than the common bad method of laying the countertop first and leveling with a bubble level resting on top.
- **Verify every lower module is in its exact designed position (check piece-by-piece against the project drawings) before installing the countertop** — a post-countertop-install position error requires extremely disruptive rework.
- **Verify all lower modules are tied/joined together before countertop install** — much faster to fix module connections beforehand than after.
- **Verify every module has both metal countertop-fixing brackets installed before laying the countertop** — otherwise there's nothing to screw the countertop down to once it's in place.
- **Verify mounting rails/planks are present on every module (front and back, except the oven module) with pre-drilled screw holes, before countertop install** — missing rails/holes can only be added after the countertop is already resting on top, which reliably over-drills an oversized hole through both the rail and the countertop itself, so the fixing screw spins in the hole instead of clamping the countertop down.
- **Unpack and inspect countertop pieces for shipping damage (especially corners) before installing** — stop and contact the seller immediately if any defect is found; post-install damage claims aren't accepted. Corroborates the identical rule already in this store for kitchen-module assembly (`yt_Y_Tuxcd-VxM`), now extended to countertops specifically.
- **Installation sequence: always start with the smallest countertop piece / the piece covering the corner module.**
- **Push the first (corner) countertop piece snug against the wall/corner with zero gap** — critical, because installing a larger piece first makes it impossible to later fit a smaller piece against it without breaking the sealant layer between them, producing a non-watertight joint that lets water seep in.
- **Countertop overhang spec: 25-30mm, consistent along the full length** — achieved by pushing all modules fully to the wall first, then pulling them out slightly until the overhang is uniform. Too little overhang exposes the raw front edge of door fronts (ugly); too much overhang blocks upper drawer/door opening on handleless (push-to-open) fronts.
- **Countertop fixing sequence**: fix with just 2 screws first (front rail of the end module and the corner module), then re-verify alignment with a carpenter's square along the whole length before driving the remaining screws — the first screws frequently shift the countertop slightly, so checking only after fully fastening means backing out and redoing every screw.
- **Critical sealant rule for joining two countertop pieces**: joining without sealant guarantees the countertop — and the cabinet module beneath the joint — will swell/warp from water ingress. Apply a continuous sealant bead along the full length of the joining plate, minimum 3mm thick, let it set ~5 minutes, then press the plate onto the larger countertop piece with light pressure; sealant should visibly squeeze out — if none does, too little was applied and the plate must be removed, cleaned, and re-sealed with the correct amount. `confirmed`, explicit mechanism and QC check.
- **Wipe/remove excess sealant immediately while wet, never after it cures** — removing cured excess sealant pulls out sealant that needs to remain in the joint, breaking the seal.
- **Let a sealed countertop joint sit ~2 hours to let the sealant partially cure before continuing.**
- **Fixing-screw length for the joining plate: 16mm.**
- **DIY-installation recommendation, with a class caveat**: only DIY-install a countertop yourself for an economy or mid-range kitchen; for a genuinely premium/high-end kitchen you paid full price for, hire a professional installer instead.
