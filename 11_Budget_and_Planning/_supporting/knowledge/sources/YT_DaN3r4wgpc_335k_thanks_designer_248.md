## Rules / Heuristics — Genuinely New Points

- **A whole-apartment "where does routine task X actually happen" check should be run as a final systematic pass, not assumed solved by room-level fixture lists.** The source's own worked example: after placing all major rooms, it explicitly checks "but where does the client dry laundry after it's washed?" — ruling out the hallway (unsightly), the wardrobe (no room), the bedroom/living-room/kitchen (all judged inappropriate) — before landing on the loggia as the only remaining option, then engineering that space (raised threshold, insulated floor) specifically to support it. **General rule: after placing all primary rooms/fixtures, walk through each routine household task (laundry drying, coat storage, cleaning-supply access, etc.) and confirm it has an explicitly designated location** — don't assume a task is solved just because its primary fixture (e.g. a washing machine) has a spot.
- **A ventilation/duct box's wall reference point should be measured from its most-protruding actual dimension, not its nominal/labeled size** — reiterates and reinforces this vault's existing riser-plumb-survey technique (chunk 1, `Eq0kg2hD-Ws`/`sJ8UZj36TMQ`) applied here to a ventilation duct box specifically, to prevent a wall from having to be rebuilt narrower once the real obstruction is measured.
- **A kitchen-to-living-room open threshold/passage should be sized to let two people pass each other comfortably — a specific data point of 900mm, distinct from this store's existing 1.2m family/child-traffic corridor figure**, suggesting the exact width needed depends on the specific traffic pattern (a kitchen-living passage vs. a general hallway) rather than one universal number.
- **When an existing wall is already thicker than a false wall would need to be, recess a radiator into that existing wall directly rather than building a new false wall** — a more material-efficient technique than defaulting to a false wall whenever radiator concealment is needed, worth checking wall thickness against radiator depth before committing to new construction.
