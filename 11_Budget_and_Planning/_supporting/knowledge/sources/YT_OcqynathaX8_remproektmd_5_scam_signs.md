## Mistakes / Warnings — Durable Facts & Rules (5 red flags)

1. **Refusal to sign a written contract**, with the excuse "we're not a formal company, we're a good crew that works by referral." RemProektMD's rebuttal: a contract can be signed with an individual ("физическое лицо"), not only a registered company — it still makes that individual liable for the work performed. The contract should also state the agreed sum for the work.
2. **Refusal to produce an itemized quote ("смета")** — instead only a rough total or a bare $/m² rate is given, with no itemized breakdown. RemProektMD's stated real-world consequence: once the client pays the full agreed sum, workers commonly claim specific tasks were "never included" in the agreement — a dispute that a proper itemized smeta (not "bare numbers, let's shake hands and part ways") would have prevented by making the scope of every included task explicit up front. Also flags that unforeseen work commonly arises mid-renovation and should be accounted for in the smeta process, not left as a later surprise.
3. **Claiming to be a "universal" crew that does everything** (electrical, plumbing, painting, plastering, tiling, etc.). RemProektMD's stated professional-experience counter-claim: **in his experience, a person can master at most one or two construction trades to a professional level in a career** — a self-described "universal" builder is, in his account, either (a) genuinely skilled in one specific area (e.g. tiling) while doing other trades (electrical, painting) only adequately, or (b) not genuinely skilled in *any* of the claimed trades, doing everything only superficially ("по верхам"). Framed as a real caution against hiring a self-described generalist for a full renovation. `single-account`/professional opinion.
4. **Refusal to show completed past projects.** RemProektMD's stated norm: any legitimate, long-operating contractor has finished projects and — with the former client's permission — can readily arrange a viewing, since a contractor who finished a project without major problems typically retains a workable relationship with that former client. A contractor unwilling/unable to arrange this is a red flag.
5. **Unrealistically short quoted timelines for a full ("капитальный") renovation** — quotes of 2-3 weeks, or even a full month, for a renovation covering electrical, plumbing, plastering, and painting work together. RemProektMD's stated floor: a genuine full renovation covering all these trades needs **a minimum of 2-3-4 months**. A quote well under that minimum signals one of two outcomes: either the timeline is fictional and the real duration will run much longer than promised, or the work will genuinely be rushed and delivered at materially lower quality, causing problems later.
