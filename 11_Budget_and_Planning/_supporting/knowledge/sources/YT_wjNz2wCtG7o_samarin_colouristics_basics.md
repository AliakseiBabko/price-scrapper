## Design Concept — Contrast Vocabulary and a Balance Model

- **⚠️ Two named selection modes, both stated by the author to apply to interiors as well as painting** ("то же самое в интерьере можно использовать", "например при составлении интерьера"):
  - **"Большой контраст" (large contrast)** — pairing diametrically opposite wheel hues. Three named pairs: red+green, orange+blue, violet+yellow. Justified by natural precedent (violets with yellow centres, orange irises with blue flecks, a red rose against green foliage), on the reasoning that natural selection has already filtered for what reads well.
  - **"Малый контраст" (small contrast), also called "подбор на нюансе"** — pairing near-neighbour hues, holding the composition inside a narrow gamut with fine gradations between two spectral colors. Corroborates, under different vocabulary, the analogous scheme described independently by Artcolor.Online in this same batch.
- **⚠️ A dated-interiors observation used as a caution, not just history**: 20th-century and specifically Soviet interiors were assembled almost exclusively "на нюансе" — sofa matched to wallpaper, wallpaper matched to curtains — producing a restrained gamut the author calls "немножко скучный… очень так однообразно." Large contrast displaced it. Concretely useful because it describes what over-applied nuance-matching actually looks like in a real Soviet-era housing stock.
- **⚠️ The "волчок" (spinning-top) balance model** — the author's own recommended method, distinct from the named-scheme lists: picture the color wheel as a spinning top that has to stay upright, and distribute the chosen colors as weights around it so it stays balanced. Configurations he says stay upright: two weights directly opposite each other; three colors where two are near-neighbours ("на нюансе") and one sits opposite that pair — **with more of the single opposite color and less of the two neighbours**; or several colors spaced evenly, in equal amounts. This is a proportion-and-position heuristic rather than a fixed scheme, and it reaches independently the same conclusion as the 60/30/10 sources in this batch: unequal amounts, with the distribution tied to where the hues sit on the wheel.
- **Gamut-count caution with a stated mechanism**: compositions using the whole spectrum are called "полноцветные" or, derisively, "светофоры" (traffic lights), and are said to be very hard to control. The reason given is perceptual, not stylistic — **the brain processes a smaller number of colors more easily** — so restraining the gamut is argued as a legibility measure.
- **Definitional aside**: in this framework white is not a color but light/tone, the thing that controls lightness and darkness, held separate from the spectral hues.
