## Design Concept — Durable Facts & Rules

### Three distinct concepts, defined and distinguished

- **Текстура (texture)**: the material's own visual signature/pattern
  from its origin or processing — wood grain/rings, stone veining, a
  fabric's weave pattern. Used to identify a material at a glance even
  without touching it.
- **Фактура (surface finish/relief micro-texture)**: how a material's
  surface has been worked — polished, oiled, given a light natural
  relief — independent of the underlying texture/pattern. The same wood
  texture (e.g. American walnut) can be given very different фактура
  finishes (fully polished, oiled/natural, lightly embossed), each
  producing a genuinely different felt atmosphere even though photos of
  all three can look visually similar.
- **Рельеф (relief)**: a 3D pattern visible from a distance, created by
  real light and shadow — either natural (rough unfinished stone) or
  deliberately manufactured (classical bas-relief, 3D MDF/veneer panels,
  slat/rail systems). Reads as an accent/focal element in its own right
  (echoes the presenter's own prior "dominant + accent" framework), not
  just a background finish.

### Material-imitation authenticity checklist

- **Check the stated pattern-repeat interval before buying an imitation
  material (tile, laminate/porcelain granite)** — printed on the
  packaging or manufacturer's site. A real natural material never
  repeats its exact grain/vein pattern; a cheap imitation with a short
  repeat interval reads as an obvious "copy-paste" tiling once installed.
  **A repeat interval of 8+ pieces before the same printed pattern
  recurs is given as a workable threshold** — allows spreading
  identical-looking pieces apart during installation for a more natural
  combination.
- **Tactile/thermal plausibility must match the material being
  imitated, not just the visual print** — touching a stone-look surface
  should feel heavy and cool; if a cheap plastic panel imitating stone
  instead feels light and warm to the touch, the imitation reads as
  fake regardless of how convincing the printed pattern looks. **Prefer
  porcelain tile (керамогранит) over plastic panels specifically for
  imitating stone**, since porcelain's thermal/weight properties are
  closer to real stone; alternatively, choose a neutral plastic color
  and stop trying to pass it off as a stone imitation at all.
- **High-end finish manufacturers run dedicated labs replicating both
  the visual texture and the thermal/tactile properties of the natural
  material being imitated** — cited example: a Porcelanosa factory lab
  near Valencia, where the speaker personally could not reliably
  distinguish real stone from its porcelain-tile replica by touch,
  detecting at most a slight temperature difference.

### Balance rules for combining materials

- **Pair cold-reading materials (stone, glossy porcelain) with
  warm-reading materials (wood, natural fabric)** to avoid an all-cold
  "stone box" effect; conversely, an interior built entirely from warm
  natural materials risks reading as dated or overly decorative —
  introduce a cooler accent/dominant material to rebalance either
  direction.
- **Matte finishes generally read warmer, glossy finishes read colder**
  — usable as a deliberate lever for nudging a material's perceived
  temperature without changing the material itself.
- **One dominant texture/pattern per composition; keep the rest
  deliberately neutral/uniform.** A strong, large-scale wood-grain
  pattern on a floor or wall calls for simpler, more uniform surfaces
  elsewhere (including furniture) — combining several strong,
  similarly-scaled textures or patterns (e.g. patterned wallpaper next
  to a heavily-grained wood surface) makes them visually compete rather
  than combine.
- **Relief can itself act as a visual dominant via light/shadow** —
  avoid placing multiple strong reliefs near each other, and avoid
  pairing a relief with a large-scale flat pattern/texture of similar
  visual scale, since both compete for the same "focal" role. **Flat-
  printed "3D wallpaper" that fakes relief via a printed pattern rather
  than real depth is flagged as generally a poor substitute** for actual
  dimensional relief, since real relief depends on genuine light/shadow,
  not a printed illusion.
