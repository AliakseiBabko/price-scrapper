# Case Study — Yana Vrublevskaya Studio Renovation, "Minsk Mir" (2023)

**Source**: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8zm_5NjbGNA_yana_vrublevskaya_minsk_mir_10k_studio|full extraction note]] — "Ремонт за 10000$. Цены на все. Минск Мир," Яна Врублевская, YouTube, published 2023-05-01.

## Why this case study exists

Per this project's broadened case-study definition (coherence over "one single verified real project" as a hard gate): this is a real, itemized, arithmetic-checkable renovation budget, documented first-person by the apartment's own owner — and it's the **closest structural match in this store to this project's own self-managed plan** found so far. Unlike the labor-only 44 m² смета (this store's previous closest comparable), this source covers **both** rough works (labor+materials) **and** the full finish/fixture/appliance/furniture shopping list for one real, complete project — a fuller picture of what a self-managed renovation actually costs end to end.

## 1. Project Basics

- **Region**: "Минск Мир" ("Minsk World," a large new-build development) — named in the video title and YouTube location metadata (level 2), but **never spoken aloud anywhere in the transcript** (checked directly, zero matches). Treat as level-2-only regional evidence, same tier as the FLATART (`yt_H61xa8n2nTk`) source — not "confirmed Minsk" on the same footing as this store's strongest sources.
- **Unit type**: a studio apartment, per video tags ("Студия 30 метров" / "Ремонт студии 27 метров" — the two tags disagree with each other on exact size, and no m² figure is stated in the transcript itself). **No $/m² figure is computed in this case study** as a result — the exact denominator is unconfirmed.
- **Date**: 2023 — the **second-oldest source in this store**, after FLATART (2017). Treat all pricing as historical; do not blend with 2025/2026 figures without inflation/exchange-rate adjustment.
- **Delivery model**: **Self-Managed / Itemized**, real project — individual specialists hired per trade (plumber, electrician, plasterers, a dedicated corner-finishing specialist, laminate installer), materials/fixtures/appliances/furniture sourced independently (mostly via the Onliner.by marketplace).
- **Currency**: BYN throughout, with the speaker's own running USD conversion — notably, she explicitly tracks the exchange rate moving from 2.6 → 2.7 → 2.8 BYN/USD **during the project's own duration**, and applies the final 2.8 rate retroactively across all figures for the video. The clearest first-person illustration in this store of why year-aware, source-specific exchange-rate handling matters (see `.agents/skills` price-conversion policy) — not just an abstract rule, but a real project visibly affected by rate drift.

## 3. Finish Materials, Fixtures, Appliances & Furniture (BYN, itemized — full list in extraction note)

Grouped totals, not individually re-tabled here (see the extraction note for the full ~50-line itemization): bathroom/WC fixtures and doors, electrical/lighting finish hardware, a full kitchen (cabinetry + built-in appliances), flooring, and starter furniture. Notable: several items were paid at a discount via a specific cashback card (kettle, axial fan, wifi router, garment steamer — the steamer at 83 BYN was effectively ~3 BYN out of pocket after cashback), illustrating a real, if modest, cost-saving lever this store hadn't recorded before.

## 4. Total Budget Outcome — the headline number

**$10,350 spent to date, ~$2,607 still needed at time of filming ⇒ ≈$12,957 total**, against the video's own **$10,000 target title** — a real **~30% overshoot** on a disciplined, itemized, self-managed budget. This is a genuinely useful data point independent of the specific currency/region: even careful line-by-line tracking (which this project clearly did — the level of itemization here is unusually granular) doesn't guarantee hitting an initial target. Worth weighing against this project's own budget-buffer assumption (currently 10–15%, per `Budgeting_Guide.md` §1) — this single data point alone doesn't justify raising that figure, but it's a concrete illustration of the kind of real-world overshoot the buffer exists to absorb.

## 5. Practical Soft-Cost Checklist — genuinely new content type for this store

A list of small, easy-to-forget line items around the edges of a renovation, most of which aren't "renovation work" in the usual labor/materials sense: a paid new-build handover inspection service (55 BYN), a temporary used toilet installed on day one of rough plumbing (35 BYN — explicitly framed as a must-do-immediately step), a per-riser water-shutoff fee paid to the management company (30 BYN), an emergency same-evening outlet relocation after a late appliance-install-method change (20 BYN), door-frame block units (13 BYN), a developer/registration administrative fee (330 BYN, exact purpose not fully clear), a balcony baseboard (30 BYN), one year of apartment insurance (31 BYN), and a fiber-optic repair after accidental damage (50 BYN). None of these appear in any other source processed for this topic so far — see the extraction note's "Practical/Soft-Cost Line Items" section for the full list with context.
