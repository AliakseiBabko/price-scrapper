> [!NOTE]

This project's own plan doesn't have a fixed "master bedroom" room — see **Household Plan** below. This page collects every accumulated bedroom-design fact/formula in one place, written so it applies to *whichever space is currently functioning as the adults' bedroom*, and is cross-referenced from each phase's actual room page rather than owning a room number itself.

## Household Plan (context for applying this page)

Per explicit user direction (2026-08-20), this project's adult sleeping arrangement is planned in two phases, not as one fixed room:

- **Phase 1 (now)**: the adults' bedroom is the small room — [[06_Small_Bedroom/Small_Bedroom_Index|Small Bedroom]], ~9.3 m² — used temporarily while children are young. This room's own long-term intended use, once Phase 2 begins, is **specifically "Kids Room #2" for the boy** (clarified 2026-08-20 — not a generic study/guest room), so its bedroom-furnishing plan should be treated as a temporary configuration, not the room's permanent design. During Phase 1, [[04_Living_and_Dining_Room/Living_and_Dining_Index|the Living & Dining Room]] has **no bedroom function at all** — it's purely a living room until Phase 2 activates.
- **Phase 2 (later, once children are old enough for school, ~3-4 years from 2026-08-20) — corrected 2026-08-20 after an initial mis-scoping**: there is **no separate, fixed living room and dining room** in this plan. `04_Living_and_Dining_Room` plus the adjoining `03_Kitchen` together form one combined ~24-25 m² open-plan space whose identity changes by time of day. **At night**, a glass sliding partition divides the combined space into a kitchen zone and a bedroom zone. **During the day**, the bed is a **wall-bed/Murphy-bed transformer unit that folds away into a wall or wardrobe**, and the space reverts to a shared family living/dining/kitchen area (the everyday dining table itself lives in the kitchen zone, not this room — an extended table is set up here only for special events). **This phase has no dedicated planning page yet** — see [[04_Living_and_Dining_Room/Living_and_Dining_Index|Living & Dining Index]] for the current (outdated, pre-transformer-bed) daytime zoning plan, flagged there as needing a redesign once the partition/bed design is actually worked out. The Phase 2 timeline also drives a **kids'-room split**: `05_Kids_Room` becomes the girl's room exclusively, and `06_Small_Bedroom` becomes "Kids Room #2" for the boy, once the adults vacate it.
- **Zone-division layout, added 2026-08-20 (verbal, further clarification)**: within the combined space, the **bigger portion (window side) is the living/bedroom zone**, and the **smaller portion (opposite the window, near the room's entrance) is the kitchen zone**, holding the dining table. The table is positioned deliberately clear of the sliding door's travel path so the door can fully extend at night to seal the zones apart. Also confirms the earlier "head office bedroom" phrase was a voice-recognition misfire for "adults' bedroom" — no home-office function is intended. See [[04_Living_and_Dining_Room/Living_and_Dining_Index|Living & Dining Index]] and [[Family_Requirements]] §5 for the full detail and verbatim quote (best-effort transcription, unconfirmed against the actual floor plan).
- **⚠️ Install the Murphy bed now, during the current (Phase 1) renovation — activate in Phase 2**: per explicit user direction (2026-08-20), the wall-bed/Murphy-bed unit and its supporting infrastructure (wall reinforcement, wardrobe/wall cavity, any wiring it needs) should be **physically installed as part of the current Phase 1 build**, even though it stays inactive/unused for ~3-4 years until Phase 2 begins. This is the same **future-flexible planning** principle already established elsewhere in this knowledge store — see the intermediate store's `13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Walls_and_Paint.md` section "Future-Flexible Planning & Regulatory Workarounds": *"A currently-open floor plan can be pre-engineered for a future partition wall or future electrical need — pre-installed anchor points let a later life-stage change... be executed cheaply without demolishing or redoing surrounding finishes."* The Murphy-bed-now directive is a direct application of that same rule to this project's own bedroom plan, not a new/unrelated idea.
- **⚠️ This page's fold-away-bed technique directly applies to Phase 2, and predates knowing this project's own plan**: this store already recorded a **fold-away-bed-into-storage-wall technique** from an independent source (`yt_JwB6L-tYd7I`) — converting a room into flexible-use space on demand by folding a bed into a wall/storage unit — before this project's own Phase 2 plan was described. That's a genuine corroboration that this is a real, workable technique, not a coincidence engineered to match. See that fact in the intermediate knowledge store's Planning Rules section for the sourced detail.

**Practical implication for every rule below**: read "the bedroom" as "whichever space is functioning as the adults' bedroom in the phase being planned," not as a specific numbered room. **Most of the 12-item sequence below assumes a conventional bed occupying fixed floor space in a full four-wall room — Phase 2's fold-away wall-bed transformer changes several of these assumptions materially**: there's no fixed "bed center point," no permanent bedside-nightstand footprint (unless the nightstands are also part of the transformer unit or wall-mounted separately), and clearance/curtain-zone/false-wall steps need to be re-derived around the transformer unit's own footprint and swing/fold path, not a static bed position. Don't assume the sequence transfers unchanged to Phase 2 — flag this explicitly when that phase is actually designed, and look specifically for wall-bed/Murphy-bed design guidance (clearance for the bed's fold path, mattress thickness vs. cabinet depth, what can/can't be stored on or near a wall-bed) rather than assuming the standard sequence's bed-placement formula (step 6) applies as-is.

## The 12-Item Design Sequence (source: `hVFmcw1H2Rk`, #314)

**Core methodology: place bedroom elements in a specific priority order, each one locking in real dimensions the next step depends on** — structural/fixed elements first, furniture next, decor/electrical-detail last. Full detail and reasoning in the [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_hVFmcw1H2Rk_bedroom_design_sequence_masterclass_314|source extraction note]]; summarized here:

1. **Interior door** — position against one wall, not centered, to maximize the remaining run for the wardrobe. 10cm reveal on the near side; opening width = leaf width + 10cm. **Leaf width: 80cm optimal** (90cm excessive/sags prematurely; 70cm acceptable but tight; 60cm only for a WC/technical room). Swings inward, never outward.
2. **Main light switch** — inside the room, on the handle side of the door. **90cm from finished floor, 15cm from the opening's edge.** Must be a pass-through (two-way) switch so the light can be turned off from either side of the bed.
3. **Wardrobe** — on the entry wall (makes the room more compact/square, improving daylight reach). Custom, floor-to-ceiling, **60cm depth**. Stops ~30cm short of the door opening so the switch ends up centered between door and wardrobe once casings are installed. Door-panel width: **35-60cm**.
4. **False wall** (conceals risers, recesses the radiator) — **150mm depth**, placed right after the wardrobe to lock in the room's true footprint before furnishing. **Radiator-niche formula**: lower return 15cm; niche height = radiator inter-axial dimension + 20cm; upper return minimum 5cm (choose a different radiator and recalculate if this comes out under 5cm).
5. **Curtain zone** — **20cm depth**, right after the false wall/wardrobe, locking in the final dimension before bed placement (wide enough for a motorized track + tulle).
6. **Bed** — center point = midpoint between wardrobe and curtain zone. **Sleep-surface width: 180cm default** (160cm if space is tight, 200cm for larger occupants/ample space). Frame width usually exceeds sleep-surface width by 10-20cm. **Clearance each side: minimum 50cm, ideally 60cm** — shrink the bed, not the clearance, if the two conflict.
7. **Nightstands** — right after the bed, before bedside electrical. **40×40cm, 50-55cm height.**
8. **Bedside electrical** — on the nightstand's own center axis, a block at **70cm from the floor**: night-light switch nearest the bed, then a pass-through main-light switch, then 1-2 outlets. Mirror on both sides.
9. **Night light** — individually switched per side. Cable outlet on the same axis, **1m from the floor**, 20cm of slack. Cable: 3-core VVGnG-LS, 1.5mm².
10. **TV zone** — wall-hung console, width ≈ sleep-surface width, centered on the bed's axis. Set its height only after step 12 (dresser/makeup table) is placed, matching that piece's height (or its third drawer tier if it has 4).
11. **TV-zone electrical** — two groups on the console's center axis: Group 1 at 160cm (TV/internet/power outlets + cable channel down); Group 2 at 10cm from the console's top edge (2 outlets + internet for a set-top box + cable channel up).
12. **Makeup table/dresser** — last, filling the leftover space between the TV zone and curtain zone. **60-100cm width.** Mirror starts directly at the countertop; hairdryer/styling outlets go under the table, not exposed.

## Supporting Facts From Other Sources (cross-referenced, not duplicated)

- **Wardrobe mirror placement**: never install mirrored wardrobe-door panels facing the bed — peripheral-vision motion from mirrors facing the sleep area is a real, common-enough discomfort that ~65% of this practitioner's clients ask to swap mirrored panels for frosted/matte ones after living with it. Mirrors should face *into* the closet, not out toward the room. [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mistakes_client_testimonial|source note]]
- **Walk-in-closet mirror clearance formula** (if a walk-in closet is built with an internal mirror): minimum width = 1m (mirror standing clearance) + 60cm (clothing zone) + 10cm (sliding-door track) = **1.7m minimum**. Below that, don't build a walk-in with an internal mirror. [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mistakes_client_testimonial|source note]]
- **Walk-in-closet access point**: enter from a shared hallway/vestibule, not from inside the bedroom itself — preserves the bedroom's own wall space for a proper wardrobe with mirrored doors, without sacrificing the closet's privacy (a closed door already grants that). [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_u28Gqg_V7eE_walkin_access_from_hallway_not_bedroom|source note]]
- **Don't shrink a bedroom to bring the TV closer** — compensate for viewing distance with a larger TV instead; keep the room's full usable size. [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mistakes_client_testimonial|source note]]
- **Never use recessed spotlights inside an enclosed mirrored closet** — even many spotlights produce harsh directional shadows on skin/clothing; use a glowing/luminous ceiling panel instead for even light.
- **⚠️ No spotlight or track fixture directly above the bed, and no motion-sensor-triggered night light near the bed** — two independent bedroom-lighting cautions from a second source, corroborating and extending this store's existing central-pendant-over-bed caution (see [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Lighting_Design|Lighting Design]]'s general reclining-glare rule): (1) track lighting or spotlights over the bed blind you the same way a central pendant does — if a fixture must go there, use one with a narrow optic aimed away from the sleeping eye-line, not a broad downward spread; (2) a motion-sensor-triggered night light sounds convenient but fires constantly on incidental bed motion (a foot or arm hanging off the edge, a dropped blanket corner) — wire bedroom night lighting to a manual switch instead. **A bedside reading light (bra/sconce) should be directional, aimed precisely at the activity** (book, phone, laptop), ideally with adjustable brightness — this is the source's own stated best implementation, not a generic bedside lamp. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]
- **Glass sliding-door dampers**: never skip soft-close dampers on heavy glass wardrobe doors — an unintentionally forceful slide can shatter the panel without one. Directly relevant to Phase 2's own glass divider door (verify the divider itself has proper soft-close/damping hardware, not just the wardrobe).
- **Irregular-shell bedroom techniques** (trapezoidal rooms, curved walls, acute/obtuse corners): see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_RF6FdHk_jgE_wildest_one_bedroom_trapezoid_layout|source note]] for a full worked case — relevant if either phase's actual room geometry turns out non-rectangular.
- **Fold-away-bed-into-storage-wall technique (directly relevant to Phase 2)**: a bed that folds into a wall/storage unit can convert a room into flexible-use space on demand — a real client wishlist item, initially assumed impossible to fit, solved this way. [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_JwB6L-tYd7I_designer_dunk_400k_replan_254|source note]]

## 10 Common Bedroom Mistakes (source: `a_i8pGVa7-w`, Kruglov/Ontario)

A structured "mistakes" comparison list, largely complementary to the
12-item sequence above rather than overlapping it (that sequence is about
furniture/dimension placement order; this is about sound, climate,
lighting-scenario, and material/order pitfalls). Full detail in the
[[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_a_i8pGVa7-w_kruglov_bedroom_mistakes|source extraction note]]; summarized here:

- **Bedroom device noise thresholds, numeric**: Kruglov states **≤25dB is
  ideal, ≤30dB still comfortable, above 30dB becomes irritating** for any
  device running in the bedroom (AC indoor unit, breather, humidifier) —
  select against these thresholds rather than general-purpose specs.
- **AC airflow must never hit the bed directly** (legs, face) — mount the
  indoor unit off the bed's own axis; direct airflow during sleep is
  described as a real illness-risk pattern, not just discomfort.
- **A fresh-air breather needs a higher-capacity humidifier than an open
  window would**, since cold intake air holds far less moisture — a basic
  humidifier keeps pace with an open window, but a breather specifically
  dries the room faster and needs a "serious" unit to compensate.
- **Three lighting scenarios are required, not one fixture on one
  switch**: base/ambient, decorative (cornice/accent), and individual/task
  (bedside) — plus **2700-3000K as the comfort color-temperature range**
  and independent per-side switching (three two-gang switches) so each
  occupant controls their own main/decorative light.
- **Bed-to-rug clearance formula**: rug's near edge at least **50cm from
  the bed, ideally 60-70cm** — closer than that, the first step out of bed
  lands half on rug/half on bare flooring, undermining both feel and the
  "finished renovation" look. A distinct measurement from this page's
  existing bed-to-wall/furniture clearance formulas (step 6 above).
- **Textile presence (fabric headboard/panels, curtains, rug) measurably
  reduces echo/reverberation** in the room — an acoustic-comfort function
  distinct from wall soundproofing, not just styling.
- **Open storage (open shelving/wardrobes, unfaced dressers) reads as
  visual clutter in a bedroom specifically** — extends this page's
  existing closed-storage-preferred rule (previously recorded for the
  entryway) to the bedroom.
- **A vanity/makeup table in the bedroom is flagged as a friction point,
  not endorsed by default**: staggered wake/sleep schedules clash over its
  bright dedicated lighting, and it tends to accumulate visible clutter —
  relocate to a walk-in closet or elsewhere when the floor plan allows it.
- **Avoid bright/saturated colors and glossy finishes in a bedroom** —
  saturated color is psychologically stimulating (harder to fall asleep);
  gloss produces glare/reflection that works against the room's calm
  function — keep bedroom surfaces matte.
- Also restates (as corroboration, not new): soundproof the headboard
  wall if it borders a neighbor (see
  [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Soundproofing|Soundproofing]]);
  never mount linear/track lighting directly above the sleeping position
  (already on this page above).

## Master-Bedroom Zoning — WC Access via the Walk-In Closet, Not Directly Off the Bedroom (source: `gWAOrislxFY`, TimRemont)

A real 10m² "master bedroom" repartitioning case (bedroom + private
en-suite WC + walk-in closet, developer block wall partially demolished
and rebuilt in drywall): the practitioner's first-pass layout put doorways
to *both* the WC and the closet directly off the bedroom, then reworked it
mid-project so **the WC is entered through the walk-in closet instead**,
stating this reads as "more convenient and somehow more correct." This is
a distinct, additional rule alongside this page's existing walk-in-closet-
access note above (which is about the closet's *own* entrance from a
shared hallway) — both point the same direction: **minimize the number of
separate doorways punched directly into the bedroom's own walls**, routing
private-space access through the closet as an intermediate buffer instead.
**⚠️ Real cost lesson**: reworking an already-framed drywall partition's
door openings took ~2 days (vs. an expected couple of hours) because the
whole frame at that opening had to be unsheathed and rebuilt — a concrete
argument for finalizing a closet/WC/bedroom doorway layout *before*
framing a drywall partition, not after. Full detail in the
[[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_gWAOrislxFY_timremont_bedroom_a_to_z|source extraction note]],
which also records a real counter-example to this page's vanity-table
friction caution above (a bedside vanity zone built deliberately and
working fine for that specific couple — one more single-account data
point on a genuinely open question, not a resolution either way).

## Bedroom-vs-Closet Floor-Area Tradeoff, and a Door-Sill Soundproofing Technique (source: `U95vMOOhKH8`, TimRemont/Тимофей Клименко)

A real own-apartment case (~40m², 90% DIY): the practitioner **deliberately
shrank the bedroom below the developer's original size specifically to
enlarge the adjoining walk-in closet**, reasoning "a bedroom just needs to
let you sleep, that's it" — a concrete data point on trading bedroom floor
area for closet capacity once the room's core sleep function is covered,
worth weighing alongside the 12-item sequence above (which assumes the
room's given footprint rather than choosing how much footprint it gets).
**U-shaped walk-in closet clearance formula, more specific than this
page's existing mirror-clearance note**: ~60cm central walkway + ~60cm to
each side (this practitioner's own closet came out 1.8m×1.8m using it). A
drywall pocket/rolling door with a soft-close damper was built for the
closet doorway instead of a quoted ≈120,000 RUB glass sliding-partition
system — a real cost-driven alternative worth remembering distinct from
this page's own Phase 2 glass-divider discussion (a much larger
room-dividing partition, different scale of decision).

**⚠️ Door-sill soundproofing gasket/threshold** — installed at the bedroom
and bathroom doors specifically to reduce sound transfer around a
sleeping toddler; directly tested and confirmed effective, but **trades
off against passive under-door ventilation** — the practitioner's own
workaround is propping the door slightly open or relying on AC/mechanical
ventilation instead. A door-perimeter product distinct from this store's
existing wall/floor/ceiling soundproofing techniques (see
[[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Soundproofing|Soundproofing]], which
doesn't yet cover door-sill sealing — worth adding there in a future
round). Also: **wall-mounted bedside fixtures were avoided specifically to
avoid drilling into a frameless-soundproofed bedroom wall**, floor-standing
nightstands used instead — a concrete real-world instance of "don't
penetrate a soundproofing assembly," previously recorded on this store
only for screed beacons. Full detail, plus this source's Living/Dining-
relevant zoning content (a fold-out sofa substituting for a guest bed in
a space-constrained combined kitchen-living room), in the
[[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_U95vMOOhKH8_timremont_39sqm_walkin_office_zoning|source extraction note]].

## Windowless Bedroom — a Real Repartitioning Case (source: `1_nKzwNZD6w`, Petrishin-Stroi, Moscow, 2019)

A genuinely windowless bedroom (created by the apartment's own interior
repartitioning) was addressed two ways in this real case: **a frosted/
matte-glass "light window" cut into the wall at the end of the bedroom,
opening into the adjoining window-bearing living room** — borrows daylight
while the frosted glass preserves privacy — plus **a dedicated supply-air
vent from the ceiling** to compensate for having no operable window at
all. A concrete, reusable technique if this project ever creates an
interior windowless room of its own. Full detail (including a foam-block-
