## Cost Drivers & Common Mistakes

- WITALT reports: **The visible price gap between a surface-mounted and a built-in (behind-the-wall) plumbing fixture is commonly much smaller than the real installed-cost gap** once pipework, fittings, sleeves, and labor are counted — comparing device sticker price alone significantly understates what a built-in install actually costs. Price the full installation, not just the fixture, before deciding between the two.
- LAB-REMONT and Zemstandart/Alexey Zemskov advise: **Do not over-engineer plumbing cabinets** with multi-tiered manifolds, extra bypasses, and cosmetic LED backlighting — this inflates plumbing hardware budgets substantially without functional benefit; a simpler, adequately-protected setup (filters, regulator, leak protection — see [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Water_Inlet_Node_Components|Water-Inlet Node Components]]) covers the actual risk without the extra spend.
- Стройплощадка × Будни сантехника explains: **Do not bypass main water shutoff valves during plumbing modifications** — carries a severe leak/flooding risk that isn't worth the time saved. **This is also a legal liability boundary, not just a functional one**: per one source, everything upstream of the main shutoff is the building management company's responsibility zone (their own plumber must inspect/sign off on any riser-side work), while everything downstream is the resident's own liability — if a downstream leak damages a neighbor, the resident pays; if it originates upstream, the management company does.
- LAB-REMONT reports: **A decorative metal (aluminum/stainless) backing panel behind a manifold board is popular but against code in at least one jurisdiction cited**, per one source — an electrical fault near water risks energizing the metal panel, a shock hazard. Waterproofing membrane plus direct wall mounting is the safer, code-compliant alternative; grounding the manifold board itself is stated as mandatory regardless of backing material.
- Zemstandart/Alexey Zemskov recommends: **A collector/manifold node needs its own dedicated, adequately-sized, non-sagging access hatch** — a small standard 60×60cm aluminum hatch sags under load over time and makes future servicing harder; a purpose-built hatch (even one dressed up with a glued-on tile and shelving) is recommended instead.
- Стройплощадка × Будни сантехника advises: **Confirm with the building's management company whether "американка" (union/compression) fittings are permitted before the water meter** — some management companies explicitly forbid this specifically because a union joint there allows disconnecting/bypassing the meter (see [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Water_Inlet_Node_Components|Water-Inlet Node Components]]'s related tamper-vulnerability point).

## Buying / Practical Guidance

- RemontHochu.ru recommends pricing rough plumbing work by fixture "points," not by linear meters of pipe — a sink needs 2 points (hot + cold), for example; pricing by pipe length is a known vector for "turns out we needed more pipe, please pay more" upselling. A points-based quote, cross-checked against the design project's fixture count, is much harder to inflate after work starts. *(See the general version of this smeta-literacy principle in [[11_Budget_and_Planning/Budgeting_Guide|Budgeting Guide]], which applies the same logic to electrical rough-in.)*
- Добродушный сантехник describes: **A standard collector/manifold default is roughly 3 hot points + 5 cold points** (bath/shower mixer, bathroom sink, and kitchen sink on hot; those three plus toilet and washing machine on cold) — useful as a sanity-check baseline when reviewing a quote's fixture-point count, expandable to 7 points (adding dishwasher and a drinking-water filter as dedicated points rather than daisy-chained off an existing line).
- Добродушный сантехник explains: **A hot-water recirculation system (instant hot water at the tap) is a real but non-trivial add-on**, requiring a second collector to receive return flow, a small storage buffer (5–10L), a pressure-relief valve, an expansion tank, and a circulation pump — weigh its cost against the alternative of simply running a few dedicated long-run points, which can end up comparable in cost for a smaller apartment.
- LAB-REMONT and Добродушный сантехник advise: **If a flat has multiple riser pairs (e.g., two bathrooms, each on its own hot/cold pair), decide deliberately whether to combine them into one node or run two**: combining is generally cheaper (shared downstream filtration/collector equipment, though still needing separate valves/meters/check-valves per pair) but only works if the bathrooms are physically close; keeping pairs separate avoids losing all water during maintenance windows where the utility only shuts a subset of risers, at the cost of needing a second meter (with its own multi-year recertification cycle) if both pairs stay active.
- Добродушный сантехник and LAB-REMONT advise: **If risers/the node sit in a shared building utility closet (МОП) rather than inside the apartment**, the basic components (reducer, meter, coarse filter, shutoff, check valve) are often already installed there by the developer — route the leak-protection system's valve and wiring out to that shared cabinet too, so a detected leak shuts the whole trunk line feeding the apartment, not just interior branches; add a backup manometer and shutoff valve inside the apartment itself, but don't duplicate a second pressure reducer back-to-back with the first (no functional benefit).
