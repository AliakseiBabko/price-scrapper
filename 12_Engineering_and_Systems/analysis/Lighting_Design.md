# Electrical — Lighting Design

Covers general fixture-count guidance, bedroom lighting, ceiling light-source types, and a detailed practitioner case against recessed spotlights. Part of [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/Electrical_and_Lighting|Electrical & Lighting]].

## General Principles

**Recessed point fixtures with a good diffusing bulb are commonly sufficient** for even room illumination — a cited example: roughly 4 well-placed fixtures adequately lighting a ~30 m² room, versus installs with far more fixtures than the space actually needs (over-specification is a common, avoidable cost driver). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]

## Color Temperature Rules (added 2026-08-20)

- **⚠️ Never exceed 4000K anywhere in a residential space.** Comfortable range for living areas: **2700–4000K**. Warm light (lower K) reads as cozier and generally makes an interior look more expensive; **3000K is the single most universal default** — cozy enough without reading yellow, works in nearly any room. Reserve **neutral ~4000K light for pure task areas** — laundry rooms, walk-in closets, storage rooms, and specifically a kitchen worktop's own task lighting (see Kitchen note below) — not for general/ambient illumination. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]
- **⚠️ Every fixture running simultaneously in the same scene must share the same color temperature.** A ceiling fixture at 3000K next to a wall sconce at 4000K running together (e.g. evening TV-watching) reads as visibly mismatched and cheap, even though each fixture is individually fine. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]
- **⚠️ Evening/decorative lighting must be the same color temperature throughout the entire home, not just within one room.** A hallway's evening lighting at a different temperature than the bedroom/living room's evening lighting stands out as visually "off" the moment you move between rooms. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]
- **⚠️ Both over- and under-powering a fixture is a real mistake, not just under-powering.** Overly bright light in the evening measurably disrupts melatonin production and circadian rhythm, making it harder to wind down for sleep — this isn't just a comfort preference. Conversely, underpowered light in a zone that genuinely needs brightness (entryway, bathroom, walk-in closet) causes eye strain and faster visual fatigue from working in insufficient light. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

## Scenario-Based Lighting: the "Minimum Three Scenarios" Rule (added 2026-08-20)

**⚠️ Golden rule: every frequently-used room needs a minimum of three distinct lighting scenarios (scenes), not one or two fixtures wired as a single on/off group.** The single most common lighting mistake, per this source, is a room with only one (or at most two) light sources total — insufficient for how differently a room is actually used across a day (cleaning/task use vs. relaxed evening use vs. a specific secondary need like mirror grooming or reading). Worked room-by-room scenario sets: [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]

- **Bathroom (4 scenarios)**: main/task light (cleaning, grooming); decorative/evening dimmed light; dedicated mirror light (see the bathroom-specific note below); a night-light scenario for occasional nighttime use. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]
- **Kitchen-living (3 scenarios + task lighting)**: main light (overcast days, cleaning); evening/decorative light (dinner, movie ambiance); worktop task lighting (see Kitchen note below) plus separate dining-table task lighting (a pendant or a narrow-beam spot). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]
- **Bedroom (3-4 scenarios)**: main/task light; evening/decorative light (same temperature as the rest of the home); an individual reading light per side, ideally dimmable, aimed directly at the activity (book, phone, laptop) rather than general room-filling — the best implementation is a **directional** fixture, not just any bedside lamp; optionally, bias/ambient lighting for TV viewing if a bedroom TV is used (see Cinema-Mode Bias Lighting below). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]
- **Hallway/entrance (4 scenarios)**: main light; evening light; mirror light; a navigation/night-light scenario — see Night/Navigation Lighting below. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]

`single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

## Cinema-Mode Bias Lighting (added 2026-08-20)

**⚠️ Light the wall/zone around the TV itself, not the whole room, to reduce eye strain during viewing — a real physiological mechanism, not just ambiance.** Watching a bright screen in an otherwise dark room forces the eye to repeatedly readjust to a large contrast swing every time your gaze leaves the screen (checking a phone, glancing around) — a small sconce, a lit niche near the TV, or a bias-lighting product that colors itself to match the on-screen content all reduce that contrast without needing to light the whole room. Applies equally to a living-room TV and a bedroom TV. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

## Night/Navigation Lighting (added 2026-08-20)

**⚠️ A dedicated low-level navigation/night-light circuit, corroborating and extending this store's existing whole-apartment night-light-circuit guidance** (see [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Lighting_and_Electrical|Bathroom — Lighting & Electrical]]): implementation options include an LED strip run under the ceiling, baseboard-integrated lighting, a small fixture built into a wall-outlet-style faceplate (matching the switch/outlet product line, if the manufacturer offers one), or a plain recessed wall fixture — purpose is safe navigation (avoiding bumped corners/doors) without triggering a full bright light at night, typically routing between the bedroom and the kitchen or bathroom. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]

**⚠️ Avoid triggering navigation/night lighting from a motion sensor in a bedroom specifically — a real, previously-unrecorded failure mode.** It sounds convenient (light auto-triggers when a foot touches the floor at night), but in practice a motion sensor near a bed fires on any incidental motion — a foot or arm hanging off the mattress edge, a blanket corner dropping to the floor — producing frequent, genuinely irritating false triggers throughout the night. **Wire bedroom night lighting to a manual switch instead.** This is a bedroom-specific caution distinct from a hallway/corridor motion sensor, which doesn't have the same false-trigger exposure. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

## Avoid Directional/Spot Light Above Any Reclining Position (added 2026-08-20)

**⚠️ A recurring pattern across rooms, worth stating as one general rule: never place a spotlight, track fixture, or narrow-beam downlight directly above a spot where someone reclines and looks upward** — a bed, a sofa, a bathtub. This corroborates and generalizes the existing bathtub-reclining-glare point in the "Five Arguments Against Recessed Spotlights" section above and this page's own existing bedroom central-pendant caution. If a fixture must be positioned there anyway, use one with a narrow, carefully aimed optic (light directed away from the reclining eye-line) rather than a broad downward spread. `single-account`, `unverified`, though converging with the page's existing recessed-spotlight and central-pendant findings above. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

## Fixture Buying Criteria Beyond Color Temperature (added 2026-08-20)

**⚠️ A fixture's suitability depends on more than color temperature and looks — check before buying, not just from a catalog photo**: brightness/lumen output for the space, beam angle, CRI (color rendering index — how accurately the light renders true colors), smart-home protocol compatibility if relevant (e.g. DALI), and whether the fixture has a lens (affects beam control/glare). For a **business-plus or premium-tier project specifically, a proper lighting calculation from a lighting designer is recommended as close to mandatory** — for an economy/comfort-tier project, a full lighting-designer calculation is described as not always necessary; the more elaborate the project, the more this step matters. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

**For bedrooms, perimeter spot lighting is generally preferable to a single central pendant/chandelier directly over the bed** — a central fixture overhead creates glare when lying down and looking up; perimeter spots avoid this. Layer in a secondary/ambient lighting option (a lamp, a dimmed accent) alongside the main circuit for flexibility. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]

**Curtain niches and window sills/reveals are worth wiring**: a recessed curtain-track niche with a built-in LED channel hides the track hardware and doubles as sleek indirect lighting; power outlets built into a window sill or reveal give convenient access for laptops or seasonal decorative lighting without a visible extension cord. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]

## Fixture Type Taxonomy (added 2026-08-24)

- **Point/spot fixtures** split into recessed (built into ceiling or wall) and surface-mounted; surface-mounted further splits into **static** (fixed aim) and **adjustable/aimable** (has a mechanism to change beam angle/direction). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Track lighting** uses a magnetic-connector rail; the rail itself comes in three mounting types — recessed into wall/ceiling, surface-mounted, or suspended/pendant. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Light panels** (flat diffused-light panels, associated with Armstrong/cassette-panel ceiling systems) are a distinct fixture type from a chandelier even though some marketplace listings market a light panel under the "chandelier" (люстра) name — track as separate product categories when specifying. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **⚠️ LED strip mounting — real failure history**: roughly a decade ago, LED strips were commonly glued directly to a wall or ceiling and would detach/fall over time. **Current standard practice is to mount the strip inside a metal profile channel** (recessed or surface-mounted, with or without a diffuser lens) so the adhesive bonds to the metal profile rather than failing on the surface directly. `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Linear LED fixtures**: an aluminum profile housing pre-assembled with both the LED strip and its transformer/driver together, mounted the same three ways as track lighting (surface, recessed, suspended) — a distinct product from a bare LED-strip-in-profile install. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]

## Function-Based Lighting Categories (added 2026-08-24)

A complementary framing to the existing "minimum three scenarios per room" rule above — four functional light categories, cutting across rooms rather than counting per-room scenarios:

- **Base/main light**: even, shadow-minimizing light for everyday use. Best candidates: a chandelier or light panel (softest/most even distribution); point fixtures work only with soft/diffused beam control, not a hard narrow beam.
- **Accent light**: narrow-purpose light for one or two specific uses (kitchen task light, illuminated bathroom mirror, art/decor highlighting) — contrasted with a chandelier's "do anything" generality. Candidates: directional point fixtures, a track fixture focused on one zone, sconces, or a narrow-beam LED strip run.
- **Background/ambient light**: soft, unfocused light for atmosphere, not task visibility — often the fixture itself isn't visible, only its glow (LED strip inside a decorative ceiling cornice, or furniture-integrated cabinet lighting).
- **Decorative light**: light that itself forms a visual pattern (e.g. crystal chandeliers casting patterned shadow/light play), plus neon signage/fixtures whose primary purpose is decoration, not illumination.

[source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]

**Independent corroboration, with one new category (Marina Izmailova / ARCHWOOD, own design-project workflow, added 2026-08-25)**: an independently-derived four-category taxonomy — bright fill/task light, dimmed accent/mood light, architectural/decorative backlighting (stair/niche/curtain LED-profile, night lighting) — broadly matches the framing above from an unrelated practitioner. Adds a category not previously on this page: **"technical" light — motion-sensor-triggered automatic interior cabinet/closet lighting**, switching on when a door/hatch opens and auto-shutting after. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_LOw05Uk3rUk_archwood_6_stages_design_project|YT_LOw05Uk3rUk]]]

## Bedroom and Home Office Color Temperature Refinements (added 2026-08-24)

- **A single central chandelier as a bedroom's only light source, if used, should be 2700-3500K** — narrows this page's general 2700-4000K comfortable range specifically for a bedroom's main/central fixture. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Bedside reading light**: corroborates the existing directional-fixture guidance above; adds a caution against a hanging/pendant-style bedside fixture near the bed, cited as prone to breaking within roughly six months from incidental hand contact at night. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Home office/study: 4000-5000K recommended** — cooler than neutral, to help sustain focus without the drowsiness warmer bedroom-range light would induce. Recommends either two distinct brightness scenarios or one dimmable fixture spanning both. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]

## Bathroom Mirror and Night-Light Technique (added 2026-08-24)

- **⚠️ Mirror light must shine horizontally toward the face, not down from above and not by lighting the tile behind the mirror.** Lighting the surrounding tile rather than the mirror/face directly is called out as a common, ineffective mistake; overhead lighting casts facial shadows. Fixture should be built into or mounted on the mirror itself, or positioned at face height/side. Corroborates and extends this store's existing bathroom-mirror-lighting guidance (see [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Lighting_and_Electrical|Bathroom — Lighting & Electrical]]). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **A dimmed/low version of the mirror light can double as the bathroom's night-light**, avoiding a separate full-brightness night fixture — also usable during a bath to avoid harsh light while relaxing. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]

## Kitchen Lighting and Navigation/General Rules (added 2026-08-24)

- **Dining/eating area needs neutral-color light, not warm or cold** — neutral light makes food look more appetizing and is more comfortable regardless of time of day. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Countertop/prep zone needs strong, non-dim task light** — cited symptom: eyes tiring within ~15 minutes of chopping vegetables under insufficiently bright light. Corroborates existing kitchen-worktop task-lighting guidance with a concrete symptom/timeframe. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **⚠️ If the home has a staircase, it must have navigational/night lighting — no exception stated ("100%")**, implemented via motion-sensor-triggered fixtures, a single always-dim fixture left on overnight in one zone, or a manual single-switch "night mode" covering the whole home. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Plan lighting during renovation design, not as an afterthought** — a single generic chandelier as the sole light source (the old, pre-2000s default) is insufficiently functional for a modern renovation; mix multiple fixture types deliberately per task/zone rather than relying on one universal source. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]

## Current Design Trends and Outdated Patterns (added 2026-08-24)

Per designer Nikita Kuznetsov (Kruglov/Ontario) — trend-focused framing, complementary to the fixture-taxonomy and function-based content above:

- **⚠️ A single chandelier as a room's only light source now reads as outdated/tacky, not just insufficiently functional** — sharpens this page's existing framing into an explicit style judgment. A two-switch chandelier where flipping one switch turns off half the bulbs reads as burnt-out bulbs — a specific, avoidable visual mistake. Recessed point fixtures arranged in visible straight rows are also flagged as a dated layout convention (distinct from this page's separate glare/cost arguments against recessed spotlights). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Track lighting is currently the leading trend fixture type**: black, geometric forms (square/rectangular/L-shaped, no curves) with a wide range of swappable attachable heads. **Wall-bounce placement technique**: mount the track on the ceiling aimed at a wall, or on a wall aimed upward/outward, so light reaches the room indirectly via reflection rather than shining straight down — a deliberate indirect-lighting method distinct from the general background/ambient category above. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Chandeliers have shifted role from main light source to decor/accent**: either a large statement decorative piece, or a smaller zonal pendant over a table/furniture piece rather than the whole room. Point/spot fixtures now trend toward surface-mounted over recessed; where recessed is still used, larger adjustable-direction units are preferred over small fixed downlights. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Linear "light line" fixtures are a current trend alternative to track lighting** — parallel, crossing, or freeform layouts. **⚠️ Overuse caution**: too many lines, or too bright a strip, over-illuminates the space and reads visually flat, defeating the depth/volume a well-lit interior should have — fewer, deliberately-placed light elements preserve perceived depth better than many. Where a light line transitions from ceiling to wall, balance brightness so the wall segment doesn't glare into the eyes. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Perimeter cove lighting (ceiling and floor), shelf and countertop backlighting are all good practice** — colored LED accent lighting is explicitly excluded, called out as never having been stylish. **⚠️ Construction-sequencing implication**: wall-wash/perimeter lighting reveals every micro-imperfection in the wall surface — plan for genuinely flat/even wall finishing before specifying this technique. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **⚠️ Bedside sconces are often positioned as mood/"intimate" lighting rather than genuine reading light** — commonly mounted over the nightstand rather than directly over the actual reading position, making them functionally decorative despite being marketed/framed as reading sconces. Nuances (doesn't contradict) this page's existing directional-bedside-reading-light guidance above — the fixture needs to be positioned and aimed at the actual reading zone, not just labeled a reading sconce. A sconce aimed at the wall itself (rather than outward) adds visual depth and is a good look distinct from the reading-function question. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Main/base light should be neutral; accent/decor lighting (LED strips behind 3D panels, cove lighting) can deliberately be warm-toned for coziness** — a pairing rule for primary-vs-accent roles within one room, distinct from this page's existing same-scene-must-match rule for simultaneous same-function fixtures. Dim/muted light in select spots can meaningfully elevate a design, achieved correctly via an indirect/wall-reflected source (see wall-bounce technique above) rather than dimming an overhead fixture to near-nothing. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **⚠️ Explicit upper bound on scenario count, complementing the existing "minimum three scenarios" rule: don't exceed roughly 3-4 lighting scenarios per zone** — beyond that, extra scenarios stop getting used day-to-day and only get set up for guests, defeating the purpose of scene-based lighting. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]

## Ceiling Light Source Types

Three distinct ceiling light-source types come up repeatedly once you move past a single generic "downlight" mental model:

- **Perimeter LED strip** (run inside a stretch-ceiling baguette channel) is **decorative/supplementary only** — never sufficient as a room's main light source, even when told otherwise. It reads well as ambiance but cannot replace true task/main lighting. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iRbTlv5Ka9o_lighting_types_taxonomy|iRbTlv5Ka9o_lighting_typ]]]
- **Flat light panels** are bright and energy-efficient, but difficult to retrofit onto an *existing* stretch ceiling since they need mounting holes the ceiling wasn't prepped for — plan for them before the ceiling goes in, not after. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iRbTlv5Ka9o_lighting_types_taxonomy|iRbTlv5Ka9o_lighting_typ]]]
- **Softboxes** (fixtures that emit light from their full surface/sides, not just the underside) give the most even room-fill light at comparatively low wattage — this source's own stated preference among the three. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_iRbTlv5Ka9o_lighting_types_taxonomy|iRbTlv5Ka9o_lighting_typ]]]

## Five Arguments Against Recessed Spotlights — per Zemskov/Zemstandart

> [!NOTE]
> Demonstrated live by Zemskov in his own rented apartment (which has recessed spotlights he criticizes as "what not to do," not his own installed work) — `single-account`, but extensively demonstrated with real counts rather than asserted in the abstract. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt`]

1. **Constant bulb burnout, per Zemskov's own live count**: in his rented apartment, bulbs fully replaced ~2 months prior already showed 5 of a group out, 2 of 9 out elsewhere, 3 of 12 out elsewhere, and one hallway group down to 1 lit out of several — verified live by testing each switch individually to rule out a wiring fault. His stated mechanism: a recessed-spotlight bulb fails on the same schedule as any bulb; with 40 installed, the *aggregate* failure rate is 40× a single fixture's. Treat the mechanism (many fixtures = proportionally more visible failures) as the durable point, not his specific ~3-month figure. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt|20260810_recessed_spotli]]]
2. **Uneven "cone" light pattern**: on textured/patterned wallpaper, narrow spotlight cones create visibly uneven illumination that reads as a bumpy wall even when the wall is flat — worse on plain wallpaper than heavily-patterned (which already masks unevenness). [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt|20260810_recessed_spotli]]]
3. **Harsh reclined glare**: lying in a bathtub or reclined chair under spotlights (especially halogen) produces direct eye glare — routinely enough to get up specifically to switch them off mid-relaxation. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt|20260810_recessed_spotli]]]
4. **Poor actual room fill despite visual brightness**: a narrow downward beam mostly hits a dark floor that reflects little light back; walls get only the cone's edge, so ambient fill stays weak even with many fixtures lit — driving occupants to keep adding more light sources without fixing the underlying problem. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt|20260810_recessed_spotli]]]
5. **Higher total cost than one premium fixture**: each spotlight needs its own wiring run, most are 12V and need a step-down transformer (an added cost and failure point), and ongoing maintenance accrues on both bulbs and supply. An 18-spotlight installation shown cost several times more than a single high-end chandelier that would give brighter, more even, lower-maintenance light in the same space. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt|20260810_recessed_spotli]]]

**Bonus injury note**: spotlight glass diffuser lenses can heat up enough that their retaining mechanism fails and the lens falls and shatters — one real incident cited in the speaker's own unit. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt|20260810_recessed_spotli]]]
