# Electrical — Lighting Design

Covers general fixture-count guidance, bedroom lighting, ceiling light-source types, and a detailed practitioner case against recessed spotlights. Part of [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/Electrical_and_Lighting|Electrical & Lighting]].

## General Principles

**Recessed point fixtures with a good diffusing bulb are commonly sufficient** for even room illumination — a cited example: roughly 4 well-placed fixtures adequately lighting a ~30 m² room, versus installs with far more fixtures than the space actually needs (over-specification is a common, avoidable cost driver). [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]

## Color Temperature Rules (added 2026-08-20)

- **⚠️ Never exceed 4000K anywhere in a residential space.** Comfortable range for living areas: **2700–4000K**. Warm light (lower K) reads as cozier and generally makes an interior look more expensive; **3000K is the single most universal default** — cozy enough without reading yellow, works in nearly any room. Reserve **neutral ~4000K light for pure task areas** — laundry rooms, walk-in closets, storage rooms, and specifically a kitchen worktop's own task lighting (see Kitchen note below) — not for general/ambient illumination. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]
- **⚠️ Every fixture running simultaneously in the same scene must share the same color temperature.** A ceiling fixture at 3000K next to a wall sconce at 4000K running together (e.g. evening TV-watching) reads as visibly mismatched and cheap, even though each fixture is individually fine. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]
- **⚠️ Evening/decorative lighting must be the same color temperature throughout the entire home, not just within one room.** A hallway's evening lighting at a different temperature than the bedroom/living room's evening lighting stands out as visually "off" the moment you move between rooms. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]
- **⚠️ Both over- and under-powering a fixture is a real mistake, not just under-powering.** Overly bright light in the evening measurably disrupts melatonin production and circadian rhythm, making it harder to wind down for sleep — this isn't just a comfort preference. Conversely, underpowered light in a zone that genuinely needs brightness (entryway, bathroom, walk-in closet) causes eye strain and faster visual fatigue from working in insufficient light. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

## Scenario-Based Lighting: the "Minimum Three Scenarios" Rule (added 2026-08-20)

**⚠️ Golden rule: every frequently-used room needs a minimum of three distinct lighting scenarios (scenes), not one or two fixtures wired as a single on/off group.** The single most common lighting mistake, per this source, is a room with only one (or at most two) light sources total — insufficient for how differently a room is actually used across a day (cleaning/task use vs. relaxed evening use vs. a specific secondary need like mirror grooming or reading). Worked room-by-room scenario sets: [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]

- **Bathroom (4 scenarios)**: main/task light (cleaning, grooming); decorative/evening dimmed light; dedicated mirror light (see the bathroom-specific note below); a night-light scenario for occasional nighttime use. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]
- **Kitchen-living (3 scenarios + task lighting)**: main light (overcast days, cleaning); evening/decorative light (dinner, movie ambiance); worktop task lighting (see Kitchen note below) plus separate dining-table task lighting (a pendant or a narrow-beam spot). [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]
- **Bedroom (3-4 scenarios)**: main/task light; evening/decorative light (same temperature as the rest of the home); an individual reading light per side, ideally dimmable, aimed directly at the activity (book, phone, laptop) rather than general room-filling — the best implementation is a **directional** fixture, not just any bedside lamp; optionally, bias/ambient lighting for TV viewing if a bedroom TV is used (see Cinema-Mode Bias Lighting below). [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]
- **Hallway/entrance (4 scenarios)**: main light; evening light; mirror light; a navigation/night-light scenario — see Night/Navigation Lighting below. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]

`single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

## Cinema-Mode Bias Lighting (added 2026-08-20)

**⚠️ Light the wall/zone around the TV itself, not the whole room, to reduce eye strain during viewing — a real physiological mechanism, not just ambiance.** Watching a bright screen in an otherwise dark room forces the eye to repeatedly readjust to a large contrast swing every time your gaze leaves the screen (checking a phone, glancing around) — a small sconce, a lit niche near the TV, or a bias-lighting product that colors itself to match the on-screen content all reduce that contrast without needing to light the whole room. Applies equally to a living-room TV and a bedroom TV. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

## Night/Navigation Lighting (added 2026-08-20)

**⚠️ A dedicated low-level navigation/night-light circuit, corroborating and extending this store's existing whole-apartment night-light-circuit guidance** (see [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Lighting_and_Electrical|Bathroom — Lighting & Electrical]]): implementation options include an LED strip run under the ceiling, baseboard-integrated lighting, a small fixture built into a wall-outlet-style faceplate (matching the switch/outlet product line, if the manufacturer offers one), or a plain recessed wall fixture — purpose is safe navigation (avoiding bumped corners/doors) without triggering a full bright light at night, typically routing between the bedroom and the kitchen or bathroom. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]

**⚠️ Avoid triggering navigation/night lighting from a motion sensor in a bedroom specifically — a real, previously-unrecorded failure mode.** It sounds convenient (light auto-triggers when a foot touches the floor at night), but in practice a motion sensor near a bed fires on any incidental motion — a foot or arm hanging off the mattress edge, a blanket corner dropping to the floor — producing frequent, genuinely irritating false triggers throughout the night. **Wire bedroom night lighting to a manual switch instead.** This is a bedroom-specific caution distinct from a hallway/corridor motion sensor, which doesn't have the same false-trigger exposure. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

## Avoid Directional/Spot Light Above Any Reclining Position (added 2026-08-20)

**⚠️ A recurring pattern across rooms, worth stating as one general rule: never place a spotlight, track fixture, or narrow-beam downlight directly above a spot where someone reclines and looks upward** — a bed, a sofa, a bathtub. This corroborates and generalizes the existing bathtub-reclining-glare point in the "Five Arguments Against Recessed Spotlights" section above and this page's own existing bedroom central-pendant caution. If a fixture must be positioned there anyway, use one with a narrow, carefully aimed optic (light directed away from the reclining eye-line) rather than a broad downward spread. `single-account`, `unverified`, though converging with the page's existing recessed-spotlight and central-pendant findings above. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

## Fixture Buying Criteria Beyond Color Temperature (added 2026-08-20)

**⚠️ A fixture's suitability depends on more than color temperature and looks — check before buying, not just from a catalog photo**: brightness/lumen output for the space, beam angle, CRI (color rendering index — how accurately the light renders true colors), smart-home protocol compatibility if relevant (e.g. DALI), and whether the fixture has a lens (affects beam control/glare). For a **business-plus or premium-tier project specifically, a proper lighting calculation from a lighting designer is recommended as close to mandatory** — for an economy/comfort-tier project, a full lighting-designer calculation is described as not always necessary; the more elaborate the project, the more this step matters. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

**For bedrooms, perimeter spot lighting is generally preferable to a single central pendant/chandelier directly over the bed** — a central fixture overhead creates glare when lying down and looking up; perimeter spots avoid this. Layer in a secondary/ambient lighting option (a lamp, a dimmed accent) alongside the main circuit for flexibility. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]

**Curtain niches and window sills/reveals are worth wiring**: a recessed curtain-track niche with a built-in LED channel hides the track hardware and doubles as sleek indirect lighting; power outlets built into a window sill or reveal give convenient access for laptops or seasonal decorative lighting without a visible extension cord. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_ligh]]]

## Fixture Type Taxonomy (added 2026-08-24)

- **Point/spot fixtures** split into recessed (built into ceiling or wall) and surface-mounted; surface-mounted further splits into **static** (fixed aim) and **adjustable/aimable** (has a mechanism to change beam angle/direction). [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Track lighting** uses a magnetic-connector rail; the rail itself comes in three mounting types — recessed into wall/ceiling, surface-mounted, or suspended/pendant. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Light panels** (flat diffused-light panels, associated with Armstrong/cassette-panel ceiling systems) are a distinct fixture type from a chandelier even though some marketplace listings market a light panel under the "chandelier" (люстра) name — track as separate product categories when specifying. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **⚠️ LED strip mounting — real failure history**: roughly a decade ago, LED strips were commonly glued directly to a wall or ceiling and would detach/fall over time. **Current standard practice is to mount the strip inside a metal profile channel** (recessed or surface-mounted, with or without a diffuser lens) so the adhesive bonds to the metal profile rather than failing on the surface directly. `single-account`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Linear LED fixtures**: an aluminum profile housing pre-assembled with both the LED strip and its transformer/driver together, mounted the same three ways as track lighting (surface, recessed, suspended) — a distinct product from a bare LED-strip-in-profile install. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **⚠️ GX53 puck-style recessed downlight — a named product-type caution (Round 14 triage, added 2026-08-28)**: this format was popular roughly 7-10 years ago and is still widely used, but is specifically called out for **low CRI** (even bright output looks dull/washed-out) and **poor-quality integrated optics**, producing flat, uninteresting light — its one advantage being low cost. Concretizes this page's existing general "check CRI before buying" guidance with a specific fixture type to be wary of. Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario, `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_86fmWWVXark_kruglov_stop_using_trends|YT_86fmWWVXark]]]

## Function-Based Lighting Categories (added 2026-08-24)

A complementary framing to the existing "minimum three scenarios per room" rule above — four functional light categories, cutting across rooms rather than counting per-room scenarios:

- **Base/main light**: even, shadow-minimizing light for everyday use. Best candidates: a chandelier or light panel (softest/most even distribution); point fixtures work only with soft/diffused beam control, not a hard narrow beam.
- **Accent light**: narrow-purpose light for one or two specific uses (kitchen task light, illuminated bathroom mirror, art/decor highlighting) — contrasted with a chandelier's "do anything" generality. Candidates: directional point fixtures, a track fixture focused on one zone, sconces, or a narrow-beam LED strip run.
- **Background/ambient light**: soft, unfocused light for atmosphere, not task visibility — often the fixture itself isn't visible, only its glow (LED strip inside a decorative ceiling cornice, or furniture-integrated cabinet lighting).
- **Decorative light**: light that itself forms a visual pattern (e.g. crystal chandeliers casting patterned shadow/light play), plus neon signage/fixtures whose primary purpose is decoration, not illumination.

[source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]

**Independent corroboration, with one new category (Marina Izmailova / ARCHWOOD, own design-project workflow, added 2026-08-25)**: an independently-derived four-category taxonomy — bright fill/task light, dimmed accent/mood light, architectural/decorative backlighting (stair/niche/curtain LED-profile, night lighting) — broadly matches the framing above from an unrelated practitioner. Adds a category not previously on this page: **"technical" light — motion-sensor-triggered automatic interior cabinet/closet lighting**, switching on when a door/hatch opens and auto-shutting after. [source: [[_Sources/YT_LOw05Uk3rUk_archwood_6_stages_design_project|YT_LOw05Uk3rUk]]]

## Bedroom and Home Office Color Temperature Refinements (added 2026-08-24)

- **A single central chandelier as a bedroom's only light source, if used, should be 2700-3500K** — narrows this page's general 2700-4000K comfortable range specifically for a bedroom's main/central fixture. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Bedside reading light**: corroborates the existing directional-fixture guidance above; adds a caution against a hanging/pendant-style bedside fixture near the bed, cited as prone to breaking within roughly six months from incidental hand contact at night. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Home office/study: 4000-5000K recommended** — cooler than neutral, to help sustain focus without the drowsiness warmer bedroom-range light would induce. Recommends either two distinct brightness scenarios or one dimmable fixture spanning both. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]

## Bathroom Mirror and Night-Light Technique (added 2026-08-24)

- **⚠️ Mirror light must shine horizontally toward the face, not down from above and not by lighting the tile behind the mirror.** Lighting the surrounding tile rather than the mirror/face directly is called out as a common, ineffective mistake; overhead lighting casts facial shadows. Fixture should be built into or mounted on the mirror itself, or positioned at face height/side. Corroborates and extends this store's existing bathroom-mirror-lighting guidance (see [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Lighting_and_Electrical|Bathroom — Lighting & Electrical]]). [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **A dimmed/low version of the mirror light can double as the bathroom's night-light**, avoiding a separate full-brightness night fixture — also usable during a bath to avoid harsh light while relaxing. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]

## Kitchen Lighting and Navigation/General Rules (added 2026-08-24)

- **Dining/eating area needs neutral-color light, not warm or cold** — neutral light makes food look more appetizing and is more comfortable regardless of time of day. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Countertop/prep zone needs strong, non-dim task light** — cited symptom: eyes tiring within ~15 minutes of chopping vegetables under insufficiently bright light. Corroborates existing kitchen-worktop task-lighting guidance with a concrete symptom/timeframe. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **⚠️ If the home has a staircase, it must have navigational/night lighting — no exception stated ("100%")**, implemented via motion-sensor-triggered fixtures, a single always-dim fixture left on overnight in one zone, or a manual single-switch "night mode" covering the whole home. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Plan lighting during renovation design, not as an afterthought** — a single generic chandelier as the sole light source (the old, pre-2000s default) is insufficiently functional for a modern renovation; mix multiple fixture types deliberately per task/zone rather than relying on one universal source. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]

## Current Design Trends and Outdated Patterns (added 2026-08-24)

Per designer Nikita Kuznetsov (Kruglov/Ontario) — trend-focused framing, complementary to the fixture-taxonomy and function-based content above:

- **⚠️ A single chandelier as a room's only light source now reads as outdated/tacky, not just insufficiently functional** — sharpens this page's existing framing into an explicit style judgment. A two-switch chandelier where flipping one switch turns off half the bulbs reads as burnt-out bulbs — a specific, avoidable visual mistake. Recessed point fixtures arranged in visible straight rows are also flagged as a dated layout convention (distinct from this page's separate glare/cost arguments against recessed spotlights). [source: [[_Sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Track lighting is currently the leading trend fixture type**: black, geometric forms (square/rectangular/L-shaped, no curves) with a wide range of swappable attachable heads. **Wall-bounce placement technique**: mount the track on the ceiling aimed at a wall, or on a wall aimed upward/outward, so light reaches the room indirectly via reflection rather than shining straight down — a deliberate indirect-lighting method distinct from the general background/ambient category above. [source: [[_Sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Chandeliers have shifted role from main light source to decor/accent**: either a large statement decorative piece, or a smaller zonal pendant over a table/furniture piece rather than the whole room. Point/spot fixtures now trend toward surface-mounted over recessed; where recessed is still used, larger adjustable-direction units are preferred over small fixed downlights. [source: [[_Sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Linear "light line" fixtures are a current trend alternative to track lighting** — parallel, crossing, or freeform layouts. **⚠️ Overuse caution**: too many lines, or too bright a strip, over-illuminates the space and reads visually flat, defeating the depth/volume a well-lit interior should have — fewer, deliberately-placed light elements preserve perceived depth better than many. Where a light line transitions from ceiling to wall, balance brightness so the wall segment doesn't glare into the eyes. [source: [[_Sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Perimeter cove lighting (ceiling and floor), shelf and countertop backlighting are all good practice** — colored LED accent lighting is explicitly excluded, called out as never having been stylish. **⚠️ Construction-sequencing implication**: wall-wash/perimeter lighting reveals every micro-imperfection in the wall surface — plan for genuinely flat/even wall finishing before specifying this technique. [source: [[_Sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **⚠️ Bedside sconces are often positioned as mood/"intimate" lighting rather than genuine reading light** — commonly mounted over the nightstand rather than directly over the actual reading position, making them functionally decorative despite being marketed/framed as reading sconces. Nuances (doesn't contradict) this page's existing directional-bedside-reading-light guidance above — the fixture needs to be positioned and aimed at the actual reading zone, not just labeled a reading sconce. A sconce aimed at the wall itself (rather than outward) adds visual depth and is a good look distinct from the reading-function question. [source: [[_Sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **Main/base light should be neutral; accent/decor lighting (LED strips behind 3D panels, cove lighting) can deliberately be warm-toned for coziness** — a pairing rule for primary-vs-accent roles within one room, distinct from this page's existing same-scene-must-match rule for simultaneous same-function fixtures. Dim/muted light in select spots can meaningfully elevate a design, achieved correctly via an indirect/wall-reflected source (see wall-bounce technique above) rather than dimming an overhead fixture to near-nothing. [source: [[_Sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]
- **⚠️ Explicit upper bound on scenario count, complementing the existing "minimum three scenarios" rule: don't exceed roughly 3-4 lighting scenarios per zone** — beyond that, extra scenarios stop getting used day-to-day and only get set up for guests, defeating the purpose of scene-based lighting. [source: [[_Sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|1gXYL99mfY4]]]

## Cove/Contour Lighting — a Dust-Trap Caution (Kruglov/Ontario, Round 13 triage, added 2026-08-28)

**⚠️ Softens, without reversing, this page's own "good practice" framing
for perimeter cove lighting above**: dust, gnats, and small insects
accumulate over time in the concealed channel between a stretch-ceiling
membrane and the light source behind it; once lit, the light casts a
visible shadow through the accumulated debris, reading as visible dirt
on the ceiling. A real, fairly fast (not many-years-out) cosmetic
failure specific to this construction detail — not a durability problem
with the ceiling material itself, and not a reason to avoid cove
lighting outright, but worth planning for (accessibility for cleaning,
or accepting the eventual look) rather than treating it as maintenance-
free. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_oDHSbp6QRRE_kruglov_worst_overrated_trends|YT_oDHSbp6QRRE]]]

## Seven Lighting Mistakes, With Mechanisms (Мария Шеврина / SMBUREAU, added 2026-08-30)

The densest single-source lighting addition to this page so far, and unusually concrete — three of the seven items carry a number. `single-account`, `unverified`, but items 3, 4 and 6 are physical claims rather than taste.

- **⚠️ LED strip run round the whole perimeter of a non-rectangular room produces an ugly broken line.** Her stated principle is that minimalist and contemporary interiors use simple geometric forms, so where a room has a projection formed by two walls (a wardrobe boxed out of a bedroom, say), **light only that external corner, or run one single line along the headboard wall under the ceiling, or use a cornice-concealed strip** — do not trace every jog in the plan.
- **⚠️ A strip you can see into glares.** Her named case is a strip above a bed headboard that shines straight into your eyes once you lean back, and she notes it is uncomfortably bright even when not looked at directly. **Fix: mount the strip to throw light upward rather than downward, on a small standoff, preferably in a corner profile**, so the output is soft and diffuse.
- **⚠️ Voltage drop over a long strip run — the most concrete item here. A reel is 5 m, and if the total run exceeds one reel fed from a single end, the far end is visibly dimmer.** The classic case is a bedroom perimeter fed from one corner, leaving the opposite corner dark, because the summed side lengths exceed 5 m. **Fix: feed from at least two opposite corners.** The same applies to multiple strips on one switch — **three vertical wall strips should each get their own cable**, not be chained off one; the fade "будет довольно заметно глазу." She notes the fitter may not warn you, since it works electrically either way.
- **⚠️ Do not choose warm 3000 K as the main light because it "looks less bright" — luminous flux is independent of colour temperature.** People compare two showroom fixtures of **identical lumen output**, one 3000 K and one 4000 K, conclude the 4000 K is brighter, and downgrade to warm. Both illuminate a tabletop identically; they are confusing *warm* with *soft*. **If 4000 K feels too bright, take 4000 K with fewer lumens.** Two further reasons she gives for neutral as the main light: warm light is strongly relaxing and sleep-inducing, which is wrong if you want to work in the evening; and **neutral light does not distort the flat colours**, so it will not push already-warm walls further yellow when the main light comes on.
- **⚠️ Switches with an indicator lamp make ceiling spots glow dimly at night, or switch on spontaneously** — current accumulates until it is enough to trigger the driver. **Fix: a shunt resistor in the circuit, possibly more than one.**
- **⚠️ Add 1 cm to a recessed fixture's stated depth when calculating the ceiling drop.** A 6 cm fixture needs a 7 cm drop; a 10 cm fixture forces an 11 cm drop. Her named failure is buying an attractive showroom spot on light quality and colour temperature alone and only then discovering the ceiling must come down 11 cm.
- **⚠️ Do not size a magnetic track power supply to the initial fixture count** — that removes the track's whole advantage. Specify headroom at purchase; she suggests telling the supplier you plan roughly three more fixtures.

[source: [[_Sources/YT_NfHyCfo1J4w_shevrina_lighting_mistakes|YT_NfHyCfo1J4w]]]

## Two Lighting Scenarios on One Track (Мария Шеврина / SMBUREAU, added 2026-08-30)

**One track does not have to mean one switch.** Two switches can drive different fixture groups on the same visual track line — her example puts the track pendants on one switch and the track general-light fixtures on another, ganged with the room's remaining spots. **Stated purpose: keep the ceiling clear of fixture clutter**, following their rule of thumb to leave only the minimum necessary lighting function visible on the ceiling.

Complements the **"Minimum Three Scenarios" rule** already on this page — that one is about how many scenarios a room needs; this is a mechanism for delivering two of them without adding hardware to the ceiling.

[source: [[_Sources/YT_z4G-ocStu9o_shevrina_ten_studio_detail_solutions|YT_z4G-ocStu9o]]]

**Related, from the same practitioner**: coloured/RGB strip lighting is argued to cheapen an interior except in a deliberately themed room (a gaming room is named as legitimate); for normal use she specifies **4000 K neutral or 3000 K warm**, preferring warm because a strip is usually part of the evening scenario. She also states that **the influence of a cornice LED strip on wall-colour choice is overstated** — people over-weight it when selecting paint. [source: [[_Sources/YT_XGI6FS2ZdCc_shevrina_25_antitrends|YT_XGI6FS2ZdCc]], [[_Sources/YT_NfHyCfo1J4w_shevrina_lighting_mistakes|YT_NfHyCfo1J4w]]]

## Ceiling Light Source Types

Three distinct ceiling light-source types come up repeatedly once you move past a single generic "downlight" mental model:

- **Perimeter LED strip** (run inside a stretch-ceiling baguette channel) is **decorative/supplementary only** — never sufficient as a room's main light source, even when told otherwise. It reads well as ambiance but cannot replace true task/main lighting. [source: [[_Sources/YT_iRbTlv5Ka9o_lighting_types_taxonomy|iRbTlv5Ka9o_lighting_typ]]]
- **Flat light panels** are bright and energy-efficient, but difficult to retrofit onto an *existing* stretch ceiling since they need mounting holes the ceiling wasn't prepped for — plan for them before the ceiling goes in, not after. [source: [[_Sources/YT_iRbTlv5Ka9o_lighting_types_taxonomy|iRbTlv5Ka9o_lighting_typ]]]
- **Softboxes** (fixtures that emit light from their full surface/sides, not just the underside) give the most even room-fill light at comparatively low wattage — this source's own stated preference among the three. [source: [[_Sources/YT_iRbTlv5Ka9o_lighting_types_taxonomy|iRbTlv5Ka9o_lighting_typ]]]

## Five Arguments Against Recessed Spotlights — per Zemskov/Zemstandart

> [!NOTE]
> Demonstrated live by Zemskov in his own rented apartment (which has recessed spotlights he criticizes as "what not to do," not his own installed work) — `single-account`, but extensively demonstrated with real counts rather than asserted in the abstract. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt`]

1. **Constant bulb burnout, per Zemskov's own live count**: in his rented apartment, bulbs fully replaced ~2 months prior already showed 5 of a group out, 2 of 9 out elsewhere, 3 of 12 out elsewhere, and one hallway group down to 1 lit out of several — verified live by testing each switch individually to rule out a wiring fault. His stated mechanism: a recessed-spotlight bulb fails on the same schedule as any bulb; with 40 installed, the *aggregate* failure rate is 40× a single fixture's. Treat the mechanism (many fixtures = proportionally more visible failures) as the durable point, not his specific ~3-month figure. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt|20260810_recessed_spotli]]]
2. **Uneven "cone" light pattern**: on textured/patterned wallpaper, narrow spotlight cones create visibly uneven illumination that reads as a bumpy wall even when the wall is flat — worse on plain wallpaper than heavily-patterned (which already masks unevenness). [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt|20260810_recessed_spotli]]]
3. **Harsh reclined glare**: lying in a bathtub or reclined chair under spotlights (especially halogen) produces direct eye glare — routinely enough to get up specifically to switch them off mid-relaxation. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt|20260810_recessed_spotli]]]
4. **Poor actual room fill despite visual brightness**: a narrow downward beam mostly hits a dark floor that reflects little light back; walls get only the cone's edge, so ambient fill stays weak even with many fixtures lit — driving occupants to keep adding more light sources without fixing the underlying problem. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt|20260810_recessed_spotli]]]
5. **Higher total cost than one premium fixture**: each spotlight needs its own wiring run, most are 12V and need a step-down transformer (an added cost and failure point), and ongoing maintenance accrues on both bulbs and supply. An 18-spotlight installation shown cost several times more than a single high-end chandelier that would give brighter, more even, lower-maintenance light in the same space. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt|20260810_recessed_spotli]]]

**Bonus injury note**: spotlight glass diffuser lenses can heat up enough that their retaining mechanism fails and the lens falls and shatters — one real incident cited in the speaker's own unit. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_recessed_spotlights_against_a91e17ca.txt|20260810_recessed_spotli]]]

## LED-Strip Transformer Placement (Kruglov/Ontario, Round 15, added 2026-08-28)

**⚠️ Centralize LED-strip transformers in one location rather than mounting each near its own strip run** (a niche, behind a shelf, under a cornice — the common practice). Consolidate every 12V transformer for the apartment into one spot (a closet, walk-in wardrobe, or the low-voltage panel), ideally somewhere with extra passive cooling. **Stated reason transformers fail: overheating** — centralizing them somewhere deliberately cooled is framed as directly extending service life, not just a convenience-of-troubleshooting argument. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_kkE25HmFciU_kruglov_worst_solutions|YT_kkE25HmFciU]]]
