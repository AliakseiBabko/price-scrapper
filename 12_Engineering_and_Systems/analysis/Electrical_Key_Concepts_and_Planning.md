# Electrical — Key Concepts & Planning

Covers rough-vs-finish electrical, the lighting-category taxonomy, and planning decisions to settle before rough-in starts. Part of [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/Electrical_and_Lighting|Electrical & Lighting]].

## Key Concepts

Alexey Zemskov / ZEMS says: **Rough electrical vs. finish electrical** — rough-in (cable chasing/routing, panel assembly, temporary switches/sockets so the site can be worked with power) happens early, concealed under plaster/putty; finish electrical (outlets, switches, fixtures, panel trim) happens near the end, after painting/flooring. Pricing and mistakes cluster differently at each stage.

Alexey Zemskov / ZEMS says: **Technical light vs. decorative light** — technical light is the functional recessed/track lighting doing the actual room illumination; decorative light is the visible fixture (pendant, chandelier, sconce) chosen for its appearance. They're often sourced and budgeted separately, and scale very differently by quality tier — technical lighting differentiates mainly on optics quality at higher tiers, decorative lighting mainly on **original vs. replica** sourcing (budget/mid tiers commonly use unlicensed replica fixtures; higher tiers move to licensed originals — know which you're buying, since price and provenance diverge sharply).

Alexey Zemskov / ZEMS recommends: **Four functional lighting categories, each with its own recommended color temperature**: main/general and task light (~4000K, neutral-white, good for visual tasks, target ≥500 lux for comfortable use), decorative and night light (~2700-3000K, warm, comfortable at low brightness). **Night light belongs in hallways and WCs but should never be installed in a bedroom** — it disrupts sleep, and decorative lighting isn't a substitute for a dedicated night-light circuit where one is needed.

## Planning & Layout

Alexey Zemskov / ZEMS recommends: **Plan furniture layout before finalizing socket placement** — a socket blocked by a bed headboard or cabinet is a common, avoidable mistake; work from the actual furniture plan, not a generic room template. **Use dedicated circuits and individual breakers for high-power consumers** (kitchen appliances, laundry equipment) — see [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Cable_Circuits_and_Panel_Design|Cable, Circuits & Panel Design]] for the full circuit-count formula this generalizes into.

Alexey Zemskov / ZEMS recommends: **Plan electrical layout against a 5-15 year family-change horizon, not just move-in day** — children aging, changing needs, and general life-stage change are cited drivers. A low-cost future-proofing move: leave one or two extra outlets wired but hidden behind the finished wallpaper, reachable later by cutting the wallpaper rather than opening the wall.

Alexey Zemskov / ZEMS recommends: **Electric-point layout via centerline referencing is worth adopting over corner-only dimensioning**: define a virtual centerline (e.g. centered on a future bed) at a set offset from the corner, then dimension every point symmetrically from that centerline. This produces provably symmetric placement regardless of minor furniture-placement shifts — and matters more than it sounds, since bed-relative asymmetry (5cm off-center one side, 11cm the other) is far more visually noticeable than corner-relative precision, even when the corner-relative numbers look cleaner.

Alexey Zemskov / ZEMS recommends: **A few specific provisioning rules worth building in during planning, not discovering later**: video door-intercoms belong in the "clean zone" (living area), not near the entry/shoe area — answering shouldn't require crossing a dirty floor in socks; a kitchen-sink cabinet needs 3 outlets provisioned (disposal, water filter, one spare) so a later retrofit doesn't need new wiring; on soundproofed walls, back-boxes need to project outward by the insulation's own thickness or sockets end up recessed behind the finished surface.

Игорь Краснов / Студия Краснов says: **⚠️ outlet/switch count is systematically undercounted at the planning stage because renderings hide it** — designers sometimes hide/minimize visible outlets in renderings for a "clean" look, but real daily electronics needs are usually undercounted at the planning stage as a result, leading to extension-cord clutter later. Recommends counting actual devices needing power BEFORE finalizing outlet placement, not working backward from a rendering's aesthetic. Cross-reference against this page's existing kitchen-sink-cabinet 3-outlet rule above and [[03_Kitchen/Kitchen_Utilities|Kitchen Utilities]]'s full outlet-count planning table (10-11 outlets for an average kitchen, 12-14 for a large one) — the same undercounting risk, restated as a general planning discipline rather than a room-specific number. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_NO6-2zAJgys_krasnov_19controversial_items|NO6-2zAJgys]]]

## ⚠️⚠️ Sockets on a Soundproofed Wall — the Acoustic Consequence, Not Just the Depth One

**This page already carries the DEPTH rule for a soundproofed wall (back-boxes must project by the insulation's thickness). A general contractor on a premium St Petersburg object supplies the two things that rule does not cover, and the second is a hard planning constraint.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_AH1INy0i5lU_nsdsgn_soundproofing_premium_object|YT_AH1INy0i5lU]]]

- **⚠️ A socket is the dominant acoustic leak in an otherwise correctly built wall — and the whole assembly is defeated by it.** «Многие зашивают стены специальным акустическим гипсокартоном, делают виброподвесы — **а потом просто ДЫРЯВЯТ эти стены, делают в них розетки, и это получается у нас МОСТИК ШУМА.**» His analogy: a waterproof membrane jacket that then has a zip sewn into it and holes punched through. And the symptom clients report: «через РОЗЕТКУ мы можем слышать разговоры даже с соседей, либо включённый телевизор».
- **The remedy is a ШУМОБОКС** mounted into the body of the frame, with the cable led in through a drilled hole and **sealed with acoustic silicone.**
- **⚠️⚠️ THE PLANNING RULE, which must be settled on the drawing and cannot be fixed afterwards: «когда проектируется дизайн-проект, надо учитывать: в разных помещениях НЕ СТЫКОВАТЬ РОЗЕТКИ ДРУГ НАПРОТИВ ДРУГА — потому что иначе вот такие ШУМОБОКСЫ они друг с другом СТОЛКНУТСЯ, и не хватит места просто для них.»**
  **→ Back-to-back sockets in adjacent rooms either cannot be executed at all, or force the wall thicker. This has to be checked when the two rooms' socket plans are drawn — which is normally done room by room, so nothing catches it.** Full build-up and the шумобокс detail on [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Soundproofing|Soundproofing]].

## ⚠️ Maximise Plain Sockets, Minimise Built-In Technology — an Asymmetry With a Ten-Year Proof

Designer Александр Синчуков, drawing the opposite conclusion for sockets than for switches (see [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Switches_and_Controls|Switches and Controls]] for the light-group cap): **«тут в отличие от световых групп всё НАОБОРОТ»**. [source: [[_Sources/YT_CSpXvPWpsgQ_nsdsgn_fifteen_post_occupancy_regrets|YT_CSpXvPWpsgQ]]]

- **⚠️ The strongest case is a room whose furniture will move — which corroborates this page's own 5-15-year horizon rule with a specific room: «особенно для помещений, которые со временем планируют меняться — например ДЕТСКАЯ: ребёнок будет расти, мебель будет менять свою локацию, и вам постоянно нужны новые места для розеток. Лучше сделать заранее, иначе у вас вся комната будет опутана УДЛИНИТЕЛЯМИ.»** *(A clinical psychologist independently gives the cadence that makes this concrete — nursery furniture changes roughly every six months to age 3 and at least every two years for a schoolchild; see [[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Developmental_Age_Bands|Developmental Age Bands]].)*
- A friend's flat taken to the limit: a socket every metre for phone charging — every balcony, every wall, every pier, several by the toilet.
- **⚠️⚠️ BUT THE PAIRED WARNING IS THE MORE VALUABLE HALF, and it separates the socket from the device: fashionable USB sockets and similar built-ins «быстро теряют актуальность». «Так устроен маркетинг: создать что-то очень модное, вы это покупаете, после этого создаётся опять новое модное… причём с каждым годом это движение УСКОРЯЕТСЯ.»**
  - **⚠️ THE TEN-YEAR PROOF he offers: «посмотрите, что произошло с АНАЛОГОВЫМ УМНЫМ ДОМОМ, когда вам нужно было целую КОМНАТУ отдать под сервер, когда нужно было провести везде провода. Сейчас, спустя 10 лет, это просто какой-то АТАВИЗМ. Тогда это стоило безумные деньги; сейчас это всё можно выкинуть и легко управлять домом с помощью смартфона, и стоить это будет совсем других денег.»** Directly relevant to the wired-versus-wireless choice on [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Smart_Home_Wired_vs_Wireless|Smart Home: Wired vs Wireless]].
  - **⚠️ THE RULE: «много розеток не бывает, а новомодные девайсы используйте с умом.»**
  - **→ The generalisable form: the SOCKET is the durable interface and the DEVICE is not. Provision the interface generously and buy the technology that plugs into it later, when it is cheaper and better.** This is the same logic as this page's hidden-outlets-behind-wallpaper future-proofing move, extended to a purchasing rule.

## ⚠️ A Socket or Switch Behind a Habitually Open Door Is Unusable

**This page's furniture-plan rule covers a socket blocked by a bed or cabinet; this covers the one blocked by a door, which no furniture plan shows.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_CSpXvPWpsgQ_nsdsgn_fifteen_post_occupancy_regrets|YT_CSpXvPWpsgQ]]]

- **«Если дверь постоянно открыта, то вы просто не сможете пользоваться розеткой, которая находится за ней.» «Постарайтесь, чтобы за дверью ничего не оказалось — ни розеток, ни выключателей.»**
- **⚠️ His own flat, and the failure is funnier and more instructive than it sounds: the switch sits on the same axis as the door handle, so a sharply opened door strikes it and switches the light on.** He came home several times, saw lights on from the yard and assumed a burglary — it was the door, and the cat.
- **⚠️ And the one he calls the stupidest, with a plausible cause: door handle and light switch on OPPOSITE sides.** He lived in such a flat — «это каждый раз меня бесило». **«Возможно, если в процессе дизайн-проекта что-то поменялось, ОТКРЫВАНИЕ поменялось, а розетку забыли поменять.»** **→ A changed door swing must trigger a re-check of the switch side. That is a change-control rule, not a layout rule, and it is why the swing decision belongs on the electrical drawing too** — see [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/analysis/Door_Swing_Direction|Door Swing Direction]] for the full swing cluster and [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Switches_and_Controls|Switches and Controls]] for the switch-side placement rules this interacts with.

## ⚠️ A Concealed CHARGING BAY in the Bathroom — Three Sockets the Checklists Below Miss

**The room-by-room checklist below includes a leak-system socket and a shoe-dryer socket but not this, and it is a three-socket item inside joinery that cannot be added later.** Александр Синчуков, his own 4 m² bathroom. [source: [[_Sources/YT__nDCLhRUojE_nsdsgn_own_bathroom_4m2|YT__nDCLhRUojE]]]

- Inside the service cupboard above the WC, alongside the manifold and the leak-protection controller: **«здесь у нас ТРИ РОЗЕТКИ. Я могу заряжать ИРРИГАТОР, ЗУБНУЮ ЩЁТКУ, ТРИММЕР — то есть ВСЁ ЭЛЕКТРИЧЕСКОЕ ОБОРУДОВАНИЕ В ВАННОЙ здесь очень легко подзаряжается.»**
- **⚠️ AND THE FAILURE IT FIXES, which is the part that makes it specifiable: «потому что на СТАРОЙ КВАРТИРЕ мне приходилось ИДТИ В СПАЛЬНЮ, ВТЫКАТЬ В РОЗЕТКУ, ТАМ ЗАРЯЖАТЬ, ПОТОМ НЕСТИ НАЗАД.»**
- **→ Bathroom electricals are numerous, all rechargeable, and all currently homeless. Three sockets behind a cupboard door solves it, keeps the chargers out of sight (which is the visual-noise argument this practitioner applies throughout), and keeps them out of the splash zone. It has to be provisioned with the joinery, so it is a rough-stage item disguised as a furniture detail.**
- **⚠️ Note the same cupboard also needs a socket for the leak-protection controller — «мозг, который втыкается в розетку» — so the real requirement in that one cabinet is four, and this vault's checklist counts one.** See [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Leak_Protection_Systems|Leak Protection Systems]].

## ⚠️ Buy the Appliances Before the Kitchen Is Drawn — With a Physical Impossibility Named

**An electrical-planning dependency as much as a joinery one, because the appliance set determines the circuits and their positions.** Same channel, 2022-01. [source: [[_Sources/YT_CN-Ab_g4CAI_nsdsgn_thirtyfive_beautiful_but_impractical|YT_CN-Ab_g4CAI]]]

- **«Когда кухня маленькая, приходится придумывать какие-то НЕВЕРОЯТНЫЕ КОМБИНАЦИИ кухонной техники — но есть те, которые просто ФИЗИЧЕСКИ НЕВОЗМОЖНО осуществить. Например НЕЛЬЗЯ ПОСТАВИТЬ ПОСУДОМОЕЧНУЮ МАШИНУ ПОД ВАРОЧНУЮ ПОВЕРХНОСТЬ — ОНА ТУДА ПРОСТО ФИЗИЧЕСКИ НЕ ЗАЛЕЗЕТ.»**
- **⚠️ «ВСЕГДА ПЕРЕД ЗАКАЗОМ КУХНИ ОПРЕДЕЛИТЕСЬ С КУХОННОЙ ТЕХНИКОЙ, А ЛУЧШЕ ПРИОБРЕТИТЕ [её], ЧТОБЫ ТЕХНОЛОГ МОГ ПРАВИЛЬНО РАССЧИТАТЬ КУХНЮ ПОД ВАШУ ТЕХНИКУ, чтобы всё встало на свои места.»**
- **→ This page's dedicated-circuit rule for high-power consumers already depends on knowing the appliance set; this establishes that the set must be FIXED, and preferably BOUGHT, before the joinery drawing exists — because the kitchen is calculated around real dimensions, not nominal ones. It also interacts with the push-to-open constraint recorded on [[03_Kitchen/analysis/Worktops_and_Backsplash|Worktops and Backsplash]], where a handle-free lower run narrows the dishwasher choice to one opening type.**

Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario recommends: **a room-by-room socket-provisioning checklist of commonly-missed locations, even in professional design projects** — window-slope outlet for a smart speaker or holiday string lights; shoe/boot/skate dryer outlet inside a closet/walk-in (not an extension cord left in the hallway); robot-vacuum charging-dock outlet; exercise-equipment (treadmill/elliptical/bike) outlet; food-waste disposal unit outlet; security-camera power (cable or outlet, planned regardless of exact camera placement); loggia/balcony sockets (1-2, "just in case" — cited uses: charging a scooter or a motorcycle helmet); leak-detection-system power in the WC/bathroom; a sofa/armchair-zone socket in the living room (explicitly flagged as a spot designers frequently omit despite being a common laptop/phone-charging location); and an iron/garment-steamer outlet located somewhere other than the hallway/entry. [source: [[_Sources/YT_1dp7alivsLQ_kruglov_safe_sockets_switches|1dp7alivsLQ_kruglov_safe_sockets_switches]]]
