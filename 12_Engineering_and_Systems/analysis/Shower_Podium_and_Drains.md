# Plumbing — Shower Podium, Freestanding-Tub Drains & Slope Constraints

Part of [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/Plumbing_and_Waterproofing|Plumbing & Waterproofing]].

- Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario explains: **A freestanding tub with no wall contact needs its own floor drain**, unlike a wall-abutting tub whose overflow/drain assembly hides behind the apron with a simpler connection. This commonly requires raising the floor level in the tub zone (or across the whole bathroom/apartment) to achieve adequate drain slope toward the stack — **unless the sewage branch is already deep enough and the tub is close enough to the stack** that the drain can hide within normal screed thickness without raising the floor. One source's own rule of thumb: this stops working if the tub sits roughly 5–6 meters from the stack, since screed thickness alone can't provide enough slope over that distance even with a shallow branch connection. `single-account`, no exact slope percentage given — treat as a planning flag to check on-site, not a precise formula.
- Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario describes: **Concrete mitigation options if the drain-slope constraint above can't be met without raising the floor**, from a second source: (1) raise the floor locally under the tub, or across the whole bathroom/apartment; (2) install a lift/macerator pump — functional, but flagged as something that "will start to annoy you after your first bath" (implying reliability/noise complaints over time); (3) in a two-story private house, route the drain along the ceiling of the floor below instead of through the slab; (4) on a ground-floor apartment, it's sometimes possible to negotiate with the management company to lower the connection point. Weigh these against simply choosing a wall-abutting tub instead, which avoids the problem entirely.
- Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario states: **The same slope constraint is the reason most shower installations use a raised tiled podium rather than a flush floor**: one source states roughly 95% of their own apartment projects lack enough screed depth for a fully flush single-level floor with a working shower drain, so the shower zone (or occasionally the whole bathroom) gets raised instead — a step is generally more acceptable than a non-functional or quickly-clogging drain caused by insufficient slope.
- **Drain/trap types**: standard round or square, a linear "щелевой" (slot) drain along one wall, or — described as a newer, less common, but aesthetically preferred option by one source — a trap mounted inside the wall itself with the drain pipe running inside the wall rather than the floor; not compatible with every layout. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_plumbing_video_3_e759e2c7.txt|20260731_plumbing_video_]]]
- Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario states: **A revision access hatch at the podium's face/end is a legal requirement**, per one source, specifically so the trap and cavity can be periodically checked for leaks and moisture — the same source notes compliance is inconsistent in practice.
- **Pre-made plastic shower cabin/enclosure units are strongly discouraged** by one source: nozzles clog quickly, drains become unreliable, and the structure itself is described as flimsy — considered appropriate only for a very low-budget job, not a standard modern renovation. ## Shower-Valve Height and Tile-Thickness Mixer-Centering Mistakes (Петришин-Строй, added 2026-08-24, Round 2)

Region level 2 (channel-level Moscow association only, no city named this
episode): **shower-valve-height design mistake, with a stated root
cause** — a frequent designer mistake is setting the shower mixer/valve
height without accounting for the *combination* of the future shower
podium height plus the specific shower-arm/showerhead reach, resulting in
an average-height adult hitting their head on the showerhead. **Fixing it
after the fact requires demolishing the finished tile** — this must be
caught and corrected at the rough-plumbing stage, before tiling, not
discovered afterward. Separately, a **tile/adhesive-thickness
mixer-centering mistake**: not accounting for the finish tile plus
tile-adhesive thickness when planning a bathtub mixer's position relative
to the tub leaves the mixer unable to center on the tub's spout/drain
once tiling is done, so the water stream runs off-center from the tub
opening instead of straight down the middle. [source: [[_Sources/YT_OgIZhrxD4v4_petrishin_plumbing_quality_checklist|OgIZhrxD4v4]]]
