## Waterproofing & Plastering Standards

- Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario reports: **A flush (level-with-the-rest-of-the-apartment) bathroom floor's legal status in Russia has reportedly changed over time** — permitted, then prohibited, then permitted again as of recent sources, **conditional specifically on waterproofing the entire apartment's floor, not just the bathroom**. If this condition isn't met and it's discovered, code enforcement can require retrofitting a raised threshold, or in the worst case a full teardown and redo. One source frames it plainly: it's cheaper and easier to just waterproof the whole apartment upfront than to risk this outcome later. **This regulatory history is Russia-specific and its current status in Belarus is unconfirmed** — treat a flush floor as requiring explicit local verification before committing to it.
- Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario states: **Roll/membrane waterproofing under the screed, with an upturn onto the walls, is mandatory by code in Russia (per one source) with no exceptions** — it always separates the screed from direct wall/floor-finish contact. After the screed, brush/paint-on ("обмазочная") waterproofing with taped joints at the floor-wall junction is applied, plus the same taped-joint treatment at wall corners.
- Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario explains: **Brush-on wall waterproofing is not legally mandatory when using cement-based plaster, but is mandatory when using gypsum-based plaster**, since gypsum plaster must be protected from moisture exposure. One source's own company uses gypsum plaster throughout its bathroom work and therefore brush-waterproofs floor-to-ceiling on every bathroom wall regardless of the cement-plaster exemption.
- Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario compares: **Gypsum vs. cement plaster tradeoff**: gypsum is faster to apply (more workable, easier to level), lighter (less structural load on the slab — relevant since tile adds further weight on top), and dries meaningfully faster (cited: cement plaster 21–30 days vs. gypsum plaster 7–14 days). One source cites Knauf as now endorsing gypsum plaster for wet rooms specifically.
- **Add an anti-fungal admixture to ceiling paint applied above a stretch ceiling, apartment-wide, not just in wet rooms** — rough-stage work (screed curing, plastering, even plain painting) generates significant condensate, and a ceiling surface can accumulate enough moisture to grow mold that's invisible once the stretch-ceiling membrane is installed over it. A preventable, easily-skipped step since the mold stays hidden until the ceiling is eventually opened. `single-account`. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_never_take_this_from_masters_70m2_4b3c72b6.txt`]
- **Always wrap the sewage stack in sound-insulating material during any bathroom renovation in a multi-unit building** — corroborated by two sources; one gives concrete product/cost detail (a bitumen-adhesive wrap kit, ~3,000–5,000 RUB, a few hours of DIY-feasible installation). Without it, plastic-stack drain noise from an upstairs flush is described as sounding like "a waterfall behind the wall" — a comfort issue, not a structural one, but a low-cost fix worth including as standard scope. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_never_take_this_from_masters_70m2_4b3c72b6.txt|20260804_never_take_this]]]
- Zemstandart/Alexey Zemskov recommends: **Brush-on/paint-applied waterproofing should always be bright blue, never gray** — gray waterproofing is visually indistinguishable from the surrounding gray plaster, making coverage gaps invisible to a non-expert inspecting the work. Bright blue makes any missed patch obvious even without training. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_split_rough_finish_contracting_and_rough_stage_checklist_9a4efa5e.txt`]

> [!NOTE]
> **Independent corroboration, with an added coat-count (Round 1, ДЕЛАТЬ НЕ ПЕРЕДЕЛАТЬ, added 2026-08-28)**: Vladimir Amelchenko independently corroborates the gypsum-plaster-needs-waterproofing rule above, and adds a specific number: **two coats of acrylic waterproofing** over gypsum plaster before tiling. Skipping it lets the tile adhesive react directly with the gypsum plaster, degrading the bond ("turns to mush") until the tile eventually detaches — the mechanism behind the requirement, not just the rule itself. He recommends applying it over cement plaster too as a low-cost extra safeguard even though not strictly required there. [source: [[_Sources/YT_Zd6yryCjyhM_sbk_10_bathroom_mistakes|YT_Zd6yryCjyhM]]]

## Rough-Stage Acceptance Checklist Items

- **A dry-trap siphon at the water-inlet node is worth including even on the most basic build** — lets filter-flush water drain automatically instead of needing a bucket; without one, infrequent manual flushing plus standing water in a regular siphon causes sewer odor. **Dishwashers get the same treatment**: a dry-trap siphon under a dishwasher prevents sewer-gas odor the same way — framed as standard practice most homeowners don't know to ask for. `single-account`. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_what_is_this_60m2_contractor_control_f7ab173e.txt`]
- **Deliberately unclamped rough-stage plumbing fittings are not a defect**: crews should leave supply fittings (e.g. at a mixer connection) resting loosely with slack and crimp-sleeve fittings pre-installed but not yet crimped, until tile is laid and the exact grout-line position is known — the fitting is then centered on a tile joint for a clean look. A rough-stage handover showing stubs with sleeves on but fittings not yet compressed is expected, not a red flag. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_what_is_this_60m2_contractor_control_f7ab173e.txt|20260804_what_is_this_60]]]
- **A freestanding-tub installation footprint should be sized only a few millimeters larger than the tub itself**, not tiled out to a wide margin before sliding the tub in — the tile edge should overlap the tub rim so water drains back into the tub rather than behind it. [source: [[_Archive/processed_sources/20260804_what_is_this_60m2_contractor_control_f7ab173e.txt|20260804_what_is_this_60]]]
