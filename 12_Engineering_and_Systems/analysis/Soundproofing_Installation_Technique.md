## Floor Membrane Installation Technique

[source: [[_Sources/YT_8CzgwqUKGF8_floor_soundwaterproofing_technique|8CzgwqUKGF8]]] — see that source note for the full detail; folded in here as this page is created rather than left solely in `Durable_Facts.md`.

### A Second Independent Build, with Structural-vs-Airborne Reasoning and a Perimeter-Damper Detail (added 2026-08-24, Sidorik Round 4)

Pavel Sidorik, individual practitioner, own apartment, Belarus (level 1): named bitumen-polymer membrane "Стопзвука М" (ТехноСонус), heat-fused at overlaps, doubles as a waterproofing layer. **His own stated reasoning for prioritizing floor (not ceiling) sound insulation**: structural/impact noise (footsteps, dropped objects, furniture) is far easier to stop at its source floor than to absorb from the receiving neighbor's ceiling below — a first-hand rationale consistent with this page's existing floor-vs-ceiling cost-asymmetry section above, from an unrelated source. Paired with 20mm XPS (rated 21dB absorption on its own per the manufacturer) directly under the screed.

**Perimeter damper strip, a construction detail not yet on this page**: 15cm strips cut from the same membrane, glued to the wall at final screed height, topped with an additional 5mm foamed-polyethylene layer stapled on top — the membrane alone (5mm) is thinner than the ~10mm gap the screed's own linear-expansion movement needs, so the extra layer closes that gap. Runs the entire perimeter of every room getting a screed; trimmed flush and sealed with acoustic sealant once finish work is done. Full technique and the screed build it sits under is on [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Flooring_Guide|Flooring Guide]]. ## Sewer-Riser Vibro-Acoustic Soundproofing (added 2026-08-24, Sidorik Round 4)

Pavel Sidorik, individual practitioner, own apartment, Belarus (level 1): a shared sewage riser ("стояк") passing through a bathroom/toilet is a significant noise source on a high floor with many floors above sharing the same stack — a large flush discharge from upper floors produces both audible falling-water noise and real structural vibration in the pipe. He's on floor 15 of a building with 8 more floors above.

**⚠️ Key material distinction**: ordinary sound-*absorbing* wrap (foamed polyethylene, mineral wool) removes airborne noise from a pipe but does **not** address the vibration transmitted through the pipe wall itself — a stack specifically needs **vibro-acoustic** soundproofing (engineered to damp vibration, not just absorb air-transmitted sound) to be effectively quieted.

**Named products**: automotive-derived foil-faced, self-adhesive vibration-damping sheet (StandartPlast/StP, a thinner/cheaper "Silver" variant also exists), or the manufacturer's purpose-built **"Noise Block Pipe"** kit sized for one 110mm-diameter, 3m sewer-stack section — pre-cut into 6 sheets, multi-layer (sound-absorbing layer + heavy sound-insulating polymer membrane + heat-insulating face), installed with aluminum mastic tape and plastic clamps rather than glued (a mis-wrap can be un-clamped and redone without wasting material). **Price: 75 BYN ≈ $30** (USD/BYN trailing-6-month average ending 2021-05-19, `currency_converter.py`) for a full one-stack kit — comparable to buying generic automotive sheet material, but pre-sized with no measuring/cutting needed. Extra material is fitted around any cleanout/access ("ревизия") fitting so that point isn't left as an acoustic/vibration gap. [source: [[_Sources/YT_zkM_gea0XVE_sidorik_bathroom_toilet_ventilation|zkM_gea0XVE]]]

## Full Frame-System Installation Technique — Wall and Ceiling, Named ASTic/Acusti Group System (Петришин-Строй, added 2026-08-24, Round 10)

**⚠️ The first genuinely systematic, step-by-step soundproofing
*installation* technique source on this page** — everything above this
section is taxonomy, product comparison, or a case-specific product
mention; these two companion videos (a wall build and a ceiling build,
same named ASTic/Acusti Group system) supply the full assembly sequence
with QC detail at every step. Ceiling video region: **level 1 direct**
(city "Реутов," a specific address named); wall video region:
unresolved (very likely the same object, given identical materials/crew,
but the city name isn't spoken in that video itself). **Both carry a
disclosed commercial affiliation** — the presenter states directly he
personally trained with this manufacturer and chooses to feature its
products for that reason (also acknowledging the product line is
premium-priced) — tag as disclosed promotional content, not a neutral
independent review, medium promotional ratio.

**Named system components**: perimeter damper tape **"Vibrostek M"**;
vibro-hangers **"Connect PS"** (a smaller-gauge wall variant and a
larger, thicker-gauge ceiling variant rated for higher load); loose-fill
insulation **"Шуманет Эко"** (staple fiberglass, acrylic — not
phenol/formaldehyde — binder, per its own included datasheet); laminated
double-GVL sandwich board **"триплекс"/"Soundline DB"** (1.2m×2.0m
sheets); dense drywall **"Акулайн"** (≈36kg/sheet, markedly heavier than
standard drywall). This is a fourth and fifth distinct ASTic/Acusti Group
product line on this page, alongside the existing Шумопласт (floor).

### ⚠️ The Decoupling Mechanism, Made Concrete

This page's existing System Type Taxonomy states that frame-based
systems mount on vibration-damping hangers "specifically to interrupt
structural/percussive noise transfer" without describing the actual
construction step that achieves it. **These sources supply that step**:
the perimeter guide track is fastened with only 3 anchors per length,
deliberately **not driven fully home** — once the frame is fully hung and
self-supporting from the vibro-hangers alone, every one of these
temporary anchors (and any track section no longer structurally needed)
is **physically pulled back out**, eliminating any direct rigid
connection between the new frame and the structural walls/slab. The
track's real function is positioning the frame during assembly, not
permanent structural support — a genuinely non-obvious step many
installers may skip or not know about.

### Depth Budgeting and Layout

- **Wall**: total finished assembly depth (frame + insulation + triplex +
  board) ≈10cm; frame alone ≈6cm. Tape and guide track are positioned by
  measuring back from the wall's single most protruding point, checked
  vertical-plane-consistent via plumb bob or laser level.
- **Ceiling**: find the room's single lowest slab point via laser level
  (real worked example: corner deviations of 0.5cm/1cm/1cm/2-2.3cm found
  in one room), then drop 9-10cm below that lowest point and snap a
  level horizontal datum line around the whole room — the frame's real
  height budget, not an assumed uniform slab height.
- **Hanger/profile spacing (both wall and ceiling)**: manufacturer spec
  is 80-90cm between hangers; the wall crew deliberately tightened to
  70cm on that specific job. Profile rows: first row 15cm from the wall,
  subsequent rows 60cm apart.

### Assembly QC Rules

- **Two-screw-per-connection rule**: every profile-to-hanger connection
  needs two screws, not one — called out as a common shortcut/mistake.
- **Straightedge three-point check**: hold a straightedge against each
  profile at top/middle/bottom; any gap anywhere (even with both ends
  flush) means the profile is out of plane and must be corrected.
- **String-line leveling for a profile row**: fix only the row's middle
  profile at both ends to the already-leveled track, stretch a string
  between those points, then adjust every other profile to within ~1mm
  of the string before final-fixing.
- **Material-quality visual tell**: a genuine system track/profile has a
  textured/dimpled ("пупырышки") surface finish — check for this when
  verifying delivered material.
- **Insulation-density myth correction**: never pack/overstuff the loose
  insulation into the cavity — it must sit loose/relaxed to perform its
  acoustic function; hanger tabs are bent back over it afterward to hold
  it in place before boarding.
- **Two-tier grid + a real fit tradeoff (ceiling)**: a second
  perpendicular profile layer is joined to the first via dedicated
  "two-level connectors." On the documented job, these connectors fit
  poorly at the crossing points; the crew candidly explains a better
  original layout (offsetting the seam to a grid-cell's middle) would
  have avoided this but increased material waste from re-cutting the
  whole layout — they accepted the poorer fit and added extra screws as
  remediation instead, a real cost-vs-fit tradeoff shown in practice.
- **Board orientation and staggered seams, two layers deep**: each
  triplex sheet has a printed installation-orientation arrow; triplex
  seams must be staggered relative to each other, and the final drywall
  layer's seams must be staggered relative to the triplex seams beneath
  — explicitly to prevent cracking at a coincident seam.
- **Finishing**: triplex seams filled with Вибросил silicone (not taped);
  excess perimeter tape trimmed with the blade at 45° so the resulting
  gap can be filled with sealant; vacuum before sealing.
- **Fastener substitution for soft exterior walls**: where exterior walls
  are a soft masonry material (cinder/foam block), coarse-thread wood
  screws replace standard anchor/dowel nails for the *temporary* track
  fixation — they hold adequately and are easier to remove later.
- **⚠️ Logistics caution**: full-size triplex sheets may not fit a
  mid-rise building's elevator and require cutting down before transport
  — plan for this before ordering full-size acoustic board.
- **Frame load-test demonstration**: a completed, unboarded ceiling frame
  held a ~70kg person standing on it with minimal deflection (the slight
  give observed was the vibro-hanger's own soft cushioning pad flexing,
  not the frame itself) — offered as evidence of a properly-built
  frame's real load capacity.
- **Eco-wool handling caution, corroborated on two separate jobs**:
  despite manufacturer eco-safety claims, installers report skin
  irritation during handling (less than mineral wool, but still
  unpleasant) — a protective coverall is worn.

## ZIPS Panel System — a Genuinely Distinct Installation Approach from the Frame System (Петришин-Строй, added 2026-08-24, Round 11)

**Explicit cross-check against the frame-system technique above**:
confirmed a genuinely distinct approach, not a variant of the same
construction method, though both share the same underlying decoupling
*principle* and come from the same manufacturer (Acoustic Group). The
frame system builds a free-standing metal-profile frame off vibration-
damping hangers, decoupled from structure via temporary/removable
perimeter anchors, with an air-gap cavity behind the finish board. **This
system instead uses a rigid, factory-made sandwich panel** (two GVL
sheets glued around an insulation core, ~5cm total with finish drywall)
**screwed directly to the wall** through silicone-filled fastening nodes
built into the panel itself — the decoupling element lives inside each
fastening point, not in a separate hanger/bracket assembly, and there's
no air-gap cavity. Shared elements: perimeter damper tape at the wall/
floor/ceiling junction, and the same dense/heavy finish drywall
(Акулайн) credited with mass-loading the assembly. 2016-vintage source
(the oldest processed on this channel), region level 1 (Одинцовский
район / Ромашково named directly), medium promotional ratio (disclosed
manufacturer-training relationship).

- **Panel construction**: two GVL sheets glued together, sandwiching a
  soundproofing insulation layer plus a resilient insert ("силомер") and
  6-8 silicone-filled fastening nodes per panel — the panel's own
  built-in decoupling mechanism. Tongue-and-groove edges let panels
  interlock and screw together.
- **Real cost figure**: ZIPS Ultra panel system, full kit (tape, silicone,
  fasteners) ≈2,500 RUB/m² (≈$40/m², trailing-6-month USD/RUB average to
  the confirmed 2016-10-13 upload date).
- **Perimeter damper-tape prep** (named: VIM100), two layers on silicone,
  fully wrapping around any obstruction — same underlying principle as
  the frame system's own perimeter tape.
- **⚠️ Mistake — excess silicone placement**: keep the damper-tape
  silicone close to the wall; excess that extends into the finish area
  is extremely difficult to remove and won't accept putty/paint.
- **Layout planning**: start from the far-left corner; measure first to
  choose which corner absorbs any partial panel.
- **Cutting technique**: jigsaw over a hand parquet saw for edge trims —
  slower, far less dust.
- **⚠️ Socket/outlet handling**: drilling for a recessed outlet box
  breaks the panel's soundproofing seal — manufacturer guidance is to
  minimize such holes, use a same-material buffer insert if unavoidable,
  or switch to surface-mounted outlets.
- **First-row leveling correction** for an unprepared wall: cut the first
  row to compensate for the wall's own deviation so later rows stay
  level.
- **⚠️ Real fastener-substitution finding, foam-block substrate**: the
  kit's default dowel anchor didn't hold in foam block; the crew used
  just the self-tapping screw (coarse thread pitch) without a dowel,
  which held securely.
- **Utility-avoidance marking**: mark an existing AC duct route before
  panel placement, then re-verify with a snap-line afterward to avoid
  drilling into it.
- **Fastening-depth QC**: countersink screws 1-2mm below the panel
  surface to avoid a protruding head catching the finish drywall.
- **⚠️ Row-offset rule, numeric**: horizontal seam stagger between rows
  must be **at least 25cm** — trim the row's starting panel if the
  natural offset comes out smaller.
- **⚠️ Counter-intuitive fastening-count rule, from the manufacturer**: a
  whole panel needs only 6 screws (3 per side) — two additional corner
  screws some installers assume are needed should specifically **not**
  be driven, since over-fastening measurably degrades the soundproofing
  performance. A cut/partial panel should instead use every retained
  fixing node.
- **Substrate-flatness requirement, numeric**: wall deviation must not
  exceed 3-4mm per running meter, or the finished panel surface itself
  comes out crooked (especially visible under paint).
- **Pre-drywall QC pass**: check every panel-to-panel connection node for
  a protruding screw head before sheathing with finish drywall.
- **Practitioner's own pros/cons**: pros are simple installation (no deep
  trade skill needed beyond a training seminar) and a thin ~5cm overall
  system versus frame/modular alternatives for a claimed comparable
  effectiveness; cons are cost ("not cheap, if not outright expensive")
  and a genuinely irritant work environment (masks/gloves needed for
  cutting GVL and handling silicone/tape) despite looking simple.

## Full Frameless (Sonoplat) Installation Technique — a Genuinely Distinct Method From the Frame System (TimRemont, added 2026-08-25, Small Bedroom batch)

**Explicit cross-check against this page's existing Full Frame-System
Installation Technique section above**: confirmed genuinely distinct, not
a restatement — this is the systematic step-by-step technique for the
**rigid, sand-filled panel ("Sonoplat")** frameless system this page's
System Type Taxonomy already names as "Rolled type"'s rigid-panel cousin,
mounted directly to the wall surface with no air-gap frame at all.
Тимофей Клименко (TimRemont), own bedroom, region not stated at level 1,
medium promotional ratio (named partner/sponsor system, "Технoсонус"/
Sonoplat "Slim"). **Chosen specifically because the room was too small to
give up a frame system's depth** — direct confirmation of this page's
existing Decision-Framework factor #2.

- **⚠️ Prerequisite the frame-system source didn't need to state**: the
  wall must be plastered dead-flat first, checked with a straightedge —
  a frameless panel has no hangers to compensate for wall waviness the way
  a frame does, so any unevenness telegraphs straight through.
- **Electrical must be fully chased into the wall before installation** —
  no air-gap cavity exists afterward to route cable through.
- **Material handling**: rest delivered panels flat for 2-3 days before
  installing so the sheet "takes" a flat shape; never store on edge.
- **Named parts**: perimeter damper tape + separate vibration tape, hammer-
  set polymer dowels, 5×30mm soundproofing-rated screws, acoustic sealant,
  a soundproofing-rated electrical box, the sand-filled Sonoplat panel
  itself, and 12.5mm acoustic-rated drywall as the finish layer.
- **Tongue-and-groove trimming**: cut off the panel's own edge profile
  where it meets a wall/corner so it sits flush against the perimeter
  tape with no gap; seal every panel-to-panel joint to prevent "sound
  bridges."
- **⚠️ Cut-edge taping rule — corroborates and extends this page's
  existing note** (previously recorded from a separate source: any cut
  edge of a sand-filled panel must be taped immediately or the sand filler
  spills, degrading the panel). This source confirms the same rule for the
  same product type and adds that the manufacturer supplies a dedicated
  tape for exactly this purpose.
- **Cutting technique**: a utility knife works but dulls fast cutting
  directly on a hard screed floor — cut on a softer foam-board surface
  instead to preserve the blade; an oscillating multi-tool is faster and
  cleaner for outlet-box cutouts specifically.
- **Fastening pattern**: hammer-set polymer dowels (not screwed) in
  pre-drilled 6mm holes, first row set to a laser level with every
  subsequent row referenced off it; panels staggered/brick-patterned, 3
  vertical fastening columns per sheet, dowels no more than 30cm apart.
  If a dowel hole hits embedded rebar, relocate the fastener rather than
  forcing it, and seal the abandoned hole.
- **Electrical box integration**: existing outlet boxes re-chased deeper
  to leave room for a soundproofing-rated box within the new build-up,
  positioned flush with the finished drywall plane, sealed around the
  opening.
- **Finish-layer fastening**: screws into the Sonoplat panel (not the
  original wall) with a 10mm counterbore drilled first so the head sits
  recessed and doesn't telegraph through the finish.
- **⚠️ Real total assembly thickness, directly comparable to the frame
  system's own ~10cm figure above**: this frameless build-up measured
  **34mm total** — a concrete number confirming the frame-vs-frameless
  depth tradeoff, not just a qualitative claim.
- **Flagged manufacturer feedback item**: trimming excess perimeter tape
  after installation reliably leaves sealant residue that's hard to fully
  clean — reported directly to the product's manufacturer as an
  unresolved process gap.

## Door-Sill Soundproofing Gasket — a Technique Not Yet Covered Above

TimRemont (Тимофей Клименко, own apartment, region not stated at level 1,
added 2026-08-25, Small Bedroom batch): a **soundproofing gasket/threshold
strip installed at the door sill itself** (bedroom and bathroom doors, in
this case to protect a sleeping toddler) is a distinct technique from
every wall/floor/ceiling method above — it targets the gap under a closed
door specifically. Directly tested (music played, door closed) and
confirmed a real, noticeable reduction in sound transfer. **⚠️ Explicit
tradeoff**: the same seal that blocks sound also blocks the passive
under-door airflow a room may depend on for ventilation — this
practitioner's own workaround is propping the door slightly open, or
relying on the room's AC/mechanical ventilation instead of passive
under-door airflow. `single-account`, no named product. [source: [[_Sources/YT_U95vMOOhKH8_timremont_39sqm_walkin_office_zoning|U95vMOOhKH8]]]
