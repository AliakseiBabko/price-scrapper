# Electrical — Lighting Colour Temperature

Which colour temperature goes where, the coupling between temperature and intensity that governs whether a room reads as comfortable, the same-scene consistency rule and the practitioner exceptions to it, and how to test a finish under the light it will actually be lit by.

**Extracted from [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Lighting_Design|Lighting Design]] on 2026-09-03**, after that page was first merged out of a fragmented state. Colour temperature is an independent decision with its own sources, its own numbers and its own live disagreement, and it was the largest coherent unit on a page that had grown past the soft target.

## Colour Temperature

Which temperature where, the coupling between temperature and intensity, and how to test a finish under the light it will actually live in.

### 2. Colour Temperature

### The rules

- **⚠️ Never exceed 4000 K anywhere in a residential space.** Comfortable range for living areas: **2700–4000 K**. Warmer reads cosier and generally makes an interior look more expensive; **3000 K is the single most universal default** — cosy without reading yellow, works in nearly any room. **Reserve neutral ~4000 K for pure task areas** — laundry, walk-in closets, storage, and a kitchen worktop's own task light — not for general illumination. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]
- **⚠️ Every fixture running simultaneously in the same scene must share the same colour temperature.** A 3000 K ceiling fixture beside a 4000 K sconce running together reads visibly mismatched and cheap even though each is individually fine. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]
- **⚠️ Evening/decorative lighting must match across the *whole home*, not just within a room.** A hallway at a different evening temperature from the bedroom reads "off" the moment you move between them. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]
- **⚠️ Over-powering is a real mistake, not only under-powering.** Overly bright evening light measurably disrupts melatonin and circadian rhythm — not merely a comfort preference. Conversely, underpowered light in a zone that needs brightness (entryway, bathroom, walk-in closet) causes eye strain and faster visual fatigue. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dJMsXYUyh7A_kruglov_lighting_guide|YT_dJMsXYUyh7A]]]

### ⚠️ The mechanism behind the same-scene rule

The rule above arrived as an assertion. **Реньжин supplies the physiology**: *"по физиологии, когда ты видишь разную цветовую температуру в одном помещении… **фокус внимания раздваивается**."* You stop reading it as a single space; mixing temperatures *"разбивает пространство на две части."*

**His scoped exception**: a genuine functional split — a workout corner under cold light beside a calm zone under warm — which is a scenario distinction, not a mistake. **He also notes cold light aids focus physiologically**, which is why offices use it, and that office lighting should ideally warm toward evening though most companies do not bother. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9MsEZVjLH2M_kuzina_renzhin_lighting_design_podcast|YT_9MsEZVjLH2M]]]

### ⚠️ Do not confuse warm with soft — the most useful correction on this page

**Luminous flux is independent of colour temperature.** People compare two showroom fixtures of **identical lumen output**, one 3000 K and one 4000 K, conclude the 4000 K is brighter, and downgrade the whole scheme to warm. **Both illuminate a tabletop identically.** They are confusing *warm* with *soft*. **If 4000 K feels too bright, take 4000 K with fewer lumens.**

Two further reasons Шеврина gives for neutral as the *main* light: warm light is strongly relaxing and sleep-inducing, which is wrong if you want to work in the evening; and **neutral light does not distort the flat's colours**, so it will not push already-warm walls further yellow when the main light comes on. [source: [[_Sources/YT_NfHyCfo1J4w_shevrina_lighting_mistakes|YT_NfHyCfo1J4w]]]

### ⚠️ The Kruithof curve — the coupling rule that explains most of this section

**This page's separate figures — warm for dim living rooms, 4000 K for a bright worktop, "if 4000 K feels too bright take 4000 K with fewer lumens" — are all consequences of one relationship, and lighting supplier Константин Цепелев (practising since 2006) and lighting designer Матвей Борисов name it: the Kruithof curve**, relating luminous flux to colour temperature. (The ASR transcript renders it «кривая Крит хофа»; the referent is unambiguous.)

**The rule: the brighter the light, the cooler it should be. The more dimmed the light, the warmer it should be.** Stated by them against illuminance: at office norms of **500, 600, 700 up to 1000 lux** the colour temperature must rise; drop the illuminance to **200, 300, 150, 100 lux** and the colour temperature must fall.

**Why this is worth having as a rule rather than as a table**: it makes the worktop figure derivable instead of conventional. A cooking zone is light-*intensive*, so **4000 K is the comfortable choice there because of the brightness, not because it is a kitchen** — and domestic lighting elsewhere is mostly not bright, which is why the rest of the home is warm. It also sharpens this page's own "do not confuse warm with soft" correction above: taking 4000 K with fewer lumens is moving *against* the curve, so it works only within limits.

**Two further refinements from the same source:**

- **A height rule: the higher the luminaire, the cooler it may be; the closer to the eyes, the warmer it should be.** Recorded as their «гистограмма» framing, `single-account`.
- **A latitude argument for the warm residential default this page already holds.** At this latitude the dominant daylight coming off the sky is bluer, so warmer artificial light suits people here. They contrast Dubai and Thailand, where «уют достигается наоборот холодом» — and the host reports finding those interiors actively uncomfortable. **Recorded as a rationale for the existing default, not a new figure**, `unverified`.

Their own residential figures — **3000 K, sometimes 3500 K, and 2700–3000 K in some cases, with 4000 K over the worktop** — are straightforward corroboration of this section's existing range from a fourth independent channel. [source: [[_Sources/YT_ZKAcnh4kKfY_nsdsgn_lighting_colour_temperature_and_gear|YT_ZKAcnh4kKfY]]]

### Per-room refinements

| Zone | Temperature | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bedroom, central chandelier as sole source** | **2700–3500 K** | Narrows the general range for this case [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|YT_xikuzV80GP4]]] |
| **Bedroom, evening scenario** | **not above 3000 K** | Cool light rouses, warm promotes sleep. Stated as settled science; recorded as her rationale, `unverified` [source: [[_Sources/YT_nYvqqkNeS4k_shevrina_bedroom_lighting_and_headboard|YT_nYvqqkNeS4k]]] |
| **Home office / study** | **4000–5000 K** | Cooler than neutral, to sustain focus without the drowsiness warmer light induces. Either two brightness scenarios or one dimmable fixture spanning both [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|YT_xikuzV80GP4]]] |
| **Dining/eating area** | **neutral — not warm, not cold** | Makes food look more appetising, comfortable regardless of time of day [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|YT_xikuzV80GP4]]] |
| **Kitchen worktop task light** | **4000 K** | See the pairing constraint below |
| **Main/base light generally** | neutral | Accent/decor lighting (strips behind 3D panels, cove) can deliberately be warm for cosiness — **a primary-vs-accent pairing rule, distinct from the same-scene rule for simultaneous same-function fixtures** [source: [[_Sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|YT_1gXYL99mfY4]]] |

**⚠️ The 4000 K worktop figure is independently corroborated from a second channel, and the constraint attached is the more useful half.** Кузина specifies 4000 K for kitchen worktop lighting because the whiter light is materially easier to work under — restating Kruglov's figure from an unrelated source. **But kitchen ceiling light is almost never 4000 K, and a white task light running under warm ambient light "reads as a mistake, not a design feature"** — so check the pairing before specifying. That is this page's own same-scene rule arriving independently. [source: [[_Sources/YT_iHViNm3dESU_kuzina_kitchen_electrics_and_lighting|YT_iHViNm3dESU]]]

### ⚠️ Dim-to-warm, and tunable white — the way out of the whole argument

- **«Dim to warm» is the specific feature to specify**, available on modern fittings and especially recessed spots and LED strip: **bright output is cool, dimmed output becomes warmer.** One fitting therefore covers both a bright cool waking scenario and a soft warm relaxing one **without a second circuit** — the cheapest route to this page's minimum-three-scenarios rule. Кузина's framing: you must control **both brightness and colour temperature**, via a dimmer. Her alternative if the fitting cannot: keep the main ceiling light and add **warmer diffuse perimeter lighting**. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dIdcLPKj6is_kuzina_home_spa_bathroom|YT_dIdcLPKj6is]]]
- **Tunable white is now baseline.** Three or four years before recording, a factory told Реньжин it did not exist and then quoted **3× list**; by 2022 it is standard on a single fixture. **Some fixtures shift warmer as they dim** — which he offers as the practical answer to a household that disagrees about colour temperature. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9MsEZVjLH2M_kuzina_renzhin_lighting_design_podcast|YT_9MsEZVjLH2M]]]

### Colour-temperature-by-function, a Round 5 refinement (Игорь Краснов, added 2026-09-01)

**⚠️ A specific numeric consolidation, largely converging with this page's existing figures above rather than adding a new mechanism**: **2700-3000K** for living/relaxation spaces, **4000K** neutral for work zones (kitchen, home office); explicit warning against **6000K** cold light, described as giving "a hospital-corridor feel." Recorded as corroboration of the existing 2700-4000K comfortable-range rule and the 4000K work-zone figure above — cross-reference, not a new range. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_4ZjCbDRhfNM_krasnov_please_buy_these_6things|4ZjCbDRhfNM]]]

### ⚠️ ~3000K residential default, corroborated twice within Round 9 (Игорь Краснов, added 2026-09-02, Round 9)

**Two of this round's own Krasnov sources independently converge on the same ~3000K figure and the same reasoning against colder light for residential rooms — recorded as a same-round, same-channel double corroboration of this section's existing 2700-4000K range, not a new figure.** [sources: [[_Sources/YT_OWIBkORaQX4_krasnov_lighting_guide_full|OWIBkORaQX4]], [[_Sources/YT_Yt3yAPjrLhk_krasnov_5solutions_clients_love|Yt3yAPjrLhk]]]

- `OWIBkORaQX4`: prefers **~3000K** for residential; **below 2000K reads "too orange,"** reserve for special occasions only; **above 4000K reads "too cold/uncomfortable"** for residential use, appropriate only for industrial/warehouse spaces simulating daylight.
- `Yt3yAPjrLhk`: keeps residential lighting **~3000K** (soft, warm, doesn't distort material colours); reserves **~4000K for WORK zones only**, explicitly warning that using ~4000K light in a bedroom or living room makes colour depth "disappear" and the space feel like it's lost its "air."

`single-account` (channel-internal convergence), `unverified`.

### ⚠️ Recessed Downlights on a Relief-Moulded Ceiling — a Genuinely New Interaction (Игорь Краснов, added 2026-09-01, Round 5)

A real-listing critique supplies a specific mechanism this page did not previously have: **recessed spotlights that wash light across a ceiling already dense with 3D moulding relief accentuate the moulding's own texture**, making an already-heavy ceiling read even more oppressive/"dome-like" than the moulding alone would. A flatter/softer light treatment (fewer recessed points, more diffuse/indirect sources) would have softened the ceiling's visual weight instead of amplifying it. **The point generalises past this one case**: recessed-lighting layout choice should account for how it interacts with a textured/relief ceiling surface, not just room-level lux targets — a genuinely new planning consideration for any room with heavy ceiling moulding or a strong plaster relief. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_Pqc19izKNXI_krasnov_dont_do_classic_interior|Pqc19izKNXI]]]

### Coloured / RGB light

**Coloured strip lighting is argued to cheapen an interior except in a deliberately themed room** — Шеврина names a gaming or computer room as the legitimate case. **For normal use: 4000 K neutral or 3000 K warm**, and she prefers warm specifically because a strip usually belongs to the evening scenario rather than the working one. She also states that **the influence of a cornice LED strip on wall-colour choice is overstated** — people over-weight it when selecting paint. [source: [[_Sources/YT_XGI6FS2ZdCc_shevrina_25_antitrends|YT_XGI6FS2ZdCc]], [[_Sources/YT_NfHyCfo1J4w_shevrina_lighting_mistakes|YT_NfHyCfo1J4w]]]

*A second Kruglov-channel voice agrees from the trend side: coloured LED accent lighting is excluded from otherwise-endorsed cove and shelf backlighting, and called out as never having been stylish.* [source: [[_Sources/YT_1gXYL99mfY4_kruglov_how_to_choose_lighting|YT_1gXYL99mfY4]]]

**⚠️ And a finish constraint that belongs with the decision, not with the fitting: some finishes look very bad under coloured light. If RGB is planned, specify plain cream walls and grey floors.** Кузина is openly sceptical of chromotherapy claims — green relaxes, red stimulates is *"всё очень относительно"* — but allows that watching one shade fade into another under the shower may simply be pleasant. **A solution she does rate: a stretch ceiling backlit by LED strip through the membrane**, giving an even wash, switchable between colour and white. [source: [[_Sources/YT_dIdcLPKj6is_kuzina_home_spa_bathroom|YT_dIdcLPKj6is]]]

### ⚠️ Cheap multi-colour-temperature "smart" fixtures — a specific product-quality warning (Игорь Краснов, added 2026-09-01, Round 4)

**Extends this section's existing same-scene colour-temperature-matching rule with a specific product-category failure mode, genuinely new to this page.** Never install bulbs of genuinely different colour temperatures in the same fixture or room (his example: 3000K+4000K+5000K mixed). **Names AliExpress specifically** as a source of cheap multi-colour-temperature "smart" LED fixtures that cycle between cold/neutral/warm output on each power-on rather than holding a fixed temperature — calling this "unusable" since the user can't reliably get back to the temperature they wanted. Specifically flags the failure mode of a second light source in the same room being a DIFFERENT FIXED temperature than the main fixture — the same underlying mismatch this page's existing same-scene rule already warns against, here traced to a specific cheap-product cause rather than a specification mistake. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_vM7inxJZ-6U_krasnov_color_combination_mistakes|vM7inxJZ-6U]]]

### ⚠️ Test a Finish at the Colour Temperature It Will Actually Be Lit At (Надежда Кузина, added 2026-09-01)

A short cross-reference into this page's colour-temperature section from a paint-selection source, because **the technique solves a sequencing problem: the finish is usually chosen before the fittings exist.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_pFCVO7AuSMw_kuzina_choosing_wall_paint|pFCVO7AuSMw]]]

- **⚠️ "Даже один цвет в разном освещении может дать гораздо больше разницы в тоне, чем два соседних цвета."** So agonising between two near-identical paint colours in a showroom is the wrong problem — the lighting will move the colour further than the choice does.
- **⚠️ The on-site problem: while finishes are being chosen there is usually a builder's bulb of unknown origin hanging from the ceiling, and nobody knows its colour temperature.** Any judgment made under it is worthless.
- **⚠️ The fix, and it is cheap: use a variable-temperature LED panel — the common photographer's and videographer's product. Set it to the temperature the room's lighting will actually be, e.g. 3000 K, hold it to the sample, and check how the finish behaves under exactly that light.** Dedicated sample-testing lamps also exist.
- **Reflexes compound it**: neighbouring surfaces reflect onto the finish too, which matters most for whitish, greyish and light neutrals.

**This complements the finish constraint already on this page** — that some finishes look very bad under coloured light, so cream walls and grey floors should be specified where RGB is planned. **The general rule behind both: a finish and the light it will sit under are one decision, and the finish is normally chosen first.**

### ⚠️⚠️ A Practitioner Breaks the Same-Scene Rule DELIBERATELY for a Task Zone — 4000 K in a 2700–3000 K Flat (Александр Синчуков, his own kitchen, 2023-11-02)

**«Обратите внимание, что это 4000 — НЕЙТРАЛЬНО БЕЛЫЙ свет — потому что ВО ВСЕЙ КВАРТИРЕ у меня 2700–3000 КЕЛЬВИНОВ, приятный тёплый цвет. ИМЕННО ДЛЯ ГОТОВКИ обычно мы используем такой нейтральный цвет: он даёт какое-то более ПРАВИЛЬНОЕ ОЩУЩЕНИЕ для приготовления еды.»**

- ⚠️ **This is a knowing breach of the same-scene consistency rule this page holds, carved out for a WORKING SURFACE, by a practitioner, in his own flat.** The worktop strip is 4000 K; everything else in the flat is 2700–3000 K.
- ⚠️⚠️ **AND THE JUSTIFICATION IS WEAK BY HIS OWN ADMISSION — he reports the effect and cannot explain it.** That is exactly how it should be recorded: **a task-zone exception practitioners make and defend ON FEEL, against a rule this page holds on measurable grounds (the Kruithof coupling and scene consistency).**
- → **The honest status is CONTESTED, not "the rule has an established exception".** A defensible reading of both is that the same-scene rule governs surfaces you LOOK AT and the task-temperature preference governs surfaces you WORK AT — but nothing in the sources establishes that, and it is offered here as inference only.
- **Note also that the strip is interrupted by the extract, which carries its own light on a SEPARATE button — so within one 3-metre run there are two fittings, two switches and (at the extract) an unstated second colour temperature.** ⚠️ *A real-world scene is less consistent than the rule assumes even when the designer is trying.*
