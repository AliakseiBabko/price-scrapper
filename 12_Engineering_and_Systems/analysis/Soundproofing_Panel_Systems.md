# Soundproofing — ZIPS and Frameless Panel Systems

The two panel systems that are genuinely distinct methods from the frame system: ZIPS, and frameless (Sonoplat). **Split out on 2026-09-02, verbatim.** Its parent was within fifteen lines of the vault's 300-line ceiling and would have breached on the next intake batch.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **These are alternatives to the frame system, not additions to it.** The frame-system technique, and the floor and riser work that applies whichever you choose, stay on [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Soundproofing_Installation_Technique|Soundproofing Installation Technique]].

## ZIPS Panel System — a Genuinely Distinct Installation Approach from the Frame System (Петришин-Строй, added 2026-08-24, Round 11)

**Explicit cross-check against the frame-system technique above**:
confirmed a genuinely distinct approach, not a variant of the same
construction method, though both share the same underlying decoupling
*principle* and come from the same manufacturer (Acoustic Group). The
frame system builds a free-standing metal-profile frame off vibration-
damping hangers, decoupled from structure via temporary/removable
perimeter anchors, with an air-gap cavity behind the finish board. **This
system instead uses a rigid, factory-made sandwich panel** (two GVL
sheets glued around an insulation core, ~5cm total with finish drywall)
**screwed directly to the wall** through silicone-filled fastening nodes
built into the panel itself — the decoupling element lives inside each
fastening point, not in a separate hanger/bracket assembly, and there's
no air-gap cavity. Shared elements: perimeter damper tape at the wall/
floor/ceiling junction, and the same dense/heavy finish drywall
(Акулайн) credited with mass-loading the assembly. 2016-vintage source
(the oldest processed on this channel), region level 1 (Одинцовский
район / Ромашково named directly), medium promotional ratio (disclosed
manufacturer-training relationship).

- **Panel construction**: two GVL sheets glued together, sandwiching a
  soundproofing insulation layer plus a resilient insert ("силомер") and
  6-8 silicone-filled fastening nodes per panel — the panel's own
  built-in decoupling mechanism. Tongue-and-groove edges let panels
  interlock and screw together.
- **Real cost figure**: ZIPS Ultra panel system, full kit (tape, silicone,
  fasteners) ≈2,500 RUB/m² (≈$40/m², trailing-6-month USD/RUB average to
  the confirmed 2016-10-13 upload date).
- **Perimeter damper-tape prep** (named: VIM100), two layers on silicone,
  fully wrapping around any obstruction — same underlying principle as
  the frame system's own perimeter tape.
- **⚠️ Mistake — excess silicone placement**: keep the damper-tape
  silicone close to the wall; excess that extends into the finish area
  is extremely difficult to remove and won't accept putty/paint.
- **Layout planning**: start from the far-left corner; measure first to
  choose which corner absorbs any partial panel.
- **Cutting technique**: jigsaw over a hand parquet saw for edge trims —
  slower, far less dust.
- **⚠️ Socket/outlet handling**: drilling for a recessed outlet box
  breaks the panel's soundproofing seal — manufacturer guidance is to
  minimize such holes, use a same-material buffer insert if unavoidable,
  or switch to surface-mounted outlets.
- **First-row leveling correction** for an unprepared wall: cut the first
  row to compensate for the wall's own deviation so later rows stay
  level.
- **⚠️ Real fastener-substitution finding, foam-block substrate**: the
  kit's default dowel anchor didn't hold in foam block; the crew used
  just the self-tapping screw (coarse thread pitch) without a dowel,
  which held securely.
- **Utility-avoidance marking**: mark an existing AC duct route before
  panel placement, then re-verify with a snap-line afterward to avoid
  drilling into it.
- **Fastening-depth QC**: countersink screws 1-2mm below the panel
  surface to avoid a protruding head catching the finish drywall.
- **⚠️ Row-offset rule, numeric**: horizontal seam stagger between rows
  must be **at least 25cm** — trim the row's starting panel if the
  natural offset comes out smaller.
- **⚠️ Counter-intuitive fastening-count rule, from the manufacturer**: a
  whole panel needs only 6 screws (3 per side) — two additional corner
  screws some installers assume are needed should specifically **not**
  be driven, since over-fastening measurably degrades the soundproofing
  performance. A cut/partial panel should instead use every retained
  fixing node.
- **Substrate-flatness requirement, numeric**: wall deviation must not
  exceed 3-4mm per running meter, or the finished panel surface itself
  comes out crooked (especially visible under paint).
- **Pre-drywall QC pass**: check every panel-to-panel connection node for
  a protruding screw head before sheathing with finish drywall.
- **Practitioner's own pros/cons**: pros are simple installation (no deep
  trade skill needed beyond a training seminar) and a thin ~5cm overall
  system versus frame/modular alternatives for a claimed comparable
  effectiveness; cons are cost ("not cheap, if not outright expensive")
  and a genuinely irritant work environment (masks/gloves needed for
  cutting GVL and handling silicone/tape) despite looking simple.

## Full Frameless (Sonoplat) Installation Technique — a Genuinely Distinct Method From the Frame System (TimRemont, added 2026-08-25, Small Bedroom batch)

**Explicit cross-check against this page's existing Full Frame-System
Installation Technique section above**: confirmed genuinely distinct, not
a restatement — this is the systematic step-by-step technique for the
**rigid, sand-filled panel ("Sonoplat")** frameless system this page's
System Type Taxonomy already names as "Rolled type"'s rigid-panel cousin,
mounted directly to the wall surface with no air-gap frame at all.
Тимофей Клименко (TimRemont), own bedroom, region not stated at level 1,
medium promotional ratio (named partner/sponsor system, "Технoсонус"/
Sonoplat "Slim"). **Chosen specifically because the room was too small to
give up a frame system's depth** — direct confirmation of this page's
existing Decision-Framework factor #2.

- **⚠️ Prerequisite the frame-system source didn't need to state**: the
  wall must be plastered dead-flat first, checked with a straightedge —
  a frameless panel has no hangers to compensate for wall waviness the way
  a frame does, so any unevenness telegraphs straight through.
- **Electrical must be fully chased into the wall before installation** —
  no air-gap cavity exists afterward to route cable through.
- **Material handling**: rest delivered panels flat for 2-3 days before
  installing so the sheet "takes" a flat shape; never store on edge.
- **Named parts**: perimeter damper tape + separate vibration tape, hammer-
  set polymer dowels, 5×30mm soundproofing-rated screws, acoustic sealant,
  a soundproofing-rated electrical box, the sand-filled Sonoplat panel
  itself, and 12.5mm acoustic-rated drywall as the finish layer.
- **Tongue-and-groove trimming**: cut off the panel's own edge profile
  where it meets a wall/corner so it sits flush against the perimeter
  tape with no gap; seal every panel-to-panel joint to prevent "sound
  bridges."
- **⚠️ Cut-edge taping rule — corroborates and extends this page's
  existing note** (previously recorded from a separate source: any cut
  edge of a sand-filled panel must be taped immediately or the sand filler
  spills, degrading the panel). This source confirms the same rule for the
  same product type and adds that the manufacturer supplies a dedicated
  tape for exactly this purpose.
- **Cutting technique**: a utility knife works but dulls fast cutting
  directly on a hard screed floor — cut on a softer foam-board surface
  instead to preserve the blade; an oscillating multi-tool is faster and
  cleaner for outlet-box cutouts specifically.
- **Fastening pattern**: hammer-set polymer dowels (not screwed) in
  pre-drilled 6mm holes, first row set to a laser level with every
  subsequent row referenced off it; panels staggered/brick-patterned, 3
  vertical fastening columns per sheet, dowels no more than 30cm apart.
  If a dowel hole hits embedded rebar, relocate the fastener rather than
  forcing it, and seal the abandoned hole.
- **Electrical box integration**: existing outlet boxes re-chased deeper
  to leave room for a soundproofing-rated box within the new build-up,
  positioned flush with the finished drywall plane, sealed around the
  opening.
- **Finish-layer fastening**: screws into the Sonoplat panel (not the
  original wall) with a 10mm counterbore drilled first so the head sits
  recessed and doesn't telegraph through the finish.
- **⚠️ Real total assembly thickness, directly comparable to the frame
  system's own ~10cm figure above**: this frameless build-up measured
  **34mm total** — a concrete number confirming the frame-vs-frameless
  depth tradeoff, not just a qualitative claim.
- **Flagged manufacturer feedback item**: trimming excess perimeter tape
  after installation reliably leaves sealant residue that's hard to fully
  clean — reported directly to the product's manufacturer as an
  unresolved process gap.
