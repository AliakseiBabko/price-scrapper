# Plumbing — Wall-Hung Toilet Installation

Two common misconceptions worth correcting before choosing between wall-hung and floor-standing. Part of [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/Plumbing_and_Waterproofing|Plumbing & Waterproofing]].

- attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is not channel-isolated: **Weight capacity is commonly underestimated**: customers often assume an in-wall toilet frame/cistern can't support much weight, but even base-tier frames are commonly rated to 200–250 kg, with reinforced versions up to 400 kg — and reinforced frames aren't described as significantly pricier, so it's argued as worth buying the reinforced version by default if there's any doubt.
- attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is not channel-isolated: **The frame's feet must rest on a solid, monolithic concrete base only** — never directly on tile, screed, or another finish material — or the installation degrades and fails over time. This is a hard installation requirement, not a preference.
- attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is not channel-isolated: **Repairability is also commonly underestimated**: the entire flush/fill mechanism is typically accessible and serviceable through the flush-button panel (remove the button plate for access to nearly everything repairable); only some components deep in the sewage-side connection are described as unreachable without more invasive work.
- attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is single-account and has no decisive channel field: **An emerging, inconsistent code-enforcement risk worth watching (not yet a settled rule)**: hiding a wall-hung toilet's in-wall frame/cistern inside a "santekhkorob" that also houses water distribution, meters, and a tankless heater has been standard practice for years, but one source reports isolated recent cases of BTI (technical inventory bureau) inspectors objecting to this specific arrangement at final acceptance. `single-account`, Russia-specific (BTI is a Russian institution, not applicable as written to a Belarus project) — flagged for awareness in case Belarus practice shifts similarly, not as a current rule.
