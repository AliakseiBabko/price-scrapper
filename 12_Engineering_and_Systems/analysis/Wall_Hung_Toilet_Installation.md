# Plumbing — Wall-Hung Toilet Installation

Two common misconceptions worth correcting before choosing between wall-hung and floor-standing. Part of [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/Plumbing_and_Waterproofing|Plumbing & Waterproofing]].

- Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario explains: **Weight capacity is commonly underestimated**: customers often assume an in-wall toilet frame/cistern can't support much weight, but even base-tier frames are commonly rated to 200–250 kg, with reinforced versions up to 400 kg — and reinforced frames aren't described as significantly pricier, so it's argued as worth buying the reinforced version by default if there's any doubt.
- Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario states: **The frame's feet must rest on a solid, monolithic concrete base only** — never directly on tile, screed, or another finish material — or the installation degrades and fails over time. This is a hard installation requirement, not a preference.
- Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario explains: **Repairability is also commonly underestimated**: the entire flush/fill mechanism is typically accessible and serviceable through the flush-button panel (remove the button plate for access to nearly everything repairable); only some components deep in the sewage-side connection are described as unreachable without more invasive work.
- Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario reports: **An emerging, inconsistent code-enforcement risk worth watching (not yet a settled rule)**: hiding a wall-hung toilet's in-wall frame/cistern inside a "santekhkorob" that also houses water distribution, meters, and a tankless heater has been standard practice for years, but one source reports isolated recent cases of BTI (technical inventory bureau) inspectors objecting to this specific arrangement at final acceptance. `single-account`, Russia-specific (BTI is a Russian institution, not applicable as written to a Belarus project) — flagged for awareness in case Belarus practice shifts similarly, not as a current rule.
- A premium bathroom-brand representative (Петришин-Стрoй Round 10, trade-show interview) **independently corroborates the 400kg load figure above from a second, unrelated brand**, and adds: the frame's **lower fixation points bear the main structural load**, not the rear/upper leveling anchors. **Repairability**: only two mechanisms exist inside a frame (inlet valve, outlet/flush valve), both reached through the maintenance hatch with hand-tightened (tool-free) fittings; typical manufacturer warranty terms are 15 years on the frame/tank body and 2 years on the fittings/valve mechanism (extendable with a certified installer), and parts continue to be produced after a model is discontinued. The most common wear failure is the outlet-valve flap gasket degrading from limescale and starting a slow leak — sold as a separate replacement part, not a design defect.
- Same source: **tile-to-metal-frame cold-crack risk** — where tile meets the frame's exposed metal maintenance-window edge with no grout gap, differing thermal expansion between metal and tile can crack the tile; keep a proper grout joint at that junction rather than a tight direct fit.
- Same source: **corner installations are handled via a separate universal corner-mount bracket kit**, not a dedicated corner-frame product — any standard frame converts to corner mounting by swapping its included bracket for the corner kit. ## Installation-Frame Buying Mistakes — Round 1 (ДЕЛАТЬ НЕ ПЕРЕДЕЛАТЬ, added 2026-08-28)

> [!NOTE]
> Vladimir Amelchenko, `4gKFfJrbQjk`. ⚠️ High promotional ratio — a single-manufacturer (ТЦ/TeCe) showroom interview; only brand-agnostic principles recorded here. **This source's own 400 kg reinforced-frame load claim is a third independent brand corroborating the figure already recorded above** (Ontario, Petrishin-Stroi). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_4gKFfJrbQjk_sbk_installation_frame_mistakes|YT_4gKFfJrbQjk]]]

- **⚠️ Mounting-point count should scale with the wall material behind the frame, not be fixed at a default**: a poured-concrete load-bearing wall needs only 2 rear anchor points; a softer wall material (aerated concrete/cinder block) should get more anchor points, since it holds anchors less securely.
- **Frame depth is a real use-case variable**: standard depth (~15cm) for ordinary in-wall installs against a real wall; slim depth (~8cm) for stud/false-wall builds specifically to save space behind the fixture. **The slim variant's rear/floor connection method is generally only practical in private/detached-house construction** — in a multi-story apartment building, the fixed sewer-stack location typically rules it out.
- **Rimless toilets are the current standard hygiene recommendation over rimmed toilets** (a rim traps dirt the manufacturer's glaze process can't reach) — but rimless designs can splash water onto the floor on flush if flow isn't tuned to the bowl. **Fix**: interchangeable flow-restrictor rings fitted inside the flush outlet (from the toilet or frame manufacturer) widen or narrow the water column — DIY-installable, no plumber required.
- A height-adjustable installation ("toilet terminal" style) exists for children's bathrooms — toilet height can be lowered while children are small and raised later without redoing surrounding tile/finish.
