## ⚠️ Itemised System Costs, and the Ceiling Void Each Type Consumes (Александр Синчуков / NSDSGN, 2023-10-17)

**A designer's systematic explainer, and it supplies the two things this page's system comparisons lacked: itemised prices per system type, and general ceiling-void figures.** Manual Russian caption track. **October 2023, converted at 89.5803 RUB/USD** (trailing 6 months to 2023-10-17). ### Costs — and the decomposition matters more than the totals

| System | RUB | USD |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ventilation design project, 100 m²** | ~50,000 | ≈$560 |
| **Бризер ventilation** | from 60,000 — three бризеры at 20,000 each | from ≈$670 (≈$220 each) |
| **Wall-mounted AC** | from 150,000 — three units at 30,000, plus install with consumables 20,000 | from ≈$1,700 |
| **Ducted AC** | from 600,000 — two ducted units at 150,000 each, ducts/copper/consumables from 100,000, install and commissioning from 200,000 | from ≈$6,700 |
| **Ducted ventilation** | from 500,000 — machine 250,000, ducts 70,000, install and commissioning from 150,000 | from ≈$5,600 |

- **The ducted-AC breakdown is the useful part: ~25% equipment offset by ~33% installation and commissioning.** A quote that looks equipment-heavy or install-light is checkable against it.
- **⚠️ Cross-check against this vault's existing pricing tiers in [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Breathers_vs_Mechanical_Ventilation|Breathers vs Mechanical Ventilation]], which are broadly consistent once dated**: his **20,000 RUB per бризер (Oct 2023)** sits below Goncharov's **~30,000 for a single unit** recorded later, in the direction inflation predicts; and his **500,000 RUB ducted supply-and-extract** sits above Goncharov's **~300,000 for a supply-only central system with electric heating**, which is the direction scope predicts. **Two independent sources, two years apart, no contradiction.**
- The project-fee figure is stated as depending on the project's composition, the volume of the space, and the designer's qualification — **50,000 is offered as an orientation for 100 m², not a rate.**

### ⚠️ Ceiling void consumed — general figures, and the sequencing trap they imply

- **Ventilation ducts most often take 150–200 mm of ceiling void.**
- **⚠️ Two ducts crossing one above the other give a double drop.**
- **Ventilation machines take more — a minimum of 300 mm.**
- **⚠️ Duct cross-section grows with run length and increases nearer the machine** — so the void requirement is not uniform across the flat, and the worst case is beside the plant.
- **⚠️ THE SEQUENCING TRAP, stated explicitly: ventilation ducts are installed FIRST on site. You must check how much void remains BELOW them, because the lighting arrives AFTER the ceilings go in — «очень часто этого пространства не хватает для установки лампочек или треков».**
  **This closes a loop the vault had open from both ends: a ceiling installer independently states «настройка начинается всегда с вентиляции» and gives 10–15 working days lead time for ceiling integration items (see [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/analysis/Ceiling_Type_Comparison_and_Cost|Ceiling Type Comparison and Cost]]), while this source gives the void arithmetic that makes the ordering non-negotiable.**
- **Plant is normally placed where ceiling height and noise matter less — bathrooms, storerooms, walk-in wardrobes** — with ducts fanning out to the habitable rooms.
- **⚠️ A trick for a habitable room: put the grille directly in the WALL and bring the duct to it across the ceiling of the ADJACENT room.** Keeps the drop out of the room that needs the height.

### ⚠️ Diffusers — and independent corroboration of the combined slot

- **Ordinary grilles are the ventilation trade's default on budget projects, and the consequence is blunt: «такая решётка сразу же превратит ваш интерьер в подсобное помещение эконом-класса».**
- **Щелевые (slot) grilles are his preference**, with slot count set by air volume — **including versions plastered flush into the wall so no joint shows and only the slots remain.**
- **⚠️ «Сейчас даже есть решение, когда щель вентиляции стыкуется с шинопроводом.» This is the same combined ventilation-plus-track slot a ceiling installer demonstrated in a manufacturer's showroom** (recorded on [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/analysis/Ceiling_Type_Comparison_and_Cost|Ceiling Type Comparison and Cost]], with aerodynamic and acoustic testing claimed). **Two sources, two trades, same product — and the designer's version confirms it is being specified in practice, not just shown.**
- **For classical interiors: perforation in the gypsum cornice instead of a grille** — «когда любая современная деталь испортит всю стилистику».

### ⚠️ Noise as a diagnostic, and a privacy failure this page did not carry

- **A flat rule: «если вы слышите шум вентиляции, это значит только одно — что вентиляция спроектирована неправильно».** Two mechanisms named: **air volume forced into a smaller diameter than the design requires**, and **excess bends.**
- **⚠️ Cross-talk between rooms is a privacy failure, not a comfort one: one duct snaking into several rooms means the occupants hear each other** — «помните в детстве баночки с верёвочкой». **Real case: a neighbour complained he could hear the flat's owner talking, through the kitchen extract.** *(Complements this page's existing separate-zone-exhaust content, which solves the same problem for two rooms sharing one building channel.)*
- **⚠️ Annual servicing is required and carries a design cost: access hatches in the stretch ceiling or plasterboard, and «это почти всегда портит дизайн».** Where aesthetics do not matter — a boiler room or laundry — **use a demountable ceiling instead: Armstrong or грильято.**

### Process rules — when to start, and who is accountable for what

- **⚠️ «Ни в коем случае не надо ждать, когда окончится ваш дизайн-проект. Как только закончен первый этап и у вас есть утверждённая планировка — сразу же передавайте её проектировщикам вентиляции.»**
- **⚠️ «Типового решения не существует, и каждый раз под каждый интерьер нужно разрабатывать свою систему.»**
- **The ventilation designer must be met ON SITE, and must also talk to the управляющая компания to obtain the building's engineering parameters.** Otherwise **«можно выйти за рамки возможностей или даже закона»**, and an expensive system will work incorrectly or, worst case, **be dismantled.**
- **⚠️ And the reason the designer must still check every intersection: «для проектировщика вентиляции самое главное, чтобы всё работало правильно, и он чаще всего не обращает внимания на всё, что связано с дизайном».** Ventilation drives ceiling level changes and collides with lamps, diffusers and service hatches — «может в самый последний момент всё испортить». **So the designer curates, the specialist designs.** *(The third independent statement of the designer-as-generalist division of labour across this channel — see [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Lighting_Fixtures_and_Fittings|Lighting Fixtures and Fittings]].)*

### ⚠️ Ventilation contractors — the failure modes, and one is a two-sided account

**«Очень часто взаимодействие с вентиляционщиками похоже на военные действия.»**

- **⚠️ The root cause: «в погоне за самыми бюджетными решениями заказчики часто находят специалистов, которые работали только с промышленными помещениями».** They promise it will be fine — «100 раз сделали эту вентиляцию» — **but do not account for a private client's raised requirements and do not communicate with the designers or builders.**
  **This is the same failure class a contractor described from the opposite side on the same channel, having watched such a crew cutting a ventilation duct on the kitchen island at final-finish stage** (see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/analysis/Contract_Practice|Contract Practice]]). **Two roles, two accounts, one pattern: competence in industrial work is not competence in an occupied, finished space.**
- **On a private project, especially a large one, there is a strict order of operations — «не получится один раз приехать, сделать работу и уехать».**
- **⚠️ Failure mode 1: the crew installs its equipment and leaves. Once finishing is done the diffusers must be fitted and commissioning performed — and the crew has vanished. New ones must be found: «это время, деньги, нервы».**
- **⚠️ Failure mode 2, the most concrete and the most expensive: they installed the air conditioning and forgot to connect the indoor unit's condensate drain into the sewer, then left. By the time they returned everything was closed up and tiled, and the drain is «где-то там, никто не знает где» — so everything must be broken open and redone.** *(This vault has a dedicated [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/AC_Condensate_Drainage|AC Condensate Drainage]] page; this is the project-management version of the same risk — the drain being forgotten rather than wrongly detailed.)*
- **Find them by recommendation from builders, designers, or friends with positive experience.**

### ⚠️⚠️⚠️ THIS PROJECT HAS A 2500 mm CEILING — the void arithmetic on this page, applied (2026-09-04)

**Established from the developer's building description (`data/canonical/building_spec.json`): ZK Dubravinskiy has
«высота потолков в квартирах 2,5 метра» — 3 m only on the 21st floor. This flat is on the 4th. The
figure had not been recorded anywhere in this repo before that date.**

**Run this page's own numbers against it:**

| | |
| :--- | ---: |
| slab-to-slab | **2500 mm** |
| less ventilation duct void, this page's general figure of **150–200 mm** | **→ 2350–2300 mm** |
| ⚠️ and this page states the void requirement **is not uniform** — duct section grows with run length and is worst **beside the plant** | worse than that locally |
| ⚠️ **no recovery from the floor:** the screed is already laid with the heating pipes distributed horizontally in it | **0 mm available** |
| ✅ **datum settled 2026-09-04:** the 2500 is **screed to ceiling**, so it is the CLEAR height — not slab to slab (~2550) | *the 2500 above is usable, not gross* |
| ⚠️ less the **floor covering** on top of the screed (tile+adhesive ≈18 mm, board+underlay ≈17) | **→ 2282–2332 mm finished under a duct** |

- → **⚠️⚠️ AND THIS PAGE ALREADY NAMES THE FAILURE THAT FOLLOWS. Its sequencing trap: ducts are installed FIRST,
  and «очень часто этого пространства не хватает для установки лампочек или треков» — there is often not
  enough left below the duct for the lighting. At a 2500 mm start that is not a risk, it is the expected outcome
  of a full-flat ducted route.**
- → **⚠️ The page's duct-intake convention is quoted for a 3 m ceiling (~270 cm). It does not transfer. Nothing
  height-related on this page may be applied to this project without re-deriving it from 2500.**
- → **✅ THE LOCALISED TECHNIQUES ON THIS PAGE STOP BEING OPTIMISATIONS AND BECOME THE ONLY VIABLE ROUTE**, and this
  page already holds both: **route the supply duct above the kitchen cabinets and box it behind a floor-to-ceiling
  kitchen facade rather than dropping the whole room — worth ≈12–15 cm of ceiling height** — and **flat-section
  ducts specifically to minimise the drop**, accepting the noise penalty this page records against them.
- → **✅ It also promotes a finding the vault held as merely the cheap option: a BARE LACQUERED CONCRETE ceiling
  costs ZERO height.** At 2500 mm that stops being a budget choice and becomes a height strategy. Its enabling
  condition is already recorded — keep the wiring OFF the ceiling, which means wall outlets and a track — and
  the same source's argument for **track lighting at a low ceiling** (a low ceiling cannot take pendants) now
  applies to this project by measurement rather than by taste.
- ⚠️ **And it puts the vault's own comfort rule in conflict with the flat: `ceiling.min_height_2800mm` states
  2.8 m as the minimum comfortable height. This flat is 300 mm under it.** Recorded as a constraint to design
  inside, not a contradiction to resolve — but it means any advice premised on a normal void must be re-checked
  before use, not adopted.
- ⚠️ **Open, and it should be settled before a ventilation system is chosen: whether a ducted MVHR is viable
  here at all, or whether this flat is a case for decentralised through-wall units** — which consume no ceiling
  void. This page carries both options; the height figure now weighs the choice.

#### ⚠️⚠️⚠️ AND THE FLAT IS SINGLE-ASPECT, WHICH REMOVES THE FALLBACK — the full chain (2026-09-04)

**From the block floor plan: this is the block's END flat, and three of its four sides are PARTY walls.
Only the bottom is external. All glazing is on that one façade. Кухня 5.24 and прихожая 9.97 have NO
external wall at all.** (`data/canonical/building_spec.json`.)

**Follow it through, because each step removes an option:**

1. **Single-aspect → NO CROSS-VENTILATION.** Opening windows on opposite sides is not available at any time of
   year. **So mechanical fresh air stops being an upgrade and becomes a requirement.**
2. **2500 mm ceiling → a ducted route through the flat is barely viable**, per the arithmetic above: 150–200 mm
   of duct leaves 2300–2350 mm before any fitting, and no height can be recovered from the floor.
3. **→ So the decentralised through-wall units this page also documents become the leading option, because
   they consume ZERO ceiling void.**
4. **⚠️ BUT A THROUGH-WALL UNIT NEEDS AN EXTERNAL WALL, AND ONLY THREE ROOMS HAVE ONE** — ~~9.36~~, 16.63 and
   19.49. **Neither the кухня nor the прихожая can take one in the developer's layout.**

   > ⚠️⚠️ **CORRECTED 2026-09-19 — THE 9.36 ROOM DOES NOT QUALIFY, AND IT IS THE WORST CASE TO GET WRONG.**
   > Antigravity raised it; reproduced from `boundary_face_inventory.csv`, which classifies **exactly one**
   > face of that room's walls as `envelope`: **`MA.cross_lo`, *"facade wall, insulated outward face"*.**
   > R6, G4a, G4d, G8 and R4 are all unclassified — none is established as external.
   >
   > **And MA's outward face gives onto the ЛОДЖИЯ, not the street.** A through-wall breather mounted on MA
   > would draw its supply from an **enclosed, unheated buffer** — one the owner intends to seal with
   > replacement triple glazing to hold it frost-free without heating, and which also carries laundry
   > humidity and storage. That is not fresh air; it is re-breathing the buffer.
   >
   > ⚠️ **So the room with the single worst acoustic adjacency in the flat** (`Soundproofing.md`: the
   > neighbour's habitable 14.64 faces it) **is also the one with no direct outdoor air path**, and both
   > phases put someone sleeping in it — adults in SC-NOW, a child in Phase 2.
   >
   > **The routes that remain, none of them costed or chosen:** carry an intake across the loggia and
   > penetrate the façade glazing `O9` or the enclosure `M2`; duct a supply from elsewhere through a 2500 mm
   > ceiling; or accept that the loggia glazing must be openable in winter, which contradicts the
   > frost-free-without-heating target. **This is a genuine design constraint on the 9.36 room and it has
   > never been confronted.** Recorded as an open item in `00_Master/project_decisions.md`.
5. **Extract is fine** — the second вентблок (400 × 1140, measured) sits exactly at the прихожая/кухня
   boundary, so the kitchen's extract has a shaft to reach. **It is SUPPLY to the kitchen zone that has no route.**

→ **⚠️⚠️ THE CONCLUSION IS A DESIGN ONE, NOT A PLANT ONE: in the developer's layout the kitchen cannot be
given fresh air without ducting it through a 2500 mm ceiling. MERGING THE KITCHEN INTO THE LIVING ROOM —
which the owner's own `v1` already does — gives that zone a façade wall, and therefore both daylight and a
supply route, without any duct run at all.** That is an argument for the move which is entirely independent
of the owner's own reason for making it. Recorded at `00_Master/Layout_Option_Review.md` §3a.

→ **✅ It also decides where soundproofing money goes.** The right-hand wall is the **block end** — a decoupled
double wall (≈200–250 + seam + 200 ≈ 400 mm) acquired for nothing. **The walls worth spending on are the
in-block party wall and the corridor wall**, not that one.
