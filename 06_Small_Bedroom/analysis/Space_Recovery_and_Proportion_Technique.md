# Small Bedroom — Space Recovery, Layout, and Proportion Technique

Techniques for reclaiming usable floor area and correcting a small or
awkwardly-shaped bedroom's visual proportions, drawn from 6 real
subscriber-makeover cases (Anuta Vlady) ranging from 14m² rectangular
rooms to a pentagonal 5-sided room, plus one long/narrow living-room
case whose proportion-correction technique generalizes directly to a
narrow bedroom.

## Space Recovery by Relocation

- **Closet-removal-and-relocation technique**: removing a small, awkward
  built-in closet (narrow door, wide interior, hard to access) can free
  a niche elsewhere in the same wall run — a full wardrobe can then move
  into that niche, and the space the wardrobe used to occupy becomes
  available for a worktable or other furniture. Anuta Vlady, `ohOpaJczckE`
  (14m² England case) — the specific niche/clearance arithmetic in this
  source doesn't fully reconcile on a plain transcript read
  (`ASR-uncertain` at the line-item level), but the relocation technique
  itself is sound.
- **⚠️ Real consequence of not planning furniture vs. door-swing
  clearance**: a wardrobe relocated into a bedroom (to escape a
  fridge-adjacency heat problem) ended up blocking the room's own door
  from opening a full 90° — a concrete, checkable hazard of placing
  furniture without checking the door's swing arc first. Anuta Vlady,
  `uwGxjv9O1kk` (43m² khrushchevka, Moscow Oblast).
- **⚠️ Furniture-heat-adjacency hazard**: a wardrobe (originally bought
  for a different room) had its side panels overheat from standing next
  to a refrigerator — a checkable placement hazard for laminate/mirrored
  furniture panels near a heat-radiating appliance side. Anuta Vlady,
  `uwGxjv9O1kk`.

## Irregular-Geometry Rooms

- **⚠️ Flooring-direction distraction technique for an irregular room**:
  in a genuinely irregular room (here, five-sided/"pentagonal"), lay new
  flooring in the standard direction relative to the window — not
  following the room's odd wall angle — and position the main furniture
  (bed, wardrobe) as if the room were regular-shaped. This deliberately
  draws visual attention to the room's "regular" portion and away from
  the extra angled corners, rather than trying to visually resolve the
  irregular geometry directly. Anuta Vlady, `ZlkBfwlhYFU` (St. Petersburg,
  pentagonal bedroom) — complements this store's existing trapezoidal-
  bedroom case (`RF6FdHk_jgE`, on
  [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/analysis/Bedroom_Design_Principles|Bedroom Design Principles]])
  with a genuinely different technique (distract via direction, rather
  than reshape).
- **Floor-thickness-matching rule for a partial flooring replacement**:
  before buying new flooring for a room-only (not whole-apartment)
  replacement, lift a small section of the existing floor elsewhere
  (e.g. the hallway) to check its thickness, and match the new
  material's thickness to it — avoids a height differential/trip hazard
  at the room-to-hallway transition. Anuta Vlady, `ZlkBfwlhYFU`.

## Long/Narrow Room Proportion Correction

- **⚠️ Accent-wall-on-the-longest-wall mistake, general rule**: putting
  an accent wallpaper/finish on the *longest* wall of an already long/
  narrow room emphasizes the room's poor proportions further. Accent
  only a **segment/portion** of that long wall instead — this both
  creates zoning and helps correct proportion at the same time. Anuta
  Vlady, `SUN8UiXAjf0` (St. Petersburg long/narrow living room; states
  the general principle explicitly).
- **Accent-wall composition rule — avoid a centered material/color
  split**: when an accent wall combines two materials or colors, an
  asymmetric split (roughly 1/3-2/3, offset to one side) reads as more
  dynamic/intentional than splitting exactly at the bed's center line.
  Anuta Vlady, `DvQTBiItLpY` (long/narrow bedroom, region unstated) —
  the worked-example version of the general rule above.
- **Photo-row proportion rule for a long wall**: a horizontal row of
  framed photos along a long wall visually emphasizes poor elongated
  proportions; a vertical block concentrated behind the headboard
  instead "cuts" the long wall and makes the ceiling read taller. Sizing
  the block to the headboard's own width (using more, smaller photos if
  needed) improves the composition further. Anuta Vlady, `DvQTBiItLpY`.
- **Match a reference accent-wall's material/color family to the room's
  existing furniture finish before adopting it** — a wood-toned panel
  treatment was rejected for a room with white laminate (ЛДСП) furniture
  even though it looked good in the reference photo; the same treatment
  would suit furniture in a compatible tone instead. Anuta Vlady,
  `DvQTBiItLpY`.

## Ceiling-Height Perception — Four Independent Mechanisms

This batch recorded four genuinely distinct techniques for the same
low-ceiling-perception goal, each usable independently or in
combination:

1. **Vertical stripes or self-adhesive moulding** on a feature wall.
   Corroborated across a small Russian khrushchevka bedroom
   (`uwGxjv9O1kk`) and a spacious French living room
   (`fkoUNvB9LV8`) — same channel, but the cross-context repetition
   suggests the underlying principle is size/room-type-general, not
   specific to either case.
2. **Large/tall art positioned above the bed or a focal wall**, sized to
   the headboard's own width. Anuta Vlady, `uwGxjv9O1kk`.
3. **⚠️ Furniture-height-contrast**: removing a wall unit's horizontal
   open shelving, leaving a stark height difference between tall
   wardrobe sections and a separate low console, visually raises the
   ceiling — a mechanism driven by *contrast between adjacent pieces*
   rather than a single element's own verticality. Anuta Vlady,
   `SUN8UiXAjf0`.
4. **Curtain/blackout-blind mounting height**: mount as close to the
   ceiling as possible, especially in a low-ceiling apartment — improves
   the room's height proportions. Also: **two narrower two-tone curtain
   panels** (rather than one wide panel) at a wide window pier add extra
   vertical seam lines for further height-stretching, beyond a single
   wide panel. Anuta Vlady, `uwGxjv9O1kk` / `SUN8UiXAjf0`.

## Root-Cause-Over-Literal-Request Methodology

- **⚠️ Override-the-literal-request methodology, worked example**: a
  client asked for a *larger* rug to cover an entire long/narrow room;
  the source explicitly declined, reasoning a full-room rug would
  emphasize the poor proportions further, and instead repositioned the
  existing, already well-matched rug to sit squarely in front of one
  zone. A concrete instance of diagnosing the underlying proportion
  problem behind a client's literal request rather than executing it as
  stated — worth applying to this project's own furnishing decisions,
  not just accepting a "bigger is better" instinct at face value. Anuta
  Vlady, `SUN8UiXAjf0`.
- **Furniture-relocation-over-replacement default**: most fixes in the
  same case were simply repositioning existing furniture to better spots
  in the same room, rather than buying replacements — an explicit "why
  replace something that isn't actually the problem" philosophy that
  kept the project's total spend low. Anuta Vlady, `SUN8UiXAjf0`.
