# Small Bedroom — General Design, Space-Recovery, and Proportion-Correction Guide

General, sourced technique/reasoning for making a small or awkwardly-
shaped bedroom actually work — recovering usable floor area, correcting
poor proportions (long/narrow, low-ceiling, or irregular geometry), and
sizing furniture for tight clearances — gathered from 7 real subscriber-
makeover cases, all one channel (Anuta Vlady) so far. Each section states
the leading recommendation(s) and *why*, so you can form a view without
clicking through; the linked `analysis/` pages carry the full
multi-source breakdown, every number, and every citation.

## Space Recovery and Proportion Correction

The core recurring problem across this batch: a small bedroom rarely
fails from lack of *technique* — it fails from a poor-fitting layout
(furniture blocking a door's swing, an under-used niche, an accent
treatment that emphasizes rather than corrects bad proportions). Real
fixes recorded here include relocating a wardrobe into a freed niche,
laying flooring in a direction that distracts from an irregular room's
extra corners, and — the single most repeated theme — **four
independent mechanisms for making a low ceiling read taller**: vertical
stripes/moulding, oversized art above the bed, deliberate height
*contrast* between adjacent furniture pieces, and ceiling-height curtain
mounting. A genuinely long/narrow room's biggest single risk is an
accent-wall treatment applied to its *longest* wall, which only
emphasizes the bad proportion further — accent a segment instead.

## Furniture and Storage for Tight Clearances

Standard nightstands often don't fit a genuinely tight bedside gap —
target 30-35cm width and ~70cm height to match a tall mattress, and
consider a wall-mounted swiveling tray as a stock-furniture alternative
when nothing fits both constraints. A 40cm-deep wardrobe only works for
shelves, not hanging clothes (needs ~60cm); a mirrored sliding-door
wardrobe can make a narrow room look narrower still, while a solid-door
equivalent with slim, concealed hardware reads modern and doesn't
amplify the room's proportions. This section also carries this vault's
first two Small Bedroom/Study sources (a dual-purpose bedroom/office
case and a genuinely tiny 2.30m-ceiling room), migrated here from the
intermediate store now that this page exists.

## Decor, Color, and Lighting Technique

Includes an open, unresolved disagreement worth flagging up front: one
source calls saturated/dark accent color "the ideal solution" for a
small bedroom specifically, while an existing source on
`Bedroom_Design_Principles.md` argues the opposite for sleep-physiology
reasons — see the Perspectives block below for both positions; this
project hasn't yet decided which to prioritize. Beyond that: a
decorative mirror belongs on the wall *behind* the headboard (not facing
the bed), pillow-sizing has real numeric floors (80cm minimum for a
long pillow, 50×50cm for a square throw pillow), and several no-
renovation lighting workarounds recur (illuminated mirrors, baseboard-
routed sconce wiring, cordless rechargeable lamps).
