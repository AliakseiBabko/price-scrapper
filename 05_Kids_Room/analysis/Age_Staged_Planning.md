# Kids Room — Age-Staged Planning

Part of [[05_Kids_Room/Kids_Room_Guide|Kids Room Guide]].

## The "Design for ~10 Years" Infrastructure Rule

ARCHWOOD/Marina Izmailova (practicing architect, mother of two mixed-sex
children) and RemPlanner/Yana Katyrina (designer) independently converge
on the same core principle, from two unrelated channels: **plan a kids'
room's underlying infrastructure — electrical (outlets/switches), wall
finish, and built-in cabinetry — to last roughly 10 years without being
redone**, even though furniture and decor will change several times
across that span. Concretely:

- **Sketch 2-3 furniture-layout plans at ~5-year intervals** (toddler,
  school-age, teen-heading-to-adult-bedroom) before finalizing outlet and
  switch placement — cover any outlet not yet needed with a blank plate
  rather than omitting it. Plan a laptop-charger/desk-lamp outlet at
  **90cm above the floor** ahead of an adult-height desk actually
  arriving, and a second outlet at "second level" height if a bunk/loft
  bed is anticipated. Where one outlet placement can serve two different
  future layouts, that's the ideal case. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]
- **Lighting should include distinct general and zone-specific
  scenarios from the start** (a night-light for sleep, decorative/accent
  lighting for play) rather than one uniform room light. [source: [[_Sources/YT_OfqkRAZUfe0_remplanner_kids_room_grow_into_15_years|YT_OfqkRAZUfe0]]]
- **Wall finish should default to a neutral, repaint-ready base**
  (white/gray/beige) using a washable/anti-vandal kids' paint, so a
  future age-stage change is a cosmetic repaint, not a strip-and-redo.
  Ordinary wallpaper is flagged by both sources as unlikely to survive a
  young child's room. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]], [source: [[_Sources/YT_OfqkRAZUfe0_remplanner_kids_room_grow_into_15_years|YT_OfqkRAZUfe0]]]
- **A floor-to-ceiling chalkboard/magnetic-paint drawing strip in a
  neutral shape** gives a sanctioned drawing surface that works
  unmodified from toddler years (drawing) through school age (pinning
  schedules/posters) — its durability comes specifically from the
  neutral shape, not from being redecorated per age. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]
- **Wardrobe interiors should be neutral (no childish motifs) with
  adult-height rods/drawers from the start**, reconfigurable in shelf/
  rod height and count as the child grows, rather than rebuilt per
  stage. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]], [source: [[_Sources/YT_OfqkRAZUfe0_remplanner_kids_room_grow_into_15_years|YT_OfqkRAZUfe0]]]

A separate, earlier ARCHWOOD real-client source (a 90m² 3-room family
apartment, `gNN7G7vNyqY`) independently touches the same "growing room"
logic at the brief-gathering stage, one step before the room design
itself: **explicitly brief a school-age transition ~1-2 years out** — a
5-year-old approaching school needs the design to anticipate a full desk
with laptop/printer capability replacing the current toddler table/chair
set, not just accommodate her current furniture. A thinner, earlier-stage
version of the same age-staged-planning principle above, not a
contradiction. [source: [[_Sources/YT_gNN7G7vNyqY_archwood_90m2_family_layout|YT_gNN7G7vNyqY]]]

## Three-Tier Design Longevity Framework

ARCHWOOD frames the practical consequence of the rule above as three
distinct planning tiers, each with its own realistic lifespan: **(1)
renovation infrastructure — plan for ~10 years; (2) themed furniture
(a themed bed, decor-heavy pieces) — plan for ~5 years**, since a
teenager may refuse to keep sleeping in a childishly-themed bed; **(3)
decor (stickers, pillows, flags, rugs) — plan for ~1-2 years**, the
cheapest, fastest layer to refresh as interests change. The practical
implication: build a neutral base at tiers 1-2 and let tier-3 decor carry
the actual theming — a themed "spaceship" or "submarine" room is
achievable via wall stickers on a neutral base, without buying themed
furniture at all. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]

RemPlanner independently arrives at the same conclusion from the
opposite direction — **"if you don't want to redo a kids' room's finish
3-4 times over childhood, keep the base neutral and carry all theming
through swappable furniture/decor"** — and Kodolov's channel corroborates
it a third way, noting that parental motivation to keep re-buying/
rebuilding a child's furniture "for every centimeter of growth" reliably
fades, which is exactly what transformer/adjustable furniture and a
neutral infrastructure base are meant to route around. [source: [[_Sources/YT_OfqkRAZUfe0_remplanner_kids_room_grow_into_15_years|YT_OfqkRAZUfe0]]], [source: [[_Sources/YT_Cr1vew4sA9g_kodolov_combine_kids_room_boy_girl|YT_Cr1vew4sA9g]]]

## A Real 14-Year-Old's Room, Redesigned to Grow Into Early Adulthood

A real case (Anuta Vlady, `ZlkBfwlhYFU` Case 2 — an 18m² room, P-44
panel building, region unresolved at level 1) redesigning a 14-year-old
boy's room specifically "на вырост" (to grow with him into early
adulthood), rather than as a typical "teen room." Not this project's own
current content — this household's own Phase 2 plan turns
`06_Small_Bedroom` into "Kids Room #2" for the boy once the adults
vacate it (see [[06_Small_Bedroom/Small_Bedroom_Index|Small Bedroom
Index]]), so this case is a **forward-looking precedent**, not a
current-occupant match. Recorded here rather than on
[[06_Small_Bedroom/Small_Bedroom_Guide|Small Bedroom Guide]] since the
occupant/room identity don't match this project's plan yet.

- **Combined wardrobe + open-backed lit textbook niches, sized to match
  the room's existing desk width (160cm)**: open-backed, internally-lit
  upper niches let daylight penetrate further into a room whose natural
  light doesn't reach its full depth — a light-conducting shelf design,
  not just storage.
- **Structured/quilted bedspread recommended for a teen who won't
  carefully smooth wrinkles daily** — a stiff, shape-holding bedspread
  that still looks tidy when just thrown over the bed.
- **Wall-hung nightstand paired with a drawer-based bed** — keeps the
  bed's own storage drawers clear to open fully.
- **⚠️ Deliberate undecorated "blank zone" for an older child/teen
  occupant's own self-expression**: rather than fully decorating per the
  parent's brief, a neutral central band was left undecorated so the
  room's actual (soon-adult) occupant can personalize it later — extends
  this store's existing "preserve one occupant accent" principle
  (`TJBcEpXymYk`, on
  [[06_Small_Bedroom/analysis/Furniture_and_Storage_for_Tight_Clearances|Furniture and Storage for Tight Clearances]])
  from "keep one object" to "reserve a deliberate blank space."
- **Foam-block partition wall, existing/retained**, dividing the room
  into two occupancy zones — corroborates this project's existing
  foam-block-vs-drywall partition comparison (`1_nKzwNZD6w`, on
  [[00_Master/Bedroom_Design_Principles|Bedroom Design Principles]])
  from an independent household — genuine cross-channel corroboration.

## A Real Multi-Year, Multi-Child Room-Evolution Case

ARCHWOOD's own multi-year client case (3 children — an older girl and
two closer-age boys, only 2 rooms available) is a real precedent for a
staged room-splitting transition: while all three were young, one room
was a shared bedroom (beds + storage only) and the second a shared
playroom (no beds); **~6-7 years later**, the playroom converted into
the older girl's own private room as she entered adolescence, and the
household re-split the remaining shared space again into two small
individual bedrooms plus a shared "mini living room" common zone (play
area, small sofa, shared sports corner) — avoiding duplicating
shared-use items across the individual rooms. Directly relevant
precedent (not a template) for this project's own Phase 1→Phase 2
transition — see [[05_Kids_Room/Kids_Room_Index|Kids Room Index]]'s own
household plan. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]

## Safety, Zoning and Layout for a Kids Room (Надежда Кузина, added 2026-08-31)

From a 78-minute planning lecture. She frames a kids' room as "мир в мире, квартира в квартире" — every function a child needs, concentrated into a limited space. Her six requirements: **ecological, safe, functional across many activities, easy to clean, anti-vandal, and easily transformable as the child grows.** The general planning method is on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Whole_Home_Planning_Method|Whole-Home Planning Method]]. [source: [[_Sources/YT_Rcd9gkPC6CI_kuzina_apartment_ergonomics_bedroom_kids_hallway|YT_Rcd9gkPC6CI]]]

### Safety — a checklist, mostly specifiable at rough stage

- **Ideally no balcony off the room**, or a fully blockable exit onto it. She has had both cases in practice.
- **Windows: tilt-opening («откидные»), not casement — and preferably key-lockable.**
- **⚠️ No Roman blinds, and nothing with a cord** of any kind — the child will pull it, "и это не всегда заканчивается хорошо."
- **Doors with limiters and slow closing** so fingers are not trapped.
- **Sockets with child shutters («шторки»).**
- **Furniture must not be able to tip onto the child — fix it to the wall** wherever there is any risk. The child must not be able to fall out of bed.
- **Fewer hard surfaces**, rugs, corner protectors — scoped explicitly to small children.
- **⚠️ Lay the electrics for the *second* layout now — the highest-leverage item in the source.** With a one-year-old, design the age-7-or-10 layout at the same time and install those sockets during the renovation. **The later change then becomes cosmetic only — re-paper, rearrange, buy new furniture — with no rewiring.**

### Zoning — four activities, all scaled to the child

- **Four zones for four activities**, and ideally **screen the sleep zone so the child cannot see the toys from the bed.**
- **The largest and lightest room is often given to the children**, treated as more necessary than the living room.
- **Play stations, each scaled to the child**: a small play kitchen, a shelf unit with books, a small drawing table — every activity with its own delimited zone and its own storage. **The mechanism she names is the child's own agency** — being able to decide "мы сегодня будем готовить искусственную картошку или мы будем переодеваться в принцессу."
- **Favour problem-solving and creative toys (Lego) — and plan where they are stored.**
- **The child touches the interior, not only looks at it** — tactile variety is developmentally useful, and a reason to vary materials rather than specify one smooth finish.
- **⚠️ Shelves scaled to the child: the smaller the child, the lower the most interesting shelf must be.** Open shelves, so a small child can reach without help.
- **Storage by category** — clothes, school books and toys held separately.
- **Do not throw away or hide the child's drawings.** She cites a recent Cosmoscow exhibition where half the show was adult artists deliberately painting like small children. It need not hang in the kids' room to be worth keeping.

### Beds and layout

- **⚠️ Girls need larger beds than boys** — girls use the bed as a play surface and boys generally do not.
- **Provide space for an adult to sit beside the bed.**
- **⚠️ Do not buy themed beds — a Cinderella carriage, a car — for two reasons.** They **constrain imagination**: a plain bed can be a carriage, a house, a ship at sea, whatever the child decides; a car can only ever be a car. And **the child tires of it within days while it is an expensive object.** She reports clients doing it anyway.
- **A guest zone inside the room** is a feature she rates: visiting children can shut themselves in and play unobserved, with somewhere to sit and plenty of free floor left.
- **Common failure: a sofa competing with a wardrobe**, leaving a dead zone behind sliding doors.
- **A bed set head-to-wall rather than along a wall is a poor option unless there is a separate playroom** — it consumes floor area and the child must constantly walk around it.
- **⚠️ Better: the bed in a corner — and her stated logic is light-based. The darkest corner goes to the bed; the lightest part of the room goes to study.**
- **Custom joinery is what makes a small kids' room work.** She highlights shelves worked from **both** sides, serving the study zone and the bed zone at once.
- **Two children without bunk beds** reduces the room to a bedroom with a workspace, and nothing else will fit.
- **⚠️ Podium beds — a plan she abandoned, and the reasons are the value.** Raise a podium 30–40 cm with beds rolling out beneath. She dropped it: **very hard to execute, few joinery shops can make a good pull-out-from-podium bed, and it depends on windowsill height** — the child must not be able to reach the window from the raised level. Podium sizing detail is on [[00_Master/Bedroom_Design_Principles|Bedroom Design Principles]].
- **⚠️ Bunk beds — the lighting must be worked out deliberately, and she tested the failure herself.** The sconce must be reachable from the bed, **and there must be a two-way switch by the door.** Without it, a child who falls asleep with the sconce on in the top bunk means a parent climbing the ladder past a sleeping child — **"я кстати пробовала, я реально сама просто на этой лестнице не помещаюсь."**

### Workspace for an older child

- **Often placed at the window, and she notes the side effect: in an elongated room it makes the room read shorter.**
- **⚠️ Her favourite detail: custom shelves left and right of the desk, turned to face the worktop rather than frontally**, so things can be stacked against them; combine orientations across the run. She has tested shelves wrapped around a window.
- **Plan for the equipment.** A client's solution she rates highly: **a printer hidden inside a desk drawer with its own socket inside and no visible cable hole** — pull the drawer out and take the sheet straight from the printer.

## ⚠️ Specify the Unchanging Base for the Oldest Future Occupant (Татьяна Безверхая, added 2026-09-02)

Moscow designer, uploaded 2026-08-08, described as an authored methodology developed over 11 years. `promotional_ratio: medium` (no sponsor; two own-studio insertions). **This page already holds age-staged planning including a 15-year-forward-planning source; her contribution is the inversion of the method.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_u8-y7QPc48I_bezverkhaia_kids_room_growing_base|u8-y7QPc48I]]]

**⚠️ The inversion: instead of planning changes stage by stage, specify the fixed infrastructure once for the *final* stage and let everything cheap change around it.**

- **Her premise, worth keeping as the comparison it is**: adults typically live in a bought apartment **at least 10 years** with an essentially unchanged interior — same bed in the same place, same wardrobe. **A kids' room in the same 10 years goes through three or four rounds of change.**
- **⚠️ Therefore build a base that does not change, and specify that base for a senior schoolchild or student even if the child is currently an infant** — because **from roughly 10–12 children are big enough for near-adult desks and study becomes their main occupation in the room.**
- **⚠️ "Base" means infrastructure, not styling — she says so explicitly** ("я говорю не про дизайн, не про рюшечки"). It is:
  - **Lighting positions**: general ambient; **a nightlight, treated as mandatory**; **and a separate light over the future work zone.**
  - **⚠️ Sockets placed where the desk will eventually be, though no desk exists yet** — plus sockets where a laptop and phone will later charge, **and more of them than seem necessary.** **Childproof sockets exist that are safe even against a child pushing two fingers in, which is what makes early installation viable.**
  - **Neutral base finishes**: light grey walls, a neutral-toned board floor (engineered board, or laminate "хоть на худой конец"), white doors and baseboards. **Explicitly not wallpaper with cars, flowers or fairies — that is the cheapest thing to add later via decor.**
  - **⚠️ A planned place for a large built-in wardrobe or dressing room from the outset, with the internal fit-out treated as changeable and the location as fixed.** Rails and shelves move up as the child grows; **"место для шкафа должно быть всегда."**
- **⚠️ The cost logic that justifies all of it: "переклеивать обои и перештрабливать электрику — это гемор."** Pendants, textiles, bedspreads, beds of different sizes, desks and chairs can be swapped twenty times cheaply; **finishes and buried electrics cannot. The base must therefore also be good quality from the start.**
- **Ventilation: fit a breather (бризер) where the apartment has no ducted system**, so the child has fresh air without an open window and the associated worry about them catching cold. **Corroborates the breather recommendation from her balcony source** on [[10_Balcony/analysis/Materials_Finishes_and_Layout|Balcony: Materials, Finishes & Layout]].

### The four stages, and what actually changes at each

- **0 to ~3 — ⚠️ the room is oriented to the parent, not the child** ("ребёнок ещё скорее кабачок и у него нет собственной идентичности"). **A place for the parent to settle the child: an armchair and a light, set up for the mother. And, flagged by her as a controversial recommendation, a sofa bed if it fits — because a parent will end up sleeping there when the child is ill.** Standard infant furniture is fine; **no sharp corners, nothing to strike against.** **⚠️ Do not invest heavily — at 3 all of it stops fitting.** Eco/hypoallergenic natural materials. **⚠️ Colour can be played with freely here, but through textile, decor and small items — not by painting the walls.**
- **3 to 7 — the base purpose is play.** Shelving with boxes and drawers, to train tidiness. **⚠️ A soft rug is required because everything happens on the floor.** Move off the cot: either a height-adjusting bed or straight to a near-adult single/1.5 bed, **with a good mattress specifically.** Second levels and climbing structures added gradually.
- **7 to 12–13 — the play zone contracts in favour of the work zone, and this is where the pre-planned base pays off**: "вот где нужны электрика, розетки на своих местах, светильники. Тут уже всё пригождается." Growing and orthopaedic furniture with correct backrests; **fully adult storage systems now.** **⚠️ Individualisation appears as a need: give them a wall they can hang things on, draw on and arrange their own drawings, toys and models on.** **⚠️ The containment lifehack is the practical half: localise all of it inside their room so none of it appears elsewhere in the apartment.**
- **13+ — privacy is the main thing.** **⚠️ Her position: a door that locks from the inside** (her own view, "мне кажется", not a professional standard — may conflict with household safety preferences). Complete reversal of priorities — a fifteen-year-old will find butterfly wallpaper insulting. **⚠️ A neutral base is what makes this survivable: black bedding, black curtains and a gloomy pendant simply get added onto it.** Computer central; soundproofing a plus. **Her honest note: from an interior standpoint a schoolchild's and a teenager's room differ little — what changes is that parents must accept teenagers keep their space chaotic.**
- **University age** — a full adult bedroom with a work place, receiving friends and partners.

**⚠️ Gap worth stating: no dimensions anywhere** — no desk depth, no wardrobe width, no socket heights — despite the source being about pre-planning exactly those positions. **And no pricing**, so "do not invest heavily at 0–3" carries no figure.

## ⚠️⚠️ Desk Dimensions and Two-Child Desk Layout — a Clinical Psychologist Fills the Dimension Gap Flagged Above

Наталья Степанова, clinical psychologist (20 years, child and adult
psychotherapy), interviewed by Александр Сенчугов. **This closes the
"no desk depth anywhere" gap stated at the end of the section above, and
it does so with a failure case rather than a recommended figure —
which is more useful, because it comes with the test.**

**⚠️ A 40 cm deep desk cannot serve a first-year pupil.** Very involved,
wealthy parents proudly showed her the desk bought for their 6-year-old
starting school — a genuinely beautiful natural-wood piece. **«Но я
понимаю, что заниматься он за ним не сможет. Знаете почему? Потому что
ШИРИНА СТОЛА 40 СМ.»** The desk was 40 × 100 cm.

- **⚠️ The test she applies is an enumeration, and it is directly
  reusable as a specification check:** тетрадь, рабочая тетрадь,
  учебник, планшет, ручки, пенал — **plus a phone, «чтобы иногда
  домашку спрашивать, потому что он её забыл».**
- **⚠️ And the specific geometric failure is the tilted exercise book:**
  «это вот положить ровно тетрадку — и даже, как нас в первом классе
  учили, "поверните тетрадку, чтобы красивым почерком" — **даже вот так
  сделать просто не получится ФИЗИЧЕСКИ**».
- **Her verdict on the piece: «это красивый стол, за который можно сесть
  написать записку или письмецо».**
- **⚠️ The general principle she states, which the interviewing designer
  summarises as function over form and she accepts: «самое основное —
  чтобы ребёнку было удобно заниматься ТЕМ, ДЛЯ ЧЕГО ЭТОТ ПРЕДМЕТ
  ПРЕДНАЗНАЧЕН. Если ему важно в этой кровати СПАТЬ, то она должна быть
  удобного размера, цвета — чтобы в ней было хорошо именно спать,
  засыпать.»**

**⚠️ Growing CHAIRS are preferred over growing DESKS, and the reason is
the footrest** — a growing desk raises the top but leaves the feet
unsupported. Worth setting against the "growing furniture" recommendation
in the 7-to-12 stage above.

**⚠️ The desk surface must be PLAIN (однотонный) and light, ideally
white — and the reason is figure-ground, not decoration.** The desk must
act as visual **background** so the brain concentrates on what is placed
on it; **wood with visible knots and grain is a named failure mode**, and
it matters most for hyperactive children and those with attention
disorders.

### ⚠️⚠️ Two children: two separate tops, never one shared worktop

Asked whether two children can share one long desk or should sit at
opposite ends:

- **⚠️ «Лучше, чтобы это были не обязательно два разных конца комнаты,
  но лучше, чтобы это были ДВА РАЗНЫХ СТОЛА — не одна столешница, а две
  разные столешницы, ОДИНАКОВЫЕ по размеру.» Adjacency is fine. A
  shared surface is not.**
- **⚠️ THE CASE (the client permitted her to share it), and it involved
  an adult:** a mother built one big long desk for herself and her
  teenage daughter. **«Мама сидит с ноутбуком в этом уголочке этого
  стола, а вся остальная столешница занята вещами дочки.»** The mother
  says «я-то на неё раздражаюсь». The psychologist's reply: «знаете, я
  тоже раздражалась бы, если бы у меня не было своего рабочего места —
  хотя я его планировала, выстраивала».
- **⚠️ THE MECHANISM, and it generalises well beyond children: a single
  shared top cannot carry a boundary, so the larger user colonises it
  and the conflict becomes interpersonal instead of spatial.** With two
  tops, «даже если бы они стояли РЯДЫШКОМ, ей бы намного проще было бы
  эту ГРАНИЦУ отстроить: "вот у тебя есть твоё рабочее место, храни там
  свой беспорядок — свой порядок; а у меня мой порядок"».
- **⚠️ The adult parallel she volunteers unprompted: «у меня были
  взрослые заказчики, которые то же самое говорили: нам нужно ровно ДВА
  ОДИНАКОВЫХ ШКАФА, один мой, другой супруги, чтобы вообще ничто нам не
  мешало».**
- **⚠️ Regardless of sex or age: «у каждого должно быть своё спальное
  место, своё рабочее место, у каждого должны быть свои игрушки — это
  важно ДАЖЕ ЕСЛИ дети БЛИЗНЕЦЫ. Для того чтобы ребёнок понимал, что
  такое МОЁ, что такое ЧУЖОЕ, чтобы у него формировалось представление
  о "я".»** Cross-reference the equal-treatment furniture symmetry
  already recorded on
  [[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Zoning_and_Partition_Methods|Zoning and
  Partition Methods]] — this supplies the reason behind it.

### ⚠️⚠️ Bunk beds — argued against, with an area budget for the alternative

- **«Вообще это обычно ВЫНУЖДЕННАЯ МЕРА. Если места в комнате
  недостаточно, то лучше делать ВЫСОКУЮ КРОВАТЬ и рабочее место ПОД
  кроватью.»**
- **The sibling dynamic:** the younger is usually put on top, the elder
  below; «старший обычно обижается на младшего, потому что СВЕРХУ КРУЧЕ
  … и вообще есть ощущение, что младшего ЛЮБЯТ БОЛЬШЕ».
- **⚠️ THE REASON THAT DECIDES IT, and it is blunt, physical, and
  appears nowhere else in this vault.** Picture the teenager on the
  bottom bunk trying to fall asleep while a parent stands at the bed
  head settling the younger child above: **«Какую часть нашего
  взрослого тела видит этот самый подросток в этот момент? Он видит
  туловище, ноги и ГЕНИТАЛИИ. Вам приятно видеть это перед сном?»**
  **→ Note the scope: this applies specifically where an adult must
  stand at the lower bunk's head to tend the upper one**, i.e. wherever
  there is an age gap large enough that one child is still put to bed.
- **⚠️ THE ALTERNATIVE, WITH AN AREA BUDGET: «лучше две разные кровати,
  и рабочее место для одного под одной из кроватей, маленький столик
  для другого. Вот у них есть их 2 м [²] личного пространства, которое
  ЛИЧНО ИХ — но это всего 4 м [²] в общей сложности, ну и маленький
  проход между ними. То есть это реально даже для СОВСЕМ МАЛЕНЬКОЙ
  комнаты.»** ~2 m² each, ~4 m² total plus a passage. She caveats
  honestly that with truly no space the bunk bed is of course an option.
- **Compare the existing staircase-over-ladder recommendation on
  [[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Common_Mistakes|Common Mistakes]]** — that
  addresses bunk safety; this argues against the bunk at all where the
  area budget above can be met.

### ⚠️ Opposite-sex children sharing — and she refuses to name a threshold age

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **⚠️ She explicitly declines to issue an age norm, and says why. This
> also upgrades a claim on this page that was flagged as weak.**
>
> «Вот я сейчас скажу: очень важно расселить их до 7 лет. **А у
> родителей нет такой возможности их расселить — финансовой например.**
> Они живут в однокомнатной квартире вчетвером, и у них нет возможности
> даже СЕБЯ отселить. Я скажу, что важно до 7 лет отселить — **и у них
> НЕВРОЗ появится из-за того, что они не могут.**»
>
> **→ She refuses to publish a norm that would only generate guilt in
> families who cannot act on it.**

**What she does say:** «вообще считается, что к моменту ПОЛОВОГО
СОЗРЕВАНИЯ хорошо бы разнополых детей разнести в разные места, потому
что они должны иметь какое-то ИНТИМНОЕ ПРОСТРАНСТВО для познания своего
тела — это нормальная история». **⚠️ But if that is impossible: «у них
есть ванная комната, у них есть какие-то ЧАСЫ, когда они проводят в
этой комнате самостоятельно одни — то есть какие-то возможности есть».**

**→ The requirement is ACCESS TO PRIVACY, not necessarily a separate
room. That reframing is the actionable part, and it makes a shared room
workable where a threshold age would just declare it inadequate.**

**⚠️ And it independently corroborates Безверхая's 13+ recommendation
above — «дверь, которая закрывается изнутри» — which she herself
flagged as her own view («мне кажется») rather than a professional
standard.** A clinical psychologist arriving at the same requirement
from the privacy argument, plus the courtesy-of-knocking rule from ~7-8
recorded on [[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Common_Mistakes|Common Mistakes]],
moves that from one designer's preference to a corroborated
specification item.
