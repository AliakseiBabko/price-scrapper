# Kids Room — Common Mistakes

Part of [[05_Kids_Room/Kids_Room_Guide|Kids Room Guide]].

- **Over-packing a small room's vertical space to fit every function**
  can leave "no air" even when every functional requirement is
  technically met — a real design was explicitly discarded for this
  reason in favor of a more open, slightly less storage-dense layout.
  [source: [[_Sources/YT_t8k09YlBbtc_tregubov_room_son_daughter_design_project|YT_t8k09YlBbtc]]]
- **Corner wardrobes in a two-child room** — each triangular section is
  only ~50cm usable, workable for one child, not two; see
  [[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Materials_Furniture_and_Storage|Materials, Furniture, and Storage]].
- **⚠️ Furniture anti-tip wall-anchoring, new safety topic (Kruglov/Ontario, Round 14 triage, added 2026-08-28)**: any freestanding dresser, bookshelf, or cabinet-type unit should be securely fastened to the wall — tipping such a unit over is described as easy for a child (or a pet, or an adult leaning on it), and this is framed as a real injury-prevention measure, not just furniture-damage prevention. Generalizes beyond the kids' room but is especially relevant here given a child's lower body weight relative to the tipping leverage involved. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_IXicju8ul1A_kruglov_indestructible_materials|YT_IXicju8ul1A]]]
- **A bed against a cold exterior wall**, and **a glass-paned (even
  frosted) interior door** into a kids' room — the latter because seeing
  movement/shadows pass by a translucent door was flagged as a real,
  if subtle, source of low-level stress for the room's occupant; prefer
  an opaque, painted door. [source: [[_Sources/YT_t8k09YlBbtc_tregubov_room_son_daughter_design_project|YT_t8k09YlBbtc]]]
- **Long/narrow ("matchbox") room with furniture along both long
  walls** (bed on one, wardrobe on the other) exaggerates the tunnel
  effect — move the wardrobe to a short wall instead if one is
  available. [source: [[_Sources/YT_lbaZ__wbLWo_atmosfera_svoboda_two_children_room_design|YT_lbaZ__wbLWo]]]
- **Building single-purpose deep toy-storage compartments with no
  future repurposing path** — plan ahead for what else that volume
  could hold later (bedding, a pull-out guest bed) rather than a
  storage design that only works for one age stage. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]
- **Assuming a themed bed fits any layout** — most themed beds (house-
  shaped, ship-shaped) are designed for along-the-wall placement only;
  check this before committing. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]
- **A loft/bunk-bed ladder instead of a storage staircase** — a
  staircase is the safer option for a child getting up at night (e.g.
  for the bathroom) and doubles as storage, preserving floor area a
  separate storage piece would otherwise use. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]
- **Skipping rough-in-stage wall reinforcement for a planned sports
  fixture** (pull-up bar, climbing wall) — coordinate plywood backing
  or chemical anchors with the foreman before the wall is closed up,
  not after. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]
- **A storage shelf/unit blocking outlets already planned above a
  future desk** — coordinate storage-unit placement against the
  90cm-height outlet plan from [[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Age_Staged_Planning|Age-Staged Planning]]
  before finalizing either. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]

## ⚠️ Kids'-Room Critique Cluster (Игорь Краснов, commenting on Kirsten Dunst's son's room, added 2026-09-01, Round 6)

[source: [[_Sources/YT_uPtvVVYKjnI_krasnov_kirsten_dunst_critique|uPtvVVYKjnI]]] — a real celebrity-home kids' room, critiqued by Krasnov; a genuinely specific cluster of concrete failures for this page.

- **An entire room saturated in a single deep, muted, heavy color** (dark green, "gloomy forest" effect) reads oppressive specifically for a CHILD's room, even though such deep/muted colors can work fine in an office/library/country-house context.
- A light fixture literally shaped like a traffic-signal light flagged as thematically random with no connection to anything else in the room.
- Insufficient/unclear main light source flagged as a genuine functional gap — a dark-toned kids' room needs MORE deliberate lighting planning, not less, since it will read genuinely gloomy at night without a clear main light source.
- **⚠️ A bold, dark, room-covering wallpaper pattern on literally all four walls (not just one) specifically critiqued as creating an oppressive "enclosed" feeling** — **Krasnov explicitly catches and names a real self-contradiction: Dunst stated earlier in the same interview that she dislikes accent walls, yet has all four walls of the kids' room in one bold pattern.** Record this as a specific, real inconsistency he identified, not something to silently smooth over.
- A dark ceiling in the same kids' room questioned as unnecessary additional heaviness.
- **⚠️ Mixed/unclear room-function zoning — a genuinely useful, specific critique**: the room combines a crib/bed, a sofa, and rattan furniture with no clear zoning logic, making it unclear whether the space functions as bedroom, playroom, or living room — the child himself reportedly calls it his "living room," not his bedroom, cited as evidence of the ambiguity. **Krasnov's stated OWN studio practice for kids' rooms, even much smaller ones**: deliberately zone distinct areas for play, study, and sleep so each has clear spatial logic, rather than blending functions without visual/spatial distinction — cross-reference [[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Zoning_and_Partition_Methods|Zoning and Partition Methods]].

`single-account` (Krasnov's commentary); the underlying room is a real, independently-documented project, not Krasnov's own claim.
