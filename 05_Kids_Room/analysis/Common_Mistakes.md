# Kids Room — Common Mistakes

Part of [[05_Kids_Room/Kids_Room_Guide|Kids Room Guide]].

- **Over-packing a small room's vertical space to fit every function**
  can leave "no air" even when every functional requirement is
  technically met — a real design was explicitly discarded for this
  reason in favor of a more open, slightly less storage-dense layout.
  [source: [[_Sources/YT_t8k09YlBbtc_tregubov_room_son_daughter_design_project|YT_t8k09YlBbtc]]]
- **Corner wardrobes in a two-child room** — each triangular section is
  only ~50cm usable, workable for one child, not two; see
  [[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Materials_Furniture_and_Storage|Materials, Furniture, and Storage]].
- **⚠️ Furniture anti-tip wall-anchoring, new safety topic (Kruglov/Ontario, Round 14 triage, added 2026-08-28)**: any freestanding dresser, bookshelf, or cabinet-type unit should be securely fastened to the wall — tipping such a unit over is described as easy for a child (or a pet, or an adult leaning on it), and this is framed as a real injury-prevention measure, not just furniture-damage prevention. Generalizes beyond the kids' room but is especially relevant here given a child's lower body weight relative to the tipping leverage involved. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_IXicju8ul1A_kruglov_indestructible_materials|YT_IXicju8ul1A]]]
- **A bed against a cold exterior wall**, and **a glass-paned (even
  frosted) interior door** into a kids' room — the latter because seeing
  movement/shadows pass by a translucent door was flagged as a real,
  if subtle, source of low-level stress for the room's occupant; prefer
  an opaque, painted door. [source: [[_Sources/YT_t8k09YlBbtc_tregubov_room_son_daughter_design_project|YT_t8k09YlBbtc]]]
- **Long/narrow ("matchbox") room with furniture along both long
  walls** (bed on one, wardrobe on the other) exaggerates the tunnel
  effect — move the wardrobe to a short wall instead if one is
  available. [source: [[_Sources/YT_lbaZ__wbLWo_atmosfera_svoboda_two_children_room_design|YT_lbaZ__wbLWo]]]
- **Building single-purpose deep toy-storage compartments with no
  future repurposing path** — plan ahead for what else that volume
  could hold later (bedding, a pull-out guest bed) rather than a
  storage design that only works for one age stage. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]
- **Assuming a themed bed fits any layout** — most themed beds (house-
  shaped, ship-shaped) are designed for along-the-wall placement only;
  check this before committing. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]
- **A loft/bunk-bed ladder instead of a storage staircase** — a
  staircase is the safer option for a child getting up at night (e.g.
  for the bathroom) and doubles as storage, preserving floor area a
  separate storage piece would otherwise use. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]
- **Skipping rough-in-stage wall reinforcement for a planned sports
  fixture** (pull-up bar, climbing wall) — coordinate plywood backing
  or chemical anchors with the foreman before the wall is closed up,
  not after. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]
- **A storage shelf/unit blocking outlets already planned above a
  future desk** — coordinate storage-unit placement against the
  90cm-height outlet plan from [[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Age_Staged_Planning|Age-Staged Planning]]
  before finalizing either. [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs_archwood_kids_room_project_zoning_outlets|YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs]]]

## ⚠️ Kids'-Room Critique Cluster (Игорь Краснов, commenting on Kirsten Dunst's son's room, added 2026-09-01, Round 6)

[source: [[_Sources/YT_uPtvVVYKjnI_krasnov_kirsten_dunst_critique|uPtvVVYKjnI]]] — a real celebrity-home kids' room, critiqued by Krasnov; a genuinely specific cluster of concrete failures for this page.

- **An entire room saturated in a single deep, muted, heavy color** (dark green, "gloomy forest" effect) reads oppressive specifically for a CHILD's room, even though such deep/muted colors can work fine in an office/library/country-house context.
- A light fixture literally shaped like a traffic-signal light flagged as thematically random with no connection to anything else in the room.
- Insufficient/unclear main light source flagged as a genuine functional gap — a dark-toned kids' room needs MORE deliberate lighting planning, not less, since it will read genuinely gloomy at night without a clear main light source.
- **⚠️ A bold, dark, room-covering wallpaper pattern on literally all four walls (not just one) specifically critiqued as creating an oppressive "enclosed" feeling** — **Krasnov explicitly catches and names a real self-contradiction: Dunst stated earlier in the same interview that she dislikes accent walls, yet has all four walls of the kids' room in one bold pattern.** Record this as a specific, real inconsistency he identified, not something to silently smooth over.
- A dark ceiling in the same kids' room questioned as unnecessary additional heaviness.
- **⚠️ Mixed/unclear room-function zoning — a genuinely useful, specific critique**: the room combines a crib/bed, a sofa, and rattan furniture with no clear zoning logic, making it unclear whether the space functions as bedroom, playroom, or living room — the child himself reportedly calls it his "living room," not his bedroom, cited as evidence of the ambiguity. **Krasnov's stated OWN studio practice for kids' rooms, even much smaller ones**: deliberately zone distinct areas for play, study, and sleep so each has clear spatial logic, rather than blending functions without visual/spatial distinction — cross-reference [[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Zoning_and_Partition_Methods|Zoning and Partition Methods]].

`single-account` (Krasnov's commentary); the underlying room is a real, independently-documented project, not Krasnov's own claim.

## A Clinical Psychologist's Objections (Наталья Степанова)

Наталья Степанова — clinical psychologist, 20 years in child and adult
psychotherapy — asked directly by designer Александр Синчуков what
designers should stop doing. **She named two things, and the second
mechanism is one no designer source on this page gives.**

- **⚠️ Bright, saturated nurseries — TWO distinct mechanisms, and the
  second is the stronger:** «меня действительно расстраивают ЯРКИЕ
  детские — во-первых потому что ребёнку в ней ТЯЖЕЛО, во-вторых потому
  что она ОГРАНИЧИВАЕТ ВООБРАЖЕНИЕ. Дети должны мечтать, воображать — а
  когда у него всё это уже есть в ГОТОВЫХ ОБРАЗАХ, воображение здесь
  ОСТАНАВЛИВАЕТСЯ.» **The imagination argument also disposes of themed
  and character schemes generally**, which the Krasnov cluster above
  criticises on purely compositional grounds.
- **⚠️ A heavy dark room — independent corroboration of the Krasnov
  "gloomy forest" critique above, now with a functional consequence and
  a worked case.** A beautiful dark-blue room for a 7-year-old in which
  homework proved impossible: **«я достаточно трудоспособный человек, но
  я в этой комнате тоже заниматься не могла».** So the oppressiveness
  Krasnov names aesthetically has a measurable output: the study zone
  stops working.
- **⚠️ Gender colour coding refuted flatly: «в генетическом коде это не
  прописано».** The interviewing designer's own live case: clients
  pregnant mid-project, sex unknown, one child already, and they will
  have children of different sexes — they specified a neutral colour
  rather than stripes or a split scheme.
- **⚠️ Wall colour is NOT physiologically active; LIGHT is** — both
  brightness *and* flicker frequency. **This is a useful boundary on the
  whole colour-psychology question**: it means the objections above are
  about cognition and imagination, not about a physical effect, and it
  cross-references
  [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Colour_Psychology_and_Its_Limits|Colour
  Psychology and Its Limits]].
- **⚠️ A bedside light switch can SLOW the resolution of the normal
  childhood fear of darkness**, because the child never has to sit with
  it. Recorded as a genuine cost against a convenience the vault
  otherwise recommends.
- **⚠️ Character or vehicle beds debunked, with a norm attached: a
  20-MINUTE sleep-onset norm.** A bed that is itself an exciting object
  works against it.
- **A mezzanine or den is endorsed**, with a supervision warning
  attached.

### ⚠️ The robot-mural conflict — a designer's real dispute with another psychologist, adjudicated

The interviewing designer and his client painted a large Chappie robot
on a child's wall. **Two weeks later the client reported that a child
psychologist had told them robots must never be used on walls, and asked
for something "kinder" — animals, cartoons.** Степанова's adjudication is
worth recording in full because the distinction is genuinely useful and
protects a designer from a client demand founded on a misapplied marker:

- She hasn't seen it; the concern was presumably that the figure is
  **inanimate.** «С одной стороны я понимаю, про что говорил психолог; с
  другой стороны, именно чёткой связи НЕТ.» A robot painted **by an
  adult** in no way indicates the child will develop a disorder — «нет
  такой связи у нас».
- **⚠️ THE ACTUAL CLINICAL MARKER, which the other psychologist
  conflated with it: «если ребёнок наоборот РИСУЕТ живое существо —
  например собаку — и в неё вплетаются куски неодушевлённости, делает её
  КИБОРГОМ по сути своей, ЭТО говорит о психическом расстройстве; здесь
  хорошо бы к психологу или даже сразу к психиатру отвести ребёнка».**
- **→ An inanimate character painted by an adult is not a risk factor. A
  child spontaneously DRAWING living creatures fused with inanimate
  parts is a referral signal. Two different things.**
- **⚠️ But she does raise a different and better objection — the scheme's
  ~one-year lifespan.** See
  [[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Developmental_Age_Bands|Developmental Age
  Bands]] for the reasoning and the school-entry break point, and
  [[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Materials_Furniture_and_Storage|Materials,
  Furniture, and Storage]] for the neutral-wall display-cord alternative
  she proposes instead.

### ⚠️⚠️ Enforcing tidiness on a teenager, and the lock on the door

- **A teenager's mess is a SEPARATION INSTRUMENT, which makes
  enforcement self-defeating.** Adolescence exists to separate from the
  parents, so «ему нужно показать, что он больше их не слушается. Если в
  доме есть правило застилать кровать, как он может это показать? Всё
  делать наоборот. **Поэтому если вы будете как попугай говорить
  "застели кровать, застели кровать", он ТОЧНО застилать не будет —
  ДАЖЕ ЕСЛИ ЕГО САМОГО БУДЕТ РАЗДРАЖАТЬ — не будет застилать назло
  вам.**» Her conclusion: «здесь мы отдаём право на уборку и на всё
  остальное самим детям, и они имеют право со своим бардаком».
  **→ Design consequence, and it is a real specification input: the
  teenage room's storage must work WITHOUT parental enforcement, and the
  room must be able to look untidy without wrecking the interior.**
- **⚠️ A lock on the door is a SYMPTOM, not a problem to design around.**
  She reframed the interviewing designer's own childhood story live — he
  had fitted a lock and his father eventually broke the door down:
  «скорее всего она у вас возникла потому, что РОДИТЕЛИ ПРИХОДИЛИ В
  ВАШУ КОМНАТУ БЕЗ СПРОСА И БЕЗ СТУКА. Если бы ваши родители сами
  догадались, что у них уже живёт подросток, и начали бы стучаться —
  идея повесить замок вам бы в голову не пришла.» He confirmed it.
  «Если ваш ребёнок вешает замок, то скорее всего он говорит: родители,
  я уже вырос, пора ПЕРЕСТРАИВАТЬ ГРАНИЦЫ.»
- **⚠️ And the brief-level rule: knocking before entering is appropriate
  «даже вот с СЕМИ-ВОСЬМИЛЕТНИМИ детьми… не потому что они там делают
  что-то запрещённое, а просто как правило ВЕЖЛИВОСТИ. Если вы хотите,
  чтобы ваши дети стучались, заходя в вашу спальню, то это история про
  ДВУСТОРОННЕЕ ДВИЖЕНИЕ.»** So a solid, lockable, knocked-on door from
  ~7-8 is a legitimate specification item — which cross-checks the
  opaque-door recommendation already on this page from a different
  direction.

### ⚠️⚠️ "The child never uses the room we built him" — a designer and a psychologist disagree on the same channel

> [!WARNING]
> **This is a genuine cross-source disagreement between two
> practitioners on the SAME host's channel, answering effectively the
> same question. It is recorded as a Perspectives split rather than
> resolved, because the two answers lead to different work.**

**The case, in both tellings.** Clients report that the child spends no
time in the room built for him, hangs out in the living room, and
disturbs the parents.

**Perspective 1 — the designer (Александр Синчуков).** A townhouse
client, ten years on, asked him to redesign the nursery «потому что их
ребёнок постоянно тусуется на первом этаже в гостиной — ему просто не
интересно подниматься в свою детскую». **His answer: «мы повысили
привлекательность детской, сделали там крутой дизайн, чтобы ребёнку было
интересно тусоваться именно у себя и НЕ МЕШАТЬ ОТДЫХАТЬ РОДИТЕЛЯМ».**
Treated as a design problem with a design solution, and he reports it as
successfully solved.
[source: [[_Sources/YT_CSpXvPWpsgQ_nsdsgn_fifteen_post_occupancy_regrets|YT_CSpXvPWpsgQ]]]

**Perspective 2 — the psychologist (Наталья Степанова)**, given a nearly
identical case (a whole top-floor children's floor in a three-storey
house, unused after five-plus years) by the same host, **declines the
design framing on three grounds:**
1. «Это о том, что ребёнок не умеет ЗАНИМАТЬ СЕБЯ САМ. **Не про
   интерьер.** Потому что если ребёнок умеет занимать себя сам и ему
   интересно что-то делать самостоятельное, то он ЗАХОЧЕТ проводить
   время один.»
2. She turns it on the parents: «я слышу довольно странную историю о
   том, ПОЧЕМУ РОДИТЕЛИ ТАК НЕ ХОТЯТ ПРОВОДИТЬ ВРЕМЯ СО СВОИМ РЕБЁНКОМ?
   Он им мешает. У него же есть комната, почему она пустует?»
3. **⚠️ And she dismantles the premise arithmetically, which is the
   sharpest part:** «вы же не знаете, сколько это "постоянно". Он с ними
   спит, что ли? На время сна он точно уходит, плюс час перед сном…
   плюс он ещё какое-то время должен в школе проводить, плюс у
   подростков куча дополнительных занятий. **Ну то есть это то время,
   когда родители в 7-8 вечера пришли домой и до 9 вечера, когда
   подросток должен идти спать.**»
   **→ The complaint reduces to roughly ONE EVENING HOUR of shared
   family time, which is normal and desirable.**
[source: [[_Sources/YT_RxU4L7ce86E_nsdsgn_child_psychologist_kids_rooms|YT_RxU4L7ce86E]]]

**Common ground.** Both agree the room must genuinely support the
activities its occupant actually does — the designer via attractiveness,
the psychologist via
[[05_Kids_Room/analysis/Developmental_Age_Bands|age-appropriate
provision]]. Neither disputes that a room mis-specified for its
occupant's stage will be avoided.

**Your priority.** **Before accepting a "make the room more appealing"
brief, establish what fraction of waking hours is actually at issue.**
If the answer is one evening hour, the redesign is being asked to solve
something it cannot reach, and the money is better spent on the
provision the age band actually calls for. If the child genuinely avoids
the room during hours he has free, the designer's reading applies.
