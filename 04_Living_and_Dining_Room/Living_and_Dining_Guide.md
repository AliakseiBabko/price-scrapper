## Kitchen-Living Combination Zoning

Room-area thresholds matter more than any single layout template: under
~20m² is generally too tight for three distinct zones (kitchen, dining,
living/TV), while ~35m² is workable for a family of four with kitchen+
dining on one side and living/TV on the other (Omikor/Elena). Size the
kitchen footprint to actual cooking intensity rather than a fixed
default, and plan storage/scope around the household's real stated
priorities (children, entertaining, serious cooking) rather than a
generic template (Diskill/Nadia Karmin) — if the space is really about
socializing, the functional kitchen can even live in a separate room
entirely. A real completed 20m² project shows one applied pattern:
near-entry U-shaped kitchen, a soft-seating group, and a separate dining
zone, with three classic layout templates named and the "dining table
next to the sofa" option flagged as the weakest of the three (Fenix
Interior). Flooring-material zoning under kitchen cabinetry has a real
structural reason, not just an aesthetic one — heavy cabinetry can
dent/compress a softer material over time (Diskill). Electrical/
ventilation planning has concrete figures worth following directly:
8-10 kitchen outlets, dedicated circuits for major appliances, and a
hood-sizing formula (area × height × 12 = required m³/h) (Diskill).
Lighting for this zone benefits from a real specialist's 3-layer approach
— a decorative dining-table accent, ~200-300 lux functional light, and
cove/perimeter backlighting as a third layer — independently converged on
by two lighting specialists under different layer names (LightLab,
Diskill).

## Living Room + Bedroom Combination Zoning

Two independent designers (Iolanta Fedotova, Ekaterina Popova) converge on
essentially the same three-way structural choice from different starting
reasoning — sofa-only, bed+separate sofa, or a wall-mounted sofa/bed
transformer — each with real trade-offs (daily assembly effort, floor-
space cost, style fit) worth reading side by side rather than treating as
a single settled answer. Fedotova gives a concrete room-sizing anchor: a
full double bed plus adequate access needs a minimum of ~9m² of dedicated
sleep-zone floor area. Placement is geometry-conditioned, not fixed —
where the room has a niche, use it for the bed; in a straight walk-through
room, put the living zone in the entry path instead. Physical
zone-dividers range from curtains (cheapest) through a glass partition to
glass+curtains combined (the most complete option); a real completed
telescopic sliding-partition installation independently corroborates the
top-track-only, no-bottom-track mounting convention already recorded for
partitions generally (Mirlay Glass). The wall-mounted transformer option
— directly relevant to this project's own Phase 2 plan — carries real
construction and lighting-safety cautions worth weighing before this
project's own build: avoid bundling in extra built-in cabinets, and never
place a low-hanging pendant near the bed mechanism's swing path (Popova).

## Dining Table Sizing & Zoning

A dense single-source theory lecture (Architect Vitaly Zlobin) gives this
page's first real furniture-dimension detail for the dining table itself
— per-person footprint (60×40cm), a worked table-width derivation
(100cm minimum, 110cm recommended), an 8-person default sizing (190×
110-120cm), round-table diameters with a formula, and two clearance tiers
(60cm general, 90-100cm on the serving side). It also contributes a
reusable open-plan zoning framework: the dining table as the actual
divider between kitchen and living sub-zones, a shelving-unit visual
screen technique flagged by the source itself as closer to apartment
scale than house scale, and a floor-area tradeoff argument for staying
open-plan (shared clearance buffers) versus enclosing the dining zone
later.
