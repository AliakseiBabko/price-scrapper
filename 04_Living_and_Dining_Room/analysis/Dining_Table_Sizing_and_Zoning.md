## Per-Person and Table-Width Sizing

- **Neufert minimum per-person dining footprint: 60cm × 40cm** — the space
  one seated diner needs in front of them with dishes/food laid out.
- **⚠️ Face-to-face table-width derivation**: two facing 40cm place
  settings + a 20cm center serving strip (platters/bottles/centerpieces) =
  **100cm minimum**. **Recommend 110cm** for genuine comfort, or
  **120-130cm** for a premium/larger table (a common furniture-market
  width tier).
- **⚠️ 8-person table sizing** (this source's own standard default for a
  family of 5-6 plus regular guests): length = 3 × 60cm (per-seat
  footprint) = 180cm, **usually rounded up to 190cm**; width **110-120cm**
  per the derivation above. Scale the seat count down using the same
  60cm-per-seat unit for a smaller household.
- **⚠️ Round-table diameter, by seat count**: 5 seats → **95-100cm**; 6
  seats → **115-120cm**; 8 seats → **140-150cm**. **Formula**: diameter =
  (seat count × per-seat width) ÷ π — then **round the result up by
  10-15cm** over the raw formula output, which tends to land at or under
  the cited minimums.
- **⚠️ Sizing correction (added 2026-08-25, same channel's practice
  follow-up)**: don't size table/chair height to one household member
  alone — **average the heights of all household members** when applying
  a height-to-furniture-height relationship.
- **⚠️ Chair height: ≈40cm ± 10cm**, adjusted using the same
  household-average-height logic — a more precise formula exists but the
  source declined to include it, calling its outputs implausible.
- **Table height, cited to Soviet furniture standard ГОСТ 13025-85**:
  minimum underside-of-tabletop clearance **0.65m**; top-surface height
  ≈ **0.70-0.78m** (`ASR-uncertain` on the exact upper figure). Modern
  manufacturers don't strictly follow this old GOST. Worked example: for
  an average 1.65m-height diner, tabletop height ≈ 0.75m. A formula
  relating table height to diner height is referenced but not stated in
  the source — a real gap, unresolved.

## Clearance Rules

- **Minimum 0.6m (60cm) perimeter clearance** around the table's
  non-service sides — enough to push a chair back and stand, or pass
  behind a seated diner without disturbing them.
- **⚠️ 0.9-1.0m clearance on the serving side** (the side facing the
  kitchen/serving point) — carrying dishes needs more room than sitting/
  standing clearance alone; the source prefers the full 1.0m.
- **⚠️ Real-world exception (added 2026-08-25): apply the higher 0.9-1.0m
  clearance tier to any side of the table that doubles as a
  through-circulation path**, not just the literal food-serving side — a
  real worked example widened clearance to a full 1m on a side bordering
  a corridor used to reach a second entrance/pantry, even though the
  non-serving-side 0.6m minimum would otherwise technically apply.

## Open-Plan Sightline and Zoning Framework

- **⚠️ The dining table as the actual zone divider**: in a shared kitchen-
  living-dining space, the table is often placed near the room's
  geometric center, splitting the open footprint into three functional
  sub-zones (cooking, eating, lounging) without a physical wall. Check TV
  sightlines from every planned activity zone before finalizing furniture
  placement — a reusable planning check, not house-specific, even though
  the source's own worked scale (~200-250m²) is a house figure.
- **A shelving unit behind the sofa can visually screen the dining zone
  from the living zone** in a more elongated open-plan footprint, while
  keeping the kitchen open behind it — the source explicitly flags this
  as the technique closer to an apartment-scale solution than a sprawling
  house layout, i.e. the most directly relevant one of this source's
  techniques to this household's own context.
- **⚠️ Kitchen island/breakfast-bar as a distinct zone from the formal
  dining table**: the dining table implies full table service (seating,
  serving) and is realistically used mainly on weekends/holidays/guest
  occasions; a raised bar-height island section is the everyday
  quick-breakfast zone instead. **Bar height = standard kitchen counter
  height (~80-100cm) + 20cm**, producing a stepped island profile (lower
  prep side / raised eating side). Cross-reference:
  [[03_Kitchen/Kitchen_Furniture|Kitchen Furniture]]'s existing island
  sizing content (clearance, minimum width) for the rest of an island's
  own dimensions.

## Open-Plan vs. Enclosed Dining Room: A Floor-Area Tradeoff

**⚠️ Enclosing a dining zone behind partition walls forfeits shared
clearance with an adjacent zone.** Each of the clearance rules above needs
its own buffer; in an open plan, two adjacent zones (e.g. the dining
table's serving-side clearance and the living room's soft-seating
circulation space) can share the same buffer strip, but an enclosed room
needs its own full buffer on every side — increasing total required floor
area versus keeping the space open. **Practical recommendation**: reserve/
plan space for a future dividing wall from the start even while keeping
the space open initially, so a later conversion to an enclosed dining
room doesn't require a full replan.
