## Materials

**Ordinary wall paint plus ordinary laminate flooring can deform over time
from repeated water contact.** Use moisture-resistant paint (wipeable with a
damp cloth without absorbing splashed water) and a genuinely water-resistant
floor — quartz-laminate (кварцевый ламинат, ~75% ground stone) is preferred
over both ceramic tile and ordinary laminate: warmer underfoot than tile,
less brittle (survives a dropped object better), and fully water-resistant
unlike ordinary laminate. Its high dimensional stability is also why it can
be used on **walls**, not just floors, in wet-adjacent rooms. [source:
[[_Sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore Design]]]

## Appliance Installation

**Never install a washing machine over a plinth/socle base — it must sit
directly on the floor on a rigid, level surface.** Per the appliance's own
instructions (per Michael Moore Design), the base must handle real
spin-cycle vibration load; a socle-mounted washer needs real structural
reinforcement (a real project used a rebar-reinforced plinth frame to make
this work, but the source explicitly does not recommend attempting this
without that reinforcement). **This conflicts with a different source's
chest-height recommendation** — see the full Perspectives block on
[[09_Laundry_Room/analysis/Essential_Components_and_Layout|Essential Components & Layout]].

**Install a floor leak-detection sensor under the washing machine and at its
water-supply connection point.** It triggers on the smallest water
intrusion, giving time to react before real damage occurs. [source:
`_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_vid1_transcript_d04723c5.txt`]

**Do not run the washing machine's drain line without a proper S-trap (dry
siphon)** — without one, sewer gas can back up through the drain into the
room. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_vid1_transcript_d04723c5.txt`, `_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_3_a0e895b1.txt`]

## Wardrobe/Cabinet Conversion Pitfalls

**Do not convert an ordinary wardrobe/clothing cabinet into a laundry
niche** — Michael Moore Design gives four distinct, stated reasons:

1. A washer needs clearance gaps on every side inside the cabinet so
   vibration isn't transmitted into the cabinet structure — and door hinges
   can physically block the machine from sliding fully into the module if
   depth calculations don't account for hinge protrusion.
2. The module needs cut openings for water supply, drain, and electrical
   connections, ideally routed via an adjacent module for serviceability.
3. The base under the machine must be a rigid floor connection — an
   ordinary wardrobe's own plinth/socle base is not designed to sit under
   the machine's own footprint.
4. The enclosure needs planned top-or-bottom ventilation so humidity doesn't
   accumulate inside a sealed cabinet box.

**Door-swing direction must match between the cabinet door and the
appliance door, both ways** — a mismatch (opposite hinge sides, or the
cabinet door blocking the machine's own detergent-drawer opening) is called
"the most frustrating mistake." Some appliance models don't support
re-hanging the door to the other side at all, so check this before
ordering, not after installation. [source: [[_Sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore Design]]]

## Electrical

**Wire a "master switch" for the room's lighting plus the iron's specific
outlet through a dedicated contactor in the breaker panel — never through
an ordinary wall switch.** This safeguards against leaving an energized iron
plugged in after leaving the house. Shelnat's independent account of the
same idea adds a second, distinct mechanism reason for combining the
iron-outlet and light on one switch: repeatedly pulling the plug out
mechanically stresses both the in-wall receptacle and the plug itself over
time. [sources: [[_Sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore Design]], [[_Sources/YT_GM_m1IJk7Hk_shelnat_ideal_laundry_room|Shelnat]]]

**Count outlets per planned appliance before finalizing the room** — drying
cabinet, ironing board/press, vacuum charging dock, garment steamer station,
and shoe dryer are the specific appliance types Michael Moore Design says to
count for, rather than assuming a generic outlet count will cover everything
added later. [source: [[_Sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore Design]]]

**Outlets placed behind pull-out drawers (not just behind the appliance
column) allow quick troubleshooting access** — if something electrical goes
wrong, a drawer can be pulled to reach the outlet rather than needing to
move the appliance. [source: [[_Sources/YT_JsRvUMfIPwc_inmyroom_22_secrets_washer_placement|INMYROOM TV]]]

## ⚠️ Drying Is Omitted From the Brief and Then Cannot Be Retrofitted (Ксения, space organiser, and Александр Синчуков, via NSDSGN)

**This page's Electrical section already says to count outlets per planned appliance. This is the failure that happens when drying never reaches the brief at all — and it is recorded from inside a professional space organiser's own flat, which is what makes it worth keeping.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_9ZwqMKiMgvc_nsdsgn_storage_organiser_family_flat|YT_9ZwqMKiMgvc]]]

**The case:** she has **no dryer. They did not allow for one during the renovation, and technical constraints now prevent adding one** — «сейчас уже есть технические особенности, которые нам не позволяют это сделать». A drying rack therefore stands **permanently in the bedroom**, which she calls «арт-объект», drying **bedding and all clothing, not only linen**. «Некуда поставить больше. Это жизнь.»

**⚠️ The generalisation is the designer's, and it names the sequence precisely**: clients complete a design project, **order an ordinary washing machine, never consider drying at all**, and remember only after moving in — at which point the rack appears «в самом неожиданном месте».

**→ The rule: state the laundry equipment explicitly in the ТЗ — washer, dryer, drying cabinet, or washer-dryer. If there is deliberately none, then plan where the rack lives.** Not planning it does not remove the drying; it relocates it to whichever room has floor space, permanently. He adds that clients frequently also ask for an **ironing board**, which likewise needs an allocated place.

**A second, related retrofit failure in the same flat**: the treadmill. The host's standing view is that treadmills become clothes racks; here she genuinely uses it — trainers and a tablet stand beside it — **but because it was never planned it stands on a mat over a radiator and looks poor.** **The pattern is the same: an unbriefed appliance still arrives, and then occupies the least appropriate spot.**

**⚠️ And the knock-on effect on the bathroom, which she calls «вторая проблемная точка после прихожей»**: because the washing machine lives there, **all the sportswear and sports bags settle there too**, and sports clothing is being dried there continuously — the radiator is not sufficient. She added a separate plastic chest of drawers purely for sportswear. **So an unplanned drying strategy does not stay a laundry problem; it becomes a bathroom storage problem.** Cross-reference [[09_Laundry_Room/analysis/Placement_Comparison_by_Room_Type|Placement Comparison by Room Type]] and the intake discussion in [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Family_Scenario_Driven_Design|Family Scenario Driven Design]], which records the same host building a dedicated laundry-and-shower санузел for one family's hockey kit once the scenario *was* briefed.

## Dryer Lint Is Normal, Not a Damage Signal (Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario, added 2026-08-28, Round 16)

**A visible buildup of lint/fabric fuzz after each drying cycle does not by itself mean the dryer is damaging clothing.** Kruglov's own framing: that lint and fuzz shed from clothing/textiles over time regardless of whether a tumble dryer is used at all — a dryer's lint trap simply collects and concentrates it in one visible place rather than letting it disperse invisibly through the apartment. **A genuine, distinct caveat**: a good-quality dryer is gentler on fabric than a poor one, so drum/cycle quality does still affect *how much* extra wear the clothing takes — but the mere presence of trapped lint after a cycle isn't itself evidence of that. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_iEm_mwCJpfA_kruglov_dust_free_interior_guide|YT_iEm_mwCJpfA]]]
