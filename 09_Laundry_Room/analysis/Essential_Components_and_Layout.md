# Laundry Room — Essential Components & Layout

Part of [[09_Laundry_Room/Laundry_Guide|Laundry Guide]]. What actually needs to
fit into a laundry/utility room, why, and how the pieces relate to each
other — appliances, drying, storage, fixtures, and the surfaces that tie the
room together.

## Leak Protection

Install a **floor leak-detection sensor** near the washing machine and at its
water-supply connection point, linked to an **automatic shutoff valve** —
this cuts water supply automatically on a hose burst or connection failure
rather than relying on someone noticing a flood in progress. [source:
`_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_vid1_transcript_d04723c5.txt`, `_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_tips_video_f23c504a.txt`]

For a laundry room combined with other plumbing (a boiler room, a technical
cabinet with a distribution/collector node), the highest-priority sensor
placement is inside that concealed cabinet itself — it's the hardest leak to
notice quickly and concentrates the most failure-prone connections — per
[[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_sd2XYBZY-K8_kruglov_bathroom_2026_top10|Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario]]'s stated sensor-priority order (full detail on [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Bathtub_and_Shower]]).

## Drain Plumbing

Install a **dedicated dry-siphon (S-trap) drain** on the washing machine's
drain line — without it, sewer gas can back up through the appliance drain
into the room. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_3_a0e895b1.txt`]

Ekaterina Popova adds a distinct mechanism point: a washing machine's drain
line **doesn't need a sloped run** the way a sink or floor drain does,
because the appliance has its own internal pump actively pushing water out —
slope is a non-issue for this specific connection, unlike the S-trap
requirement above, which still applies. When running a drain hose through a
floor screed during construction, route it so it can't kink or deform over
time, and ideally so it stays replaceable later without breaking open the
screed. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_BdztT9OUWVY_popova_where_to_install_washer|Popova]]]

## Appliance Selection: Washer, Dryer, or Combo

**Separate washer + dryer units are the "ideal" setup when budget and space
allow**, each fully dedicated to its own task rather than trading off inside
one machine — this preference now has real cross-channel support, not just
one source's opinion:

- [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_sd2XYBZY-K8_kruglov_bathroom_2026_top10|Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario]] states this as the source's own stated "ideal."
- [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_GM_m1IJk7Hk_shelnat_ideal_laundry_room|Shelnat/Natalia Shelkovskaya]] independently corroborates it via an **informal Instagram poll of her own audience**: households that bought a combo unit were mostly dissatisfied specifically with the drying function, while households with separate units were satisfied and often enthusiastic about the dryer. This is a genuinely different channel reaching the same conclusion, though both remain informal/anecdotal evidence (a practitioner's client base, an audience poll — not a controlled study).

**When space is the binding constraint, a combo unit is a reasonable choice, but only above a stated quality bar**: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore Design]] explicitly gates this — only "high-quality models that can actually do what each machine does separately" should be considered a 2-in-1 substitute, not any combo unit. [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_JsRvUMfIPwc_inmyroom_22_secrets_washer_placement|INMYROOM TV]]'s real-apartment case adds the specific *driver* for choosing a combo in a small apartment: it avoids needing floor space for a separate drying rack, not just appliance-hardware budget (this project's own existing Dos_and_Donts framing). [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials|Diskill/Liza Robizon]] frames the combo-vs-separate choice by apartment class ("Comfort" tier or space-constrained → combo; higher-volume bedding needs → separate stacks).

**Heat-pump tumble dryers are preferred over any other drying technology**,
regardless of standalone or combo casing, per Kruglov/Ontario — the gentlest
drying technology on fabric, leaving clothes soft/warm/dry and often
skipping ironing entirely; the only downside is longer cycle time. [source:
[[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_sd2XYBZY-K8_kruglov_bathroom_2026_top10|Kruglov/Ontario]]]

**Dryer myth check, per Diskill**: the common "dryers wrinkle clothes"
objection is a timing issue (leaving items in too long after the cycle
ends), not a flaw in the technology itself — pull items out promptly.
Dryer-dried towels also stay noticeably softer/fluffier longer than
air-dried ones, and the lint/pet-hair collection tray captures material that
would otherwise be airborne if line-dried indoors — a hygiene argument for
owning a dryer beyond convenience. `single-account`, `unverified` folk
mechanism claim, internally consistent. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials|Diskill]]]

## Washing Machine Selection Criteria

> [!NOTE]
> This channel's first dedicated general washing-machine buying-guide video for this store —
> a structured 2026 buying guide, distinct from this section's existing separate-vs-combo/
> heat-pump-dryer content above. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_-PfMz_L6pmI_kruglov_which_washing_machine_2026|Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario]]]

- **Loading type**: front-load is the standard/optimal choice (~90% of models sold) — needs
  floor clearance for the door swing, and is required if a dryer will stack on top in a
  column. Top-load only makes sense in width-constrained spaces where a front door swing
  won't fit, or for elderly users avoiding bending — with a real counterpoint: pulling wet
  bedding (a duvet cover specifically) out of a top-loader takes noticeably more physical
  strength than lifting it from a front-loader, which can be harder for an elderly user than
  the bending a top-loader is meant to spare them.
- **Full-size vs. narrow washer**: full-size ≈60cm wide, 55-60cm deep, 7-10kg capacity —
  better balance, less vibration, higher motor longevity, quieter. Narrow: 33-45cm deep,
  5-7kg capacity — worse stability, shorter service life, worse spin-cycle balancing,
  stronger vibration.
- **Inverter motor only**, at any normal 2026 price point — quieter, more efficient, longer
  service life; non-inverter only makes sense at the absolute bottom of the market.
- **⚠️ Drum-capacity labeling caveat**: baseline bands are 5-6kg (too small), 7-8kg
  (optimal), 9kg+ (needed for a family) — but there's no single industry standard for
  measuring capacity, and a stated figure (e.g. "9kg") usually reflects one specific fabric
  type (typically cotton); other fabrics fit less by weight, and in practice a machine
  labeled 9kg sometimes only actually holds 6-7kg. Don't take the spec-sheet number at face
  value. Machine weight is an imperfect proxy for drum size (heavier usually = bigger drum),
  but real named exceptions exist (a 63L drum at only 65kg total weight vs. a Candy model at
  the same 65kg weight with only a 48L drum).
- **Spin speed, with a motor-longevity nuance**: minimum viable 1,000rpm; optimal 1,200rpm.
  Chasing higher rated speeds (1,400rpm+) doesn't meaningfully improve dryness — quality
  plateaus around 1,000rpm. But running a machine at its own true maximum rated speed
  maximizes motor stress and shortens service life — a machine rated 1,400rpm but routinely
  run at 800-1,000rpm will outlast a machine whose max rating is only 1,000rpm and is always
  run at that ceiling.
- **Balancing**: driven by drum size, total machine weight, and control-algorithm quality;
  poor balancing means louder operation, faster wear, and in extreme cases the machine can
  physically "hop" and damage nearby structures. Weight bands: 55-60kg = light/mediocre;
  65-75kg = normal; 75kg+ = best — with named exceptions where a lighter machine still
  balances well due to superior algorithm tuning.
- **⚠️ Noise/vibration root cause — installation quality dominates price tier**: even the
  most expensive premium machine will vibrate and wear prematurely if installed on an
  uneven, unlevelled base or pressed flush against adjacent cabinetry; correctly installed,
  the noise gap between economy and premium tiers is modest (not 5-10x), far smaller than
  price alone might suggest.
- **Controls**: touch panel, mechanical buttons/dials, hybrid, or app (Wi-Fi) control — app
  control is called irrelevant for ~99% of users by this source's own opinion, with one
  acknowledged real exception (remote configuration for an elderly relative in another city).
  Touch controls on very budget machines can work poorly, especially with wet hands.
- **Programs**: only ~6 core programs (cotton, mixed fabric, delicate, quick wash, spin,
  rinse) are used by ~99% of people; the other 20-30 "extra" programs on some machines are
  just recombinations of the same underlying parameters (time, water volume, spin
  count/speed, temperature) — algorithm/tuning quality matters far more than program count.
- **⚠️ Steam-function taxonomy — one implementation that doesn't actually work**: cheap
  heating-element-based steam (water never reaches the element on a dry-refresh cycle, so it
  doesn't work for that use case) vs. a real steam-generator mechanism (works well). Named
  example: LG's own "Steam" line (element-based, poor, low adoption) vs. "True Steam"
  (generator-based, performs well). Real benefit where it works: freshens/de-odorizes and
  lightly reduces wrinkles without a full wash — doesn't replace real ironing, and
  meaningfully raises price.
- **Auto-dose (detergent)**: fill a reservoir once, machine doses per cycle automatically —
  mostly implemented for gel/liquid detergent, rarely powder. Downsides: added mechanical
  complexity/failure point, needs periodic system flushing.
- **⚠️ Washer+dryer combo caution — new mechanism, extends the existing separate-vs-combo
  preference above**: a combo unit doubles wear on a single shared motor/mechanism versus two
  dedicated machines, and there's a real capacity mismatch — a machine that washes 9kg may
  only be able to dry 5-6kg of that same load in one cycle. Corroborates this channel's own
  existing preference for separate units, with new reasoning.
- **Economy/mid/premium tiers**: economy — cheaper/more logical to replace than repair if it
  breaks; low motor durability, unsophisticated electronics, light chassis (raises noise),
  high failure risk right after warranty ends. Mid-tier: solid, longer-lasting, better
  balance tuning, quieter, predictable 5+ year service life. Premium: higher-quality
  materials, more sophisticated algorithms, styling focus, typically premium service/support.
  **Wash quality itself is roughly equal across all three tiers** — correct program
  selection and correct installation matter far more than price tier.
- **Micro-compact wall-mounted washers**: a niche category, max 1-2kg load, for short-term
  rental apartments/guest houses needing only minimal incidental laundry — not for regular
  household use.
- **The source's own "ideal" spec checklist**: front-load; full-size body (55cm+ depth); 7kg+
  drum capacity; inverter motor; 1,200rpm+ spin speed; a proven manufacturer and reputable
  seller.

[source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_-PfMz_L6pmI_kruglov_which_washing_machine_2026|Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario]]]

## Tumble Dryer Selection Criteria

> [!NOTE]
> This channel's first dedicated general tumble-dryer buying-guide video for this store —
> extends this page's existing heat-pump-dryer preference and separate-vs-combo preference
> with real mechanism-level detail across all three drying technologies. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_IuyGPfH85dg_kruglov_how_to_choose_tumble_dryer|Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario]]]

- **Why a dryer beats a washer's spin cycle alone**: spin removes only excess water, leaving
  at least 40-60% residual moisture; a dryer removes that remaining moisture, leaving laundry
  warm/soft/"hotel-like," sometimes wrinkle-free enough to skip ironing with the right
  program/fabric match — and removes the need to air-dry laundry around the apartment.
- **⚠️ "Dryers ruin clothes" myth, mechanism-level rebuttal**: only happens from picking the
  wrong program or overheating fabric (e.g. a cotton program on wool); with correct program
  selection a dryer never damages clothes and actually reduces wear vs. wet laundry stretching
  out of shape on a drying rack. Parallel: incorrect program/color-mixing ruins laundry in a
  washer too — a general correct-program-selection principle, not a dryer-specific flaw.
- **No sewer/drain plumbing connection required, in most cases**: nearly all dryers have a
  built-in condensate tray and a direct drain-connection port for the central sewer line if you
  prefer not to manually empty the tray every 1-2 cycles.
- **⚠️ Three-tier drying-technology taxonomy, with real named tradeoffs**:
  - **Vent/exhaust drying** — oldest, now largely unavailable. Heats air, passes it through the
    drum, vents outside; needs a ventilation-duct connection (indoor humidity rises noticeably
    without one); highest electricity consumer of the three; runs hottest, most likely to
    overheat/damage fabric. Only advantage: cheapest, simplest construction.
  - **Condensation drying** — heats air to dry laundry, moisture collected in a reservoir or
    drained away, warm air recirculates in the drum. **⚠️ An undisclosed drawback**: running
    this type measurably raises ambient room temperature — a bathroom/laundry room can rise
    **~6°C** during a cycle. **⚠️ ≈3.5 kWh per cycle.** Advantages: most affordable/most
    commonly stocked; no ventilation-duct connection needed, simple hookup (ideally with a
    sewer drain connection).
  - **Heat-pump drying** — the source's recommended type. A built-in heat pump recirculates
    air rather than heating it directly, extracting moisture with minimal heat — gentlest on
    fabric. **⚠️ Price: a good model starts at ≈45,000-50,000 RUB (≈$560-$620), reaching
    ≈100,000 RUB (≈$1,200) and above.** Tradeoff: noticeably longer cycle time. **⚠️ ≈0.8-1+
    kWh per cycle** — a large efficiency gap vs. condensation drying. Also quieter, and longer
    component lifespan from reduced mechanical wear.
- **Stacking**: any dryer type can stack on a washing machine via a dedicated stacking
  belt/kit made for that purpose.
- **⚠️ Separate vs. combined unit, three configurations with a named-brand caution**: (a)
  fully separate units — maximum efficiency/quality, no compromises, and lets you wash and dry
  *simultaneously* (finish load 1's wash, move to dryer, start load 2's wash) — roughly
  doubles effective throughput; also lasts longer since a combo unit's components do double
  mechanical duty every cycle. (b) A combo unit with **one shared drum** — saves space, but
  that's the only real advantage. (c) **⚠️ A combo unit with two independent drums in one
  shared chassis** — named brands: **LG, Hyundai, Haier** — explicitly flagged as **not yet
  proven/well-established enough to fully trust**; if either drum fails, the whole unit stops
  working (unlike two separate machines), and these twin-drum units cost noticeably more than
  two separate machines.
- **Price drivers**: heat pump presence, filter quality, drum material (stainless steel raises
  price), electronics/Wi-Fi sophistication, brand, and named extras (garment-refresh,
  anti-allergen, anti-crease, self-cleaning, a dedicated shoe-drying program, drum lighting,
  reversible door-hinge side).
- **⚠️ The source's own bottom-line recommendation**: buy two separate quality-brand machines
  rather than any combo configuration if budget/space allow — corroborates this page's
  existing separate-units preference, with an added twin-drum-combo brand caution.

[source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_IuyGPfH85dg_kruglov_how_to_choose_tumble_dryer|Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario]]]

## A Full-Size Washer, and One Per Floor in a Multi-Story House

A full-size washing machine is the baseline — for a household with 3+
bedrooms, bedding volume alone overloads a single small-capacity machine.
For a two-story-plus house (200m²+), Diskill's own client base reports
installing a washing machine **on every floor** works well, and the source
explicitly warns against retrofitting plumbing for a second machine later
("improvised on-the-fly pipe hacking" in a bathroom or closet) instead of
planning for it from the start. `single-account` (this instructor's client
base), but the bedding-volume-scales-with-bedrooms reasoning is a plain
mechanism. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials|Diskill]]]

## Reserve Non-Mechanical Drying Space, Even With a Dryer

Multiple independent sources agree a laundry room needs planned space for
**non-mechanical drying**, not just a tumble dryer:

- **Underwear specifically is damaged by tumble-drying**, per Architect Vitaliy Zlobin, citing experienced homemakers' own reports — reserve floor space for a foldable metal drying rack regardless of whether a dryer is also installed. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1NqrFHrIC3k_zlobin_laundry_modern_home|Zlobin]]]
- Diskill distinguishes three vertical-drying implementations — floor-standing, wall-mounted/foldable, and a simple fixed rod/bar — with the rod specifically for delicate hanger-dried items that then get steamed on the hanger. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials|Diskill]]]
- Michael Moore Design adds retractable/pull-out drying racks built directly into cabinetry, tucking away once laundry is dry so they don't obstruct the room. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore]]]

**A drying cabinet (сушильный шкаф)** is a distinct, larger appliance from a
tumble dryer — refrigerator-sized, dries/warms an entire outfit including
outerwear and footwear in about two hours (Diskill traces the format to a
Finnish ski-resort entryway use case), with internal hangers suitable for
bedding, bedspreads, and delicate items that shouldn't go in a tumble dryer
at all (Michael Moore). Shelnat and Zlobin both tie demand for this
appliance specifically to **country houses with children**, driven by kids'
regular contact with mud/snow/puddles outdoors and the cabinet's large
footprint, which doesn't always fit a city apartment. One brand name was
mentioned in the Diskill source as a well-known producer but not
independently verified — recorded as a commercial mention only, not a
recommendation. [sources: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials|Diskill]], [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore]], [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_GM_m1IJk7Hk_shelnat_ideal_laundry_room|Shelnat]], [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1NqrFHrIC3k_zlobin_laundry_modern_home|Zlobin]]]

**A compact minimum for a small apartment**, per Shelnat: washer + dryer +
garment steamer — her own subjective "best combination," a smaller footprint
than the full checklist above. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_GM_m1IJk7Hk_shelnat_ideal_laundry_room|Shelnat]]]

## Perspectives on Record: Should a Washer Ever Be Elevated Off the Floor?

Two sources in this batch genuinely disagree, not just differ in detail:

| Source | Position | Stated Reasoning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials\|Diskill/Liza Robizon]] | Raise washer/dryer to **chest height** | A client's own suggestion; avoids bending over, "especially useful for older household members" |
| [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes\|Michael Moore Design]] | Washer must sit **directly on the floor on a rigid, level base** — never on a raised plinth/socle | Per the appliance's own instructions: the base must handle spin-cycle vibration load. A real project needed plinth lighting and mounted the washer on a reinforced (rebar-framed) socle to make it work — but the source explicitly says **"I don't recommend doing this yourself"** and floor-mounts on every other project |

**Common ground**: both sources agree the *dryer* can safely sit elevated —
Diskill's chest-height point is really about the ergonomic win a stacked
column already gives via the top unit; Michael Moore's hard floor-mounting
rule is specifically about the **washer** (the heavier, higher-vibration
machine), not the dryer. Read narrowly, the disagreement may be smaller than
it first appears: Diskill's real-world implementation (a stacking kit) may
still put the washer on the floor with the dryer raised on top, which
Michael Moore's own stacking-sequence rule (washer below, dryer above, for
load-bearing reasons) doesn't actually contradict. **Where they clearly
still conflict**: Diskill's client also raised the *washer itself* (not
routed through a stacking kit) via a client-driven modification the source
doesn't describe in installation detail, while Michael Moore treats any
non-floor washer mounting as a real structural risk best avoided.

**Your priority**: not yet decided. The deciding factor both sources point
to is whether the raised mounting has genuine structural reinforcement
(Michael Moore's rebar-framed case) versus an unengineered platform — worth
resolving with an installer/appliance manufacturer directly before
committing to any elevated washer mount, rather than defaulting to either
source's position.

## Storage

- **Laundry basket subdivision, two rules**: use several baskets (including
  narrow, manufacturer-color-labeled ones) rather than one bin, to avoid
  digging through a mixed pile; and separate adult and children's laundry
  into different baskets for hygiene and convenience. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials|Diskill]]]
- **Color-coded floor-mounted flip-top baskets let you skip presorting
  entirely** — load directly by color at the point of use, then carry the
  matched basket to the machine at wash time. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore]]]
- **Chemical storage needs a full cabinet, not a shelf** — multi-kilogram
  bulk powder, softener, bleach, and other cleaning chemicals, plus
  basins/buckets, steam generators, and irons in the same system. Architect
  Zlobin adds a logistics reason to keep this storage *in* the laundry room
  rather than a separate pantry: avoiding repeated trips carrying bottles
  back and forth. [sources: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials|Diskill]], [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1NqrFHrIC3k_zlobin_laundry_modern_home|Zlobin]]]
- **Pull-out shelves cost more than fixed shelves but avoid reaching into a
  niche** — a stated cost/convenience tradeoff specific to laundry-room
  shelving. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore]]]
- **A sock-loss hook system**: a row of hooks for single "lost" socks, so a
  missing sock's mate is easy to find — useful for large families. [source:
  [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_JsRvUMfIPwc_inmyroom_22_secrets_washer_placement|INMYROOM TV]]]
- **A shallow niche behind a door (as little as ~25cm) is real dedicated
  storage** for a cordless vacuum, mops, and mesh-shelved tools — "a
  technical closet every home should have." [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_JsRvUMfIPwc_inmyroom_22_secrets_washer_placement|INMYROOM TV]]]
- **A laundry chute** (a vertical shaft with an upper-floor intake hatch and
  a lower collection basket) is a real, if niche, solution for multi-story
  homes with the laundry room in the basement. [sources: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore]], corroborated independently by [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_GM_m1IJk7Hk_shelnat_ideal_laundry_room|Shelnat]]]

## Work Surfaces and Transfer Points

- **A clean-laundry folding/staging zone** — a clear surface for laying out
  and stacking folded laundry, with room for a bulky item like a basin.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials|Diskill]]]
- **A dedicated flat, smooth counter for garment inspection**, per Architect
  Zlobin — a distinct purpose from folding: laying out shirts/blouses to
  spot-check for stains or damage before folding or hanging. Can be
  integrated as a countertop over the washer/dryer column; siting it near a
  window helps since inspection work is visually demanding. [source:
  [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1NqrFHrIC3k_zlobin_laundry_modern_home|Zlobin]]]
- **A "transfer closet" (разгрузочный шкаф) between the laundry room and
  bedrooms**, per Zlobin — a plain closet with shelves/hangers that acts as
  a hand-off point for folded/ironed laundry, avoiding piece-by-piece
  carrying trips or piling clean laundry on the floor. Distinct from the
  in-room folding zone above — this is a separate storage closet positioned
  as an intermediate stop before items reach bedrooms. [source:
  [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1NqrFHrIC3k_zlobin_laundry_modern_home|Zlobin]]]
- **Combining the laundry room with a walk-in closet** lets clean laundry be
  hung directly in its storage place immediately, no intermediate carrying
  step — per Shelnat. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_GM_m1IJk7Hk_shelnat_ideal_laundry_room|Shelnat]]]

## Fixtures: Sinks and Utility Taps

A **utility/service sink**, distinct from a bathroom or kitchen sink, is for
pre-treating stains, filling a bucket, washing shoes, and washing a dog's
paws — functions multiple sources explicitly say shouldn't be pushed onto a
bathroom or kitchen sink. Architect Zlobin extends the philosophy further:
a small sink belongs not just in the laundry room but in a garage or any
technical/mechanical room too, since a washer handles bulk loads but doesn't
help with one small stain or a quick hand-wash. The smallest available sink
models run about **15×25cm**, small enough to fit almost any layout, per
Shelnat. [sources: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials|Diskill]], [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore]], [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1NqrFHrIC3k_zlobin_laundry_modern_home|Zlobin]], [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_GM_m1IJk7Hk_shelnat_ideal_laundry_room|Shelnat]]]

Zlobin separately recommends a **floor-level hot/cold tap with a floor drain
pan (поддон)** as a third, lower-tech fixture option distinct from a raised
sink — for filling a bucket or hosing off very dirty boots without using the
sink or bathroom, and for washing a pet's paws or one's own feet. Michael
Moore's raised pet-shower-trap platform (below) is a related but distinct,
more elevated implementation of the same general need. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_1NqrFHrIC3k_zlobin_laundry_modern_home|Zlobin]]]

A **raised platform for a pet paw-washing trap** avoids the user bending all
the way down — an ergonomic/back-health reason, not just a drainage
consideration. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore]]]

## Ironing and Steaming

- Reserve a **specific footprint for an ironing press** (гладильный
  press) early in planning if considering one — faster than an iron, has
  multiple temperature settings, costs substantially more, and is easy to
  forget once shelving/layout is already fixed. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore]]]
- **Ironing board placement inside the laundry room, a staff-privacy
  rationale beyond floor space**: when household staff do the ironing,
  keeping the board out of a shared living space lets them work
  uninterrupted/out of sight. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials|Diskill]]]
- **Task lighting for ironing/sorting needs its own fixture at 4000-5000K**
  (daylight range), separate from general room lighting — this color
  temperature specifically makes stains on dirty laundry easier to spot; a
  general ceiling light alone is explicitly called insufficient. [source:
  [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_bjfqQUgsyNs_michael_moore_10_mistakes|Michael Moore]]]
- A **heated towel rail repurposed for hanging cleaning cloths** (not bath
  towels) after use — the opposite of its usual bathroom application.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8wo1qImfpDU_diskill_laundry_room_essentials|Diskill]]]
