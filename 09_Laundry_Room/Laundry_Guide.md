# Laundry Room — Planning Guide

General, sourced technique/reasoning for laundry-room and utility-room
planning — what belongs in the room, how to size and place it, and the
mistakes that recur across projects. Gathered from six independent
Russian-language design channels/practitioners (four framed around country
houses, two around apartments). Each section states the leading
recommendation(s) and *why*, so you can form a view without clicking
through; the linked `analysis/` pages carry the full multi-source breakdown,
every number, and every citation.

## Essential Components, Appliance Selection & Storage

What actually needs to fit into a laundry/utility room: full-size washer and
dryer (heat-pump dryers preferred; separate units over a combo where
budget/space allow, now with cross-channel poll corroboration), reserved
non-mechanical drying space even alongside a dryer (underwear specifically
is damaged by tumble-drying, per one architect source), a drying cabinet as
a distinct larger appliance, laundry-basket subdivision rules, chemical
storage as a full cabinet, a utility sink or floor tap+drain-pan for
pretreating/pet-washing, work surfaces for folding vs. garment inspection, a
"transfer closet" hand-off point to bedrooms, and leak-protection/drain
mechanics.

## Placement Comparison by Room Type

Dedicated room (3.5-7m², child-safety case for a full room over a closet)
vs. closet/niche (≤2m²) vs. bathroom (four sub-options with real
clearances: under-sink 45cm depth, beside-sink 90×50cm, full-height niche,
opposite-sink) vs. hallway (disguised-cabinet technique) vs. kitchen
(50cm-deep freestanding limit, corner-access workaround). Standard washer
dimensions, the depth-budgeting formula for a hidden niche, stacking
sequencing, ventilation/heat/noise mechanisms, and a real electric-meter
relocation precedent.
