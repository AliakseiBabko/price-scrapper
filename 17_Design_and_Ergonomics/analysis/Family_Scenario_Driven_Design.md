## The core principle

A layout/fixture/storage decision should be driven by how *this specific household* actually uses the space — its guest patterns, its members' habits, its daily routines — not a generic/default answer. Every example below is the same underlying discipline applied to a different decision.

## Worked examples

- **Kitchen/living-room combination**: decided by guest frequency and overnight-guest duration, not defaulted. A household with a mother-in-law who sometimes stays a month kept the living room separate and closable as a guest bedroom. A household that rarely has overnight guests combined kitchen+living for more usable everyday space.
- **Windowsill-height desk**: must be verified per-apartment (windowsill height varies unit to unit) and must be paired with blackout/opaque blinds regardless of orientation — prolonged facing of bright/direct light is described as a near-certain eyesight risk, worse on a sun-facing exposure.
- **Washing-machine placement**: should allow laundry access without entering the bathroom/WC when household laundry frequency is expected to be high (worked example: a two-child household).
- **Shower vs. tub**: prioritize the *primary* occupants' stated preference over a guest's, decided via direct client conversation rather than a default layout. Two real examples: a couple who shower together, chosen over a mother-in-law's tub preference; an elderly couple's difficulty climbing into a tub led to a shower stall with a fold-down seat instead.
- **Closet/storage allocation**: sized by actual use case, not one generic closet — separate guest-vs-resident storage; gendered/by-gear-type split closets (a multi-athlete household); one large dedicated pantry sized so infrequently-used items stay off display but remain quickly retrievable.
- **"The most expensive thing in an apartment isn't expensive materials, it's air"**: an open, low-per-m²-efficiency floor plan is framed as the real cost premium being paid for, not finish quality. The speaker explicitly scopes this to one client profile (a single, well-earning occupant) — not general advice.

All items: `confirmed` as the source's own stated framing/decisions, single-account (one designer's five case walkthroughs).

## Petrishin-Stroi (design lead "Клара") — Routine-Walkthrough Technique (added 2026-08-24)

- **Mentally walk through each planned room simulating actual daily
  actions before finalizing socket/outlet placement** — worked example:
  where does a toothbrush charge, where does an electric razor plug in —
  place outlets by real use scenario rather than a generic default
  layout. Framed as something a self-managed planner can do without a
  designer, though the source notes it's easier with professional help.
- **Bedroom lighting should be planned around the household's actual
  morning/evening routine, not a default fixture list**: a bright
  overhead fixture for waking, indirect cornice/backlighting for evening
  relaxation, a bedside sconce for reading, plus a dimmed low-level
  option — decide the fixture set by what light task is needed at each
  time of day.
- **A child's-room wall mural should target relevance up to roughly age
  10, not the child's current toddler age** (worked example: a
  tropical/bird-theme mural framed as broadly age-appropriate); for an
  older child/teen, choose the mural together with them. A
  molding-bordered mural can be swapped for a new one later without
  redoing the room.

## Petrishin-Stroi-Featured Case — Pet-Ownership-Driven Design Decisions (added 2026-08-24, Round 6)

From a 38 m² secondary-market renovation featured (not executed) by
Петришин-Строй, foreman "Александр":

- **Dual electric towel warmers in one small bathroom, by explicit
  design intent**: the lower unit is dedicated to the household dog's
  towels/cleaning cloths, kept separate from the human towel warmer
  above — a real household-composition-driven fixture decision, not
  redundancy.
- **Under-bed pet nook**: a raised sleeping platform's built-in storage
  base includes a recessed, doghouse-like space underneath for the
  household's dog.
