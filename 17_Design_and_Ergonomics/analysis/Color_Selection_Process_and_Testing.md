# Colour Selection Process & Testing

## Scope

**How to actually arrive at a palette, and how to verify it before committing money to it** — where to start, how to get a palette without learning colour theory, how to handle disagreement inside a household, how window orientation and lighting distort what you chose, and the sampling procedures that catch a bad choice while it is still cheap to change.

The companion page [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Color_Harmony_and_Combination_Rules|Colour Harmony & Combination Rules]] covers what makes colours work *together*; this page covers the process around that decision. [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Neutrals_and_Earth_Tone_Palettes|Neutrals & Earth-Tone Palettes]] covers beige/grey/white specifically.

Created 2026-08-30 from a 16-video batch dispatched on combining colours in interior design.

## Where to Start: Not With the Wall Paint

Every process-oriented source in this batch independently says the same thing — **choosing the wall colour first is the mistake.**

- **⚠️ Start from an "якорный предмет" (anchor object) — PRIDE / Алёна.** "Профессионалы почти никогда не начинают с выбора цвета стен. Они начинают именно с якорного предмета." Defined as **an object that already contains a good combination of several colours** — most often a rug, a decorative cushion, patterned textile, a favourite armchair, or a painting you genuinely love. Derive the palette from that. Her diagnosis of why Pinterest copying fails: people pick the wall colour first, "примерно как вы выбираете обувь ещё до момента, когда вы решили, куда вы по итогу пойдёте." [source: [[_Sources/YT_S9F9TGVG4x8_pride_60_30_10_anchor_object|YT_S9F9TGVG4x8]]]

> [!NOTE]
> **Three unrelated channels have now arrived at this same starting move.** PRIDE's anchor object, **Anuta Vlady's** "прототип" method (derive a palette from a patterned object — [[_Sources/YT_LRtedmEn8mQ_anutavlady_3_color_selection_methods|YT_LRtedmEn8mQ]]), and the derive-the-wall-colour-from-a-fixed-material technique already on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Whole_Apartment_Coherence|Whole-Apartment Coherence]] (Anuta Vlady's fireplace-stone eyedropper case) are the same mechanism at three scales. This is one of the better-corroborated process claims in the vault.

- **⚠️ Ask about atmosphere before style, and style before colour — Надежда Кузина.** She states she does not open with "какой цвет вам нравится." She reads the client's references to infer style and gamut, then interviews in the order **atmosphere → style → the specific colour**. Her stated reason: the same blue "рассказывает совершенно разные истории" depending on what surrounds it, so the colour is the *last* decision, not the first. [source: [[_Sources/YT_gEhVxuxtOjc_kuzina_why_you_cant_choose_a_color|YT_gEhVxuxtOjc]]]

- **⚠️ Pick the main colour before picking a scheme — Artcolor.Online / Julia Omelchenko.** Before choosing any harmony, nominate the "основной цвет": the one that will definitely be present and will most likely dominate. [source: [[_Sources/YT_ZY_Xp8b_G5M_artcolor_color_harmony_schemes|YT_ZY_Xp8b_G5M]]]

## The Ready-Made-Palette Shortcut

**ARCHWOOD / Марина Измайлова** makes this recommendation twice, three years apart, and flags that it goes against her own profession's instinct ("сейчас дизайнеры будут, конечно, меня ругать"):

> If you need to choose colours **once**, for your own current renovation, take a ready-made harmonious palette rather than learning colouristics. "Если нужно быстро и однократно, берите готовую палитру, приятную глазу, и всё."

- **Mechanism**: services exist that ingest any interior photo, painting or image and extract its colours, which can then be converted to real tinting codes — **RAL** named. "Не один клик, но задача простая."
- **⚠️ A structural fact about ready-made palettes worth knowing, and checkable**: almost all of them are built on **opposing wheel positions** — forming a square or a triangle — typically pairing a nominally cool hue (blue, green, or derivatives) against an opposing warm/dark family, **plus intermediate tones diluted with white and one neutral (white, grey or beige)**. This connects the shortcut back to the formal schemes without the user needing to know them.
- She is explicit that the theory is worth learning for its own sake — the shortcut is scoped to the one-off case.

[sources: [[_Sources/YT_AKxOtRhIcJY_archwood_color_qa_orientation_ceilings|YT_AKxOtRhIcJY]], [[_Sources/YT__Vw-eaRekDk_archwood_three_approaches_main_color|YT__Vw-eaRekDk]]]

**A dissenting pedagogy worth noting**: **Vittoria Selina** teaches colour deliberately without the colour wheel or complementary theory at all — "зачем вам нужно знать эту теорию, когда вам нужно просто выбрать какой-то оттенок и покрасить им стены." She works entirely from demonstrated pairings and physical samples. Not a contradiction of ARCHWOOD so much as the same conclusion pushed further. [source: [[_Sources/YT_jJ05zqugmSc_vittoria_selina_beige_combinations|YT_jJ05zqugmSc]]]

## Selecting the Palette Is the Easy Half — Distribution Is the Hard Half

This is the single most emphasised process point in the batch, stated by the same practitioner in both her colour videos and demonstrated with a worked example.

- **⚠️ ARCHWOOD / Марина Измайлова**: "Подобрать цвета — огромное дело, но это далеко не всё и не гарантирует гармоничный интерьер. **Вторая задача — определить, какой из этих цветов сделать основным, один или несколько, и в каких пропорциях распределить по помещениям.**" If a palette contains terracotta, whether that becomes a side table, a vase, a whole wall, or a wall *and* ceiling is "разница огромная."
- **⚠️ Her worked example, one palette → two opposite interiors.** Take a blue + terracotta palette:
  - For a **calm, light** room: the neutral intermediates (white, grey, light beige) take **60–70% of the wall and floor volume**, and the saturated members appear only as accents — light walls, one large blue volume, bright terracotta accents.
  - For a **bold, maximally contrasting** room: the saturated opposites become the base (a terracotta element against a dark wall) and the neutral is **minimised to the floor alone**.
  - Both stay harmonious because neither leaves the palette. "Здесь вопрос атмосферы — что лично вы хотите получить в итоге."
- **⚠️ The volume decision is independent of design training** — an unusually candid claim: "этот выбор вообще никак не связан с опытом и образованием в дизайне или архитектуре. Даже если архитектор или дизайнер делает интерьер себе, он задаётся ровно теми же вопросами: сделать смелее или так, чтобы не надоест." Her own worked counter-example, against her own taste: she rejected a dusty burgundy ceiling she genuinely liked for her own home, because she knew she would tire of it — "он бы очень прикольно смотрелся здесь в обзоре интерьера, но я знаю, что он быстро надоест."

[sources: [[_Sources/YT__Vw-eaRekDk_archwood_three_approaches_main_color|YT__Vw-eaRekDk]], [[_Sources/YT_AKxOtRhIcJY_archwood_color_qa_orientation_ceilings|YT_AKxOtRhIcJY]]]

## Household Disagreement: Why Most Palettes End Up Neutral

**ARCHWOOD / Марина Измайлова**, from observing dozens of the bureau's own projects — a real practitioner sample, though not a study:

- **⚠️ Clients living alone far more often choose bold or unusual combinations.** It is much rarer for a couple's taste to coincide on something bold, or for one partner to have convinced the other; sometimes the second spouse simply doesn't care.
- **⚠️ The consequence, stated plainly**: "чаще необходимость найти компромисс в семье — это основная причина выбора нейтрального оттенка в качестве основного." Larger families, children of different ages, or several generations in one space converge on **a base that at minimum irritates nobody, with accents added on top.** A neutral natural-toned gamut is convenient because almost everything combines with it — **and because each family member can then add their own preferred colour on their own personal territory.**
- **⚠️ Stated process order when opinions differ**: find a shared style (or an intersection of styles) **and** a shared gamut first; only then decide whether the interior is built on light tones, dark tones, bold colour, or small bursts. **"Нужно общее техническое задание, прежде чем идти в детали"** — because if one person disagrees, the work cannot proceed at all.
- **Reversibility as a design criterion**: a bright throw or pouffe that palls is far easier to swap than finishes. "Чем больше возможностей легко изменить интерьер, тем дольше он проживёт, если правильно собран с точки зрения композиции." (The same argument appears from **ГК «Авальремонт»** — a neutral base means accents can be swapped seasonally without touching finishes — and from **Студия Wall Street** for a grey monochrome.)
- **A professional duty she states explicitly**: an architect should warn a client about the role of colour and materials before a serious investment, analogised to a good plastic surgeon declining further work at some point.

[source: [[_Sources/YT__Vw-eaRekDk_archwood_three_approaches_main_color|YT__Vw-eaRekDk]]]

**Directly relevant to this project**, which has two adults and two young children and an unfilled `00_Master/Design_Concept.md`. This is general practitioner knowledge, not a decision — but the "shared TZ before details" sequencing is the concrete process step it implies.

## Why a Technically Correct Palette Still Gets Rejected

**Надежда Кузина** gives the batch's only real account of *why* colour choice paralyses people, and it is the best-sourced argument in the set — it cites named outside authorities rather than asserting.

- **⚠️ The buyer is solving two problems at once and only recognises one.** A *rational* task (compatibility, lighting, the rules — hence mood boards, выкрасы, reference piles) and a *cultural* one, which is the actual source of the paralysis. Symptom: looking at an already-approved palette and still feeling "что-то не так."
- **⚠️ Two physically related colours can read as different worlds.** A terracotta interior and a cool-leaning-red one are "родственники внутри одной цветовой семьи," but the first reads warm/ecological/nostalgic and the second conceptual/active/aggressive. The reaction is to the association, not the wavelength.
- **⚠️ Harmony rules are not physical constants — the historical argument, attributed to colour historian Michel Pastoureau.** "Цвет — явление прежде всего социальное." In the Middle Ages the commonest clothing pairing was **red + green**, perceived as almost *monochrome* rather than contrasting, while **yellow + green** read as hellish kitsch fit for a jester or for forcibly dressing a criminal. Today those readings are reversed, because the operative model shifted from Aristotle's colour sequence to Newton's physics and Itten's wheel. `unverified` against the primary source, but a concrete and checkable counterexample to the assumption that the wheel is objective.
- **⚠️ Three mechanisms by which culture enters an interior**, offered as her working model:
  1. **"Образы среды"** — a colour cites a kind of place. Green → an old library; whitewashed lime → the Mediterranean; red-with-gold → palace aesthetics or China; white-with-gold → classicism; light wood + white → Scandinavia. Her own project: a brief for "простота и нейтральность" delivered almost entirely through oak-tone wood plus white, reading unmistakably as сканди/эко purely from the colour decision.
  2. **"Социальные коды"**, citing Bourdieu — "вкус классифицирует не только вещи, но и того, кто их выбирает." Named market observation: Armani Casa, Fendi Casa and Gianfranco Ferré Casa all build on the same family of complex neutrals (warm greys, grey-beiges, lime plasters, muted natural stone). **Her stated mechanism is exposure, not physiology**: "дело не в том, что человеческий глаз физиологически предпочитает серо-бежевый цвет" — those shades simply recur in luxury visual culture, while simple saturated colours recur in advertising, packaging, mass retail and children's goods. **Named trap: a status palette is not automatically a personal one.**
  3. **"Личная память"** — the strongest of the three. The same olive: a trip through Tuscany for one person, a district clinic's walls for another, a grandfather's jacket for a third.
- **Closing position**: "здесь нет правильного цвета. Есть только цвет, который поддерживает именно вашу историю."

[source: [[_Sources/YT_gEhVxuxtOjc_kuzina_why_you_cant_choose_a_color|YT_gEhVxuxtOjc]]]

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **This puts Кузина in direct conflict with the per-hue colour-psychology framing already on this vault.** She states she treats universal advice such as "оливковый успокаивает," "оранжевый бодрит," "бежевый делает интерьер дорогим" **with caution**, because the same colour produces different emotions in different people for biographical rather than perceptual reasons. See the Perspectives block on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Color_Palette_and_Material_Direction|Colour Palette & Material Direction]], where the Kodolov per-hue table lives.

## Window Orientation and Lighting

**ARCHWOOD / Марина Измайлова** gives the only full orientation table in the vault. Stated on the assumption that nothing outside the window is reflecting into the room — she notes that a tree or a brightly-coloured neighbouring building will override all of this.

| Orientation | What the light does | What to do about it |
|---|---|---|
| **North** | "Нечто вроде серого фильтра" — any colour, including white, reads greyer or dirtier. Notably, **distorts colours the least overall.** | **Choose a shade slightly warmer than the one you actually want.** For white walls use a warm or milky white; replace grey with grey-beige or terracotta. Warm or cool gamut both workable — a taste question. |
| **East** | Pink cast in the morning, slight green at midday. **The largest distortion of any orientation, because the light changes so much across the day.** | Blues and greens distort least. **The riskiest choices are complex grey-beiges**, which can shift substantially. If that worries you, use clearer simpler colours — white, grey, серо-голубой. |
| **West** | Evening sun gives an orange cast. | Warm shades (beige, terracotta) distort least; cool colours and cool greys shift somewhat. |
| **South** | A warm yellow filter lies over everything. | Complex deep shades, mostly cool ones, work well. When picking a complex neutral, **choose it slightly cooler than the one you want** — a cool grey will read warmer than its sample. |

[source: [[_Sources/YT_AKxOtRhIcJY_archwood_color_qa_orientation_ceilings|YT_AKxOtRhIcJY]]]

Corroborating and extending:

- **PRIDE / Алёна** names the same failure as one of four common mistakes, with a concrete instance: **grey drifts bluish under cool light and toward beige or even a pinkish undertone under warm light.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_S9F9TGVG4x8_pride_60_30_10_anchor_object|YT_S9F9TGVG4x8]]]
- **Школа дизайна Маши Черной**: a calm light beige under bright yellow lighting goes emphatically yellow and "может убить всю актуальность интерьера"; a cool grey under cool lighting reads sharper still, "и про уют нам придётся забыть." [source: [[_Sources/YT_zF1YLMqIvNw_masha_chernaya_beige_vs_gray|YT_zF1YLMqIvNw]]]
- **ГК «Авальремонт»**: a cool white under daylight turns the room office-like; the same cool white under warm artificial light produces "желтоватый хаос." [source: [[_Sources/YT_2__5Y0sLAxo_avalremont_10_color_lifehacks|YT_2__5Y0sLAxo]]]

**Undertone stability as a selection axis in its own right.** A US paint-listicle source (Best Color Design, Sherwin-Williams, `unverified`, probable AI narration — the specific products are irrelevant here since the brand is not distributed in Belarus) makes one transferable structural point: some neutrals are **stable** across lighting because they don't lean strongly yellow or grey, while others are **deliberately unstable and chosen for it** — one shade is described as reading warm and creamy in bright south-facing rooms and staying soft without going flat or grey in cool north-facing ones. **The claim is that stability-under-changing-light is a separate axis from lightness**, so a room whose light shifts dramatically across the day is a reason to pick deliberately for one or the other. [source: [[_Sources/YT_D9NZROUB3ZI_bestcolordesign_sherwin_williams_neutrals_lrv|YT_D9NZROUB3ZI]]]

## LRV: A Numeric Handle on Lightness

Every Russian-language source in this batch discusses lightness qualitatively — светлота, «на тон светлее», «растяжка по светлоте». **LRV (Light Reflectance Value)** is the published number that makes the same property comparable across shades before any physical sample exists, on a scale where higher means more light reflected. From the Sherwin-Williams listicle source, with the specific products deliberately not recorded as recommendations:

| Band | Behaviour described |
|---|---|
| **~80+** | Reflects a lot of light without harshness; the "soft white" band |
| **~66–76** | Light but not white; used for whole-house schemes with room-to-room flow |
| **~40–58** | Mid-tones giving walls presence without weight |
| **~8–29** | Deep neutrals for accent walls, built-ins, dens, exteriors |

`unverified`, US-brand-sourced, non-practitioner channel. Recorded because the *concept* is a genuine gap-filler and transfers to any manufacturer that publishes LRV; the eleven specific values quoted matched the manufacturer's published figures as far as could be checked, but should be read from the current catalogue before specifying anything. [source: [[_Sources/YT_D9NZROUB3ZI_bestcolordesign_sherwin_williams_neutrals_lrv|YT_D9NZROUB3ZI]]]

## Testing Before You Commit

The most strongly corroborated procedural content in the batch — **four independent channels** say some version of "test it physically, on site."

- **⚠️ Выкрасы are mandatory even when you already have the colour code — ARCHWOOD / Марина Измайлова.** "Даже если вы знаете номер цвета, обязательно нужно делать выкрасы." Small paint test patches on the wall, compared against the reference **only after drying**. Her stated reason from repeat experience across sites: a colour already proven on other objects can still distort, from something as ordinary as trees or a brightly-coloured neighbouring building outside the window. [source: [[_Sources/YT_AKxOtRhIcJY_archwood_color_qa_orientation_ceilings|YT_AKxOtRhIcJY]]]

- **⚠️ The fan-deck lightness correction — Школа дизайна Маши Черной.** The most specific, most actionable rule in the batch, and one this vault did not have. A tinting fan deck is judged on a very small chip **held perpendicular to the light source**, so light falls on it fully and the shade reads brighter and fresher than it will in place. Paint a whole wall in that same shade, with light falling *along* rather than onto it, and **"оттенок на стене будет намного намного темнее и насыщенней."**
  > **Her prescription: when choosing from a fan deck, pick one — or even two — tones *lighter* than the beige or grey that appealed to you.** The finished wall will then be the tone you wanted.

  She adds that rougher surfaces read more saturated and darker still, so texture is a further correction at selection time. [source: [[_Sources/YT_zF1YLMqIvNw_masha_chernaya_beige_vs_gray|YT_zF1YLMqIvNw]]]

- **⚠️ Physically juxtapose every sample — don't judge them separately. Школа дизайна Маши Черной.** Named failure mode: beige, grey, a wood finish and a kitchen front each look fine viewed alone, "но стоит приложить их друг к другу, и какой-то из образцов будет явно выбиваться, уходить в розовый, в зелёный, будет просто слишком тёплым или слишком тёмным." **Instruction: bring together everything that can be anticipated — door finishes, kitchen, furniture order, wall выкрасы, flooring, skirting — and at minimum compare the samples side by side.** This is the concrete procedure that catches the подтон error Кузина warns about on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Neutrals_and_Earth_Tone_Palettes|Neutrals & Earth-Tone Palettes]]. [source: [[_Sources/YT_zF1YLMqIvNw_masha_chernaya_beige_vs_gray|YT_zF1YLMqIvNw]]]

- **A cheaper выкрас variant — Vittoria Selina.** Tape a sheet of **ватман** to the wall with masking tape, paint the ватман rather than the wall, then look at it **morning, midday and evening, and in different weather.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_jJ05zqugmSc_vittoria_selina_beige_combinations|YT_jJ05zqugmSc]]]

- **⚠️ Never judge wall paint before the interior is assembled — Vittoria Selina, with a real client case.** A client visited during painting, saw the first or second coat of a deep pink on bare walls, judged it too bright, and had it changed to a paler pink. Her position: "никогда не оценивайте краску на стене, когда ещё нету готового интерьера — так нельзя, это могут делать только профессионалы," because the professional is extrapolating to how it will sit with the furniture, wallpaper and decor. A concrete and expensive failure mode; she reports the client's decision as final and respected. **Note this sits in mild tension with the выкрас rule above** — выкрасы are for catching a *lighting or undertone* error early; this is a caution against making the *final aesthetic* judgment from a bare-wall coat. [source: [[_Sources/YT_jJ05zqugmSc_vittoria_selina_beige_combinations|YT_jJ05zqugmSc]]]

- **ГК «Авальремонт»** independently prescribes the same: paint a patch of the actual wall and look at it morning, midday and evening. [source: [[_Sources/YT_2__5Y0sLAxo_avalremont_10_color_lifehacks|YT_2__5Y0sLAxo]]]

## The Same Paint Code on a Wall and on a Cabinet Front Will Not Match

**Мария Шеврина / SMBUREAU** — a new mechanism for this page, and one backed by a controlled experiment on their own site.

The intuition is obvious and wrong: pick one NCS or RAL number, use it for both the walls and the kitchen or wardrobe fronts, and they will match. **They will visibly differ.**

> **Their experiment.** On a site where the walls were already painted to a specific NCS article, they had their joinery shop produce a perforated radiator panel to *exactly the same NCS number*. The panel came out visibly pinker than the wall.

**Her stated mechanism, in three parts**: the paint formulations differ (what goes onto MDF is not what goes onto a wall, so the same colour recipe yields a different film); the substrate differs; and the application method differs entirely.

**⚠️ The fix is counter-intuitive and is the practical payoff: to achieve a tone-on-tone match, deliberately specify *different* shades for the wall and the front.** That is how they got the matched result they show. The general rule attached: always do выкрасы **and** commission a front sample — don't skip the sample stage on joinery.

She restates the same claim independently in her white-paint video, where she reports repeatedly receiving messages from people who painted walls and fronts to one code and got an obvious mismatch, the fronts reading as though "заляпанные, им уже 15 лет" — and notes it is visible to anyone, not only a perfectionist.

[sources: [[_Sources/YT_avRNMkNdOBs_shevrina_top7_renovation_mistakes|YT_avRNMkNdOBs]], [[_Sources/YT_b5oeFxmaubI_shevrina_no_perfect_white|YT_b5oeFxmaubI]]]

### The remedy for the wall-vs-front mismatch, and what it costs

The section above establishes that walls and cabinet fronts painted to the same code will not match. **A later source from the same studio supplies the practical answer**, which that video left open:

- **⚠️ Facade colour samples must be real MDF-in-enamel front samples, not paint on paper.** "Нельзя делать на фасады выкрасы чисто на листочке… МДФ другой материал, эмаль это другой материал, это не просто краской покрасить." A выкрас on a sheet tells you nothing about how the same code behaves on a sprayed MDF front.
- **⚠️ A front sample costs about 3,000 RUB (≈$50)** from a joinery shop — cheap enough to order several and compare them on the actual site. *(5b: `USD/RUB` trailing 6-month mean before the 2023-03-18 publish date, 2022-09-20..2023-03-18, 121 samples, 66.1279 RUB/USD; rounded to the nearest 10. **Region unresolved at level 1**, so the standard comparability caveat applies to the absolute figure — the useful part is that it is a small, repeatable, per-unit cost.)*
- **⚠️ Sequencing rule: let the less flexible material constrain the more flexible one.** On a live project they went to look at acrylic and quartz slabs **first**, chose the stone, and only then **eliminated the too-light NCS enamel shades** and ordered front samples in the survivors. Stone comes in fixed slabs; enamel can be tinted to anything — so the enamel adapts to the stone, never the reverse. Their shortlist ended up within two or three tones of each other.

[source: [[_Sources/YT_KI2GvB0jzHs_shevrina_podcast01_kitchen_material_procurement|YT_KI2GvB0jzHs]]]

## There Is No "Perfect White" Article Number

**Мария Шеврина / SMBUREAU**, and this is a direct challenge to the genre of naming shades.

- **⚠️ Their studio has never reused the same paint article on two objects.** Not once. The finished results read as the same warm white; the articles behind them are entirely different.
- **⚠️ The demonstration**: take the paint used on object A and on object B, make two выкрасы, and view them side by side under identical conditions. **They differ visibly in lightness, in saturation, and in pigment** — one may read distinctly pink, the other greyish or even greenish.
- **⚠️ Her named failure case**: she found a warm white she loved — **Little Greene "Flint"** — and carried it to the next project, where it came out "настолько отвратительно грязно-серовато-зеленоватым," with none of the warmth that made her choose it.
- **What makes a shade unrepeatable**, in her list: floor area; room configuration (elongated with one window vs. elongated with five along a wall); the storey; the glazing itself; the view; the buildings opposite; window size and count; and the other shades already present in the flat. She notes the list is incomplete.
- **⚠️ The method: выкрасы, expecting two iterations and sometimes three.** Buy a first batch of sample pots, paint them, assess, **correct on what you actually see**, buy a second batch. Correcting intelligently requires understanding pigment, saturation and lightness — which is why she treats white as genuinely difficult rather than a safe default. She is explicit that designers go through the same iterations, and not from inexperience.

[source: [[_Sources/YT_b5oeFxmaubI_shevrina_no_perfect_white|YT_b5oeFxmaubI]]]

### RAL is the wrong fan deck for wall colour

Same source, and a specific correction to a common assumption:

- **RAL is "самый ужасный, самый маленький веер, который только может быть"** for this purpose — it is intended for painting plastic and metal constructions, her example being the aluminium profile of a partition. She notes that nearly every question she receives is phrased as "what RAL is on the walls," and that designers rarely pick up a RAL deck for wall colour at all.
- **In ~99% of cases the working reference is a paint manufacturer's own fan deck** — shades devised by the paint-makers' own designers. She has used an NCS deck once, to find a particular grey. **This independently corroborates ARCHWOOD's "сложные цвета" argument** on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Neutrals_and_Earth_Tone_Palettes|Neutrals & Earth-Tone Palettes]]: take a ready-made complex shade from a manufacturer's collection rather than tinting to a code from a simplified catalogue.
- **And the reason people reach for RAL is itself the mistake** — they want one code they can reuse for cabinet fronts later, which is exactly the разнотон trap above.

[source: [[_Sources/YT_b5oeFxmaubI_shevrina_no_perfect_white|YT_b5oeFxmaubI]]]

### A lighting caveat on colour selection

**Мария Шеврина / SMBUREAU** adds two points from the lighting side. **Neutral (4000 K) main light does not distort the flat's colours**, which makes colour selection easier and avoids pushing already-warm walls further yellow when the evening light goes on — a reason to prefer neutral over warm for the *main* light. And, cutting the other way, she argues **the influence of a cornice LED strip on wall-colour choice is overstated** and that people over-weight it when picking paint. [source: [[_Sources/YT_NfHyCfo1J4w_shevrina_lighting_mistakes|YT_NfHyCfo1J4w]]]

## Wood as a Palette Member

**ARCHWOOD / Марина Измайлова** gives a named, explicitly non-scientific but workable technique for the question "does this wood tone fit my palette":

> Mentally — or in Photoshop — **strip the texture from the wood**: the rings, the fibres, the grain that make you recognise it as wood. Treat the flat residual tone as an ordinary colour.

Then either:
- **Forward**: look at your chosen palette, decide which member the wood *would* be (usually the light or mid beige), and pick a wood of that tone.
- **Backward**: take the tone of wood you already own, lay it against the palette, and if it clearly doesn't belong — "видим, что он совсем не отсюда" — change the palette.
- To fit a darker species (тёмный дуб, орех), **add a brown shade on the warm side and widen the palette accordingly.**

[source: [[_Sources/YT_AKxOtRhIcJY_archwood_color_qa_orientation_ceilings|YT_AKxOtRhIcJY]]]

## Composition Self-Checks

Two techniques from **ARCHWOOD / Марина Измайлова**, applicable to any element being chosen (kitchen, curtains, sofa). She notes that composition is what most of an architectural education is actually spent on, and that it is the correct starting point:

1. **Simplify the whole interior to plain rectangles and squares** in your head, then look at whether everything sits where it should, and try moving elements around.
2. **Convert the view to black-and-white**, because visual weight and the sense of colour read differently without hue.

[source: [[_Sources/YT_AKxOtRhIcJY_archwood_color_qa_orientation_ceilings|YT_AKxOtRhIcJY]]]

And one from **Надежда Кузина**, for testing whether a candidate colour belongs to a palette at all — the **"случайный гость" test**: picture the palette's characteristic materials lying together, mentally place the candidate next to them, and ask whether it looks like it was always part of that company or like a chance visitor. Stated in the context of earthy palettes but general in form. Full version on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Neutrals_and_Earth_Tone_Palettes|Neutrals & Earth-Tone Palettes]]. [source: [[_Sources/YT_n5ZBqdq0wH8_kuzina_earthy_palette_practice|YT_n5ZBqdq0wH8]]]

## Applying It Without Repainting

**PRIDE / Алёна** — a low-cost intervention path for a finished renovation. If the walls are already done, **the existing walls simply *are* the 60%**; assemble the 30% through textiles, furniture and wood, and the 10% through decor, lighting, cushions and throws. "Иногда в целом достаточно поменять просто несколько наволочек, добавить какой-нибудь один плед, заменить картину."

Her stated action plan: pick one room (the one you spend most time in, not the whole apartment), find the anchor object, name three colours in the three roles, then audit what you already have — often the walls and furniture already are the 60% and 30% and only the 10% is missing.

**One diagnostic caution attached to this, worth keeping**: before reaching for colour at all, check whether the problem is actually colour. "Иногда люди думают, что интерьер немножечко скучноватый, и начинают срочно добавлять цветные вещи. Но на деле комнате может просто не хватать глубины, фактур, света, или более выразительных форм." [source: [[_Sources/YT_S9F9TGVG4x8_pride_60_30_10_anchor_object|YT_S9F9TGVG4x8]]]

**ГК «Авальремонт»** makes the same point about scale-blind copying more vividly: copying a Pinterest interior without accounting for your own lighting and floor area means "то, что круто смотрится в 200 м², в твоих сорока превращается в грустный подвал." [source: [[_Sources/YT_2__5Y0sLAxo_avalremont_10_color_lifehacks|YT_2__5Y0sLAxo]]]

## A Complete Working Method for Choosing Paint (Надежда Кузина, added 2026-09-01, Round 4)

**⚠️ The most executable content routed to this page.** Four sources from one round; the paint-selection source is a full procedure rather than a set of principles, and it becomes this page's backbone.

### Why in-store advice never answers the question

**Salespeople are usually competent but never recommend a specific colour in the abstract — "потому что если вам вдруг потом не понравится этот цвет на стене, то им будут выслушивать ваши претензии."** So they talk about opacity, consumption, application and storage instead. **The advice is real; it is simply never about the decision.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_pFCVO7AuSMw_kuzina_choosing_wall_paint|pFCVO7AuSMw]]]

### Before the shop

- **Fix the effect you want from the whole interior first** — wall colour strongly affects the mood of the entire space. Decide the broad class: neutral or bright, light or dark.
- **⚠️ Better: arrive with an abstract colour *name* already fixed** — *"это должен быть лиловый интерьер"* — so you look only at lilacs and stop registering the rest.
- **⚠️ Never select paint abstractly, and never start material selection with paint. Bring a floor sample, and ideally the door colours. Choose the specific option together with the paint.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_pFCVO7AuSMw_kuzina_choosing_wall_paint|pFCVO7AuSMw]]]
- **⚠️ The sequencing rule stated in full from a second source: on site walls are finished first and furniture arrives later, but in *design* the order inverts — furniture, upholstery, finishes and rugs FIRST, wall paint LAST. The reason is supply-side: "спектр вариантов краски для стен всегда будет больше, чем любой набор обивок."** Paint is the most freely matchable element, so it should absorb the constraint. [source: [[_Sources/YT_ocoPRS3d8sA_kuzina_colour_mistakes|ocoPRS3d8sA]]]

### ⚠️ Choose a supplier with FEWER colours

- The only case where thousands help is an exact match to something you already own — **and then the choice is not hard, because you know what you want.**
- **Otherwise prefer a small palette, "потому что глаза не так разбегаются."** Her working catalogues run **156** and **~160** colours and she calls that ample; if something is genuinely missing, move to the next supplier and close the question.
- **⚠️ The reason is physiological — the eye tires of colour as other senses tire under load.** Her analogy: by the fourth perfume you have stopped smelling anything.
- **⚠️ The scheduling consequence for a whole flat: look at the colours for ALL rooms in one session.** One room per visit across several days **does not work.** **The method: look at everything and shortlist, come back the next day, take a break, return and look again — "иначе глаз замыливается очень быстро."**

### ⚠️ The order of choosing, and how many samples

- **If the main colour (the one covering most area) is pronounced: choose it first, then the accents.**
- **⚠️ If the main colour is neutral and should recede as far as possible: choose the bright accents FIRST and fit the background to them.**
- **⚠️ Order three shades, not one — the chosen paint plus the tones one step lighter and one step darker. At home the paint can look nothing like it did in the shop, and choosing from a *set* pins it down.** **Three variants for every colour you need**, including the moulding if that is different again.

### ⚠️ The tester procedure

**Never choose from the fan deck** — a small chip cannot tell you how a wall reads at scale.

- **Paint testers onto hardboard or plasterboard**, or onto a piece of the paintable wallpaper if that is what goes up.
- **⚠️ No smaller than A3** — below that you lose the sense of the colour's brightness.
- **⚠️ Do NOT paint testers on the wall. They must be mobile so you can carry them round the room.**
- **⚠️ Never leave a margin of white paper or backing showing around the paint — especially the green face of plasterboard. It strongly distorts perception.**

**Where to hold each tester:** at **ceiling level** (**and on the ceiling itself if the paint runs up there — the colour reads much darker there**); at **eye level**; at **floor level**, **since the floors are often already laid by this point, so you can check the interaction directly**; on **the wall opposite the window**; on **the side walls**; and **in shadow** — under the window and in corners. **⚠️ That last one especially for a near-white with an undertone: a faintly greenish tint shows up most in the corners.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_pFCVO7AuSMw_kuzina_choosing_wall_paint|pFCVO7AuSMw]]]

### ⚠️ Ignoring accidental context

- **We perceive colour inside a context, and it will not be the context of your interior.** **Her proof: Photoshop and other graphics programs use a grey background specifically so it does not interfere with perception.**
- Worked: a grey among lilacs and aubergines reads as having pink in it. **Alone it may not — and in a room with an orange armchair against grey walls the orange may cancel the pink entirely.**
- **⚠️ So look at one colour at a time**: carry a single chip, or **cut a hole in a sheet of paper and look through it.** **The flaw: white paper is itself very bright, and a white like paper practically never occurs in an interior** — one manufacturer's viewing card is deliberately neutral beige instead.
- **⚠️ The same problem on site: pre-painting walls are white gypsum filler, and against that background neutrals look far dirtier than they will once the white is gone.**

### ⚠️ Testing under real light

- **"Даже один цвет в разном освещении может дать гораздо больше разницы в тоне, чем два соседних цвета."** Agonising in the shop between two near-identical colours is the wrong problem.
- **⚠️ Test in the room that will be painted** — a west-facing and a north-facing room have different light. Do not judge every tester in the living room.
- **⚠️ And at different times of day.** Weather matters too; **some rooms change by season** — a tree outside a window throws green reflexes in summer and none in winter. **Nobody waits for the seasons, but check the times of day.**
- **⚠️ The artificial-light problem, and the best fix in the source: on site there is usually a builder's bulb of unknown colour temperature. Use a variable-temperature LED panel — the common photographer's product — set to the temperature your lighting will actually be, e.g. 3000 K, and hold it to the sample.** Dedicated sample-testing lamps exist, and some manufacturers supply **three-dimensional room samples** showing the paint opposite the window, on side walls and on the ceiling.
- **⚠️ Reflexes: neighbouring surfaces reflect light onto the paint too** — most relevant for whitish, greyish and light neutrals.

### ⚠️ Three procedural rules from the mistakes source

[source: [[_Sources/YT_ocoPRS3d8sA_kuzina_colour_mistakes|ocoPRS3d8sA]]]

- **⚠️ The A3 trap, stated from the other direction: A3 is not large relative to a room, so impressions still differ once the whole room is painted.** She says this is normal — **some designers repaint several times.** **The fix: buy the paint slightly less bright than the result you want.** *(Read together with the A3 minimum above: A3 is the floor, not the target.)*
- **⚠️ Never choose colour from memory.** Her worked case goes to buy upholstery without bringing the wallpaper. **She runs a live test — memorise a palette, then identify it a minute later among four similar options.** *"А если бы прошёл день? А если бы прошла неделя?"*
- **⚠️ Never rely on a colour's name.** «Песочный» wallpaper and a «Песочная» paint swatch are not the same sand. **"Люди, которые придумывают эти названия, придумывают их просто вот так, потому что им надо придумать по 50 названий в день."** **"Названия лучше не читать."**

### ⚠️ The greyscale photograph — her standard working check

**Recorded once here because it recurs across three separate sources in this round, used for three different purposes:**

| Purpose | Source |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Verify a colourless scheme has tonal structure** — it must not read as one grey amorphous mush; you should easily pick out dark, mid and light parts, **and they must not be equal in scale** (an all-dark floor with everything else white will not work) | [source: [[_Sources/YT_ubB1GSFtN20_kuzina_afraid_of_colour|ubB1GSFtN20]]] |
| **Verify shared lightness before mixing warm and cold** — no very bright or very dark patches | [source: [[_Sources/YT_yU2zKPpDghM_kuzina_colour_difficulties|yU2zKPpDghM]]] |
| **Verify two colours are genuinely the same lightness** on a two-tone wall — photographed, it should read almost uniformly grey | [source: [[_Sources/YT_lptrnXyf0as_kuzina_two_colours_one_wall|lptrnXyf0as]]] |

### ⚠️ Adding colour when you are afraid of it — a low-commitment ladder

**The client reasoning she is answering is a *cost* fear**: *"эксперименты — это дорого, поэтому если мы промахнёмся с цветом, потом придётся всё переделывать. Ну нафиг, сделаем всё нейтрально."* **And the tension: the same people do not want a faceless hotel interior.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_ubB1GSFtN20_kuzina_afraid_of_colour|ubB1GSFtN20]]]

1. **Neutral ground with bright accents** — cushions, **coloured piping on curtains and bedspreads, cords, tassels, tiebacks**, coloured lampshades or lamp bases. **⚠️ But an all-beige room with only pink tiebacks looks strange — there must be several such objects.** **⚠️ Choose the coloured items to be the cheap, replaceable ones — that is the direct answer to the cost fear.**
2. **⚠️ Colour dosed into details**: paint the **window reveal**; paint the **end panels of cabinets** while fronts stay neutral; a chair with neutral seat and **bright legs**; a wardrobe with neutral fronts but bright **interior or handles**.
3. **When braver: coloured cornice and skirting** — **but mind how the skirting relates to the door; architraves and skirting are usually one colour, and the door can stay neutral.**
4. **⚠️ «Скрытый цвет» — hidden colour**: behind cabinet doors, inside drawers, or **as a lining on the reverse of a curtain or a folded-back bedspread.**

**Then add gradually** — if the bright handles worked, change the curtains next.

**⚠️ Limit the palette so combination cannot fail: use nuance combinations, colours adjacent on the wheel**, where the chance of clashing is low — greens with blues, several shades of blue alone, oranges with yellow and brown elements. **⚠️ And match the neutral base's temperature against the accents: cool accents → a slightly warmer beige; warm accents → a slightly cooler beige, or a cool grey instead.** **⚠️ Put the bolder experiments in transit zones**, where the colour has less chance to tire you.

## ⚠️ Two Testing Cautions That Contradict the Intuitive Method (Надежда Кузина, added 2026-09-01, Round 5)

- **⚠️ Small bright objects do NOT predict how you will feel about large ones.** Buying a couple of bright vases or cushions, liking them, and concluding you are ready for a bold scheme is wrong — **"однотонные цвета в больших масштабах производят совершенно другой эффект."** **The cheap test is not a scaled-down version of the real one.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_HENjGv0DGjI_kuzina_colour_block|HENjGv0DGjI]]]
- **⚠️ The staged adoption path that replaces it: transit zones first (a picture, curtains), then bathrooms, then bedrooms. "Если вас не устроит колорблок в коридоре, то вы не будете делать его в жилой комнате."** A bathroom curtain works as a first test if it is large enough. **And do not start with a children's room** — *"если он не устраивает вас в гостиной, вряд ли он устроит ваших детей."*
- **⚠️ Complex shades look wrong in a showroom, and this is a property of the format rather than of the shade.** They rarely produce an instant effect, **they do not reveal themselves in a small sample**, and on a stand *"выглядят грязными или какими-то пыльными."* **"Сложная палитра начинает работать только внутри интерьера целиком."** **The retail-economics consequence of this page's own A3-sample and accidental-context rules — and the reason the market stops stocking such shades at all.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_vnLZqiYxY1Q_kuzina_mass_market_palette_economics|vnLZqiYxY1Q]]]
- **⚠️ An attribution for the relativity principle this page already carries unsourced**: she credits **Josef Albers** — *"цвет почти никогда не воспринимается таким, какой он есть на самом деле."* Her worked consequence: **a green kitchen effective in a showroom arrives in a flat with winter light and "выглядит как пластиковая папка для документов."** [source: [[_Sources/YT_vnLZqiYxY1Q_kuzina_mass_market_palette_economics|vnLZqiYxY1Q]]]

[source: [[_Sources/YT_vM7inxJZ-6U_krasnov_color_combination_mistakes|vM7inxJZ-6U]]]

## ⚠️ The Fullest Testing Protocol on This Page, and a Third Cautionary Case (Игорь Краснов, added 2026-09-01, Round 5)

**The densest single source of Round 5, and the canonical, fullest version of the paint-testing protocol already on this page from Round 4's `vM7inxJZ-6U`** — read that entry as a simpler, converging precursor; this one is the fuller version to cite going forward. [source: [[_Sources/YT_98EE-fC-4IY_krasnov_choosing_lasting_color_shade|98EE-fC-4IY]]]

- **⚠️ Protocol, in full**: minimum **5-7 separate paint swatches**, each **50cm×50cm**, painted onto a MOVABLE/PORTABLE panel (a large sheet of wallpaper backing or drywall) rather than necessarily the wall itself — the portability is the specific new detail, since it lets the same swatch be carried and held up in different rooms/orientations. Each swatch must be viewed at **morning, midday, evening, AND under artificial lighting SEPARATELY** — four distinct conditions, not just "day vs. night." Converges closely with this page's existing Кузина protocol above (A3-minimum testers, mobile, viewed at multiple times of day and in different light) — independent convergence from an unrelated channel, not new methodology, but the 50×50cm/5-7-swatch specifics are worth keeping as a second concrete figure alongside Кузина's A3 minimum.
- **⚠️ "Eyes-closed" test — a specific, novel technique, genuinely new for this page**: stand in the actual space, close your eyes for several seconds, then open them and note your immediate/involuntary reaction. If the colour does NOT "shout" or create an immediate sense of visual dominance/discomfort on reopening, it has been correctly chosen as a supportive background tone rather than a demanding foreground element — a specific, reusable subjective self-test distinct from any physical sampling procedure already on this page.
- **⚠️ RAL/catalog desaturation adjustment — a specific, actionable correction**: when starting from a RAL or manufacturer catalog colour as a reference point, reduce its saturation by roughly **10-15%** before applying it to a real living space — a catalog colour's full-saturation value reads too harsh/intense once applied at real room scale. Complements, rather than repeats, this page's existing "RAL is the wrong fan deck for wall colour" finding above (Шеврина) — that entry argues against using RAL at all for wall colour; this one gives a concrete correction for the case where a RAL/catalog value is the only reference point available.
- **⚠️ Four named common colour-selection mistakes, worth recording as a discrete checklist**: (1) painting without a real-light test swatch first; (2) installing light fixtures with the wrong colour temperature AFTER the wall colour has already been chosen, causing a mismatch between the intended colour and how it actually reads under the installed lighting; (3) mismatched warm/cool temperature between wall colour and furniture/material choices — frames the general warm/cool-matching rule (also on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Color_Harmony_and_Combination_Rules|Colour Harmony & Combination Rules]]) specifically as a **sequencing** failure; (4) choosing wall/paint colour only AFTER furniture has already been purchased, rather than the reverse — **independently converges with Кузина's existing "furniture, upholstery, finishes and rugs FIRST, wall paint LAST" sequencing rule above** (`ocoPRS3d8sA`), from an unrelated channel.
- **Pinterest-photo unreliability claim**: roughly half of Pinterest interior photos are meaningfully retouched, the other half shot with camera settings/white-balance choices that measurably alter colour perception — choosing a paint colour purely from a photo reference is fundamentally unreliable. Tag as his own stated observation/estimate, not a cited statistic. Consistent with, though independent of, this page's existing scepticism toward reference photos elsewhere.
- **⚠️ A real, detailed, quantified third cautionary case — the Ostozhenka repaint overrun, alongside Round 3's `DQr8J5rkaPk` and Round 4's `vM7inxJZ-6U` cases**: a 120m² apartment on Ostozhenka Street, Moscow, north-facing. The client chose a "greige" colour purely from a Pinterest photo/catalog reference, without a real in-space test swatch, to save time. Once painted, the colour visibly shifted to a "grey-lilac" tone reading "puffy"/tired, especially under evening lighting; the client reported genuine discomfort living with it for roughly two weeks. The wall was repainted approximately **three times**, each requiring new materials and idle contractor time. Total result: roughly a 2-week schedule delay and **≈300,000 RUB (≈$3,700) in cost overrun** — resolved only once the studio insisted on and performed the 7-swatch, multiple-time-of-day test above.
  - **5b decision**: location (Moscow, named directly) and year (2025, upload date 2025-11-29 confirmed via `yt-dlp`) both clear this project's level-1 price-comparability bar. Converted at the trailing-6-month USD/RUB mean before 2025-11-29 (80.2905 RUB/USD, 130 daily samples via `tools/pricing/currency_converter.py`): 300,000 ÷ 80.2905 = 3,736.43 USD, rounded to the nearest 100 per this project's rounding rule → **≈$3,700**. One of only a handful of priced figures this channel has surfaced across all five rounds — usable for cross-source comparison as a Moscow/2025 turnkey-studio repaint-overrun figure.
- **⚠️ Ownership/accountability principle**: colour decisions should be made by the project's own original architect/designer, not delegated to a contractor, because genuine understanding of the room's light/orientation/material/functional programme is required to choose well. Cites an explicit **~80% error-risk estimate** when colour is chosen by someone other than the project's original designer — tag as his own unverified estimate, not a cited statistic.
