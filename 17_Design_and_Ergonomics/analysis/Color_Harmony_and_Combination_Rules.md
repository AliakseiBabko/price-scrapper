# Colour Harmony & Combination Rules

## Scope

**What actually makes two or more colours work together in a room** — the formal harmony schemes, the non-hue axes (lightness, saturation, temperature, "dustiness") that several practitioners argue matter more than hue, the proportion/hierarchy rules that govern how much of each colour goes where, and the accent-wall and ceiling rules that follow from them.

Companion pages: [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Color_Selection_Process_and_Testing|Colour Selection Process & Testing]] (how to *arrive at* and *verify* a palette — anchor objects, ready-made palettes, выкрасы, orientation, LRV), [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Neutrals_and_Earth_Tone_Palettes|Neutrals & Earth-Tone Palettes]] (beige, grey, whites, earthy palettes specifically), and [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Color_Palette_and_Material_Direction|Colour Palette & Material Direction]] (low-light rooms, colour psychology).

## Perspectives on Record — Is Monochrome Wrong for Interiors?

A genuine, unresolved disagreement surfaced by this batch, worth reading before the rules below.

| Source | Position | Stated reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Artcolor.Online / Julia Omelchenko** (drawing school) | Monochrome is **not recommended for interiors** | Such rooms "плохо воспринимаются человеком и могут вызывать ощущение психологического дискомфорта." Fine for illustration and clothing. |
| **Vittoria Selina** (interior designer/decorator) | An all-one-colour interior is **one of two legitimate paths**, and she teaches it | It fails only when executed as one flat tone. Done with several lightnesses of the colour plus mandatory texture variation, it is "тот самый обнимающий классный интерьер." |
| **Студия Wall Street / Виктор** | Full grey monochrome is the **safest recommendation he gives** | Differentiate by texture (silk, plaster, wood, glass) rather than hue; "его любят архитекторы, дизайнеры отелей." |
| **PRIDE / Алёна** | Monochrome is fine and **the 60/30/10 hierarchy still applies to it** | Via saturation and depth steps rather than hue: light-grey walls 60%, mid-grey textiles 30%, graphite accents 10%. |

**Common ground.** All four agree that *a single flat tone across a room fails*. Artcolor.Online's own analogous-scheme warning is that hues sharing similar lightness and saturation read "монотонной и скучной" — which is precisely the failure the other three prescribe against by varying lightness and texture. **The dispute is narrower than it looks: it is about whether "monochrome" means one hue at one value (all four reject it) or one hue stretched across values and textures (three endorse it, one doesn't address it).**

**Your priority.** Not yet decided. The deciding factor all four point to is the same: whether the scheme carries enough lightness spread and texture variation to give the eye somewhere to go.

## Two Different Answers to "Which Colours Go Together"

The batch splits cleanly into sources that answer by **hue position on the wheel** and sources that answer by **matching on other axes**. They are not in conflict — they answer different halves of the question — but only the second group gives you a test you can apply to two physical samples in your hand.

### Axis 1 — Hue position (the formal schemes)

**Artcolor.Online / Julia Omelchenko** gives the fullest taxonomy, with a per-scheme verdict on interior use. A prerequisite she states before any scheme is chosen: **pick the main colour ("основной цвет") first** — the one that will definitely be present and will most likely dominate.

| Scheme | Structure | Her interior verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Monochrome | One hue, varied brightness/saturation | Not recommended — but see Perspectives above |
| Complementary ("комплементарная") | Two opposite hues | Where formality and energy are wanted — **living rooms** |
| Analogous ("аналоговая") | Neighbouring hues | Where calm and coziness dominate — **bedrooms** |
| Analogous-complementary | Analogous set + one complementary accent | Accent must be the **opposite temperature** to the base |
| Triad | Three equidistant hues | Universal; **don't add further hues, extend with achromatics only** |
| Split-complementary | Base + the two hues flanking its complement | **Most universal, hardest to spoil** — "практически в любой области" |
| Tetrad (square) | Four hues on a square | "В интерьере, прямо скажем, на любителя" |
| Tetrad (rectangle / trapezoid) | Four hues, unequal spacing | Easier to balance than the square because it contains near-neighbour pairs |

Her analogous scheme carries a structural prescription worth pulling out: **one dominant hue, one supporting hue, one accent — and the accent may be an achromatic (white, black, grey)**. Her named failure mode for it is that analogous hues sharing similar lightness *and* saturation read monotonous, so the scheme has to be worked by deliberately varying those two. Beginner advice: skip tetrads entirely; use complementary or split-complementary. [source: [[_Sources/YT_ZY_Xp8b_G5M_artcolor_color_harmony_schemes|YT_ZY_Xp8b_G5M]]]

**Андрей Самарин** reaches the same territory with different vocabulary that is more usable in conversation: **"большой контраст"** (opposite hues — he names red+green, orange+blue, violet+yellow) versus **"малый контраст," also called "подбор на нюансе"** (near-neighbours, narrow gamut). His justification for large contrast is natural precedent — violets with yellow centres, orange irises with blue flecks, a red rose against green foliage — on the argument that natural selection has already filtered for what reads well. [source: [[_Sources/YT_wjNz2wCtG7o_samarin_colouristics_basics|YT_wjNz2wCtG7o]]]

### Axis 2 — Lightness, saturation, temperature, dustiness (the matching rules)

This is where the batch's most immediately usable content sits.

- **⚠️ Match on lightness and/or saturation, not on hue — SHELNAT / Наталья Шелковская's central rule.** She opens by rejecting the permitted-pairs genre outright ("можно сочетать любой цвет" — any colour can go with any other; her demonstration case is a green-and-blue stairwell, a pair most people assume clashes). What actually decides it is that the two colours be **equivalent in lightness ("светлота"), in saturation ("насыщенность"), or in both.** She defines both operationally: lightness is what changes when you add white or black to a pigment; saturation is what changes when you add grey. **The physical proxy: on a paint fan deck, two shades taken from the same row are equal on both axes and will pair; the further you move across rows, the more you risk.** Her worked failure — a soft peach against a dark saturated blue-violet — has the peach reading as "a wall yellowed with age" and the blue-violet reading "practically black." [source: [[_Sources/YT_jOXPHR9Mpek_shelnat_two_color_combination_rules|YT_jOXPHR9Mpek]]]

- **⚠️ Warm pairs with warm, cool with cool — including *inside* what looks like one colour. Anuta Vlady's** worked case is a dark blue sofa (cool) against a light "blue" armchair that is actually turquoise (warm): **"бирюзовый цвет — это синий, в который добавили небольшую каплю жёлтого, от чего он потеплел."** Two separate gradients exist — blue-dark→light-blue, and turquoise-dark→light-turquoise — and the fix is to stay on one. She offers the blunt version as a fallback for anyone who can't yet see the shift: "тёплые оттенки хорошо сочетаются с тёплыми, а холодные с холодными." [source: [[_Sources/YT_PGrxKyLhxp8_anutavlady_typical_color_mistakes|YT_PGrxKyLhxp8]]]

- **⚠️ Clean pairs with clean, dusty with dusty. Anuta Vlady** defines the terms: a **чистый** colour is bright, straight from the tin with nothing added; a **припылённый** colour carries an admixture of grey and is the more complex one. Her worked failure is a child's room in soft dusty warm pinks wrecked by heavy deep violet on the rug and bedspread — "они делают эту комнату очень грубой." Second case: bright orange curtains in a bedroom where every other shade is complex and dusty, fixed by matching them instead to the more complex terracotta-brown already present in the cushions. [source: [[_Sources/YT_PGrxKyLhxp8_anutavlady_typical_color_mistakes|YT_PGrxKyLhxp8]]]

- **⚠️ The same rule stated as the difference between an interior and a Christmas display — Студия Wall Street / Виктор.** Applied to complementary pairs specifically: use the muted versions, never the pure ones. "Если взять ёлочный зелёный и канцелярский красный, будет просто новогодняя реклама. Но если эти оба цвета глубокие и глушёные, то получаем элегантный, зрелый, стильный интерьер." Same for scarlet + blue-grey: "и красные, и синие здесь не чистые, а такие слегка пыльные, глушёные." [source: [[_Sources/YT_EipFPdVqNgs_wallstreet_2026_color_pairings|YT_EipFPdVqNgs]]]

> [!NOTE]
> **Three independent channels converge here.** Anuta Vlady's чистый/припылённый matching, Wall Street's muted-complementary rule, and ARCHWOOD's "сложные цвета" argument (below, on the Selection page) are the same underlying observation reached from three directions. This is the most strongly corroborated single idea in the batch.

## Proportion and Hierarchy

### The 60/30/10 rule — now on record from six independent sources

Already on this vault from **Kruglov/Ontario** and from the curtains batch; this colour batch adds **Anuta Vlady** (as 60:30:10, `LRtedmEn8mQ`), **PRIDE / Алёна**, and **ГК «Авальремонт»**. 60% base (walls, floor, large surfaces — PRIDE adds that a large sofa, rug, curtains or an open-plan kitchen run can legitimately be part of the 60%), 30% secondary (furniture, textiles, wood, an accent wall, a headboard), 10% accent (decor, cushions, vases, fixtures, art). Anuta Vlady's own framing of it is deliberately loose — "очень приблизительные пропорции, не требующие строгого соблюдения," generalising past three colours provided no two are used in equal amounts.

Three additions from this batch that the existing entries did not have:

- **⚠️ It is a hierarchy claim, not an arithmetic one — PRIDE / Алёна.** "Все цвета в комнате не должны быть равноправными — у них должна быть иерархия." The base should be calm and non-tiring specifically because it is the thing you cannot escape.
- **⚠️ Neutrals don't count against the three-role budget — PRIDE / Алёна.** The answer to "three colours isn't enough": white, black, grey, cream and natural wood "не всегда нами воспринимаются именно как отдельные активные цвета." A room can carry a warm light background, a wood secondary, an olive accent, black lines, a white ceiling and grey textiles and still be a correct three-role scheme, because the *active* hierarchy is still three components.
- **⚠️ A numeric threshold for when the accent stops working — PRIDE / Алёна.** At 10% it enlivens; **at roughly 20–30% it stops being an accent and starts competing with the base.** The only quantified version of this failure in the vault.

[sources: [[_Sources/YT_S9F9TGVG4x8_pride_60_30_10_anchor_object|YT_S9F9TGVG4x8]], [[_Sources/YT_2__5Y0sLAxo_avalremont_10_color_lifehacks|YT_2__5Y0sLAxo]], [[_Sources/YT_LRtedmEn8mQ_anutavlady_3_color_selection_methods|YT_LRtedmEn8mQ]]]

### Proportion rules that are not 60/30/10

- **⚠️ Colour proportion and accent contrast (Kruglov/Ontario)** — moved here 2026-08-30 from `Color_Palette_and_Material_Direction.md`, where it originally landed:
  - **The accent colour must repeat at least three times** across distinct objects (a painting, a cushion, a vase sharing one accent tone) — a single accent instance doesn't read as a deliberate choice.
  - **Three-criterion contrast rule**: an accent must differ from the base along at least one of **hue**, **temperature**, or **material/texture**. If unsure about hue contrast, temperature or material contrast is the safer default.
  - **Named warm/cool base pairings**: a warm base (beige, sand, cream, terracotta, warm grey) pairs with deep blue, olive or forest green, black, brass, gold; a cool base (concrete grey, cold white, graphite) pairs with terracotta, caramel, cognac, warm wood, bronze, warm green.

  `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_qPi_0cW7aHI_kruglov_expensive_interior_10_rules|YT_qPi_0cW7aHI]]]

  > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Kruglov's three-repetitions rule and Anuta Vlady's large-block rule are about different things and must not be collapsed.** Kruglov: an accent needs to appear **at least three times across small objects** or it doesn't read as deliberate. Anuta Vlady: an accent repeated across **several large planes** (sofa + curtains + accent wall + chair, all one shade) stops being an accent at all, because "он переходит в разряд дополнительных цветов" and starts to dominate. Repetition across small touchpoints is the goal; repetition across large blocks is the failure.

- **⚠️ The "волчок" (spinning-top) balance model — Андрей Самарин's** own preferred method, and a genuinely different way of thinking about proportion: picture the colour wheel as a spinning top that must stay upright, and distribute your colours as weights around it. Configurations he says stay balanced: two weights directly opposite; **three colours where two are near-neighbours and one sits opposite the pair — with *more* of the single opposite colour and *less* of the two neighbours**; or several colours evenly spaced in equal amounts. Note this reaches the same conclusion as 60/30/10 from an entirely different direction: unequal amounts, with the distribution tied to wheel position. [source: [[_Sources/YT_wjNz2wCtG7o_samarin_colouristics_basics|YT_wjNz2wCtG7o]]]

- **⚠️ One accent colour on a grey base — Студия Wall Street / Виктор.** "Дальше просто добавьте любой один цвет. Главное — один, не два, не три." Named options: grey + yellow / wine / pistachio / lavender / ochre / coral. [source: [[_Sources/YT_EipFPdVqNgs_wallstreet_2026_color_pairings|YT_EipFPdVqNgs]]]

- **⚠️ Within a complementary pair, one is deep and one is warm/textured, and only one is the accent — Студия Wall Street / Виктор.** "Один из цветов делайте акцентным и насыщенным, а второй спокойным и растянутым по объёму." [source: [[_Sources/YT_EipFPdVqNgs_wallstreet_2026_color_pairings|YT_EipFPdVqNgs]]]

- **⚠️ Restrain the gamut, for a stated perceptual reason — Андрей Самарин.** Compositions using the whole spectrum he calls "полноцветные" or, derisively, **"светофоры"** (traffic lights) and says are very hard to control. His argument is not stylistic: **the brain processes a smaller number of colours more easily.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_wjNz2wCtG7o_samarin_colouristics_basics|YT_wjNz2wCtG7o]]]

## Colour Has Weight: Composition Before Palette

- **⚠️ Every colour and material carries a visual weight, and darker or brighter means heavier — ARCHWOOD / Марина Измайлова.** "У каждого оттенка, цвета, материала есть свой вес. Чем темнее или ярче цвет, тем он больше." The consequence she draws is a concrete acceptance test: **a restrained interior is a composition of planes, so if you strip out all the decor and leave only the large built-in furniture, it should still be balanced.** [source: [[_Sources/YT__Vw-eaRekDk_archwood_three_approaches_main_color|YT__Vw-eaRekDk]]]

- **⚠️ One flat base colour on every wall will be boring, and decorating won't fix it — ARCHWOOD / Марина Измайлова.** Asked directly, her answer is "скорее всего получится [скучно]." **Her prescribed fix is to complicate the base rather than add colours**: decompose it into half-tones and textures — smooth surface, микробетон, wood grain — use dusty neighbouring shades, drift toward grey or white. Her justification is natural precedent: "любой камень, кора дерева, горы имеют сложные переходы. Представьте, если бы они были в одном цвете." **The more complex the transitions, the more alive the interior reads — and this matters most in calm interiors without accents, "иначе есть все шансы получить продвинутый ремонт от застройщика."** [source: [[_Sources/YT__Vw-eaRekDk_archwood_three_approaches_main_color|YT__Vw-eaRekDk]]]

- **⚠️ Colour choice is also a decision about where the eye goes — Anuta Vlady.** Saturated contrasting curtains pull attention to the window. If the intended focal point is elsewhere — her example is a printed headboard wall — the fix is to bring the curtains down to roughly the wall colour so the accent moves to the bed. [source: [[_Sources/YT_PGrxKyLhxp8_anutavlady_typical_color_mistakes|YT_PGrxKyLhxp8]]]

- **A historical caution worth keeping in mind for a Soviet-era-adjacent housing stock — Андрей Самарин.** 20th-century and specifically Soviet interiors were assembled almost entirely "на нюансе" — sofa matched to wallpaper, wallpaper matched to curtains — producing a restrained gamut he calls "немножко скучный… очень так однообразно." It is a concrete description of what over-applied nuance-matching actually looks like at scale. [source: [[_Sources/YT_wjNz2wCtG7o_samarin_colouristics_basics|YT_wjNz2wCtG7o]]]

## Accent Walls

**Anuta Vlady** gives the fullest rule set in the vault, all three rules demonstrated against real interior photos with her corrected versions:

- **⚠️ An accent wall should be at least somewhat *darker* than the room's other walls.** A lighter one "как будто бы проваливается, вместо того чтобы выглядеть каким-то главным цветовым пятном." Whether it is achieved with patterned wallpaper or plain paint makes no difference. Easiest choice is a darker shade of the same hue; a contrasting colour already present elsewhere in the room also works.
- **⚠️ One accent wall per room.** With two — her example is a patterned blue wall and a wood wall separated by a stripe of the base colour — "комната как бы разваливается на две равнозначные части" and neither reads as the accent. **This is the same rule already recorded on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Accent_Walls_and_Pattern_Combination|Accent Walls & Pattern Combination]] — but from the *same* channel (her Alaska decor-refinement case, 2026-08-25), so it is a restatement by one practitioner, `single-account`, not independent corroboration.**
- **⚠️ Placement must be justified by an object.** "Акцентную стену нужно размещать именно там, где есть какой-то объект, который мы хотим дополнительно выделить" — a fireplace, a console, an entrance zone, a furniture composition, artwork. **Named as a большая ошибка: putting an accent wall on an arbitrary wall just to liven the room up.** The accent wall is a tool for reinforcing something, not a decoration in itself.

[source: [[_Sources/YT_PGrxKyLhxp8_anutavlady_typical_color_mistakes|YT_PGrxKyLhxp8]]]

Also on record from Zemstandart/Alexey Zemskov, on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Accent_Walls_and_Pattern_Combination|Accent Walls & Pattern Combination]]: never put an accent finish on a long/narrow room's *longest* wall — accent a segment instead.

## Ceilings as Part of the Palette

**ARCHWOOD / Марина Измайлова** gives the full rule set; three other sources in this batch corroborate parts of it.

- **A ceiling darker or brighter than the walls lowers the perceived height, and the effect scales with the ceiling-to-wall contrast.** A *warm* dark ceiling lowers it more than a cool one, since warm advances.
- **When lowering is actually wanted**: ceilings above roughly **3.2–3.5 m**, where the height itself has become a defect and the room reads uninviting. (Spoken figure, approximate.)
- **⚠️ Execution rule when doing it**: paint **the ceiling plus the upper band of the wall** — or, in a classical scheme, the ceiling plus the cornice — rather than the ceiling alone. The transition then reads softer; a bare contrast breaking exactly on the wall/ceiling angle "часто смотрится грубо."
- **Standard height, no intent to lower**: walls and ceiling **the same colour**, so you enter "такую коробочку" where only the floor stands out — and the floor should be lighter than the chosen colour. The ceiling then reads at roughly its true height. **Independently corroborated by ГК «Авальремонт»**, who names the same technique and adds a **one-tone-darker-than-the-walls** variant for depth and an enveloping feel.
- **Low ceilings, ~2.5 m or below**: keep light — either one light colour across walls and ceiling (white with a hint of tint), or the ceiling lighter than the walls, in which case there is no visual distortion even if it isn't white.
- The ceiling "тоже участник общей выбранной палитры," and need not be delivered with colour at all — concrete, wood, or a combination. Her own project: a ceiling partly clad in parquet board with white fragments.

[sources: [[_Sources/YT_AKxOtRhIcJY_archwood_color_qa_orientation_ceilings|YT_AKxOtRhIcJY]], [[_Sources/YT_2__5Y0sLAxo_avalremont_10_color_lifehacks|YT_2__5Y0sLAxo]]]

- **⚠️ Don't default a coloured or dark-walled room to a *pure* white ceiling — Vittoria Selina.** With bright or dark walls, the hard white/colour contrast makes the ceiling jump forward, the central chandelier reads "как паучок на этом белом потолке," and the room's exact height becomes explicit — bad at ~2.50 m. **Her fix short of painting the ceiling in the wall colour: buy a warm-white / экрю ceiling paint instead of a pure white one.** Against coloured walls it is still *perceived* as white, but it softens the transition and works as a correcting tone. [source: [[_Sources/YT_jJ05zqugmSc_vittoria_selina_beige_combinations|YT_jJ05zqugmSc]]]

## Warm Advances, Cool Recedes

**ARCHWOOD / Марина Измайлова** states both the effect and its mechanism, and is unusually honest about its size: warm colours (yellow, orange, warm beige, red) slightly **reduce** perceived space and cool ones (blue, cool green) slightly **increase** it, because an intense warm colour reads as nearer to the eye and a cool one as further. She is explicit that this is a real but small illusion — "разница будет не такой заметной, но эффект есть" — and **explicitly not worth tolerating cool shades you dislike for.** Her practical form: prefer neutral (white, grey) or cool (серо-голубой) in small rooms, then add **warm contrasting details** so the room doesn't read cold overall while the walls still visually step back. [source: [[_Sources/YT_AKxOtRhIcJY_archwood_color_qa_orientation_ceilings|YT_AKxOtRhIcJY]]]

**ГК «Авальремонт»** states the same directional rules more bluntly (light enlarges, dark shrinks but adds coziness, cool expands, warm draws in) and prescribes light walls and ceiling for a small room. Note this puts him in **direct opposition to Vittoria Selina** on dark rooms — see the Perspectives block on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Color_Palette_and_Material_Direction|Colour Palette & Material Direction]]. [source: [[_Sources/YT_2__5Y0sLAxo_avalremont_10_color_lifehacks|YT_2__5Y0sLAxo]]]

## Colour as a Zoning Instrument

**ГК «Авальремонт»**: "не обязательно ставить перегородки, чтобы разделить комнаты на зоны. Цвет справится с этим лучше" — a kitchen distinguished by a different wall shade or tile, a rest zone by a soft rug or contrasting sofa, a work spot by an accent wall. Framed as "цвет — это инструмент планировки, просто он визуальный."

Worth flagging because this vault's zoning coverage — [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Functional_Zoning_and_Furniture_Arrangement|Functional Zoning & Furniture Arrangement]] and its Room-Zoning Partition Methods reference — is built almost entirely around **physical** mechanisms (slats, curtains, shelving, sliding glass, furniture-as-divider) and did not previously list colour among them. ## Dated Trend Reference (not adopted)

**Студия Wall Street / Виктор** named five pairings as forecast top-of-2026. Recorded as time-bound reference, not guidance — the durable content from this source is the muted-complementary rule above.

1. Алый/бордовый + сине-серый (complementary; "действие плюс контроль")
2. Кофейные оттенки + натуральное дерево (warm monochrome; **three to five coffee shades together, deliberately not matched** — "смело миксуйте: венги плюс выбеленный дуб, песок плюс орех")
3. Карамельный/песочный + оливковый/шалфейный + warm or neutral greys (analogous; add graphite or black-metal hardware "чтобы интерьер не расплывался")
4. Индиго + терракота/охра (complementary; matte and soft materials only — "с глянцем это сочетание может зашуметь")
5. Роза/бордо + олива/шалфей (complementary; works bright or dusty provided the muted rule is honoured)

[source: [[_Sources/YT_EipFPdVqNgs_wallstreet_2026_color_pairings|YT_EipFPdVqNgs]]]

## How Much of Each Colour — the 60/30/10 Dispute, and a Better Answer (Надежда Кузина, added 2026-09-01, Round 4)

Three sources from one round, kept under one heading so this page does not fragment. **Together they do something no single source on this page has done: they attack the vault's recorded proportion rule and then replace it.**

### ⚠️ Perspectives — is 60/30/10 usable at all?

**This vault records the 60/30/10 formula as *shared* — independently stated by several sources on the curtain pages.** Кузина is the first source to argue against it, and she does so with worked counter-examples. [source: [[_Sources/YT_GEBJkzYd874_kuzina_60_30_10_rule_rebuttal|GEBJkzYd874]]]

**First, the rule in more operational detail than the vault previously held**, which is worth keeping whatever you conclude:

| Share | What it maps to |
| :--- | :--- |
| **60%** | Walls, **or** large objects — sofa, large casework, kitchen units, built-in wardrobes. Very often neutral: white, beige, light grey |
| **30%** | Smaller furniture, an accent armchair, bed linen and other textile, **and painted doors** |
| **10%** | Decor — cushions, pictures, lampshades — **and mouldings** |

- **⚠️ One colour used at different lightnesses and shades counts as ONE colour.**
- The neutral need not be the 60; it can be the second colour, with 10% still held for the brightest accents.
- **⚠️ The rule's own escape hatch, and the first sign it does not close: to add a fourth colour, keep the 60/30/10 and add the extra accent also at 10% — "совокупно получается как бы 110."**

**Her objections:**

1. **Interiors exist with one, two or many colours and the count does not make them good or bad.** She concedes the real point underneath — **limiting the palette is correct if you are designing for yourself** — but there is no evidence successful three-colour interiors outnumber others.
2. **The number of colours depends on the style.** Minimalism runs on one or two; **for maximalism three is too few.**
3. **⚠️ "What counts as a colour?" is never answered — her sharpest objection.** On one reading there are only three primaries and everything derives from them, so every interior is three colours. And whether neutrals count is decided by convenience: **"нейтральные то считаются, то не считаются за цвет — в зависимости от того, набирается у автора три цвета или нет."**
4. **⚠️ Her demolition of the rule's own illustrations.** Counting what is actually in the images used to demonstrate 60/30/10: a white ceiling, white shutters, a large black artwork, gold fittings and golden parquet in one; white marble, a white ceiling, a black chandelier, black window framing and black in the artwork in another — more prominent still from other angles. **"Совершенно непонятно, как можно считать пропорцию в таком помещении."** The rule cannot be checked, so it cannot be wrong.
5. **Why this ratio?** No reason is offered for 60/30/10 over two colours at 70/30 or four in some other proportion. Her counter-example runs at roughly **30/30/30** and is hard to call unsuccessful.
6. **⚠️ It fails outright where a pronounced pattern occupies more than a small cushion** — a patterned surface cannot be assigned to one share.

**What she says people actually do instead: «нейтральная база плюс один цвет».** It keeps an interior from falling apart and works well where forms are unusual or texture is abundant — **but she names its cost: it produces the most banal result imaginable, "а всё остальное такое беленькое, серенькое, бежевенькое."**

> [!NOTE]
> **The disagreement is left open.** The formula's supporters on the curtain pages state it as a working heuristic; Кузина attacks its checkability, not its usefulness as a starting point. **Her own closing position is compatible with keeping it: use it if you want, but the goal is a logically built interior, not hitting a ratio.**

### ⚠️ The replacement she offers — Itten's lightness-to-area principle, with a flagged error

**The correct approach considers not only which colours are present but their quantitative proportion and how evenly they are distributed — and the proportion depends on which specific colours are involved.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_GEBJkzYd874_kuzina_60_30_10_rule_rebuttal|GEBJkzYd874]]]

Her source is **Johannes Itten, *The Art of Colour*** — the part past the wheel — crediting **Goethe** with assigning numeric lightness values. **At equal saturation the six main colours differ in lightness**: convert the wheel to greyscale and **yellow is lightest, violet darkest. Goethe's values: yellow 9, violet 3, green 6, red 6.** Saturation interacts with area — **reduce red's saturation against an unchanged green and you need more red to balance.**

> [!WARNING]
> **⚠️ Her two worked examples invert the standard formulation. Do not use them as a formula without checking Itten directly.**
>
> She says yellow 9 against violet 3 gives 3:1, so **"три части жёлтого и одну часть фиолетового"** — and similarly three parts yellow to two parts green.
>
> **Itten's harmonious *areas* are inversely proportional to the light values.** With yellow at 9 and violet at 3 the harmonious areas are **3 parts yellow to 9 parts violet** — **more violet than yellow**, which is also what the eye expects, since a small amount of a light brilliant colour balances a large amount of a dark one.
>
> **Her red-against-green example (6 and 6, so 1:1) is unaffected**, which is probably why the error survived. **The principle she is importing is sound and is the valuable part; the direction of the ratio is not.** `⚠️ verify before use`.

**Her own limit on all of it**: nobody works with a calculator, **because perception is also affected by illuminance, by texture and fаktura, and because it differs between people** — which is, on her own account, why the simpler rule spread.

### ⚠️ The bank hierarchy — the most complete answer on this page to "how much of each colour"

From a separate source, and it is the framework the 60/30/10 argument was missing. [source: [[_Sources/YT_ocoPRS3d8sA_kuzina_colour_mistakes|ocoPRS3d8sA]]]

**Premise: colours compete for the viewer's attention. More area pulls more attention; greater brightness pulls more attention.**

**The structure — a simplified bank: board of directors → top managers → heads of departments → rank-and-file staff.** Start with the board: the brightest or largest-area colours. Ask which surfaces there are most of, which matter most, and of floor, walls and furniture which is brightest, which most passive, which largest.

**The four failure modes, each from a worked interior:**

| Failure | What it looks like |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The whole bank is the board** | All colours equally bright and roughly equal in area |
| **Board plus a few rank-and-file, nothing between** | **The gap is too large and the range is not stretched out** |
| **Board plus top managers only** | More logical and harmonious — but still odd |
| **Two tiers, evenly spread** | Harmonious and it does not fall apart, **but boring — no real difference between the colours** |

**⚠️ The success case, tier by tier**: **board** — bright green armchairs and curtains; **top managers** — black wall panels; **heads of departments** — the details: pouffe, table, cushions; **rank-and-file** — the neutrals, **occupying a fairly large area**: a neutral rug, greyish-green walls, a greenish lampshade.

**⚠️ The extension — the outside contractor.** Episodic, more arbitrary colours that can later be swapped (a fuchsia cushion that could become muted orange with nothing else changing). **Two constraints: the invited contractor must not compete with the board — "чем больше цвет отличается от своего окружения, тем больше он перетягивает на себя внимание" — and banks do not hire off the street.** Decor colours are freer but not arbitrary.

**⚠️ And the proportion rule that falls out, which is its actual answer to 60/30/10: there are fewer bosses than staff, so do not divide the areas equally. "Начальников меньше, но при этом они ярче и контрастнее."**

### Rules that are not a panacea, and the relativity that underlies everything

[source: [[_Sources/YT_ocoPRS3d8sA_kuzina_colour_mistakes|ocoPRS3d8sA]]]

- **⚠️ "Можно пользоваться цветовым кругом и всё равно получить плохой интерьер."** Her evidence is a controlled series: **the same two colours (green and pink) across six interiors**, one a textbook triad that comes out mediocre, one a correct dyad that is "ничего особенного," and three that work. **Following the rules is right; departing is not automatically an error, and obeying guarantees nothing.**
- **⚠️ Her criterion is ownership, not correctness: "хороший интерьер — это тот, который нравится хозяину."**
- **The real problem is not the colour count but the distribution** — *"когда цветов много, их нужно правильно распределять по пространству, и вот в этом основной затык."* **And a limited palette does not make an interior poorer**: the same author delivers a good result with one emerald plus white and black as with many colours.
- **⚠️ Pure colours: the primaries are the purest, and mixing or adding white/grey/black costs them their звонкость.** *(Scoped to pigment, not light.)* **The purer the colour the more obtrusive it is; interiors usually work on complex shades.** **Few styles accept pure primaries — the exception is Bauhaus and modernism, on a neutral white or grey ground with black and wood, and blue/red/yellow as accents.**
- **⚠️ The closing statement, and the standard worth adopting: "цвет воспринимается всегда относительно других цветов, которые его окружают."** Which is why she refuses questions like *"у меня будут зелёные стены — подойдут ли серые шторы?"* — the words are too abstract to answer with.

### Colour support, and the deliberate refusal of it

[source: [[_Sources/YT_yU2zKPpDghM_kuzina_colour_difficulties|yU2zKPpDghM]]]

- **⚠️ «Поддержка цвета»: where an object is loudly coloured, add accents of the same colour elsewhere in the room.** Worked: a large blue sofa supported by a blue picture across the room. **And a second rule inside the same image: the sofa's blue is broken up by cushions of another colour — "большое цветовое пятно лучше разбить небольшими деталями других цветов."**
- **⚠️ The counter-move, which is the part received advice omits: an object can be left unsupported on purpose.** A large green sofa with no other green — **large, noticeable, self-sufficient.** **Artworks are often left unsupported precisely to draw attention.** **The same designer can do both in one project.**
- **⚠️ Her analogy for why it works: it is the absence of a rhyme where you expect one, or a deliberate break of rhythm in music. It wakes the viewer and stokes interest.**
- **⚠️ Contrast: if you want it, let one colour be the saturated one and darken or mute the other.** Worked: turquoise walls against an orange bedspread, **where the larger surface is deliberately the less active colour.** Contrast makes an interior more interesting and more characterful **and more tiring.**
- **⚠️ The shared-parameter rule, which is the governing one: colours must have something in common — one lightness, OR one saturation, OR one warmth.** Making an interior wholly warm or wholly cold is easier precisely because it fixes a parameter.
- **⚠️ And the condition for mixing warm and cold, the most actionable item: mix them on a shared LIGHTNESS.** Her worked case, checked in greyscale, has no very bright and no very dark patches. **Low lightness-contrast is what buys permission to play with warm and cold.**
- **Warm/cold is relative.** Primaries are easy; **tertiaries break down** — yellow-green is cold against orange and warm against light blue. **The warmest on the wheel is yellow-orange, the coldest blue-green** *(attribution ASR-garbled, most likely Itten, not asserted)*. **⚠️ Adding white makes a colour colder; adding black makes it slightly warmer.** **Green is borderline with no unambiguous reading.** **⚠️ When you cannot judge a green's temperature, take a more contrasting colour, or a more neutral one.**

### ⚠️ Trim colour — a three-group decision taxonomy

Reusable structure for skirting, cornices, mouldings and opening surrounds. **All trims sit on planes, so the decision always accounts for the adjoining surface's colour.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_I-F2ULBDc5A_kuzina_trim_and_moulding_colour|I-F2ULBDc5A]]]

**Group 1 — the colour of an adjoining plane.** A skirting can take the wall colour, the floor colour, **or the colour of the door architraves it runs into**; a cornice takes the wall or ceiling colour. **⚠️ Cornice and skirting in the wall colour visually increase wall height**, and push the moulding into the background, freeing you to work with textile and furniture. **⚠️ White trim on white walls works specifically when there is a lot of moulding, when it is complex and finely detailed — the more detail the heavier it reads, and white lightens it back.** *Use when you want detail without accent, when the ceiling is low, when the moulding is oversized, or when you do not know what to choose.*

**Group 2 — neutrals.** **⚠️ The greater the lightness difference between trim and wall, the greater the accent — the trim stops being background.** **⚠️ White edging on bright walls softens them, because the wall is broken up and its area reduced — but it visually LOWERS ceilings**, the opposite of Group 1. **⚠️ White works as a frame where another room shows through an opening, acting as a buffer between two colours.** **⚠️ On neutral walls, white is what stops subtle light shades reading as merely colourless.** **Grey picks details out delicately.** **Black adds energy, graphic quality and drama, pulls attention off an over-active wallpaper, frames openings like pictures, and "обнимает" a room with too many scattered elements.**

**Group 3 — another colour.** **Adds a colour to the palette while occupying little area. ⚠️ But never one element alone — use two, preferably three, or support the colour in accessories.** Choose it by the wheel: analogous to the walls; the same colour more saturated, darker or lighter; complementary for strong contrast; or contrast by warmth. **⚠️ And a cross-room idea worth keeping: repeat a colour from another room in the trim here** — a blue sofa in the living room, blue skirting and cornice in the corridor.

**Two lifehacks that avoid the decision entirely**: paint **almost** the wall colour, one or two tones off; **or keep the colour and change the sheen — super-matte walls with eggshell trim, which alone gives extra volume.**

### ⚠️ A standing caution about numeric design rules, from the same round

**The Ильяхов analogy, which generalises past colour and is worth applying to every numeric rule in this vault.** A copywriter invented that every product should have **7.2 advantages**, and now copywriters force every product to 7.2 — **padding one that has three, cutting one that has ten.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_GEBJkzYd874_kuzina_60_30_10_rule_rebuttal|GEBJkzYd874]]]

**"Нужно думать сначала о смысле и только потом о формуле. Формулой можно пользоваться как вспомогательным инструментом"** — but first make the thing good.

## Two Corrections to the Colour-Support Rules on This Page (Надежда Кузина, added 2026-09-01, Round 5)

**Both qualify material routed here in Round 4 rather than adding to it, and both should be read against it.**

### ⚠️ Correction 1 — colour support needs VARIATION, not repetition

This page carries «поддержка цвета» from `yU2zKPpDghM`: support a loud object with accents of the same colour elsewhere. **`uRK4OPR4dak` names the failure mode of doing that badly.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_uRK4OPR4dak_kuzina_decorating_mistakes|uRK4OPR4dak]]]

Her worked negative example runs the identical logic to a positive one: a neutral scheme with yellow elements added, **"но их оттенок везде одинаковый, и он с такой педантичностью, я бы сказала занудством, повторён"** — and the yellow does not rescue the interior. **Better to take several different shades that agree with each other.**

> **⚠️ Supporting an accent with identical instances of itself is a distinct failure from not supporting it at all.** The rule is *distribute in varied shades*, not *repeat*.

**A related item from the same source**: **the personal colour threshold — "почти похож на болевой порог" — and the problem is usually not that a bright colour is present but that there is too much of it, or it repeats in one shade until it dominates.** Her fix: **"вышибить клин клином" — introduce a very bright object of a DIFFERENT colour**, which softens the sense that one colour is bearing down.

### ⚠️ Correction 2 — colour block reverses the distribution rule on purpose

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **This must be read as a scoped reversal, not as more of the same.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_HENjGv0DGjI_kuzina_colour_block|HENjGv0DGjI]]]
>
> **In colour block the rule is: "лучше использовать три ярких пятна, три цвета, чем восемь цветов в мелких элементах." The patch must read easily, and elements of one colour are grouped TOGETHER — "здесь нет задачи размазать или как-то распределить цвета по интерьеру."**
>
> **Colour support distributes an accent; colour block consolidates it. They are different techniques with opposite rules, and mixing them yields neither.** The full treatment is on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Accent_Walls_and_Pattern_Combination|Accent Walls & Pattern Combination]].

**Two further colour-contrast rules from the same source, which do generalise:**

- **⚠️ The more contrasting the colours, the stronger their influence — and the strongest colour contrast comes from the PRIMARIES. Intensity, and therefore contrast, declines as a colour moves away from the centre of the wheel: orange, violet and green give less than red, yellow and blue.**
- **⚠️ Separating each colour from the others by white or black stripes makes each colour's individual character more pronounced and reduces their mutual influence.** *(Attributed in the source to Itten, but the attribution is ASR-garbled — recorded as probable.)* Her worked instance is **a black outline holding a bright scheme together: "как чёрный держит этот интерьер вместе, как складывает его воедино."**
- **⚠️ And a composition rule for any bright scheme: dilute bright patches with more neutral ones — "тогда интерьер меньше похож на коробку с карандашами и больше похож на дом."**
