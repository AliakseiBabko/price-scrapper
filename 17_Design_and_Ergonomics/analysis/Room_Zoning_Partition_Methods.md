## Room-Zoning Partition Methods (new topic area, added 2026-08-25 — crosses the 3+-source page-creation threshold this session)

Cross-cutting partition/zoning-mechanism techniques — applicable to any
room needing to split one open space into two functional zones, not
specific to Living/Dining or any single room type (already cross-
referenced from `01_Entrance/analysis/Layout_and_Zoning.md` and
`05_Kids_Room/analysis/Zoning_and_Partition_Methods.md`).

**Ten ranked methods from a design-company channel (13DS/Olesya)**:
wood slat/batten partitions (light passes through, no acoustic privacy;
needs a ceiling mounting point/"закладная"; cheap dimensional lumber
warps — use custom MDF slats for a real structural partition); curtain
zoning (cheapest/fastest, full light-blocking with blackout fabric, but
a dust magnet with zero sound isolation; conceal the track in a
dropped-ceiling niche or between two short drywall stub walls at
cornice height); Japanese/panel curtains (collapse to one panel's
width when open, suit minimalist/japandi interiors); shelving-unit
dividers (affordable, mobile, doubles as storage, but becomes "a
vertical junk pile" without real household discipline — mix open and
closed sections to mitigate); sliding glass/plastic partitions (a real
2020 company project: a glass partition separating bedroom from
kitchen-living cost ~100,000 RUB; **key mounting caution, generalizable
to any wardrobe-style sliding door**: top+bottom track mounting creates
a trip-hazard threshold — prefer top-track-only, but confirm the
ceiling-backing requirement with the manufacturer before the ceiling is
closed up); rope zoning (wood slats + taut cord, low-budget/DIY-
buildable); milled/routed MDF panels (custom shape/texture, also needs
a ceiling mounting point); a screen/room-divider ("ширма", maximally
mobile but can feel flimsy); a permanent wall (most final; adding stub
walls on both sides of a glass partition reduces the glass width
needed, lowering cost); furniture-as-divider (a sofa or wardrobe marks a
zone boundary — a divider-wardrobe's unfinished rear panel is a real
downside, mitigated by combining it with slats/rope behind it, and
should run full ceiling height since it's freestanding).

**Three additional mechanism types from a doors/partitions retailer
channel (Sergey Gusev/ПРО ДВЕРИ)**: a **two-sided mirrored static
partition** (mirrors on both faces of a partial-width partition
compound to make the whole apartment read as roughly double its size);
an **arched double swing-leaf partition with curtain layering** (fixed
side sections plus a center hinged double-door opening, with a curtain
rod above for full closure on top of the leaves); a **"book"/rotary-fold
partition door** (folds in half into the wall opening rather than
swinging out, needing far less floor clearance than a standard hinged
door — a genuine small-apartment space-saver, buildable with up to 3
hinged leaves per side for a wider opening while still folding flat).

**Related structural-reasoning caution (Diskill/Nadia Karmin)**:
flooring-material zoning can have a genuine structural reason, not just
an aesthetic one — see this store's Kitchen-Living Combination Zoning
topic area for the full hard-flooring-under-heavy-cabinetry rule, which
generalizes to any zone boundary defined by a flooring-material change
rather than a physical partition.

**Windowless-room caution (Studio57/Alexander Kasperovich)**: a room
with no natural light source generally cannot be officially registered
as a "жилая комната" (habitable/living room) per applicable building
norms — relevant when an *opaque* partition would cut off a windowless
carve-out's only borrowed-light path; a transparent/glass partition
avoids this specific problem. Region unstated at level 1 for this
caution — not routed to `16_Legal_and_Regulations/`.

`single-account` per source except where corroboration is noted.

**Glass block partition — a new decorative divider material, flagged as a likely fading trend (Kruglov/Ontario, Round 14 triage, added 2026-08-28)**: glass blocks used as an internal partition or accent surface (including as a shower/WC divider) have become increasingly common — roughly every third design project per the source, a steep rise through 2024. Explicitly framed as a **temporary/fading trend** associated with a dated 1980s industrial-daylighting material now repurposed decoratively — can look genuinely good in the right composition, but the source flags the real question before installing it: whether the household is actually prepared to redo it in 5-7 years once the trend passes, which he doubts most clients would actually follow through on. `single-account`, `unverified`. ## ⚠️ Framed Glass Partitions — Acoustics, Span Limits and Why They Are Not Cheap (Руслан / Декус, via NSDSGN, 2026-07-14)

**Filmed in the manufacturer's showroom, and the promotional ratio is the highest in its intake round** — plugs for their furniture, a collaboration brand, three showrooms, and a closing pitch for the host's own studio. **⚠️ The title asks «дорого или нет?» and the video never gives a single price** — not one figure for a partition, a door, or a square metre. What survives that is recorded below: real numbers on acoustics and spans, and two "myths" conceded as true. [source: [[_Sources/YT_pR1t7zlT2Qk_nsdsgn_glass_partitions_10_myths|YT_pR1t7zlT2Qk]]]

### Acoustics — the first hard numbers on this page for a glazed divider

**Directly relevant to this page's central question, since acoustic separation is the usual reason a zone divider fails to do its job.**

| Build-up | Rating |
| :--- | :--- |
| **8 mm single glazing** | ≈ an average interior door |
| **Two 4 mm panes laminated as триплекс** | **≈40 dB** |
| **Триплекс with an acoustic interlayer film (шумоизолирующая плёнка)** | **up to 50 dB** |

- **The mechanism is stated correctly: to improve glass acoustics you increase its mass.** Offered against the common assumption that glass gives no isolation at all — «стекло достаточно неплохой звукоизолирующий материал».
- Reference points he supplies: **jackhammer ~100 dB, angle grinder ~80–90 dB.**
- **⚠️ One claim in the passage is wrong and is recorded as such**: he glosses 50 dB as reducing noise «на 50, в половину». **Decibels are logarithmic — a 50 dB reduction is not "half".** The dB ratings themselves are ordinary product specifications and are recorded normally; only his interpretation of them is flagged.
- Decorative option that does not cost acoustics: **metallised fabric laminated into the triplex** (copper and gold tints shown).

### ⚠️ Span limits, which decide the material rather than the aesthetics

- **Aluminium: corrosion-proof and popular, but soft. Structures over 4 m on a thin profile become unstable.** Some rigidity can be recovered from glass thickness, but not indefinitely. *(The transcript's stated glass-thickness ceiling, «80 мм», is implausible for architectural glass and is recorded `ASR-uncertain`; the operative figure is the 4 m span, which he states twice.)*
- **Steel: «практически не ограничен».** It welds, it is rigid, any profile tube size can be chosen, and **they have repeatedly built 6 m and 12 m runs.**
- **→ Decision rule: aluminium up to ~4 m; beyond that, steel.**
- **⚠️ A logistics constraint that gets forgotten at design stage and is not a manufacturing question at all**: on a live St Petersburg project the client wanted a single continuous glass sheet for a walk-in wardrobe, and the real problem was **getting it into the flat — cranes, removing a window, passing the glass through.** «Там уже вопрос не к вам, а к тому, как это всё занести в квартиру.» **Check access before specifying an oversize single pane.**

### Materials, wet rooms and safety

- Four options: **aluminium** (does not corrode at all), **painted steel**, **stainless steel**, **all-glass in a profile**.
- **For bathrooms they recommend stainless steel — painted or brushed — or aluminium.** Shower enclosures and glass walls in place of walls are both normal applications.
- **Safety glass only — триплекс or закалённое: «опасное стекло мы никогда не используем».** Tempered shatters into very small fragments: a light cut at worst, not a serious laceration.
- On the children's-safety objection: **a triple safety factor claimed, and no fixing torn out in over ten years of their work.** Recorded as the manufacturer's own claim, `unverified`.
- **Style is not restricted to loft, and the method is specific**: classical forms plus **natural materials — латунь (brass), timber, marble** — and **combined infills** (a timber panel with glass) rather than plain glazing; **ribbed or frosted glass**, not modern clear. Their own doors are **frosted glass painted from the inside**, claimed to resist scratching and clean with a wet cloth, «гораздо лучше, чем тот же MDF».

### ⚠️ Two of the ten myths conceded as true — the maintenance costs of the type

**Both are admissions against interest, and they are what a zone-divider comparison actually needs.**

1. **Dust on horizontal glazing bars is real and recurring.** «Друзья, пыль существует. Это не миф. Есть определённая сложность в уборке.» Standing in front of a partition with many horizontal rails, he says it needs wiping **roughly once a month** to stay clean. **→ A many-barred glazed divider carries a standing cleaning obligation that a plain partition does not.**
2. **Painting metal well is genuinely difficult.** Their own process is **degrease → prime → powder-coat**, done in-house for 90% of showroom stock. **Many skip the priming step because some powder-paint manufacturers state it is unnecessary**, and painting also requires a dust-free environment. His verdict: «очень мало хороших, качественных покрасок.» **→ Finish quality is a real differentiator between suppliers of the same-looking product, and priming is the specific question to ask.**

### Why the type is expensive — the cost structure, with named failure points

**Told against himself: before entering the trade they visited a Rimadesio showroom, priced the partitions, and concluded they had found a gold mine — glass and aluminium are cheap, steel cheaper. They were wrong, and the reasons are checkable:**

- **Door closers (доводчики): outside Italian and German makers, few can produce one that lasts 5 years without failure.**
- **⚠️ Rollers are the concrete failure mode: cheap soft rollers go oval, and the partition starts jumping within about two months.** **A specific, short-horizon test — if a sliding partition begins to judder inside the first season, the rollers are the suspect.**
- **Labour content: steel work is entirely by hand; aluminium is more machine-adaptable but still carries much manual work.** Assembly is **without welding**, so tolerances run «с волосину» hair-thin, with milling and fitting.
- **⚠️ On the aluminium-versus-steel price question his actual answer is the useful one: there is no price without a specific specification.** More aluminium is dearer both from material cost and from the complexity of combining thick and thin profiles — but «есть алюминиевые, которые значительно превзойдут по стоимости любую стальную». **The host draws the parallel to renovation costing explicitly, which corroborates the same rule from the builder podcast in [[11_Budget_and_Planning/analysis/Cost_Benchmarks_Live_Intake|Cost Benchmarks Live Intake]]**: «надо сначала нарисовать ремонт, посчитать ремонт, и потом мы уже скажем».
- **Their «Бали» door as a construction data point: a 15 mm frame, heavy from the glass and the many rails, with no side hinges — pivot-style hinges bearing into the floor and the head of the frame, all load on the bottom floor pivot.** That is what buys the very thin sightline.

### The fashion-durability argument, and the one distinction worth keeping

He dates the type to argue it will not go out of style: Japanese sliding rice-paper partitions from the 1800s; the French window at «350 лет» (he says «вторая половина XV века», but the arithmetic points to the 17th century — recorded as his approximation); and **Rimadesio, 70 years old at the time of recording**, still making aluminium partitions.

**⚠️ The transferable part is the distinction, not the history: «мода скоротечная… а металл, дерево, мрамор — это уже стиль. Это то, что действительно никогда не надоест.»** And the market driver he names for the whole category is structural rather than aesthetic: **living space is shrinking while its price rises**, so partitions earn their place by combining rooms and extending perceived space.
