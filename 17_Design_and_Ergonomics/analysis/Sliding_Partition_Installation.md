# Sliding Partitions — Mounting and Installation Technique

The no-floor-rail mechanism, the three mounting-method families, embedded backing versus a suspended pre-mount box, retrofit into an already-installed stretch ceiling, and combined-profile construction. **Split out on 2026-09-02 under the vault's 300-line page ceiling, verbatim.**

## The Core "No Floor Rail" Mechanism, Clarified

**⚠️ A genuinely ceiling-hung ("no floor rail") partition still touches
the floor at exactly one point — just not a track.** Per dver1.ru
(Andrei Ivanov, company owner) — the single richest source in this
batch — sliding partitions never have a true bottom *track*: the actual
guide channel is machined into the bottom edge of the leaf itself, and
the floor only carries a single small pin/peg ("флажок"/"штырь") that
this internal channel rides along. This holds regardless of leaf count
(1, 2, or 5 leaves = still one floor pin). **This is the real mechanism
behind "no visible floor hardware" marketing claims** — it isn't zero
floor contact, it's one small, low-profile point instead of a visible
rail. Independently confirmed by a customer (not the installer) for a
different company's product in
[[_Sources/YT_sFE1EyKRHvE_kupedsgn_suspended_partition_testimonial|YT_sFE1EyKRHvE]]
("направляющие находится сверху, снизу ничего не мешает").

Holzberg (via Дело в дизайне) gives the floor-stopper hardware taxonomy
in more detail — three real options, from most to least visible: a
classic surface-mounted barrel/block stopper; an **adhesive magnetic
stopper** (no drilling, used where floor drilling isn't allowed); and a
**recessed/drilled magnetic stopper** (this installer's own preferred,
most-reliable option — a metal rod on the leaf's underside snaps to a
below-floor magnet). **⚠️ Recessed-stopper depth caution**: the stopper
body recesses 42mm into the floor — a real risk of hitting embedded
floor-heating or plumbing lines in the screed at that depth; check
before drilling.

[source: [[_Sources/YT_Fu-GMSDLg_8_holzberg_invisible_doors_sliding_partitions|YT_Fu-GMSDLg_8]]]

## Three Mounting-Method Families

Per dver1.ru and Holzberg, independently converging on the same basic
taxonomy:

1. **Ceiling-mounted** — upper guide rail fixed directly to the
   structural ceiling, with a visible baguette/cover trim, OR fully
   embedded/hidden within the ceiling for a minimalist look (see the
   pre-mount-box technique below).
2. **Wall-mounted** — upper guide fixed to a wall instead of the
   ceiling. **Limitation**: only supports two parallel-moving leaves, not
   3+. Because the leaf doesn't terminate against a wall the way a
   room-bisecting ceiling-mounted partition does, opening it leaves a
   visible gap between the outer leaf and the wall — fixed either by
   building a return/box the width of a cornice for the leaf to rest
   against, or by adding a leaf-widening extension element.
3. **Cassette/pocket-mounted** — the partition fully retracts into a
   newly-built stud wall. Sizing formula (Holzberg): added wall
   thickness minimum ~70mm; total buildup = existing opening + ~50mm
   (leaf clearance/gaps) + ~70mm (added stud wall) ≈ **190mm** in their
   own minimal reference example.

[source: [[_Sources/YT_a_vchjD7CHQ_dver1_telescopic_partition_buyer_guide|YT_a_vchjD7CHQ]]],
[source: [[_Sources/YT_Fu-GMSDLg_8_holzberg_invisible_doors_sliding_partitions|YT_Fu-GMSDLg_8]]]

## Embedded Backing vs. a Suspended Pre-Mount Box — Two Different Techniques for a Hidden Ceiling Track

Two independent manufacturers describe **two genuinely different**
approaches to hiding the track in the ceiling, worth distinguishing
directly since either could apply to this household's own build:

- **Embedded backing block ("закладная"), dver1.ru**: sized to the gap
  between the structural ceiling and a *planned* stretch-ceiling
  membrane (~5cm thick), width 15cm for a 3-leaf/3-rail partition or
  10cm for a classic 2-leaf/2-rail partition. **⚠️ Don't sandwich the
  membrane between two backing blocks** (one under the structural
  ceiling, one under the membrane) — traps the fabric. Better: a single
  ~10cm-tall block installed *before* the stretch ceiling goes in,
  accepting two independent stretch-ceiling pieces in that room (costs
  more, but a damaged/flooded piece near the partition can be replaced
  independently of the rest of the room's ceiling).
- **Suspended pre-mount box ("предмонтажный короб"), Holzberg**: a
  pre-fabricated box hung from threaded rods/studs directly into the
  structural ceiling — **no embedded backing plate needed at all**.
  Installed during the rough-construction stage (box only); the actual
  partition leaves, track, and decorative cover plates go in later, at
  the finishing stage, once floors/walls/ceiling are fully done — a
  clean, dust-free, no-cutting step. Box profile depth: 67mm minimum;
  can be dropped further via the rod to any depth (up to ~1m
  demonstrated in their own showroom) with no effect on structural
  stability, and independent of whether the finish ceiling itself is
  level.

**Practical implication**: the pre-mount-box approach avoids the
embedded-backing sizing/sandwiching problem entirely, at the cost of
needing to plan and install the box during the structural/rough stage —
still well before the finish ceiling goes in, but a different sequencing
commitment than an embedded backing block.
[source: [[_Sources/YT_a_vchjD7CHQ_dver1_telescopic_partition_buyer_guide|YT_a_vchjD7CHQ]]],
[source: [[_Sources/YT_Fu-GMSDLg_8_holzberg_invisible_doors_sliding_partitions|YT_Fu-GMSDLg_8]]]

## Retrofit Into an ALREADY-Installed Stretch Ceiling — Three Distinct Techniques

Directly relevant if this household's stretch ceiling goes in before the
partition decision is finalized (cross-reference
[[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Ceilings_Guide|Ceilings Guide]] for this
project's own stretch-ceiling content). Three independent
sources give three different answers, worth comparing side by side
rather than treating as interchangeable:

1. **Floor-track fallback (Kupe-dsgn, installer)**: when no embedded
   backing exists and the stretch ceiling is already finished, this
   installer's own stated fix is a perimeter LDSP-batten frame with the
   upper guide fixed to the ceiling-side batten — but states directly
   that **"such a partition variant is only possible with a lower guide
   rail, not a suspended system."** For this installer's own method, a
   genuinely floor-rail-free result requires the backing to have been
   planned before the stretch ceiling went in.
   [source: [[_Sources/YT_LB2oYxyYL3c_kupedsgn_partition_stretch_ceiling_no_embed|YT_LB2oYxyYL3c]]]
2. **Suspended-rod technique, no cutting the membrane (dver1.ru,
   manufacturer)**: glue reinforced tape to the membrane, cut a small
   hole, insert threaded rods spaced ~0.5m apart through the hole into
   the structural ceiling, and hang a "floating" backing beam from the
   rods — genuinely avoids the floor-track fallback above. Demonstrated
   stable for several years in the company's own showroom (`single-
   account`, not independently verified elsewhere).
   [source: [[_Sources/YT_a_vchjD7CHQ_dver1_telescopic_partition_buyer_guide|YT_a_vchjD7CHQ]]]
3. **Membrane cut-and-reweld (Mikhail Mishchenko, independent DIY
   hobbyist)**: cut the membrane along the new partition's path, glue a
   new pre-fabricated welt strip ("гарпун") along the cut edge with a
   cyanoacrylate adhesive, partially free the membrane near the work
   zone (heat gun softens it for easier handling), then anchor the new
   partition framing to the **structural ceiling**, not the
   stretch-ceiling track — this source's own explicit warning: anchoring
   to the membrane/track itself causes wobble. **⚠️ Real material-age
   risk**: an older membrane may not fully re-tension after cutting and
   reheating, leaving visible wrinkles near the new seam (mitigated with
   ceiling trim); a newer/less-aged membrane re-stretched cleanly in this
   source's own test. Real labor time: close to a full day for one
   person. Demonstrated for a drywall partition, but the anchor-point
   principle and membrane-cutting technique transfer directly to a glass
   partition retrofit.
   [source: [[_Sources/YT_qcIz2--60Ts_mishchenko_partition_retrofit_stretch_ceiling|YT_qcIz2--60Ts]]]

## Combined-Profile Construction and Glass Specification (added 2026-08-25, DELI/Ivan Degtev)

The same DELI channel's company owner, in a dedicated materials/
construction video (same channel as the case-study section below, so not
a new independent source, but genuinely new construction-spec content):

- **Three profile-material families**: metal (aluminum or steel), wood-
  MDF, and **combined** — DELI's own product uses the combined type.
- **⚠️ Combined-profile internal construction**: a finger-jointed/glued
  solid-wood core (knot-free, glued from smaller pieces for rigidity) is
  faced/wrapped in MDF, which acts as a cushioning/stabilizing outer layer
  specifically to help the finished profile hold its geometry over time
  (resist warping) — more construction detail than this page's existing
  general "MDF profile" descriptions.
- **Finished profile dimensions**: cross-section **60×38mm**; max
  single-piece profile length **2.8m** (max single-leaf height ≈2.75m);
  spliced/joined profile can reach heights up to **5m** — cited against a
  real completed project with a leaf height of **3.7m**
  (`ASR-uncertain` correction from a literal "37 meters" transcription
  error).
- **Two external coating options**: wood-textured film ("эко-шпон",
  various colors), or enamel paint (matte or glossy, various colors).
- **Glass surface-area share**: glass makes up roughly **85%** of a
  partition's total surface area — the stated reason glass-choice quality
  matters disproportionately. Tempered safety glass used, described as
  "6 times stronger than ordinary glass." **⚠️ Same-channel inconsistency**:
  the sibling case-study video from this exact channel states "7 times
  stronger" — a minor, unresolved discrepancy between two videos from the
  same company; both figures are `single-account`/`unverified`.
- **⚠️ Frosted-vs-clear glass selection principle, generalized (not just
  one project)**: frosted glass is most commonly used because it passes
  up to 85% of natural light while still occluding a direct view; clear
  glass is used specifically to physically separate two spaces while
  preserving visual connection — restating (as a company-wide design
  principle, not one specific project) the exact kitchen-living zoning use
  case (cook watching children while odors stay contained) recorded below
  from the sibling case-study video, a genuine reinforcement of that
  case's generalizability.
