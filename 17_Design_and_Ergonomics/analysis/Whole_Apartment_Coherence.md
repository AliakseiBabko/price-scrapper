# Whole-Apartment Design Coherence

## Scope

How a color scheme, material palette, and design language stay connected across different rooms of the same apartment, rather than each room being designed in isolation — what should genuinely stay consistent room-to-room (e.g. flooring transitions, door/hardware style, a repeated accent material) vs. what can legitimately vary by room's function and mood.

## First Real Source: Unifying a Style-Mismatched Kitchen-Living Space

Anuta Vlady (interior-design educator/consultant, real subscriber-photo
makeover case) works through a concrete case of a previously-renovated
kitchen and living room that read as two disconnected spaces despite
sharing a wall — genuinely useful as a worked methodology, not just
principles:

- **Derive the wall-paint color from a fixed material already present**
  (here: fireplace stone), using a color-picker/eyedropper tool on a
  photo of that material, rather than choosing wall color independent
  of the room's unchangeable elements.
- **Paint color must be physically tested on the actual wall via sample
  cans before committing** — no photo, screen, or paint-code reference
  reliably predicts how a color reads under a specific room's actual
  lighting.
- **Repaint/refinish an existing "must-keep" furniture piece into an
  already-present anchor color (here: white, matching the kitchen
  cabinet fronts) as a first concrete step toward visually unifying two
  adjoining spaces** — cheaper and often more effective than replacing
  the piece.
- **Scatter small black accent details (hardware, small objects) across
  multiple points in a room to add contrast/expressiveness without
  introducing a bold color** — a lower-commitment alternative to a
  saturated accent color.
- **Unify curtain treatment across every window in a combined open
  space**, even where different zones previously had different
  treatments (sheer-only vs. lined curtains) — reinforces that the
  zones are one continuous space rather than two separately-decorated
  rooms.
