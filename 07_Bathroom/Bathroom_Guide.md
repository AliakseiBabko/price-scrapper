# 🚿 Bathroom Renovation Guide

Compact overview of layout, structural/framing, fixture-selection, and finishing decisions specific to the bathroom room. Each section below is a short summary with a link to the full detail page — go there for reasoning, numbers, and sources; this page is for scanning, not deep reading. Plumbing rough-in, the water-inlet node, and waterproofing *code* requirements live on [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/Plumbing_and_Waterproofing|Plumbing & Waterproofing]], cross-referenced rather than repeated here.

> [!NOTE]
> This page draws primarily on three long-form sources, all from the same channel (Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario, Moscow renovation company, self-promotional), plus several Zemskov/Zemstandart (Moscow) sources added later. **Because many sources share a channel, `single-account` tags mean exactly that — one experienced source's opinion, not independently corroborated** — check against other sources before treating any specific number as load-bearing. Regulatory/approval content is Russia-specific and explicitly **not confirmed to apply to Belarus** — flagged inline wherever it appears. Full source list in [[#Source Notes]].

## ⚖️ Perspectives on Record — Practitioners Disagree

Two topics on this page have a genuine, unresolved disagreement between named practitioners, not just a single source's opinion. Both are flagged inline below and get the full comparison on their own page:

- **Thermostat mounting height** — one source ties height to how often you'll adjust it; Zemskov says flat eye-level always. See [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Heated_Floor_and_Thermostat|Heated Floor & Thermostat]].

## Ceilings

**Stretch ceiling is used in ~95% of bathrooms and is recommended as the default choice** even by designers who'd otherwise prefer drywall — fast install, cost, reliability, repairability, and full immunity to temperature/humidity swings. A drywall ceiling's failure mode is severe by comparison: even a minor leak from the upstairs neighbor requires replacing the entire ceiling structure. PVC panels, Armstrong grid ceilings, and metal/plastic slat ceilings are all flagged as essentially obsolete.

## Towel Warmer

See [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/Plumbing_and_Waterproofing|Plumbing & Waterproofing]] for the full case for electric over hydronic towel warmers. Placement-relevant summary: **an electric towel warmer has no placement constraint** (any wall, even the ceiling) and costs roughly **~2,500 RUB to install**, versus a hydronic unit's **~10,000-15,000 RUB** (requires the management company to weld shutoff valves into the riser) plus, if you want it covered by leak protection, a dedicated second leak-protection branch on top of that.

A pure design/personal-preference point: some people strongly dislike seeing the toilet immediately upon entering and design the layout around hiding it; others don't consider it worth trading entry convenience for. ### Do's

| Orient the bathroom doorway so the vanity mirror is the immediate focal point upon entering | Bathroom Entrance Layout | Improves visual aesthetics and room ergonomics compared to facing a toilet bowl or tub edge | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_mistakes_budget_371bd212.txt` |

| Seal decorative plaster on bathroom walls with transparent epoxy varnish | Bathroom Walls | Seamless, fully washable, water-resistant wall finish while saving tile installation labor | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_5_dfc91747.txt` |

| Budget an 80 cm door opening (70 cm leaf) if a full-size washer/dryer is planned | Bathroom Entrance | A standard 60 cm door leaf is too narrow for a ~60x60 cm appliance to pass through after finishing | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_video_6lacLnqpJbM_4b68d812.txt` |
| Choose an electric towel warmer over a hydronic one, and disconnect existing hydronic units where possible | Bathroom | Hydronic units are a recurring leak source severe enough that some management companies now offer free disconnection | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_video_6lacLnqpJbM_4b68d812.txt` |
| Sit on a floor-model toilet in person before buying, even if ordering online | Bathroom, WC | A toilet that looks fine in a listing can be uncomfortably small or large in practice | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_video_s1cWUR4l90w_8a6aadd3.txt` |
| Grout a built shower cabin's tiled base with epoxy grout | Shower Area | The floor sees more sustained water exposure than almost any other tiled surface; cement grout isn't fully waterproof | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_video_s1cWUR4l90w_8a6aadd3.txt` |
| Use the classic tile layout (remove one row, center the rest) on visible walls and floors | Bathroom, WC, Kitchen | Guarantees corner cut-pieces are wider than half a tile, avoiding an "ugly" narrow sliver | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b054_tiles_64563b4e.txt` |

| Crimp each PEX fitting twice, rotating 90 degrees between crimps | Bathroom, WC, Kitchen | A single crimp can leave an uneven seal around the collar's circumference | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b057_boiler_fa53ce72.txt` |
| Place the bathroom/WC exhaust fan switch inside the room, never in the hallway | Bathroom, WC | Door-sensor auto-on leaves no way to re-trigger the fan from inside without reopening the door | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b100_ventilation_d8c0351d.txt` |
| Default to a curtain, not glass, for a bathtub splash guard | Bathroom | Multi-year warranty data shows glass causes far more injuries and renovation damage than curtains | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b128_neverput_810584eb.txt` |
| Pressure-test rough plumbing to 10 atmospheres before closing walls | Bathroom, WC, Kitchen | Code-minimum 4.5 atm often fails to reveal micro-cracks or marginal joints | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b142_masterclass_8c608293.txt` |
| Route washing machine water/electrical connections through the tub apron if the adjacent wall is load-bearing | Bathroom | Horizontal chasing for supply/drain lines is not permitted in a load-bearing wall at any depth | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b160_lifehacks_6b617f42.txt` |
| Face a tub-alcove utility column's access opening toward the hallway, not the tub | Bathroom | A tub-facing opening lets in water and leaves the upper portion unreachable | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b171_brilliant_adfb12d8.txt` |
| Run dedicated wiring to the mirror location during rough-in, even before a mirror model is chosen | Bathroom | No general room light is ever sufficient for grooming; retrofitting power later means fishing wire through finished walls | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b174_wildbath_4e5e4ea1.txt` |

### Don'ts

| Rule | Applies To | Risk | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Do not place sockets near shower spray zones without IP-rated protection | Wet Zones | Electrical safety hazard | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_vid1_transcript_d04723c5.txt` |
| Do not install a 3-wall recessed vanity sink in a niche narrower than 80 cm | Vanity Niche | Causes constant elbow impact against wall tiles during daily hygiene routines | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_mistakes_budget_371bd212.txt` |
| Do not install vessel bowl sinks on countertops situated over washing machines | Vanity Countertop | Countertops over washing machines sit at ~90 cm height; a vessel bowl raises the rim too high, causing water to run down user elbows | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_3_a0e895b1.txt` |
| Do not build doorless open-entry walk-in showers if household has shedding pets | Shower Area | Shedding pets sleep on heated shower floor, causing frequent pet hair drain clogs | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_3_a0e895b1.txt` |
| Do not use sliding doors for a shared-household bathroom | Bathroom Entrance | Worst soundproofing of the three common door types; acceptable only for a private master-suite bathroom used by one couple | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_plumbing_video_3_e759e2c7.txt` |
| Do not install toe-kick/under-cabinet floor lighting without a daily cleaning routine | Bathroom Floor | Highlights dirt, hair, and dust, making the room look perpetually dirty | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_plumbing_video_3_e759e2c7.txt` |
| Do not use a pre-made plastic shower cabin/enclosure unit in a standard renovation | Shower Area | Nozzles clog quickly, drains become unreliable, structure is flimsy — suitable only for a very low-budget job | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_plumbing_video_3_e759e2c7.txt` |
| Do not add a drop-down/lift threshold draft seal to a bathroom door | Bathroom Entrance | Blocks the makeup air the exhaust fan needs to function, causing it to run against resistance and fail early | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_video_6lacLnqpJbM_4b68d812.txt` |
| Do not cut a through-hole/vent opening in a bathtub apron | Bathroom | Lets water seep in, causing a persistent damp smell; also a known entry point for small animals to get trapped | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b065_screen_ac9e5552.txt` |
| Do not build a raw door-frame reveal of exactly 100mm at a wet-room door | Bathroom, WC Entrance | Tile and adhesive thickness reduces the finished reveal below 100mm, cramming the door casing against the tile | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b028_door_bdc70cee.txt` |
| Do not wire an exhaust fan to turn on automatically (humidity sensor, motion sensor) | Bathroom, WC | Unexpected automatic activation is startling and can run against what the room is being used for | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b100_ventilation_d8c0351d.txt` |
| Do not inspect or work on a plumbing joint while the system is still pressurized to 10 atm | Bathroom, WC, Kitchen | The joint can rupture suddenly; the resulting water jet is forceful enough to cause eye injury | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b142_masterclass_8c608293.txt` |
| Do not build ordinary furniture doors or open shelving facing a bathtub/shower splash zone | Bathroom | Standard furniture door seals aren't waterproof; splash soaks contents and breeds mold | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b171_brilliant_adfb12d8.txt` |
| Do not buy black plumbing fixtures | Bathroom, WC, Kitchen | Shows every water mark and residue; a second independent source recommends against it outright | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b174_wildbath_4e5e4ea1.txt` |
| Do not use plain particleboard for a moisture-adjacent furniture access panel | Bathroom, WC | Swells within about a week of moisture exposure; use laminated/enameled panel instead | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b197_wildrepair_c4619c2b.txt` |
| Do not skip the reinforcing brace on a shower glass installation | Bathroom | Without it, leaning on the glass can shatter it or tear the mounting clips from the wall | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b197_wildrepair_c4619c2b.txt` |
| Do not use an ordinary water-trap siphon for AC condensate or filter-flush drainage | Bathroom, WC | Dries out during seasonal disuse, letting sewer odor migrate back through the drain line | `_Archive/processed_sources/20260731_zems_b197_wildrepair_c4619c2b.txt` |
