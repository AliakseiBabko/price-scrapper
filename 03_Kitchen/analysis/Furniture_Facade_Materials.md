## Small-Kitchen Facade Formula: Monochrome + Minimal Handles + Muted Tones (added 2026-08-20)

**⚠️ Formula: single-color (monochrome) facades + minimal-or-no visible handles + calm/muted tones = the recommended combination for a small kitchen specifically.** The underlying reasoning: a kitchen already concentrates an unusually high density of small visual elements in one place (fixtures, appliances, dishware, food-storage containers, switches/sockets) — in a small kitchen that concentration is compressed into less visual area, effectively squaring the perceived clutter. Reducing every *avoidable* source of visual complexity (facade color contrast, protruding handle hardware) compensates for that unavoidable density. **Explicit anti-pattern**: never mix strongly contrasting facade colors on the same run (the source's own example: red upper cabinets over green lower cabinets) — even a milder version of mismatched tones works against the same goal. Minimizing visual noise this way is stated to make even a genuinely small kitchen read as more spacious, tidy, and clean. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_ihx8gUDO3vI_kruglov_small_kitchen|YT_ihx8gUDO3vI]]]

## Glass Display Facades: A Recurring Clutter Critique, and Sliding/Pocket Hardware (added 2026-08-28)

> [!NOTE]
> From a ~25-photo Pinterest kitchen review, generalizing a complaint repeated across most of the "bad" examples shown. [source: [[_Sources/YT_N6UZiZ1-sNI_kruglov_pinterest_kitchens|YT_N6UZiZ1-sNI]]]

- **⚠️ Glass display-facade critique**: glass cabinet facades — especially full vertical glass runs to the ceiling, or open wine-bottle display shelving — are flagged as a near-universal source of a "visually cluttered/untidy" impression: the interior contents (dishware, wine bottles) stay visible and read as messy however carefully arranged, and open glass-front wine racks specifically accumulate dust that's hard to clean. **Recommendation: skip glass facades entirely, or limit to a single small display unit** — more than one glass-fronted run in the same kitchen is called excessive regardless of how well-organized the contents are.
- **Sliding/pocket-facade hardware, first named-brand mechanism content for this page**: a facade type that opens and slides fully away (flush-recessed) rather than swinging on a hinge is available from at least two named manufacturers — **Blum** (premium/more expensive) and **Hettich** (a genuinely cheaper alternative offering the same mechanism).

## Facade Material Tier Ladder: Economy, Mid, Premium (added 2026-08-28)

> [!NOTE]
> This store's densest facade-material source to date — a structured, five-criteria comparison (price/quality, popularity, durability, manufacturer reliability, appearance) across all three price tiers, with named brands and construction detail. **Directly extends** (not duplicates) this page's existing Egger/DTC budget content above from `YT_2I77xJIeRwM` — that source named Egger in passing; this one supplies the full manufacturing detail behind the same recommendation. [source: [[_Sources/YT_W2KvnHPQdjM_kruglov_best_kitchen_facades_2026|YT_W2KvnHPQdjM]]]

### Economy tier — LDSP (laminated particleboard)

- **⚠️ Leader: Egger.** Lamination thickness **0.3-0.4mm**, applied to both faces equally (some economy competitors only laminate one face to the same standard) — plus the category's largest lamination color palette.
- **⚠️ Board thickness varies by manufacturing country**: an Egger board made at a Russian Federation plant arrives at **16mm**; the same brand made at a Belarusian plant arrives at **18mm**, since Belarusian production follows European standards. Both equally reliable/durable — the difference is standards-driven, not quality.
- **Named popular color/texture codes**: solid — grey "Cashmere" (**U702**); wood-look — Hickory Natural (**H3730**), oak-brown (**H1386**), forest green (**U606**), diamond-grey (**U963**).
- **⚠️ LDSP limitations**: edge banding always visible (1-2mm), bond quality determines failure speed — though even budget/garage production can now bond it adequately; a PUR-glue-bonded edge is functionally permanent but pricier. **Cannot be milled/routed into any relief or curved profile** — stays perfectly flat, wood-grain texture the only surface variation available.

### Mid tier — plastic-on-MDF and film-on-MDF

- **⚠️ Plastic-on-MDF, two named leaders with a quantified price gap**: **Mateelux** (premium end of mid-tier) and **AGT** (comparable quality, ~**30% cheaper**). MDF substrate (denser/more stable than LDSP) can be milled/routed, unlike LDSP.
- **Plastic facade properties**: laminated both faces before cutting/edge-banding; finishes include gloss, matte, super-matte, soft-touch; decorative layer substantially thicker than LDSP lamination, harder to scratch/damage. **Fingerprint-visibility finding**: any dark facade shows fingerprints, but plastic (especially light/matte/soft-touch) shows them far less than other materials at comparable darkness.
- **⚠️ Film (плёнка) on MDF, two named supplier brands: Adilet and Greenwood.** Total facade thickness **18-25mm** depending on flat vs. framed profile.
- **⚠️ Delamination-risk comparison**: LDSP and plastic finish layers are factory-bonded under heat (effectively monolithic); film is applied separately at the kitchen company's own local production, carrying genuinely higher long-term delamination risk than either — concentrated at high-steam-exposure zones (above a cooktop, near a kettle). Risk varies considerably by film brand and installer.
- **Film's milling/edge-wrap advantage over plastic**: film can be applied over a milled/routed MDF profile and wraps fully around contours/edges, so a filmed facade needs **no separate edge banding at all** — unlike plastic, which stays flat only. Rear side is white by default; some manufacturers can film the rear too on request.

### Premium tier — enamel and natural/artificial veneer

- **⚠️ Enamel (эмаль) on MDF**: MDF used in ~99% of enamel facades (density holds enamel with minimal "orange peel" texture — LDSP-based enamel shows heavy orange-peel and isn't a true premium result). **Painted entirely at the kitchen company's own local production** (unlike factory-applied LDSP/plastic) — finish quality depends heavily on that specific shop's spray equipment/booth professionalism, so two "enamel" facades from different shops can differ dramatically even with the same paint. **Layering**: primer → base coat → color coat → sealing/clear coat.
- **⚠️ Enamel cons, quantified**: scratches extremely easily (a bare-finger swipe leaves a visible scratch, demonstrated in the video); some formulations fade/discolor under prolonged direct sun.
- **⚠️ Enamel pros**: **2,000+ shades** (RAL or NCS), far exceeding LDSP/plastic/film's palette; no visible edge banding (painted both faces as one continuous surface, or one face left unpainted on request); a manufacturer-supplied touch-up repair kit exists for chips, though the repair stays visible on close inspection.
- **⚠️ Veneer (natural/artificial) on MDF, named leader: Woodstock** — 1,000+ available wood species/texture/color variants. **Natural vs. artificial distinction**: natural veneer is a thin slice of a specific real wood species; artificial veneer starts from a cheaper fast-growing species (poplar, alder) reconstructed to visually replicate a pricier species — still genuine wood veneer, just a substituted base species.
- **⚠️ Veneer properties and cost-saving technique**: unmatched tactile feel, genuinely unique grain per facade (a feature, not a flaw); edge banding done in matching veneer for a near-invisible seam. **Cost-saving technique**: order the visible front face in an expensive species' veneer and the hidden rear face in a cheaper species of matching color — visually indistinguishable, price difference between species can be 3-10x.
- **Veneer's living-material behavior** (framed as a benefit, not a defect): genuine wood veneer continues to change color/tone unevenly over time, since it's a living material — a real characteristic to set expectations on before ordering.
- **Veneer's one clear con: scratches very easily** — applies to veneer flooring as well as facades.

`single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_W2KvnHPQdjM_kruglov_best_kitchen_facades_2026|YT_W2KvnHPQdjM]]]

## Carcass Material: Standard LDSP Sufficient, "Moisture-Resistant" Label Flagged as Low-Value Upsell (added 2026-08-28)

> [!NOTE]
> Complements this page's Egger LDSP economy-tier facade content above with a carcass-material (not facade) opinion from the same channel's "useless kitchen items" checklist video. [source: [[_Sources/YT_e3bHUlP0PMY_kruglov_useless_kitchen_items|YT_e3bHUlP0PMY]]]

- **⚠️ Standard LDSP carcass recommended over MDF carcass** — MDF carcass material called unnecessary premium spend for the structural box (distinct from a facade, where MDF is the norm for milled/enamel finishes per this page's tier ladder above).
- **⚠️ "Moisture-resistant" (влагостойкая) LDSP flagged as a low-value upsell**: paying extra for the "moisture-resistant" label doesn't meaningfully extend a carcass's real service life. **What actually determines carcass longevity is the fabricating company's own workmanship** (accurate cutting, correct edge banding, ideally PUR-glue-bonded edges) applied to a quality base LDSP board (Egger named again here as an example quality brand) — not the board's "moisture-resistant" or material-type label itself.

## "Whole Truth About Kitchens 2026" — Additional Facade Types, Hardware Cycle Ratings, Carcass Tiers (added 2026-08-28)

> [!NOTE]
> This channel's single densest kitchen video to date (~75 min encyclopedic guide) — heavily cross-checked against this page's existing economy/mid/premium facade ladder (Round 8, `W2KvnHPQdjM`) before recording anything below; brand identities already named (Blum, Hettich, DTC, Egger) aren't repeated, only genuinely new detail. **One genuine cross-source disagreement found on quartz-agglomerate integrated sinks — see callout below.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_FdJLbYEpViU_kruglov_whole_truth_kitchens_2026|YT_FdJLbYEpViU]]]

### Additional facade types

- **Acrylic facade** (LDSP or MDF substrate, acrylic-coated) — a distinct category from the existing plastic-on-MDF tier: extreme "grand piano" gloss is the most common form, though matte/soft-touch acrylic exists. Moisture/UV/temperature-stable, scratch-resistant even glossy. Same fingerprint rule as elsewhere: gloss shows prints heavily, matte/soft-touch barely shows them.
- **Stone veneer (каменный шпон) facade** — same MDF-plus-edge-banding construction as wood veneer, but with a thin stone layer instead. Heavier than wood veneer, requires reinforced premium hardware/carcass; a genuinely rare, high-cost, high-complexity choice.
- **⚠️ "Феникс" (Phoenix) facade material, fully specified**: manufactured by **Arpa Industrial (Italy)** — a next-generation HPL laminate, ultra-matte Soft Touch surface. **Self-repair mechanism**: a small scratch can be heat-buffed out on-site with a cloth-covered iron, without removing the facade — a genuine first for a plastic-family facade. No visible fingerprints, water-resistant, chemical-cleaner-tolerant. Con: no gloss option, no relief/milled profile possible (flat only).
- **Solid wood (массив) facade** — classic-style only, single continuous piece of properly dried wood; fully restorable (sand out scratches/dents). Cost scales geometrically with wood-species rarity. Requires active home climate control (humidity/temperature) or the facade (and any matching solid-wood flooring/furniture) can warp.
- **Facade-mixing technique**: combine different facade materials within one kitchen run by cost/style zone (e.g. acrylic upper, veneer lower) on a custom-built order.
- **⚠️ Edge-banding (кромка) three-tier classification**: economy — **0.4mm PVC, hand-applied**, seam not watertight, vulnerable to peeling with cheap adhesive; comfort — **1-2mm, factory-applied**, seam visible but properly sealed; premium — **PUR-glue bonded**, seam nearly invisible, fully watertight for the kitchen's life.
- **Facade summary ranking**: most dirt/fingerprint-prone = any gloss finish; most dirt-absorbing = natural wood veneer; least dimensionally stable = solid wood; most wear-resistant = plastic, enamel, film, LDSP, and Phoenix (roughly tied); source's own aesthetic favorite = natural wood veneer.

### Hardware, with quantified cycle/lifespan figures

- **⚠️ Blum (Germany): 200,000+ cycle rating, a lifetime warranty equivalent to ~50 years of regular use, Blue Motion soft-close on all mechanisms, widest product range of any manufacturer.**
- **⚠️ Hettich: comparable reliability to Blum, slightly cheaper, narrower range, meaningfully harder to configure correctly** (more adjustment points per mechanism) — recommended only with an installer who has direct prior Hettich experience. Shorthand: Blum = comfort/smoothness of motion; Hettich = practicality/fine-tuning precision.
- **Two more named premium hardware brands: Austria and Salice Italia** ("Слайс Italia").
- **⚠️ Named comfort/economy hardware, cycle comparison**: **Boyard and Firmax** — China-manufactured under brand quality control, ~30,000-40,000 fewer cycles than Blum-equivalent, ≈**10-15 years of regular use** (called optimal for an average kitchen's expected lifespan). **GTV (Poland)** — quality-controlled, partly China-manufactured.

### Carcass (Корпус) material and QC tiers

- **⚠️ Formaldehyde emission class by tier**: economy LDSP meets class **E1** (edge of the legal threshold); comfort/premium boards (Egger, Kronospan) achieve **E0.5 or E0.**
- **"Moisture-resistant LDSP" flagged as more marketing than real benefit — independently corroborates this round's own `e3bHUlP0PMY` claim.** The real vulnerability is the edge/seam, not the LDSP body — if edge banding fails, standard and "moisture-resistant" LDSP swell at essentially the same rate.
- **New carcass material: Kronospan laminated MDF board** — LDSP-like surface with MDF-level strength; priced above standard LDSP. **Carcass thickness tiers: 16mm (economy-only), 18mm (meaningfully stronger, comfort/premium), 25mm (rare, premium-only exception).**
- **Carcass QC by tier**: economy sometimes skips edge banding on hidden/rear joints (moisture-ingress risk) and shows more assembly-hole defects; comfort/premium always factory-edge-band every joint. **Milling for concealed wiring/lighting channels: absent at economy, present at comfort/premium.**
- **Carcass color convention**: economy interior white/light-grey only; comfort/premium carcass color matched/coordinated to the facade — signals price tier the moment a door opens.
- **Back-panel material tier ladder**: economy — 3mm HDF, nailed on; comfort — 4-5mm HDF, grooved in for rigidity; premium — same as comfort, occasionally LDSP (flagged as overkill).

### Countertop material tiers, extending this page's existing content

- **⚠️ Economy (LDSP) detail**: moisture-resistant LDSP core, **28-38mm thick**, HPL-laminated, edges fold-wrapped or PVC-banded. Lifespan **5-7 years** (up to 10-15 with care); can't support an undermount sink; seams managed by sealant (weakest), trim profile, or factory mitred "euro-cut" joint (best, used on corners).
- **⚠️ Comfort (acrylic solid surface) detail**: mineral filler + acrylic resin; any shape/curve achievable including a fully integrated same-material sink with zero seam; non-porous; highly repairable (professional sand-and-polish); low heat resistance; scratches easily but repairs easily — **periodic light sand-and-polish keeps it looking new.**
- **⚠️ Premium (quartz agglomerate) detail**: 90-95% natural quartz + polymer binder + pigment; slabs **20mm or 30mm** (rare 12mm); second only to diamond in scratch hardness; genuinely heat-resistant; non-porous; realistic lifespan **10-30 years**. Cons: heavy/brittle under bending stress; **the slab seam is never fully hideable**; a chip is very hard to repair.
- **⚠️ Open disagreement — quartz agglomerate + integrated same-material undermount sink**: this source states flatly that a quartz-agglomerate countertop **cannot** have an integrated same-material sink — a hole can be cut for an undermount sink, but the sink itself must be a separate material. This directly contradicts Round 8's `SaMpFOPm_4U` (already recorded above in the 2026-trends section), which states some manufacturers *can* now produce a matching same-material quartz-agglomerate undermount sink (visible seam, not universal). Both positions recorded, attributed to their own source, rather than resolved — this may reflect genuine manufacturer-to-manufacturer variation rather than a factual error on either side.
- **Natural stone (granite/marble/travertine/slate)**: granite strong but porous (wine/coffee stains can be permanent); marble more porous still (even lemon juice can stain permanently); travertine/slate more exotic, harder to fabricate; all need periodic re-sealing — chosen more for aesthetics/status than durability.
- **⚠️ Quartzite — a new natural-stone tier added above granite/marble/travertine/slate (Kruglov/Ontario, Round 13 triage, added 2026-08-28)**: combines strength, low porosity, durability, and wear-resistance better than any of the other four natural stones in this list, with very high cost as its sole tradeoff. **Travertine resealing, new maintenance-recurrence framing**: sealing travertine's pores is not a one-time treatment — it must be periodically re-applied, directly analogized to reapplying anti-corrosion undercoating on a car ("you can't do it once and be done for life"). `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_oDHSbp6QRRE_kruglov_worst_overrated_trends|YT_oDHSbp6QRRE]]]
- **Compact-plate/HPL self-supporting panel** — corroborates and extends the existing compact-laminate content: no LDSP/MDF substrate at all, **10-12mm thick**, sold/jointed like stone slabs; priced like stone/acrylic despite being fundamentally plastic.
- **Natural wood countertop** — rare, needs constant oiling, absorbs moisture, scratches easily — more decor than function.
- **Out-of-league materials**: stainless steel (commercial kitchens); glass (costly, shows every mark); tile — **modern large-format porcelain slab now makes a genuine tile countertop feasible** (no integrated sink, full-slab replacement on damage, joints filled with two-component epoxy).
- **⚠️ Concrete worked cost comparison**: switching a kitchen's countertop from LDSP to quartz agglomerate roughly **doubles the entire kitchen's total cost** — source's own example: **≈300,000 RUB (≈$3,700) → ≈600,000 RUB (≈$7,400)**, from one material change alone.
- **Countertop tier leaders**: economy = LDSP; comfort = acrylic with integrated same-material sink; premium = quartz agglomerate.

`single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_FdJLbYEpViU_kruglov_whole_truth_kitchens_2026|YT_FdJLbYEpViU]]]

## ⚠️ Facade Colour, Internals, and Worktop Restorability (Надежда Кузина, added 2026-09-01, Round 5)

### The cost of a non-standard colour, and why the showroom does not offer one

**⚠️ "Нестандартный цвет эмали увеличивает стоимость фасада на 10, максимум 20%."** `single-account`, `unverified`, Russian market, 2026. [source: [[_Sources/YT_vnLZqiYxY1Q_kuzina_mass_market_palette_economics|vnLZqiYxY1Q]]]

**Context for why the choice is not offered**: in mass-market segments most standard fronts are white, grey or wood plus a few open accent colours — **and per the kitchen-trend research she cites, white fronts have been the mass buyer's choice for years, at roughly 40–45% in various years, followed by grey and wood.** *(Research house name not recoverable from the ASR; treat as her reported figure.)*

**⚠️ Her structural explanation, which is why demand does not fix it: complex shades are inconvenient to SELL. Hard to show quickly; hard to explain if the floor salesperson is not a colour expert; and they require accounting for colours belonging to other products the company does not sell. "Для них это время и усилия впустую."** The full argument is on [[11_Budget_and_Planning/analysis/Design_Services_and_Fees|Design Services — What They Cost, and What They Include]].

### ⚠️ Internals are a 2× multiplier

**Functional internals let you fit far more into a kitchen of the same size — "если вы оплачиваете вот эту крутую Blum начинку, это действительно очень практично, но это может стоить в два раза дороже, чем та же кухня с более простой начинкой."** `single-account`, `unverified`, 2020.
