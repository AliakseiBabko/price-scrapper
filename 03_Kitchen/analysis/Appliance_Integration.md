# Kitchen — Appliance Integration

Where appliances go, what clearances they need, and how they are fitted into cabinetry. **Split out of `03_Kitchen/Kitchen_Furniture.md` on 2026-09-02, verbatim.**

Related: cabinet-front and carcass materials are on [[03_Kitchen/analysis/Furniture_Facade_Materials|Facade Materials]]; the drawer-vs-door and fridge-configuration comparison from Краснов sits with the storage content on [[03_Kitchen/analysis/Storage_and_Hardware|Storage & Hardware]].

## Appliance Placement

- **Tall units (fridge/oven column) go at the end of the run nearest natural light (a window), not mid-run** — a tall unit placed where the main prep zone would otherwise be casts a shadow over that zone; placing the tall unit in the shadowed position instead "sacrifices" a unit that doesn't depend on good lighting as much as a countertop workspace does. Mid-run placement of a tall unit is also flagged as visually disruptive.
- **Cluster plumbing-dependent appliances (sink, dishwasher, washing machine) near the riser/stack**; place the fridge and any tall column (neither needs water/drain) at the far end instead — minimizes plumbing run length/complexity.
- **Washing machine at the run's far end, away from the main cooking zone** — keeps laundry-related activity (detergent, dirty laundry) out of the active food-prep area.
- **Oven in a tall column, elevated above floor level** (not under the cooktop) where kitchen dimensions allow — easier to use without bending down.
- **Microwave needs adjacent countertop space, not fridge proximity** — the real usage sequence is fridge → plate/countertop → microwave → back to fridge (or eaten), not fridge-straight-to-microwave.
- **Ask the client which hand they use to hold a dish while scraping food waste before loading it into the dishwasher — that hand determines which side of the sink the dishwasher goes on.** A specific-preference check, not a generic right-handed assumption. **Corroborated and sharpened by an independent source (added 2026-08-18)**: for a right-handed dishwasher, place it to the sink's *left* — the plate stays in the working (right) hand near the sink, and the free (left) hand needs the dishwasher on that same side to load it without an awkward reach/twist.
- **Cutlery drawer near the dishwasher; large pot/pan drawers near the stove.**
- **Upper cabinets are unconstrained client preference** (no electrical/plumbing dependency, unlike lower cabinets) — the one designer obligation is planning task lighting for the prep zone beneath them.
- **⚠️ Avoid white built-in appliances (oven/microwave/cooktop), Round 13 triage (added 2026-08-28)**: far more limited model selection than black across every brand and price tier (black is available from essentially every manufacturer, easing replacement/matching later); **white shading genuinely differs between manufacturers, and even between different appliance types from the same manufacturer** (an oven and a built-in microwave from one brand, made in different factories, can show visibly different whites) — a color-mismatch risk unique to white that black doesn't share. Minor secondary note: mismatched control-panel display colors across units (e.g. red digital clock on one, blue on another) reads as a small but real inconsistency. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_JIWmxboS-oM_kruglov_16_things_never_designers_home|YT_JIWmxboS-oM]]]

## Freestanding-Fridge Niche Sizing (added 2026-08-19)

> [!NOTE]
> For a client who wants a freestanding (not built-in) fridge to sit flush in line with the rest of the cabinetry run rather than protruding into the walkway. [source: [[_Sources/YT_fSEPr5fpfPM_kitchen_stubouts_ac_fridge_niche_166|note]]]

- **Why a niche is needed**: a quality freestanding fridge is typically slightly over 600mm deep (vs. a kitchen cabinet run's standard 600mm depth) and needs 50-70mm of rear clearance for its compressor/heat exchanger — a recessed niche solves the depth mismatch and the clearance requirement at once.
- **Niche dimensions**: recess 70-100mm behind the main cabinet face line; width and height each 50mm larger than the fridge's own external dimensions.
- **Finishing**: all outer/visible corners of the niche opening get a plastic corner bead (малярный уголок) during framing; the niche's side returns and back wall are finished with the same wall-finish material as the surrounding walls, not a contrasting one.

### Second Channel on Fridge Clearances — and an Argument Against Forcing a Freestanding Unit Into the Run (Надежда Кузина, added 2026-08-31)

Interior designer Надежда Кузина reaches the section above's problem from the opposite direction: she quotes similar clearances but concludes **don't do it**. [source: [[_Sources/YT_iHViNm3dESU_kuzina_kitchen_electrics_and_lighting|YT_iHViNm3dESU]]]

- **Her clearance figures for a freestanding fridge placed inside a run: ~10 cm at the sides and 20 cm above.** Broadly consistent with the 50–70 mm rear clearance above, but she counts the *side and top* gaps as the real cost — the side gaps cannot be cleaned behind and become dust traps. Her visual objection: it reads like "протез, который в два раза меньше остальных зубов."
- **⚠️ Side-by-side is the case she rejects outright, and the reason is a hinge, not a clearance.** Depth is **never 60 cm — it is 68–70 cm** — so it cannot sit in a 60 cm run without deepening the neighbouring cabinets. **And even then the door hinge is designed on the assumption the unit protrudes, so flush-mounting stops the door opening.** Deepening the cabinetry does not solve it.
- **Cost ladder she gives for the same capacity** (Q&A, 2021): built-in appliances are **not** inherently more expensive. **Side-by-side costs substantially more than two built-in fridges of the same combined capacity** — the unit itself perhaps **+15%**, but "очень дорогое удовольствие" once the cabinetry consequences are counted. A single freestanding fridge is the cheapest option outright, but then the kitchen must be built around its clearances. `single-account`, `unverified`.
- **Built-in is also the easiest electrically** — it sits on a shelf, clear of the floor, so the outlet can go in the same base or the plinth.

### ⚠️ Third Channel: a Higher Top-Clearance Figure, and the Perforation Mechanism the Section Was Missing (Александр Синчуков / NSDSGN, 2025-07-22)

**A genuine divergence on the number, and a new mechanism that explains why the number matters.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_-6QKr4HerCQ_nsdsgn_kitchen_25_ordering_mistakes|YT_-6QKr4HerCQ]]]

- **His figure for a free-standing fridge fitted into a furniture niche: minimum 300 mm, «а то и 500 мм» of free air above it**, closed with a **perforated grille**. **That is materially more than Кузина's 200 mm above** — recorded as a divergence rather than reconciled, since neither source states how the figure was derived. **Take the larger figure if the cabinetry allows it**; the consequence of being wrong is stated below.
- **⚠️ The mechanism, which neither existing account gives: convection needs an inlet as well as an outlet.** Below a **built-in** fridge there must be **perforation so air can enter and convect**; perforation above as well «точно лишним не будет». Above a niche-fitted free-standing unit, the 300–500 mm gap *is* the outlet. **Stated consequence of omitting it: «очень большая вероятность, что ваш холодильник прослужит гораздо меньше»** — a service-life cost, not a performance one.
- **The same concern extends to ovens**, which draw air low and vent steam forward.
- **His procedural advice is the reusable part: consult the appliance supplier on how to build the furniture around each unit**, rather than treating clearance as a cabinetmaker's question. **This is the same "ask the specialist, not the generalist" pattern recorded across this round** — see [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Lighting_Fixtures_and_Fittings|Lighting Fixtures and Fittings]].

### ⚠️ Start the Kitchen Design From the Appliances — Two Reasons, One of Them New (same source)

- **The kitchen is fabricated to the specific appliances and adjusted to them**, so an appliance that arrives and does not fit is a serious problem. **Buy or at least definitively select the appliances first, and check the models will not be discontinued before delivery.**
- **⚠️ The second reason is a budget-sequencing one: appliances now often cost more than the kitchen itself**, so leaving them last risks the budget running out — «потом у вас может просто не остаться на неё бюджета, и это может подпортить вам очень сильно планы». **«Бытовая техника — первое, о чём надо подумать.»**
- **His own honest caveat against over-reading his advice: «стоимость не всегда гарантия долгожительства вашей бытовой техники.»** The recommended approach is to prioritise deliberately — a better dishwasher that will not damage crockery and lasts longer, economising on the freezer or the hob.

### ⚠️ Under-Sink Volume Is Now Contested and Must Be Planned as a Whole (same source)

The bin is no longer the only occupant, and this is a planning list rather than a rule:

- The **bin** itself.
- A **измельчитель** (waste-disposal unit) — bulky and normally central. **One manufacturer offsets the sink's drain valve to the corner specifically so the disposal can be pushed into the corner and free the space**, which is worth asking about when selecting a sink.
- **Drinking-water filters** feeding the mixer.
- **With reverse osmosis and its tanks there is effectively no room left for a bin at all.**

**→ Decide the full under-sink inventory before ordering the sink cabinet**, or the bin ends up somewhere else or too small to be useful.

## Appliance Fit, Powered Hardware, and Concealing Outlets in Cabinetry (Надежда Кузина, added 2026-08-31)

Cabinetry-side consequences of the same seminar; the electrics themselves are on [[03_Kitchen/Kitchen_Utilities|Kitchen Utilities]]. [source: [[_Sources/YT_iHViNm3dESU_kuzina_kitchen_electrics_and_lighting|YT_iHViNm3dESU]]]

- **⚠️ Upper-cabinet depth is typically 32 cm, sometimes 35 cm, IKEA 37 cm** — she has never seen deeper — **and many microwaves exceed this and simply will not fit a wall unit.** Real case from her practice: even 40–45 cm columns did not accept the chosen model. She names this as a concrete argument for author's supervision («авторский надзор»).
- **She dislikes both built-under and wall-mounted microwaves ergonomically** — low is awkward for lifting a hot dish out, high is worse — while acknowledging there is often no choice. On the worktop she considers it "не комильфо."
- **Align appliance edges to a continuous horizontal.** A single microwave's lower edge should sit on the same line as the neighbouring base tops; a drawer beneath it should repeat a height used elsewhere. **A microwave and oven stacked in one column break that line by necessity** — otherwise the oven sits too low — and the outlet then goes either in the plinth or as **a shared double behind the microwave** (preferred over two separate ones: the microwave is shallower than the oven, so there is more room behind it).
- **Governing composition rule, which is also what fixes the outlet lines**: "чем больше у вас повторяется размеров, тем лучше это выглядит" — repeat and alternate a small set of dimensions across the run rather than using many distinct ones.
- **Powered lift-ups for upper cabinets solve a real conflict**: a wide (e.g. 60 cm) upward-opening unit looks calm and avoids the head-strike of a side-opening door that size, but a heavy raised door is then hard to reach and pull closed. A servo with a small button handles both directions. Needs a stub and a hidden driver block at design time.
- **Sensor-operated pull-out bin — genuinely convenient with full or dirty hands, with one reported downside from use: the sensor LED is permanently lit**, which she found irritating in a dark kitchen. Foot-pedal or knee-press servo alternatives give the same hands-free result without the standing light.
- **⚠️ Concealing an outlet in a cabinet back — her solution is a hatch («лючок»).** On cheaper carcasses (ЛДСП with a thin hardboard back) the on-site cut-out is visibly rough. Make the back panel a hatch with the shelves above and below **fixed**, so the plug and cut-out are never seen in daily use and the shelf is only cleared on the rare occasions access is needed.
- **Appliance garage with a door**: cutting an outlet hole into it is ugly; better is an outlet that pulls forward so its frame seats flush with no visible cut-out — but this **requires the electrician to attend the kitchen installation**, which she notes is not always easy to arrange.
- **⚠️ Two fittings she judges portfolio-only, in the same words both times — "хорошо выглядит в портфолио, плохо во всех остальных ситуациях"**: a **sliding backsplash** hiding outlets in extra worktop depth (a permanently plugged-in kettle means the panel never closes), and **outlets mounted under the wall units** (a clean backsplash until anything is permanently connected, then a cord hangs down it — worse than the standard position).
- **Invisible / under-worktop hob**: the worktop stays usable for cutting when off, and burners can be spread along its length rather than clustered. Costs: a ceramic worktop is required and is more expensive, **and if the hob fails the worktop must be dismantled to extract it from below** — she confirmed this with a showroom directly.
- **"Eco kitchen," asked and answered honestly (worth recording for the reasoning, not the conclusion).** The one concrete lever is bin capacity for waste sorting — obstructed by the fact that bins usually land in a corner base whose dead volume "magic corner" fittings only partly recover. **She then undercuts her own answer**: you sort the waste and the carcass is still ЛДСП, "это вообще не экологичный материал." Her framing is that these are trade-offs chosen consciously, not solved — parallels drawn to real vs. engineered stone, and to cotton curtains as "адски непрактичная вещь" against a synthetic blend.

## Small-Kitchen Appliance Sizing (added 2026-08-20)

Companion to [[03_Kitchen/Kitchen_General|Kitchen — Layout & Configuration]]'s small-kitchen layout principles — appliance-specific sizing/type decisions for a genuinely constrained footprint.

- **⚠️ Fridge decision tree, by actual usage**: if you cook regularly and store real quantities of food, use a **freestanding (non-built-in) fridge even in a tiny kitchen** — it holds more usable volume than a built-in of the same external height. If the kitchen is small *and* lightly used (e.g. the fridge mostly holds delivery/takeout leftovers, no real freezer need), skip the tall vertical fridge entirely and use a small fridge built into a lower cabinet module instead.
- **⚠️ Dishwasher compact-size compromise**: this store's existing guidance defaults to a 60cm dishwasher; for a small kitchen with genuinely light use (little cooking, few dishes), a **45cm compact dishwasher is the accepted compromise size** — explicitly framed by the source as a deliberate tradeoff for a space-constrained kitchen, not a universally-preferred size.
- **⚠️ Cooktop sizing for a small kitchen: 2 burners maximum, never 4.** For genuinely infrequent cooking (roughly weekly or less), consider a **portable plug-in induction cooktop** instead of a fixed built-in unit — plug it in when needed, store it away afterward, and keep the countertop clear the rest of the time.
- **⚠️ Combined oven+microwave unit recommended as the default heating appliance for a small kitchen** — one multi-functional footprint instead of two separate appliances, sized to fit a **tall pantry column** that also uses the room's full vertical height (consistent with the vertical-storage principle on the Kitchen — Layout page) — freeing the rest of the run for additional shelving/pull-out drawer storage.

`single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_ihx8gUDO3vI_kruglov_small_kitchen|YT_ihx8gUDO3vI]]]

## Integrated Downdraft-Extractor Cooktop — Floor Ventilation Routed Before Screed (Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario, added 2026-08-28, Round 16)

**⚠️ A cooktop with an integrated (in-counter/downdraft) extraction system needs its own ventilation trench routed through the floor *before* the screed is poured**, not retrofitted afterward — on a real jobsite, the floor-level duct run for a planned downdraft cooktop location was installed and confirmed prior to the screed pour, alongside the electrical cable run feeding that same cooktop. A concrete sequencing dependency worth flagging early in design for anyone specifying a downdraft-style cooktop rather than a conventional overhead hood. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_A16VC0VYjSQ_kruglov_5_site_tour|YT_A16VC0VYjSQ]]]
