## Layout, the Work Triangle and Open-Plan Constraints

### ⚠️ Six Non-Obvious Kitchen Mistakes — Integration, Ergonomics, and the Work Triangle (Игорь Краснов, added 2026-09-01)

[source: [[_Sources/YT_LU35znr1jVM_krasnov_kitchen_6mistakes|LU35znr1jVM]]]

- **⚠️ Fridge must be flush-integrated, with mandatory ventilation clearances**: a fridge that visibly protrudes from the cabinetry line reads as an afterthought and can obstruct neighbouring cabinet doors or the walkway. Fix: a depth-matched integrated panel, or a purpose-built niche with side panels/overhead cabinetry visually absorbing the fridge into the run. **Minimum ventilation clearances: 2 cm sides, 2.5 cm back, 2–4 cm top** — check the manufacturer's own spec. Built-in fridges are not inherently pricier than freestanding — **the genuinely expensive configuration is side-by-side**, not built-in vs. freestanding as such.
- **⚠️ Upper-cabinet-to-ceiling gap is an instant "mass-market" tell**, especially with stock/modular kitchens where cabinet height is fixed but ceiling height varies. The empty strip visually lowers the room and is a hard-to-clean dust/grease trap with no storage value. **Three fixes**: cabinets built to full ceiling height; a second tier of upper cabinets (recovers storage); or a decorative panel/cornice/dropped-ceiling detail closing the gap. Best answer: a made-to-order kitchen sized to the actual ceiling height.
- **Microwave over the cooktop breaks the upper-cabinet sightline and usually forces sacrificing a proper hood** for that position, letting grease/steam settle on nearby fronts and walls. **Preferred placement: microwave stacked with a built-in wall oven as one compact module**, microwave's lower edge at roughly **chest height** — corroborates and adds a specific module configuration to this page's existing built-in-appliance-height content elsewhere in this vault.
- **⚠️ Kitchen backsplash (фартук) is functionally necessary, despite the current no-backsplash Pinterest trend**: a bare painted/plastered wall behind the worktop picks up grease/water/steam stains within months and gets progressively harder to clean. Modern alternatives beyond tile: tempered glass panels, porcelain slab, acrylic/aluminium panels, stainless steel — all wipeable and heat/chemical-resistant. A backsplash colour-matched to the wall keeps a minimalist look while retaining full protective function.
- **⚠️ Kitchen ergonomics, a cluster of figures**: **countertop height default 86 cm, but 92–95 cm for tall users** — height should be chosen per household. Rule of thumb: standing straight with elbow bent 90°, leave **10–15 cm clearance** between palm and countertop. **Upper-cabinet lower edge: 55–60 cm above the countertop.** **Walkway/aisle width: 100–110 cm** between cabinet runs/island/wall (two people passing, doors/drawers opening); **90 cm minimum in genuinely compact kitchens.**
- **⚠️ The classic "work triangle" (sink–stove–fridge), fully quantified**: **sink-to-fridge 1.2–2 m; sink-to-stove ≈1 m; stove-to-fridge 1.5–3 m; perimeter sum of all three legs within 4–8 m; enclosed triangle area 5–7 m².** **Landing zones**: **40–60 cm** of clear countertop beside the stove/oven/fridge for hot pans — even this modest amount meaningfully improves function and safety.
- **⚠️ Waterfall countertops — an honest tradeoff, not a hard mistake**: visually striking, reads monolithic, works well in **large kitchens**; in **small kitchens can overwhelm and visually shrink the space.** More material and requires precise, skilled installation (mismatched seams destroy the monolithic effect). Blocks open shelving/seating at the island end. Higher-maintenance stone (periodic resealing) vs. a lower-maintenance porcelain/ceramic alternative mimicking the look.

`single-account`, `unverified`.

### ⚠️ Work-Triangle Refinement, Cabinet Mechanisms, and Ergonomics Cluster (Игорь Краснов, added 2026-09-01, Round 3)

[source: [[_Sources/YT_gJfXl9Q3CIw_krasnov_kitchen_14mistakes|gJfXl9Q3CIw]]] — **the round's densest single source, 18 distinct facts.**

- **⚠️ Sink-to-cooktop distance, refining this page's existing work-triangle content above (`LU35znr1jVM`, Round 1)**: explicitly states **80% of all kitchen actions happen in the zone between the sink and the cooktop**, and this distance should be minimum **80-90cm**. Cross-reference Round 1's sink-to-stove ≈1m figure — treated as a refinement (an explicit minimum floor plus the "80% of actions" framing), not a contradiction.
- **⚠️ Cabinet mechanism placement — a real, specific, checkable rule**: avoid push-to-open mechanisms on LOWER cabinets (trigger accidentally from a knee/hip bump while working nearby) and avoid lift-up/flip-up mechanisms on UPPER cabinets (short users can't reach to close them without a step stool; heavy fronts wear out the lift hardware faster — a specific client anecdote of replacing lift-up hardware five times). **Correct approach**: push-to-open only on UPPER cabinets (used less often, avoids fingerprint smudges accumulating where lower fronts get touched constantly); everywhere else use integrated pulls or visible/graspable handles instead of push-to-open.
- **⚠️ Sink setback from the counter's front edge**: set back **5-10cm from the front edge of the counter** rather than flush with it, so you don't lean/reach forward while washing dishes — reduces back/neck strain during extended dishwashing.
- **⚠️ Oven and microwave heights, nuancing this page's existing appliance-height content (`LU35znr1jVM`, Round 1) rather than flatly agreeing**: oven should be at **waist height** (not chest height as some sources suggest) for safe/comfortable access to hot trays; microwave should sit **slightly below eye level** (not above it) to avoid the burn/spill risk of pulling a hot bowl down from above head height. **Round 1's `LU35znr1jVM` recommended a microwave stacked with a built-in wall oven as one compact module, microwave's lower edge at roughly chest height** — these may describe genuinely different configurations (a stacked oven+microwave column vs. a separate microwave placement) rather than a true contradiction. Recorded side by side rather than merged; revisit if a third source clarifies which configuration is being described.
- **⚠️ Countertop height — a specific, individualized formula, distinct from this page's existing flat 86cm/92-95cm figures**: measure your elbow's bent-arm crease height, then SUBTRACT 15cm — that is the correct individual countertop height. Even a 3-5cm deviation causes measurable discomfort, especially for tall users who end up bumping their forehead reaching for the sink or compensating with spinal curvature. A more precise, individualized method than this page's flat height ranges — cross-reference both rather than picking one as "more correct."
- **Avoid placing an oven directly beside a window with curtains** — grease/steam splatter stains curtain fabric quickly, heat exposure can yellow or deform fabric over time, and there's a real fire risk from fabric near a hot oven.
- **⚠️ Under-cabinet task lighting installation detail**: mount the LED strip/fixture **5-10cm forward from the wall, near the front edge of the cabinet bottom**, rather than tucked flush against the back wall — light aimed straight down at the counter reads much brighter/more useful than light hitting the backsplash/wall first. Recommends narrow LED strips or linear fixtures with a matte/frosted diffuser, colour temperature **3500-4500K neutral white** — a range slightly wider than this vault's existing single-4000K figure (`03_Kitchen/Kitchen_Furniture.md`); a refinement, not a contradiction.
- **⚠️ Dedicated electrical circuits required for high-draw appliances** (cooktop, oven) — cannot share standard household wiring circuits, which will overheat; requires separate dedicated lines run to separate breakers in the panel.
- **⚠️ Backsplash installation sequencing**: install and level the cabinet carcasses FIRST, verify their exact dimensions and front-face overhang, and only THEN install the backsplash tile/panel to fit flush. Installing the backsplash before the cabinets forces force-fitting tile edges around cabinet variations later, filling gaps with a thick silicone bead (looks unfinished) and risks damaging the backsplash material during the subsequent cabinet-mounting process.
- **⚠️ Small-appliance outlet clearance and positioning around the cooktop and sink**: outlets for small countertop appliances (kettle, toaster, blender) should sit roughly **10-20cm below/away from the cooktop's bottom edge** so cords don't dangle across a hot surface. Never place an outlet directly behind/opposite the cooktop or directly behind the sink — maintain a horizontal offset of roughly **20cm from the sink edge** and **15-20cm from the cooktop** to avoid water/hot-oil splash reaching the outlet.
- **Hidden storage/appliance "garage" for small countertop appliances** (blender, kettle, multicooker) — a dedicated cabinet nook or pull-out shelf under the counter keeps these off the visible countertop.

### ⚠️ L-Shaped Kitchen Mistake Cluster (Игорь Краснов, added 2026-09-01, Round 6)

[source: [[_Sources/YT_QFa98_RyV0M_krasnov_lshaped_kitchen_mistakes|QFa98_RyV0M]]] — this round's single-topic deep-dive on L-shaped ("угловая") kitchens.

- **⚠️ Corner dishwasher placement — a two-layer failure mode**: a dishwasher tucked into the inside corner of an L-run can fail to open fully because its door strikes the adjacent perpendicular cabinet; a spacer/setback panel fixes the clearance. **The deeper, more commonly missed problem**: the sink and dish-rack must ALSO be planned around the dishwasher's corner position so loading/unloading doesn't require standing in the corner — otherwise this is one of the worst possible dishwasher placements.
- **⚠️ Conflicting cabinet-door swing radii at an L-junction**: adjacent fronts meeting at a corner can physically collide when opened together — must be resolved at the planning/furniture-design stage (draw door-swing radii on the plan), not discovered after installation.
- **Corner sink placement critiqued as inherently awkward**: requires reaching further into the corner, and typically conflicts with the sink-stove-fridge work-triangle since a corner position rarely sits efficiently between the other two points.
- **Dishes stored far from the dishwasher** forces walking back and forth across the kitchen between load point (sink) and unload point (dish rack/cabinet) — extends this page's existing kitchen-ergonomics content to the L-shaped case specifically.
- **⚠️ Freestanding oven-column horizontal placement — distinct from this vault's existing oven mounting-HEIGHT content (a different axis: horizontal proximity to counter, not vertical height)**: an oven column placed far from the main counter run forces walking extra distance with a hot tray — a real safety issue, not just convenience. Correct placement: an oven column adjacent/flush to the main countertop run so a hot tray can be set down immediately on adjacent open counter. A coffee machine, by contrast, can sit further away since it doesn't carry the same hot-tray urgency.
- **Work triangle, applied to the L-shaped-kitchen case**: fridge → sink (wash) → prep → stove (cook); scattering the three points far apart across an L-run is both potentially unrealizable given plumbing/structural constraints and simply exhausting daily.
- **A first-person disclosure**: Krasnov personally prefers linear/straight-run kitchens visually, yet chose an L-shaped kitchen for his own home specifically because it's more functional for his actual cooking habits — a real example of prioritizing functional fit over pure aesthetic preference.
- **Final framing**: none of these L-shaped-kitchen mistakes are about budget — all are pure planning/ergonomics issues.

`single-account`, `unverified`.

### Open-Plan Kitchen-Living Acoustic, Odor, and Regulatory Constraints (Игорь Краснов, added 2026-09-01, Round 6)

[source: [[_Sources/YT_yg5ul2oeCuM_krasnov_kitchen_living_combo_mistake|yg5ul2oeCuM]]] — **⚠️ note the unusual statistics-heavy tone of this source; all uncredited percentage claims are excluded here, only the specific checkable figures below are extracted.**

- **⚠️ Kitchen extractor-hood noise**: a hood at high power can produce noise up to **70 dB**, comparable to street-traffic noise — directly relevant to open-plan kitchen-living acoustic planning.
- **Cooking-odor persistence on textiles** (cited as a research finding, tagged `unverified`): fried onion/fish/spice odor molecules can settle on rugs/upholstery/curtains and remain detectable for up to **48 hours**.
- **⚠️ Gas-stove building-code constraint — generic, non-jurisdiction-specific**: many countries' building codes prohibit a gas stove in a fully open (partition-free) kitchen-living space — requires a sliding/removable partition to satisfy fire-safety code even within an otherwise "open" plan. Presented generically, not Russia-specific — kept here as general technical content, **not routed to `16_Legal_and_Regulations/`** per that folder's strict Belarus-only scope.
- **⚠️ Sink-to-sewage-stack drainage distance**: should not exceed **3–4 meters** without pump/lift equipment, for proper gravity-fed drainage.
- **Walkway/clearance for open-plan zoning**: main circulation walkways minimum 90cm; comfortable bar-counter use requires ≥90cm clearance; **kitchen island width should be at least 80–100cm** — a specific island-sizing figure not clearly logged elsewhere on this page.
- **Zoning-without-walls summary**: flooring-material transitions (tile/porcelain in the kitchen zone vs. wood/engineered board in the living zone); sliding or glass partitions (thin-framed or frosted/matte glass) to close off the kitchen zone when needed without permanently blocking light.
- **⚠️ Style-mismatch-in-open-plan reasoning**: an open kitchen-living space requires STRONGER overall stylistic coherence than separated rooms, since a mismatch (his example: ornate classic kitchen fascias next to industrial concrete-panel living-room walls) reads far more jarring with no wall between the zones.

`single-account`, `unverified`.

### U-Shaped Solo-Use Kitchen, Kitchen-Rug Context, and Glass-Front Cabinet Endorsement (Игорь Краснов / Dakota Johnson, added 2026-09-02, Round 7)

Real celebrity-home critique format — Dakota Johnson's own kitchen, her own words kept distinct from Krasnov's critique. [source: [[_Sources/YT_lFySLwtUKW4_krasnov_dakota_johnson_home|lFySLwtUKW4]]]

- **⚠️ U-shaped compact kitchen — solo-use finding, distinct from this page's L-shaped-kitchen content (`QFa98_RyV0M`)**: works well for one person's own cooking rhythm/routine but becomes genuinely cramped with two people cooking simultaneously — a "U-shaped suits solo use, not shared cooking" finding, attributed to Johnson's own kitchen and Krasnov's synthesis.
- **A rug on a kitchen floor** — a specific, honest, context-dependent critique: adds warmth/breaks sterility of metal/tile, but only practical for someone who doesn't cook often; explicitly notes this specific example works BECAUSE the rest of the kitchen is otherwise restrained/minimal — if there were more color/texture elsewhere, the rug would visually overload the space.
- **Glass-front upper cabinets specifically praised as a good choice WHEN the displayed contents are genuinely worth looking at** — a specific conditional endorsement, distinct from this vault's many existing open-shelving critiques (glass-FRONT cabinets specifically, not open shelves).
- **Final kitchen framing**: functionality + one well-chosen accent color + genuine daily use/life happening in the room reads as more successful than many "designer" kitchens that are never actually used.

## Specification, Dimensions and What a Typical Project Contains

### The Typical-Project Kitchen, Written Down (Надежда Кузина, added 2026-09-01)

Reverse-engineered from the convergence in Russian design portfolios and delivered as satire — **but she is explicit that the recipe works and produces what designers sell.** It reads as a do-list or an avoid-list depending on whether you want the kitchen to look conventional. [source: [[_Sources/YT_zrnhNEgk-Sk_kuzina_typical_project_recipe|YT_zrnhNEgk-Sk]]]

- **⚠️ The ideal layout in this idiom is a single linear run beginning and ending with a tall column — the "кухня-рамка" (framed kitchen).**
- **Fronts**: a small profile if classical; **flat with no visible handles** if contemporary. Either way — **all fronts the same shape, all matte, all solid (no glazing), in cream / light grey / light beige.**
- **Backsplash: porcelain or quartz, marble-effect, pale veining, grey or golden** — most often Calacatta. **The worktop should resemble the backsplash**; if the backsplash is quartz, the worktop probably is too.
- **⚠️ A free-standing extractor is only ever cylindrical** — either fully integrated and invisible, or a tube.
- **⚠️ One rule sets the metal finish across five items at once: classical → gold; contemporary → black.** Pendant, table base, chair legs, handles and any visible extractor. *(Cross-check against the metal-finish hierarchy on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Combining_Finishes_Wood_Metal_and_Gloss|Combining Finishes — Wood, Metal and Gloss]], which is a much more developed treatment from the same practitioner.)*
- **Handles either recessed or long surface-mounted, most often gold.** The same rule governs wardrobe fronts elsewhere in the flat.
- **Small kitchens get round tables more often than rectangular**, with a light, thin worktop. **Chairs: metal legs with a visible frame, or wooden legs tapering downward; soft backs wrapping around the sitter.**
- **Kitchen floor: marble-effect tile, wood-effect tile, or wood-effect laminate.** If marble-effect porcelain, **the same material usually runs onto the backsplash**, always light — near-white with grey or golden-beige veining.

### Countertop-Height-for-Window Case and Same-Color-Different-Texture Cabinet Fronts (Игорь Краснов, real Peterhof project, added 2026-09-02, Round 7)

- **⚠️ Stepped/split countertop-height case, a real concrete architecture-interior coordination detail**: the counter height had to be LOWERED at one specific section specifically so the window directly above it could physically open — a real, concrete "counter height compromise for window operability" example.
- **⚠️ Same-color-different-texture material-variation technique, genuinely reusable and named**: using the SAME base color across multiple cabinet-front textures (smooth with hidden handles, milled/fluted, glass-fronted) creates visual interest and material variety WITHOUT introducing new colors — Krasnov's own explicit framing: "просто обычные законы дизайна" (just the laws of design).
- Named real brands mentioned in this project (flag per advertising filter): Cattelan Italia (dining/coffee tables, granite tops), a "лезер"/"leather-look" textured granite countertop finish from Italy, NEFF and Liebherr appliances.

### ⚠️ Narrow-Cabinet Waste, Rental-vs-Owned Drawer Tiering, the Island-Plumbing Floor-Raising Constraint, and the "Чёрная Кухня"/"Парадная Кухня" Dual-Kitchen Concept (Игорь Краснов, added 2026-09-02, Round 10)

**Genuinely rich source, 9 numbered points, several novel items.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_FsGLnB6llsw_krasnov_useless_kitchen_items2|FsGLnB6llsw]]]

- **⚠️ Narrow bottle/spice cabinet insert ("узкая секция под маслёнки/специи") critiqued, with a cited storage-science claim**: sunflower oil should be refrigerated, not stored at room temperature near the stove; spices can go in a regular upper cabinet — so this narrow, structurally-more-complex, higher-cost insert is largely wasted. Recommends a standard drawer instead — cheaper, same useful capacity.
- **⚠️ Lower-cabinet hinged doors vs. drawers, with a genuinely new rental-vs-owned decision tier**: cheaper hinged-door cabinets for a rental/investment unit, drawers unconditionally for an owner-occupied home.
- Open dish-drying racks left on the countertop waste counter space and read visually messy — commonly seen in short-term rentals specifically.
- **⚠️ Bar-height counter + full dining table combined in a small kitchen-living space — a specific real threshold**: pick ONE for compact layouts; this combination genuinely works only once the combined kitchen-living area reaches roughly **50–60 m²**, where the dining table can be placed well clear of the kitchen counter zone.
- **⚠️ Kitchen islands in apartments — a real infrastructure/plumbing constraint distinct from generic footprint advice**: in an apartment (unlike a private house), plumbing for an island sink or extraction/hood commonly needs to run to a floor waste stack at a slope, which typically requires RAISING the floor level in that zone — a concrete reason islands with plumbing are often impractical in apartments specifically. Recommends against an island purely for the aspirational "cooking at an island" idea without this infrastructure reality in mind.
- **⚠️ Gadget-zone design-sequencing principle**: plan DEDICATED zones for specific appliances (coffee machine, kettle) at the design stage so the REAL remaining usable counter space is known upfront — concrete example: a 1.2m run reduced to just 30cm of usable counter after appliances are added post-hoc.
- **⚠️ Particleboard (ДСП) countertops with plastic corner-joint trim strips** — cheapest, visually unattractive, and functionally risky: if a silicone seal fails, moisture swells the chipboard core, requiring full replacement. Avoid regardless of budget in favor of any solid/composite alternative.
- **⚠️ Backsplash rail/utensil-hanging systems ("рейлинги") — a nuanced, style-contextual qualification of this page's existing flatter critique (`ddRN-vIf3iI`, Round 9, above)**: rails ARE contextually appropriate in certain styles (e.g. American/country-kitchen) where they read as functional and coherent, but genuinely wrong in minimalist, Japandi, or Art Deco contexts, where the clutter/grease downside isn't offset by any stylistic payoff. **The real principle is CONTEXT-DEPENDENCE, not a blanket rule** — this page's existing entry stands as the general case; this is the qualification.
- **⚠️ Open shelving — a narrow, genuinely novel exception, distinct from the general dust/grease-magnet critique**: legitimate only in a "parade kitchen" ("парадная кухня", quoted inline) — a real practice in some wealthy Russian/CIS households of maintaining a SEPARATE "black kitchen" ("чёрная кухня", quoted inline) where actual cooking happens (often staffed by a private chef), while the visible "parade" kitchen serves only a display/coffee-making function maintained by household staff. A real, named dual-kitchen concept, explicitly applicable only to large/staffed households — not broadly relevant to a standard apartment renovation, but genuinely new for this vault.

### ⚠️ Ten Kitchen Design Mistakes From the Fabricator's Side — Dimensions, Plinths, and a Hardware Failure Mechanism (FLAT, added 2026-09-02)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A furniture manufacturer's own channel** (Михаил, owner of FLAT, St Petersburg) — advertising filter applies by default. **`promotional_ratio: low` here, and the pivotal item argues against his own margin** (see the allocation rule below). Closing advice is to have *someone* competent audit your project, not necessarily to buy from him. RU, 2026-01. [source: [[_Sources/YT_CEKciia5vh0_flat_ten_kitchen_design_mistakes|CEKciia5vh0]]]

#### ⚠️ Working dimensions, with his own definitions

- **⚠️ Minimum ~1,000 mm of work surface — and he defines the term, which matters because it is used loosely: the distance from the hob to the sink**, or from the hob to a tall unit depending on configuration. Below that it is uncomfortable, especially for people who cook actively.
- **⚠️ Depth: the trend is away from 600 mm toward 700 or even 750 mm — but there is a ceiling and he gives it: "больше 800 я бы не делал."** Deeper and you cannot reach across, which is its own problem.
- **⚠️ Sink: for a 600 mm cabinet take the largest bowl the cabinet allows. 400 mm is too small for active cooking; 550 mm is acceptable; larger is better. A drainer wing occupies roughly 250 mm — and that comes straight off the work surface**, so it trades directly against the 1,000 mm minimum. **Under-mounted (подклейка) looks better; surface-mounted — "нет."**
- **Quartz countertops are generally 20–30 mm; at 30 mm it starts to read heavy. ⚠️ A chamfer on the front edge makes a thick top look thinner while staying structurally thick.**

#### ⚠️ Plinth height — three standards, and a real appliance constraint

- **Standards: 100, 120, 150 mm.** **150 mm has no practical purpose in most cases** — the one justification is where the design uses tall skirting and the skirting meets the kitchen, so matching heights avoids cutting it and reads as one line.
- **Otherwise 100 mm is materially more aesthetic; in some cases they use 50 mm, more aesthetic again — with one catch.**
- **⚠️ The catch is a specification trap worth planning around: not every built-in dishwasher fits a 50 mm plinth.** Models that do are limited — **Smeg** now, **Siemens** formerly — **and cost around 30–40% more than a standard machine ("не чуть, прилично").** **So a 50 mm plinth is an appliance-budget decision, not a styling one.**

#### ⚠️ Under-cabinet lighting — position, not just presence

- **Place the LED profile at roughly the mid-depth of the wall units; for tall units, recess it 100 mm from the front of the carcass.**
- **⚠️ Putting it in the corner makes it decorative only — it washes the backsplash and leaves the backsplash and countertop unevenly lit.** The lighting has to work as task light while cooking.
- **⚠️ Power and switching must be agreed at design stage** — and this channel's separate source describes the failure where a kitchen arrives already built with lighting and nothing to power it (see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/Renovation_Sequence|Renovation Sequence]]).
- **On controls, from their own trials: hand-wave motion sensors are unreliable and need replacing often — use either an ordinary switch on the wall panel or a touch sensor.** **⚠️ A touch sensor with light indication is his recommendation: it glows faintly in the dark so you can find it without being dazzled**, where an unlit one has to be hunted for. **Nuances this vault's existing under-cabinet touch-sensor caution (Kruglov/Ontario) and Кузина's wall-switch-plus-proximity-sensor alternative on [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Switches_and_Controls|Switches & Controls]] — three accounts now, all sceptical of gesture control, differing on what replaces it.**

#### ⚠️ Hardware — the failure mechanism this vault did not hold

- **What he means by фурнитура: the runners a drawer rides on and the hinges that open a swing door.**
- **⚠️ Cheap hardware cannot be adjusted** — a facade cannot be set in all planes with correct gaps — **so the aesthetic result is unobtainable regardless of what else was spent.**
- **⚠️ The mechanism: a cheap hinge with cruciform mounting plates fixed on self-tappers gradually destroys the carcass. Nothing is visible for years, then the door stops working properly and "у тебя уже корпус весь в труху превратился вместе с креплениями петель." The repair is then a side panel, not a hinge — and for a central section, dismantling half the kitchen.** **Complements this page's existing hardware cycle-rating content (Blum 200,000+ cycles, Boyard/Firmax at 10–15 years) by supplying the collateral-damage path rather than a lifespan number.**
- **⚠️ A concrete instruction for corner mechanisms**: do not buy a cheap corner basket at **5,700–9,000 ₽ (2026-01) ≈ $70–$110** (12-month trailing mean to 2026-01-01, 83.2108 RUB/USD; comparability aid) — the stainless rusts, it works loose on cheap runners, the door hangs crooked. **"Купи нормальную, либо просто поставь полку. Просто полка будет намного лучше, чем вот эта вот дешёвая."** **LeMans is named as the optimal corner solution** if bought at proper quality.

#### ⚠️ Cost allocation — stated against his own margin

- **The mistake: good facades with a cheap countertop.** Expensive veneer or enamel under a plastic top "просто убьёшь весь дизайн. Это 100%."
- **⚠️ The correction, with the conflict flagged by him: "так нам как будто бы, как компании мебельной, в теории выгоднее продать кухню подороже" — but when a client wants to optimise cost they insist on the reverse: simpler facades (ЛДСП, laminate, a plastic) with a well-chosen colour, plus a stone countertop. "Это будет намного лучше."**
- **Corroborates the "economise on what is replaceable, never on what is buried" principle already on [[11_Budget_and_Planning/analysis/cost_saving_strategies_full|Cost Saving Strategies]]** — here applied *within* one object rather than across a renovation, and by the trade that would profit from the opposite advice.

#### Colour, the island, and the backsplash

- **⚠️ A kitchen reads laconic with a maximum of two colours, plus a third as the countertop.** The failure case: base units one colour, tall units a second, wall units a third, countertop a fourth. **⚠️ And an equally useful permission: do not try to match everything — appliances, handles and profiles carry their own shades, and internal hardware and drawers will never be one tone and need not be.** Full monochrome "будет выглядеть как раз скучно."
- **⚠️ The matching criterion is warmth, not hue: "они должны быть подобраны по теплоте оттенков."** Warm wood needs warm stone; a warmth mismatch produces dissonance.
- **⚠️ "Остров ради острова" — two tests.** An island needs enough space; forcing one into a small kitchen-living room is wrong and **in most such cases a kitchen plus a kitchen table is the more logical pairing.** **And if the island has neither a hob nor a sink built into it, it is also an island for its own sake.** **A third, constructional check: islands are often designed so you cannot sit at them — no leg room.**
- **Terminology: «фартук» is the domestic word; the trade term is «стеновая панель».** **Return "ears" (загибы) on it look unprofessional** — without side pieces the wall material simply shows, which is fine.
- **⚠️ Wall-panel installation order is conditional and there is no universal answer — the sequencing item to carry: closed contour (base units, wall units and two tall units) → install the wall panel BEFORE the kitchen. Open contour (tall units top and bottom only) → wall panel AFTER the kitchen is installed.** **Colour: in ~90% of kitchens the wall panel matches the countertop's colour and material.**

#### ⚠️ His own kitchen, as a worked failure — specify mixer and sink together

- He bought an expensive German mixer with a dual outlet and filtered water, then **economised on the sink from a chain retailer at the end of the renovation with budgets overrunning.**
- **The failure: the stream lands directly on the drain valve and sprays across the whole kitchen.**
- **⚠️ Why it was never fixed: the sink is bonded into a quartz-agglomerate top and is a non-standard size, so correcting it means dismantling part of the kitchen, lifting the countertop, un-bonding the sink and cutting a new opening. "Дорого, долго, больно." They lived with it five years.**
- **The rule: mixer and sink geometry are one decision, checked before either is bought — and a bonded sink in a stone top is effectively permanent.**

#### The "main mistake," in its usable form

- His headline is that designing the kitchen yourself is the main mistake — a person does it two or three times in a lifetime, and habits are not necessarily good ones.
- **⚠️ But the form of the advice is what makes it usable rather than a pitch: bring your own project and ask to have it audited.** He contrasts the two answers you may get — "окей, за деньги, давай сделаем, как ты хочешь" versus "у тебя здесь такие-то ошибки, давайте оптимизируем" — **and says to choose the second.** Ask directly whether anything is wrong and what they would recommend.
- **⚠️ And a caution against copying: "не всегда правильная позиция смотреть, что где сделано у других. У каждого человека свой сценарий эксплуатации кухонной мебели."** Consistent with this vault's scenario-driven planning content.

**⚠️ Caveats: the dimensional figures are his studio's working minima, not standards — no code or ergonomics source is cited anywhere. The 30–40% dishwasher premium has no base price attached. And the 7 m island he shows is his own showroom, not a delivered domestic project.**

## ⚠️ RUSSIAN Legality and Gas Constraints

### ⚠️ Gas Appliances and Pipework May Not Be Boxed In (Надежда Кузина, added 2026-09-01)

**This vault has had no gas-safety content at all, which is the kind of gap that only shows up when something goes wrong.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_UnCjxyDtWG0_kuzina_tiktok_lifehacks_debunked|UnCjxyDtWG0]]]

Debunking a short-form clip showing a false panel neatly hiding "ugly pipes" in a kitchen. **She pauses it and identifies what is actually being enclosed**: two flexible hoses with something yellow — **gas pipes**; behind them cold and hot water marked red and blue; and a hatch that appears to be **ventilation for a gas water heater.**

> [!WARNING]
> **⚠️ Enclosing a gas water heater in a cabinet is PROHIBITED, as is hiding gas pipes behind panels, inside cabinets, or chasing them into walls.**
>
> **The consequences she names, in order: "это может привести к пожару или к взрыву газа, или как минимум к долгим и не очень приятным обсуждениям с газовой службой."**
>
> **⚠️ Jurisdiction: she is describing Russian practice and names «газовая служба».** The prohibition is near-universal in substance, **but the specific rules and the enforcing body differ — verify against Belarusian norms before relying on this, and do not treat it as settled for this apartment.**

**And the point about the format, which is why it is worth recording**: the clip shows only *"какой крутой лайфхак, как всё аккуратненько и как всё прекрасно сделано."* **The neatness is exactly what makes it persuasive.**

### ⚠️ RUSSIAN Kitchen Legality — the Kitchen Niche's Three Conditions, and the Gas Workaround (Татьяна Безверхая, added 2026-09-02)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Russian norms, jurisdiction flagged, and deliberately NOT routed to `16_Legal_and_Regulations/`** (Belarus-only). No code cited; `single-account`, `unverified`. **The full set of ten prohibitions, the ground-floor exception, the neighbour-below constraint and the resale mechanism are consolidated on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Whole_Home_Planning_Method|Whole-Home Planning Method]]** — only the kitchen-specific items are here. [source: [[_Sources/YT_JgDzAoWlyJI_bezverkhaia_ten_illegal_solutions|JgDzAoWlyJI]]]

- **⚠️ A kitchen may not be expanded at the expense of a bathroom/WC, nor a bathroom at the expense of a kitchen — "ни при каких случаях, даже на 10 см."** **Her stated rationale: even though both are wet zones, the law treats the swap as worsening your own and your neighbours' housing conditions.** **Nor may a kitchen be expanded onto a living room** — the common wish to open a kitchen into a living room — "никогда и ни при каких обстоятельствах."
- **⚠️ A kitchen niche (кухня-ниша) IS legal, and the three conditions are the actionable part.** A kitchen may be expanded onto or relocated onto non-living space including a corridor, subject only to:
  1. **area not less than 5 m²**;
  2. **the extract connected to the *kitchen* ventilation system, not the bathroom's vent duct — even if the bathroom's duct is closer**;
  3. **⚠️ no appliance — sink, refrigerator or hob — extending beyond the corridor's own boundary as drawn on the БТИ plan.**
  She reports fully approved projects of exactly this kind and calls it "очень крутое решение для многих квартир."
  - **⚠️ Read this together with the drain-run caution on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Whole_Home_Planning_Method|Whole-Home Planning Method]]** (Кузина): even where approved, the drain run grows from ~0.5 m to ~3 m, **so it clogs more and is harder to rod out. Legality and buildability are separate tests, and this source only settles the first.**
- **⚠️ A studio (open-plan) apartment is not permitted where there is gas.** With gas, **the kitchen must be separated from the rest of the flat by full interior doors with a tight closure (плотный притвор)** — sliding or hinged, single or double-leaf, the type is immaterial, but they must be real doors that close properly. **Stated rationale: in a gas leak the doors are deemed to hold gas out of the living rooms.** She is candid that these are Soviet-era norms kept in force while everyone leaves doors open, so the point is to work around them legally rather than argue.
- **⚠️ The workaround, which is the genuinely useful item: you may formally renounce gas.** Apply, **the gas pipe is cut and welded shut, and you receive a certificate that the apartment is not gasified** — after which, **if the electrical capacity allows**, an electric hob and an open-plan studio become possible. **The capacity condition is not incidental: her own sequencing source names checking the apartment's allocated kilowatts, and possibly buying more, as a step-two constraint** — see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/Renovation_Sequence|Renovation Sequence]].
- **⚠️ Ventilation and plumbing shafts may not be touched at all** — not reduced, not notched for niches or shelves. **The reasoning is ownership: by the documents the shaft may be common building property and not part of your apartment**, so apparent dead space inside it confers no right to encroach. Relevant to any kitchen-side boxing-in or niche assumption.
