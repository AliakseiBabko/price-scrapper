# Kitchen Furniture & Layout

## Cabinet Modules & Sizing

- **Default every cabinet module to a standard width (40/50/60cm etc.)** — custom-sized modules (e.g. 537mm, 649mm) typically carry a real cost premium and integrate less reliably with built-in appliances sized for standard modules. If a run's total length doesn't divide evenly, make only the *last* module in the run custom-sized so every other module stays a round 5cm/10cm multiple, or use a plain filler/spacer strip instead of resizing a functional cabinet.
- **Filler-strip technique, two concrete uses**: (1) between the fridge and an adjacent wall/window, sized so the fridge door swings a full 90-95° without hitting a nearby window-sill overhang — also eliminates a dead, unreachable gap behind the fridge that otherwise becomes an accumulation point for stray plastic bags; (2) a narrow pull-out filler strip beside the cooktop for oil/small-item storage, doubling as a buffer between the cooktop and an adjacent module (some cabinet manufacturers require a minimum cooktop-to-module gap, so including this strip by default keeps a layout compatible with more manufacturers).
- **Route plumbing/electrical for a kitchen wall run through a furring/false wall, never by chasing a load-bearing wall directly** — never disturb the load-bearing wall's internal rebar; build the furring wall out from it instead (worked example: a 50mm furring wall carrying sink/dishwasher/washing-machine plumbing).

## Appliance Placement

- **Tall units (fridge/oven column) go at the end of the run nearest natural light (a window), not mid-run** — a tall unit placed where the main prep zone would otherwise be casts a shadow over that zone; placing the tall unit in the shadowed position instead "sacrifices" a unit that doesn't depend on good lighting as much as a countertop workspace does. Mid-run placement of a tall unit is also flagged as visually disruptive.
- **Cluster plumbing-dependent appliances (sink, dishwasher, washing machine) near the riser/stack**; place the fridge and any tall column (neither needs water/drain) at the far end instead — minimizes plumbing run length/complexity.
- **Washing machine at the run's far end, away from the main cooking zone** — keeps laundry-related activity (detergent, dirty laundry) out of the active food-prep area.
- **Oven in a tall column, elevated above floor level** (not under the cooktop) where kitchen dimensions allow — easier to use without bending down.
- **Microwave needs adjacent countertop space, not fridge proximity** — the real usage sequence is fridge → plate/countertop → microwave → back to fridge (or eaten), not fridge-straight-to-microwave.
- **Ask the client which hand they use to hold a dish while scraping food waste before loading it into the dishwasher — that hand determines which side of the sink the dishwasher goes on.** A specific-preference check, not a generic right-handed assumption. **Corroborated and sharpened by an independent source (added 2026-08-18)**: for a right-handed dishwasher, place it to the sink's *left* — the plate stays in the working (right) hand near the sink, and the free (left) hand needs the dishwasher on that same side to load it without an awkward reach/twist.
- **Cutlery drawer near the dishwasher; large pot/pan drawers near the stove.**
- **Upper cabinets are unconstrained client preference** (no electrical/plumbing dependency, unlike lower cabinets) — the one designer obligation is planning task lighting for the prep zone beneath them.

## Cooking-Zone Definition

- **Primary cooking zone = the counter run between the sink and the cooktop** — where most hands-on food prep happens; maximize this span when laying out a kitchen. **Secondary zones** (outside that span) are the right place for "parking spot" storage of occasionally-used small appliances (e.g. a meat grinder stored in a drawer) or a permanently-plugged-in appliance kept out (e.g. a coffee machine on a dedicated countertop spot).

## Task Lighting & Countertop Material (added 2026-08-19)

- **Cooking-zone task lighting should be diffuse, not sharp directional spotlighting — a mechanism-explained rule, not just a style preference.** Any directional light source, however bright, casts a hand/head/body shadow directly onto the work surface as the cook leans in, risking both reduced visibility and unnoticed contamination (hair, debris) falling into food unseen. **Prefer a diffused light source over the primary cooking/prep zone specifically to avoid this shadow-casting failure mode.** `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_6syO3dTButw_designer_disaster_66m2_259|YT_6syO3dTButw]]]
- **Ultra-thin countertop material (as thin as 9mm) exists specifically to resolve a height conflict between a tall appliance (e.g. a maxi-height dishwasher) and a shorter user's ergonomic counter-height needs** — using a standard-thickness countertop in this situation pushes the whole counter uncomfortably high for a shorter user; the thin material keeps the counter at a workable height while still clearing the appliance. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_6syO3dTButw_designer_disaster_66m2_259|YT_6syO3dTButw]]]

## Space & Cost Tricks

- **Twin-fridge trick for a very wide fridge footprint**: install two ordinary standard-width (~60cm) fridges side-by-side facing each other instead of one wide built-in unit of the same total width. Claimed significantly cheaper than a single large built-in fridge, with the added benefit that one unit still functions as a partial backup if the other fails. `single-account`, `unverified` relative cost claim (no absolute figures given).
- **Prefer a fresh-zone-only fridge paired with a separate standalone freezer** over one combined fridge/freezer unit — substantially more usable freezer capacity than a combined unit's small freezer compartment. (added 2026-08-18)
- **Upper cabinets are frequently never actually used functionally in practice, per one practitioner's stated real-world observation across many projects** — a legitimate cost-saving measure is making them decorative-only (blank door fronts, no functional hinges/opening hardware, no interior fitting cost) with no visual downside. `single-account`. (added 2026-08-18)
- **Corner-kitchen-without-a-corner-module technique**: when the true corner is unusable for a full corner cabinet (a utility riser or adjacent bathroom wall intrudes into it), build a dedicated **60×60cm technical column** at that corner instead of defaulting to one cramped straight run — the two straight cabinet runs on either side dock cleanly against the column (their countertops don't need to form one continuous surface). (added 2026-08-18)

## Kitchen Island Sizing (added 2026-08-18)

- **An island is only appropriate in kitchens ≥14 m²** — smaller kitchens lack adequate passage clearance for one.
- **Clearance: minimum 80cm on every side except one facing a sofa/dining area** — anything less doesn't let two people pass comfortably, and if the tight side faces a window, curtains will catch on the island.
- **Minimum island width: 1 meter.** If available depth is ≤60cm, skip the island entirely.
- **Never split a 1m-wide island into two back-to-back storage zones accessed from opposite sides** — this eliminates all usable knee space, leaving the island functional only as storage, not as a seating/eating surface. For comfortable seated use, leave a **minimum 35cm (ideally 40cm) knee recess**.
- **Island height should match its actual intended use, not a universal standard**: cooking-zone height should match the main counter's chosen height (fix that first via showroom trials); bar/eating-surface height should be chosen by trying several stool heights — no single correct height exists for either use case.
- **Avoid countertop-embedded pop-up/retractable outlets on an island** — food/liquid residue reportedly jams the extend mechanism over time; prefer a flush, statically-lidded (non-extending) outlet housing instead.

## Cabinet & Countertop Assembly Technique (added 2026-08-18)

Two DIY masterclasses (companion sources, same channel) contribute a fully sequenced, self-managed-relevant install checklist — see [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_Y_Tuxcd-VxM_28_mistakes_lower_kitchen_modules_153|module-assembly note]] and [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_QXaSRAjfWwU_wildest_countertop_mistakes_201|countertop-install note]] for the full 28+15-item checklists. Highlights:

- **Assemble and fully install lower modules (leveled, joined, and countertop-fixing brackets/mounting rails checked) before starting upper modules or the countertop** — working out of order forces disruptive rework at every subsequent stage.
- **Level modules with a laser level before laying the countertop**, checking both height and depth (front-face plane) — un-leveled modules leave the countertop-fixing rails at mismatched heights or fronts in visibly different planes, both requiring a full teardown to fix.
- **Critical countertop-joint sealant rule**: apply a continuous sealant bead (≥3mm) along the full length of the joining plate before pressing two countertop pieces together; confirm visible squeeze-out (if none appears, too little sealant was applied); wipe excess only while wet, never after it cures. Skipping sealant, or under-applying it, guarantees the countertop and the cabinet module beneath the joint will swell/warp from water ingress.
- **Countertop overhang: 25-30mm, consistent along the full length** — too little exposes raw door-front edges; too much blocks upper-drawer opening on handleless (push-to-open) fronts.
- **Never drill your own confirmat pilot holes, and never fasten a back panel with regular screws** — factory pre-drilled holes are the only reliable fixing points; screws (vs. the manufacturer-supplied nails/staples) frequently split panel material, forcing a replacement and a multi-week wait for a custom part.

## Countertop Field-Trim Repair (added 2026-08-19)

> [!NOTE]
> A real field-recovery story, not a planned technique: a countertop delivered 6mm oversized by a separately-sourced materials supplier gets trimmed and reinstalled on-site. Extends (doesn't replace) the sealant-joint rule above. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_DHxz4KytQjU_countertop_field_trim_repair_165|note]]]

- **Supply-chain caution**: an exact-to-the-millimeter project and kitchen-manufacturer cut order doesn't guarantee an exact slab if the actual material is separately sourced from a building-materials market rather than supplied by the manufacturer directly.
- **On-site trim sequence**: unscrew countertop and joining plate, scrape old sealant off; tape the visible laminate front edge to prevent chip-out; flip face-down and cut from the *back* face with a fine-tooth mitre saw (dust-collected); re-verify the fixed dishwasher clearance (**450mm**) as the live reference before cutting; flip back over and **dry test-fit before any edge finishing** — a step people skip at their peril.
- **Silicone joint reassembly, time-gated**: re-tape the front edge, apply sealant to the cut edge and joining plate (matches the ≥3mm continuous-bead rule above), seat and screw (12-16mm self-tappers), wipe excess while wet, **wait 5-7 minutes for the sealant to begin curing before reinstalling** — a specific cure-time figure not previously recorded this precisely for this page. Reinstall the rear edge first, then the front.

## Freestanding-Fridge Niche Sizing (added 2026-08-19)

> [!NOTE]
> For a client who wants a freestanding (not built-in) fridge to sit flush in line with the rest of the cabinetry run rather than protruding into the walkway. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_fSEPr5fpfPM_kitchen_stubouts_ac_fridge_niche_166|note]]]

- **Why a niche is needed**: a quality freestanding fridge is typically slightly over 600mm deep (vs. a kitchen cabinet run's standard 600mm depth) and needs 50-70mm of rear clearance for its compressor/heat exchanger — a recessed niche solves the depth mismatch and the clearance requirement at once.
- **Niche dimensions**: recess 70-100mm behind the main cabinet face line; width and height each 50mm larger than the fridge's own external dimensions.
- **Finishing**: all outer/visible corners of the niche opening get a plastic corner bead (малярный уголок) during framing; the niche's side returns and back wall are finished with the same wall-finish material as the surrounding walls, not a contrasting one.

## Upper Module Assembly & Range-Hood Duct Cutout (added 2026-08-19)

> [!NOTE]
> Distinct from the lower-module content above — upper modules have their own hardware (eurorail hangers, range-hood duct routing) not present in a lower module. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_19AUqOlliys_upper_kitchen_module_assembly_161|note]]]

- **Back-panel sizing rule**: measure the module's rear opening width and add 4mm — that fit allowance, not the raw opening width, is the correct cut dimension.
- **Range-hood duct cutout, a 3-pass alternating-direction drilling technique to avoid chip-out on a laminated panel face**: mark the duct-hole center (front-face centerline, then 45mm back from the rear plane), center-punch, then cut with a wood-boring hole saw matched to the duct's own diameter in three passes — a shallow guide cut from the front until the pilot bit just breaks through the back, then roughly halfway from the back, then complete the cut from the front. Repeat the same sequence for the bottom panel, referencing the top panel's already-cut hole for alignment; for the back panel, mark and jigsaw-cut it into three pieces (discard the center piece, keep and refasten the two side pieces with 16mm screws and board-specific corner brackets).
- **Eurorail (евронавес) hanging-rail install**: seat flush against the side panel with its retaining hook engaged in a pre-cut notch, secure with screws, cap the fastener with a decorative cover.

**Additional mistakes to avoid, from a second, independent upper-module source (added 2026-08-19)** — [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_Ljji4-Sxmzk_upper_module_mistakes_189|note]]]:

- **Range-hood duct hole: oversize by a minimum of 10mm over the corrugated duct's own diameter, never an exact match** — an exact-diameter hole makes flexible corrugated tube difficult to feed through and reliably deforms it. (Complements, doesn't contradict, the rigid-duct cutting sequence above — this is specifically for the flexible corrugated section.)
- **Never fasten a back panel with self-tapping screws — staples or the supplied nails only**: screws split the side-panel material at the fastening point, forcing a full panel replacement and a multi-week wait for a custom part.
- **Back-panel fastening spacing: nail/staple the entire perimeter, ≤100mm apart — not just the four corners.** The back panel defines the module's overall squareness/rigidity; under-fastening lets it rack out of true over time.
- **Never skip the mounting strip (монтажная планка)**, even though it's sometimes dismissed as unnecessary — it ties the side panels together and locks the back panel in place; without it, the back panel can flex loose from vibration and pop out of its grooves, requiring a full disassemble-reinstall-reassemble cycle to fix.
- **Never unpack-and-assemble one module at a time** — unpack and inspect every module for shipping damage *before* starting assembly on any of them; once assembly has begun, no damage claim against the manufacturer is possible.
- **Wire/cable pass-through hole for under-cabinet lighting or a motion sensor**: drill on the same vertical line as the sensor/switch itself, as close to the module's bottom edge as practical — avoids a long, awkward router channel to reach a misaligned hole.

## Lower Module Assembly — Additional Techniques (added 2026-08-19)

> [!NOTE]
> Genuinely new content only — the general assembly principles (confirmat torque, two-person lift, diagonal-squareness check, eccentric-hardware sequencing, drawer-runner orientation) are already covered above from the existing 28-item checklist and not repeated here. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_-RsGqvH_hbo_lower_kitchen_module_assembly_148|note]]]

- **Countertop-support metal bracket placement**: offset 50mm from the module's side panel, front edge aligned to that mark, top edge aligned to the side panel's own top edge.
- **Inter-module tie (межсекционная стяжка) technique**: clamp adjoining modules together first; mark two drill points on the shared seam at the module's half-depth, ~50mm up from the bottom plane and ~50mm down from the top plane; drill through both side panels with a 5.5mm bit; vacuum thoroughly before inserting the tie hardware; thread the barrel-and-screw fastener through, holding the barrel stationary with a flat screwdriver while driving the screw.
- **Bottle-rack (бутылочница) module runner placement, a genuine exception to normal drawer-runner symmetry**: mount the two runners at *different* heights (one low, one high) rather than level with each other, as a normal drawer module would need — installing them level is a common, hard-to-notice-until-later error.
- **Cross-module height alignment**: transfer each module's design-spec height to a floor-referenced mark, set a laser level to that mark, then adjust each module's height-adjustable legs (clockwise lowers, counter-clockwise raises) until the top surface matches the laser plane — start from the corner/anchor module and align each subsequent module against both the laser and its already-set neighbor.
- **Cross-module depth alignment**: push all modules back against the wall, then use a long straightedge against the front faces to identify and pull forward any module sitting recessed relative to its neighbors.

## Cabinet-to-Ceiling Clearance & Built-In-Fridge Panel Alignment QC (added 2026-08-19)

> [!NOTE]
> Two corroborating Category 5 QC-critique sources, independent apartments/practitioners. [sources: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_ROv1BuBfECU_designer_disaster_35m_281|note 1]], [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_cWRgenv4B40_full_disaster_30m_274|note 2]], [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_EnSpVCSUiqg_designer_lost_it_apartment_307|note 3, chunk 1]]]

- **A "cabinet-to-ceiling" kitchen design must be checked against actual ceiling levelness and any recessed-light clearance before finalizing the upper-cabinet run's height — a real, corroborated rough-in-QC mechanism, not just a design nicety.** Two independent defects found: doors colliding with a slightly sagging ceiling and nearby recessed lights on opening, and the "wrong" fix in both cases — dropping the entire cabinet run down rather than adding the correct spacer/filler strip — leaving a dust-collecting gap at the top of the run too high to reach by hand. **Rule: survey the ceiling plane and any fixture clearances before specifying a flush-to-ceiling upper-cabinet height, and always use a spacer/filler strip to correct for minor unevenness rather than dropping the whole run.** [sources: notes 1, 2]
- **⚠️ A built-in refrigerator's door-panel type must be checked against the cabinetry's own swing/clearance design before installation — now a two-source corroborated finding.** Both documented defects had a fridge door colliding with an adjacent counter/cabinet on opening because the panel type didn't match the design; both were "fixed" by pulling the fridge forward out of flush alignment, and in one case, ordering a costly custom-matched replacement cabinet panel that still visibly "stepped" relative to its neighbors. **Rule: confirm a built-in fridge's actual door-panel/hinge clearance against the cabinetry's planned depth and swing path before committing to a flush-integration design**, not after the appliance is delivered. [sources: notes 2, 3]
