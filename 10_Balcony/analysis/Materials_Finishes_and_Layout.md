# Balcony/Loggia — Materials, Finishes, Lighting & Layout

Part of [[10_Balcony/Balcony_Guide|Balcony Guide]].

## Flooring / Subfloor

*Per Zemskov*: a complete insulated timber subfloor method exists (see [[10_Balcony/analysis/Source_Notes|Source Notes]]). Height governed by 3 hard constraints: step height ≤20cm at the doorway, final ceiling height ≥2.45m, final railing height ≥95cm (**≥110cm specifically if young children will use the loggia**) — plus a 4th, softer reason to raise ≥15cm (baseboard/trim clearance). Framing: 50×70mm timber, cross-joists set to the uneven slab, only the 2-3 longitudinal lags leveled precisely. Mineral wool insulation, full coverage including the section adjoining the "warm" apartment wall (a downstairs neighbor's own insulation does **not** warm this floor — a real myth Zemskov explicitly debunks). Sheathing: moisture-resistant chipboard (ДСП) over plywood/solid board (most durable of the three under humidity swings), 2-3mm sheet gaps to prevent squeaking. **Final step: seal the subfloor's own perimeter with foam** — the under-panel cavity is ventilated, and an unsealed perimeter lets wool fibers migrate into the room through the convection grilles.

**Baseboard**: *Placeholder* (Length: ~`8 m`) — not yet covered by a processed source; the general baseboard-height/trim-clearance interaction with subfloor height is noted above.

## Walls

*Per Zemskov*: PVC panel cladding remains a valid, well-tested choice for a temperature/humidity-swing space like this one, though modern decorative plaster now also works **if the space is genuinely well-insulated** — either way, some cracking along the frame's edge over time is never fully avoidable, per Zemskov's own honest caveat. PVC panel technique: seamless inner/outer corners (cut the panel's back rib, heat-bend — no separate corner trim profile needed), a retrofit method for hanging heavy items on finished panel cladding (a protruding dowel bushing prevents panel crushing), pre-attaching an outlet to the panel before installation (avoids a wobbly retrofit mount), and a fast cosmetic repair for scratches/holes (hole-saw + matching convection grille, disguises damage in minutes).

**Two budget wall-finishing options** (Петришин-Строй/Mosbuild expo, added 2026-08-24, Round 10): painted tongue-and-groove wood paneling ("вагонка"), a cheap standard option; and decorative façade plaster, which tolerates outdoor-style temperature swings (relevant for an unheated balcony) and can be stencil-patterned (e.g. a damask-style pattern to visually continue a classic-style interior onto the balcony) via gel/spatula/roller/brush application for varied textures — framed as visually nicer than plastic panel cladding at a comparable budget. `single-account`, region unresolved. [source: [[_Sources/YT_k9CrEU5RuIE_petrishin_flooring_baseboard_mosbuild_expo|k9CrEU5RuIE]]]

**⚠️ Real 3-year cork-panel failure** (individual DIY source, added 2026-08-25): a homeowner's own cork wall-panel finish, revisited 3 years after installation — shrank and developed gaps, corners abraded, faded unevenly in sunlight, cleaned poorly, and changed dimension with humidity swings. Conclusion stated directly: cork is not suited to a loggia's temperature/humidity-swing environment, especially at corners. He replaced it with rigid quartz-vinyl laminate ("кварцевый ламинат", stone + primary PVC composite) as both wall and floor finish — self-extinguishing, dimensionally stable under heat/humidity swings in his own account, and (contra a Petrishin-Stroi finding, see [[10_Balcony/analysis/Insulation_and_Heating|Insulation & Heating]]'s disagreement note) reported as working well as a **floating** floor over his own heated loggia floor. Corner-forming technique for this rigid panel type (score-and-heat-gun-fold from the panel's back side, or router-with-jig for multiple identical corners) is a new technique for this page, distinct from the existing flexible-PVC-panel corner method above. `single-account`, region unresolved. [source: [[_Sources/YT_kZ4cZs-2Xhc_stroim_dlya_sebya_loggia_insulation_revisit|kZ4cZs-2Xhc]]]

**Wood-paneling ("вагонка") installation technique** (Московские балконы,
added 2026-08-25, `single-account`, Moscow region level 2/title-only): a
genuine third wall-finish option for this page (alongside PVC panel and
plaster/vagonka-as-budget-option above) with real install detail —
pneumatic nail-gun installation (two nails into each board's tongue/
groove) explicitly preferred over metal clip fasteners (кляймеры), which
the crew calls unreliable and a real hand-injury risk; horizontal board
orientation preferred over vertical ("outdated"); boards hand-sorted by
visible defect level, with defect-free boards routed to the exposed wall
and minor-defect boards routed to a wall section that will later be
hidden behind a built-in cabinet. [source: [[_Sources/YT_AI6gdNR_LiE_moskovskie_balkony_wood_paneling_case|AI6gdNR_LiE]]]

## Lighting & Electrical

**Lighting rules** — *per Zemskov*: a loggia should always have its own dedicated light fixture — light from the adjoining room is never sufficient. **Never mount the fixture on the ceiling** — it blocks the sashes from opening; put it on the "warm" (back) wall instead. If a ceiling fixture is used anyway, it must be the flattest model available, *and* still needs a minimum 100mm frame jamb-extension at the top for sash clearance (same minimum extension applies if a stretch ceiling is planned there).

**Outlet-box (подрозетник) mounting on a balcony/exterior-type wall**
(Forcemontage, added 2026-08-25, `single-account`, Moscow region level 2/
title-only): a documented bad-practice warning — gluing the outlet box
into foam adhesive alone risks it tearing out of the wall together with a
plug on a hard yank. Correct technique for this exterior-type wall: use
cement-based adhesive (not gypsum, interior-only) with a keyed/undercut
recess so the adhesive flows behind the box lip and functions like a
mechanical anchor. Final box depth must be back-calculated from the full
finished-floor buildup (insulation + screed + tile adhesive + tile)
before drilling. [source: [[_Sources/YT_09G3Q-LuD_s_forcemontage_penoplex_two_layer_install|09G3Q-LuD_s]]]

**Sockets**: *Placeholder* (Recommendation: IP55 protective cover socket for outdoor laptop use or chargers) — underfloor-heating-thermostat placement rules (visibility-first mounting, eye-level height, the door-reveal technique) are documented in [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Heated_Floor_and_Thermostat|Bathroom: Heated Floor & Thermostat]] (added from a different, apartment-wide source, including a real disagreement between two practitioners on mounting height) and apply here too if a thermostat is ever added to this space.

**⚠️ Baseboard/door-swing conflict — a planning lesson** (Большая Стройка,
added 2026-08-25, `single-account`, region unresolved): a crew discovered
only at baseboard-installation time that a newly installed door's swing
arc struck a continuous baseboard run, forcing a notch/plug at that point
and a floor transition strip substitute — a real example of a clearance
conflict worth checking for early (door swing vs. baseboard path) rather
than catching after the baseboard is already being installed. Same source
also reports a foam-adhesive-strength anecdote worth noting for repair
context: removing an old foam-glued windowsill tore the surrounding brick/
mortar joint before the foam bond itself failed — their own conclusion is
that foam adhesive can be strong enough to reset a displaced brick course
for a small-scale repair (not something they'd rely on at full-building
scale). [source: [[_Sources/YT_2rOr5Lwt7kA_bolshaya_stroyka_loggia_storage_closet|2rOr5Lwt7kA]]]
