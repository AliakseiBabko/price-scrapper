## Flooring — the four things people call "паркет"

She opens by splitting the word, which is a genuinely useful disambiguation this vault's flooring pages do not currently state as a four-way split:

- **Паркетная доска (parquet board)** — a very thin valuable-wood wear layer, "буквально пару миллиметров," over a cheaper cross-glued softwood base (pine, larch — "в любом случае из хвои"). Laid floating on underlayment, glued to plywood, or in some cases glued to screed. **Looks good, tactilely pleasant, not very expensive — but cannot be sanded/refinished ("нельзя циклевать") and is not durable, because the wear layer is thin.**
- **Инженерная доска (engineered board)** — "по сути улучшенная паркетная доска." **Plywood base, which is what makes it dimensionally stable**; thicker overall, and the valuable-wood layer is **~4 mm**, so **it can be sanded two or three times** and fully renewed. Laid on plywood or glued to a level screed.
- **Массивная доска (solid board)** — plain solid boards, mostly oak, nailed to plywood like engineered board. **"Красиво, вечно будет вам служить"**, but **highly exposed to humidity swings, so poorly suited to apartments in this climate** unless humidifiers are used and humidity is actively kept even. Expensive because it is solid wood.
- **Наборный художественный паркет (patterned/artistic parquet)** — the classical-interior inlay type, mixed species and tones. **"Дорого, как крыло от самолёта."** Only makes sense in a large classical space.

**⚠️ Her stability claim for engineered board is stronger than "dimensionally stable" — she claims flood survival**: "Она даже потоп легко выдержит. Тоже это экспериментально проверено." `single-account`, `unverified`, and she does not describe the test.

**Price, engineered board**: **~6,000–7,000 RUB/m²** (2024-04, Russia) ≈ **$70–$80/m²** (USD equivalent, 12-month trailing mean to 2024-04-25, 90.6660 RUB/USD; comparability aid, not a quote).

## Flooring — underfloor-heating compatibility matrix

Collected across the whole video, because she states it per material rather than in one place:

| Material | Her verdict on underfloor heating |
| :--- | :--- |
| Parquet board / solid board / artistic parquet | **No** |
| Engineered board | **Yes — but only engineered board among the wood family, and only infrared** ("только под инженерную доску и только инфракрасный") |
| Laminate | **⚠️ No — "под него вообще нельзя класть тёплый пол"** |
| Quartz-vinyl | Yes |
| Porcelain / ceramic tile | Yes — and in Russia she says it should be **assumed**, see below |
| Poured floor (наливной пол) | Yes |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Perspectives — underfloor heating under laminate.** Her flat "never" conflicts with the more conditional position already on `13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/analysis/Flooring_Material_Selection.md` from Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario: *some manufacturers permit laminate/floating parquet/floating quartz-vinyl over a heated floor, but only when the heating element is embedded within the base (screed or self-levelling layer), not directly beneath the floating floor's own underlayment.* **Common ground**: neither treats laminate-plus-heating as a default good idea. **Difference**: she rules it out categorically; the installer source rules out one specific build-up and permits another. The installer-sourced conditional is the more actionable form and should not be overwritten by her categorical one.

## Flooring — laminate, argued down

- **Composition, stated to make the point that there is no wood in it**: multi-layer over an **HDF/particleboard (ДВП/ДСП) base**, with layers of special paper impregnated with **melamine resins**, the whole "pie" pressed under high pressure and temperature. **Quality/wear class is regulated by the amount of melamine impregnation and the thickness of the top layer.** "Ни грамма натурального дерева в ламинате нет."
- **⚠️ Minimum class 31** — "класс ламинату нужно брать не менее тридцать первого," otherwise it degrades almost immediately.
- **Does not tolerate moisture at all, "даже от влажной уборки"** — not a moisture-resistant material by construction.
- **Noisy, and tactilely unpleasant**; imitates wood but is not confusable with it "ни по степени блеска, ни по фактуре."
- **Exactly two advantages she will grant**: very easy click-lock installation on underlayment, even over an existing covering (she notes this specifically for offices), and it is cheap.
- **SPC laminate** (with a high proportion of calcium carbonate) is stronger and "ок для коммерческих помещений," but in her framing still laminate.
- **A cultural aside she uses as an argument, worth keeping as a framing device rather than a fact**: laminate's dominance is a post-Soviet regional preference, not a global quality verdict — an Englishman would say carpet is far better, a Scandinavian would lay larch board. She notes she gets abuse in the comments for criticising it.

## Flooring — quartz-vinyl: a commercial 10/10 she still argues against for a home

- **Composition**: durable polymer — **PVC with quartz sand added** — pressed into plates with a decorative top layer imitating wood, stone or other patterns.
- **Service life "до 20 лет"** without losing appearance or performance; moisture-resistant; highly resistant to mechanical abuse (heels, office-chair castors, dropped toys); simple to maintain; now available in patterns beyond wood imitation. **"Год просто 10 из десяти по всем характеристикам"** — for commercial premises.
- **⚠️ Her objection is tactile and it is her stated reason for not using it at home**: "резиновый на ощупь." She generalises it into a design principle worth recording on its own: **a home should be pleasant to walk on barefoot and to touch — not merely easy to clean** ("дом должен быть не только простым в уборке… Дома должно быть приятно пройтись босиком"), applied equally to a worktop, a kitchen front, porcelain tile, the floor, or bedlinen.
- **⚠️ Her price-parity argument, which is the actionable part**: quartz-vinyl is **comparable in price to engineered board** — therefore "за эти деньги вы можете положить не менее износостойкое покрытие с верхним слоем из натурального дерева дуба." **This corroborates the price overlap already recorded on this vault's flooring page from Sergey Kodolov** (engineered board ≈4,000–7,000 RUB/m²; quartz-parquet ≈4,000–6,000) and turns it into a recommendation.

> [!NOTE]
> **Perspectives — quartz-vinyl for residential use.** The existing vault content treats quartz-vinyl favourably for apartments on performance grounds (water-damage recovery, low formaldehyde emission, wear resistance). She does not dispute any of that — she accepts the performance case and declines the material on tactile/experiential grounds at equal cost. **Common ground**: quartz-vinyl is genuinely durable and is the right answer commercially. **Difference**: whether "durable and easy to clean" is the correct objective function for a home floor. Both belong on the page; hers is the design-side counterweight.

## Flooring — porcelain and ceramic tile

- She declines to litigate the porcelain-vs-ceramic distinction ("отличаются составом, способом изготовления"), treating both as an excellent floor material — "не зря же он так сильно распространён по всему миру."
- **Her signature pairing: porcelain plus wood is "всегда беспроигрышный варик для любого интерьера."**
- **⚠️ With epoxy grout in the joints she claims it becomes effectively unbreakable** ("если швы затерять эпоксидкой, так вообще не бьётся, не ломается"). `single-account`, `unverified` as stated — epoxy grout hardens the joint, not the tile, so treat the strength claim as loose phrasing about the assembly.
- **⚠️ Climate-conditioned rule**: without underfloor heating porcelain is cold. She explains its Mediterranean popularity as a summer-comfort effect (a rented flat in southern Italy) and concludes: **"в России под керамогранит везде стоит делать тёплые полы."** Treat porcelain and underfloor heating as a package in this climate, not as two separate decisions.

## Flooring — linoleum, rejected outright

Recorded because the rejection is unqualified and the one exception is specific:

- **Her position is a flat no** ("что хорошего в линолеуме? Ничего"), on the grounds that it is soft, cheap, easy to lay, and feels like rubber.
- **The one carve-out: марморлеум / specialist commercial linoleum** genuinely meets standards for hospitals and clinics — easy to wash, seamless. **Her framing: that is precisely why it makes a home look like a clinic** — "если вы хотите, чтобы у вас дома была больничка, ради бога, кладите линолеум."
- **A composite "cheap interior" signal worth keeping as a checklist item**: linoleum below plus a stretch ceiling above, with a puffy sofa and a kitchen corner-bench between, as the archetypal combination she is arguing against.

## Flooring — poured floor (наливной пол)

Rarely used residentially in this market, and she thinks that is a miss for minimalist interiors specifically:

- **Two types**: **cement** (effectively a very fine-dispersion screed; available plain, concrete-look, terrazzo) and **epoxy**.
- **Takes underfloor heating**; gives a perfectly flat, smooth surface; reasonably strong.
- **⚠️ Not cheap, and the cost is in both parts** — the mix itself is expensive *and* the labour to pour it level is expensive. This is the counter-intuitive bit she flags herself ("казалось бы, что там делать, но нет").
- **Fit**: cement poured floor suits **minimalism only**; epoxy suits commercial spaces, garages and utility rooms.

## Flooring — carpet (ковролин), defended

This is her most contrarian section for this market and it is the one that squarely disagrees with existing vault content.

- **Framing**: carpet is "абсолютно незаслуженно обойдён вниманием" in Russia. It is **the most popular home flooring in the UK, the USA and Canada** — because it is warm, and you are always walking on something warm.
- **⚠️ Her pre-emptive rebuttal of the Russian-climate objection**: Canada is not warmer than Russia; the UK is less cold outdoors but very damp with poor heating, so a British home in winter is colder and less pleasant indoors than a Russian one.
- **On washability, the standard objection**: "если бы его было нельзя мыть… его бы просто никто никогда не стелил." Carpets differ; some can be washed indefinitely; washing vacuums and dedicated products exist. Maintenance is "совсем не сложно."
- **Installation is as easy as linoleum** — glued or simply laid, on any reasonably flat base, **with no need for a perfectly levelled floor**. (Recorded as her claim; it sits against her own earlier point that other materials demand levelling, so it functions as a budget argument.)
- **Carpets with a high natural-fibre content are "супертактильно приятно"**, in any colour, plain or patterned.
- **⚠️ Her durability argument, which is the checkable one: hotels.** Carpet is in hotel rooms, corridors and lobbies, walked on constantly with dirty shoes, and does not look destroyed.
- **A concrete whole-house scheme**: in a country house, **porcelain on the ground floor** where people walk in outdoor shoes, **carpet on all upper living floors** — "вообще идеальное решение."

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Perspectives — carpet in a Russian apartment.** Directly opposed to the existing `Carpet (Ковролин) — Rarely Used in Residential Apartments` section on `13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/analysis/Flooring_Material_Selection.md` (Петришин-Строй / Mosbuild expo), which says carpet is mainly relegated to hotels because high traffic soils it quickly, installation quality is inconsistent, and bacteria/insects can colonise it. **They agree on the observable facts and read them oppositely**: both note carpet's association with hotels — the trade-fair source treats that as evidence it does not belong in a home, she treats it as proof of its traffic durability. **Common ground**: carpet is uncommon in Russian apartments, and its performance depends heavily on which carpet. **Difference**: whether the uncommonness reflects a real defect or a regional habit. Neither source resolves the hygiene question, which the trade-fair source raises and she does not address at all — that gap should be stated on the page rather than hidden.

## Flooring — cork, for kids' rooms specifically

- **⚠️ Her recommendation is room-specific, not general**: cork is a good material **for children's rooms**, on two grounds — the natural cork texture works with brightly-coloured children's furniture and white Scandinavian-style walls, and **it is soft, so the child is not hurt by it and things dropped on it mostly do not break, they bounce** ("спружинит").
- **Two forms**: **glue-down cork** rolled out like linoleum, and **cork board** laid like laminate.
- **⚠️ The limit, and it is short: not durable — "дольше лет пяти не продержится в адекватном товарном виде"**, whatever the coating.
- **⚠️ Only in natural cork colour.** Printed cork imitating wood is, in her words, "абсолютная мерзость" — she compares it to a supermarket bag printed with a luxury monogram. Natural cork in a children's room she rates "решение 100% хорошее."

## Flooring — natural stone, and the de-icing-reagent rule

- Expensive, and the most beautiful option available ("ничего красивее натурального камня, натурального дерева вы не найдёте никогда").
- **Granite in an entrance hall**: she pre-empts the metro-station association by noting the range of decorative granites, figured cutting and inlay patterns available.
- **⚠️ A genuinely regional rule, and the most transferable fact in this section: do not lay marble in an entrance hall anywhere de-icing reagents are used** ("в той полосе, где есть некогда реагенты, прихожие класть не надо"). In southern regions marble in an entrance hall is fine. **Marble is fine in wet rooms** — floors and walls, "банально в хамамах мрамор везде используется," and it interacts with water well. The constraint is the reagent tracked in on shoes, not water.

## Flooring — mosaic

- **Do not use smalt mosaic on a floor.** Porcelain or natural-stone mosaic is fine.
- **⚠️ The practical objection is grout lines, not the tile**: dirt packs into the joints, so mosaic is "не суперкомфортна для полов" — beautiful "если за вами ходят штат уборщиц."
- **⚠️ If mosaic is used, grout must be epoxy only.**

## Cost Drivers

- **Flooring is the finish you do not replace** — her opening premise, and the reason she treats the decision as high-stakes: "его-то вы не поменяете, в отличие от штор."
