## Windows / Slopes — Splay Angle Measurement Standard

- **A window slope's "splay" (angle relative to the window frame, so light spreads into the room and the slope reads as intentionally flared rather than either perfectly perpendicular or visibly caved-in) is measured in centimeters at the widest point, not in degrees** — degrees are impractical to measure accurately on-site with hand tools; the practical method is to set a square against the frame at the corner and measure the maximum gap between the square's edge and the slope's actual surface (equivalent to extending a straightedge and measuring the offset at its far end).
- **Correct splay figure, stated as ending disputes between clients and installers who commonly cite anywhere from 10 to 30 degrees**: **35mm measured this way**, valid across a slope width range of 15-50cm — covering effectively any apartment's window. This produces a subtly flared appearance (reads as "properly splayed" rather than either a flat/boxy "coffin" profile at 0mm or an excessively flared "gun-slit" profile at a much larger offset).
- **Asymmetry tolerance rule**: the two side slopes of one window should always match each other's splay angle exactly, but the top slope may differ from the sides by 5-10mm without being a defect — in fact the source states a top slope splayed slightly *more* than the sides (up to +5-10mm) looks *better*, not worse, since the top is viewed from below at a steeper angle and reads as straighter than it measures. Explicit caution to clients: don't insist an installer rework a top slope to match the sides exactly — the deliberate difference improves the result.
