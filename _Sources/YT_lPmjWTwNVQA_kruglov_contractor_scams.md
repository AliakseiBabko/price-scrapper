## Mistakes / Warnings — Contract-Fraud Mechanisms Not Covered by Round 11

- **⚠️ Named contract due-diligence checklist item, not previously this
  specific in this store**: verify a contracting organization's legal
  registration (OOO or IP) directly online — registration date, tax
  filings, and litigation/court-case history are all stated as
  realistically checkable before signing, a more concrete due-diligence
  step than this vault's existing general "check genuine reviews"
  guidance.
- **⚠️ A named, previously-undocumented contract clause**: the contract
  should specify penalty terms not just for the *client's* early
  termination, but explicitly for the **contractor's own unilateral
  termination** — without such a clause, a crew can complete only the
  most profitable rough-work stages and walk away at the hardest
  stage with no financial consequence. Stated directly from the
  speaker's own business experience: a meaningful volume of this
  company's incoming work is clients asking them to finish a
  renovation abandoned by a previous crew — a real, stated
  consequence of contracts lacking this clause.
- **⚠️ A named contract-appendix mechanism, distinct from Round 11's
  discount-withheld-until-final-act trick**: beyond the smeta itself,
  some companies attach a full separate price list for potential
  additional/change-order work — priced less favorably than the
  original smeta's own rates. **Worked example**: a smeta specifies
  60×60cm tile; if the client later decides to use 120×60cm tile
  instead, that substitution is billed as "additional work" against
  the separate appendix price list, not at the smeta's original,
  more favorable rate — a distinct mechanism from Round 11's tile-size-
  banding-ambiguity trap (that one was about ambiguous wording within
  the smeta itself; this one is about a wholly separate, less
  favorable price list a client may not scrutinize as closely as the
  main smeta).

## Mistakes / Warnings — Smeta-Fraud Section: Restated Core, Two Sharper Variants

**Restated/corroborating, not re-extracted**: the three-category smeta-
fraud taxonomy itself (missing work, volume understatement, slippery
wording); the design-project-doesn't-specify-everything mitigation
(electrical panel/manifold-node fit-out, windowsill type, slope
finishing all commonly missing from a design project, not the
designer's fault); the ceramic-vs-porcelain floor-tile substitution
mechanism; the floor-area volume cross-check (sum per-room
laminate/tile areas against known total m²); and the discount-only-on-
final-act trick — all already documented from `rzpkTJYsY0Q` in Round
11, restated here almost verbatim.

- **⚠️ A sharper, more specific electrical cable-length rule of thumb
  than Round 11's "unknowable" framing**: for roughly 50 outlets/
  switches, a realistic cable-run figure is **≈450-500 running
  meters** — a smeta quoting only ≈100 running meters for the same
  point count is not adequate. Complements, rather than duplicates,
  `rzpkTJYsY0Q`'s point that cable length is hard for a client to
  independently verify at all — this gives a concrete sanity-check
  ratio to apply regardless.
- **⚠️ A sharper screed-thickness standard than Round 11's generic
  "banding" framing**: only two real screed-thickness standards
  exist in practice — **2cm on a hard/rigid base** (concrete or
  monolithic slab) and **4cm on a soft base** (keramzit fill,
  soundproofing, waterproofing layers). **Worked example**: with a
  4cm height differential across a floor, the low point needs
  4cm (soft-base minimum) + 2cm (compensating for the differential)
  = 6cm, which no longer fits inside a smeta line reading "screed up
  to 3cm" — triggering an extra per-centimeter charge. This is a more
  specific technical mechanism than `rzpkTJYsY0Q`'s existing "up to
  5cm/10cm" banding-trap framing (different numbers, and a concrete
  worked calculation this vault didn't have before).
- **⚠️ A genuinely new third slippery-wording example, not present in
  `rzpkTJYsY0Q`**: a wallpaper-hanging line item reading "installation
  without pattern matching" (без подбора) is misleading because almost
  100% of real (non-paint-ready) wallpaper requires pattern matching
  per its own label — a repeat interval typically **10-30cm**. Since
  virtually no wallpaper actually qualifies as "no matching needed"
  except paint-ready rolls, a "without matching" line item is
  effectively bait for an under-quote, triggering an extra charge once
  the client's actual (matching-required) wallpaper is installed.
- **A minor refinement, not a standalone new fact**: this video states
  the porcelain-vs-ceramic substitution applies to **80% of wall tile
  and 100% of floor tile** — slightly more specific than
  `rzpkTJYsY0Q`'s floor-tile-only framing (~100% of floor tile is
  porcelain) — folded into the existing note rather than counted
  separately.
