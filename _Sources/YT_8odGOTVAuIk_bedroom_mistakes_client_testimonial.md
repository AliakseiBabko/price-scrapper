## Mistakes / Warnings — Durable Facts & Rules (client-confirmed real outcomes)

- **Never install mirrored wardrobe-door panels where they'd reflect motion visible in peripheral vision from the bed** — client reported real discomfort: mirrors were installed backward from the design (facing the bedroom instead of inside the closet), and any nighttime movement produced an unsettling "someone moving beside me" peripheral-vision effect; corrected to match the original design (mirrors inside the closet). Stated market data point: **~65% of this practitioner's clients ask to swap mirrored panels for frosted/matte ones** specifically because of this peripheral-motion discomfort. `confirmed`, real correction + a specific (though `unverified`, self-reported) percentage.
- **A walk-in closet needs its own mirror — never rely on a mirror in another room.** If not using an exterior mirrored door, always place a mirror inside the closet. **Minimum clearance rule**: at least 1m from the mirror to where a person stands to dress. This drives a minimum walk-in width for a mirrored closet: **1m (mirror clearance) + 60cm (clothing zone) + 10cm (sliding-door track) = 1.7m minimum** — if the space is under 1.7m wide, don't build a walk-in with an internal mirror at all. `confirmed`, precise arithmetic rule, directly complementary to `RyHzFDGgqKA`'s width formulas.
- **Scratch-test glass wardrobe-door panels with a fingernail before ordering** — cheap glass shows permanent nail-scratch marks from ordinary door-opening contact; client reported visible scratching on installed glass from normal fingernail contact despite short nails. `confirmed`, real defect observed post-installation, concrete pre-purchase test given.
- **Never skip soft-close dampers (доводчики) on heavy glass sliding doors** — without them, an unintentionally forceful door slide can impact the end stop hard enough to shatter the glass panel; the furniture installer in this case pushed back against including dampers, but the practitioner insisted on keeping them. `confirmed`, real installer-vs-designer disagreement resolved in favor of dampers.
- **Never use recessed spotlights inside an enclosed mirrored closet** — even a large number of spotlights (the source says "even 50") still produces a harsh, directional cone of light that emphasizes every skin/clothing-fold shadow, making anyone using the mirror feel they look worse than they do. **Use a glowing/luminous ceiling panel instead** for even, flattering light. `confirmed`, real installed mistake (installer defaulted to spotlights despite the plan) plus client-reported discomfort using the space afterward.
- **Do not shrink a spacious bedroom just to bring the TV closer** — viewing distance is compensated by using a larger TV instead; keep the room's full size and size the TV to the distance, don't sacrifice room area for proximity. `confirmed`, explicit design principle, both against a designer's competing suggestion in this case (designer wanted the TV positioned closer; the practitioner's plan kept it at a considered distance, and the client endorsed the outcome).
- **TV height is always a compromise between an "off" aesthetic and lying-down viewing comfort** — mounting a TV low enough for a comfortable lying-flat viewing angle looks awkward when off (interrupts symmetry with flanking wardrobes/millwork); mounting it high enough to look intentional/symmetric makes lying-flat viewing require sitting semi-upright or angling the head. **There is no dimension that resolves both** — this is presented as an inherent, unavoidable trade-off to make consciously, not a mistake with a fix. `confirmed`, explicit design-tradeoff framing (not a checkable "rule," but a durable planning caveat worth recording so a future viewer doesn't assume a mistake was made).
- **Never place a regularly-used work desk/computer setup inside a bedroom** — even a quiet keyboard's clicking is reported as disruptive to a sleeping partner over repeated regular use; acceptable only as a rarely-used decorative surface (e.g. opening a laptop once a month), never as a daily workstation. Client explicitly corroborates on camera that she rarely uses the space precisely because it wasn't set up as a regular desk, and confirms this was the right call. `confirmed`, real client corroboration, echoed as a recurring complaint pattern ("many clients have told me this") — `single-account` for the recurrence claim specifically, `confirmed` for this client's own experience.
