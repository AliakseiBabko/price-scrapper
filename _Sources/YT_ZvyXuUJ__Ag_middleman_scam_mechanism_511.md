## Mistakes / Warnings — Construction-Middleman Scam Mechanism & Red Flags

- **⚠️ A specific fraudulent business structure documented in detail (mechanism, not personal identity)**: an operator with no real construction crew or licensed company positions themselves as a general contractor, using a downloaded or borrowed "typical" construction contract template (obtained from the internet, or from a real company under the pretense of being a prospective client) reformatted with the operator's own company name. No real registration, licensing, or track record required to start. **Red flag for a self-managed buyer**: a contractor who can't produce verifiable past-project references or a real, findable business registration should be treated with real suspicion, especially if their contract template reads as generic/boilerplate.
- **⚠️ Pricing-inflation method documented**: build a price list by calling ~5 real, independent tradespeople (via a classifieds site) for common line items (plastering, wall leveling, puttying, wallpapering), always recording the **highest** quoted price as the baseline "cost," padding that by an extra 10% margin "in case something was missed," then adding an operator markup on top (25-30% at the low end for an inexperienced operator, 40-50% once established with a regular crew). **Practical implication for a self-managed buyer getting quotes**: if using a general contractor at all, ask directly what their own markup structure is over subcontracted labor, and independently spot-check at least one or two line items against direct-tradesperson pricing to sanity-check the total.
- **⚠️ A particularly predatory variant documented**: an operator taking a full 50% cut of the **total client payment** (not a 50% markup over cost — literally half of everything the client pays) while still requiring the actual tradespeople to supply their own tools and workwear — an unusually extractive split worth recognizing as a red flag if a subcontractor-facing arrangement is ever described this way.
- **⚠️ Deposit/prepayment red flag**: a documented pattern requests 40-50% of total project value upfront, ostensibly to fund initial labor/materials, but with a portion pocketed directly as the operator's own income rather than tracked against actual work completed. **General guidance for vetting any contractor** (turnkey or self-managed-crew): a large upfront deposit relative to any verifiable work-in-progress, especially from an operator with no fixed, real, findable office address, is a meaningful warning sign — cross-references this store's existing consumer-protection/anti-upsell heuristics (`yt_cdNwbqsLUK4`).
- **⚠️ "Fake legitimacy" setup tactics documented, useful as things to check for when vetting a contractor**: renting a desk/office space for just a few hours at a time from an existing tenant (documented cost: ~2,000 RUB per client meeting) specifically to appear to have a real office; listing that address as the company's contact address online despite not actually operating from it; recruiting crew anonymously through job-classifieds and government employment-center placements via a separate "work phone" line, keeping the operator's own identity and true business structure opaque even to the people working for them. **A buyer meeting a contractor at an office should ask direct questions confirming the company genuinely operates from that location** (how long they've been there, ask to see other ongoing work/staff presence) rather than treating a physical office visit alone as sufficient legitimacy verification.
- **Illustrative business-setup cost figures given for how cheaply this kind of operation can be stood up** (not this project's cost benchmarks, purely illustrative of how low the barrier to entry is): company seal ~3,000 RUB, freelance-built website ~10,000 RUB, self-funded ad spend ~30,000 RUB, one-time office-space rental ~5,000 RUB — totaling roughly 48,000 RUB to appear as a legitimate construction company with no actual construction capability behind it.
