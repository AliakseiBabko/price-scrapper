## Rules / Heuristics — New Detail

1. **3D-printed reducer adjustment key**: the pressure reducer's
   adjustment cap uses a small key not sold separately anywhere Mikhail
   has found — he 3D-printed a replacement specifically shaped so it
   can't be set down and lost (it stays attached/can't easily separate
   from the cap), solving a real recurring small-tool-loss problem on
   this specific fitting.
2. **Cheap-substitute economics is a distinct, worse failure mode than
   simply omitting a component** — a genuinely new mechanism, not just
   restating "don't skip these parts": swapping a real reducer/filter for
   a cheap Chinese-made equivalent doesn't just under-perform, it can
   actively work in reverse (a cheap reducer stated to *increase* pressure
   downstream rather than reduce it) and degrades so fast it needs
   replacing annually instead of lasting the normal 5–10 years — cheap
   substitution is framed as costing *more* over time than either the
   genuine part or skipping the component outright and accepting the
   risk.
3. **Aluminum-sheet lining as a waterproofing alternative for a
   compromised masonry riser shaft behind the cabinet**: normally the
   cabinet's back wall just needs a waterproofing coating, but on this
   specific secondary-market object the shaft behind the cabinet had old
   cracked brick, holes, and four disused/abandoned pipes — judged too
   risky to coat directly, so an aluminum sheet was installed as a
   physical barrier instead. A concrete substrate-condition decision
   rule (coating vs. rigid barrier), not a universal recommendation.
4. **Minimum ~1 year hands-on experience recommended before a self-
   renovating owner attempts this assembly** — Mikhail's own answer to
   "could a novice do this from YouTube alone": no, specifically because
   packing pipe-thread flax/tow ("пакля") correctly so it doesn't slide
   is a hands-skill that isn't reliably learnable from video alone, on
   top of general fitting/tool familiarity. A DIY-risk-assessment
   heuristic for this project's own self-managed delivery-model planning.
5. **Backward-running water meter as a QC red flag, with a real fraud-
   suspicion consequence**: Mikhail describes a real repeat callout where
   a water meter had been installed backward (mechanically counting in
   reverse) despite already being tamper-sealed — nobody had checked
   orientation before sealing it. Consequence stated: a resident whose
   meter reads unusually low risks the utility company suspecting
   deliberate tampering and issuing a fine, even if the real cause was
   an installer's mistake, not fraud. A concrete post-install
   verification step (check meter direction against actual flow) worth
   adding to a handover/acceptance checklist.
6. **Mandatory RCD/differential-current breaker on the cabinet's own
   electrical outlet, framed as the floor-level minimum**: if water
   reaches the socket and grounds out, the breaker trips — Mikhail calls
   this "the most minimal protection required," implying it's a baseline
   that shouldn't be skipped even on a budget build.
7. **Whole-node maintenance schedule, stated as a routine**: inspect
   every 4–6 months — flush filters (100-micron fine filter, roughly
   every 4–6 months per this source, broadly consistent with this
   project's existing self-flushing-filter cadence) and separately check
   for leaks specifically at rubber-gasket/threaded-joint connection
   points on the manifold and cabinet, not just at the filters
   themselves.
8. **Ventilation inside the plumbing cabinet is called mandatory, not
   optional, specifically to prevent mold/fungus** — the cabinet's
   elevated humidity (open water-bearing components) needs active air
   exchange to keep moisture from accumulating; framed as a real defect
   risk if skipped, not a comfort feature.
9. **Water-quality add-on filters are client-request-driven, not
   default**: if a client complains about hard water, a softening
   ("умягчающий") mainline filter/cartridge is added; if the system shows
   elevated iron, an iron-removal ("обезжелезивающий") cartridge is
   added instead — corroborates and slightly extends this project's
   existing "get a water analysis, let it decide the cartridge type"
   rule with the concrete client-complaint trigger for each branch.
