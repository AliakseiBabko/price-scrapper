## Kids Room — Durable Facts & Rules

### Stable infrastructure "foundation," independent of age (applies 0-18)

- **Four developmental age bands used to structure planning**: 0-3, 3-6,
  6-12, 12-18 — after 18 the room either becomes an adult bedroom or the
  child moves out. For a client with an infant, the designer explicitly
  recommends sketching a couple of additional "grow-into" layouts up
  front, not just the immediate nursery layout.
- **Electrical planning**: outlet count/placement should work across
  several different furniture-arrangement scenarios, decided in advance
  (directly consistent with `YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs`'s 5-year-staged outlet
  planning — corroborating, not duplicating, that source's more detailed
  treatment).
- **Lighting should include both general and zone-specific scenarios
  from the start**: a night-light for the sleep zone, and optionally
  decorative/accent lighting for a play zone — planned as distinct
  lighting circuits/scenarios, not one uniform room light.
- **Prepare walls properly for repainting from the start** so future
  redecoration is cosmetic-only (a repaint) rather than requiring
  wallpaper stripping — even if wallpaper is added later, a paint-ready
  wall underneath keeps future changes cheap. Recommended finishes:
  hypoallergenic/eco paper or plant-fiber wallpaper, or organic paint
  (e.g. chalk paint).
- **Flooring recommendation**: cork or engineered wood, plus small-pile
  rugs — corroborates the cork recommendation already recorded from
  `YT_Cr1vew4sA9g`. If the floor is cold, quartz-vinyl with underfloor
  heating is offered as an alternative (soft underfoot, sound-dampening).
- **Ceiling should be kept simple** — paintable or a satin stretch
  ceiling, explicitly avoiding an elaborate/bright/fantastical design,
  since the ceiling is the surface a household will least want to redo
  later and a novelty design gets "old" fastest.
- **Climate/comfort infrastructure to plan up front**: air conditioning
  provision, window locks on every operable sash (a genuine safety item,
  not optional), and blackout curtain provision built in from the start
  — blackout is specifically called out as important in the first two
  years for daytime nap quality.
- **Wall-color restraint, corroborating `YT_Cr1vew4sA9g`'s color-
  restraint principle**: walls can be colored but should not be overly
  bright/saturated — bright toys alone already provide enough visual
  stimulation; an overstimulating room can interfere with the child
  settling down to sleep in the same space they play in.

### Age 0-3 — nursery-to-toddler specifics

- **Crib/bed sizing progresses through defined stages**: a newborn
  bassinet/crib of **~70×80cm** up to ~6 months (the infant can't roll
  yet, so no maneuvering room is needed); grows to **~120cm** from 6
  months to ~1.5-2 years, at which stage the crib needs either
  height-adjustable rails or an adjustable base so the now-standing
  child can't fall out; from 2-4 years, rails become removable
  entirely, letting the child get in/out independently.
- **Montessori-style floor mattress as a crib alternative**: from
  roughly 6-9 months (once crawling starts), a floor-level mattress
  instead of a raised crib lets a child access their own bed
  independently — explicitly requires the whole room to be
  babyproofed/safe under this approach, quoting Maria Montessori's own
  stated principle: a child should have the right to sleep when tired,
  wake when rested, and get up when they choose.
- **Changing table can be a temporary attachment to a crib that later
  converts to a full bed, or combined with a dresser** — clothes storage
  starts in a dresser, with a small child's wardrobe optionally added
  slightly later.
- **Self-dressing independence at ~3 years (sometimes from 2)**: this is
  described as a genuine developmental drive ("I do it myself"), and the
  practical planning implication is to **store only in-season clothing
  at the child's reachable height, moving off-season items higher** —
  avoids the classic conflict of a child wanting a specific favorite
  item (sandals) instead of what's practical (rain boots) by making the
  practical in-season options the only ones within reach. A larger adult
  wardrobe can double for this if its lower shelves are dedicated to the
  child and its upper shelves to the adults (including keeping
  medicine/care items on adult-only upper shelves) — or open clothing
  rails/rieling are an acceptable substitute if no wardrobe exists yet.
- **Mirror placed near the dressing/wardrobe area, at the child's own
  height** — motivated by toddlers' documented fascination with
  studying their own reflection from around age 1.
- **Toy/book storage accessibility rule**: open shelving/baskets sized
  and placed so a young child can both retrieve and put away items
  independently — open display shelving for books doubles as a wall
  decor element. Storage baskets specifically help make put-away trivial
  for a small child.
- **Rug pile choice for a play area**: small/low-pile for
  machine-washability, or a foam mat type that wipes clean — practical
  cleaning considerations specific to a play-zone floor covering.
- **Reserve floor space for large mobile/temporary toys** (ride-on toys,
  rocking toys, etc.) separate from furniture footprint — these are
  bulky and need open floor, not shelf space.
- **A small desk and chair appear around 1-1.5 years**, ideally placed
  by the window.
- **Safety/comfort equipment for this stage**: baby/video monitor,
  humidifier/air purifier, blackout curtains — described as jointly
  making a major difference to a child's sleep quality when combined
  with air conditioning and blackout.
- **A dedicated parent-seating spot inside the nursery** (a chair with
  ottoman, or a small loveseat/sofa, plus a floor lamp for reading) —
  motivated by how much time a parent spends physically in the room
  during infancy (feeding, settling the child).
- **Child-size cleaning tools (a small broom/dustpan) given a spot in
  the room** from around walking age (~1 year+), so a child can
  participate in cleaning up their own spills/messes, mirroring parent
  behavior they're already imitating at that age.
- **A hypoallergenic houseplant placed in the child's own room**, with
  its own small watering can, specifically to let the child practice
  a caretaking responsibility early.

### Age 3-6 specifics

- **Bed length grows to ~180cm**; since a bed at this stage often no
  longer has side rails, a bedside table with a lamp can be added at
  the head of the bed.
- **Desk/work area grows in size and use** as the child does more
  craft/table activities; window-side desk placement by handedness
  applies here (**left of window if right-handed, right if left-
  handed**) — a 3rd independent corroboration within this vault of the
  same hand-shadow rule already recorded from `YT_Cr1vew4sA9g` and
  `YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs`.
- **Play is described as both entertainment and learning at this age**,
  so the play zone gets the most floor area of any age band. Children
  particularly favor scenario/role-play (kitchen, shop, workshop) and
  gravitate to den-like, self-scaled enclosures (playhouse beds, teepees,
  blanket forts) — being inside something sized to their own body is
  specifically what they find cozy/appealing.
- **Reserve open shelf/display space for showing off finished crafts or
  built constructions** — explicitly don't force an immediate put-away
  or disassembly; let a child enjoy the visible result of their own work
  for a while before it's cleared.
- **Wardrobe use extends to higher shelves as the child grows**; open
  hooks (rather than only closed storage) work well for daily-use items
  like a jacket or backpack, for lower-friction daily access.
- **A pet corner (fish tank, hamster cage) can be sited directly in the
  child's own room at this age**, framed as an early responsibility-
  building opportunity, with the child taking on part of the animal's
  care.

### Age 6-12 (school age) specifics

- **Bed upgrades to a sturdier, standard size, typically ~90×200cm.**
- **Desk becomes a full workstation** (pedestal drawers, shelving) for
  organized school-supply access.
- **Outlet count near the desk increases, including USB outlets**,
  specifically because this is the age a child gets their first
  chargeable personal device (e.g. a smartphone) — directly consistent
  with `YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs`'s desk-outlet planning, adding the USB-specific
  detail.
- **A chalkboard/magnetic wall from the toddler years repurposes
  directly into a study aid** at this age; if none exists, a cork board
  or wall organizer is offered as an alternative.
- **Toy shelving converts function to textbook/notebook shelving**,
  while some play space is retained (board games, puzzles) since
  friends now visit and play together in the child's own room.
- **Large mobile-toy floor space converts to serious sports equipment**
  (e.g. a "Swedish wall"/wall-mounted gym ladder) as the child's
  interests mature.
- **A hobby corner may need dedicated space** for a specific pursuit
  (e.g. music lessons requiring instrument storage/practice space).
- **Sleepover provision shifts to a genuinely mobile/foldable spare
  bed** as friends start staying over by late elementary years.
- **Wardrobe internals should be reconfigurable** (adjustable shelf/rod
  heights and rod count) to track the child's changing height and
  wardrobe composition without a full rebuild.
- **Privacy transition begins**: a door that previously had no lock (or
  was blocked open) can start being closable by the child around school
  age — paired with an explicit **reciprocal-knocking household norm**
  (parents knock before entering the child's closed room; modeling this
  makes the child more likely to knock at the parents' own door too).

### Age 12-18 (teen) specifics

- **Bed can stay the same 90×200cm, or go up to ~160cm wide** if
  designing from scratch for this age band specifically.
- **Play zone converts to a lounge zone** (a comfortable sofa/armchair)
  for reading, watching films, and socializing with friends.
- **Desk changes minimally**, mainly a more "grown-up" desk chair
  upgrade.
- **Sleepover provision moves fully onto the lounge sofa** rather than a
  separate mobile bed.
- **For a teenage girl's room specifically**: consider a small
  vanity/boudoir corner (mirror, seating, storage for jewelry/cosmetics/
  perfume), with its own dedicated supplementary lighting.
- **Full wardrobe access matures to the teen's own full control** — the
  source stresses spending real effort at this stage helping the teen
  organize their own wardrobe well, as a habit-forming point, not just
  handing over space.
- **Technology provision (TV/projector) is explicitly flagged as the
  hardest single element to plan years in advance**, since consumer tech
  form factors change fast (portable projectors, tablets replacing
  laptops, VR glasses potentially replacing consoles) — the source
  explicitly declines to over-commit infrastructure to any one current
  tech format for this reason.

## Design Concept — Durable Facts

- **If you don't want to redo a kids' room's finish 3-4 times over
  childhood, keep the base finish neutral and carry all age-specific
  theming through swappable furniture/decor accents instead** —
  corroborates (with a specific "3-4 redos avoided" framing) both
  `YT_9ykrlQ5tpBs`'s 3-tier longevity framework and `YT_Cr1vew4sA9g`'s
  transformer-furniture-motivation-fade lesson.
- **Design the room around the child as its actual primary user from as
  early as ~6 months old**, not purely around what looks good to an
  adult — stated as a first-principles design starting point, not just
  a nicety.
- **The child's own room is explicitly framed as the sanctioned place
  for safe creative experiments not allowed elsewhere in the home** (the
  source's own example: drawing on walls is fine in a child's own room
  even though it wouldn't be allowed in the living room) — a child
  shouldn't be scolded for using their own room this way; explicitly
  framed as potentially nurturing a real creative/decorating interest
  later in life.
