## ⚠️ A worked case that dates a cost-optimised project, and the year needs care

A client rejected their proposed specification and suppliers as too expensive and **sourced everything himself across Russia — metalwork from one producer, structures from another — «максимально запарился с точки зрения экономии»** — then sent them his own final смета.

- **Result: ~120,000–125,000 RUB/m² for a minimalist modern flat with no elaborate features.**
- **⚠️ But he states this was «3 года назад» — i.e. approximately 2020, not 2023. It is deliberately NOT converted here**, because a 2020 figure requires a 2020 rate and the month is unknown.
- **The panel's own escalation estimate: «процентов на 40 всё можно прибавить» — and one of them immediately says «сильно больше».** Reasons given: materials rose, logistics rose, tools rose, labour rose, **specialists left the country and the market shrank and got dearer.**
- **⚠️ And the line worth keeping: «всё дорожает, кроме дизайн-проекта».** Design fees are named as the one component that has not tracked the rest.
- **On the trajectory generally: «цены только растут, я не видел ни разу иначе».**

## ⚠️ Why a minimalist interior costs more, not less — the best insight in the source

A real project, **~135–140 m², at 250,000–270,000 RUB/m² (≈$2,800–$3,100/m²)**. Microcement on floor and walls, bare concrete ceiling treated only with a dust-binding impregnation, **no doors at all**, effectively one material throughout. The natural question — «за что столько денег?» — is answered mechanically:

> **«Когда ты делаешь минималистичные интерьеры, они тебя не прощают недорогих подходов. Недорогие решения будет видно — они будут на виду. Когда у тебя пустой минималистичный интерьер, все косяки мебели, плохие стыковки нескольких материалов в одной точке — это всё заметно.»**

**And the conclusion, which is a genuine planning rule: «когда это делают рукожопы, которых ты нашёл, чтобы сэкономить — тогда лучше вообще не делать, чем вот так делать, потому что вся концепция испортится».** **Minimalism removes the visual noise that hides poor execution, so it raises the minimum acceptable trade quality rather than lowering the material cost.**

**⚠️ The mirror-image failure, and it is the one to watch for: chasing luxe in a detail-heavy style while economising along the way produces «некая пародия настоящего дорогого проекта» — practically the same result as simply having a small budget.** In that style **«одна малярка, один гипс может составлять приличные суммы»**.

**They also name «стоимость стиля» as its own variable**: two projects that look comparable in a photograph are not comparably priced, because a detail-rich style carries more elements — «сравнимый минимализм дорогой и дорогое нео — у тебя будет дороже, потому что больше элементов».

## ⚠️ Match the designer to your budget and style, not just to your taste

**«Если ты придёшь к Диме и скажешь: давайте мне за 120 тысяч — Дима просто не сможет, потому что он не умеет это делать. Он не ездит по Леруа Мерлен и не ищет вместо гипса какой-то полиуретан.»**

- **The point is that cost-optimisation is itself a distinct skill, not a lesser version of the same skill.** Some designers are specifically good at «из говна и палок сделать максимально круто» and are «супер в этом»; others cannot work below a certain specification at all.
- **His stated main advice: go to designers who work with your budget AND in the stylistic segment you want.** Their earlier version of the same rule adds **similar values, cultural level and outlook — «тогда всё будет по маслу»**.
- Курилов is candid that in his own direction he «ничего не смогу сделать» below a certain budget given the number of details, execution complexity and materials — and that clients often conclude at the negotiation stage that they like the picture but are not ready for it, **«это нормально абсолютно»**.

## A small-room absolute figure

**⚠️ Parents' bathroom plus separate WC in a panel building, a small bathroom: ~500,000 RUB (≈$5,700) for the complete renovation of the bathroom alone**, with him ordering the materials. **The first absolute small-wet-room total in this vault from this channel**, and useful precisely because panel-building bathrooms are a standard size.

## ⚠️ What never to economise on — independent corroboration of Round 1

- **«Экономить на том, что сложно заменить.»** And: «если можно не экономить — зачем экономить?»
- **All the engineering must be as good as the budget allows.**
- **The worked negative, which is the memorable form: cheapest plumbing pipes, but the most expensive radiator «потому что его видно» — and six months later everything leaked and destroyed the mouldings and the gilt chandelier below.** «Скелет должен быть хорошим.»
- **⚠️ This independently corroborates Round 1's «лучше не купить шкаф, чем сэкономить на автоматах в электрощите и проводке» from a contractor two months later — same rule, same logic (visible versus concealed), different practitioners.** Recorded as corroboration, not as a new rule.

## ⚠️ The procurement-commission disclosure — the most valuable item for reading any designer's advice

**A specific, checkable disclosure of how designer procurement fees actually work, volunteered by designers against their industry's interest.**

- **The «дизайнеры всех обманывают» accusation is really about комплектация — procurement and specification.** It is «не секрет» that **designers hold agency agreements with suppliers — an agency or wholesale discount.** Analogy they use: buying a flat through an agency, where the developer pays the agent 3% and buyers sometimes demand that 3% back.
- **⚠️ The markup mechanism named from the builder side too: «сколько там смета на электрику? Ну закинь там ещё мои 10–15» — some add 2%, some 3%, some 10–15%.** And the exposure: **after the project is delivered the client can work out that they could have saved that amount on each line.**
- **⚠️ Their stated alternative model, and this is the reusable part: they take the lowest price the supplier will give — «ещё даже отжимаем» — pass that price through to the client, and then charge the client a percentage of that reduced total as their own fee.** So the client «по сути получает услугу комплектации бесплатно», because the fee is offset by the discount secured.
- **⚠️ With a transparency mechanism attached: every month they send the client everything purchased with all receipts, on a shared drive, so the client can verify — and the client pays their percentage against that verified sum.** «Максимально прозрачно, и тут нету никаких вариантов кого-то обманывать.»
- **They confirm the accusation is founded — «реально есть такие люди. Ну конечно есть»** — and he says he is waiting for the whole market to move to the transparent model: «рынок сразу очистится».
- **A scope note worth keeping: комплектация is described as MORE work by volume than the design project itself** — «это работа, которая больше по объёму, чем дизайн-проект».
- **→ Practical consequence for reading any designer-sourced material recommendation in this vault: the recommendation may carry an undisclosed 2–15% commission. Ask which model the designer uses, and ask for the receipts.**

## How a competent estimate is actually built

**Normal contractors take the design project, read the item list, and discuss with you HOW they will solve each task — «желательно у тебя на квартире, а не по фотографии».** Weaker ones cost «от балды». *(Corroborates the "asks questions back" green flag in [[YT_SEdNCGA0Ttg_nsdsgn_choosing_builders_roundtable]].)*
