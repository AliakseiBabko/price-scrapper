## Genuinely New Content

**⚠️ Curved columns/walls with integrated lighting — extends this
store's existing curved-wall trend content (previously bathroom-
specific) to general living spaces, with a new cost/technique
conflict**: a curved half-column or wall section finished with an
integrated linear light source specifically requires Q4-standard
plaster prep on the curved surface — described as expensive, slow, and
in the source's stated experience **practically impossible to achieve
well on a curved surface**. **New practical recommendation**: either
drop the integrated lighting or drop the curved element — trying to
keep both is the combination that doesn't work. This generalizes the
existing bathroom-specific curved-wall cost/complexity caution
(`IFnZxitFeNk`) to any room, and adds the specific lighting-conflict
mechanism not previously recorded.

**Kitchen bar counter lowered to standard dining-table height — new
kitchen design trend, no prior coverage on this store**: rather than a
traditional elevated bar counter (uncomfortable for extended seating,
especially with young children), this trend keeps the counter at a
normal dining-table height while retaining the visual "bar" look/
concept as part of the kitchen cabinetry composition — framed by the
source as one of the best kitchen layout trends of the last 1.5-2 years,
solving the comfort-vs-bar-aesthetic tension directly.

**Projector with a curtain-integrated retractable screen as a bedroom
TV alternative — new topic, no prior coverage on this store**: mounting
a projector (accepting it will never match OLED/QLED image quality) with
a motorized retractable screen built into the same track/mechanism as
blackout curtains, positioned so it can be watched lying down (feet
toward the window/screen wall). **Stated tradeoffs**: image quality is
explicitly secondary to a clean wall (no visible TV/mount hardware),
eye-safety framing (softer large-format image vs. a bright TV panel),
and avoiding complex swing-arm TV-mount hardware. Recommended
specifically for a bedroom, with a separate, higher-quality TV kept in
the living room for when better image quality matters.

**"Invisible/hidden kitchen" trend — new topic, a design-marketing
critique**: fully concealing the entire cooking zone (including the
sink) behind cabinetry so the space reads as having no kitchen at all
when closed. The source's own position: this suits summer kitchens/
terraces in private houses or office pantries, but **not apartments** —
he considers washing dishes immediately after use "not stressful
enough to justify" the added cost/complexity, despite seeing it
increasingly marketed specifically for apartment kitchens on social
media.

**Glass block trend — new topic, decorative partition material**: an
increasingly common design element (roughly every third design project
per the source, a steep rise through 2024) used as an internal partition
or accent (including as a shower/WC divider surface). Explicitly framed
as **a temporary/fading trend** the source associates with a dated
1980s industrial-daylighting material now being repurposed decoratively
— acknowledges it can look genuinely good in the right composition, but
flags the real question before installing it: whether the household is
actually prepared to redo it in 5-7 years once the trend passes,
something he doubts most clients would actually follow through on.

**⚠️ Disguised humidifier styled as a decorative fireplace — new topic,
with an explicit regional health rationale**: recommends a humidifier
built into fireplace-style decorative housing (a "flame" effect created
by illuminated vapor, not real fire — no fuel, no heat, no flue/vent
requirement) as close to a mandatory device for Moscow/Moscow-region
apartments specifically, citing the region's severe winter indoor-air
dryness from central heating. Framed as solving both a genuine air-
quality problem and a decor want simultaneously, rather than a purely
cosmetic choice.
