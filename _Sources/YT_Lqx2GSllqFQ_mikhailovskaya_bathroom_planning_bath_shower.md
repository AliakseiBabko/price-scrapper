## Labor / Delivery Model — ⚠️ the planning method

**Her framing of why the room is hard**: a bathroom concentrates **high functionality and many services into a small area — sanitaryware, electrics, air conditioning** — and you must simultaneously account for **sanitaryware placement, storage, lighting, ventilation, materials, style and decor.** *"Одним из ключевых и сложных моментов планировки квартиры."*

### ⚠️ The first move: the sewer riser decides the WC, and the WC decides everything else

> **"Самое важное — это никуда не переместить унитаз. Унитаз первое, что мы располагаем, если он есть."**

**You cannot take the WC far from the sewer riser**, so the riser's position eliminates most walls before any design begins. **Also fix at the outset: ventilation channels that cannot be demolished, the door, and any pipes or meters.**

### ⚠️ Rough blocks first, detail second

**Place the key items as plain rectangles — WC, shower, basin, washing machine, boiler — and check whether everything fits at all.** Only if it does do you work the layout up.

> **"Сначала мы начинаем с грубых блоков, просто всё примерно, а потом уже, если видим, что окей, тогда разрабатываем дальше."**

**⚠️ And where the room takes a bath rather than a shower, start from the BATH, because it is the largest single object.**

**⚠️ A whole-flat consequence she states plainly**: where the bathroom needs replanning, the walls move and **"планировка должна разрабатываться в комплексе"** — you must first establish how much space the flat can afford to give the bathroom by laying out the bed and storage in the other rooms, **or work the other way and start from the bathroom.** Either way it is not a local decision.

## Fixtures — ⚠️ constraints and failure modes from the three worked layouts

- **⚠️ A boiler cannot be hung on a non-load-bearing wall as-is.** It **requires a welded metal frame to reinforce that wall**, and **that reinforcement has to be entered into the project** — it is not a site decision.
- **⚠️ A glass partition sitting between a built-in cupboard and the shower is a maintenance trap: dust and condensate collect in the gap, that glass gets significantly dirtier than the rest, and you cannot wipe it.** The junction has to be designed rather than left.
- **⚠️ A WC directly facing the door is unacceptable to some clients — so offer both variants.** Where the WC must sit opposite the door, **the installation zone needs a deliberate decorative treatment to carry the attention**; in the alternative layout the basin is the first thing seen, which is *"красивая зона, на которую мы можем привлечь внимание."* `[partial restatement — the vault holds the toilet-visibility rule; the "then design the installation wall as the focal zone" remedy is new]`
- **⚠️ Where the entry is unavoidably tight, that specific fragment must be widened visually — "материалы, фактуры, текстуры, мебель" all chosen to work on that one problem.** A layout compromise creates a materials brief.
- **⚠️ Storage has to be analysed explicitly and by object: mop, buckets, basins, washing powder, shampoos.** Where a shower is pushed tight to the walls **no built-in niches are possible and you are left with surface-mounted shelves — which is a decision, not an accident.** Where nothing fits, the answer may be a section of a wardrobe or store elsewhere in the flat.
- **⚠️ A deep blind pencil unit belongs against a wall, not mid-room** — and if it is the first thing seen on entering, move it.
- **⚠️ From experience, between a shower and free-standing washing and drying machines build a STATIONARY tiled partition rather than glass** — *"чтобы она была глухая"* — because the machines will get wet otherwise.
- **⚠️ But a glass enclosure reads semi-transparent even when frosted, so it consumes less space visually than a solid unit** — *"не так глухо, как шкаф"* — which is why she prefers the shower in the tight position over a cupboard.

### Towel rail — sequence and a selection consequence

**Decide the key zones first — WC, basin, bath, washing machine, boiler — and add the towel rail where it fits.** *"Я советую сначала определиться с ключевыми зонами… а потом уже добавлять полотенцесушитель."*

**⚠️ And the consequence: if the rail is ELECTRIC rather than water-heated, it can go almost anywhere, and the range of models, forms and sizes is far larger.** So **where the towel rail is the awkward item, switching to electric is the move.** *(Corroborates this vault's existing electric-over-hydronic preference, from the planning side rather than the plumbing side.)* `[partial restatement]`

## Materials — ⚠️ bath selection

**Three materials, ranked by cost as she gives it:**

| Material | Cost | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Steel** | **cheapest** | |
| **Acrylic** | **mid** | **⚠️ Her universal recommendation** |
| **Cast iron** | **most expensive as a rule** | **Heavy; requires real effort to carry in; and notably fewer forms available than acrylic** |

> **⚠️ "Самое универсальное — это акриловая ванна: она лёгкая, её легко занести, легко монтировать, очень много разных форм, цветов, вариаций… Если вы предпочитаете выбирать сразу из акриловых, это не будет ошибкой."**

**Rectangular is the most popular and most universal shape for a flat.** The drain may be **left, right or centre**, and that decides which mixer arrangements are open to you.

**⚠️ What to check on a specific model, in her order**: overall dimensions; **the corners**; **the rim thickness**; and capacity, if that matters to you.

- **⚠️ The rim rule, with two competing considerations she does not fully reconcile — recorded as she states them.** Where the bath is built in wall-to-wall, **choose the sharpest possible outer corners and the MINIMUM rim thickness**, because **a thick rim is harder to clean, water collects on it, and it does not look good.** But separately she advises against very sharp corners generally, **because rounded forms are easier to maintain and sharp corners accumulate dirt.** *(Read as: thin rim and crisp junction at the wall; rounded internal geometry.)*

## Fixtures — ⚠️ mixer selection, the strongest section

**Four types: wall-mounted concealed; wall-mounted surface; floor-standing; and bath-rim mounted.**

> **⚠️ "Длина излива — это самый главный параметр подбора ванны и смесителя."**
>
> **The spout must project beyond the bath rim, or the water pours onto the rim rather than into the bath.** Her worked reading off a manufacturer drawing: **~280 mm to the drain, a rim of roughly 100 mm** — and **the bath's parameters and the mixer's have to be checked against each other before either is ordered.**

- **⚠️ Bath-rim mixers fit an acrylic bath without difficulty; with other bath materials there may be problems.** **So choosing a rim mixer effectively decides the bath material.** A coupling most people would not anticipate.
- **⚠️ And a procurement instruction: the mixer's widest part must be checked against the rim, and some manufacturers' drawings omit that dimension. "Нужно или звонить посреднику, или поставщику… или смотреть на других сайтах."** Do not assume it fits.
- **⚠️ Floor-standing mixers: they need clear space around them for cleaning access, they are essentially decorative, and they suit a hotel or a special bathroom. "В 90 процентов случаев для людей этот смеситель будет не актуальный"** — water runs off them and they are inconvenient in use.
- **⚠️ Where the bath doubles as the shower, you need a shower set with a spout** — and the spout length matters again, *"чтобы не попасть в неприятную ситуацию, что вода будет литься на борт ванны."*
- **⚠️ Where there is only a shower and no bath at all, still choose a mixer WITH A SPOUT.** Her reason is concrete: *"по крайней мере набрать ванную для ребёнка или помыть ребёнку ноги."* **She always specifies one; it costs a little more; the alternative is running a separate tap.**
- **A thermostat prevents temperature swings while you are bathing.** Costs more; take it if the budget allows.
- **⚠️ Form has a maintenance consequence: square and angular mixers are harder to look after than round ones. "Если из критериев стоит максимальная простота в обслуживании, лучше предпочесть округлые формы."** Square reads stylish at first, **and then things collect in the corners.**
- **Budget guidance**: if the budget is tight, take something reliable in chrome; **if there is slack, black or coloured becomes available.** Concealed and surface-mounted differ in price.
- **A combined spout-and-overflow («излив-перелив») is a further option.**

## Fixtures — ⚠️ shower enclosure, tray and drain

- **900 × 900 minimum, and bigger is better.** With or without a door; with or without a tray.
- **⚠️ With a tray you must select a SPECIFIC model, because the design then works from its fixed dimensions.**
- **⚠️ Without a tray — a floor drain — is "гораздо проще, гораздо красивее," but then you must select the trap and design the shower floor: where the trap sits, its capacity, its shape, and the fall and form of the floor.**

### ⚠️ The flush-floor shower, and why she argues against it in a flat

> **"Красиво на картинке, но максимально непрактичные в жизни"** — the trap can block or break, and you need a genuinely good fall.

- **⚠️ Minimum fall: 2 cm. "Это прям минимум минимум для того, чтобы вода хорошо уходила."**
- **⚠️ Better still, sink the tray into the floor — feasible if the screed is not yet poured, and mostly achievable in houses rather than flats.** Then the glass sits at the main floor level and the enclosure is recessed, with no threshold needed.
- **⚠️ In a flat, make a threshold. "Я понимаю, что не совсем это красиво, этот порожек, но это гораздо практичнее."** `[partial restatement — the vault holds the threshold argument from Кузина's leak-sensor timing; this is an independent second voice arguing it from drainage and serviceability instead, which strengthens it]`

### ⚠️ Trap selection

- **The main parameter is how much water it can take. More is better.**
- **⚠️ Width matters independently: a wider trap accepts more water even where its rated throughput is lower.**
- **⚠️ Chromed cover plates get dirty and need cleaning. A tile-insert trap with a small visible cover is more practical — but costs more. "А если они стоят недорого, то стоит задумываться, почему они стоят недорого."**

### ⚠️ Screens and enclosures

- **Clear glass is much easier to care for than satinated.**
- **⚠️ A 900 × 900 shower is better with a door.** Also with a door **if anything that can get wet is adjacent.** Where the adjacent space is empty, **a simple glass or tiled partition suffices — but some water barrier is mandatory**, with a curtain only for a very tight budget or where the style calls for it.
- **⚠️ Bath screens — opening, sliding, stationary, folding — each need their technical characteristics and their use understood.** A solid screen **is beautiful in a visualisation and not always practical**; a folding one **collects water on the folded face**, and **needs somewhere to fold to.**
- **⚠️ And the planning consequence: the screen must be designed together with the layout.** If the brief requires a screen that opens a particular way and the layout puts a pencil unit where it must swing, **the layout is wrong, not the screen.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ shower versus bath, and a duty she puts on the designer

> **"Душевая кабина — она не ванная."**

- **If there is any way to fit even a small sit-bath, fit a small sit-bath AND a shower.**
- **⚠️ The case she names specifically: a young couple with no children who prefer showers.** *"Актуальность непосредственно ванны резко вырастет с появлением детей."* A child can be bathed in a baby bath up to about a year, **after which it becomes inconvenient — and even a sit-bath can be far more convenient than a shower enclosure.**
- **If both will not fit, make a bath with a shower over it** — the universal answer.
- **⚠️ And she frames the warning as an obligation: "ваша обязанность как дизайнера — предупредить, для того чтобы потом не было претензий."** **Recorded because it is a client-side entitlement as much as a designer's duty: this is a foreseeable change of requirement, and being told about it is part of what the fee buys.**

## Mistakes / Warnings

- Planning the bathroom before locating the sewer riser.
- Detailing a layout before checking whether the rough blocks fit at all.
- Sizing a partition without adding adhesive and tile.
- Assuming a free-standing washing machine occupies the same 600 as a built-in one.
- Hanging a boiler on a non-load-bearing wall without a reinforcing frame in the project.
- A glass panel enclosing an unreachable gap.
- Ordering a bath and a mixer without checking spout length against rim width.
- A rim mixer specified onto a non-acrylic bath.
- A shower-only bathroom with no spout on the mixer.
- A flush floor-level shower in a flat, especially at less than a 2 cm fall.
- A folding screen with nowhere to fold to, or a pencil unit blocking a screen's swing.
