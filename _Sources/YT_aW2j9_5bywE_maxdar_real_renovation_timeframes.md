- **⚠️ Explicit sequential-dependency rule, not just a scheduling preference**: these rough-work sub-stages **cannot run in parallel** — partition walls must be finished before either electrical or plumbing rough-in can begin; plastering must be finished before water outlets can be brought out of the wall. ## Durable, Reusable Rules (Level 1)

- **Finalize all design wishes/decisions during the design-project stage, not mid-renovation** — the speaker's own worked example: a client requesting a "shadow-gap baseboard" ("теневой плинтус") after plastering is already complete is a common, avoidable schedule-extender, since that baseboard type requires embedded backing installed *during* the plastering stage. Requesting it later means adding new, unplanned work rather than a simple substitution.
- **Plan materials (type, quantity, color/finish) before or during rough-work, not after** — a commodity material (e.g. a bag of cement) can be sourced same-day from any building-supply store, but a specific wallpaper pattern/brand is typically special-order with its own lead time; planning this early avoids the finish-stage material-delivery delays described above.
- **Choose an experienced contractor specifically for their ability to advise correct technical sequencing and realistic material lead times** — framed by the speaker as the single most impactful lever for avoiding schedule overruns, beyond the planning tactics above.
