## Durable Facts

- **Floating/wall-hung furniture and wall-mounted TVs must be planned at the design stage, not decided at the end of a renovation** — both require reinforced wall backing (not standard drywall/masonry) installed before finish work, since ordinary wall attachment risks the furniture detaching under normal use. A dedicated electrical outlet group is also needed directly behind a wall-mounted TV so all connections (set-top box, receiver, multiroom equipment) route invisibly behind it rather than trailing visible cables below.
- **A dedicated cable channel routing TV signal cable to a lower electrical-point group must never be cut into a load-bearing wall** — the channel needs a minimum 60mm groove depth, but load-bearing-wall rebar typically sits at only 40mm depth, so cutting a channel of adequate depth risks striking structural reinforcement. Concrete, checkable installation-safety constraint, genuinely new for this store.
- **Run a dedicated Ethernet (twisted-pair) drop to every TV location regardless of Wi-Fi quality** — corroborates this store's existing Ethernet-for-TV content with a pointed anecdote: an IT professional who publicly argued Wi-Fi was sufficient for any use case later became a client and requested two Ethernet runs per TV once it was his own installation.
- **Duty/night light placement differs by room type**: in general living spaces it should sit low, near the floor (illuminating the floor, not the eyes) — practical rationale given: low duty lights are for avoiding pets/obstacles underfoot at night and letting eyes adjust before the main light comes on, not (per the practitioner's dry joke) bat attacks. **In a WC specifically, duty light should be positioned high**, since there is usually nowhere suitable to mount one low, and it should be bright enough to use the toilet accurately at night without needing the harsh main light.
- **Floating furniture needs a minimum 15cm (ideally 20cm) clearance behind it for curtains to pass** — decided at design time, since (unlike ordinary furniture, which can simply be pulled forward later if curtains don't clear it) floating furniture is expensive and disruptive to unmount and reposition once installed.
- **Concealed curtain cornices recessed into the stretch-ceiling plane** are now a standard recommendation for tall/near-ceiling windows — a dedicated ceiling profile now forms this junction (previously hand-built from drywall). Corroborates the same technique already recorded in this batch from `yt_fx1vISZZK9s`.
- **Back-to-back wardrobe niche technique**: at the design stage, one dividing wall between two rooms can be split into two offset segments, creating a recessed niche on each side (mirrored) for a wardrobe in each adjoining room. **Niche depth must match the wardrobe's purpose**: minimum 50cm for a technical/utility closet (e.g. vacuum storage), minimum 60cm for an outerwear closet.
- **Radiator wall-recessing technique, with a specific depth rule**: recess directly into a soft (non-load-bearing) exterior wall, or furr out a load-bearing exterior wall with a false wall if it can't be recessed into. **The radiator niche must be at least 15cm deep**; if the exterior wall is too thin for a full 15cm recess, the two approaches can be combined (e.g. 10cm recessed + 5cm furred out) to reach the minimum. A concealment grille (metal or wood, any pattern/color) is recommended over an exposed radiator for cleaning ease and design flexibility, and is described as low-cost enough to be broadly affordable.
- **Quartz-vinyl (SPC) flooring installation-cost distinction**: locking ("замковый") installation is standard labor with no separate charge; glue-down ("клеевой") installation is a specialized install billed as an extra line item. Genuinely new pricing-structure nuance for this store's existing quartz-vinyl content.
- **Textured (not smooth) paintable wallpaper is specifically recommended for high-wear rooms** (kitchen-living areas, kids' rooms) — texture hides small dings/scuffs that would be visible from across the apartment on a perfectly smooth painted or paintable-wallpaper wall, and also better conceals seams between wallpaper widths than a smooth finish does.
- **Reinforced-edge interior doors cost the same as standard doors but last longer** — a metal edge profile replaces the standard laminate edge strip that chips at corners over time, at no price premium. Genuinely new door-durability upgrade for this store.
- **Magnetic silent door locks recommended as a near-mandatory modern-door feature** — open/close without sticking. `single-account`, `tier-steering`-adjacent origin comparison: the company states it uses only Italian-made locks on its own projects (no specific brand named), citing Chinese-made locks as noisier and prone to jamming/locking the door shut. Recorded as the company's own stated preference/experience, not adopted as a verified universal quality claim.
- **Concealed door hinges are now buildable by any qualified installer** (previously a rare, hard-to-execute skill) — recommended as a standard, no-longer-risky upgrade.
- **A center divider section splitting a wardrobe into two halves** serves both a practical function (separates siblings sharing a room, or gives a couple separate his/her halves) and a visual one (makes the wardrobe read as visually lighter/less bulky than a single blank door front). **A dedicated electrical cable for future interior wardrobe lighting must be run before installation** — cannot be added afterward once the wardrobe is built.
- **Mirrored door insert panels are explicitly flagged as one of the worst common design choices** — while intended to create a sense of spaciousness, they frequently create the illusion the door is ajar (a gap appears to exist between door and frame), described as especially unsettling in a WC; they also show fingerprints immediately and increase door cost. A clear, named anti-recommendation.
- **All openings (not just doorways) in a comfort-class renovation should be trimmed with the same door-casing-extension material used on interior doors** — and the top-casing height on these trimmed openings must match the top-casing height of the interior doors themselves; failing to match produces a visible 3-4cm height discrepancy between adjacent openings. Extends this store's existing casing-consistency content with a specific numeric mismatch penalty.
- **Baseboards should continue the same color as the door casing extensions** — historically hard to source since baseboards and doors were made by different manufacturers, but most door manufacturers now also produce matching baseboard lines, removing the sourcing problem. Corroborates the same fact independently noted in this store from a different (non-Zemstandart) door-retailer source.
- **Built-in wardrobes are described as strictly better than framed/corpus wardrobes in every practical dimension** (cheaper — pay only for the door, a small attachment particleboard panel, and shelves, not full side/top/bottom panels; more interior space; faster to install; easier to swap/repair) — **with a candid, named conflict-of-interest disclosure**: designers are stated to frequently push corpus wardrobes anyway because furniture companies pay designers large kickbacks on corpus-wardrobe sales, whereas a built-in wardrobe is typically contracted directly between the client and the furniture company, leaving the designer no commission. `single-account`, an unusually candid industry-incentive claim from a practitioner about designers generally (including, implicitly, some of this same industry) — flag prominently as a self-interested-party's disclosure of a structural bias, not a neutral fact, but genuinely valuable for a self-managed buyer to know to ask about.
- **A built-in wardrobe's base/plinth must be built taller than the standard baseboard height**, or the baseboard running up to the wardrobe door will physically block the door from opening, forcing an expensive, space-losing insert fix after the fact. Even experienced furniture installers reportedly forget this because neither doors nor baseboards are typically installed yet at the time of on-site measurement — tracking this detail falls to a project manager or the client themselves absent one.
- **Full-length mirror combined with dedicated makeup lighting is recommended for small entryways** where space doesn't allow separate full-length and makeup mirrors — requires planning a dedicated electrical cable at design stage, since the mirror doesn't light itself out of the box. Extends this store's existing full-length-mirror content.
- **Dedicated Ethernet drop recommended specifically for gaming setups**, beyond the general TV-Ethernet rule — anecdote: clients have called to report losing a competitive online match to high ping and asked (after the fact, awkwardly retrofitted under a baseboard) for a wired connection at their gaming desk. **Rule stated plainly: never rely on Wi-Fi for online shooters/competitive gaming.**
- **A dedicated bedroom makeup zone (separate lit mirror) is recommended for households with only one bathroom**, to avoid a common household friction point (one partner occupying the bathroom for makeup while the other needs the toilet). **Requires a minimum of 2 electrical outlets nearby** — cited client rationale: "if I plug one hairdryer in, where do I plug the second?"
- **Always include a spare/backup sleeping spot in every large room, even a primary bedroom with a couple's own bed and no children** — covers guests, in-laws, or one partner needing to sleep separately during illness. **Sofa-bed type matters**: a split-mattress-cushion sofa bed (mattress separates into distinct cushions when folded) should always be chosen over a single continuous-mattress design, at only a marginal price difference — described as faster/easier to fold out and noticeably more comfortable to actually sleep on (a continuous mattress creates a gap/trough between cushions when unfolded).
- **A 40cm clearance beside a bed/nightstand is a normal, walkable width** — explicit reassurance aimed at clients who worry this feels too tight; stated as comfortable for anyone except an unusually large-bodied person.
- **A lift-mechanism storage bed costs roughly 10,000–15,000 RUB more than a standard bed**, but the recovered storage volume is roughly comparable to an average wardrobe's capacity — presented as a strong value trade-off, recommended broadly.
- **TV-facing-a-bed placement forces a real compromise between two options**: mount the TV high on a wall bracket (better viewing angle, but can look visually imposing) or leave it lower on a stand (looks more modest but forces an uncomfortable, neck-straining chin-to-chest viewing angle). The practitioner recommends bracket-mounting at a higher position, prepared for during wall construction (bracket-rated backing).
- **A dedicated walk-in-closet exhaust fan is described as strictly required when a walk-in closet exists** (achievable in roughly only 20% of apartments per the practitioner's estimate) — **this fan must tie into the building's shared exhaust ventilation with a mandatory check-valve**, or a strong kitchen exhaust fan running elsewhere in the unit can pull cooking odors backward into the closet, tainting stored clothing.
- **Above-bed pass-through switch for the main light, plus separate switches for each bedside reading light** — lets an occupant turn off the main light without walking to the door. If the headboard is tall/high, the switch group should split into two separate switches, each positioned above its own nightstand. Requires furniture to be selected before the design/electrical-point stage is finalized.
- **Avoid light fixtures that cycle through fixed color-temperature presets by click order (warm → cold → neutral)** — corroborates the same anti-recommendation already recorded in this batch from `yt_O_Gp1FPzkqQ` (independent restatement, same channel): clients reportedly love these fixtures for about a month, then find the color mismatch between adjacent fixtures constantly irritating and eventually replace them.
- **Specialized wardrobe-interior storage mechanisms** (dedicated pull-out organizers for trousers, ties, watches, even shoelaces) make full use of a walk-in closet's storage depth and are recommended as worthwhile — but **don't need to be purchased immediately at renovation completion**; a standard closet can be ordered first and upgraded with these mechanisms later once budget allows.
