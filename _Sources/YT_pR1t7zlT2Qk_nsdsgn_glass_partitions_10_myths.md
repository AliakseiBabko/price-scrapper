# Source Note — NSDSGN with Руслан (Декус), ten myths about glass partitions

**Filmed in the manufacturer's showroom.** Guest is from **Декус**, which makes
partitions, doors, case and soft furniture. **⚠️ Note this is a different Руслан from
the builder in [[YT_M0Wv4SOSUhs_nsdsgn_builder_qa_costs_and_estimates]]** — same
first name, unrelated source.

**Promotional ratio HIGH, and it is the highest of this round.** Mid-video the host
is invited to film their furniture on a large house in Солнечное; there are plugs for
their soft furniture, their case furniture, a collaboration with the brand «Тепло»,
their showrooms in Moscow, St Petersburg and Baku, and a long closing pitch for the
host's own design studio («интерьер мечты под ключ без головной боли»).

**⚠️ The title asks «дорого или нет?» and the video never gives a single price.** Not
one figure for a partition, a door, or a square metre. **This was the sharpest
tier-steering test in the round and it is a clean fail on the price question** — the
"expensive?" myth is answered entirely with a cost-*structure* argument that justifies
the price rather than quoting it. What partly redeems it is recorded below: **two of
the ten myths are conceded as true rather than debunked.**

Region level 1 — Moscow, St Petersburg and Baku all named as their own showroom
locations.

## ⚠️ Soundproofing — the one hard-number passage, with a caveat

The most valuable content in the source, and directly relevant to any glass partition
used to divide a living space.

- **8 mm single glazing ≈ the performance of an average interior door.**
- **Two 4 mm panes laminated as триплекс ≈ 40 dB.**
- **Триплекс with an acoustic interlayer film (шумоизолирующая плёнка) — up to 50 dB.**
- Reference points he offers: **jackhammer ~100 dB, angle grinder ~80–90 dB.**
- **Mechanism stated correctly: to improve glass acoustics you increase its mass.**
  «Стекло достаточно неплохой звукоизолирующий материал» — offered against the common
  assumption that glass gives no isolation at all.
- **⚠️ One claim in this passage is wrong and is recorded as such**: he glosses 50 dB
  as reducing noise «на 50, в половину». **Decibels are logarithmic; a 50 dB reduction
  is not "half".** The dB figures themselves are ordinary product specifications and
  are recorded normally; only his interpretation of them is flagged.
- Decorative option that does not cost acoustics: **metallised fabric can be laminated
  into the triplex** (copper and gold tints shown).

## ⚠️ Span limits — the constraint that decides material choice

- **Aluminium**: corrosion-proof and popular **but soft. Structures over 4 m with a
  thin profile become unstable.** Some rigidity can be recovered from glass thickness,
  but they will not go beyond a limit the ASR garbles as «80 мм» — **implausible for
  architectural glass and recorded as `uncertain`; the operative figure is the 4 m
  span limit, which he states twice.**
- **Steel**: «практически не ограничен». It welds, it is rigid, any profile tube size
  can be selected, and **they have repeatedly built 6 m and 12 m runs.**
- **→ The decision rule: aluminium up to ~4 m; beyond that, steel.**
- **⚠️ A logistics constraint that gets forgotten at design stage**: on a live St
  Petersburg project the client wanted a single continuous sheet of glass for a walk-in
  wardrobe, and the real problem was **getting it into the flat — cranes, removing a
  window, passing the glass through.** «Там уже вопрос не к вам, а к тому, как это всё
  занести в квартиру.»

## Materials, corrosion and wet rooms

- Four options: **aluminium** (does not corrode at all), **painted steel**,
  **stainless steel**, and **all-glass in a profile**.
- **For bathrooms they recommend stainless steel — painted or brushed — or aluminium.**
  Shower enclosures and full glass walls in place of walls are both stated as normal
  applications.
- **Safety glass only: триплекс or закалённое.** «Опасное стекло мы никогда не
  используем.» Tempered shatters into very small fragments — a light cut at worst, not
  a serious laceration.
- **On the children's-safety myth: triple safety factor claimed, and in over ten years
  of their work no fixing has ever torn out.** Recorded as the manufacturer's own claim
  (`unverified`), and note the host's framing that this is «так же боятся, как боятся
  жить в квартире с окнами».

## ⚠️ Two myths conceded as true — the counterweight to the promotional framing

**Both are maintenance/quality admissions against interest, and they are why this
source is not simply an advert.**

1. **Dust on horizontal glazing bars is real.** «Друзья, пыль существует. Это не миф.
   Есть определённая сложность в уборке.» Standing in front of a partition with many
   horizontal rails, he says it needs wiping **roughly once a month** to stay clean.
2. **Painting metal well is genuinely difficult.** «Это не миф. Это тоже правда.»
   Their own process: **degrease, then prime, then powder-coat** — 90% of what is in
   their showrooms is powder coating done in-house. **Many skip priming because some
   powder-paint manufacturers state it is unnecessary.** Painting also requires a
   dust-free environment. His verdict: «очень мало хороших, качественных покрасок.»

## ⚠️ Why it is not cheap — the cost structure, with named failure points

He tells this as a story against himself: before entering the trade they visited a
Rimadesio salon, asked the price, and concluded they had found a gold mine — glass and
aluminium are cheap, steel cheaper still. They were wrong, and the reasons are
specific and checkable:

- **Door closers (доводчики): outside Italian and German makers, few can produce one
  that lasts 5 years without failure.**
- **⚠️ Rollers are the concrete failure mode: cheap soft rollers go oval, and the
  partition starts jumping within about two months.**
- High-tech aluminium, expensive rollers, expensive closers and expensive paint each
  raise the unit cost.
- **Labour content: steel work is entirely by hand; aluminium is more factory- and
  machine-adaptable but still carries much manual work.** Assembly is **without
  welding**, so tolerances are «с волосину» hair-thin, involving milling and fitting.
- **On the aluminium-versus-steel price myth, his actual answer is the useful one:
  there is no price without a specific specification.** More aluminium means dearer,
  both from material cost and from the complexity of combining thick and thin profiles
  — but «есть алюминиевые, которые значительно превзойдут по стоимости любую
  стальную».
- **The host draws the parallel to renovation costing explicitly, and it corroborates
  the same rule from the builder podcast**: «когда вы спрашиваете, сколько стоит этот
  ремонт — надо сначала нарисовать ремонт, посчитать ремонт, и потом мы уже скажем.
  Пока что можно сказать только ориентировочные цены.»

## Style, and the durability-of-fashion argument

- **Myth that metal partitions suit only loft interiors: rejected with a method.**
  They integrate into classical interiors by using classical forms and **natural
  materials — латунь (brass), timber, marble** — and **combined infills** (a timber
  panel plus glass) rather than plain glass. For classical work, use **ribbed or
  frosted glass**, not modern clear.
- **Their doors are frosted glass painted from the inside, not painted MDF or enamel.**
  Claimed advantages: **does not scratch, and cleans with a wet cloth** — «этот
  материал гораздо лучше, чем тот же MDF».
- Fashion-durability argument: Japanese sliding rice-paper partitions from the 1800s;
  the French window, «350 лет» (he says «вторая половина XV века» but the arithmetic
  points to the 17th century — recorded as his approximation); and **Rimadesio, 70
  years old this year**, still making aluminium partitions. **⚠️ The distinction he
  draws is the transferable part: fashion is fleeting, but metal, timber and marble are
  style — «это то, что действительно никогда не надоест».**
- **Market driver he names for the whole category: living space is shrinking while its
  price rises**, so partitions earn their place by combining rooms and extending
  perceived space rather than by looking fashionable.

## One furniture durability finding, incidental but concrete

**They stopped using ordinary furniture leather because of actual warranty claims.**
Chairs bought for use beside dressing tables produced dye transfer: women sitting down
after a bath **with wet hair in a silk robe** found paint marks left on the robe. They
switched to a **deep-matte automotive leather that does not transfer dye.** Recorded
because the failure mechanism is specific and the fix is nameable.
