## ⚠️⚠️ Why he does it — and both reasons are about quantities, not drawings

> 1. «**Очень сложно посчитать необходимое количество материала**, как оно будет располагаться, не будет ли что-то там перекрывать, мешать друг другу — **те же вот уголки, они тоже занимают место, они не должны нигде пересекаться** — а чтобы весь гипсокартон в нужном месте поместился, все закладные и так далее. Это реально помогает.»
> 2. «**Объём действительно не маленький, и всё это вручную посчитать можно либо долго, либо с большой погрешностью.**»

**→ The model is a quantity-surveying instrument first and a drawing second.** The
clash-detection benefit (connectors occupying space, board fitting, backings
landing) is real but secondary in his own ordering.

## ⚠️⚠️ The take-off workflow — this is what the vault had nothing on

1. **Measure the room exactly once the walls are built and plastered, and enter the real dimensions.** On this job **180 cm deep and 178.5 cm wide**. **⚠️ Adjacent rooms are modelled at approximate, "conditional" sizes purely for orientation** — exact where it matters, conditional where it does not.
2. **Mark the services that already exist** — here two risers — before designing anything.
3. **Model the frame system, the embedded backings, the frames and the hatches.**
4. **⚠️ Run the program's report: it returns quantity, article number and name for everything in the model — «почти 100 позиций», and that still excludes the profiles.**
5. **Export to Excel and edit** — **he uses a different sewer system than the library's, so those article numbers have to be swapped by hand.** «Но тем не менее это реально ускоряет процесс, помогает.»
6. **⚠️⚠️ A SEPARATE spreadsheet for the profiles, and this is the best single item in the video**: enter each required piece's length and count, and it outputs **how many stock lengths to order AND the cutting plan** — which stick to cut into which pieces.

**His worked example, and the figures reconcile:**

| Figure | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| Stock lengths to order (3 m sticks) | **38** |
| Total ordered | **114 m** |
| Actually consumed | **~113 m** (spoken imprecisely) |
| **Total offcut waste across the whole job** | **86 cm** |

`arithmetic-consistent`: 38 × 3 m = 114 m, and 114 − 0.86 = 113.14 m consumed — so
the "113" is a rounded reading of 113.14. **Under 1 % waste on a large framing
package, achieved by nesting the cuts rather than by buying carefully.** «Реально
очень сложно всё это вручную посчитать.»

**⚠️ What he deliberately does NOT model**, which is the scoping discipline that makes
the method affordable:

- **The pipes themselves** — «сами трубы я не проектировал, без надобности».
- **The hatch hinges** — «я их тоже конечно здесь не стал проектировать, но это неважно».
- **A third riser hidden behind an existing boxing** whose exact position he does not know — «это и не принципиально, его переделать я не собираюсь».
- **⚠️ But fixings ARE now modelled, and he flags this as a change in his own practice**: «все крепежи сейчас, да — **раньше я этого не делал, теперь стараюсь закладывать сразу в проект**».

## The project itself — decisions worth carrying

- **⚠️ Top-floor freedom, used for the second time in this series**: on the last floor the upper stack is the **vent section («фановая часть»), carrying only air, with no discharge from above** — «сверху только уже крыша» — **so he can bend and reroute it freely.** Compare the ventilation-valve trick in [[_Sources/YT_4SZG3yJqtQk_sanuzel_p44_ep2_plaster_and_screed|episode 2]]: the top floor keeps buying him options.
- **The node was originally planned inside the partition between the two rooms and then moved into the туалет** — the reason is given only obliquely and is not recoverable; **not carried.**
- **⚠️ When the profile system is justified — a criteria list rather than a preference**: (a) the client had seen it in showrooms and online and wanted it; (b) **it is genuinely warranted here because of the sheer number of embedded elements** — a shower frame with a wall drain, two wall-hung WCs, plus a sewer stack passing through; (c) **the туалет would have needed a boxing anyway**, so the marginal cost is smaller than it looks.
- **Backings («закладные») designed in for**: the vanity unit, a wall cabinet, a second cabinet above it in a column, a **mirror cabinet**, and **the overhead shower** — «там он крепится к стене… чтобы хорошо держалась, **потому что большой рычаг**». **⚠️ And one deliberately omitted, with the uncertainty stated: the small second basin — «раковина будет маленькая, я думаю, я в профиль войду… либо, ну, посмотрим».**
- **⚠️ Two hatches at deliberately different heights** — one onto the water node, one in the shower niche **for cleaning kit (mops, buckets)**. Behind the lower one the depth is **~30 cm**, with a small gypsum-board shelf inside so things can stand on it, and **the rodding eye may be reachable through it.** Access designed as a real dimension, not as a panel.
- **The node as designed**: instantaneous water heater, leak-protection control block, **100-micron flush filters**, magistral filters with **softening / iron-removal or universal cartridges**, water-hammer compensators, manifolds — plus **pressure reducers, meters and check valves changed in the common riser**. Pipes then **run across the ceiling on clamps** down to the node. Corroborates the built node in episode 3.
- **⚠️ Kitchen manifold economy, with the tradeoff visible**: four outlets — cold to the mixer, cold to the washing machine, cold to the dishwasher, hot to the sink — but **the two appliance colds are taken off tees so the manifold needs only ONE outlet to the kitchen.** «Небольшая такая экономия.» **A deliberate trade of per-fixture isolation for a cheaper manifold**, which is worth naming because this vault's manifold material argues *for* per-fixture isolation.
- **A double siphon for the washing machine and dishwasher**, sold for concealed installation — **but he notes it can simply sit openly behind a cabinet drawer, «ничего страшного».**
- **⚠️ Why this riser and not the other one**: distances are about equal, **but from this one the run is straight with no extra turns.** A clean tie-break criterion.
- **Sizes: a 110 tee with a 90 outlet for the installation frame, connecting almost directly.** ⚠️ And an honest tolerance statement: «понятно дело, тут некоторые погрешности монтажа есть в проектировании, но это не критично».
- **⚠️ The drainage rule stated for the third time on this channel, and here as a design-stage conclusion rather than a site fix**: the drain always rises, «это называется уклон, для того чтобы вода самотёком уходила» — **«чем ниже вход, тем ниже будет и конечная точка»**, so the bottom tee is lowered (breaking a little screed out) specifically to get the shower tray down.
- **An AC condensate drain was designed in** — «тоже не забыл его».

## ⚠️ A modelling-literacy point worth keeping

> «Не надо ориентироваться, что вот оно, высота, допустим, 60 — надо всё вместе это поместить. **Нет: с разного угла обзора мы можем увидеть разные элементы.**» — Василий

**A single view is not the constraint; rotate the model.** He publishes the model
itself as a view-only link in two states — **unclad and clad** — so a client can turn
it without being able to edit it.
