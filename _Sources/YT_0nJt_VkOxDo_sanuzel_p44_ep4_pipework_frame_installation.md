## Distribution pipework

- **PEX with an internal aluminium layer**, sold in coils as one continuous piece — «сгибается, принимает определённую форму и держит её». One unbroken length from manifold to fixture, consistent with the vault's existing PEX case.
- **Insulation is fitted to every pipe, 6 mm wall** — and he grounds it in regulation rather than preference: **«теплоизоляция обязательно по нормативам, по ГОСТам — все трубы должны быть изолированы».** ⚠️ Russian GOST, asserted without citation — carry the practice, flag the jurisdiction.
- Pipes in chases fixed with **perforated band («перфолента»)**; where the chase is tight the pipe is held by its width anyway, and fixed additionally regardless.
- **⚠️ Bending into an internal corner needs a pipe bender**, to reach the minimum radius: «так точно не согнуть» by hand. **No joints in the corner** — the pipe turns through it.
- **Label the pipe ends** — «я здесь подписал, чтобы не потерялись, потому что всё-таки они очень похожи».
- **Joint anatomy at the manifold**: **euroconus, which he calls «самое надёжное соединение из разборных»**, and **conical union nuts sealing on an O-ring rather than a flat gasket**. The sleeve is **double-ended** (either way round), the pipe end must be **expanded** to take the fitting, and **it must enter right up to the last shoulder («буртик») — a visible acceptance check.**
- Fixings deliberately set so **pipes can also run behind the manifold.**

## ⚠️ Water outlets («водорозетки») — the template trick

**Metal templates with plugs, and they are double-sided**: one way sets the outlet **1 cm proud of the wall**, the other **flush with the plaster**. He needs flush here because the wall is to be painted. Set on gypsum, **~5 minutes to grab**, then plugs and templates come off for reuse. **Level is taken from the box's centre.**

- **He sees no point in fixing the outlets through their own lugs** — they hold, and get embedded further when the chase is filled.
- **Kitchen sink outlets: template spacing chosen at 150 mm centres, cold on the right and hot on the left** — corroborating the hot-left/cold-right convention already recorded in this vault.
- Mixing gypsum in **half a rubber ball** — it flexes, so leftovers pop out and nothing sets in the vessel.

## ⚠️⚠️ Concealed mixers — depth, a product defect, and the serviceability answer

**Installation depth: 50–70 mm from the instructions — and the number already includes the finish covering.** «Эта глубина указана уже с учётом чистового покрытия, в данном случае плитки, поэтому не надо забывать о её толщине.» He builds a template to prove it: **5 cm of structure + 1 cm of tile = 6 cm**, inside the range. **A classic error source: measure to the tile face, not to the substrate.**

**⚠️ And he names a defect class rather than praising the product**: this particular concealed basin mixer has **a mounting plate that is not machined flat** («площадка для крепления не фрезерованная»), so **it rocks however it is fixed** — «придётся что-то придумывать». Worth checking before purchase, because it is invisible in a catalogue photograph.

**For the shower mixer the instructions gave no depth at all**, so he measured it with a tape and found that fixing the **plywood backing to the back face of the frame** landed it correctly **allowing for two board layers plus tile**.

**⚠️ The serviceability answer — the strongest passage in the episode, and it addresses the fear directly** («у многих есть переживание: а что же будет со скрытым смесителем, а вдруг он потечёт»):

> Once tiled, what remains reachable is whatever sits inside the embedded box, and the plaster is deliberately kept back so the opening survives. Through it you can **service the internal valves — unscrew them, clean the strainer, change the seals — or replace the insert entirely**, and **change the lever or the thermostat**. **What stays permanently in the wall is only the brass body: «цельный кусок металла, с которым ничего произойти не может».**
>
> **His general claim: every wall-recessed mixer has a service path designed in by its engineer for whatever actually needs servicing — and the brass body is not on that list because nothing happens to it.** — Василий

**And the verification is the pressure test at install time**, which is what he does next.

## ⚠️ The height of the sewer outlet is set from the furniture, not from a standard

He knows which vanity and which basin are coming, **draws them on the wall**, then **holds the actual siphon under the basin position and marks off it** — deliberately slightly low, **because that siphon is height-adjustable** and can be brought up to the exact figure later.

**⚠️ And he uses a purpose-made «сифонный отвод» rather than a 90° elbow**: it is **shorter**, so it **protrudes less at finish stage and allows a shallower chase** — «не всегда толщина стен позволяет сделать глубокую штробу». A specific component that solves the thin-partition problem, and this vault has nothing on it.

## The carcass, and the hatch

- **Профиль ТЦ: galvanised steel, nominally square in section but effectively cruciform.** Fixed to the walls on **adjustable brackets**, so wall irregularities can be levelled out or projections bypassed. **The profiles are not screwed to each other — they join via dedicated corner connectors clamped by a single screw**, giving a true perpendicular.
- Sequence: **perimeter first with brackets left adjustable**, set to the laser; then **vertical posts** clamped with triangular clamps, levelled, temporarily fixed, with ceiling corner pieces working in two planes at once.
- **⚠️ Posts must be cross-tied to one another or they deflect** — «потом их между собой перевязать, чтобы не было вот такого прогиба». The system is then **self-supporting and rigid.**
- **Plywood inserts («закладные») where a concealed mixer or an outlet needs backing.**
- **⚠️ A push-to-open concealed hatch built from the same profile**, using inserts sold separately for it: holes drilled in the profile, the hinge-kit insert dropped inside and bolted tight, a bearing over the stud, the rotating axis assembled, capped and locked with the same corner connectors. One press and it opens, giving **full access to the inlet node**.
- **⚠️ The hinge axis was deliberately set further back because the towel rail lands there** — so the door clears it and has somewhere to swing. **A clash resolved at frame stage that would be unfixable after tiling.**
- **Overhead shower rose at a non-standard spacing: two 1/2" outlets at 50 mm centres, with no insert available** — solved with **a template whose increments are 25 mm**, so 50 mm becomes trivial. Tooling that turns an awkward dimension into a routine one.

## ⚠️⚠️ Perspectives — must a wall-hung WC frame bear on the floor?

**This source mounts the installation module into the carcass only, and defends it:**

> «Обратите внимание, что модуль установлен только в систему каркаса. Сам каркас является несущей системой, поэтому он встраивается сюда, в модуль — **и не надо путать её с другими рамами, которые крепятся в пол и в стены.** Здесь достаточно много креплений, соответственно нагрузка распределяется равномерно.» He then hangs off it, jumps and pulls on it on camera. — Василий

**⚠️ This diverges from a rule this vault holds from two other sources** — Kruglov's «the critical requirement isn't the frame's rated capacity but its footing: the feet must rest on the structural slab», independently confirmed by Sidorik. **The divergence is real and should not be smoothed:** he is not skipping the footing carelessly, he is using **a load-bearing profile carcass that redistributes the load into many wall fixings** and using a **frame model sold for exactly that mounting**. That is a different structural concept, not the same one done badly.

**What is genuinely open**: whether the many-fixings-into-walls approach is equivalent in service to a floor-borne footing, and whether it depends on the wall substrate. **Neither side offers a load figure.** Note also that this job took **a cheaper frame model from the same maker that is not quite matched to the system** — and that **a universal insert-bracket kit with a template exists that fits almost any installation frame**, which is the part that generalises.

## ⚠️ Pressure test — a distinct protocol, and one independent corroboration

- **Water run, all taps opened, then the riser valves closed and the test pump connected.**
- **⚠️ The physics stated, which is why bleeding matters**: «важно помнить, что воздух сжимается, а вода — несжимаемая жидкость, поэтому воздух из труб нужно согнать». He fits **Mayevsky (bleed) valves everywhere** for this — they hold pressure and release air.
- **Hot and cold bridged with a jumper to test both circuits in one pressurisation** — **independent corroboration of the same "lifehack" already recorded in this vault from Zemskov.**
- **Working pressure 3 atm; test at 10 atm.**
- **⚠️ The protocol adds a step this vault's version does not have**: hold **~30 minutes**, then **top back up to 10 atm** — «за это время… может, где-то ещё воздух остался» — and hold **~5 minutes more**. If it holds then, it passes. **The re-pressurise-and-re-hold catches residual air that would otherwise read as a pass or as a false drop.**
- **⚠️ Instrument caution**: his own gauge's scale ends at **6 atm**, so at 10 atm the needle is «на второй круг пошёл». Worth knowing before trusting a reading.
- **Only after it passed did he fill the chases** — «могу быть уверен, что передаю заказчику полностью исправную и рабочую систему».

## Electrics and the towel rail

The client's electrician left cable ends; he routes them to the points — **mirror, two sockets, a switch, the fan, and the electric towel rail.**

- **⚠️ He solders the twists inside junction boxes**: «кабель я в них паяю — это надёжное соединение, которое разрешено скрывать и не обслуживать в дальнейшем», by **dipping the twisted joint into molten solder**. ⚠️ The "permitted to conceal and leave unserviced" claim is a **Russian regulatory assertion with no code cited** — carry the technique, flag the jurisdiction.
- **⚠️ Two lifehacks that are unrecoverable if missed:**
  1. **Run the towel-rail cable deliberately off the rail's mounting axis**, so it cannot be drilled through when the rail is fixed.
  2. **Fit a socket box where no socket will go** — the rail uses a concealed connection, but the box **gives the cable travel and slack**, so after tiling there is a small hole through which the cable can still be pulled or pushed.
- **Towel rail (Indigo, Russian maker)**: plug **or** concealed connection, **left- or right-hand entry as a purchase option**, connectors he rates highly («первый раз такое встречаю»), and unusually thorough instructions. Mild product praise, low stakes.

## Sheathing

- **Cement board (Aquapanel type)** — «сделаны из цемента, не боятся влаги, не подвержены грибку… идеальный вариант». ⚠️ He states a thickness of **«3 мм»**, which is **not a real Aquapanel thickness — treat as ASR garble and do not carry the figure.**
- **Screws made for cement board: large head and a drill point at the tip**, entering the ТЦ profile «как в масло». **⚠️ They must sit slightly below the surface, about 0.5 mm.**
- **Mark where the profiles are before driving screws, so as not to hit a pipe.**
- **Two layers**, matching the two-layer rule recorded from his ПИК job.
- **The tile will extend well past the hatch's own panel** — the panel is deliberately undersized for his own access convenience.
