## ⚠️⚠️ The One Toolchain Item Worth Keeping: generation as OPTION EXPLORATION

**Beyond tracing an existing sketch, the tool GENERATES a plan from a boundary plus a prompt.** His worked prompt is specific enough to record as a shape: *"generate a floor plan for a single-storey two-bedroom beach house, a 10 by 12 m footprint, which is 120 sq m, open-plan living, dining and kitchen along the sea-facing wall, which is the north wall…"* - **footprint, area, room programme, and an orientation constraint tied to a real site condition.**

**⚠️ And his scope statement is the part that transfers, because it is the same honest framing he gave for tracing:**

> *"This is not meant to be like a final generation where you just click send to AI and then you actually get the floor plan. **It's meant to be a starting point to get you over the BLANK CANVAS SYNDROME**, which is where the hardest part is to draw the first wall - and then you can make tweaks as you go, **or even generate multiple options and explore stuff much faster.**"*

> **→ GENERATION AS OPTION EXPLORATION, NOT AS ANSWER.** The output's value is that it is *something to react to*, and that several can be produced cheaply. **Consistent with the concept-versus-measurement line this vault already draws on [[18_Digital_Toolchain/analysis/AI_For_Concept_And_Visualisation|AI for Concept and Visualisation]]** - and it is the same distinction, arriving at the plan stage rather than the render stage.

**⚠️ What he actually corrects is telling, and it supports the framing: his fixes are DESIGN JUDGEMENTS, not geometry errors.** He removes a walk-in closet because the bedroom cannot take one, re-sizes the kitchen, switches a door's opening side, and reconsiders what a leftover space should be. **The generated plan was buildable-looking and functionally coherent; what it lacked was judgement about this particular brief.**

- A minor but real usability note: doors are **configured** rather than chosen from a library - sliding, then pocket, in two clicks - which he contrasts with hunting through a door library.

## ⚠️⚠️ Design Concept - the entrance-transition passage, which is real knowledge and not about software

**Correcting the generated plan, he articulates why a narrow entrance is wrong, and the reasoning is transferable interior-planning knowledge that has nothing to do with the tool.** He shrinks the kitchen to widen the entrance, and gives three distinct reasons for wanting a **transition from outdoors to indoors**:

1. **A feeling of arrival** - the entrance should read as a threshold rather than as a doorway straight into the plan.
2. **⚠️ Privacy - so that the bedrooms and living room are NOT immediately visible from the entrance.**
3. **⚠️ A buffer - stopping noise from outside, and the pollution coming from the road and cars.**

> **→ Three independent justifications for an entrance transition zone: arrival, sightline privacy, and an acoustic/air buffer.** **⚠️ Directly relevant to this project's own `01_Entrance/` and `02_Hallway/` pages**, which hold layout and storage content; **the sightline-privacy argument in particular is a checkable test to apply to any plan - stand at the front door and ask what you can see.**
