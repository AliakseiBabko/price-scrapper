## Design Concept — Why Beige and Grey, and How They Differ in Reading

- Beige is framed as a warm natural colour with direct natural referents (sand, sun) that lets a person relax at home; its long popularity is attributed to that rather than to trend. She addresses the "простенький, бежевенький" dismissal directly and rejects it: beige-brown interiors were current before and still appear in well-worked contemporary ones.
- Grey is equally natural and neutral and serves as an equally good background, **but reads differently** — beige as enveloping, warm, domestic; grey more often as technical, office-like, cold, "более невзрачный."
- **Both have a saturation failure mode, stated symmetrically**: overdo a warm beige's saturation and the interior "может играть всеми жёлтыми красками" and read as a **баня** or aged dacha panelling, losing all polish. Overdo grey's darkness/saturation and the interior goes gloomy and cold.
- **⚠️ Climate/daylight as a real input to that decision**: in a place with few sunny days (St Petersburg named) a sufficiently gloomy grey interior leaves you with "совсем грустное настроение"; in a high-sun location (Sochi named) the same gamut may be optimal. Directly transferable reasoning for a Belarus project.
- **Taupe defined**: "серо-коричневый с элементами какой-то лиловой гаммы" — the grey-beige mix that she says is what most contemporary interiors are actually running.

## Design Concept — The Actual Decision Criterion

- **⚠️ Style does not decide it, and material availability does not decide it.** Beige appears in classical, neoclassical, japandi, minimalism and contemporary work; grey slightly less in classical but present as a background. Paint, decorative plaster, wallpaper, porcelain stoneware and tinted timber/parquet all exist in both, so the material category doesn't force the choice either.
- **⚠️ Texture *does* correlate, and is the first real constraint**: a sand-like plaster or a timber surface reads naturally in beige; a concrete-effect plaster in a beige gamut "будет выглядеть, ну, как будто бы чужеродно." So the intended surface finishes narrow the choice even when the material category doesn't.
- **⚠️ The decisive criterion — balance against the dominant material already in the room.** This is the source's most useful single rule:
  - **A cold, "звонкий" interior with a lot of metal (stainless steel named) and glass**: grey will only deepen the cold gloom — **balance it with a warm, calm, light beige background instead.**
  - **An interior with a lot of wood and warm rough textures**: **grey dilutes that gamut** and stops the room reading overloaded and over-saturated.
  - She states this must be settled at the collage/concept stage, before choosing the shade, since it depends on which materials will dominate.
- Second input: whether the beige or grey is acting purely as a background supporting an idea, or as an accent element in its own right, and whether the scheme stays near-monochrome or carries bright additions. She explicitly declines to give fixed pairings ("вряд ли сейчас мы с вами найдём какие-то идеальные сочетания, что, например, бордовый сочетается только с бежевым"), on the grounds that shade, saturation and temperature decide it — grey itself can be warm or cool.
- **Lighting can invalidate the choice after the fact**: a calm light beige under bright yellow lighting goes emphatically yellow and "может убить всю актуальность интерьера"; a cool grey under cool lighting reads sharper still, and "про уют нам придётся забыть."

## Design Concept — Sampling Technique (the strongest practical content here)

- **⚠️ The fan-deck lightness correction — a specific, checkable, actionable rule not previously in this vault.** A tinting fan deck is judged on a very small chip, and **the deck is held perpendicular to the light source**, so light falls on it fully and the shade reads brighter and fresher than it will in place. Paint a large wall in that same shade, with light falling *along* rather than onto it, and **"оттенок на стене будет намного намного темнее и насыщенней."** **Her prescription: when choosing from a fan deck, pick one — or even two — tones lighter than the beige or grey that appealed to you**, and the finished result will be the tone you wanted.
- **Confirm with a real выкрас**: tint a small can, put several выкрасы directly on the object, on the actual surface, under that room's actual light and daylight, and judge the shade "уже в массе." Independently corroborates the mandatory-выкрас rule from ARCHWOOD (`AKxOtRhIcJY`) and the ватман variant from Vittoria Selina (`jJ05zqugmSc`).
- **⚠️ Physically juxtapose every sample, don't judge them separately.** Named failure mode: beige, grey, a wood finish and a kitchen front each look fine viewed alone, "но стоит приложить их друг к другу, и какой-то из образцов будет явно выбиваться, уходить в розовый, в зелёный, будет просто слишком тёплым или слишком тёмным." **Instruction: bring together everything that can be anticipated — door finishes, kitchen, furniture order, wall выкрасы, flooring, skirting — and at minimum compare the samples side by side.** This is the practical counterpart to Кузина's undertone warning (`n5ZBqdq0wH8`) and is the concrete procedure that catches it.
- Texture is a further correction factor at selection time: rougher surfaces read more saturated and darker.
