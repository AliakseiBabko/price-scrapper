## Furniture/Built-ins, Materials, Family Requirements / Preferences

- **⚠️ Leg-based (vs. fully-upholstered-to-the-floor) sofa silhouette — a specific applied psychology-of-furniture principle, Krasnov's own home as the example**: furniture raised on visible legs reads as subconsciously "lighter" because the eye/brain registers visible floor space beneath it as open, uncommitted space, while furniture that sits flush to the floor reads as fully "occupying" that footprint even if the object itself is the same size. States he deliberately chose a LEG-based sofa for his own living room specifically for this reason, explicitly acknowledging the tradeoff that leg-based sofas typically can't fold out into a sofa-bed, prioritizing his own everyday living experience over guest-sleepover flexibility.
- **⚠️ Large-vs-small-rug investment rule**: recommends neutral-toned or universal-pattern rugs specifically for LARGE rugs (expensive, hard/costly to replace when trends shift) — but small entry/kitchen/bathroom rugs are the right place to experiment with bold colour/pattern, since they're cheap and easy to swap seasonally.
- **⚠️ Long-pile/shag rugs — a specific practical restriction**: critiqued for trapping dirt/pet hair in high-traffic zones (living room, hallway, kitchen specifically named) — recommends confining shag/long-pile rugs to LOW-traffic zones only, specifically bedrooms, where they serve a genuine "soft under bare feet in the morning" function.
- **⚠️ Glass/acrylic furniture — an "eye needs anchors" critique**, distinct from the pure durability critique already logged (Round 8's mirrored-surface/acrylic critique was about wear-and-tear; this is about visual PERCEPTION): excessive transparent furniture creates a disorienting effect because the brain struggles to process a space lacking clear visual forms/supports to anchor on. Recommends using glass/acrylic pieces as limited accents paired with warmer, more solid-reading materials.
- **⚠️ Real safety anecdote — self-acknowledged restatement, not a new incident**: recounts a friend's child breaking a glass coffee table and needing stitches — but explicitly flags this as a story already told on this channel before ("я уже рассказывал на этом канале... историю про ребёнка моих знакомых"), confirming this is the SAME incident already logged from Round 7's `PiNvT_jnhbw`, not a second independent case.
- **⚠️ Faux-marble laminate/veneer furniture — corroborates the Round 8 (`NAqGs6ygykU`) faux-material critique** (fading, scratching, "gives itself away up close") — recorded as a corroborating cross-round data point. **Adds a genuinely new extension**: recommends large-format porcelain slab (**керамогранит**, quoted inline) specifically as capable of imitating not just marble but ANY natural stone, with a new application not previously logged — using large-format porcelain slab as an ACCENT WALL FEATURE in a living room, not just as a countertop/flooring material.
