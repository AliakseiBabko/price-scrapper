## Wallpaper & Paint — Practitioner Q&A

1. **⚠️ "Wipeable paint" manufacturer-claim myth**: paint marketed as
   wipeable/washable is not, in the practitioner's real experience,
   actually wipeable without visible consequence — wiping it with a
   damp sponge removes the surface sheen/gloss, leaving a visible dulled
   patch. **Leaving that residue/dulled patch uncorrected is called out
   explicitly as a common painter mistake**, not an unavoidable outcome
   of washing the wall.
2. **Wallpaper substrate prep, worked example**: for vinyl wallpaper,
   two coats of a named finishing putty (Vetonit) plus sanding plus
   priming is sufficient; some delicate wallpaper types intended for a
   paint-look finish need the same finish-putty-grade prep as an
   actual paint job, not a lighter wallpaper-grade prep.
3. **Wallpaper-hanging skill differentiator, glue open-time**: some
   wallpaper types have an adhesive open/working time of only 2-3
   minutes before over-saturation causes the sheet to stretch — pasting
   and hanging within that window is explicitly framed as something
   only an experienced, practiced installer can physically execute fast
   enough; an inexperienced person (implicitly including a DIY
   homeowner) realistically cannot. Vinyl wallpaper is comparatively
   more forgiving; thinner materials (flizelin, textile, paper) can
   show adhesive bleed-through/pattern smearing on a delicate print if
   over-wetted.
4. **⚠️ Vinyl-wallpaper door-casing cutting technique, with a real
   failure mechanism**: cut wallpaper precisely to a door-opening's own
   boundary, leaving no excess overlap — vinyl wallpaper visibly
   shrinks slightly as it dries, and any unsupported excess overhang at
   an opening will curl and peel loose as a direct result. **Leaving
   excess "to be safe" is explicitly called a common, avoidable
   mistake** — cutting precisely to the opening is correct even before
   the door casing/trim (which will cover roughly 5-6cm) is installed;
   the casing is not a substitute for a clean cut.
5. **Electrical-outlet documentation vs. wallpaper concealment**: an
   engineering/design project documents exactly where every outlet and
   switch is even once a wall is fully wallpapered over, so a live
   point is never truly "lost" behind the finish — offered partly as
   dark humor ("if it's undocumented, we'll find it by getting
   shocked") but underscoring a real practical risk of finishing over
   an undocumented electrical point without that documentation.
6. **Real friction case from a missing design project**: a described
   real project had no design/engineering project and was repeatedly
   re-corrected mid-work — including a kids'-room wallpaper choice that
   went out of production between selection and installation, forcing
   an unplanned redesign. Distinct from this store's existing
   design-project-value content in emphasizing the *finishing-trade's
   own* perspective specifically (see item 15 below for the fuller
   framing).
7. **Ceiling paint vs. wall paint are not interchangeable even at the
   same visual color** — a ceiling needs its own ceiling-specific paint
   formulation (different sheen/matte behavior and washability
   properties) rather than reusing wall paint of a matching color; a
   real worked example given uses a specific US-import ceiling paint
   selected for its washability/humidity-tolerance, suited to a kids'
   room or bathroom-adjacent space. `ASR-uncertain` on the exact brand
   name.
8. **Named premium wall-paint brand and primer dependency**: Benjamin
   Moore (Бенджамин Мур), used with its own matched primer — the
   practitioner states the primer/paint pairing is genuinely higher
   quality, to the point that a **primer-only base coat looked finished
   enough that the client mistakenly believed the wall was already
   painted** before the actual topcoat was applied — a real, concrete
   testament to primer quality rather than a marketing claim.
9. **Work-pace and scheduling discipline**: a normal professional
   workday runs to roughly 18:00-19:00; the practitioner explicitly
   avoids pushing further when tired, framing over-extension as a real
   quality risk (a rushed, fatigued installer is more likely to produce
   a bubbling/lifting wallpaper seam or a botched surface). **A realistic
   quality timeline for repainting/rewallpapering a full apartment,
   working weekdays at a normal (non-rushed) pace: about 2 months.**
10. **Self-QC walkthrough discipline**: the practitioner does repeated
    post-installation walk-throughs of her own work, specifically
    checking every wallpaper seam for lifting/bubbling and any residual
    glue, under multiple lighting conditions (including a flashlight
    check in low-light areas) before considering a room complete.
11. **Trade-specialization stance, corroborating this channel's own
    designer/foreman-coordination content**: the practitioner does not
    do plastering herself — a separate dedicated crew handles it —
    explicitly framed as "everyone should stick to their own trade"
    (an electrician, for instance, lacks both the patience and the
    fine-motor practice for finish puttying work).
12. **Design-project value specifically for the finishing trade**: with
    a design project, the practitioner already knows the exact
    wallpaper/paint article numbers, brands, and required substrate
    prep before starting — without one, she has to guess or ask the
    client for reference photos, and clients frequently can't answer
    basic material questions (vinyl vs. paper, required prep). **Real
    material-substitution risk flagged**: if a client tries to
    substitute a cheaper material than what the design project
    specifies, the resulting look/effect deviates from the originally
    designed outcome — a chain that can't be broken partway through
    once the finish selection is planned.
13. **Real client-frustration case, staying-within-one-company
    tradeoff**: a described bathroom rework needed retiling plus
    relocating outlets that had been lost/covered behind the original
    convex tile install; the client paid for the redo, but staying with
    the same company for the rework was faster/cheaper than hiring a
    separate contractor to fix it.
14. **Seam adhesive residue technique (corroborates and extends Round
    2's finding)**: seams can show a shiny glue residue at the joint —
    must be wiped first with a slightly damp cloth, then a dry cloth,
    or the residue stays visible; brand-dependent (this practitioner
    uses imported Italian/German wallpaper with its own matched
    adhesive, which she states reduces this problem's frequency).
15. **Dry-lay pre-hanging planning technique (corroborates Round 2's
    finding, extended with a numbering step)**: pre-lay wallpaper rolls
    flat on the floor to plan the cut sequence for a given wall (e.g. 6
    cut sheets from 2 rolls) — **additionally number each cut sheet in
    sequence** so a non-repeating or shaded pattern is hung in the
    correct order and consistent light-reflection direction across the
    wall, not just to catch a factory pattern defect as in the existing
    note.
