## Windows / Slopes — Developer-Window Repair & Adjustment Technique

- **Lock strike-plate (ответная часть) alignment procedure**: simulate the sash's closed position by turning the handle down *first* (this is what determines where the lock's moving part actually seats, not the sash's physical position against the frame), then press the sash to the frame and observe whether the strike plate on the frame aligns with the lock mechanism on the sash. If misaligned, loosen and slide the strike plate (mounted on the frame, adjustable) until it lines up correctly relative to the sash's true locked position — repeat for every lock point on all three sashes on the door. `confirmed`, genuinely new lock-adjustment procedure for this store, distinct from the existing sash-position/seal-pressure hex-key adjustments already recorded (`yt_qwIRfgn1Tog`).
- **Sash-to-frame overlap (притвор) consistency check**: mark the sash's overlap edge in pencil against the frame edge where the seal runs, close the sash, then open it and measure how far the marked edge overlaps the frame at multiple points around the perimeter — inconsistent overlap distances at different points indicate the sash sits in the wrong position relative to the frame (not a seal problem). Fix with the standard 4mm hex adjustment key: separate up/down and left/right adjustments at the hinge (this store's existing hex-key adjustment content, applied here specifically to *fix uneven overlap* rather than sagging).
- **Center-pressure-part (средний прижим) relocation for a two-part poorly-aligned fitting**: the part consists of a sash-side piece and a frame-side piece; if misaligned (causing draft at that point), simply unscrew and flip/reposition the *frame-side* piece to the correct mounting position — leaves old screw holes exposed, which must be cleaned/degreased and sealed with tube-type sealant before repositioning the part. `confirmed`, genuinely new fitting-relocation technique.
- **Sealant application technique for old fastener holes on a window frame**: use a white cleaning cloth (never colored, since the degreaser used is also a solvent that can transfer dye from a colored cloth onto white/unlaminated windows) and long, sweeping wipe strokes (a short, repeated dabbing motion risks the cloth momentarily sticking to the surface). Apply sealant with a finger (preferred over squeezing straight from the tube) for better control on a small area. Swap the white cloth frequently during the job so it doesn't itself transfer picked-up dirt/dye back onto the window.
- **Seal (уплотнитель) inspection and replacement decision, by seal type**: visual check specifically at the *corners* — some developer-window seals are welded at the corners during frame fabrication, and a welded corner joint is not elastic (loses the seal's core sealing property exactly where continuity matters most), causing four hard-point contact spots instead of continuous even pressure around the sash. **Decision rule by seal profile**: single-lobed seal → replace immediately (assume already inadequate); double-lobed seal → inspect condition, replace only if degraded; chamber-type seal with a small lobe → generally fine as-is, no action needed. Also check the seal's own splice/joint location — a joint positioned at the *top* of the frame is the correct/intended position (any resulting micro-draft there goes unnoticed by an occupant); the same joint at the *bottom* is a defect (draft there is felt).
- **Final-step hardware check, easy to overlook**: after adjustment work, retighten the handle's own mounting screws (they loosen with normal handle use over time) — but use a manual cross-head (Phillips) screwdriver, never a powered screwdriver/drill, because a powered driver risks stripping/over-torquing the screws to the point the handle no longer holds securely to the sash.
- **Repair-vs-replace framing, source's own stated scope**: this repair sequence is explicitly framed as a stopgap ("a couple more years") for a developer-installed window the source's own company would otherwise discard outright on its own projects, not a permanent fix or an endorsement of developer-grade windows generally.
