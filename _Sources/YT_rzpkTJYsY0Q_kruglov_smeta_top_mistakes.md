## Mistakes / Warnings — Three Named Smeta-Manipulation Categories, Plus Two Bonus Schemes

### 1. Volume understatement (занижение объёмов)

- **Wall-area understatement is harder to catch than floor-area
  understatement, because wall area is never independently documented
  anywhere** (not in utility bills, not usually in a design project
  unless the designer computed it) — floor area is easy to cross-check
  (sum of per-room laminate/tile areas against the known total m²), but
  a client has no independent reference figure for total wall area to
  catch a shortfall against. **Mitigation stated**: verify wall area
  was actually computed (by the designer or the estimator) rather than
  assumed.
- **⚠️ Electrical cable-length quantity is effectively unknowable to
  the client without a real engineering project** — the same 50 m²
  apartment's total electrical cable run could legitimately be "100 m,
  500 m, 750 m, or a kilometer" depending on routing, and this line item
  is invisible to a client scanning a smeta for a lump total. **Stated
  fix**: commission a separate electrical engineering project
  (**20,000–30,000 RUB → ≈$230–$340**) that states cable-length
  requirements by type explicitly — without one, the client has no way
  to independently verify this line. *(Extends, doesn't duplicate, this
  vault's existing РемонтХочу note that electrical rough-in should be
  priced by floor area, not cable length — that note protects against a
  different manipulation direction: this video's point is that even a
  stated cable-length figure is unverifiable without a real project.)*
- **A bathroom smeta can list gypsum plaster on wet-zone walls while
  silently omitting the waterproofing line that should accompany it** —
  a specific, checkable volume-omission variant not previously recorded
  in this store in this exact form.

### 2. Missing work entirely (отсутствие работ)

- **⚠️ Concealed-mount ("scrytogo montazha") interior doors leave a
  real, priceable touch-up-work gap frequently missing from smete**:
  after the door frame/leaf is installed, painters must still finish
  the wall-to-frame junction on both sides of the wall and repaint both
  door faces — **stated per-door touch-up cost: 10,000–15,000 RUB
  (≈$120–$170)** if it has to be added as a change order rather than
  priced up front.
- **A "baseboard for painting" (плинтус под покраску) line item may
  cover installation only, with the paint pass itself as a separate,
  unstated line** — check whether "install" implies "and paint" or not.
- **⚠️ A hidden, structurally-linked upsell trap named with a concrete
  worked price gap**: a client independently purchases a basic
  hygienic-shower spray unit (≈2,000 RUB, ≈$20) not realizing their own
  smeta only priced a *surface-mounted* install; the unit turns out to
  require **concealed/built-in installation (≈8,000 RUB, ≈$90) — a 4×
  cost jump discovered only once the fixture arrives on-site**. The same
  omission pattern applies to manifold-node components (fine-particle
  filters, leak-protection systems, water heaters) that are frequently
  left out of a smeta's plumbing section even though clients commonly
  buy them separately, assuming the smeta already covers their install.
- **Best-practice mitigation stated directly**: sending only a design
  project is not sufficient for an accurate smeta — the ideal process is
  design project + photo/video of the actual site, sent in advance, plus
  an in-person site visit to clarify anything the design project doesn't
  specify. A smeta produced this way is checkable line-by-line against
  what was actually discussed and agreed (e.g. tile vs. paint-ready wall
  reveals) — an item appearing in the smeta that was never discussed
  (e.g. PVC reveal panels swapped in for agreed tile) is itself a red
  flag worth catching before signing.

### 3. Ambiguous/"slippery" wording (скользкие формулировки) — named as the currently most common technique, "used by every second contractor"

- **⚠️ Screed-thickness banding trap**: a line item reading "floor
  screed up to 5 cm" with no stated ceramzite-backfill or thicker-tier
  price leaves the door open to reclassify an actual 5cm+ pour as a
  different, pricier line item, or add a separate ceramzite-backfill
  charge (its own sub-steps: install, "milk" [cement-slurry] pour, more)
  as an unplanned extra. **Mitigation**: get quoted rates for multiple
  thickness bands (up to 5cm / up to 10cm / above) before work starts,
  once your own approximate screed depth is known.
- **⚠️ Plaster-thickness banding trap, same mechanism**: "gypsum
  plaster up to 1cm / up to 2cm" with no stated rate for 3cm, 4cm, or a
  reinforcement-mesh variant — ask for rates above the stated band and
  for any reinforcement scenario up front.
- **⚠️ Ceramic-vs-porcelain floor-tile substitution trap, with a
  concrete worked price example**: a smeta line item reading
  "ceramic tile floor covering" ("керамическая плитка") is misleading
  because **~100% of floor tile purchased is actually porcelain
  (керамогранит), not ceramic — a true ceramic floor tile essentially
  doesn't exist as a product** — so once the client's own purchased
  porcelain tile arrives, the crew can claim it doesn't match the
  "ceramic" line item and demand the (much higher) porcelain rate.
  **Worked example**: base ceramic-tile line stated at 2,000 RUB/m²
  (≈$20/m²); the crew's porcelain up-charge demand started at 4× (8,000
  RUB/m², ≈$90/m²) and was talked down to 5,000 RUB/m² (≈$60/m²) — still
  a real, avoidable cost jump. **Mitigation**: get the porcelain
  installation rate quoted explicitly up front, since virtually every
  real floor-tile purchase will be porcelain regardless of the smeta's
  wording.
- **⚠️ Tile-size banding trap**: "porcelain tile installation, long
  side up to 60cm" is ambiguous about whether a 60×60cm tile itself
  falls inside or outside that band, and gives no rate for a 60×120cm or
  120×120cm tile a client might actually order against a design project
  calling for a larger format. **Mitigation, this company's own stated
  practice**: state explicit inclusive size *ranges* with named worked
  examples (e.g. "60–120cm inclusive: 60×60, 80×60...") attached to the
  smeta line, not a single ambiguous size cutoff.
- **⚠️ Flooring-installation-method ambiguity, a genuinely new
  three-tier mechanism not previously recorded in this store in this
  form**: the same designer-specified material (e.g. engineered/parquet
  board) can be installed three different ways at three different
  prices — floating on underlay (**≈1,000 RUB/m² ≈$10/m²**), glued
  directly (**≈2,000 RUB/m² ≈$20/m²**), or glued to a plywood
  underlayment requiring its own plywood-install-and-sanding pass
  (**≈3,000 RUB/m² ≈$30/m²**) — and a design project frequently doesn't
  specify which method it assumes. *(This is a distinct axis from this
  vault's existing installation-*pattern* caution — straight/diagonal/
  herringbone — already documented in `Budgeting_Guide.md`; this is
  installation *method*, not *pattern*, and both should be confirmed
  independently before signing.)*
- **A named three-way baseboard-terminology ambiguity, restated
  briefly**: micro-baseboard, shadow-gap ("теневой") baseboard, and
  fully concealed-mount baseboard are three distinct products at three
  distinct price points — a design project or smeta naming one term
  loosely could mean any of the three; confirm which is actually meant.
  *(Corroborates, doesn't extend, this vault's existing "micro-baseboard
  as a cheaper approximation of concealed" note and the a-e5f7yQDRY
  baseboard-comparison content from Round 7 — recorded as reinforcement,
  not double-counted as new.)*

### Bonus scheme A — ⚠️ rough-work-inflated / finish-work-deflated bait-and-switch, with a named sabotage mechanism

A distinct, more granular mechanism than this vault's existing general
"lowball-then-inflate" schemes (Petrishin-Stroi, `dfXZ66EcGQQ`): a
competing smeta's **total** can look only modestly cheaper (worked
example: 800,000 RUB vs. this company's own 1,000,000 RUB quote) while
its **internal stage split is inverted** — rough-stage work overpriced
by roughly the same amount the finish stage is underpriced (worked
example: 600,000/200,000 vs. a "real" 500,000/500,000 split). Once the
client signs at the lower headline total and pays through the rough
stage, **the crew deliberately sabotages workmanship during finish work
specifically to get fired** (the video's own example: intentionally
crooked tile-laying) — because the underpriced finish-stage rate (here,
stated as low as 1,000-1,500 RUB for tile installation, a rate "no real
tiler will work for") isn't profitable to actually deliver. Once the
client fires them expecting to find a replacement for the "remaining"
200,000 RUB, **no contractor will pick up someone else's abandoned
finish-stage object at that price**, since it's actually worth closer to
500,000 RUB — leaving the client stuck mid-project. **General
underlying reason stated**: a construction company's real profit margin
concentrates in the rough-work stage (when the client is freshest,
best-funded, and most motivated); finish-stage work is the least
profitable, most defect-prone, latest-discovered stage, so a company
racing toward an unprofitable finish-stage price has a direct incentive
to never actually deliver it. **Practical framing**: the single most
important vetting criterion is finding a contractor who will complete a
project start-to-finish, not simply the lowest headline total — because
once a project is abandoned mid-finish, no one takes over the remaining
work at the price implied by the original smeta.

### Bonus scheme B — ⚠️ fake pre-summed "discount" psychological trick

A named, previously-unrecorded psychological pattern: a smeta shows a
room's line items (e.g. 5,000 + 5,000 + 20,000 RUB) already summed to
30,000 RUB, then displays "price without discount: 40,000 RUB, with
discount: 30,000 RUB" — even though **30,000 RUB is simply the correct
arithmetic sum of the line items shown, with no real discount applied at
all**. The stated mechanism: a client checks one or two individual line
prices against a competitor's, finds them comparable, assumes the
displayed "discount" is a genuine saving layered on top, and doesn't
re-sum the full line-item list to catch that the "discounted" total is
just the honest total relabeled.

### Bonus scheme C — ⚠️ discount-withheld-until-final-payment-act contract trap

A distinct contract-structure trick (not a smeta-wording trick): a
company advertises a large discount (worked example: 20% off a
1,000,000 RUB total, landing at 800,000 RUB) but the **contract clause
defers the entire discount to the final payment act** — every earlier
act (1st, 2nd, 3rd...) is invoiced and paid at full, undiscounted price,
with the *cumulative* discount subtracted only from the last act.
**Stated consequence, tying directly into Bonus Scheme A's incentive
structure**: a company already incentivized to avoid finishing a project
(because rough-stage margin is what it actually wants) has every reason
never to reach that final act — the client effectively never collects
the advertised discount, since the relationship typically ends before
the last payment milestone. **Mitigation implied**: read the discount
clause's payment-timing structure carefully, not just the headline
percentage.
