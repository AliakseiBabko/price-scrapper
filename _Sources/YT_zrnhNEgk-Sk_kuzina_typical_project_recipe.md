## Design Concept — the governing claim

- **⚠️ "В типовом проекте стиля как такового нет."** There is only *slightly more classical* or *slightly more contemporary*, and that exhausts the style conversation. Her jab at course curricula follows: **if you were going to study styles on a design course, that knowledge will not be needed here.**
- **⚠️ The same treatment applies identically to living room, kitchen and bedroom.** Bathrooms excluded. One recipe, three rooms.
- **Her closing concession, which keeps this honest**: *"типовой проект не значит плохой"* — the designer producing it is not a bad designer, they are **serving the request the majority actually has.** She then reopens the question: she said there is no style, but a style *is* a set of shared traits, and these interiors share a great many — so what should it be called? Left to the comments.

## Design Concept — the palette recipe

- **Kitchen fronts: always matte, plain, enamel or foil-wrapped**, always the same colours — **cream, light grey, light beige.**
- **Living-room sofa: velvet, light-brown / beige / greyish.** Bedroom bed: the same, light grey / brownish / beige velvet.
- **⚠️ Living-room walls are cream and only cream.** Bedroom walls may be cream or beige. **Coloured walls are not permitted in the living room.**
- **Rug: grey, greyish, beige-ish, light brown. Plain only — ornament is forbidden in the typical project.** At an absolute stretch, abstract streaks or a distressed effect; at a stretch, a hint of blue.
- **⚠️ The brightest object in the scheme is the dining chairs** — emerald green, blue, dusty blue, dusty pink. If they are grey or beige instead, then nothing in the room is the bright piece.
- If sofa and chairs share a space, **cushions on the sofa pick up the chair colour.** The sofa itself is occasionally coloured — usually pale blue, sometimes a barely-readable dusty pink or dusty green.
- **⚠️ Ceiling: always flat and white.** In the classical variant, a very simple laconic white cornice around the perimeter. **No coloured ceilings and no level changes** — she reports those as read as bad taste in this idiom.
- **Bedroom exception: the headboard wall may be a strong colour** — dusty blue, dusty green or dusty pink. If that is too much, put the colour in part of the bedspread or the throw instead.

## Lighting — the recipe

- **General light is "technical" light**: recessed or surface-mounted spots. **If the body is visible it is white.** Tracks are always narrow and almost always black; white at a stretch.
- **Pendants hang over the dining table or bar counter**, centre of the living zone, more rarely centre of the bedroom. **⚠️ Her deadpan catalogue of the permitted forms: balls, sticks, balls-on-sticks, and rings.** With extra effort, Vault-type fittings.
- **Wall lights over the sofa or in the bedroom: balls, sticks, sticks-with-balls.** Bedside pendants over nightstands: sticks, balls, sticks-on-balls, or rings.
- **⚠️ The joke is a real observation about the market**: the entire mainstream decorative-lighting vocabulary reduces to two primitives and their combinations. Useful as a *negative* specification — if you want the flat not to read generic, the fitting geometry is where it shows first and cheapest.

## Materials / Surfaces — the recipe

- **Kitchen floor: marble-effect tile, wood-effect tile, or wood-effect laminate.** If marble-effect porcelain, **the same material usually goes on the backsplash**, and it is always light — near-white with grey or golden-beige veining. She names Calacatta specifically, twice.
- **Other zones: mid or mid-light floors, usually golden beige.** Laminate, quartz-vinyl or parquet — **"внешний вид особо сильно на рендерах не отличается, всё зависит от бюджета."** **⚠️ That is a budgeting fact worth extracting on its own: on a render the three flooring tiers are near-indistinguishable, so the render cannot justify the upgrade.** Dark floors are rare.
- **⚠️ No wallpaper in the typical project — painted walls only.**

## Design Concept — the empty-wall method (the core of the recipe)

Identical for every room. Worked through on the wall behind the sofa, which is large and highly visible; plain cream paint alone reads boring, so:

- **More classical variant: rectangles of moulding, trade term «зеркала» ("mirrors").** The scheme: **a wide one in the centre, narrower ones either side**; if the wall is long, a further pair of narrow ones outboard. **A picture hangs in the centre of the wide rectangle; wall lights in the centres of the narrow ones. Wall and moulding are painted the same colour.**
  - At a stretch the central rectangle can be split in two with the picture hung across the mouldings — **but she flags that this already risks leaving the typical envelope.**
- **More contemporary variant: mouldings placed asymmetrically**, or replaced by **slats («рейки») or polyurethane wall panels**, running floor to ceiling and **occupying either a third or a half of the sofa's width.** Picture, or picture plus wall light, on the remainder.
- **Frescoes are an alternative to a picture** and combine with both mouldings and slats — **⚠️ but must have no bright colours and no pronounced subject.** Usually something botanical, grey or beige on a light ground.
- **The TV wall opposite is treated exactly the same way.** In more expensive projects, **natural stone or large-format marble-effect porcelain** is added there — **veining sparse, grey or golden.**

## Design Concept — kitchen specifics

- **⚠️ The ideal layout is a single linear run that begins and ends with a tall column — the "кухня-рамка" (framed kitchen).**
- Classical variant: a small profile on the fronts. Contemporary: **flat fronts, no visible handles.** Either way — **all fronts the same shape, all matte, all solid (no glazing).**
- **Backsplash: porcelain or quartz, marble-effect, pale veining, grey or golden. The worktop should resemble the backsplash** — if the backsplash is quartz, the worktop probably is too.
- **⚠️ A free-standing extractor is only ever cylindrical** — either fully integrated and invisible, or a tube.
- **Metal finish is set by the style axis: classical → gold (pendant, table base, chair legs, visible extractor); contemporary → black.** A single rule covering five items — cross-check against the metal-finish source `LAuZhM9tJWA`.
- **Small kitchens get round tables more often than rectangular**, with a light, thin worktop. **Chairs: metal legs with a visible frame, or wooden legs tapering downward; soft backs that wrap around the sitter.**

## Design Concept — living room and bedroom specifics

- **⚠️ One sofa model appears in every typical project: the Lawson.** High legs, removable seat and back cushions, **and the seat is always divided into several cushions.** The style axis lives entirely in the arms — **more rectangular/square for contemporary, more rounded for classical.**
- **Only two living-room layouts exist**: sofa against a wall, or **sofa in the middle of the room because the kitchen is combined with the living room.** In the second case it is the same Lawson with an added chaise, **its back dividing kitchen from living zone, with the dining table or bar counter set along that back.**
- **Coffee table: usually round**; base in gold or brass for classical, black for contemporary.
- **TV unit: a low elongated rectangle with flat or slatted fronts**, optionally joined to a floor-to-ceiling flat-fronted cabinet. **A bedroom TV zone looks identical, except the cabinet may be a dressing table instead.**
- **Bed: a simple rectangular upholstered headboard.** The headboard wall is the main wall and gets the moulding-or-slat treatment, **at a 1:2 ratio or symmetric.** Upholstered wall panels are allowed there **only as vertical stripes**; for especially lavish cases the pattern may become herringbone, with mirrored inserts possible at the sides.
- **Wardrobe fronts follow the kitchen-front rule exactly** — thin perimeter framing for classical, plain flat for contemporary. **Handles either recessed or long surface-mounted, most often gold.**
