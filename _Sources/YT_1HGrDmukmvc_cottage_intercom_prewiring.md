## Durable Facts

- **Cottage intercom layout differs structurally from an apartment's**: in an apartment, the indoor unit and the outdoor call panel are typically separated only by a single wall/door, so cabling is trivial. In a cottage, the indoor unit is inside the house while the call panel sits at the property's outer gate/fence — a much longer cable run that must be planned during design, not improvised later. `unverified`, single-account, but describes a structural/geometric fact rather than an opinion.
- **Electromagnetic door/gate lock as an intercom option**: lets the owner remotely unlock the gate/door from the intercom panel without going outside (e.g., in rain) — described as a common added feature on cottage intercom systems. `unverified`, single-account.
- **Underground/exterior cable sealing**: any cable run outdoors (e.g., to a gate call panel) must have its ends sealed/waterproofed, or water ingress causes copper conductor oxidation. `unverified`, single-account, but a plausible general electrical-installation principle.

- **Recommendation**: the intercom system (model, whether it includes an electric lock, call-panel type, backlighting) should be decided and its cabling laid at the earliest design/rough-in stage of a cottage project — before the shell and driveway/paving are finished — even for owners not using a formal project/designer. `unverified`, single-account, but a reasonable planning heuristic.
