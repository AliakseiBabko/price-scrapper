## Mistakes / Warnings — Stretch-Ceiling Acceptance Checklist

- **⚠️ Membrane surface check**: no holes, punctures, or worn/thinned patches anywhere on the film; no creases/folds; visually flat overall.
- **⚠️ Edge trim/baguette tape check**: the trim strip running the ceiling's perimeter should look visually straight with no waviness, and must show no separation gap from either the wall or ceiling surface along its run.
- **⚠️ Corner tightness, a specific qualitative threshold**: corners must be tight/dense with no visible gap — the speaker's own bar is "no gap you could fit half a finger into," used as a practical, easy-to-apply field test rather than a millimeter spec.
- **⚠️ Recessed-downlight thermal-ring gap must be *consistent* across all fixtures, not merely present**: a small gap between the ceiling film and a downlight's thermal collar is normal and expected — the defect to check for is *inconsistency* (e.g. ~1mm at one fixture vs. ~3mm at another in the same room), which reads as sloppy installation even though each individual gap might be "within spec" alone.
- **Chandelier/light-fixture mounting check**: the fixture base must sit flush against the ceiling with no skew and no gap at its base — checked visually and by hand.
- **Final functional check**: cycle every switch and confirm each controls the intended fixture/zone as originally planned, and that everything actually turns on and off.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Post-Acceptance Liability Gap and This Company's Fix

- **⚠️ Named real-world failure mode: damage discovered only after the installers have already been paid and left**: once the client starts cleaning up post-install, they commonly discover wall paint smudges/stains and chipped tile that went unnoticed during the acceptance walkthrough (which focuses attention on the ceiling itself, not the surrounding room). At that point the installer is already paid and gone.
- **⚠️ Asymmetric provability by damage type, a concrete practical distinction**: a reputable installer/company that values its reputation will typically repaint a damaged wall section or compensate for repainting costs when asked after the fact. **Chipped tile is functionally unprovable after the fact** — there's no way to demonstrate the chip happened during this specific installation (e.g., from a ladder impact) rather than pre-existing or from a different trade, so the cost of tile repair/replacement typically falls on the owner regardless of who actually caused it.
- **⚠️ This company's own standing fix: a signed pre-work room-condition act, executed before installers touch anything**: before starting, the installer signs a document stating the specific condition of the room as found — walls intact, floors intact, ceiling surface (if applicable) intact, windows and doors intact, and no visible chemical/mechanical damage on any surface. **Two stated mechanisms this produces, not just paperwork for its own sake**: (1) it forces the installer to actually inspect the room's pre-existing condition carefully before starting (in their own interest, so they aren't later blamed for damage they didn't cause), which surfaces any pre-existing defects the installer photographs/documents at that point — giving the owner independent knowledge of pre-existing damage they can raise with their *original* renovation contractor separately; (2) once signed, all later damage claims are attributed to the work just performed, with no dispute period afterward — this cleanly reallocates the burden of proof from "owner proves the installer caused it" to "the act already establishes the room was undamaged beforehand."
- **⚠️ The document also functions as a screening filter for installer quality/integrity**: a competent, confident installer who works cleanly has no reason to avoid signing such a document or spending the few minutes needed to inspect the room first; refusal or reluctance to sign is offered as a practical, low-cost signal for filtering out installers unwilling to accept accountability for their own work — independent of whatever quote or references they've provided elsewhere.
- **Practical adoption note**: the speaker acknowledges most individual clients won't bother drafting such a document themselves, so the company published a ready-to-use template as a free download (linked from the video's own description/social post) rather than leaving it as an abstract recommendation.
