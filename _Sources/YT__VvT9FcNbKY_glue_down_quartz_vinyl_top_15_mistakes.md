## Flooring — Durable Facts & Rules (specifically: glue-down/non-locking quartz-vinyl installation)

Zemskov's stated "top mistakes," in the order given:

1. **Never install immediately after delivery — let the product acclimate in the target room a minimum of 2 days, ideally 3, before laying.** His stated mechanism: quartz vinyl is often delivered in hot or cold weather; if laid before its temperature normalizes to the room, it will, per Zemskov, guaranteed either shrink (opening gaps between planks) or expand (planks buckling against each other, "standing up like a little house") — and this shows up days *after* installation, once baseboards are already on, forcing baseboard removal and prying up the glued vinyl (with real damage risk to both the vinyl and the substrate, per his account, since quality adhesive won't release cleanly).
2. **Acclimate at the room's actual future operating temperature, not just "indoors."** Zemskov's cited real failure case: material acclimated 3 days at a site running ~16°C (weak heating) will still buckle once the occupied apartment is later heated to a normal 20-23°C, because glued (not free-floating) planks have nowhere to expand once fixed down.
3. **Store/stage delivery packs flat — never on edge, and never standing upright.** Per Zemskov, storing on edge guaranteed bows the planks (lengthwise or widthwise "boat" warping), causing edges or centers to lift and pull away from the adhesive during installation — Zemskov states he's seen real cases (including installers stacking packs on edge against a wall specifically to save floor space) that cost real money to fix.
4. **Never glue quartz vinyl to a gypsum-based self-leveling floor.** Per Zemskov this applies to any glued flooring (including solid wood) on a gypsum self-leveling substrate — only a floating installation is permitted there; glued flooring will pull away taking a layer of the gypsum substrate with it.
5. **Fully clean the substrate of paint/plaster blobs and debris before gluing** — quartz vinyl is thin and soft enough (in its glue-down form specifically) to telegraph every substrate defect once installed and lit by window light, even defects invisible before laying.
6. **Prime the substrate even if it looks dust-free** — Zemskov's claim: no vacuum, however powerful, fully removes bonding-relevant dust from a substrate; only a deep-penetrating primer does, and glue-down quartz vinyl typically needs **two coats** of a two-component polymer primer specifically. Skipping this causes the adhesive layer (and vinyl with it) to peel away from the substrate later.
7. **Verify substrate moisture ≤5% before installing**, checked after the primer has soaked in and dried — higher moisture prevents the adhesive from polymerizing correctly and the vinyl won't bond.
8. **Verify substrate flatness with a 2-meter straightedge before installing — max deviation 2mm over the full length.** Per Zemskov, a 3mm deviation will be visibly noticeable once glue-down quartz vinyl is installed over it; calibrated wedge shims (used under the straightedge) are recommended over eyeballing the gap.
9. **Install at ≤21°C ambient, ideally around 18°C (slightly below typical room temperature) — never above 24°C.** Zemskov's stated mechanism: installing warm/soft material at a comfortable-feeling high temperature produces guaranteed perimeter gaps at every plank once the room later cools to its normal operating ~20-21°C and cold air entering (e.g. from an opened window) contracts the material, since a glued plank (unlike a floating floor) has no expansion room.
10. **Determine laying direction from the design project's floor-plan arrows** — Zemskov references his own separate video (already captured in this batch from `yt_PwJsksBs4Ek`/`yt_lOMxNoyW_NE`: light-direction default, traffic-direction in corridors, diagonal rarely justified) as the authority to consult before starting.
11. **Determine the room's "open" wall (visible, no furniture in front of it) vs. "closed" wall (will be blocked by furniture) before laying, and orient full-length planks toward the open wall, hiding narrow cut pieces along the closed wall — with a stated minimum cut-piece width of 50mm.** Zemskov states attempting to balance/split narrow cut pieces symmetrically (as done with ceramic tile per his other sourced content) is usually impractical here — excessive material waste, and even a precisely-cut narrow plank at this scale still reads as visually poor.
12. **Glue-down installation sequence, given step by step**: strike a centerline down the room from the door opening to the opposite wall; lay the first 3 rows of planks along that centerline toward the window; work outward left and right from there, walking only on already-laid rows (never directly on wet glue). **Position the centerline so the open-wall side gets full, uncut rows** — if the last row toward that wall would end up too narrow, trim the *first* row near the centerline instead to widen the last row, never leave a narrow strip unresolved.
13. **Wait roughly 1 hour after spreading the adhesive before laying planks onto it — check by touch (fingers no longer tacky), not by trusting the package's stated open time alone**, since humidity, temperature, and even direct sun exposure change actual working time. Laying too early (onto fresh glue) causes planks to slide and leaves visible gaps; laying too late (onto glue past its working life) means the planks simply won't bond — Zemskov notes a small, localized reactivation trick (heat gun on a small dried patch) works only for a small area, never a whole room, and never double-layer adhesive over dried glue.
14. **Spread adhesive evenly** — an uneven, locally-thick layer won't cure/bond correctly at the thick spots, letting those specific planks slide even after the rest of the room has set properly.
15. **Shuffle planks from multiple boxes/batches before laying, don't lay box-by-box in sequence.** Per Zemskov, quartz vinyl is a printed/laminated product whose color can vary noticeably batch to batch (even within one production lot) — laying strictly by box produces visible ~1-1.5m² color-blotch patches; mixing planks from several opened boxes at once, laid in a deliberately randomized order, is what produces an intentional-looking varied pattern instead.
16. **Inspect every plank on both faces and all four edges/corners before gluing it down** — a dusty back face won't bond (and will detach later); shipping damage commonly shows up as crushed/damaged corners from packs being dropped, and this only becomes visible if checked before, not after, installation.
17. **Never try to slide a glue-down plank into final position after it's been pressed down — it bonds instantly and immovably.** Zemskov's framing: this isn't like a click-lock (замковый) product where a misplacement can simply be lifted and corrected; position and sight the plank precisely over its intended spot *before* lowering and pressing it — "measure many times, glue once."
18. **Lay each 3-row set in a specific internal sequence, not straight across**: place the longest (first) plank on the centerline; cut the next plank to 1/3 length for the row beside it; cut the plank after that to 1/2 length for the third row — this offsets the transverse joints correctly between rows (the same joint-staggering logic already captured from `yt_lOMxNoyW_NE`, applied here specifically to quartz vinyl). **Within a 3-row block, lay row 2 before row 3** (row 2 sits closer/more reachable) rather than working strictly row 1 → row 2 → row 3 in physical order — Zemskov also warns against laying one full single row across the whole room before starting the next, since standing on one already-laid single-plank-wide row risks losing balance and falling onto wet glue.
19. **Tape down the first few laid planks with paper/masking tape** before continuing — otherwise the act of walking on them while laying subsequent rows shifts them, opening gaps that can no longer be closed once the adhesive sets.
20. **Never set tools directly on freshly laid quartz vinyl.** Utility knives and squares dropped or dragged across it leave permanent, unremovable scratches — Zemskov recommends deliberately keeping the product's own shipping packaging to lay down as a protected surface for tools.
21. **Glue-down (non-locking) quartz vinyl has near-zero installation waste, unlike click-lock quartz vinyl — but only if offcuts are saved and reused, never thrown away.** Per Zemskov, a glue-down offcut can be flipped/rotated up to 180° and reused anywhere in the room (since there's no locking profile constraining orientation, unlike click-lock product, where a cut piece's cut edge loses its locking tab and becomes unusable in some positions) — the stated technique: start each new set of 3 rows using the offcuts saved from the previous set's cuts, rather than fresh full planks, until the offcut is exhausted. **Practical consequence flagged explicitly**: a retailer typically sells quartz vinyl calculated by exact room area with no waste buffer (unlike products with typical cutting loss); throwing away offcuts under that assumption means running short of full planks near the end despite having a pile of unused offcut scrap that could have covered it.
22. **Do not walk on or otherwise use the floor until the adhesive is fully cured — minimum 48 hours after installation.**
