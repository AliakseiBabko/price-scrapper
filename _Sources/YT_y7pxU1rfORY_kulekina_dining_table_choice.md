## ⚠️⚠️ 1. THE FINDING — the base, not the top, is what makes a table uncomfortable

> ***«На эту деталь не часто обращают внимание при выборе и потом удивляются отсутствию комфорта.»***

- **Especially acute on ROUND and OVAL tables**, where instead of four corner legs there are *«сложные конструкции»* — pedestals, splayed feet, cross braces.
- **The requirement: legs must not foul a seated person — *«не должны упираться в колени или другие части тела»*.**
- **⚠️ And a two-sided constraint on the base itself: not excessively massive, but not fragile either** — *«важно, чтобы стол не расшатывался со временем»*.
- **⚠️⚠️ Her test, and it is the actionable part: sit at the actual table before buying, FROM SEVERAL DIFFERENT SIDES** — *«лучше всего посмотреть его вживую, посидеть за таким столом, причём с разных сторон»*.

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ New to the vault, and it is the kind of defect that is invisible in a catalogue photograph and unfixable after delivery.** It also explains a failure the vault's clearance figures cannot predict: **a table can satisfy every per-person and circulation dimension and still be unusable at two of its seats**, because the obstruction is under the top rather than around it. **The "sit at it from several sides" instruction is the whole finding — one side is not a test.**

## ⚠️ 2. Seat count and shape — corroboration on one, conflict on the other

- **⚠️ Seat count: *«всегда берите стол с запасом посадочных мест на один больше»*** — it barely changes the dimensions but covers a guest. **Independently matches [[_Sources/YT_xt3B2aC3S7Q_faynblat_kitchen_table_choice|Файнблат's]] "household plus one or two" in the same batch. Two designers, same rule, unrelated sources.**
- **⚠️⚠️ Shape: she says round and oval tables seat MORE people than square and rectangular ones, and calls round *«довольно универсальное и симпатичное решение, особенно для компактных кухонь или столовых»*.**

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ This reads against Файнблат in the same batch, who makes SQUARE the small-kitchen answer and warns that a round table cannot be pushed into a corner and that not every room can take one.**
>
> **The reconciliation, and the page should carry it rather than pick a winner: they are optimising different quantities.** Кулекина counts **seats per unit of tabletop** — true, a round top has no wasted corners and admits an extra chair. Файнблат counts **floor area the table permanently locks up** — also true, because a round table must stand clear of walls. **In a room with space to centre a table, Кулекина is right; in a room where the table must park against a wall when not in use, Файнблат is.** ⚠️ **The deciding question is not the shape but whether the table ever needs to move.**

## ⚠️ 3. Tabletops — a practicality ranking, and one specific failure

**Her recommended set for daily household use: quality ЛДСП, glass, natural or artificial stone, керамогранит.**

- **⚠️⚠️ The failure mode she names: LOW-quality ЛДСП swells when the surface is damaged and then gets wet — and she notes a textile tablecloth is not protection, because *«она может промокнуть, и столешница испортится»*.** **→ The non-obvious half: the cloth people buy to protect the table is the mechanism that holds water against it.**
- **⚠️ Массив and natural-veneer tops require gentler treatment and are *«зачастую ставятся в зону столовых, где их используют не каждый день»*.** **→ A placement rule rather than a quality claim: solid wood is a dining-room table, not a kitchen one.** ⚠️ **Note this is more restrictive than [[_Sources/YT_xt3B2aC3S7Q_faynblat_kitchen_table_choice|Файнблат]], who puts массив in the "lasts essentially forever" tier with maintenance. Consistent if you read Файнблат's maintenance requirement as exactly the reason Кулекина moves it out of daily use.**
- **⚠️ Glossy glass shows fingerprints — choose matt if that will annoy you.** Small, specific, and the kind of thing discovered after purchase.

## ⚠️⚠️ 4. Extending tables — and the planning error that makes them useless

She uses extending tables often, *«особенно в малогабаритных квартирах»*, and notes the market now has elegant ones rather than only utilitarian ones.

- **⚠️⚠️ Rectangular and square tables extend to a LARGER area than round and oval ones do.** **→ Directly relevant to §2: the shape that seats more when closed extends less when open. The two shape arguments invert depending on which state you optimise.**
- **⚠️⚠️ THE PLANNING ERROR: *«стоит задуматься и о том, чтобы в интерьере хватило места для стола в разложенном состоянии»*. Her practice is to put BOTH footprints on the plan — *«при разработке планировок мы всегда указываем габариты стола и в сложенном, и в разложенном виде, и это позволяет избежать ошибок»*.**

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ This is a drawing-discipline instruction and it belongs in the vault's own planning method, not only on the furniture page.** An extending table has **two footprints**, and a plan that shows one of them is a plan that has not checked the case the table exists for. **Pair it with Файнблат's clearance figures: the open-state footprint is the one that must satisfy them.**
- **⚠️ Inspect the extension mechanism from slightly BELOW eye level** — on budget models *«конструкция раздвижной системы зачастую видна с торца, и это выглядит некрасиво»*. A specific, checkable showroom test.

## 5. Colour — recorded as her aesthetic position

The dining table as the room's accent (a red table in a black-and-white dining room; a saturated yellow one against blue walls), with the note that a tablecloth achieves the same thing reversibly — so **if you do not use a cloth daily, the table's own colour is the decision.** ⚠️ **Attributed opinion, not a finding.**
