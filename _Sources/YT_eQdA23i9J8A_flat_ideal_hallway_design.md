## Quantities / Measurements — his working dimensions

- **⚠️ Hallway area, from their experience: 4 to 8–10 m².** The more regular its shape, the more logical the furniture arrangement and the more usable the result.
- **⚠️ Built-in clothes wardrobe: 600 mm depth minimum** — so hangers with bulky outerwear fit. **The failure at less: either the facades do not close fully, or you hang garments sideways — and then a jacket sits behind a row of others, so "тебе нужно всё остальное снять," you cannot see the full range, and "это просто дико неудобно."**
- **⚠️ Opening width: optimally ~1,200 mm with no intermediate post (стойка).** **But the carcass must be engineered for it — a 1,200 mm span with heavy coats hanging on a rail must not sag.** **If a post is needed it is not a disaster — you simply lose some internal space.** Not always technically avoidable, but often it is.
- **⚠️ Shoe storage does NOT need 600 mm — 350 to 420 mm is the range.** A separate floor-to-ceiling cabinet with **inclined shelves**, used only for shoes.
  - **⚠️ Why inclined, and the reasoning is the extractable part: a flat shelf holding a standard men's size-42 shoe consumes more depth. Tilting it reduces the cabinet's depth AND improves visibility and retrieval — "ты всё под наклоном видишь… и брать их удобнее."**
- **⚠️ Keep shoes and clothes in separate cabinets where space allows, because shoes are dirtier and can carry odour.** **This bears directly on the open-versus-closed shoe-storage disagreement already recorded on `01_Entrance/analysis/Storage.md` — his position is closed and separated.**

## Furniture / Built-ins — what the hall must contain

- **⚠️ A console surface**, for the things you arrive holding — scarf, hat, gloves, keys. **"Если у тебя будет всё зашито шкафами, естественно, это будет неудобно."**
- **⚠️ A place to sit to put shoes on and take them off — and it must match the overall design.** His criticism is specific: people install built-in wardrobes and then buy a random green-upholstered pouf from a marketplace, "он просто там не смотрится." **"Общая концептуальная картина должна при этом сохраняться обязательно."**
- **⚠️ In his own hall the seating is wall-hung (подвесная), for two stated reasons: cleaning is easier underneath, and guests' shoes can go under it without using your own storage.**
- **⚠️ A «дежурная вешалка» — a duty rail, and this is a detail worth keeping: a metal bar standing off the wall with the hooks on its BACK.** You hang a coat without opening the wardrobe; the function is preserved; **"но при этом это выглядит так, что у тебя нету никаких крючков."**

## Surfaces / Finishes — flooring and furniture materials for a hall specifically

- **⚠️ Flooring: porcelain is optimal, because the hall is "агрессивная среда."** **Laminate risks swelling** with wet or dirty footwear and frequent wet cleaning — "вероятность повышается." **Quartz-vinyl is acceptable, being more wear-resistant than laminate.** **Corroborates the flooring-by-room reasoning already in this vault.**
- **⚠️ Furniture materials — and his reasoning is hall-specific rather than general: avoid painted horizontal surfaces, because you will throw keys onto them.** Scratches first, then chips over time, then restoration or replacement. **Plus enamel is dearer than ЛДСП.**
- **⚠️ So: ЛДСП is the most rational choice here; veneer if you want natural, though "он тоже всё равно поцарапается" and is "средним по надёжности"; and the most sensible options overall are ЛДСП or stone.** **This is a room-specific inversion of the material ladder on `03_Kitchen/analysis/Furniture_Facade_Materials.md` — in a hall, the cheaper material is also the more appropriate one.**

## Furniture / Built-ins — the mirror, with a mechanical argument

- **A mirror is mandatory, preferably full-length, with the lighting arranged so you can actually see your reflection.** **The common error: no mirror in the hall at all, or one located outside it — so you dress, put outdoor shoes on, and then walk through part of your own flat to look at yourself.**
- **⚠️ A mirror mounted ON the wardrobe: "неуместно… в большинстве случаев это выглядит стрёмно."**
- **⚠️ A mirror on the inside of a door leaf (the old-wardrobe trick) is rejected on mechanical grounds, not only aesthetic ones: "оно вес имеет. У тебя получается одна створка весит больше, чем все остальные. Рано или поздно это приведёт к тому, что у тебя будут зазоры, у тебя ушатается фурнитура на нём за счёт тяжести."** **An asymmetric-load failure path that this vault's mirrored-door content did not carry.**
- **⚠️ Best: a separate mirror, either glued to the wall or set in an aluminium profile fixed to the wall.** **Floor-standing or ceiling-hung mirrors as interior objects need sufficient hall area — "они всё равно сжирают место."**

## Mistakes / Warnings — clutter, and hidden technical items

- **⚠️ "Чем у вас больше открытых всяких полок, ниш, тем больше хлама всякого. Это вообще стопроцентный проверенный факт."** **Corroborates the open-shelf-clutter critique already recorded on the entrance page from Игорь Краснов — second independent account.**
- **⚠️ Technical items in the hall must be concealed — "это 100%": the electrical panel, and revision hatches (his example: hatches for transformers serving the building's common lighting).** Routes he names: **within the wardrobe body; revision hatches made from furniture material; or behind mirrors** (less convenient but admissible).
- **⚠️ And a cost note attached: do not economise on recessing the panel — "не стоит каких-то гигантских денег проштробить эту нишу," not possible everywhere but possible in most cases.** Otherwise it protrudes.

## Electrical / Lighting — three hall-specific items

- **⚠️ A master switch (мастер-выключатель) in the hall: "очень классная штука, нужно всем его ставить"** — one key kills every light source in the flat, and on return pressing it restores the lights that were previously on.
  - **⚠️ But he scopes it honestly, which is what makes it usable: in a small flat where all the lighting is within your field of view you can skip it, "потому что всё-таки какой-то бюджет он заберёт." It earns its place in a large flat with rooms scattered out of sight, where otherwise "тебе нужно раздеться, опять прийти в комнату, проверить, всё ли ты выключил."**
  - **Corroborates the master-switch content already on `12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Switches_and_Controls.md`, and adds the small-flat exemption.**
- **⚠️ Hall lighting must be diffuse, not directional spots.** With spots **you cannot see properly in the mirror, cleaning is uncomfortable, and you get "одно пятно засвеченное, а другое там тёмный угол постоянный."**
- **⚠️ Provide a «дежурный свет» — a low-level duty light — especially where the hall is visible from a child's room.** Coming home late you would otherwise switch on the main light "и ты будешь просто всем своим мешать." Low-powered but enough to undress and cross to where you are going.

## Furniture / Built-ins — sliding wardrobes rejected again, with three reasons

- **"Если есть возможность не ставить шкаф купе — не нужен он вам."** Three grounds: **opening one leaf blocks the other; it looks worse, owing to the facade offset and the visible hardware; and "это работает тоже не всегда корректно."**
- **⚠️ And the cost of doing it properly: "если ты хочешь скрытую какую-то систему, корректно работающую, ты за это ещё и заплатишь кучу денег."**
- **Prefer hinged facades wherever space allows.** **Corroborates the wardrobe-type tradeoff content already on `14_Furniture/`; a fourth reason (a shared wardrobe's morning door-conflict) comes from this channel's own retrospective source.**

## ⚠️ Planning Rules — a real replanning case from his own flat, and the lesson

- **The problem: a wardrobe for outerwear would not fit the pier (простенок) available — the depth was insufficient, with only about 5 cm / 50 mm to play with.** Using that space rationally was impossible, **"либо это будет дико некрасиво — ты заходишь, у тебя половину входной двери перекрывает торец шкафа."**
- **⚠️ The solution was not cleverness within the constraint but replanning: shoe cabinets went into the niche, the clothes wardrobe went opposite the entrance — which rationalised the hall's area AND yielded a correctly-shaped dressing room belonging to the master bedroom.** "Адекватное решение пришло со временем."
- **⚠️ And the general lesson he draws, which is the transferable half: an initial rough sketch is always potentially wrong. "Ты не можешь сразу всех нюансов учесть, это невозможно. А потом у тебя начинает всплывать: вот это я думал, что здесь размещу — это невозможно… изначально твой план нереализуем по техническим причинам."** Buy the flat having already thought about what and how.

## Planning Rules — when a hall is optional

- **⚠️ Not having a hall at all is admissible, and the test is the usage scenario.** In serviced apartments where one or two people arrive and drop a jacket, it hardly matters. **"А когда ты квартиру используешь каждый день и с семьёй, это уже совсем другое дело."**
- **⚠️ Layouts where the entrance opens directly into a kitchen-living room: same test. "Если ты там живёшь с семьёй и с детьми — не совсем комфортная история."** **Complements the pass-through-room rules recorded from Безверхая on `17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Whole_Home_Planning_Method.md`.**

## ⚠️ Mistakes / Warnings — "нету ничего антивандального"

- **A corrective worth keeping, aimed at a common client expectation: "у нас есть у людей такое, что всё антивандальное должно быть. Иногда такое ощущение, что люди домой с битой заходят… Нету ничего антивандального."**
- **⚠️ And the trade-off he attaches: "чем более натуральный у тебя материал, тем больше он требует ухода."** His example — **a nubuck sofa: spill wine and the stain is there "до конца своих дней," and you paid for the nubuck.**
- **Consistent with the material-appropriateness thesis across this channel, and it is the same argument his own four-year retrospective makes against solid timber in a wet location.**
