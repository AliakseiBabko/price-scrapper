## Durable Facts

- **Avoid momentary/push-button ("возвратные"/relay) light switches** in favor of standard two-way ("проходной") switches — push-button switches route a low-voltage signal to a relay in the panel and always return to the same physical position, which some clients want for visual consistency, but they don't solve the underlying problem (switch position no longer indicates on/off state at all — only the light itself does) and cost significantly more: they require thin 0.75 mm² signaling wire but a **relay panel 2–3x larger** than standard, which forced this client to build an extra false wall just to fit the panel. Speaker's stated house rule: standard two-way switches are the ZEMS default, relay/momentary switches are not recommended despite marketing pressure. `single-account`.
- **Incoming cable upsizing**: on this object the developer ran a cable sized "at the edge" of the declared load capacity (6 mm² cross-section) — replaced with a heavier cable (stated as ~12 mm²/"quadrates") to comfortably support supply ventilation + tankless water heater + kettle + cooktop + fridge simultaneously, where the developer's original sizing was adequate only for a powerful cooktop alone.
- **Waterproofing standard**: bathroom/WC floor can be laid flush (same level) with the rest of the apartment's floor **only if a full waterproofing membrane covers every room**, not just wet rooms — and this must be photo-documented at time of installation as proof, in case of later dispute. Wet-room walls also require waterproofing in addition to the floor membrane.
- **Riser insulation**: replace/inspect insulation on sewage, hot-water, and cold-water stack risers rather than trusting developer-installed wrapping — developers sometimes fake compliance by wrapping risers in a generic gray material taped on as a decorative sleeve rather than genuine sound/thermal insulation; always remove and verify, and check whether the riser material itself is sound-insulating.
- **Extra access hatch for interfloor ventilation valves**: standard practice is to seal these valves behind drywall since they're rarely serviced, but if a client requests future access, install a non-sagging hatch at that location rather than refusing.
- **"Nano niche" for ultra-thin TVs**: some thin-profile TVs have a genuine dedicated slim connector (power+signal+data in one thin cable) that mounts flush to wall; others are marketed as "thin" but use standard connections (HDMI, TV cable, separate power) with a short cable, making flush wall-mounting impractical without a cable channel. The fix used here: a small recessed niche in the wall behind the TV with the outlets set back, so cables and connectors tuck inside rather than protruding past the wall plane.
- **Fake-wall door openings should be reinforced with plywood, not wood blocking ("brus")**: the traditional method (screwing a wood block into the metal stud profile before hanging the door) has two failure modes — installers commonly forget to include it, and wood blocking cracks the surrounding wall over time as it expands/contracts with humidity. The stated better method: widen the rough opening by the thickness of plywood on both sides, then face the opening with plywood before hanging the door — faster, cheaper, and doesn't crack. Concrete sizing note: opening width increased by roughly 2× the plywood thickness ("2D" per side) for an 800 mm door.
