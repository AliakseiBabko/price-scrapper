## Materials — terminology, and why the category is confusing

- **Styles that presuppose these elements**: classic, neoclassic, classic-contemporary mixes, fusion, transitional, and variations like Provence.
- **⚠️ And a use that cuts against the obvious one: moulding is a way to *mix* classic with other styles — she names putting moulding on concrete walls for a classic-plus-loft result — "но конечно, это надо продолжать и в самом интерьере."** The mixing must be carried through, not left as one gesture.
- **⚠️ «Лепнина» is a slippery term** — it implies something moulded from gypsum. **But cornices, skirtings and architraves are also made of polyurethane, MDF, or (cheapest) expanded polystyrene; floor skirtings can be MDF or aluminium — and you would hardly call an aluminium skirting «лепнина» — and there are plastic skirtings too.**
- **⚠️ Russian has no single word covering all of them; English does — "trim," which translates as отделка or окантовка and is used beyond interiors**, e.g. trimming a dress with braid.
- **⚠️ And she argues the concept contains the reason to bother at all**: some people pay extra for coloured trims on a car's wheel arches, sills and mirrors, and it is the same as paying for braid on a dress — **attention to detail that makes the thing individual.**
- **⚠️ The framing claim for the whole video: different tintings of the same moulding change the mood of the entire interior. "Вместо заученной мелодии вы организуете в своём доме такую джазовую интерпретацию."**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ the structural rule the taxonomy rests on

**All trims sit on planes** — on a wall, at the wall/floor junction, or at the wall/ceiling junction. **So the colour decision always accounts for the colour of the adjoining surface.** Often all, or some, of the trims are one colour.

**Three groups of options: (1) the colour of one of the adjoining planes; (2) a neutral — beige, white, grey or black; (3) another colour.**

## Design Concept — Group 1: the colour of an adjoining plane

- **A skirting adjoins wall and floor, so: wall colour, floor colour, or the colour of the door architraves it runs into.** Three options.
- **A cornice touches wall and ceiling: wall colour or ceiling colour** — usually white, the most obvious answer. **Her analogy: buying jacket and trousers from the same cloth.**
- **⚠️ Cornice and skirting in the wall colour visually increase wall height.**
- **⚠️ It pushes the moulding into the background — it complicates the space while staying behind, which frees you to work with other elements: textile, furniture finishes.**
- **⚠️ White moulding with white walls is the most widespread variant, and she gives the condition under which it works: when there is a LOT of moulding, when it is complex, when it has small details.** The mechanism — **the more detail, the heavier the moulding looks and the more it weighs the interior down; white makes it lighter, so the two balance.** She adds that the Parisian/Haussmann apartment reference strongly influences this choice.

**⚠️ When to paint trim in the wall colour**: when you do not want an accent on the moulding but do want more detail; **when the ceiling is low**; when the moulding is too large; **and when you do not know what colour to choose** — i.e. it is the safe default.

**Two lifehacks she attaches, and they are the most usable items in this group:**

- **⚠️ Paint *almost* the wall colour — one or two tones lighter or darker.** Her own bedroom: blueberry walls with black mouldings, the cornice dark and the moulding darker still, **which produces additional volume.**
- **⚠️ Or do not change the colour at all — change the paint's sheen. Walls super-matte, architectural details in eggshell, i.e. slightly glossy. That alone gives extra volume and interest.**
  - **⚠️ Cross-source note: this independently corroborates the eggshell/~15%-gloss category described in `pFCVO7AuSMw` from the same round**, where the product name was ASR-garbled beyond recovery. **Two separate videos garble the same word to «excel» and «action», which together make eggshell the confident reading.**

## Design Concept — Group 2: neutrals

### White moulding on coloured walls

- **⚠️ The governing rule: the greater the lightness difference between moulding and wall, the greater the accent on the moulding — it stops being background and becomes one of the interior's key elements.** Whether that is good depends on the style.
- **On restrained colours white reads as a delicate addition; against bright walls it reads bold and adds freshness.** Works well with classic-leaning detail such as stained glass.
- **⚠️ With bright walls, white edging softens the brightness so the walls stop being oppressive — and she gives the mechanism: the wall begins to be broken up, or at minimum its area is reduced, so there is simply less of that brightness in the room.**
- **⚠️ But the device visually lowers ceilings — so not for a low-ceilinged room.** *(Note this is the direct opposite of the Group 1 effect, which raises them. The two are the same lever pulled in opposite directions.)*
- **⚠️ White is excellent where another room is visible through an opening: it becomes a frame, almost a picture frame, and acts as a buffer separating the two colours.**

### White moulding on neutral walls

- **⚠️ The mechanism, and it is a good observation: with no white nearby, subtle light shades are often read as simply colourless. Adjacency with white shows off both — the white becomes ideal and clean, and the neutral acquires the depth it needs.**
- **A good choice for interiors with a lot of wood, because it removes the rustic feeling an abundance of wood can produce.**

**When to choose white**: when the walls are white (the default); as an overtone for neutral walls; to lower the brightness of bright walls; and as a frame.

### Grey and other neutrals

- **Neutral paint picks out architectural details but delicately and with restraint.**
- **Good for suggesting a country house — but it depends on form as well as colour.** Her example pairs simple forms with a slightly rough-textured floor.
- **⚠️ Her analogy: like adding a little cold water to hot tea — the tea should be hot by default, but the water makes it a bit softer and pleasanter.**
- **⚠️ Lifehack: the moulding need not be one colour. It is often assembled from several elements — a skirting can be two or three — and they need not match.** In her example **an intermediate grey softens the transition from black to a white wall.** Her analogy: testing the sea with a toe before going in.

**When to use neutrals**: interiors mixing classical and contemporary elements; when you want the effect of an old house, a generational inheritance; **and when you want to pick out doors and windows as architectural elements while the walls stay white.**

### Black

- **Adds energy, graphic quality and drama, with a touch of high fashion. A bold decision not everyone can accept.**
- **⚠️ Her own worked project: very active wallpaper, and adding a black cornice and black skirting distributed the viewer's interest around the room and drew attention off the pattern's brightness — because black pulls attention to itself.**
- **Like white, black can frame — so door openings and windows read as pictures.**
- **⚠️ Black trim pulls a room together where there are too-bright or too-scattered elements: "когда в интерьере много подробностей, чёрный как бы обнимает, ограничивает комнату." Her analogy: a good sauce in an exotic salad.**
- **⚠️ Lifehack: you need not paint every element black.** A pair of elements is enough if you are nervous — in her example the black graphic appears on the ceiling but **not** in the skirting or the French-window surrounds, **and the result reads more airy.**

**When to use black**: when you want drama and classic at once; **to set off wood on the floor or in furniture finishes**; to add a "фишечка" to a predictable neutral palette; **and when you have good views and want to frame them like pictures.**

### Wood trim (she files it with the neutrals)

- **Less common. People who order veneered skirting in the door colour or the parquet colour could just as easily have ALL the trims veneered — "не такая уж сложная или нерешаемая задача на русском рынке."**
- **The style can be almost anything, depending on the wood's finish and colour.**
- **⚠️ Best to choose a colour slightly different from your floors.**
- **⚠️ Two problems she names: with wood-coloured trim it is quite hard to select wallpaper — better to stop at plain painted walls; and you can create an excess of wooden elements. Keep to a maximum of THREE wood-effect finishes in an interior.**
- **When to choose wood**: when the whole interior is built on one wood tone; **when you want an Arts and Crafts feel — combining the trims with each other reads authentic**; when you do not have wooden floors but want warm notes; or simply if you love wood.

## Design Concept — Group 3: coloured trim

- **⚠️ The argument for it: it adds a new colour to the palette, giving interest while occupying little area and not being oppressive.** A cheap way to introduce colour with low commitment. *(Directly complements the low-commitment ladder in `ubB1GSFtN20` from the same round, where coloured skirting and cornice are the "when braver" rung.)*
- **⚠️ But do not colour a single element — do not paint only the skirting a new colour. Use a minimum of two, preferably three elements. Or, if you use one, support that colour somewhere else in the accessories.**
- Her worked project: a swamp green appearing as **cornice, ceiling moulding and door-opening surround — three elements** — while textile and wallpaper sit in other shades. **Her analogy: a tie — a man wearing one often adds a pocket square, and the two details combine.**

**Which colour — the same logic as any interior colour choice, via the wheel:**

- **Analogous**: take the wall colour and add a neighbouring, similar-but-different one. Her bedroom: sea-green walls with greener cornices, **and the same green then supported by two cushions on the bed.**
- **The same colour but more saturated, or darker, or lighter than the walls.** Her living room: light-blue walls with a slightly more saturated ceiling cornice.
- **Complementary** — the opposite side of the wheel. **A very strong contrast and a bold decision.**
- **Other contrasts**: a colour plus white walls; two adjacent colours, which already form a contrast; **or contrast by warmth — warm against cold.**
- Another example she rates: a lilac supported in small textile details on the bed and in the bed base — **"благодаря этому палитра выглядит собранной; там не так много цветов в палитре, а разнообразие крутое."**

**When to choose coloured trim**: when seeking bold solutions; to diversify the palette; **⚠️ when the colour of the architectural details repeats a colour from ANOTHER room — e.g. a blue sofa in the living room, and skirtings and cornices of the same blue in the adjoining kitchen or corridor, which she calls "очень крутая тема"**; and when you want to support accessories whose colour you already know.

## Design Concept — ⚠️ her closing, which is the contested part

**"Архитектурные детали — это не всегда бабушкин стиль."** She acknowledges that many people now refuse them and says she does not insist on using them — **but they can look very contemporary.** Her final example is essentially **Japandi** carrying a great deal of trim. *"Если вы их не любите, возможно, стоит просто попробовать и научиться их готовить."*

## Mistakes / Warnings

- Colouring a single trim element and nothing else.
- White trim on coloured walls in a low-ceilinged room.
- More than three wood-effect finishes.
- Wood-coloured trim combined with wallpaper.
- Wood trim matched exactly to the floor colour rather than slightly offset.
- Treating moulding on concrete as a one-off gesture without continuing the mix through the interior.
