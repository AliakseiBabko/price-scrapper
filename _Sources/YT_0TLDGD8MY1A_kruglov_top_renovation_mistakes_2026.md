## Genuinely New Content

**Ventilation fan tied to the light switch — new topic, no prior page
coverage found**: wiring a bathroom's exhaust fan so it switches on
automatically with the main light (rather than its own independent
switch) is called a clearly bad practice — always give ventilation its
own dedicated switch.

**Entry door reveal (откос) finish material — new topic, distinct from
this vault's existing entrance-door-relocation content**: avoid
wallpaper or a thin coat of paint on the entry door's own reveal —
this is a high-mechanical-wear zone (large items carried in/out
routinely) that will get scuffed, scratched, or chipped. **Prefer a
genuinely anti-vandal finish**: decorative plaster, a rigid panel, or
an interior-door jamb-extension ("добор") trim piece repurposed for
this location.

**Kitchen open hanging-rail storage (rails/rods for utensils) — new
clutter critique, distinct from this page's existing eurorail-hardware
installation-technique content**: an open utensil rail system reads as
constant visual clutter unless the household genuinely cooks and uses
every hung utensil daily — for anyone else, it's better skipped.

**Glass kitchen backsplash — new material critique**: called an
increasingly outdated choice, with the same practical downside as
glossy facades (readily shows fingerprints/water marks/smudges) on top
of reading as visually dated.

**Bedside pendant/hanging glass sconce over a nightstand — new safety
concern, distinct from this vault's existing bedside-reading-light
placement content**: a hanging glass-shade fixture positioned within
arm's reach of the bed is a real breakage risk for a half-asleep
occupant (e.g. reaching to silence an early alarm) — recommends against
this fixture type/placement combination specifically.

**Cutlery-drawer partial-pull nuance, minor refinement of this page's
existing "nested drawer" critique**: even without full nesting behind
a large facade, splitting a wide cutlery/utensil drawer into
independently-openable sections (rather than one continuous wide tray)
reduces the physical effort needed to reach a single item, especially
once the drawer carries real weight.
