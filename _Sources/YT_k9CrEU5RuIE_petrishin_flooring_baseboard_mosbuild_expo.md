## Flooring Material — Carpet Exclusion

**Carpet (ковролин) is rarely used in Russian apartments**, mainly
relegated to hotels: high foot traffic soils it quickly, it's not always
installed well, and bacteria/insects can colonize it over time. More
durable alternatives (quartz-vinyl, laminate, parquet, engineered board)
are chosen instead for residential use; carpet stays more common in
bedrooms/children's rooms in hospitality settings than in a typical
apartment.

## Baseboard Selection

1. **Cable-channel baseboard, a distinct purpose-built product from a
   plain plastic baseboard's incidental wall-gap routing** (already noted
   on this vault via RemProektMD): a baseboard with a **built-in cable
   raceway** lets a budget renovation add new low-voltage wiring
   (network cable, phone, TV, etc.) into a room later without chasing or
   cutting into the wall — a deliberate feature, not a side effect of
   the baseboard's own gasket/gap.
2. **White plastic baseboard, ~8cm rectangular profile**, recommended
   specifically for Scandinavian-style interiors: doesn't yellow/darken
   over time (8-10 year no-yellowing warranty cited on a Polish import
   product), corner-cap accessories don't detach, and its paint finish
   resists household cleaning chemicals — can also be repainted to a
   different color if needed since the base material takes paint well.
3. **Tile external-corner trim profiles, a thickness ladder matched to
   tile format size**: profile thickness options (e.g. ~8mm/10mm/12mm)
   correspond to different tile thicknesses/formats — thicker profiles
   for larger-format tile. Material choice is a cost/aesthetic tradeoff:
   thin aluminum (cheaper) vs. thicker galvanized metal, and separately,
   unplated aluminum painted to color-match the tile (blends in) vs.
   chrome/brass-plated trim (a deliberate shiny accent, pricier) — both
   valid depending on whether the trim should disappear or stand out.
4. **⚠️ Baseboard-width-to-expansion-gap coordination rule, a genuine
   cross-trade planning technique**: the flooring installer's thermal
   expansion gap ("термошов") left around a laminate/engineered-board
   floor's perimeter and pipe penetrations typically runs 8-12mm, but
   varies installer-to-installer by several millimeters even within one
   room — and a too-thin baseboard sole/foot can leave that gap visible
   if the installer's execution isn't perfectly even. **Fix: specify the
   exact baseboard width and foot depth in the design project itself, so
   the flooring crew knows in advance how much irregularity the baseboard
   will actually cover** (a ~30-32mm-wide baseboard with adequate foot
   depth comfortably covers even a sloppy ±1cm gap variance) — rather than
   both trades separately assuming "the baseboard will cover it" with no
   shared number.
5. **Decorative pipe/riser escutcheon collars ("гребёнки")**: trim rings
   sold in multiple colors specifically to close the expansion-gap/
   cosmetic seam around a heating-pipe riser where it passes through
   laminate, quartz-vinyl, or parquet flooring — a named product category
   for this specific penetration-trim problem, matched to the flooring
   finish color.

## Balcony/Loggia Budget Finishing Options

Two named budget-friendly alternatives to plastic wall panel cladding for
a balcony/loggia: **painted tongue-and-groove wood paneling
("вагонка")**, a cheap standard option; and **decorative façade plaster**,
which tolerates outdoor-style temperature swings (relevant for an
unheated balcony) and can be stencil-patterned (a damask-style pattern
was shown as a way to visually continue a classic-style interior onto
the balcony) using gel/spatula/roller/brush application for varied
textures — framed as visually nicer than plastic panels at comparable
budget tier.
