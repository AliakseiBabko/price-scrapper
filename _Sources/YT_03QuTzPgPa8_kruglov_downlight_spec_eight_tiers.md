## Durable Facts — The Six Measurable Parameters

### 1. CRI/RA, and R9 as the sub-metric that makes CRI misleading

- **CRI (= RA; the source is explicit they are the same thing) measures how accurately light renders colour against natural daylight**, 100 being sunlight. Budget fixtures sit at **65–80**; professional fixtures are **not below 90**; improved models reach **97+**.
- **⚠️⚠️ The genuinely new mechanism, and the most useful item in the source: CRI is an ARITHMETIC MEAN over ten samples — eight primary plus two additional — so a high average can conceal a near-zero component.** **R9 is the red-spectrum sample**, and a fixture can read **CRI ~90 with R9 = 10, or even R9 = 0**, which is what makes two fixtures both labelled "CRI 90" look completely different. Shown on camera: one marketplace lamp with a high overall CRI and **R9 of zero**, against an unbranded module with a barely-higher overall CRI and a very high R9, the latter visibly better.
- **⚠️ Cheap components cannot produce a high R9 at all** — stated as a materials limit, not a tuning failure: «из дешёвых комплектующих невозможно добиться высокого результата по показателю R9». And **manufacturers of cheap lamps never publish R9**, precisely because a buyer who knows only CRI will read 90 and stop.
- **⚠️ The ceiling this puts on the whole GX53 format, which is the practical conclusion: even a good GX53 lamp from a reputable maker does not exceed R9 ≈ 10%.** So the format caps colour quality regardless of brand.
- **Stated clarification worth keeping**: a high CRI **does not add colour or improve anything** — it shows objects as they actually are. Stated failure at low CRI: strawberries that are bright red in daylight read a sickly grey.

### 2. Flicker / pulsation coefficient (IRF)

- **Cheap LED lamps pulse at 50–90%.** Above ~90% it is visible to the naked eye. A good GX53 lamp from a reputable maker is **near zero**; the professional fixtures measured in the lab read **0%**.
- **⚠️ Stated cause, which ties flicker to a component rather than to luck: the simplest capacitive driver («ёмкостный драйвер») plus cheap parts.** This is the same root cause as the poor heat management in §4 — both follow from driver and diode being sealed in one cheap body.
- **⚠️ A free test the reader can run at home, and it is the most actionable item in the source: film the lit fixture on a phone with the shutter speed set to minimum.** The pulsation becomes visible on screen. The source's own on-camera footage was shot this way.
- **Stated consequences of high flicker: eye fatigue, headaches, general tiredness, and — claimed — degraded eyesight over time from sustained visual-system overload.** `single-account`, `unverified` as a health claim. **Stated priority zones: wherever you spend long periods under artificial light — reading, working, cooking.**

### 3. Beam angle and UGR

- **Beam angle**: a GX53 lamp throws **150–180°** — effectively in all directions. A professional fixture can be specified **from 5°** upward to whatever is wanted.
- **⚠️ UGR is named as the glare/discomfort parameter** — «параметр ослепления», how much a source strikes the eye, a measure of visual comfort. **Stated failure of the GX53 puck: it is bright and unshielded enough that you avoid raising your eyes, and are forced to look straight ahead or down.**
- **Stated remedies, all only available in the better tiers**: **anti-glare grids («антибликовые решётки»), tubes («тубусы»), and rings**, plus recessing the source deeper into the body.
- **⚠️ Optical accessories as a zoning tool, not just glare control**: an **oval-forming filter («овалорисующий фильтр»)** lights the oval of a work surface rather than filling the room from that point. The stated principle behind all of it: **under a properly designed scheme every fixture performs a named function rather than merely being on or off.**

### 4. Colour stability — LED binning («биновка»)

- **⚠️ A nominal colour temperature is a band, not a point.** Several spectra qualify as "3000 K"; within that band **one lamp drifts green and another pink**, and both measure as 3000 K. This is why a room of cheap lamps shows visible colour mismatch between fixtures.
- **⚠️ The named industrial fix: binning («биновка») — diodes are measured and sorted before assembly**, and only those inside the required window go into that fixture line. Those outside are resold or used in a line with looser tolerances. **Binning is what a premium price buys on this parameter, and it is not a feature a buyer can retrofit.**

### 5. LED lifespan and degradation, and the remote driver

- **⚠️ The mechanism, and it is structural rather than a quality lottery: in a GX53 or GU10 lamp the driver and the diode are sealed in one body, usually plastic, with no heat path.** The driver heats, nothing carries the heat away, and **the diode degrades fast — noticeable brightness loss and colour divergence within 1–2 years**, leaving a ceiling where one fixture is a different shade from the next and a third has visibly dimmed.
- **⚠️ The replace-a-lamp answer does not work, and the reason is §4**: you fit a new lamp, it is also nominally 3000 K, and it does not match its neighbours — so in practice you replace them all.
- **Professional construction, stated as three separate things**: the **driver is physically remote from the diode**, connected only by a wire, so driver heat never reaches it; the body carries a **proper metal heatsink**; and the rated life is **45,000–55,000 hours**, with a slow, gradual brightness decline after that rather than failure.

### 6. Build quality and everything the cheap tier cannot do

- Cheap: poor plastic and components, poor assembly, poor paint, **unreliable spring mechanisms**.
- **⚠️ In 99% of cases a cheap fixture cannot be dimmed via a professional control protocol — DALI is named** (also TRIAC at the professional tiers). If a protocol-controlled scheme is wanted, the fixture tier decides whether it is possible at all.
- Tunable colour temperature within one fixture (e.g. 4000–5000 K in the morning, 3000 K in the evening) is a better-tier capability.
- **Named professional-only capabilities**: **framing light («кадрирующий свет»)**, which lights a defined rectangle — a picture, a door — and is described as available only in top lines; **gobo filters** for coloured or light-and-shadow patterns; and **wall washers**, mounted in the ceiling to wash a wall or a defined corner.

## Rules / Heuristics — The Eight-Tier Ladder

**⚠️⚠️ Explicitly the source's own subjective scale, and he says so twice** — «максимально субъективную шкалу… Я их сам придумал» — derived from his five expert meetings and the lab visit, not from any standard. **Record it as one practitioner's structured framework, not an industry classification.** Its value is that it makes the 100× span navigable in steps rather than as a binary.

1. **Economy L1 — avoid entirely.** The 110-RUB GX53 pair. **The defining property is not poor specification but dishonest specification**: "CRI 90" on the box bears no relation to the measurement, and a buyer cannot tell except by price. Stated heuristic: between a 50-RUB and a 150-RUB lamp, the 150 is the logical pick — but L1 is best not touched at all.
2. **Economy L2 — the same GX53, honestly specified, from a reputable maker.** Named: Philips, Osram, Gauss, Навигатор (plus one ASR-mangled name). **You get: no or minimal flicker, an honest CRI near 90, better lifespan.** **You still get every defect of the format**: the ~180° puck, high UGR, no heat path, wide colour spread inside one nominal temperature, R9 capped ≈10%, and no adjustment of any kind. Described as extracting the maximum the GX53 base can give.
3. **L3 — an MR16 fixture taking a GU10 or GU5.3 lamp.** The step change is that **the fixture is now separate from the lamp, so the source can be recessed into the body** — UGR improves immediately, and an anti-glare grid or tube can be fitted. Example named: ArtLamp "Inter". **The GU10 lamp itself still has driver and diode in one plastic body, so §5's degradation is unchanged.**
4. **L4 — the same fixture, but with an LED module instead of a GU10 lamp.** Better diode, better and more expensive driver, **choice of beam angles**, proper binning, materially longer life — **but the driver is still inside the module body** (a denser, possibly ceramic body; the source is openly unsure how much better it dissipates: «если я не ошибаюсь, он каменный. Не знаю, насколько он сильно лучше отводит тепло»).
5. **L5 — the same module class with the driver brought OUT.** Named example: an SVG module with a separate remote driver. No overheating, so the whole assembly reaches its full rated life. **This is the single structural break in the ladder** — every tier above it has a remote driver and every tier below it does not.
6. **Premium L6 — complete fixtures with no replaceable source.** One-piece body, always a remote driver, aluminium body and aluminium heatsink, **CRI not below 90**, binning, wide angle choice, DALI/TRIAC dimming, plus moisture-protected and aimable variants. Entry lines named: SVG "mini Combo", ALED "Pola" — described as the door into professional light.
7. **Premium L7 — a constructor.** Choose the body, then a module with the required beam angle and temperature, then the frame, then optionally an anti-glare grid, tube or diffuser. Named: SVG Combo 43 + Combase module; ALED "Trix/Trace" (ASR-uncertain). **CRI 90–97; Philips drivers named at one maker.** The distinction from L6 is variability within one fixture family.
8. **L8 — maximum customisation.** Named: SVG "KOB 2.0" and ALED "Aspect SPTW68" (CRI to 97). Shown component by component: a **mounting frame** (black or white, suiting stretch and plasterboard ceilings alike), a module with a **needle/pin-type heatsink («игольчатым способом радиатор») maximising dissipation area**, **six lens options**, **variable recess depth of the diode itself**, and an anti-glare ring that hides the source until you stand directly beneath it.

- **⚠️ Stated world trend, and it explains the L8 feature set: minimise the visibility of the light source.** The source points at design-project renders and Pinterest as where this is already visible.
- **⚠️ A buyer-side action the source offers against his own tier argument: take your own intended fixture to a professional lighting company and ask them to run the same measurement on it**, the way he did in the lab. A concrete, free verification step that does not require buying anything.
- **A second one: you need not buy the top line.** Reputable makers have entry ranges where the parameters are at least honestly stated — the stated point of the video is to make the axes legible, not to sell the top tier.

## Planning Rules — Biodynamic Lighting and Colour Temperature

- **⚠️ A specific physiological threshold, new to this vault at this precision: at 5000 K and above melatonin production is blocked; at 3000 K it proceeds normally.** The stated corollary is symmetric and is the useful half — **waking to 3000 K is markedly harder, so 4000–5000 K is better for a morning scene** while 3000 K suits the evening.
- **Biodynamic lighting described as the coming requirement**: a scene that ramps from zero to ~60% over roughly two hours at ~4000 K to wake, and a warm ~3000 K evening scene. Named as available on a newcomer, Light Pro, alongside high CRI and angle choice — **flagged as a commercial mention and as a product the source states he has not himself used** («Я ещё не использовал их освещения»).
- Stated prediction, recorded as opinion: within 3–10 years buyers will expect not just good light but light that adapts to their daily rhythm.
