## ⚠️⚠️ «Попал на деньги» — a costed error, and the lesson applied on camera

instead **one large slab, 180 × 90, was ordered to cut the 120 × 90 out of.** He cut
it wrong.

## ⚠️ He breaks the classic laying order deliberately, and the reason is a module

**The convention he names**: lay the far wall from the entrance first, then the side
walls, so the side tiles cover the joint. **He refuses it — «я так делать
принципиально не буду» — and the reason is dimensional coordination:**

- The shower zone was **built to an exact size** so the tile lands as **exactly 120 cm plus 4 mm for the corner joints**.
- **The linear drain's insert is also exactly 120 cm.** «Чтобы всё везде сошлось у меня и был изначальный расчёт под это всё.»
- The resulting visible joint doesn't matter: it is treated afterwards and invisible, **and a shower enclosure will stand over it anyway.**

**→ The room was built to the tile-and-insert module, so the module wins over the
layout convention.** A cleaner statement of module-driven wet-room planning than the
convention it displaces.

## Cutting and drilling porcelain

- **Guide rail plus a scoring carriage, three slabs at once**, held by suction cups; **two sets of marks — the cut line, and where the rail itself sits** (it stands off the line).
- **⚠️ Wet the score line with plain water before breaking**: «плитка, какой бы хороший ролик ни был… вода просто минимизирует эти риски». It can still run off, but less often.
- **A breaker tool («ломатель»)** pressing from above and below on aligned marks, tightened by bolt — he takes the microphone off so the crack can be heard progressing.
- **⚠️ Dress the fresh edge with a diamond block immediately** — «этот край очень острый», purely so as not to cut yourself.
- **Delivered slabs had chips («брак в виде скола»)** on one edge; those parts were simply cut away.
- He also demonstrates a **classic bench cutter** and says he rarely uses it — the long rail with several slabs at once suits him better. Presented as a preference, not a verdict.
- **⚠️ Drilling a hole that straddles two tiles**: dimensions taken as squares from the reference, then **the two tiles are taped tightly together along the joint** — «это позволит сверлить отверстие, которое попадает на две плитки» — before a **60 mm core bit** on an angle grinder with a vacuum hose does the cut. Fast-cutting core bits chip slightly, «но они вообще никогда не критичны, потому что всё это впоследствии [закрывается]».
- **⚠️ His own verdict on where the work is**: «**самая сложная часть — это, конечно же, разметка и вырезы**… сама укладка уже намного проще и быстрее».

## ⚠️ External corners — the 45° mitre, with the caveat

**No overlap: each tile's edge is mitred («запил») at 45° so the corner reads as a continuous turn.** Method: a **portable rail-guided cutter with bearings riding the edge**, which **cuts slightly more than 45°**, followed by **grinding the remaining edge with progressively finer discs**.

> **⚠️ The caveat that matters: preserve the tile's factory chamfer («фаска») — it is there to prevent chipping.** «Она вот здесь со всех сторон есть. Вот её надо оставить.» — Василий

A test piece shows the joint closing up markedly after grinding, **with a deliberate small gap left for grout**. **It can be done by hand; the machine is only faster.** Corroborates the mitre-not-plastic-trim rule this vault already holds, and supplies the method and the chamfer caution.

## ⚠️⚠️ Tile adhesive classes — the clearest explainer in the round

**The mechanism that drives the whole decision, stated first:** a long or large tile
carries **a bow («дуга») you cannot see** — it sits either bowed out or «лодочкой».
**The bigger the tile, the bigger the deformation, so the adhesive must also be able
to deform.**

| Code | Meaning as he gives it |
| :--- | :--- |
| **C0** | Cheapest — sand, cement, a few additives. **«Использовать я его не рекомендую нигде.»** |
| **C1** | Acceptable; suitable up to about **30 × 30** (and 20 × 30) |
| **C2** | **Enhanced adhesion, bond > 1.5 MPa.** Minimum for **45 × 45, 60 × 60, 120 × 60 and larger** |
| **T** | **Thixotropic** — holds its combed shape; on a floor it stiffens fast enough that you can move on the tile a little while laying |
| **E** | **Extended open time** — critical on large formats, where a big area must be covered on both tile and substrate before the adhesive skins |
| **S1** | **«Супер эластичный»** — **and he separates elasticity from plasticity: plasticity is how workable the wet mix is; elasticity is the CURED adhesive's ability to deform without failing**, which is what absorbs the tile's bow. **S2 exists for very large formats** |

**⚠️ The asymmetry of the risk, stated plainly**: C2 under small tiles «ничего
страшного не произойдёт — ну немножко переплатить», but **C0 or C1 under 120 × 60 and
up «может привести к печальным последствиям в виде отслоения, быстрого разрушения»**.
His two-part conclusion: **«не нужно экономить на клее»** and **«правильно
подбирайте»** — don't overpay for small tiles either.

**⚠️ A product-property preference worth knowing**: the adhesive he uses **contains no
sand — marble dust as the filler instead, like a putty** — so it is soft and **a sand
grain cannot end up sitting in a joint.**

## ⚠️ Notched-trowel arithmetic — divide the notch by four

**12 mm notch → about 3 mm of adhesive under the tile.** His rule: **divide the notch
size by four**, and he shows the derivation — **combing at 45° leaves roughly half the
notch height (~6 mm), and bedding the tile halves it again.** A conversion this vault
did not have in any form.

- **Double buttering for large formats: combed on the wall AND a thin skim on the back of the tile**, deliberately **filling the tile's own ribbed pattern** — «его как раз-таки нужно заполнить».
- **Comb in one direction only.** Excess taken off immediately.
- Suction cups to place, **a vibrating tool to bed the slab**, spacers, and the laser as the reference; first row checked and adjusted («надо придвинуть низ», not the top).
- **⚠️ Clean the joint as you go — «обязательно у шва почистить».**

## ⚠️ Levelling clips (СВП) — mandatory for large tiles, and what they do NOT do

**«Для большой плитки они обязательны»**, and the reason is the bow again: **they pull
adjacent tiles into a common plane and eliminate lippage steps.**

> **⚠️ And he draws the distinction explicitly: «она стягивает между собой — она не делает уровень, она делает плоскость общую».** Clips deliver a **plane**, not a **level**; the level comes from the laser and the setting-out. — Василий

**He also admits the limit on camera**: «вон видно, какая дуга — это сама плитка
такая, ну с этим я ничего не сделаю», and a residual gap in one corner. **A named
tolerance of the material rather than a claim of perfection.**

## ⚠️⚠️ Epoxy grout — an opinion about contractors, then the full method

> «Многие люди почему-то боятся использовать эпоксидную затирку… мастера аргументируют: зачем это вам надо, она дорогая. **Любые аргументы, которые мастер говорит против эпоксидной затирки, означают лишь одно: либо он не умеет с ней работать, либо он работал с ней давным-давно, у него ничего не получилось, и с тех пор прошло много времени** — производители прислушиваются к мастерам и адаптируют свой продукт.» — Василий

**⚠️ Usable as a vetting signal**: a contractor's objection to epoxy is, on this
practitioner's account, evidence about the contractor rather than about the product.
**And he concedes the cost honestly rather than dismissing it**: «она действительно
дорогая, дороже обычной, **и работать с ней тоже дороже**».

**Method:**

- **Mix per the instructions in small batches — ~400 g**, which can be mixed by hand.
- **Joints must be clean before it goes in.** Apply firmly, take off the excess.
- **⚠️ The fear is the greasy epoxy film, and the removal is a four-stage sequence:**
  1. **Water with liquid soap and a fibre sponge** — lifts the excess **and forms and smooths the joint**; produces an emulsion; turn the sponge, repeat, rinse in the soapy water. **The sponge must be damp, not wet.**
  2. **A cellulose sponge, well wrung** — takes up the surplus moisture **together with the remaining epoxy emulsion**, while the joints are still wet.
  3. **A microfibre pad, well wrung** — removes what is left, leaving only a very thin damp film.
  4. **⚠️⚠️ «В эпоксидной затирке главное — не оставить вот этот влажный слой на высыхание, его нужно полностью высушить сразу.»** Dry that film off deliberately rather than letting it dry by itself. **Then the tile is clean and the joint is done — «больше ничего с этой плиткой делать не нужно».**
- **⚠️ And the sting in the tail: «самое интересное, что все эти шаги описаны в инструкции, которые прикладывают к каждой упаковке. Ну почему-то её не хотят читать.»**
- **⚠️ Internal corners are NOT grouted with epoxy — silicone goes there.** **Independently consistent with his own post-occupancy finding** that silicone corners were intact after a year ([[_Sources/YT_h7JYk20m9mQ_sanuzel_bathroom_one_year_later|the one-year revisit]]).
- **⚠️ External corners are done differently**: **mask with tape**, fill the joint generously, **pull the tape immediately after filling**, form a reasonably even joint at once, then **leave it alone for 1.5–2 hours until the grout is like plasticine — then wet it and smooth it again.** Internal corners are easier: degrease, wet, remove excess.

**⚠️ Sequencing constraint recorded**: a **stretch ceiling** was due to be installed
shortly, so **all wall joints had to be grouted before then** — the floor could wait.
The grouting order was set by another trade's date, not by the tiling.

## Grinding the podium slab to size

Straightedge clamped as a stop against self-set marks, then: **a coarse diamond pad
first, taken slightly off-square from beneath so less has to come off the face
later; then a finer pad; then a small chamfer to remove micro-chips and stop new ones
forming.** **The work is done on the underside, not the visible face.**
