## Planning Rules / Budget — Why a Renovation-Company Discount Structurally Backfires

- **⚠️ Named revenue split, sharper than this store's existing ≈30-35%-margin figure**: market-typical split is **70% of the total estimate (смета) to crew+foreman labor, 30% to the company** — the company's 30% is *revenue*, not net profit. A stated discount (this video's worked example: 20% off the total estimate) is modeled as coming entirely out of the company's 30% share, not out of labor pay, meaning a 20% discount on the whole estimate consumes two-thirds of the company's entire revenue share (30% → 10%).
- **⚠️ Named tax-regime mechanism not previously recorded in this store**: a Russian small-business renovation company on the simplified tax regime (УСН, "доходы" variant) pays **7% of total revenue in tax** (6% base rate + a stated 1% surcharge once annual turnover exceeds 300,000 RUB, ≈$3,300) — leaving only 3% of the original estimate as pre-expense margin after a 20% discount (10% company revenue share − 7% tax).
- **⚠️ Named fixed monthly operating-cost floor for this business model, itemized**: advertising (300,000-1,000,000 RUB/month, ≈$3,300-$11,000, across YouTube/Instagram/social/Yandex Direct — high-volume-claims companies are argued to necessarily spend near this range to sustain "thousands of renovations" claims), office rent (≈50,000 RUB, ≈$550, example figure), 3 sales managers at 80,000-100,000 RUB (≈$880-$1,100) each (≈240,000 RUB/≈$2,700 total at the low end), an in-house accountant (50,000-80,000 RUB, ≈$550-$880 — argued cheaper in-house than outsourced at this claimed transaction volume), and an in-house lawyer (≈50,000 RUB, ≈$550) — **total minimum ≈690,500 RUB/month (≈$7,600)**.
- **⚠️ Worked breakeven-turnover conclusion**: given the fixed-cost floor above and the post-discount 3% margin, the company would need **≈23,000,000 RUB/month (≈$254,000) — ≈276,000,000 RUB/year (≈$3,048,000) — in signed contract volume just to break even (zero net profit)** while offering a 20% discount.
- **⚠️ Named tax-bracket-cliff mechanism, the video's central structural argument**: Russia's patent-based tax system (ПСН, a fixed annual sum instead of percentage-of-revenue) would be materially cheaper than УСН at this company's scale, but ПСН legally caps annual turnover at **≈60,000,000 RUB (≈$663,000)** — far below the ≈276,000,000 RUB breakeven turnover calculated above, so a discount-driven high-volume business model is structurally excluded from the cheaper regime. - **⚠️ Named quality-degradation feedback loop, extending this store's existing segment/labor-sorting mechanism from `AosCvLCh6WA`**: since the discount is modeled as coming from the company's own margin (not crew pay directly), the company's structural response to shrinking margin is argued to be hiring cheaper, lower-skilled staff to preserve some profit — in a market already short on skilled labor (per this store's existing sorting-effect note), this predictably worsens build quality, which in turn increases client disputes/litigation (justifying the in-house lawyer line item above) and adds further cost, closing a self-reinforcing loop.
