## Design Concept — 1. Functionality

- **⚠️ Her own position stated up front, and it is more nuanced than the usual: she does NOT think every object in a room must be functional, and is not an adherent of "form follows function."**
- **But she accepts that function and beauty often conflict in one space, and the scales tip one way or the other.**
- **⚠️ The rule she does commit to: "если интерьер не работает так, как задумано, то становится уже неважно, насколько красивая у вас палитра или насколько выдержан у вас стиль."**
- **The questions to answer first**: what do you plan to do in this space — eat, sleep, read, or something specific like cross-stitch or assembling mosaic panels; **will you receive guests here, which strongly affects both layout and possibly style; and is the room a through-route** — if so it needs higher wear-resistance materials, or upholstery protected against claws. **As you answer, a layout forms in your head.** `[restatement — this is a compressed version of the verb-list planning method already held from `Rcd9gkPC6CI`, now on `Whole_Home_Planning_Method.md`. The through-route/wear-resistance link is the one part stated more directly here.]`

## Lighting — 2. Light sources

- **Lighting can make even a fairly simple room attractive and cosy.**
- **⚠️ Two problems, and she ranks them, which the vault did not have: "когда света слишком много, это портит интерьер гораздо быстрее, чем когда света слишком мало" — but in practice she meets too little more often.** **The ranking is the new part.**
- **Attend to sources other than the ceiling light — sconces and floor lamps can give considerably more interesting effects than the top light.**
- **A minimum of three light sources per room.** `[restatement — the minimum-three-scenarios rule is long-held on `Lighting_Design.md` from Kruglov, and Реньжин's deflation of it is recorded there too. Her figure adds a third voice but no new mechanism.]`

## Design Concept — ⚠️ 3. The right rug

**The densest genuinely-new item in the source.**

- **Many of her clients do not want a rug at all. Acceptable in a small bedroom.**
- **⚠️ In a living room, a design without a rug looks incomplete — and if you refuse outright you must devise some other floor zoning, "и это всегда более сложное в исполнении дизайна, чем просто положить ковёр."** **The refusal has a cost, and it is a design-complexity cost rather than a money one.**
- **⚠️ Avoid too-small rugs: they spoil the room's proportions and make the room visually smaller.**
- **⚠️ "Правильный размер ковра имеет больше значения для дизайна, чем его цвет и рисунок."** A clean, quotable priority rule.

## Design Concept — ⚠️ 4. Occasional and side furniture

- **⚠️ "В дизайне считается хорошим тоном, что рядом с любым местом для сидения есть поверхность, куда можно поставить стакан."** A single checkable test for a seating layout.
- **So a central coffee table plus side tables improve both the design and the function.**
- **⚠️ In a bedroom, if there is no room for a nightstand, provide another surface — for instance a surface behind the headboard for small objects.**

## Design Concept — 5. Art, as three parameters

**Good when a room reflects its owner's personality, and art is the best instrument for that. Choosing it causes insecurity, but you need not be an expert in painting.** Her three parameters:

1. **It must fit the colour range** — account for what is already in the interior, **and note the palette can be extended to meet it, e.g. with cushions or a throw that support the picture.**
2. **It must suit by size** (she points to her own proportion video).
3. **⚠️ You must personally like it — a pleasant story or association behind it, or simply pleasure in looking at it. "Очень важно покупать именно то, что вас привлекает."**

`[partial restatement — the vault holds a fuller art-selection rule set from `bVmE50y33Ls` (abstract, ≥1 m, good frame, no famous reproductions). The colour-range and personal-attachment parameters are consistent with it; parameter 1's "extend the palette to meet the art" is the new half.]`

## Design Concept — 6. Variety

- **⚠️ Mix styles, colours, textures: "чем больше разница между предметами, тем комната становится энергичнее."** When everything matches as a suite it is boring, **which is why designers like to mix.**
- **⚠️ And a practical second-order benefit that is genuinely new: it is easier to make small changes over time if the interior is diverse from the start.** A diverse scheme has slack in it; a perfectly-matched one does not.

`[partial restatement — `uRK4OPR4dak`, processed earlier in this same round, states the everything-matches-too-well rule at greater length with its constraint. Only the change-over-time argument is new.]`

## Design Concept — ⚠️ 7. Something old

- **Convenient for the mix, and she gives the diagnostic: "бывает так, что смотришь на очень схожие по стилю интерьеры, но один интерьер цепляет, а другой как-то не очень — и всё дело в том, что там есть старинные предметы или старинные материалы."**
- **⚠️ "Следы времени вселяют душу в новый интерьер."** An old vase, an old painting, a restored armchair, a small table — anything.
- **⚠️ "Когда в интерьере есть вот это что-то старое, и может быть даже немножко обшарпанное, вы начинаете воспринимать его уже по-другому."**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ 8. Something living

**The observation behind this is the best thing in the source, because it explains a convention rather than repeating it.**

- **Interiors are rarely published with people in them — at most one photograph of the designer, or the designer and owners. The rooms are usually empty.**
- **⚠️ "Чтобы убрать вот это чувство покинутости, на съёмках добавляют растения."** Plants behave better on camera than dogs or cats, and fish can die if the tank is moved back and forth. **Hence the houseplants and fresh bouquets in every published interior.**
- **⚠️ Her point: they look good in the photograph, and they look just as good in the interior without the photograph. "Этим нужно воспользоваться."**
- **What counts**: an orchid in a pot, **branches brought in from outside — she notes this is especially relevant for Japandi** — cones in a small vase. **"Всё живое, имеющее отношение к миру за пределами интерьера, можно использовать как декор."**

## Materials — 9. Gloss

- **It is pleasant to be in an interior with plain matte walls that carries reflective elements** — a mirror, polished metal, a gilded frame, a glass tabletop.
- **⚠️ "Это даёт красивые блики, и комната становится светлее."**
- **⚠️ And the register they add differs by material: such finishes add luxury and glamour — or fineness and fragility, if the material is glass.**

## Design Concept — 10. Handmade objects

- **Objects made by hand, or that look handmade** — she calls it one of that year's trends. **Ceramics and macramé.**
- **⚠️ The conditional is the useful part: "если вместо живописи вы выбрали постер или фотографию, то добавить таких предметов жизненно необходимо."** A printed image needs handmade objects around it in a way an original does not.

## Rules / Heuristics — her closing

**"Самый лучший параметр, который точно улучшит ваш интерьер, — это удовольствие."** If you buy things you like and take pleasure in using or looking at them, it is fine.
