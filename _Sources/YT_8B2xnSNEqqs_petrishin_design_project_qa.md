## Design Concept — Practicality Rationale Behind Criticized Design Choices

1. **Concealed/flush-mount doors still need a baseboard** — a baseboard
   is a technical joint between wall and floor (compensates for surface
   unevenness, hides gaps and sometimes cables, and allows natural
   material movement/"breathing"), not a purely decorative element; a
   fully "floating"/baseboard-free wall-floor junction is possible but
   needs perfectly flat walls and floor, a special profile, and a budget
   3-5x a standard baseboard job — framed as a deliberate cost/complexity
   tradeoff, not a design oversight.
2. **Baseboard miter-corner ("45°") seam visibility depends on the
   baseboard material**: a painted-film or laminated-finish baseboard
   shows the 45° miter seam clearly; a duropolymer ("дюрополимер")
   baseboard that's primed and painted after installation hides the seam
   effectively.
3. **Baseboard material comparison by tradeoff**: solid-wood
   ("массив") — premium, eco-friendly, durable, repeatedly repaintable;
   MDF — more budget, works well on flat walls, but weak against
   prolonged water exposure; plastic — most practical option, genuinely
   moisture-resistant. Choice depends on budget and the specific wall
   conditions/room.
4. **Wall mural ("фреска") age-targeting for a child's room**: rather
   than picking a toddler-specific mural, target a design that stays
   visually appropriate up to roughly age 10 (worked example given: a
   tropical/bird-theme mural framed as broadly age-appropriate); for an
   older child/teen, involve them directly in mural selection. A mural
   bordered by molding is easy to swap out later (remove old, glue new)
   without redoing the room.
5. **Soft/cushioned windowsill seating — practicality defended, not a
   flaw**: doesn't necessarily need a cushion pad to be comfortable;
   dust/dirt buildup depends mainly on how polluted the street the window
   faces is, not the feature itself; the radiator typically positioned
   below keeps it warm in winter; doesn't require a large room to
   implement.
6. **Open/glass display shelving critique, split by context**: overhead
   glass kitchen display shelving near the ceiling is impractical —
   items placed there are effectively permanent (nobody keeps a step
   stool out to rotate glassware); prefer closed storage for anything
   above reach. For a handleless-upper-cabinet kitchen (no upper cabinet
   fronts at all), open shelving only works if the household genuinely
   maintains visual order and owns few items — clutter on an open shelf
   reads as visual noise, and the household must consciously accept that
   tradeoff. Recommended middle ground: a small, reachable glass display
   column near an upper cabinet, for a limited amount of frequently-used
   dishware, not full open-shelf storage.
7. **Bedroom lighting design method — plan by daily-routine timing, not
   generically**: identify what light is needed on waking (a bright
   overhead fixture) versus in the evening (indirect cornice/backlighting
   for relaxation) versus for reading (a bedside sconce/бра), plus a
   dimmed/low-level option — design the fixture set around the household's
   actual morning/evening routine, not a default fixture list.
8. **Night-lighting options ranked by reversibility/commitment level**: a
   pre-made illuminated baseboard with motion-sensor or button control
   (fixed/permanent look); a wall niche chased above the baseboard with
   built-in fixtures (permanent, more custom); or battery-powered
   plug-and-place lights (e.g. "Xiaomi" branded corner lights) as a
   reversible trial option to test placement/preference before committing
   to either permanent solution.
9. **Kitchen-living room combination cooking-smell concern, addressed
   directly**: framed as generally a non-issue in a modern apartment with
   working ventilation and an openable window, at ordinary (non-industrial)
   home-cooking volumes — contrasted with pre-modern housing, where
   cooking spaces were sometimes physically separated specifically to
   keep cooking smells away from living spaces, a distinction the source
   argues no longer applies with modern ventilation.
10. **Plywood ("фанера") kitchen-cabinetry cost claim disputed**: in
    response to a viewer claim that plywood is used in ~90% of Israeli
    kitchens (implying it's a cheap/practical default), the source states
    a plywood kitchen actually costs *more* than an enamel-finish kitchen
    in this market — flagged here as the source's own disputed claim
    (`unverified`), not independently confirmed by this project.
11. **Whole-apartment routine-walkthrough technique for socket/outlet
    placement**: mentally walk through each planned room simulating real
    daily actions (e.g., "where does my toothbrush charge," "where's the
    outlet for an electric razor") to place outlets by actual use
    scenario, rather than a generic default layout — explicitly
    recommended as something a self-managed planner can do themselves,
    though the source frames professional design help as easier.
12. **Jacuzzi/whirlpool tub — practicality caution, not a rejection**: the
    source's own stated preference is against spending extra on a
    jacuzzi given it costs more than a standard tub and requires frequent
    filter cleaning/rinsing, but frames it explicitly as a valid choice
    for a household that specifically wants the feature and is willing to
    maintain it — a lifestyle/maintenance-burden tradeoff, not a
    universal technical objection.
