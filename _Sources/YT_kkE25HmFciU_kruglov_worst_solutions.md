## Genuinely New Content

- **Motion sensors flagged as unreliable specifically for apartment
  interior lighting** (as opposed to street/entryway/stairwell lighting,
  where the source concedes they genuinely help): described as
  unpredictable in practice even with correct setup — "you'll never
  trust that the light will turn on when you actually need it." Explicit
  recommendation: standard switches, or two-way/crossover switches at
  most, for interior apartment rooms; reserve motion sensors for
  exterior/common-area use. New to this store's Smart_Home_Systems
  content (existing motion-sensor mentions there are all
  garage/exterior/cat-litter-box use cases from a different source, not
  a general apartment-interior reliability critique).
- **Passthrough (проходной) switch overuse creates real cognitive
  load — a new caution distinct from this store's existing cost-based
  crossover-switch-count formula**: passthrough switching genuinely
  simplifies life only when the household can mentally track which
  switch controls what from where. Recommendation: apply it selectively
  (e.g. one bedroom entry-to-bedside pair in a 30m² apartment) rather
  than wiring every room as a passthrough chain — an apartment fully
  saturated with passthrough switches becomes a "piano of switches" the
  household never fully learns.
- **LED-strip transformer centralization, with a stated failure
  mechanism**: rather than mounting each 12V transformer near its own
  LED strip run (a niche, behind a shelf, under a cornice — the common
  practice), consolidate every transformer for the apartment into one
  location (a closet, a walk-in wardrobe, the low-voltage panel),
  ideally somewhere with extra passive cooling. **Stated reason
  transformers fail: overheating** — centralizing them in one
  deliberately-cooled spot is framed as directly extending service life,
  not just a convenience-of-troubleshooting argument.
- **Vertical heating-riser drywall-boxing cost figure and a curtain-
  flexibility argument, extending this store's existing riser-boxing
  content (`Radiators_and_Convectors.md`, Round 3)**: a simple L-shaped
  drywall box around an exposed vertical riser costs roughly **3,000 RUB
  (too small a figure for this store's rounding-bucket convention to
  produce a meaningful $ value at this video's 2024-08-23 date)**. **New
  argument**: leaving the riser exposed and just relying on curtains to
  visually hide it locks you into curtains specifically — switching
  later to roller/roman blinds (mounted flush to the glass, no fabric
  drape to hide anything behind) removes the concealment entirely,
  since roller blinds don't drape past the window reveal the way a
  curtain does.
- **Stretch ceiling + solid moulded baguette framing cancels the
  ceiling's own core repairability advantage — a new mechanism for this
  store's existing five-way ceiling repairability comparison
  (`Ceilings_Guide.md`, Round 5)**: a stretch ceiling's membrane can
  normally be locally unclipped and re-tensioned (to add a fixture, fix
  a neighbor's leak, etc.) without disturbing the rest of the ceiling.
  Framing the perimeter in a rigid, puttied-and-painted baguette molding
  removes that flexibility — accessing the membrane later means
  physically breaking off a baguette section and re-finishing
  (puttying/sanding/painting) it from scratch. Recommends a shadow-gap
  profile or "eurokant" perimeter finish instead, both already
  documented on this page as compatible with the membrane's
  repairability.
- **Old parquet floor, capital-renovation-specific caution — distinct
  from this store's existing gap-sealant/refinishing content (which
  addresses a secondary-market apartment not undergoing a full gut)**:
  in a genuinely full capital renovation (walls re-plastered, wet
  processes throughout), an old parquet floor absorbs ambient moisture
  during the wet-trade stages and then dries/shrinks unevenly afterward
  — the source's recommendation for this specific project type is to
  replace the floor rather than attempt to preserve/refinish it, even
  where the existing parquet is otherwise high-quality/valuable. Framed
  as a different recommendation than the existing gap-sealant page
  content specifically because of the wet-process context, not a
  contradiction of it.
