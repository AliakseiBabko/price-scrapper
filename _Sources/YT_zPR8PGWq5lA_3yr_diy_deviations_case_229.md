## Mistakes / Warnings — Genuinely New Findings

- **⚠️ A real documented case of a load-bearing wall being chased/notched for plumbing routing, despite an otherwise professionally-designed project** — the source (Zemskov, narrating the tour) flags this explicitly as illegal ("tell the whole building inspection") and attributes it to the executing client deviating from the design's false-wall plan to avoid extra construction. **Reinforces this vault's existing "never chase a load-bearing wall" guardrail with a real-world case of the violation actually happening**, not just a hypothetical risk.
- **A bathtub shortened by 10cm from its designed length during execution (a mid-project deviation) directly caused a downstream defect**: the shortened tub left an exposed horizontal ledge where water pools and silicone degrades — reinforcing this vault's existing "bathtub-in-three-walls, avoid horizontal ledges" finding, but with a new causal mechanism: **a seemingly-minor dimensional deviation from a design can break an assumption the rest of the design depended on** (here, the tub-to-wall relationship that eliminated the ledge in the original plan).
- **A missing ceiling backing/blocking provision for a sliding (купе) wardrobe door forced a far more expensive full custom cabinet instead** — the client forgot to include a ceiling mounting provision during rough-in; by the time this was discovered, the ceiling was already finished, making a sliding-door system impossible without redoing finished work, so a full built-in cabinet was substituted at higher cost. **General rule: sliding-door ceiling/floor track provisions must be locked in before ceiling finish work, not treated as a detail that can be added later.**
- **Windowsill lateral overhang ("ears"): 4cm (40mm) on each side is the standard** — a specific numeric spec for how far a sill should extend past the window opening on both sides, not previously recorded in this store this precisely.
- **Plaster material must be checked for substrate compatibility with paz-greben (tongue-and-groove gas-block) walls specifically** — a documented case had the wrong plaster type applied to this substrate, which detached after roughly 6 months; the source notes this is a real risk to catch *before* tiling over it, since discovering the failure after tiling means paying for both the tile removal and redo. **General rule: verify plaster-to-substrate compatibility for gas-block walls before applying, and definitely before any tile goes over it.**
- **A DIY-built wood slat wall/ceiling panel system can be significantly heavier than expected, especially when extended onto a ceiling** — a documented case found the panels "titanically" heavy to mount overhead, and the real cost of that choice surfaced later: a leak from the unit above required fully disassembling and remounting the heavy overhead slat installation for repair access. **General caution: verify a decorative slat panel system's actual weight and mounting method before committing, especially for any ceiling application, since a heavy overhead installation is both harder to install safely and harder to service later.**
- **Keep safety/reliability-critical controls (a room's main light switch, a WC/bathroom's light control) on physical, non-networked hardware — reserve voice/app/smart-home control for genuinely optional comfort features only.** The source's explicit reasoning, stated with real personal conviction: if a basic function (turning on a light to see, in this case a kitchen backsplash light on a voice assistant) depends on an internet connection, a service outage means you can't perform an ordinary daily task. A specific real anecdote: the household "used the voice-controlled backsplash light effectively twice — once when it was installed, once when we found someone dumb enough to buy it from us" — presented as evidence that voice-only control for something this basic wasn't actually wanted once the novelty wore off.
