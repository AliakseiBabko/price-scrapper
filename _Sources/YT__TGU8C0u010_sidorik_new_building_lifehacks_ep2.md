## Cost Drivers / Labor Prices — Construction-Phase DIY Tools

- **Heavy-duty DIY hand cart (тележка) for moving renovation debris/
  materials**: built from 15×15 mm square steel tube frame (welded),
  4× swivel casters (125 mm diameter, rubber-tired — chosen specifically
  so wheels don't mark stairwell/lobby tile), a DSP/particleboard deck
  reused from an old wardrobe. Sized deliberately to fit in a standard
  elevator while carrying 3 bags of dry mix (e.g. plaster) side by side.
  **Total material cost ≈$25 (stated directly in USD by the speaker), ≈3
  hours labor** — a comparable ready-made cart is cited as costing roughly
  **5× more** than the self-built version. **Practical load ceiling: no
  more than 150-200 kg movable by one person alone**, stated as the
  speaker's own tested experience regardless of the cart's structural
  capacity — sizing a cart larger than this is called impractical
  (maneuverability suffers with no real added benefit). Real-world
  validation: by the time of filming, the cart had already moved roughly
  **25 tonnes** of debris/materials in and out of the apartment.
- **Construction-phase handwash station (умывальник)**: built from a new
  stainless-steel sink (a used sink/faucet was priced out and found not
  worth buying secondhand once compared to new), an inexpensive Chinese
  faucet ("недорогой китайский, но свою функцию выполняет"), and a simple
  wooden-batten frame at a custom-fit **87 cm** working height, connected
  to the developer's pre-capped water and drain stub-outs. Designed to be
  mobile/disassemblable so the frame can be reused on a future project.
  A labor+materials cost table is shown on-screen (not read aloud in
  enough detail to transcribe exact figures) with the stated methodology
  above (Minsk-average hourly finisher rate for the labor portion).
  **Practitioner's own stated rationale**: a proper handwash setup
  materially improves on-site hygiene/tool cleanliness and crew
  motivation/productivity compared to washing hands or tools from a hose
  stuck in a toilet bowl — treated as a real productivity lever, not just
  a comfort upgrade.

## Materials — Protective Prep Before Renovation

- **PVC protective tape, "Blue Dolphin" brand, red color, for
  window/door/radiator masking**: leaves no adhesive residue even after
  extended exposure — speaker's own real-world test on a prior
  (khrushchevka) project left this tape on a sun-facing window for **over
  10 months** with zero adhesive residue on removal. Lets a renovation
  mask windows/doors/radiators **once for the whole project**, instead of
  the more common practice of re-taping several times as ordinary painter's
  tape degrades or leaves residue. Technique: construction film taped to
  the window mid-pane with small tape tabs, then taped around the full
  perimeter with the same PVC tape; window handles individually wrapped in
  film then taped, kept operable (window must still open during the
  renovation); radiators fully wrapped in film, taped around the
  perimeter with ordinary painter's tape (not necessarily the same PVC
  tape).
- **Sun-facing windows get an additional layer of foil (фольга) over the
  masking film**, specifically to block heat gain into the apartment
  during hot-weather work and to prevent the sun from washing out a laser
  level's beam (also incidentally interferes with filming, per the
  speaker).
- **Dust-sensitive item storage during demolition**: an apartment with a
  PVC-glazed loggia functions as an effectively airtight side room —
  tools and belongings are moved there before demolition starts, avoiding
  the need to wipe dust off every tool afterward. Cited as a specific
  advantage of new-build apartments (which the speaker says reliably have
  a glazed loggia) over older housing stock.

## Material Prices — Debris/Garbage Bags

- **Used (secondhand) polypropylene bags — originally packaging for
  grain, sugar, or bulk polypropylene raw material — are recommended over
  new construction-store garbage bags for demolition debris**, since the
  bags are discarded with the debris regardless of original purpose.
  **Real price comparison stated directly**: a comparable good-quality,
  thick, used white bag costs **0.25 BYN (kopecks)** bought secondhand
  online vs. **0.69 BYN** for the same-size new bag at a building-supply
  store — roughly **2.8× cheaper**. ≈**$0.10 vs. ≈$0.28** (USD-equivalent,
  trailing-6-month BYN/USD average before 2020-09-13, 2.4627 BYN/USD per
  `tools/pricing/currency_converter.py`; note the bucket-rounding
  convention doesn't apply meaningfully at this sub-dollar scale, so exact
  cents are kept). For this specific renovation's stated need of **600
  bags**, buying used instead of new saves roughly **$108** (600 × ~$0.18
  difference) — speaker calls this "significant" at this volume. On this
  particular project the speaker got lucky and received nearly-new bags
  from the secondhand source.

## Planning Rules — Pre-Renovation Administrative Prep

- **Insure the apartment and neighbor-liability coverage *before* starting
  renovation, not after** — explicit counter to the intuitive instinct to
  insure only once the renovation is finished. Rationale: renovation work
  is a real, common cause of flooding incidents in both directions (a
  neighbor's water damage ruining your already-finished drywall
  ceiling/skim coat, or your own plumbing accident damaging a neighbor's
  unit below) — the liability-to-neighbors coverage is called out as the
  more important half of the policy.
- **Obtain the building's own communication/utility routing schematic
  from the HOA/condo association (товарищество) before starting work** —
  specifically the heating-pipe routing under the screed — plus physical
  keys to the electrical distribution panel and the heating/thermal-
  network panel, before beginning any demolition or rough-in work.
