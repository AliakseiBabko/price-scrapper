## ⚠️ Stretch Ceilings — the Argument This Vault Was Missing

This is the important item, because it **materially changes a Perspectives block already on `13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Ceilings_Guide.md`.** That block (written 2026-08-30 from `0WXiKNXPD_0`) recorded her flood argument as the weak half — correctly, since RemProektMD have a documented real flood fully contained by a PVC film — and her fit-out sequencing-damage point as her only strong contribution. **She now supplies a third argument that is stronger than either, and it is physical and checkable:**

- **⚠️ Sag is unavoidable and manufacturers publish it: "производители полотен так и пишут — провисание 2,5%."** Her worked consequence: **on a room already exceeding 8 m², centre sag can reach 7 cm.**
- **The cause is gravity acting on a soft material** — so it is independent of installer skill, of membrane price, and of whether an air vent is fitted behind the ceiling. She reports attending 4–5 manufacturer presentations of premium European membranes, at prices exceeding a plasterboard ceiling, and still seeing the defect.
- **Sag is most visible where the membrane is drawn in around spots and tracks** — those junctions are where the material distorts, and she describes them as what "выдаёт всю эту бутафорию."
- **A stretch ceiling can't be painted** (fabric can, but she calls it laborious, expensive and pointless), **can't be locally restored**, and **you can't move a pendant later** if you reposition the table beneath it.

**Her recommended cheap alternative — and note it is cheaper than plasterboard, not just than stretch:** plaster the existing structural slab, **chase the wiring into that plaster**, and use neat surface-mounted fixtures. "Дешевле намного, чем ГКЛ потолок, но выглядит намного аккуратнее."

## Cost Drivers — A Budget-Priority Rule

**⚠️ Spend on the rough and finish work first; buy furniture later.** "Если у вас нет нескольких десятков миллионов рублей на ремонт, то лучше потратьтесь на отделку… лучше не докупить диванчик, не закупить пока что кровать, а вложиться действительно в качественную отделку." Her stated reason is reversibility: **furniture can be bought at any time; finishes cannot easily be stripped out and redone.** A clean statement of an irreversibility-ranked budgeting heuristic.

**A real budget-project reference point**: on a small rental flat where they deliberately economised, they used **porcelain at about 1,000 RUB/m² (≈$10/m²)** and laminate — **but still refused both a stretch ceiling and wallpaper**, plastering the slab instead. ## Materials — Wallpaper, Laminate, Grout

- **⚠️ Wallpaper: designers won't specify it** except locally and deliberately (a headboard, in genuinely good paper with proper substrate prep). Reasons: **it was never designed to be a durable covering** and will start lifting at joints and corners; and **it cannot be restored**. Paint can — she gives her own worked example of plastering and repainting a single section over a settlement crack with no visible difference.
- **⚠️ Myth corrected: wallpaper does not hide cracks — it accentuates them.** A settling building cracks; wallpaper draws the eye straight to those places and nothing can then be done. On reinforcing mesh she is dismissive: for real cracks it is "как пластырь на дыру в асфальте."
- **Laminate**: expands and contracts with temperature and humidity; with incorrect perimeter gaps you get either a hump in the middle of the room or gaps between planks that collect dust and water. **The plank joints are the vulnerable point** and swell into warped plastic. Reinforces the Round 1 no-cork-no-sealant rule from `avRNMkNdOBs`.
- **⚠️ Cement grout cracks over a heated floor or a heated wall** — a failure mode she says people routinely miss. Cracks then admit water and dirt and cannot be cleaned out. Separately, cement grout grows mould in a bathroom with poor extraction. **Workers prefer it because it is easier to work than epoxy**, and "рабочий сделал и ушёл — не ему там жить," so designers have to argue for epoxy. **Directly relevant to this project's heated-wall discussion** (see the Round 2 microcement/heated-wall material), though not to underfloor heating, which this project has ruled out.

## Doors — Reverse Swing, in Full

⚠️ **A numeric conflict with Round 2 that must not be silently resolved.** Here she says a reverse-swing door costs **"процентов на 30"** more. In `UDrpyZE_V38` (Round 2, the bill-of-quantities video) she says **20%**. Same practitioner, same claim, two figures. **Both are recorded; neither is averaged.** What *is* consistent across both videos is the **ordering**: a floor-to-ceiling door is a smaller premium than a reverse-swing one — here stated as "наценка за высокие двери куда меньше, чем наценка за обратное открывание," and in Round 2 as +15% vs +20%. **Her advice follows from the ordering, not the absolute figure: prefer a floor-to-ceiling door over a reverse-swing one.**

Three further costs beyond price, which Round 2 did not cover:

- **⚠️ It looks different.** On a concealed door, reverse swing shows visible steps on the leaf when open. With some makers it differs **even closed**, because the leaf sits in an aluminium frame visible from the face side — **which then forces the other doors on the same wall into aluminium frames too**, with extra markups on each.
- **⚠️ Functionally worse**: entering the room you have less space for yourself. Direct swing — push in on entry, pull on exit — is more comfortable, especially into small rooms.
- **⚠️ Switch placement.** A door opening inward covers the wall where switches would sit. She gives a worked case where inward opening would have made their switch position impossible, and the worst case is a door that blocks the only wall available. Mounting switches on a wardrobe end panel is possible but the electrician will not thank you.

## Storage — Sliding Wardrobe Fronts

Avoided: they consume space (either circulation or interior storage depth), and the doors don't sit in one plane. **Use a co-planar system instead** — restating `QES02ExtmAg` from Round 2, same channel.
