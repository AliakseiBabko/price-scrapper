# Source Note — "Большая Стройка", full loggia rebuild with a partitioned storage-closet zone

## Balcony / Loggia — Independent Corroboration

1. **Independently corroborates Zemskov's "never use jamb-extension
   (добор) profiles" rule** (see
   [[10_Balcony/analysis/Glazing_and_Structural_Work|Glazing & Structural Work]])
   — a second, unrelated crew reaches the same practical conclusion with
   *different* stated reasoning: a добор filler profile is framed as
   both a real leak-risk (depends entirely on a good foam/sealant bond
   at an extra joint that "not every installer can seal properly") and
   simply more expensive than the alternative — a rigid external corner-
   angle bracket, glued/foamed in place, that becomes structurally
   sufficient once insulation and finish are applied over it. Framed as
   this crew's own deliberate preference, not an absolute rule ("with a
   jamb-extension it also works, but sometimes issues come up... in this
   case it's practically excluded").
2. **Independently corroborates the seam-staggering principle**: the
   crew's second insulation layer is explicitly checkerboard-staggered
   against the first layer's seams "specifically to rule out any random
   drafts" — the same underlying logic as Petrishin's and Forcemontage's
   staggered-seam rules on
   [[10_Balcony/analysis/Insulation_and_Heating|Insulation & Heating]],
   a third independent crew reaching it.
3. **Independently corroborates the no-cold-bridge-from-furring principle**
   with an added mechanism explanation: furring battens are foam-sealed
   around their fasteners, and — critically — the finish material is
   built up so it never directly touches the metal frame/furring once
   insulation is in place, which the crew states directly rules out a
   cold bridge at that junction.

## Balcony / Loggia — New Technique: Steel-Tube Parapet Reinforcement

4. **Genuinely different structural-reinforcement method** from
   Zemskov's gas-block masonry support course (same page): this crew
   built an all-steel-tube reinforced parapet instead — a thick-walled
   25×50mm profile tube welded directly to the base as a vertical brace
   (its job specifically to stop in/out racking of the rail, not side-to-
   side), topped by a 40×40mm horizontal tube that becomes the actual
   glazing-frame fixing point. Presented as a deliberate strength upgrade
   over the original railing, not merely a replacement-in-kind.

## Balcony / Loggia — New Technique: Handling a Badly-Sloped Slab

5. **Furring-depth technique for an extreme floor slope**, distinct from
   Zemskov's "level only the top masonry course" method: this loggia's
   slab varied from ~0cm to ~15cm buildup across its run. The crew
   resolved it by varying furring (обрешётка) depth instead of masonry —
   single furring where the gap was small, double where it was larger,
   and nearly triple at the most extreme point — while keeping the old
   windowsill mounting hooks as a reference point for setting the
   plywood subfloor's level.

## Balcony / Loggia — Storage-Closet Partition (Target Framing for This Batch)

6. **A distinct unheated storage nook, walled off from the heated main
   loggia zone**: rather than insulating/heating the entire loggia, the
   crew built a partition wall separating a small end section (left
   deliberately unimproved/unheated) that keeps functioning as cold
   storage, from the rest of the loggia which gets the full insulation/
   heating/finish treatment. Existing stored items in that nook were
   left in place rather than removed during the renovation — a real
   worked example of splitting one loggia into "warm usable zone" +
   "cold storage zone" rather than treating the whole footprint
   uniformly.

## Balcony / Loggia — Other Practical Notes

7. **Reused an old glazing frame at the client's request** rather than
   scrapping it, cut into sections for possible reuse elsewhere — framed
   as an unusual but real request the crew accommodated.
8. **Foam-adhesive strength anecdote**: removing an old windowsill that
   had been foam-glued to brick tore the surrounding mortar/brick joint
   before the foam bond itself failed ("кладка проиграла пене" — the
   masonry lost to the foam) — the crew's own conclusion is that foam
   adhesive is strong enough to reset a displaced/loose brick course
   without mortar for small-scale repairs (not something they'd rely on
   at full building-construction scale, their own explicit caveat).
9. **⚠️ Baseboard/door-swing conflict, a lesson for planning**: the crew
   discovered only at baseboard-installation time that the new door's
   swing arc would strike a continuous baseboard run — had to notch/plug
   the baseboard at that point and substitute a floor transition strip
   instead. Recorded explicitly as something they'd plan for earlier next
   time (door clearance vs. baseboard run) rather than catching it this
   late.
