## Clearances and dimensions — the hard numbers

| Rule | Figure |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hob to adjacent furniture or fridge** | **minimum 300 mm** |
| **Countertop to upper cabinets** | **500–600 mm, no less** |
| **Sink to hob** | **600 mm absolute minimum (his own home); 1,000 mm ideal** |
| Standard lower-cabinet depth | minimum 450 mm |
| Standard upper-tier depth (first tier) | 300 mm |
| **Free air above a free-standing fridge fitted into a furniture niche** | **300 mm, up to 500 mm** |
| Standard kitchen heights available | 2,210 mm; tallest standard 2,400 mm — **three-metre standard kitchens do not exist** |

- **The 300 mm hob clearance has a stated failure mode, not just a number**: too
  cramped to cook, and you permanently soil the fridge side or the cabinet housing it
  with food and steam. (Someone suggested gluing plastic to that wall; he says it
  looks poor.)
- **The sink-to-hob metre is justified by the task**: room to lay out food, set a
  board, cut, and put down plates and pans. His fallback if the run is tight: **put
  the dishwasher between them** — there is worktop above it anyway — plus a pull-out
  drawer or **карго** for glasses.
- **Sink in the corner: never put the bowl in the corner.** You may intrude into the
  corner with the drainer «ухо», but not the bowl — otherwise it is awkward to
  approach and to wash up in.
- **Work triangle: fridge → sink → hob, in that order, with the fridge nearest the
  kitchen entrance.** The sequence is derived from the task: unload shopping straight
  into the fridge, take food out to the sink to wash, prep in the run between sink and
  hob, then cook.
- **Sink in front of a window: check the window's opening arc against the mixer.**
  Named as the classic error of beginner designers and of clients doing a house
  without one — the casement fouls the tap. Fixes: offset the tap left, place it to the
  side, or an Italian mixer that **retracts into the sink on a double press**. Cheap
  fix: a tap that simply swivels aside.

## Costing ratios — the transferable part

- **⚠️ Installation is typically ~10% of the kitchen's cost — and must be agreed
  before ordering.** The named failure: unscrupulous kitchen firms appear on site
  after the client has spent everything on appliances and furniture, sometimes on
  credit, and present a bill well above 10% plus «допы». **Assembly quality can also
  ruin or improve the kitchen**, so it is not a line to buy blind.
- **⚠️ Anything non-standard costs at least 40% more.** Sticking to standard hanging
  cabinets, standard low cabinets and standard pencil units saves «порядка 40%».
- **One large item is cheaper than several small ones** — a single tall pencil unit
  beats several wall cabinets for the same storage.
- **A ready-made cabinet is «на порядок дешевле» than a made-to-order one** — his
  advised fallback where a corner sink is unavoidable.
- **A built-in microwave costs 2–3× a free-standing one on a shelf.** Same argument
  applies to coffee machines.
- **Dark ДСП or PVC facades are 2–3× cheaper than matte enamel or deep-matte Fenix** —
  «но смотреться будет, конечно же, тоже дешевле». Stated plainly rather than sold.
- **Gloss and matte paint on MDF cost about the same**, so choosing matte is free;
  the saving is switching MDF to ДСП.
- **European vs Chinese hardware: up to ~10× cheaper.**
- **Fit appliances into ready standard modules**: a 400 mm sink into a 600 mm module;
  a nominally 600 mm hob (actually ~550) also sits in a ready 600 module.

## ⚠️ The hardware rule — spend by frequency of use, not uniformly

The clearest reusable heuristic in the source, and he supports it with his own
five-year experience rather than asserting it:

- **Buy good hardware where you use it daily — fridge, dishwasher. Economise on a
  drawer you open once a year and there will be no problem.**
- Cheap hardware — «фольга», as they call it — fails fast.
- **Concrete personal evidence on push-open (типон / tip-on), stated against his own
  prior opinion**: he hated them; his furniture makers persuaded him to try a good
  **Blum** tip-on; **one door, eighteen months, in constant use, no problems.** In his
  previous flat cheap tip-ons «постоянно ломались, вылетали, отскакивали». So the
  mechanism type was never the problem — the grade was.
- **Where you cannot economise at all: газлифты** (he says so directly), **sockets**,
  and **worktop LED lighting**.

## Facades, worktops and materials

- **⚠️ The two-tier upper cabinet is a dated detail he has abandoned.** It was
  fashionable ~2020 (first tier 300 mm deep, second overhanging to pencil-unit depth).
  It works **only** on a symmetrical П-shaped kitchen in one colour and style. He now
  makes uppers **one single depth** to stretch the perceived ceiling height. Reasons
  against: deep upper boxes bury their contents, **upper boxes can never be pull-out
  drawers**, and **the worst aesthetic failure is a step that terminates showing an
  exposed end (торец)**. Saving: open shelves instead of facades — «только не делайте
  много открытых полок», which produces clutter.
- **Dark kitchen with no accents reads as oppressive.** Fix: add contrast, a light or
  bright patch — «избавляйтесь от монотонных, больших давящих объёмов». Dark works
  best in matte enamel or deep-matte **Fenix**.
- **Gloss shows everything**: fingerprints, distortions, micro-scratches catching
  sunlight, and colour cast from luminaires. **Dark plus gloss is the worst
  combination.**
- **⚠️ A test the buyer can perform in the showroom: ask to touch the samples and see
  which retains marks.** «Это будет наглядно показывать, как будет проходить жизнь на
  вашей кухне.»
- **Worktop and backsplash material must be chosen against whether you actually
  cook.** Oak, white marble or solid timber on both surfaces is a designer's move that
  fails in a working kitchen; **white or black marble absorbs everything**, and
  restoring a solid-timber or marble worktop is «и дорого, и ненадолго, и
  бессмысленно». Saving: **ДСП or компакт-плита for both worktop and backsplash** —
  cheapest, practical, «будет жить вечно».
- **Backsplash saving that avoids a trade: glue glass** instead of buying expensive
  porcelain stoneware and hiring a tiler — withstands any dirt and cleans easily.

## Drawer and door operation — check before cutting

- **⚠️ The check has a specific timing and a specific artefact.** Before the facades
  are sent for cutting, **the supplier must send you the production drawing of your
  kitchen** — the one they will cut from. **Opening directions are marked with small
  triangles.** On that plan verify: doors do not clash with each other, and there is
  clearance for **handle depth** (a surface handle has depth that can foul the
  adjacent facade as the door swings).
- Corner facade junctions often need small filler pieces — **доборные глухари** — so
  the hinges can actually work.
- Named failure modes: a drawer that hits another drawer, a handle that strikes and
  scratches the facade, and a lift-up flap you cannot close.
- **Lift-up (лифты) fittings on high upper cabinets are a specific ergonomic trap** —
  the flap opens upward out of reach and you need a stool. Affects people of standard
  height with a tall kitchen. Use conventional opening, or gas struts including remote-
  or button-operated versions.
- Saving on handles: flea-market vintage handles for a loft kitchen cost almost
  nothing; **a milled finger-pull in solid timber is the most expensive option**, and a
  **metal profile integrated into ДСП is much cheaper**. **Ask the supplier to cost
  ordinary, integrated and profile variants side by side so the budget is comparable.**

## Appliances, services and the ordering sequence

- **⚠️ Start the kitchen design from appliance selection, not from the kitchen.** Two
  reasons given: the kitchen is fabricated to the specific appliances and adjusted to
  them, so an appliance that arrives and does not fit is a serious problem; and
  **appliances now often cost more than the kitchen itself**, so leaving them last
  risks running out of budget. Buy or at least definitively select them first, and
  check they will not be discontinued before delivery.
- **His own honest caveat on that advice: «стоимость не всегда гарантия долгожительства
  вашей бытовой техники.»** Prioritise — e.g. a better dishwasher that will not damage
  crockery and lasts longer, economising on the freezer or the hob.
- **⚠️ Built-in appliances need a ventilation gap, and this is where the space
  actually goes.** Below a built-in fridge there must be **perforation** so air enters
  and convects; perforation above will not hurt. Without it «очень большая вероятность,
  что ваш холодильник прослужит гораздо меньше». Same concern for ovens venting steam
  forward. **Consult the appliance supplier on how to build the furniture around it.**
- **Under-sink space is now contested and must be planned as a whole**: the bin,
  plus a **измельчитель** (waste disposal, usually central and bulky — one
  manufacturer offsets the drain valve to the sink corner specifically so the disposal
  can be pushed into the corner), plus **drinking-water filters**, and with reverse
  osmosis and its tanks there is effectively no room left for a bin.
- Large appliances should all be built in; the small-appliance collection needs its
  own dedicated pencil unit rather than an antresol — «если у вас что-то стоит в
  неудобном месте, это сразу же заканчивается бардаком».
- **Sockets: count everything you use in your current kitchen, then add spares.**
  Simple rule offered — **a block of two or three sockets every metre**. Too many looks
  odd but is better than too few. Only saving available is surface-mounted instead of
  flush, which looks worse; he says sockets are the one thing not to economise on.
- **Worktop lighting is mandatory** — LED tape, profile, diffuser; «это сейчас ничего
  не стоит… нет ничего ужаснее, чем готовить в темноте». Particularly where upper
  cabinets cast shadow. Budget fallback: a few surface-mounted spots aimed at the prep
  area, avoiding an expensive driver.

## Storage inside the carcass

- **Pull-out drawers everywhere they are possible in lower cabinets**, because at
  450 mm depth a fixed shelf buries its contents — «там лежат какие-то новогодние
  украшения, и потом о них вспоминают, или вообще никогда не вспоминают».
  **⚠️ He flags honestly that this is the one piece of advice with no saving attached**
  — pull-out hardware significantly raises the price and a plain shelf is always
  cheaper.
- **Corner sections get "buried" between two abutting runs** — wall, wall, and a
  глухарь between them. Use a mechanism or **карусели** to retrieve corner contents in
  one movement. **His own cheaper alternative, which he uses at home: a plain shelf
  running deep into the corner for rarely-used items** — dishwasher salt and rinse aid
  — and it works only because he remembers what is there.
- Upper cabinets set too high create space that is never used and contents that are
  buried permanently; he lived with антресоли and «реально находил там постоянно
  какие-то клады из прошлого».

## The closing rule

**«Хорошо спроектированная кухня — это экономия на переделках. Сначала сделайте план
и только потом приступайте к покупке.»**
