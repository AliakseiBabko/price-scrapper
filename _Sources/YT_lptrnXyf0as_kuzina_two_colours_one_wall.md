## Design Concept — the objection she starts from, and the reframe

- **⚠️ The client objection is specific and cultural: schools and hospitals painted the lower wall green and the upper white, so clients say they do not want to go back to school.** She notes that **if the school did it properly it was probably about one third to two thirds** — i.e. the objection is to the association, not the proportion.
- **⚠️ Her reframe, which is the interesting move: private housing increasingly borrows devices from bars and restaurants**, and this is one of them. Read the same wall as *"мы на самом деле сидим за барной стойкой"* rather than as a school corridor **and it acquires a completely different register.** The device is unchanged; only the reference is.

## Design Concept — ⚠️ the proportion, and the boundaries it must not hit

- **⚠️ Divide one third to two thirds. Her justification is the golden section**, hedged honestly: *"не могу сказать, что это прямо единственный идеальный вариант, но ваш глаз будет постоянно сталкиваться именно с этой пропорцией,"* so the division feels comfortable.
- **It works in either direction**: lower third darker with the upper two thirds lighter; **or** lower two thirds darker or brighter with a calmer upper third — **and that upper third can be allowed to flow onto the ceiling.**

**⚠️ The collision rules are the load-bearing content, and they are the part a plan gets wrong:**

- **The upper boundary must not coincide with the top of the doors.** Fix by lowering the boundary, or by raising the doors if the ceilings allow — **she prefers lowering.** **Worked figure: with a 2100 door, run the boundary at roughly 1800 from finished floor.**
- **The lower boundary must not coincide with low furniture standing along the wall.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ the bathroom case, with dimensions

The technique is common in bathrooms — **tile below, and increasingly wallpaper or paint above.** Two collisions to plan around:

- **⚠️ The lower boundary at roughly one third must not land at the basin height. Worked: a basin at 800, plus roughly 200 of splashback above it, puts the boundary at about 1000 from the floor.**
- **⚠️ The gap between basin and mirror is usually small.** Where the division carries a depth change — between tile and paint — or a moulding, **that detail should end up *under* the mirror. Bring the mirror as close as possible down to the boundary.**
- **⚠️ Her stated reason for hiring a designer, which is a rare concrete statement of what the drawing stage buys**: *"именно для этого вы нанимаете дизайнера, который может на развёртке, на чертеже перепроверить пропорции"* — mirror size, vanity size, where the boundary runs, and whether it all lands.

## Design Concept — ⚠️ the furniture trick

**Where tall furniture crosses the boundary, paint the furniture in the two colours as well — you dissolve it into the wall plane.**

- **Especially effective for built-in furniture**, which already sits in the wall plane.
- **But also works for a freestanding buffet or étagère standing in front of the wall** — paint its lower part in the lower register and its upper part in the upper one.

## Design Concept — which two colours

Three schemes she names:

1. **The classic: darker below, lighter above.** It **grounds the interior at the bottom while the top reads more airy and more voluminous.**
2. **⚠️ Two different colours at the same lightness** — her example is light green with light blue. **The check: photograph it and the wall reads almost uniformly grey.** *(The same greyscale test she uses in `yU2zKPpDghM` for mixing warm and cold, applied here.)*
3. **One colour split into a darker, more saturated part and a lighter, paler part.**

## Design Concept — scope and materials

- **⚠️ It can run round every wall in the room, or on one wall only — but dividing a *short* wall reads more harmoniously than a long one.** Her named bad case: **3 m ceilings and a 5 m long wall, divided horizontally — "не очень хорошее решение."**
- **⚠️ It is not only paint plus paint.** Also: **wallpaper above with paint below**; **wallpaper above with darker paint below carried onto the skirting and the doors** — which she calls *"тоже очень красиво"*; **wall panels below matched in colour to the wallpaper**; or **tile.**
- **⚠️ With tile or wall panels, always resolve the edge («кромка») — how the end of the material is closed.**
- **⚠️ With wallpaper plus paint, separate the two zones with a moulding.** Two reasons, both practical: **you do not see the wallpaper's cut edge, and it cannot start to peel.**

## Mistakes / Warnings

- Letting the boundary coincide with the door head, with low furniture along the wall, or with the basin.
- Leaving the moulding or depth-change detail sitting above the mirror rather than beneath it.
- Dividing a long wall horizontally.
- Butting wallpaper against paint without a moulding.
- Leaving a tile or panel edge unresolved.
