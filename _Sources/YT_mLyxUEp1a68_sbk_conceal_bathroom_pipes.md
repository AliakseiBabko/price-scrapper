## Plumbing / Bathroom — Recessing an Exposed Towel-Warmer Riser and Transit Pipe

- **⚠️ Named defect**: a steel towel-warmer riser stack that stands proud of the wall (rather than being recessed) blocks flush tile installation and forces a visible boxed-in or exposed pipe run — commonly found standing well clear of the wall face in developer-standard bathrooms.
- **⚠️ Named recessing technique**: chase (штробить) the wall to create a cavity, cut the existing riser stack, weld or press-fit (опрессовка) a sleeve/coupling at the cut point (access to this joint is required regardless of method), then route the towel-warmer branch and the riser continuation fully inside the wall cavity — eliminating the external boxed-in pipe run entirely.
- **⚠️ Transit-pipe rerouting to a drop ceiling**: where the transit hot-water line can't be fully recessed into a wall (e.g. it needs to reach a point across the room), route it up and hide it above a drop/stretch ceiling instead, running it along the ceiling and back down behind the toilet — trading a wall chase for ceiling void space where wall thickness or structure doesn't allow full recessing.
- **⚠️ Named cost breakdown**: **≈15,000 RUB (≈$200) for a welder/pipefitter's labor** plus **≈15,000 RUB (≈$200) for consumables and demolition work**, totaling **≈30,000 RUB (≈$400)** for this recessing work.
- **Named inferior alternative, explicitly discouraged**: building a furniture-style box (мебельный короб) around the exposed pipes is possible but the source states it typically looks poor compared to fully recessing the pipe — framed as a fallback, not a real solution, when the ≈30,000 RUB recessing work isn't in budget.
