## Planning Rules — the governing idea: an unchanging "base" specified for the oldest future occupant

**⚠️ This is the whole source in one rule, and it is a sequencing argument rather than a taste one.**

- **Her premise, stated as a comparison worth keeping**: adults typically live in a bought apartment **at least 10 years** with an essentially unchanged interior — same bed in the same place, same wardrobe. **A kids' room in the same 10 years can go through three or four rounds of change.**
- **⚠️ Therefore: build a base that does not change, and specify that base for a senior schoolchild or student — even if the child is currently an infant.** Her reasoning: **from roughly 10–12 children are big enough to use more or less adult desks, and study becomes their main occupation in the room.**
- **⚠️ She is explicit that "base" means infrastructure, not styling** — "я говорю не про дизайн, не про рюшечки." The base is:
  - **Lighting positions**: general ambient light; **a nightlight, treated as mandatory**; and **a separate light over the future work zone.**
  - **⚠️ Electrics and sockets placed where the desk will eventually be, even though no desk exists yet** — plus sockets where a laptop and a phone will later be charged. **And more of them than seem necessary.** She notes **childproof sockets exist that are safe even if a child pushes two fingers in**, which is what makes early installation viable.
  - **Neutral base finishes**: light grey walls, a neutral-toned board floor (engineered board, or laminate "хоть на худой конец"), **white doors and baseboards** — explicitly **not** wallpaper with cars, flowers or fairies, because that is the cheapest thing to add later with decor.
  - **⚠️ A planned place for a large built-in wardrobe or dressing room from the outset — with the internal fit-out treated as changeable and the location as fixed.** Rails and shelves go higher or lower as the child grows; **"место для шкафа должно быть всегда."**
- **⚠️ The cost logic that justifies all of it: "переклеивать обои и перештрабливать электрику — это гемор."** Pendants, textiles, bedspreads, beds of different sizes, desks and chairs can be swapped twenty times cheaply. **Finishes and buried electrics cannot.** **The base must therefore also be good quality from the start.**
- **⚠️ Ventilation: fit a breather (бризер) if the apartment has no ducted ventilation system**, so the child always has fresh air without an open window and the associated worry about them catching cold. **Corroborates the breather recommendation in her balcony source from Round 1.**

## Planning Rules — the four age stages

**0 to ~3 (infant)** — "ребёнок ещё скорее кабачок и у него нет собственной идентичности," so **the room is oriented to the parent, not the child.**
- **⚠️ A place for the parent to settle the child: an armchair and a light, set up for the mother.**
- **⚠️ And, flagged by her as a controversial recommendation: a sofa bed if it fits, because a parent will end up sleeping there when the child is ill.**
- Standard infant furniture (playpen, cot, small clothes cupboard, changing table) is fine. **No sharp corners, nothing to strike or injure against.**
- **⚠️ Do not invest heavily at this stage — at 3 all of it stops fitting** and goes to charity or storage.
- **Eco/hypoallergenic and natural materials and fabrics.**
- **⚠️ Colour can be played with freely here — pink, blue, beige, whatever — but through textile, decor and small items, not by painting the walls.**

**3 to 7 (preschool)** — no desk needed; **the room's base purpose is play.**
- **Shelving for toys with boxes and drawers**, to train tidiness.
- **⚠️ A soft rug is required, because everything happens on the floor.**
- **Move off the cot**: either a bed that adjusts with the child's height, or go straight to a near-adult single or 1.5-size bed. **A good mattress specifically.**
- Children this age love games, second levels and climbing structures — fill the room with these gradually.

**7 to 12–13 (junior school)** — **the play zone contracts in favour of the work zone, and this is where the pre-planned base pays off**: "вот где нужны электрика, розетки на своих местах, светильники. Тут уже всё пригождается."
- **Growing furniture and orthopaedic children's furniture with correct backrests** — framed as protecting the child's health.
- **Fully adult storage systems now: large wardrobes, dressing rooms.**
- **⚠️ Individualisation appears as a need at this age.** The child has favourite music, films, characters, Lego. **Her recommendation: give them a wall they can hang things on, draw on, colour, and arrange their own drawings, toys and models on.**
- **⚠️ The containment lifehack, which is the practical half: localise all of it inside their room, so none of it appears elsewhere in the apartment** — "но вот в его комнате будет всё, как он хочет."

**13+ (teenager)** — **privacy is the main thing.**
- **⚠️ Her position: a door that locks from the inside**, so the person can shut out the outside world.
- **Complete reversal of priorities** — her example is a fifteen-year-old daughter who will find butterfly wallpaper insulting. **⚠️ A neutral base is what makes this survivable: black bedding, black curtains and a gloomy pendant can simply be added onto it.**
- Computer/laptop are central; a speaker or voice assistant likely. **Soundproofing is a plus.**
- **Her honest note to parents: from an interior standpoint a schoolchild's and a teenager's room differ little — what changes is that parents have to accept teenagers will keep their own space chaotic.**

**University age** — the room becomes a full adult bedroom with a work place, receiving friends and partners.

## Planning Rules — two children sharing one room

- **⚠️ Each child must have personal space, and her specific sleeping rule: when they are asleep they should not be able to see each other.** Achieved by separating the beds — **curtains or blinds around them, or a bunk bed where the upper and lower berths cannot see each other.** The method varies; **the zoning is non-optional.**
- **⚠️ Especially for opposite-sex children — and she states opposite-sex children sharing one room is advisable only up to adolescence.**
- **⚠️ Two work places, and the symmetry requirement is the additive insight: it can be one long countertop under the window, but there must be two shelf units and two chairs, symmetrically.** **"Не должно быть такого, что у одного ребёнка больше полок, вешалок. Это сразу будут обиды и разрушенные взаимоотношения."** The rule is about sibling equity, not composition.
- **For children of different ages, privacy matters more still** — and here she recommends professional planning "вплоть до сантиметра."
