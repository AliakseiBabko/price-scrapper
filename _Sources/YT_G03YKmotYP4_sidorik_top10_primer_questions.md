## Materials — What Primer Actually Does and Why It's Not Optional

- **Core mechanism**: primer is a bonding layer between a substrate and
  the next-applied material (plaster, tile adhesive, self-leveling
  compound, etc.) — it binds surface dust, reduces substrate absorbency,
  and promotes even distribution/longer service life of the material
  applied over it via adhesion.
- **Demonstrated test**: brick given 2 coats of deep-penetration primer
  ("грунтовка глубокого проникновения", Ceresit CT17) showed dramatically
  reduced water absorption vs. an untreated control brick (real-time
  water-pour demonstration, not sped up).
- **Self-leveling floor ("наливной пол") cannot be done properly without
  primer**, per the speaker — a technique that didn't exist in the same
  form in Soviet-era construction (which built without primer, relying
  instead on ad-hoc adhesion boosters like diluted PVA glue, admixed glue,
  or a cement "обрызг"/splatter-coat).
- **Absorbency-tier framework**: substrates are highly-absorbent /
  medium-absorbent / low-absorbent / non-absorbent; manufacturers print a
  substrate-specific application table on the container (e.g. a porous
  substrate gets a diluted first coat, then a full-concentration coat).
- **Over-application risk on non-absorbent substrates**: repeatedly
  coating a monolithic/non-absorbent substrate can build a continuous
  plastic-like film that can delaminate together with whatever material
  is applied over it — "more is not better," follow the manufacturer's
  specified coat count exactly.
- **Concrete-bonding primer**: on very smooth/dense substrates (bare
  monolithic concrete), use an adhesion-type "бетон-контакт"
  (concrete-contact) primer rather than a standard deep-penetration one.
  Speaker's fastest personal application method for бетон-контакт: a
  compressor + texture gun ("текстурный пистолет") — fast and
  high-quality result. (This corroborates and extends existing
  бетон-контакт coverage already on
  `12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Waterproofing_and_Plastering.md`.)

## Planning Rules — Three Application Methods, Ranked

- **Spray/airless ("безвоздушка")**: fastest and most productive method;
  suited to all work types/surfaces; downside is that anything that could
  be damaged by overspray must be masked off first.
- **Powered sprayer/paint-gun ("краскопульт")**: second-fastest; same
  masking downside as airless.
- **Roller**: second-fastest overall but the practical default for many
  jobs because nothing needs masking. Long-nap ("мохнатый") roller for
  textured surfaces (e.g. brick); short-nap velour roller for a smooth
  finish-coat substrate (e.g. putty before painting). **For floors
  specifically, roller is the recommended method** (no masking needed),
  even though spray/gun is faster on walls.
- **Brush/маклoвица**: slowest method, suited only to small areas.
  **Must not be used on a finish-coat putty substrate before painting**
  — the brush's stiff bristles scratch the putty surface and leave
  visible marks (demonstrated on camera).
- **Universal control point regardless of method**: watch for
  drips/running streaks; if they occur, immediately roll them out flat
  rather than letting them dry as a defect.

## Rules / Heuristics — When You Do and Don't Need to Re-Prime Between Coats

- **Re-priming between putty (or similar) coats is only needed if
  dust-generating work was done on the prior coat** (sanding, scraping).
  If no dusty work occurred between coats, skip the intermediate priming
  step — applies to putty and other coated materials generally.

## Mistakes / Warnings

- **"Will the plaster/tile adhesive detach along with the primer?"
  Addressed directly and resolved as a non-issue**: primer polymer
  concentration is engineered so its own adhesion matches or exceeds the
  material applied over it; manufacturers of cement-based plasters/
  adhesives *deliberately limit* that material's own adhesion strength
  ("защита от дураков"/idiot-proofing) — an overly strong plaster bond can
  tear itself off a weak substrate (e.g. aerated concrete) as it shrinks
  while curing. Primer doesn't form one continuous film either — it
  follows the substrate's microscopic surface irregularities and
  increases adhesion at that irregular-surface level, binding dust and
  making the substrate itself more "tacky." With a cement-based
  substrate/material pairing specifically, the cement crystals bond to
  the substrate directly, not to the primer layer.
- **PVA glue is not a valid primer substitute.** Primer is an engineered,
  specifically-formulated polymer blend; PVA glue is ~90-95% polyvinyl
  acetate — different chemistry, not interchangeable. Home-mixed
  substitutes shift all liability for a resulting failure (e.g. finish
  material delamination) onto the person doing the substitution; the
  finish materials at risk are often expensive enough that the speaker
  sees no real economic case for cutting the primer cost this way.

## Materials — Safety / PPE by Application Method

- **Brush or roller application**: no respirator/mask strictly required
  (primer is not described as broadly hazardous), though it has a
  noticeable smell; speaker personally wears a mask regardless as
  standard practice.
- **Spray application**: a respirator/mask **is** required, because
  airborne primer concentration builds up and gets inhaled; a painter's
  coverall is also recommended to protect clothing during spray work.

## Materials — Why "Which Primer to Buy" Confusion Exists, and Pricing Drivers

- **Same manufacturer, many near-identical SKUs, explained**: (1)
  different national manufacturing plants (Belarus, Russia, possibly
  Ukraine/elsewhere in Europe) each use their own packaging design; (2)
  different concentration formats — ready-to-use vs. concentrate (Belarus
  market specifically offers 1:2 and 1:3 concentrate ratios; a 1:9
  concentrate diluted per instructions yields a fully-diluted working
  primer); (3) ordinary marketing/packaging differentiation. **Underlying
  product quality is presented as essentially equivalent across these
  variants** — buy any of them and follow the dilution instructions.
- **Three cost drivers for primer pricing**: (1) quantity and quality of
  active polymer ingredients, (2) the testing/certification the
  manufacturer performs, (3) the warranty/guarantee terms offered. The
  cheapest primer on the shelf can perform equivalently if from a
  reputable manufacturer — cost differences track these three factors,
  not necessarily end-use performance for a given job.
- **No single truly universal primer exists for every task**, but
  deep-penetration primer is described as the closest to universal,
  covering the widest range of jobs.
