## Planning Rules — what is actually wrong with an irregular shape

- **⚠️ Two concrete costs, not an aesthetic complaint:**
  1. **Loss of square metres** — the central part of such a flat cannot really be used, serving only as circulation to the rooms around its perimeter.
  2. **Without right angles, bulky furniture can usually only go in the centre or along a single wall** — the sofa, the bed — **which severely limits the layout options available at all.**
- **Some irregularities are a find rather than a defect** — she shows distinctive window shapes used as a feature. **But the qualification is the useful part: had the client wanted neoclassical, that window shape would have had to be worked around.** An irregularity is only an asset if it happens to suit the intended direction.

## Planning Rules — check before buying, and do not believe the sales pitch

- **⚠️ Test the layout before purchase.** At least roughly place the furniture. Otherwise the discomfort appears only after moving in — **"вы можете неожиданно обнаружить себя в спальне с непрямыми углами или вообще треугольной формы,"** or a living room with rounded corners where nothing fits, or a small kitchen half-occupied by a shaft.
- **⚠️ A pre-purchase checklist to run against the developer's own plan**: rounded, obtuse or acute angles; niches and projections; beams; non-standard openings; **and any room configuration that departs from a rectangle.**
- **⚠️ A commercial warning worth keeping verbatim.** Distinguish an interesting layout you devised from **a developer's salesperson telling you the asymmetry is a feature. "Не надо на это вестись"** — the developer needs to sell, and how you use the space afterwards is your problem.
- **⚠️ And the figure that makes the point: a flat with skewed angles is not cheaper than one with right angles**, while entailing a greater loss of functional area. **"Не надо путать огрехи архитектуры с преимуществами планировки необычной квартиры."**

## Planning Rules — walls and replanning

- **⚠️ If you already own one, accept that replanning will be necessary** — she treats this as a given rather than an option.
- **⚠️ The governing objective: straighten the *habitable* volumes at the expense of circulation and storage.** "Лучше иметь кривой коридор, чем кривую спальню" — and better a crooked dressing room than a crooked living room. **Push the irregularity into the spaces where it costs least.**
- **Watch inward-projecting corners**, commonly created by pylons and ventilation ducts. Beyond breaking symmetry, **materials at that junction often come out untidy and wear faster** — a durability argument, not only a visual one. *(Consistent with the wear framing in `fSBAMSIgnBs`.)*
- **Worked example of good handling**: a pylon clad in the same furniture-board panel as the adjacent wall, helped by part of the wall and ceiling being painted a single strong colour so the corners stop reading.
- **⚠️ Combining rooms is the strongest wall-level move: the larger the room, the less noticeable the shape defect.** Her example carries at least two pylons — one along a wall, one protruding mid-room and preventing a sofa sitting flush — **but volume, good light and large windows mean the geometry stops mattering.**
- **⚠️ And the consequent furniture rule: when you combine zones and end up with an irregular room, pull the furniture away from the walls and group it by function.** Her Scandinavian example combines kitchen and living; **had they not combined, the living room would have been "кусок пиццы" taking one sofa along one wall and nothing else.**

## Furniture / Built-ins

- **⚠️ The "islands" technique, from her own project.** A living room semi-circular in plan with other irregular parts, where it was unclear what to do. **She built two independent islands**: one axis on the sofa with a centred table and two pouffes; **a second axis not tied to the architecture at all**, with two armchairs, floor lamps and another central table.
  - **⚠️ With this technique, use paired items to emphasise symmetry** — "так оно лучше читается." The pairing is what makes an invented axis legible.
- **⚠️ The alternative is to lean on the architecture instead**: a skewed or chamfered wall gives the space extra dynamism, and furniture can be oriented to it. **Scoped by length: "если у вас скос 30 сантиметров, то не нужно на него ориентировать мебель."** Her example has a skew long enough to take a good-sized sofa; the rug can run along it too, or the flooring can be laid perpendicular to that angle.
- **⚠️ Accept that non-standard furniture will be required, and know why.** In a room under **~20 m²**, **any bulky object with right angles will emphasise the room's irregular shape.** Triangular or radiused rooms will not take standard furniture at all, so pieces must be ordered to individual dimensions — **which costs more.**
- **⚠️ The cost-reduction lifehack, and it is the most practical item in the source**: a wardrobe running along an irregular wall, **divided into regular modules of roughly 60–65 cm that turn as the wall turns, with a single non-standard corner element joining the two runs.** Using standard construction elements for everything except the one joint is what brings the price down.

## Design Concept — the counter-intuitive option, and decoration

- **⚠️ "Усложнить ещё больше" — make it *more* irregular on purpose.** If a distortion already exists, adding more is not difficult, and she gives the mechanism as an analogy: **hiding a needle in a haystack, or a tree in a forest.** Not everyone likes strict, prim symmetry, and irregularity can become an occasion for play.
  - Examples: **two floor coverings whose joint turns, echoed by a matching joint on the ceiling**, so the niche reads as a volume cut into the room's volume — plus arches and vaults. Or an entrance door obviously set at an angle to the kitchen wall, which reads as a mistake **until furniture "как масло, отрезанное ножом" makes the whole configuration appropriate.**
- **Switch attention to something more interesting** — an artwork, an interesting furniture piece, or an architectural element such as a window or niche. Her example: **a pylon breaking the wall plane with an artwork placed on it deliberately, after which it stops reading as an accidental element and reads as a gallery stand acting as a background.**
- **⚠️ Optical illusions with colour, and there are two distinct moves**: bright colour to **distract** from a problem zone, **or painting the problem zone in a shade that also continues onto other zones, dissolving it into the space.** Her example does both — a strongly coloured ceiling and far wall in the same green so the inward corner is not what you notice first, plus a bright geometric rug adding dynamism.
- **Mirrors**: a large panel **undivided into diamonds or squares** reads as an extra opening, adds light and air, and distracts from the shape. Her memorable example is a whale's tail of which half is real and half reflected in a mirrored floor, so it reads as a whole whale — **and the artwork flowing from floor to wall stops you thinking about the room's shape at all.**

## Mistakes / Warnings

- **Believing a developer's "the asymmetry is a feature" pitch** — and paying no less for it.
- **Buying without test-placing furniture on the plan.**
- **Straightening a corridor at the expense of a bedroom** — the priority runs the other way.
- **Orienting furniture to a chamfer shorter than ~30 cm.**
- **Buying standard right-angled bulky furniture for an irregular room under ~20 m².**
