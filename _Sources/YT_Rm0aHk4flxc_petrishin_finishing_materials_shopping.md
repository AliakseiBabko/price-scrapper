## Rules / Heuristics — New Detail

1. **Two-copy printed design-project habit, and why physical beats
   digital for shopping trips**: designers reportedly print two copies
   of the design project — one for the builders, one for the client to
   carry to showrooms — specifically because a physical copy can be
   marked up (underlined, crossed out, receipts stapled to pages)
   without losing track the way digital files tend to get scattered
   across folders.
2. **Physical paint color fan-decks are necessary shopping tools, with
   a low-cost fallback for a non-designer**: professional color fan
   decks (named: Sherwin-Williams, NCS) let you compare a candidate
   paint color against tile/flooring samples on the spot, since color
   perception varies too much by monitor/lighting to trust digitally.
   A non-designer without access to a professional fan deck can visit a
   large building-materials hypermarket's own paint-sample chip display,
   or ask a paint retailer to lend/deposit-hold a fan deck for a couple
   of days. **Cost-saving tip**: some paint brands sell small (~300 RUB)
   trial tint cans specifically for on-site test-painting before
   committing to a full liter, cheaper than tinting a full liter can
   just to test a color.
3. **Large-format tile with a scored mid-line to visually mimic a
   smaller format, easing installation**: a 45×45cm tile scored to
   read as 22.5×22.5cm once grouted gives the smaller-format look the
   client wanted while being easier and faster for the tile-setter to
   lay than genuinely small-format tile.
4. **Grout-color selection rule and a deliberate mosaic-effect
   technique**: avoid a grout shade too close to the tile's own
   lightness — it visually merges/washes out and reads as flat/cheap;
   choosing a grout shade the same as or darker than the tile gives
   better definition. The same principle can be used deliberately in
   reverse for a mosaic tile: choosing a grout that closely matches the
   mosaic's own tone changes how the grid pattern reads overall — an
   intentional design lever, not just a match-or-clash binary choice.
5. **Decorative micro-cement/concrete-look plaster recommended over
   paint specifically for high-traffic/abrasion-prone zones** (this
   project's own example: a hallway where furniture gets moved through
   and could scuff a wall) — framed by the showroom consultant as more
   durable and, if damaged, locally repairable/restorable, unlike some
   paint finishes. A separate **anti-vandal silk-finish decorative
   plaster** was also recommended as a comparatively low-cost yet
   durable option for the same kind of high-traffic zone.
6. **Directional-lighting sample distortion warning, general to any
   textured/patterned finish sample**: a decorative-plaster or flooring
   sample displayed at an angle under a showroom's own directional
   lighting can look meaningfully different from how the same material
   reads once installed flat under the actual room's lighting —
   explicit advice to lay a flooring sample flat on an actual floor
   (and, ideally, bring a real plank home to test in-room) rather than
   trusting an angled display sample.
7. **Laminate-flooring selection criteria stated explicitly**: bevel
   type (4-sided beveled edge on every plank vs. 2-sided or none — a
   visible difference worth checking before buying), wear class
   (example selection: class 33), and thickness (8-10mm examples
   compared against a 12mm option). A textured "artificially aged" wood
   grain finish was explicitly rejected in favor of a smoother
   matte-oil-look finish once compared on a real flat sample.
8. **Wood-plastic composite decking ("терасная доска"/декинг) chosen
   for a balcony's curved bench specifically for weather-durability
   across all three non-summer seasons**, and noted as usable
   cost-effectively even for general balcony flooring, not just deck
   edges/trim. Real price point: ≈968 RUB per 3m plank (2019).
9. **Baseboard fixation reality-check, described as a repeated
   real-world finding**: manufacturer-recommended plastic mounting clips
   underperform in practice; installers instead secure baseboards with
   dedicated screw anchors ("шпильки") plus adhesive for a genuinely
   secure mount — stated as something the practitioner has "run into
   again" across projects, not a one-off.
10. **Real-time foreman consultation habit during a shopping trip**:
    when uncertain which grout brand/type to buy, the client phoned the
    site foreman directly from the showroom floor to confirm the
    recommendation (Ceresit cement-based grout) before purchasing — a
    concrete example of keeping the foreman in the material-selection
    loop rather than deciding unilaterally at the store.
