## Rules / Heuristics — Genuinely New Content

- **⚠️ Wall thickness beyond 100mm brings no additional soundproofing benefit once an interior door is present in that wall** — stated explicitly: a 100mm-thick partition *with* an interior door installed already provides more soundproofing than the door leaf itself does, meaning the door leaf (not the wall) is the binding acoustic weak point once a door opening exists in that wall. **Practical implication: don't over-invest in thickening a partition specifically for soundproofing purposes if it contains a standard interior door opening** — the extra wall thickness won't meaningfully improve on the door leaf's own (lower) sound-isolation ceiling. Distinct from, and a genuine nuance to, this store's well-established 150mm-partition-for-soundproofing figure (which applies to solid partition runs, not door-containing walls specifically).
- **⚠️ Detailed wall-positioning-near-load-bearing-columns technique, three options compared explicitly**: when a new partition needs to span between two fixed load-bearing columns, (1) positioning it centered/sandwiched directly between the columns leaves an awkward recessed pocket requiring a large wedge-shaped filler and worse room proportions; (2) positioning it flush with the columns' *outer* face creates a protruding step that then forces extending every adjacent wall segment to match, wasting significant floor area, creating a door-opening conflict, and costing extra for interior-door jamb-extension trim; (3) **the correct middle option**: position the wall's inner face flush with an already-built-out filler/adjustment segment (not either extreme), avoiding both the recess and the cascading extension problem. A genuinely detailed, three-way-compared worked example of a planning decision this store had previously only documented in simpler binary terms.
- **⚠️ Pre-purchase inspection checklist item: verify every intended room's doorway geometry can actually fit a standard door before buying.** Documented real case: a corner-to-load-bearing-column gap of only 91cm (raw, pre-finish) forced a door-opening reveal down to 55mm per side just to fit the smallest standard interior door (700mm leaf / 800mm opening) — described as leaving only two bad options: install "the most shameful door in the world" (a 600mm leaf, narrower than some WC doors), or abandon the room as a separate space entirely and make it pass-through instead. **General guidance for evaluating a prospective purchase (or an existing unit before finalizing a redesign)**: check whether every planned room's doorway geometry can genuinely fit at least a 700mm-leaf door with an acceptable reveal — a bad fixed-corner-to-column gap can force exactly this no-good-options dilemma, and it isn't fixable by redesign once the load-bearing geometry is fixed.
- **Asymmetric opening-reveal narrowing driven by independent furniture needs on each side**: a single opening's two sides were narrowed independently and for two unrelated reasons — one side narrowed specifically to fit a large wardrobe, the other side narrowed specifically to correctly position a double bed relative to the room's TV sightline (avoiding the TV ending up between two windows or off-center from the bed). **General principle: an opening's reveal can be deliberately asymmetric when each side's narrowing serves a distinct, justified furniture-placement need** — this doesn't contradict the "keep passage openings at their functional minimum" rule from `8Xy-h8cS_-s`; it's the furniture-driven-reveal-sizing case specifically, applied independently per side.
