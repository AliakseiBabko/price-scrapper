## Materials / Doors / Trim

- **Never caulk over the natural expansion gap between a door frame and
  its slope panel with matching-color sealant** — the thin dark line
  visible between a white door frame and a white slope is a deliberate,
  normal expansion/movement joint (the same kind of line exists,
  unremarked, around weatherstripping elsewhere on the same door);
  filling it solid with sealant looks tidier short-term but cracks again
  as the materials move, producing a worse result than leaving the clean
  gap alone. `confirmed`, real before/after shown on camera.
- **A soundproofing entry-door gasket (full-perimeter contour, not just a
  standard door seal) is worth the added cost specifically when the unit
  entrance sits near a noisy elevator shaft** — praised as a correct,
  deliberate design decision in this unit, not a universal requirement.

## Flooring

- **Decorative floor-material insert accents (e.g. a different-colored
  porcelain tile patch set into an otherwise uniform floor) are a
  planning mistake, not a design win**: the insert is virtually always
  later covered by a rug and furniture placed on top of it, permanently
  hiding the accent while the owner still paid for expensive fitted
  offcuts to create it. Default to full uniform-coverage flooring
  (quartz vinyl/LVT favored for seamless whole-apartment coverage)
  instead of a decorative insert. If porcelain tile genuinely must be
  used somewhere, plan its piece-by-piece layout in advance so no narrow
  cut strips result at the edges.

## Furniture / Built-ins

- **Always build a wardrobe/closet flush to the ceiling** — a gap left
  above a closet is a permanent dust collector that's invisible except by
  reaching up and feeling for it; over time it accumulates dust/allergens
  and can develop its own microbial growth the household unknowingly
  lives alongside. `confirmed`, safety/health-relevant mechanism.
- **Build a wardrobe/closet flush with the adjacent wall face, not
  protruding past it** — a protruding corner is both visually ugly and a
  genuine stubbed-toe/collision hazard in a corridor-adjacent location;
  resolve this at the design stage, not after framing.
- **Pre-check a built-in wardrobe's footprint against an adjacent door's
  full swing clearance before framing**, not just against the door
  opening's static position — a real mistake shown: a wardrobe was built
  right up against a door opening, physically obstructing normal door
  operation, because the wardrobe alcove wasn't measured against the
  door's actual swing arc early enough. Correct fix demonstrated: extend
  the wardrobe fully to the adjacent load-bearing wall, offset the door
  opening ~10cm to give the wardrobe room, and accept a somewhat narrower
  finished door — the source notes a standard 80cm-leaf interior door has
  more clearance than almost anyone actually needs, so losing ~10cm to
  resolve a wardrobe conflict is a low-cost tradeoff.
- **Sliding-wardrobe-door design must account for both open and closed
  positions of any adjacent hinged door**, so the hinged door's swing
  path never strikes the sliding panel's face — a coordination check
  between two independently-designed fixtures in the same zone.
- **Never substitute a curtain for a proper door/opening on a closet
  located off a narrow hallway** — a real safety hazard shown: two people
  passing in a narrow hallway can be pushed by the curtain's soft
  boundary into an exposed structural corner behind it, risking real
  injury. Correct alternatives: a centered swing-in door (accepts reduced
  access convenience to the space behind it); an offset door forming a
  proper L-shaped closet; or double sliding doors — **but sliding doors
  only when the opening is wider than ~175cm**, since a sliding panel's
  parked end position is otherwise visibly intrusive. `confirmed`, a
  genuinely new numeric threshold for this store's wardrobe/closet
  planning content.

## Kitchen

- **Kitchen electrical-point spec differs by zone**: the primary
  cook zone (between sink and cooktop) needs **"3 electro-points" meaning
  2 sockets plus a lighting switch**, not 3 full sockets as might be
  assumed; a secondary/auxiliary prep zone gets **3 full sockets**.
  `single-account`, but a specific, checkable spec.
- **Countertop-height vs. window-sill-height conflict**: a common mistake
  raises an entire counter run to match a window sill positioned at
  ~110cm, sacrificing the ergonomically comfortable ~90cm counter height
  and making cooking physically uncomfortable. Correct sequencing: fix
  the counter height for comfort first, then lower the sill to match it
  if the building's facade design allows lowering the sill — not the
  reverse.
- **Countertop condensation-relief holes near a window only function if
  positioned centered over the glass unit and correctly aimed** —
  otherwise they're purely decorative and do nothing to reduce window
  fogging. **Cross-reference**: this directly corroborates and extends
  `yt_qwIRfgn1Tog` (same batch, earlier same day) — that source's DIY
  windowsill-vent-drilling fix is a homeowner-facing version of the same
  underlying mechanism this source describes from the design/build side.
- **Route a kitchen's water shutoff valve / riser access away from the
  kitchen itself** (into the WC/bathroom) rather than behind a wall hatch
  inside the kitchen — avoids a hatch that's frequently installed with
  the wrong swing direction or is otherwise obtrusive in a finished
  kitchen. **Cross-reference**: complements `yt_PO9kd1cqCyA` (same batch,
  earlier same day) on utility-riser access planning, though that source
  covers a commercial project's riser-boxing demolition specifically,
  while this one covers where to route a kitchen's own shutoff access.

## Kitchen Appliances

- **Prefer a built-in (panel-integrated) refrigerator over a freestanding
  unit** — a freestanding fridge, or a decorative "niche" built around
  one, leaves a dust/dirt-collecting gap around the unit with no
  compensating benefit stated. `single-account`, stated as a blanket
  preference.

## Bathroom

- **The "gap under the tub for your feet" convention is explicitly
  obsolete** — described as an outdated 1980s-era design habit. Correct
  modern practice: tile runs flush to the floor line directly under the
  tub's front edge with grout sealing the joint, leaving no reachable
  gap — a gap there provides no real functional benefit (doesn't help
  reach the taps or clean the tub) and only collects dust/dirt/mold.
- **A tile "step" ridge insert marketed as saving bathroom floor space by
  "eating into" the tub has no real functional benefit** — presented as
  purely a dirt/grime collector; the tub itself is already spacious
  enough without it.
- **Wall tile above a tub should overhang the tub's edge substantially**
  so that wall-surface run-off water drains directly into the tub rather
  than pooling on an exposed tile ledge. Corroborates this store's
  existing waterproofing-technique content, not a new mechanism.
- **Bathroom switch-usability pattern**: place the main-light switch
  right at the doorway (naturally hit on the way out, so the light isn't
  left on) and a separate exhaust-fan switch near the toilet/sink (so a
  user doesn't need to stand up and reach the door to trigger the fan).
  **Never place the fan switch outside the bathroom itself** — cited
  specifically because a user needing the fan needs to activate it
  without leaving the room. `confirmed`, checkable usability rule.
- **Reject an unexplained "always 3 sockets near the sink" rule of
  thumb** — size actual socket count to genuine appliance/usage need
  (the source's own practice: typically 2 in one ganged plate) rather
  than defaulting to a folk convention with no stated rationale.

## Lighting

- **Bedroom lighting-temperature consistency rule**: use at most 2-3
  lighting circuits per room (primary + secondary, occasionally a third),
  and ensure every fixture across those circuits shares the **same color
  temperature** — mixing temperatures across multiple circuits (this
  video shows 4-5 different apparent temperatures in one room) reads as
  visually chaotic and uncomfortable. Extends this store's existing
  three-layer-lighting concept (`yt_OCjVd5HOcac`) with a concrete
  circuit-count ceiling and an explicit same-temperature constraint not
  previously stated that specifically.
- **Bedroom switching pattern**: the entry-point switch for main light
  should be a 2-way/pass-through circuit duplicated at the bed head (so
  the main light can be turned off from bed without walking back to the
  door); each side of the bed gets its own dedicated reading-light switch
  located at that side. Avoids the "no way to turn off the light I just
  turned on without walking across the room" scenario shown as the
  critiqued mistake.

## Windows

- **Window sash sizing must account for the finished floor height before
  the window order is placed** — the sash's lowest swing position must
  clear the finished floor covering with margin; ordering window
  dimensions before floor buildup height is finalized risks a sash that
  eventually sags enough to scrape/scratch the finished flooring.
  Genuinely new sequencing rule (order-of-operations across two
  different trades).
- **A tilt-and-turn sash's scissor hardware can be damaged by improper
  closing technique**: opening the sash past the point where its scissor
  mechanism disengages, then attempting to push it not-quite-closed,
  causes repeated impact damage to the window slope over time (shown as
  visible chip/repair marks on a real slope). A misuse-driven failure
  mode, distinct from the manufacturing-defect mechanisms already in this
  store for tilt-and-turn hardware (`yt_XFrmhhM1ogg`).
