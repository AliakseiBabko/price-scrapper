## Lighting / Walls — the conventional advice, delivered straight

- **Two problems in a small space**: fitting everything in, and stopping the flat reading as a cramped box. **The second is a natural-light question.**
- **Replace parts of walls with glass partitions.** They need **not** run floor to ceiling — a partial one **reads as an internal window**, which she finds attractive in its own right. Also: swap solid doors for glazed ones, using frosted or patterned glass where sightlines must be limited.
- **Glass blocks**, specifically for **hallways and corridors, which most often have no daylight at all.** Use relief-textured blocks where living zones need visual isolation.
- **⚠️ Zone with light-transmitting fabric instead of glass — and her reason is the useful part**: light interiors often read like a hospital, and **textile is what removes that**, adding cosiness and texture while still passing light.
- **Lighter walls reflect more light, so white walls are the best choice for a dark room.** She is candid that all-white interiors are not to her taste — *"мне такие интерьеры не очень близки"* — **but concedes the technique works**: the room stops looking cramped.
- **⚠️ She pushes back on the "matte walls only" rule** repeated online: if gloss is acceptable on decor, furniture and light fittings, there is no reason it cannot go on a wall. **"Конечно же можно, просто это сложно."** Permitted, but demanding.
- **Textured walls and concrete-effect plaster absorb more light than they reflect** — avoid where light is the priority.
- **A natural palette is enough; not everything must be white.** White walls suffice, and the result reads more homely.
- **⚠️ The furniture principle, which generalises well: fewer shadows means a lighter interior.** Hence **open-back chairs** and **upholstered furniture on high legs**.
- **⚠️ Round off corners.** Any sharp change of form casts a noticeable shadow; a rounded corner makes the shadow gradual, so the room reads lighter. *(Note this is the same move `fSBAMSIgnBs` recommends for corner wear — two independent reasons for the same detail.)*
- **Declutter, and windowsills specifically** — pots and books on the sill block the light you are trying to keep.
- **Window reveals («откосы») can be mirrored or glossy**, or finished in light glossy porcelain.
- **⚠️ Rooms with a balcony are the darkest ones**, and she names the specifically Russian aggravating factor: the balcony holds everything that did not fit in the flat. **Remove bulky items blocking the glazing, and do not hang laundry where it blocks the light.**
- **Minimise textile.** Heavy curtains, especially dark ones, weigh the interior down. Choose lighter fabrics that work on translucency, **hung without lining** — she notes this does not always look good and depends on the tailoring and the fabric. For morning sun, add roller blinds, blackouts or venetian blinds closed in the evening.
- **Other textile eats light too** — several layers of bedding or throws should come down to one.
  - **⚠️ She flags that this contradicts her own zoning advice and does not paper over it**: *"я знаю, что это противоречит тому, что я говорила о зонировании. Ну вот так получилось. Да, такая я противоречивая."* Worth recording as an acknowledged tension between two of her own rules rather than resolving it.
- **Light floors** reflect and distribute window light better.
- **Minimise ornament** — no wall ornament, no accent wall. **If you want ornament, put it in a more interesting rug instead.**

## Design Concept — the pivot, and the open question

- **⚠️ Her critique of the whole list, and the most valuable part of the source.** Follow every recommendation seriously and you will most likely get **the interior Russians commonly end up with whether or not they hired a designer**: creamy beige, no decor, no ornament, meagre textile, light floors, mirrors opposite the windows. **It will certainly maximise natural light.**
- **⚠️ Her question, left genuinely open**: *"Стоит ли борьба за естественный свет того, чтобы решать её таким способом?"* Is a more banal but brighter interior an acceptable trade, or not? She asks viewers to answer rather than asserting one.
- **Closing rule, which is the answer she does commit to**: **"недостаток света — это недостаток, но это не конец света."** Defects should be corrected by design — **but not at the expense of other parameters. Maximising natural light does not by itself produce a good interior.**

## Design Concept — her own way out

- **Contemporary meets most of the criteria above**, and **the secret is that it still has texture.**
- **⚠️ The underlying model, stated compactly: the two main design parameters are texture and form.** If light and dark cannot be played with — because everything must stay light — **only texture and form are left to work with.** The form then has to be **sculptural, non-banal and preferably flowing.**
- **⚠️ And the cost consequence, stated plainly and rarely admitted**: this route is more expensive, **but not uniformly.** *"Синяя краска или белая краска — цена примерно одинаковая, но скульптурный стульчик и обычный стульчик — цена скорее всего будет разная."* **Colour decisions are close to cost-neutral; form decisions are not.**
- **A caution attached to her own recommendation**: sculptural furniture is often made so that **it is nicer to look at than to sit on.**

## Cost Drivers — the glass-partition reality check

Given straight after recommending them, which is what makes it credible:

- **⚠️ More than one moving part means the mechanism must be chosen very carefully, and those mechanisms are expensive.**
- **⚠️ Glass blocks impose a style** — most likely one of the contemporary styles. They are not a neutral choice.
- **Loft-type light-transmitting partitions fit almost any style but read banal** — *"только ленивые ещё их не ставил"*. Something more original needs a designer.
- **⚠️ The one most likely to be missed: in a cramped flat with a storage problem, you cannot stand furniture along a glass partition.** A partition that solves the light problem can worsen the storage problem, which was the *other* of the two problems she opened with.
