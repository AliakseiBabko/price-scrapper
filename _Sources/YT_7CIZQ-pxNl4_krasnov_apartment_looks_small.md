## Mistakes / Warnings — Why a Space Reads Small

- **⚠️ Named measuring/mock-up technique before buying furniture**: use a tape measure for the room's real dimensions and key clearances (to door, window, radiator), then use **painter's tape to mock up a prospective piece's actual footprint on the floor/wall** before purchase — checks real clearance for walking, opening doors/drawers around the piece. A concrete, transferable, named technique distinct from a generic "measure first" caution.
- **⚠️ Pull furniture away from walls/corners rather than pushing it flush into them.** A bed centered with breathing room on both sides (rather than jammed into a corner) reads as a deliberate focal point instead of "boxed in," and improves functional access (shelf reach, walkway) — not just aesthetics. Same principle applies to living-room seating/side tables pulled off the wall.
- **⚠️ Dark/deep-toned walls, ceiling, and large furniture absorb light and visually shrink a room, even under strong lighting** — described directly as acting like "a black hole for light": rays are absorbed rather than reflected/distributed. **Restrict dark palettes to an accent wall or detail rather than full-room coverage**, especially in a compact room, unless the room genuinely has strong natural light. Light palettes are framed as the safer, trend-independent default for a compact/uncertain space.
- **Single flat ceiling light with no layering reads flat/plain even with a light palette** — restates this vault's existing layered-lighting principle (three-tier: general/accent/decorative), already fully captured from Round 1's `GpkgVoPesDI`. **Restatement — not re-extracted as new**, no wiki edit needed beyond a corroboration note if genuinely useful.
- **Continuous flooring material across adjoining rooms preferred over an abrupt material-transition line** — restates Round 1's flooring-transition content (`NvHEQ7vnxfI` and other Round 1 sources). **Restatement — not re-extracted as new.**
- **⚠️ Avoid busy pattern/texture on every surface simultaneously** (walls + furniture + textiles all patterned) — creates visual chaos that reads as smaller/more cramped than it is. **Use calm/plain surfaces as backdrop for a few chosen accents** — "less is more" on pattern density.
- **Open/display shelving in moderation**: a few curated items read as a stylish accent; a shelf crammed with every souvenir/knick-knack reads as clutter and visually shrinks the room. **Prefer closed storage for the bulk of items** — partially restates this vault's existing storage/decluttering content (`14_Furniture/analysis/Household_Storage_and_Decluttering_Method.md`), but frames it specifically as a room-size-perception mechanism rather than a tidiness mechanism — worth a brief cross-reference, not full re-extraction.
