## Case 1 — Living Room (Elena)

- **Reverse-image-search technique to recover real furniture
  dimensions from a client's own photos**: upload/crop the photographed
  piece into Google Images (or Yandex/Pinterest image search) to find
  the exact product listing and its real dimensions — used here to
  reconstruct an accurate scale floor plan from photos alone.
- **⚠️ Furniture placed along a room's long walls exaggerates an
  already narrow/elongated room shape** — deliberately break the
  strict long-wall placement and/or the room's central symmetry axis to
  make a long narrow room read more balanced/square.
- **Avoid placing two similar large case-furniture pieces (e.g. two
  chests/dressers) adjacent to each other** — reads as visually
  redundant ("too much").
- **Paint a ceiling cornice/molding the same color as the walls, not
  white/neutral by default** — a contrasting-color cornice visually
  shortens/cuts off the wall height; a wall-matched cornice preserves
  the perceived height.
- **Upholstery/sofa color close to the wall paint color reads as a
  deliberate, cohesive tonal-harmony technique**, not a missed accent
  opportunity — recorded as the source's own stated design judgment.
- **Respect an irrational client fear rather than arguing it away, but
  still reject an objectively poor workaround**: a client feared a
  heavy antique mirror falling above the sofa; rather than insisting
  the fear was unfounded (the source's own view — a properly mounted
  mirror would be fine), the antique mirror was swapped for a new
  mirror in a similar classic style, preserving psychological comfort.
  **Separately, adhesive mirror-tile stickers were explicitly rejected
  as a substitute** — flagged as a dated, discouraged product
  regardless of the fear-management question.
- **Pendant/hanging light fixtures should always be positioned over a
  supporting surface (table/console), never left floating over open
  floor space with nothing below them.**
- **Relocate an existing, still-functional furniture piece to a new
  supportive spot in the room rather than discarding it**, when its
  original position stops working in a redesign (here: a freestanding
  decorative cat house moved to a newly-freed corner).

## Case 2 — Small Bedroom, 3m × 3.15m, 2.30m Ceiling (USA, directly relevant to `06_Small_Bedroom`)

- **⚠️ Low-ceiling proportion rule (2.30m ceiling)**: avoid heavy
  horizontal design elements (decorative horizontal curtain trim, a
  wide horizontally-oriented painting) — they visually compress an
  already-low ceiling further. Favor vertical-emphasis elements instead
  (floor-to-ceiling curtain panels, a tall floor-to-ceiling headboard
  panel) to visually stretch perceived ceiling height. Framed as
  applying regardless of the target style (classic or, as here, a more
  contemporary "contemporary/relaxed modern" direction).
- **DIY plumb-line technique without a laser level**: tie a small
  weight (a coin, a ring) to a length of string or ribbon, tape the top
  to the wall, and let it hang — an improvised true-vertical reference
  for hanging panels/molding straight when a laser level isn't
  available.
- **Floor-to-ceiling self-adhesive soft wall panels as a combined
  headboard-replacement and ceiling-height-proportion fix** — peel-and-
  stick padded panels extended the full wall height directly behind the
  bed, functioning as both a headboard and the vertical-emphasis
  element from the rule above.
- **Stated budget philosophy, independent of any specific product
  recommendation**: even when a client explicitly states no budget
  constraint, the source deliberately sources moderately-priced items
  rather than premium versions across the board, reasoning that money
  not spent on interior finishes can go toward other life priorities —
  recorded as the practitioner's own general design philosophy.
- **Deliberate small contrast elements (e.g. darker nightstands) inside
  an otherwise fully light/neutral-palette room** — prevents an
  all-pale scheme from reading bland, without abandoning the client's
  stated preference for light/pastel colors.
- **⚠️ Explicitly undecided design call, presented as such rather than
  forced**: the source could not confidently choose between keeping or
  removing a pair of ornate mirrors (each choice changes the room's
  weight/formality noticeably) and presented both options to the
  audience/client rather than picking one and asserting it as correct —
  a real example of a professional not manufacturing false certainty
  when a genuine trade-off exists.
- **A fixed, uncoverable mechanical obstacle (a floor heating convector
  near a window) can force an asymmetric, off-center rug placement** to
  preserve required access — a real, previously-lived-with household
  workaround, not a hypothetical.
- **A wanted feature can be a hard no under a real clearance
  constraint**: a client's wish for a floating shelf under the TV was
  explicitly dropped, not compromised on, because of a genuinely narrow
  (~30cm) walkway beside the bed — recorded as an example of a real
  physical constraint overriding a stated preference outright rather
  than finding a smaller version of the same feature.
