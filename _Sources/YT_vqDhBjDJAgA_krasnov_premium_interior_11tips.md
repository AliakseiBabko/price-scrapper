## Techniques — 11 Premium-Interior Principles

- **Furniture grouping into functional clusters** — e.g. a reading nook groups armchair + lamp + side table together rather than scattering them around a room, for a "collected/intentional" read.
- **⚠️ Repeat a chosen accent color 2–3+ times across different materials/finishes in the same space** (and ideally echo it across adjoining rooms) for a sense of depth and cohesion, rather than using it once. Distinct from and more specific than the general "single cohesive palette" principle — record as its own numbered technique.
- **⚠️ Curtain drapery specification: fabric width should be roughly 2× the window's own width** to achieve proper deep, even folds. **Mount the rod/track near the ceiling, or use a concealed shadow-gap ceiling-recessed track («теневой карниз»)**, and let curtains reach the floor, for an unbroken vertical line reading as more architectural. Extends `4UJIa7eVlYo`'s floor-length-curtain point with real drapery-math specifics — cross-reference, add as new numeric detail to the same wiki entry rather than duplicating.
- **Directional accent lighting** aimed at specific objects (wall-mounted or track spotlights on a large artwork, an empty wall, open shelving, or uplighting a tall plant/art object from below) as a technique distinct from general ambient layering — creates a "gallery/hotel" quality.
- **Concealed LED strip lighting must be fully hidden** (diffused glow, not a visible light-source line) and color-temperature-matched to the room's overall light scheme — used as a deliberate accent in modern styles but only as a subtle addition (never a visual focal point) in classical styles. **Largely restates this vault's existing concealed-LED-strip content (`12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Lighting_Design.md`)** — the classical-styles-never-a-focal-point nuance is the one genuinely new detail; flag as corroboration with that one addition, not full re-extraction.
- **Soften "cold" materials (glass, MDF, metal) by substituting or layering in soft materials** — e.g. a pouf instead of a hard side table, throws/cushions added, mixing smooth/velvet/rough/knit textile textures together rather than one uniform textile finish.
- **⚠️ Balance sharp rectilinear forms (squares, right angles) with at least one or two rounded/curved elements** (furniture, an accessory, a piece of art) to avoid an overly static, rigid composition. **Pairs with `ufKHek_TU30`'s (this same round) prospect-refuge-adjacent curves-vs-angles psychology content — cross-reference the shared "curves read as calm, angles read as dynamic" idea rather than treating as two separate facts; this video's framing is about styling coherence, `ufKHek_TU30`'s is psychology-mechanism-based — note the distinction rather than merging into one bland restatement.**
- **One or two deliberate "wow" focal accents** (an oversized statement light fixture, a fireplace, a large art piece) elevate perceived status — pick a small number, not many.
- **Frame artwork properly** (a substantial mat/frame, not a bare canvas) for a more "finished"/gallery look — the frame's presence and quality matters as much as the image itself.
- **Tall potted plants in room corners add volume/life** — names specific species that tolerate typical indoor conditions well: **ficus lyrata (fiddle-leaf fig), ficus elastica (rubber plant), sansevieria (snake plant), olive tree.**
- **⚠️ Pillow-insert oversizing technique**: when buying a pillow cover and insert separately, buy an insert slightly LARGER than the cover's stated size so the finished pillow looks fuller/plumper rather than flat — a specific, checkable styling tip.
- **Declutter surfaces to only functional/aesthetic/personally-meaningful objects, everything else stored away** — restates existing declutter content elsewhere in this round and vault, not re-extracted as new.
- **⚠️ Cable management**: visible cable clutter (behind a TV, at a desk) instantly undercuts a premium look; use cable trays/channels/raceways matched to the room's style, hidden behind furniture or routed inside walls.
- **Secondhand/vintage sourcing** (flea markets, Avito-equivalent resale platforms) as a legitimate route to a unique statement piece rather than only new/retail furniture — some genuinely unique finds are called out as achievable this way.
- **Scent as part of atmosphere** (candles, diffusers, fresh air) — brief mention here; fuller treatment (including a kitchen-exclusion caveat) is on `dHpdI35EWY8` (this same round) — cross-reference, don't duplicate.
- **⚠️ Custom/handmade/small-studio pieces (ceramics, lighting, textiles, art) create a "premium" read via genuine scarcity/craftsmanship** that mass-produced goods cannot replicate — a real, extractable claim about *why* bespoke/limited-production goods read as higher-status, worth recording as a stated design-psychology rationale rather than just "buy nice things."
