## New facts

1. **Exclusions stated upfront**: gas ovens excluded (worse temperature precision, fewer modes,
   worse safety); 45cm "compact" ovens excluded — the source's own quick usability test is
   whether your typical baking tray/dish fits an A4-sheet-sized footprint (roughly the usable
   area of a compact oven); in ~90% of cases a compact oven loses badly to a full-size 60cm
   model. Freestanding (non-built-in) ovens also excluded — don't fit a modern integrated
   kitchen; only suit retro-style kitchens or specific niche projects.
2. **Heating-mode taxonomy**: top heat, bottom heat, and combined top+bottom heat form the
   base mode, covering ~70% of standard oven tasks. Convection (an internal fan circulating
   air for even heating) is a genuinely useful next tier. "Enhanced"/professional convection
   adds a heating ring element behind the fan itself, reaching target temperature
   significantly faster than plain convection — ideal for meat. Grill is mainly for browning
   or cooking meat.
3. **⚠️ Microwave-function critique, mechanism-level**: the "avoid microwaves" trend is
   overstated on health grounds (measurements show an intact-housing microwave doesn't harm
   health), but reheated food does come out drier/worse-textured — because a microwave heats
   *water molecules* within the food, not the food evenly; different foods heat unevenly
   because they contain different amounts of water (a meat patty, more water, heats faster/
   hotter than potato on the same plate, even on a rotating turntable) — this is why
   reheated pizza is specifically called out as bad. **A combined oven+microwave unit has a
   real functional tradeoff**: you can't use the oven at full capacity while the microwave
   function is running — recommends splitting into two separate dedicated appliances if you
   use the oven often; a small studio/one-bedroom apartment is the stated case where a
   combined unit still makes sense. **⚠️ Extends this store's existing oven+microwave-combo
   disagreement** (`ihx8gUDO3vI`/`2I77xJIeRwM` recommend combining; `-1HBQkULK4Y` recommends
   against combining on repair-cost/reliability grounds) — this source adds a third, distinct,
   conditional mechanism (a functional capacity conflict when both run at once, not a repair
   risk) rather than taking a flat side; recorded as an extension of the existing open
   disagreement, not a resolution of it.
4. **⚠️ Steam-function three-tier taxonomy, with one tier called explicitly useless**:
   (a) simple one-time water-pour humidity boost — uncontrolled, evaporates almost
   immediately, "looks nice on paper, useless in practice" (a named competitor example,
   Kuppersberg, is cited via an inserted clip as an example of this poorly-implemented tier);
   (b) automatic continuous water feed — meaningfully better, sustains humidity but without
   fine control over the humidity level, which matters for many dishes; (c) full combi-steam
   with 100% steam control — precise temperature *and* humidity control throughout cooking,
   the tier the source actively recommends.
5. **Steam-function benefits, concrete examples**: reheated chicken/pizza retain
   just-cooked texture and moisture (vs. a microwave's dry result); bread/baked-goods dough
   rises better with a crisper crust from the added humidity (professional bakeries and
   restaurant kitchens use industrial combi-steamers for the same underlying reason — a home
   100%-steam oven is described as a scaled-down version of the same equipment); meat and
   fish retain internal moisture and get a browned crust without drying out; whole-dish
   reheating (e.g. lasagna) heats evenly through, avoiding the common microwave/plain-oven
   failure of a burnt/dried top with a still-cold center. **Named tradeoff**: reheating with
   full steam takes ~10+ minutes vs. ~1-2 minutes in a microwave, though some steam-oven
   models reach target chamber temperature in 3-4 minutes via the added professional-convection
   heating ring.
6. **Sous-vide oven mode**: a restaurant-derived low-temperature (50-75°C) vacuum-sealed
   cooking technique, taking anywhere from an hour to a full day depending on the dish;
   normally done via a water bath with a dedicated device, but an oven with a built-in
   sous-vide function automates temperature and steam-level maintenance for the whole cook.
7. **⚠️ Named budget-tier "steam-blocking" feature, undocumented by manufacturers**: some
   budget ovens can't implement real steam generation but instead implement a function that
   blocks steam/moisture from escaping the chamber (rather than generating steam) — a
   worthwhile addition even at a budget price point. Named example: Elica implements this on
   budget-tier models; the manufacturer itself doesn't market or disclose the feature —
   only specialist installers/technicians know it exists and can advise on it.
8. **Controls**: mechanical, electronic/touch, or (most commonly) a hybrid — mechanical dials
   for temperature/mode selection plus some touch buttons. Wi-Fi/app-connected ovens are
   explicitly not recommended — often useless or forgotten, and less convenient than
   mechanical controls; the source's stated rule of thumb: "simpler is better and more
   reliable."
9. **⚠️ Four-tier cleaning-method taxonomy, with a real recurring-cost item flagged**: manual
   wipe-down (the baseline); hydrolysis (a marketing term for a special wall/floor/ceiling
   coating meant to resist baked-on grease — works "so-so," now near-universal as a basic
   construction feature rather than a real differentiator); catalytic panels (cassette-style
   liner panels that chemically break down grease, part evaporating and part absorbed into
   the panel) — **a real maintenance cost**: these cassettes need replacement after 2-3 years,
   since they have a finite absorption capacity; pyrolysis (door locks automatically, chamber
   heats to 500°C, burning all grease residue down to ash/dust that's then vacuumed or wiped
   away) — the most effective method.
10. **⚠️ Pyrolysis vs. 100%-steam is currently a forced tradeoff**: no current oven model can
    combine full pyrolysis and full 100%-steam function in one unit — you must choose one.
    The source's own recommendation: prioritize 100% steam over pyrolysis, since steam
    changes daily cooking/reheating quality directly, and the chamber's own humid environment
    from regular steam use also keeps grease from baking onto the walls in the first place —
    making manual/basic cleaning easier anyway, partially offsetting the lack of pyrolysis.
11. **Door construction**: a soft-close hinge (safer, more convenient — closes fully on its own
    after a partial push, including from a child) and glass-pane count (2, 3, or 4 panes) —
    more panes means a cooler exterior door surface during cooking, safer around children/pets.
    Caveat: some 2-pane and 4-pane models perform similarly in practice despite the pane-count
    difference, a nuance not disclosed in spec sheets — ask a specialist if this matters to you.
12. **⚠️ Telescopic rails/runners, a concrete price add-on**: add **≈2,000-5,000 RUB
    (≈$30-$60)** to the unit price — worth it for the convenience of pulling a full tray/dish
    out to a stable, level position rather than reaching/lifting inside the chamber. **Hand-wash
    telescopic rails, never in a dishwasher** — high-pressure jets can force water past internal
    seals in the rail mechanism, causing it to jam.
13. **⚠️ Economy price tier: ≈30,000-50,000 RUB (≈$380-$630)** — the source names a single
    dominant Chinese factory (Midea) that manufactures a large share of the global economy-tier
    oven supply, rebadged under many different brand names: named examples include Mefery,
    Hbeg, MEA, Coopersberg, Lex, and History, among roughly 50 companies the source says order
    from the same Midea factory.
14. **⚠️ Comfort price tier: ≈70,000-100,000 RUB (≈$890-$1,300)**, framed as a real tradeoff
    between "a loaded Chinese-made unit" and "a bare-bones European-branded unit" at the same
    price. Named brands: Teka (acquired by Midea; the source notes a ~6-month supply gap into
    Russia at filming time, with new-model supply expected to resume around July), Bosch,
    Electrolux, and Elica. **Concrete named-brand price/spec comparison**: Elica ovens are
    made at the Italian IVA factory; at **≈130,000 RUB (≈$1,600)**, an Elica model comes fully
    loaded — tinted glass, steam-blocking function, telescopic rails, soft-close door, full
    touch controls, professional convection, and a 7-year warranty. **The same factory-made
    unit sold under the IVA brand name costs roughly twice as much for the identical spec.**
15. **⚠️ Premium price tier: 100%-steam ovens start at ≈300,000 RUB (≈$3,800)** — a large
    jump from the ≈100,000 RUB comfort-tier ceiling. Named premium brands: AEG, SMEG, Teka,
    and Neff. **A second named IVA-factory/Elica price-doubling example**: Elica's 3-in-1
    device (oven + steamer + microwave in one unit) sells for **≈280,000 RUB (≈$3,500)** under
    the Elica brand, while the same factory-made unit sold under the IVA brand costs
    **≈550,000 RUB (≈$7,000)** — the source states it cannot explain the mechanism behind this
    roughly 2x brand-name price gap on an identical device.
