## ⚠️⚠️ Why this series matters more than its view counts suggest

**[[00_Master/Planning_Project_Deliverable_Set|Planning_Project_Deliverable_Set.md]] is written from ONE observed album (Долгушев), and it names its own gap in plain words:**

> *"**Not yet modelled at all (5 sheets)**: underfloor heating, lighting + switch grouping, reflected ceiling, sockets/switches, sanitary equipment. These need an engineering-systems layer that does not exist yet."*

**This series is a second real album, and it is precisely those five sheet types, shown sheet by sheet with their internal conventions.** Долгушев's cheaper *планировочный проект* tier explicitly OMITS the engineering sections; Шемчук's album is mostly engineering. **The two are complementary rather than competing: Долгушев gives the scope tiers and sheet counts, Шемчук gives what is actually on an engineering sheet and why.**

⚠️ **2021, five videos, 2.3k–58k views. Not a standards document — one practitioner's own album, and his conventions are his.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 1. THE METHOD — «модель, альбом, объект»

**Every video in the series has the same three-act structure, and that IS the method: build it full-size in a 3D model → derive the album from the model → execute on site.**

> ***«В модели выполняется вся работа таким образом, как она потом будет выполняться на объекте.»***

**And the stated goal, which is the sharpest line in the series:**

> ***«Я задачей своих проектов ставлю стопроцентную реализуемость — отсутствие необходимости в каких-либо дополнительных размышлениях и переделках уже во время выполнения работ.»***

**⚠️⚠️ FULL-SIZE MEANS FULL-SIZE.** In plumbing he models every element as the real component, **including the mounting plates**. In plasterboard he models **every profile at true size AND draws the actual boards** (2 m moisture-resistant sheets here), **with the sheet layout chosen so the quantity can be counted exactly.**

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ That is quantity take-off falling out of the model as a by-product, which is what [[18_Digital_Toolchain/analysis/Quantity_Takeoff_and_Cost_Join|Quantity Take-off & Cost Join]] is about — and it is done by modelling the real parts rather than by a separate estimating step.**

**⚠️ THE ORDERING PRINCIPLE, stated outright:** *«Все работы в проекте показаны в той последовательности, в которой они выполняются на объекте.»* **The album's sheet order IS the construction sequence** — which is why it ends with furniture, *«так как чистовая мебель устанавливается уже в самом конце»*.

**⚠️ And the design sequence inside each discipline is the same every time: plan the rooms and place ALL the furniture first, and only then run the services to suit.** Electrics, plumbing and plasterboard all start from the furnished plan.

## ⚠️⚠️ 2. The album's overall shape — initial state, final state, then the path

1. **Title sheet** — the address, a short description, and **⚠️ the people RESPONSIBLE for the project and for running the works, with phone numbers.** The reason is operational: *«альбом всегда находится на объекте»*, so a tradesman or subcontractor with a question resolves it directly **without involving the client, who may be busy and not across the detail.**
2. **Contents sheet** — every sheet and its page number.
3. **⚠️⚠️ THE STRUCTURE: first what exists at survey, then what we must arrive at, then a step-by-step instruction for the path between them.**
4. **Survey plan** with the replanning — dimensions in plan **plus a 3D view for comprehension**; entrance, riser positions (water, ventilation, sewer), radiators, all levels, as handed over by the developer or the previous owner.
5. **Post-replanning dimensions** and the final state.
6. Per-room sheets: a plan fragment plus every finish material, decor element and furniture item. **⚠️ Where something is not yet chosen it is fixed by TYPE and left open by model** — e.g. "track light" or "downlight" without a specific fitting. **→ A useful convention: the sheet records the decision that has been made and is honest about the one that has not.**
7. Visualisations — his own simple massing; photoreal is outsourced per-view for extra money. ⚠️ **Same position Долгушев takes in this vault: massing sells understanding of volume, renders sell a picture.**
8. **⚠️ Order-of-works sheet(s)** — main stages down the page, detail per stage across it.
9. Then the disciplines in build order: demolition → partitions → glazing → plastering → heating → screed → AC → electrics → ventilation → water → plasterboard → tiling → decorating → ceilings → flooring → doors → furniture.

## ⚠️⚠️ 3. THE DATUM RULE — pick a mark that cannot move

> **A clean-floor datum is chosen as a point that WILL NOT CHANGE during the works — he uses a point on the STAIRWELL LANDING, on the door-handle side. It is drawn in the project and physically marked in red on site.**

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ The reasoning is what makes it transferable: marks get knocked out and contractors get replaced, and then nobody knows what anything was measured from.** *«Они открывают проект, видят эту метку, меряют всё от неё, и в конечном итоге все сходятся в одном едином горизонте.»*
>
> **→ Bears directly on this repo's own datum and setting-out discipline — see [[00_Master/Evidence_Reading_Discipline|Evidence Reading Discipline]], whose standing rule is that a scale must be independent of the thing being measured. This is the same idea applied to a building site: the datum must be outside the work.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 4. What is actually ON an engineering sheet — the part this project was missing

### Electrics

- **Circuit split**: per room, a separate breaker for sockets, another for light, another for AC if present. **Plus consumers requiring an UNBROKEN run from panel to appliance: hob, oven, boiler, washing machine.**
- **⚠️ Cable types colour-coded in the model and carried into the album** — internet blue, TV grey, 3×2.5 socket cable red, and so on.
- **⚠️⚠️ ROUTES ARE CHOSEN AGAINST THE OTHER TRADES, NOT JUST THE SHORTEST PATH.** He routes with AC lines, ventilation ducts, sanitary fittings and water mains already in the model, plus ease of installation and tidiness — **specifically so that when the plasterboarders arrive the cables are already where they need to be and nobody has to unclip and re-route them.** *«Подход не точечный, а комплексный.»*
- **Per-room point sheets, with a readable convention**: top-left a key plan with **the room highlighted yellow** and the corner points the развёртка is taken from; on the развёртка every point with horizontal setting-out **to the centre of an isolated point, or to the nearest point within a group**, heights given per group, plus a table of what each point serves.
- **⚠️ Point marking = letters + number**: letters abbreviate the room (км = комната, кх = кухня, су = санузел), the number is the point's index in that room.
- **⚠️⚠️ JUNCTION-BOX SHEET, and the best argument in the series for as-built documentation**: box schedule plus setting-out to box centre. Not only for the installers — *«этот проект остаётся в качестве альбома-паспорта объекта, и даже через 10–15 лет он легко найдёт каждую распаечную коробку за натяжным потолком или за коробом из гипсокартона»*.
- **⚠️ Fixing-clip sheets, purely for the fitters**: clip positions, setting-out to clip centre, and **the number of clips per group**, so marking out needs no on-site arithmetic.
- **Cable-run sheets per room**: ceiling runs on plan, then the wall развёртка showing where each cable drops and into which back-box.
- **⚠️ Underfloor-heating sheet drawn WITH the sanitary fixtures shown**, so it is visible that the cable does not run under the fixtures but only where a person actually stands.
- **⚠️⚠️ CABLE SCHEDULE — the most take-off-like artefact in the series.** Per cable: number, name, colour on the drawing, type, marking, **start and end point, LENGTH**, and a comment — e.g. *«длина кабеля рассчитана с учётом запаса 15 см на подключение розетки и опуск кабеля до нулевой отметки в месте расположения электрощита»*. **The payoff: every cable is cut to length in advance and loaded into conduit, with no re-measuring at ceiling level.**

### ⚠️ A first-fix provision for an unknown future ISP — he flags it as his own idea, applied for the first time

> **The problem: the flat is finished before the internet provider is known. You do not want to drill a finished renovation later; the low-voltage panel may sit somewhere awkward for concealed wiring; and surface cable spoils the finish.**
>
> **His solution: a CAST-IN CONDUIT to the stairwell** — flush with the landing wall, capped, under the ceiling where it is invisible. When the ISP eventually arrives they feed the cable straight through into the panel. Separately, the flat's own three cables (two internet, one TV) reach the low-voltage panel through a **32 mm PVC conduit** landing on a back-box inside it.

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ Directly applicable: this project is a new build, and the provider question is exactly this open.** **Cheap at first fix, impossible afterwards.** ⚠️ **He says it is the first time he has tried it, so it is untested by his own account.**

### Plumbing

- Inlet from **two risers in the stairwell reached through an inspection hatch**; the sewer riser inside one of the wet rooms.
- **⚠️ Meters positioned so the utility inspectors can read them WITHOUT entering the flat** *«чтобы доступ к показаниям был у коммунальщиков… и не беспокоить заказчика»*. **→ A placement rationale that has nothing to do with plumbing and everything to do with living there.**
- Node order: shut-off ball valves → **leak-protection system** → self-flushing coarse filter → distribution manifolds → consumers, with the boiler over the node.
- **⚠️ The filter's backwash discharge to the sewer is designed in, not improvised.**
- **First album sheet is a fixture layout with a table stating, per fixture, whether it needs cold, hot and drainage** — then a 3D scheme of the main **with references to the per-fixture detail sheets**, then those sheets with setting-out and a materials list each.

### Plasterboard — and the decisions are better than the details

- **⚠️⚠️ A plasterboard box where built-in furniture meets a stretch ceiling, so the ceiling and the wardrobe can be installed INDEPENDENTLY of each other, in any order.** The box's size is set by the future wardrobe.
- **⚠️⚠️ A box above the kitchen run, because a stretch-ceiling fabric inevitably sags a few millimetres — invisible to the eye — and upper kitchen doors opening to the ceiling would rub it and risk tearing it.**
- **⚠️ The whole store-room ceiling in plasterboard**, because shelving runs to the ceiling and moving suitcases and boxes onto it would damage stretch fabric.
- **⚠️⚠️ A DELIBERATE 3 cm SACRIFICE, honestly explained.** A false wall was unavoidable: the structure is monolithic RC — a column and an RC wall that **cannot be chased**, and the internal reinforcement must not be cut — while a lot of wiring plus a conduit linking upper and lower socket groups had to get there. He could have set the wall at half the depth, **but that would have left a 3 cm step complicating both the finish and the furniture. He gave up the 3 cm and brought the wall forward flush.**
  **→ A decision to LOSE floor area to avoid a step. Worth holding against pure area-maximisation.**
- **⚠️ Embedded backing (закладные): at box ends for fixing stretch ceilings, and across the FULL AREA of walls**, in case something needs fixing through them later.

### Ventilation — three sheets, and a services position dictated by the TILE

- **⚠️⚠️ The bathroom extract fan is positioned by tile setting-out.** The boiler and its cabinet will occupy the spot where the shaft outlet sits, so the fan must move — and when moving it, two constraints: **it must not foul the cabinet door**, and **it must sit correctly on the tile, placed so the fan centre lands exactly on a tile joint.**
  **→ ⚠️⚠️ A SERVICES POSITION DRIVEN BY THE FINISH SETTING-OUT rather than the other way round.** **Only possible because the tile layout was resolved in the model first — which is what makes the tile-layout work on [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Tile_Selection_and_Layout|Tile Selection & Layout]] a prerequisite for the services drawings, not a later decoration step.**
- **⚠️ The duct is chased INTO the wall** — possible because the partition is non-loadbearing aerated concrete — to the depth needed so the fan sits flat on the tile at final fix and enters the duct cleanly.
- **Kitchen**: hood over the hob per the kitchen layout; **the duct's VERTICAL leg recessed into the aerated-concrete wall so it does not eat cupboard space**, its horizontal leg inside a plasterboard ceiling box, with the kitchen meeting that box below and the stretch ceiling at its side.
- **The whole duct is assembled from real components modelled at full size, occupying exactly the space it will occupy on site.**
- **⚠️ The album section is only THREE sheets**: a kitchen scheme with setting-out to the centre of the hood outlet, the centre of the duct and the centre of the shaft; a 3D route scheme **plus a scheme of the assembly components, all numbered against the materials schedule**, with written assembly notes; and the bathroom equivalent. **→ Worth noting for scope planning: a complete, buildable ventilation section came to three sheets.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 5. Two drawing conventions worth stealing outright

1. **⚠️⚠️ CABLE DROPS MARKED IN RED on the ceiling and plasterboard sheets.** His reason: installers very often drill through those cables, *«особенно если они где-то скрыты, не видны»* — and he has seen it happen even where they were visible. *«Со своей стороны я стараюсь минимизировать влияние человеческого фактора.»* **He states he has not seen this in other people's projects.** **→ Free to draw, and it prevents a defect that is expensive and invisible until it fails.**
2. **⚠️⚠️ FLOOR-LAYING START POINT AND DIRECTION SHOWN WITH ARROWS**, chosen so the narrow cut is hidden under the kitchen — *«в этом случае у нас узких подрезок не останется вовсе»*. Plus the junction point between two different floor finishes, and a screed detail giving **a step so different floor finishes meet under the door leaf at one level.**

> **→ Both belong with the "Graphical conventions worth copying" block already on [[00_Master/Planning_Project_Deliverable_Set|Planning_Project_Deliverable_Set.md]] and in [[18_Digital_Toolchain/analysis/Drawing_Conventions_From_Practice|Drawing Conventions From Practice]].**

## ⚠️ 6. The recurring meta-pattern: every sheet is detachable

**Said explicitly, repeatedly, about different sheets: the demolition sheet carries work VOLUMES so a contractor can price it without him recalculating; the plastering sheet carries areas and linear metres for the same reason; the stretch-ceiling sheet can be sent to several ceiling companies for quotes; the internal-door sheet carries leaf sizes and the WALL THICKNESSES they go into so the client can walk into a shop with it; the kitchen sheet numbers every module against a decoding table so a kitchen firm can quote.**

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ THE ORGANISING INSIGHT: each sheet is designed to be detached and handed to ONE trade or supplier, priced and executed without the designer mediating.** **That is a different design goal from "a complete set of drawings", and it explains most of the conventions above.** **It is also the property this repo's own target set should be judged against.**
