# Source Note - Степан Огурцов: ⚠️⚠️ model to FINISHED faces, not rough — and the minimum dimensions a small bathroom actually runs into (YouTube 5Ojk5tKyil8)

## Evidence levels
(1) transcript text - (2) YouTube metadata - (3) contextual inference - (4) external validation (none)

⚠️ **407k views, the widest reach in this batch, and directly relevant: this project has a small bathroom and a separate WC.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 1. THE FINDING — layers arithmetic, with a worked failure

> **In a small bathroom every centimetre counts, so the model must account for the LAYERS: plaster, tile adhesive, tile.**

**His worked failure, and it is the kind that cannot be undone:**

| Step | Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| Measured on the ROUGH substrate | **67 cm** for the washing machine — *«место вагон»* |
| Plaster around the perimeter | **~2 cm** |
| Tile adhesive | **5 mm** |
| Tile | **10 mm** |
| **Result** | **not enough room for the machine** |

**And why it is fatal: the bath is already bought. Turning the machine sideways does not work — the door will not open.**

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ MODEL TO FINISHED FACES, NOT ROUGH ONES.** **This lands squarely on this repo's own wall-dimension work, where `solid_mm` vs `clear_mm` and the ±50 mm build tolerance are already tracked** — see Geometry Variance Study. **The transferable rule: an appliance clearance checked against a rough dimension is not checked at all, and in a small wet room the finish build-up is of the same order as the tolerance.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 2. Minimum dimensions, and the order in which things get sacrificed

> **Of the three large fixtures — bath, WC, basin — the BASIN is what gives way first.** (Hence the Soviet habit of overlapping the basin onto the bath.)

| Fixture | Minimum |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Bath** | **700 mm wide — and it cannot shrink further** |
| **WC** | **⚠️ 700–800 mm, set NOT by the cistern but by the SEATED PERSON**, depending on build |
| **Basin** | **400 mm standard minimum**, then widen in 100 mm steps |
| **Shower** | **⚠️ he would think twice below 90 × 90 cm** |
| Basin + washing machine side by side | **⚠️ 450 mm for the smallest (40) basin + 700 mm for a 60 machine** — and such items are barely made |

> **⚠️⚠️ The WC figure is the useful one: sizing from the seated person rather than from the fixture is a different and better test, and this vault's bathroom pages did not have it.**
>
> **⚠️ And his decision rule when the shower's advantage does not materialise: if basin and washing machine will not both fit alongside, prefer the BATH as the more universal fixture** — soaking things, bathing a child or a pet, washing hair, lying in it. **He otherwise favours a shower precisely because it frees a run for basin + machine.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 3. Partition thickness ladder — real numbers for a room where 2 cm matters

| Build-up | Finished thickness |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Brick** | **⚠️ ~17 cm after plaster and tile** — and *«его нельзя использовать в старых домах, так как перекрытия просто могут не выдержать»* |
| **Tongue-and-groove slab, 70 mm** | **85 mm** with 15 mm tile + adhesive. **⚠️ Flat enough not to need plaster** — that is where the saving comes from. **Must be the SILICATE (moisture-resistant) type** |
| **Plasterboard on 50 mm profile**, one 12 mm sheet each side | **89 mm** with 15 mm tile. **Must be ГКЛВ — moisture-resistant, greenish** |
| **⚠️ Plasterboard on a ceiling profile or welded steel frame** | **down to 67 mm** in extreme tightness |

- **⚠️⚠️ SERVICES CONSTRAINT: most thin partitions will only take WATER pipes, so plan the SEWER along the apartment wall. If that is impossible, use plasterboard on a 50 mm profile — it takes everything except the WC pipe.**
- **⚠️ Where anything heavy hangs on a thin partition, provide backing — plywood or a reinforced frame.**
- **⚠️ And he concedes the cost honestly: at these thicknesses soundproofing will be weak and you have to accept it.** He asks commenters whether anyone has solved it — **an openly unresolved problem, which is worth recording as such.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 4. Riser reconfiguration — only two points are fixed

> **On a riser, only TWO points are immovable: where it enters the slab and where it leaves it. Everything between can be changed.**

**So after combining or expanding a bathroom, a water riser or towel rail left protruding awkwardly can be re-routed** — simplest is a straight pipe you design around; better, press it tight to the wall; **and, if the management company does not object, take it into the wall or into the screed.**

> **→ ⚠️ A genuinely liberating geometric insight for a tight room, and this vault's bathroom and plumbing pages did not state it.** ⚠️ **The "if the management company does not object" clause is Russian practice; the geometry transfers, the permission does not.**

## ⚠️ 5. Fixtures in a small room

- **Prefer a concealed cistern (инсталляция) even here** — almost the same footprint, much better appearance and far easier cleaning. **⚠️ Reasons for a standard WC: total economy, or when a small ROTATION of the pan materially improves the layout.**
- **⚠️ With a washing machine adjacent, a separate bidet is out.** A hygienic spray is far easier to place; **or a BIDET SEAT, which sits on the pan and needs no wall.** Downside is price — *«если на маленькую площадь пихать много запросов, это всегда неизбежно приведёт к лишним тратам»*.
- **⚠️⚠️ THE COST MYTH, stated as rule: smaller does NOT mean cheaper.** You will not lower your comfort requirements, and cramming them into a small area pushes you toward **non-standard fittings and appliances, which cost more.** **→ Worth carrying, because the opposite assumption is the default one.**

## ⚠️ 6. Washing machine placement — the hardest problem, with the full option list

Soviet standards never allowed for one. His options, in order of preference:

- **Replanning**, including **⚠️ an opening through the wall that also gains storage in the hallway.**
- **Build it into a wardrobe next to the bathroom.**
- **Put it in the kitchen if the kitchen is less cramped.**
- **With 1 m of width: machine beside the basin under a single worktop.**
- **Vertical-loading models** — though front-loading is preferable.
- **⚠️ Under the basin — but then either the basin sits too high for children, or you need an expensive non-standard machine.**
- **⚠️ Wall-mounted versions, including miniature ones — several times dearer, but they open placements nothing else does.**

**⚠️ Drying, given a narrow opening**: a separate tumble dryer is hard to fit; **washer-dryers are a fair alternative when space is limited** — smaller capacity, worse energy class, more to go wrong; **or a plain drying rack, whose energy class is unbeatable and where *«при поломке выкинул, новую купил»*.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 7. The door opening — two consequences people meet late

- **Short walls mean the opening must be pushed as tight to the wall as possible, or the WC protrudes into it.** The opening will be lucky to be **70 cm for a 60 door**; **⚠️ he has had to fit 50 doors.**
- **⚠️ Bad news for detailing: there is no room for отбортовки or elaborate junction details.**
- **⚠️⚠️ AND A SEQUENCING TIP THAT COSTS NOTHING AND IS IRREVERSIBLE IF MISSED: carry the BATH into the bathroom BEFORE the door is installed.** **→ Same shape as this batch's other sequencing findings, and cheaper than all of them.**

## ⚠️ 8. Russian regulatory and stock context — flagged, not routed

> [!WARNING]
> **⚠️⚠️ RUSSIAN CLAIMS — flagged in place and deliberately NOT routed to `16_Legal_and_Regulations/`, which is Belarus-only.**
>
> - **In Russia a bathroom may be freely EXPANDED only on the first residential floor**; on other floors it may not overhang a neighbour's kitchen or living room, **so expansion is normally only into corridors or store rooms.**
> - **Rebuilding walls in the SAME position is said to need no БТИ approval.**
>
> **Recorded as the shape of the constraint. This project's Belarusian position is the authority — see [[16_Legal_and_Regulations/Legal_and_Regulations_Guide|Legal & Regulations Guide]].**

**⚠️ And a health note about old stock, worth knowing even though it does not apply to a new build:** Soviet panel series used prefabricated **сантехкабины**, craned in and never re-set if they went in crooked, undersized so they left large voids — lost space, mould smell, insect harbourage — **and asbestos was often used in their manufacture.** His advice is to demolish them. ⚠️ **Asbestos claim uncorroborated here; it is well documented elsewhere, but this source does not evidence it.**
