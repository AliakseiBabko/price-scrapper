## Lighting — colour temperature mapped to rooms

The first and, in her framing, the most common mistake is getting light temperature wrong. Her mapping:

- **Warm light** — associated by the brain with fire, a candle or campfire flame, therefore read as home and comfort. **Mandatory in bedrooms**, and **mandatory for all evening scenarios** anywhere: LED cove lighting in a living room, evening dining-table light.
- **Neutral white, 4000 K** — for **every space used during the day**: **bathrooms/WCs specifically so that skin tone is not distorted**, plus living room, dining room and kitchen for daytime cooking, tasks and cleaning. Also the right choice for commercial spaces, offices, showrooms and shops.
- **6000 K** — "больничка." Institutional; she treats it as a mistake in a home.
- **2700 K** shown on a graphite-grey furniture front reads pleasant, yellowish and warm; **~3500 K** on the same front already reads noticeably colder, with an "office vibe."

**⚠️ The generalisable mechanism she draws out, which is the part worth keeping**: **the same neutral object changes appearance substantially under different colour temperature**, and this is used deliberately by gallerists to present exhibits. Her consumer-facing version: the reason people look bad in clothing-shop fitting rooms is the blue light used there.

**⚠️ Practical instruction that follows: test finish materials under the colour temperature they will actually live under.** Maytoni's showroom uses a lamp that cycles colour temperatures for exactly this — checking how paint, fronts and other materials behave at 2700 / 3500 / 6000 K. The transferable rule is the method, not the gadget: **choose material and light temperature together, not sequentially.**

## Lighting — decide changeable-vs-fixed colour temperature at design stage

- **⚠️ Stated by the brand manager as a planning-stage decision, and this is the genuinely new item**: **some fittings cannot change colour temperature at all** — it is whatever you bought — **while others can.** So at planning stage you must decide **whether you want to change temperature later**, and **by what means: smart home, or a mechanical method.**
- Her own on-camera reaction confirms this is not obvious to buyers, which is why it is worth recording as a checklist item at the lighting-specification stage rather than at purchase.

## Lighting — trimless (plaster-in) fixtures versus visible-flange fixtures

- **The mistake as she frames it**: clients decline modern fittings whose external part is concealed, in favour of classic ones where the flange is visible on the ceiling — "вот такая вот блямба снаружи."
- **⚠️ Where it matters most**: in a detail-rich interior a visible flange is less noticeable; **in minimalism or Japandi the eye lands directly on those "коробочки, кружочки и прямоугольнички."**
- **The alternative: fittings recessed under filler (встраиваемые под шпаклёвку)** — the external part is built into the plasterboard, which is then filled and painted, leaving only the fitting's inner part visible.
- **⚠️ Two buyer objections, and her answers**:
  - **Price** — her position is not to economise on light **at least in the main rooms (living room, kitchen)**: you save "несколько тысяч рублей или несколько десятков тысяч рублей" and get a dated design. (This is the only quantitative gesture in the video: **single-digit thousands to a few tens of thousands of RUB**, 2025, no per-fitting basis given — too vague to convert.)
  - **"You can't change the bulbs"** — **false; bulbs are replaced exactly as in ordinary models.**
- **Scope has widened beyond downlights**: plaster-in mounting now covers **track fittings of any configuration** — wide, narrow, thin, curved, radiused — with the track itself available in any colour, and even large magnetic systems can be made **flush with the ceiling.**

## Lighting — "light burns" (световые ожоги) on walls

- **The defect**: with the same fittings on the same track, one arrangement gives even, soft light and another gives a harsh scallop of light on the wall — "классический ожог."
- **⚠️ The stated causes, and the fix is a positioning rule not a product choice**: incorrect **aiming of the fitting** (its rotation toward the wall), and **placing it too close to the wall.** The brand manager also attributes it partly to lamp choice (energy-saving or LED).
- **Her one-line rule, which is what to carry forward: do not aim the fitting straight at the wall, and do not position it too close to the wall.**

## Lighting — neon, and one claim to treat sceptically

- Neon is presented as a current trend across forms — flexible neon, and neon formed into a straight tube — in many colours and temperatures, expected to stay in fashion "ещё как минимум пара лет."
- **⚠️ Her stated distinction between trend-following as a mistake and as a requirement**: not using sharply trend-led items is **not** a mistake in a country house meant to last twenty years, but **is** a mistake in a commercial interior, where the trends are the point. Recorded because it is a clean articulation of a principle this vault applies elsewhere — **the correct trend exposure is a function of the interior's intended lifespan.**
- **Do not buy the cheapest neon arcs** — they read cheap.
