## Walls / Ceilings — Non-Woven ("Флизелиновые") Wallpaper Hanging Technique

1. **Substrate prep sequence, stated as the top priority**: walls must be
   plastered on beacons ("по маячкам"), then puttied in **two coats**
   (named product: "Vit LR" — likely "Vetonit LR," ASR-uncertain exact
   rendering), fully sanded under raking/inspection light, then **primed**
   before adhesive goes on — **priming is explicitly flagged as a step many
   installers skip, but is "obligatory"**: an unprimed wall absorbs
   wallpaper adhesive too fast, and the panel won't bond properly as a
   result.
2. **Named glue product and mixing ratio, with a paper-type-specific
   detail**: adhesive named (ASR-uncertain rendering, likely "Метилан
   Флизелин" — a universal glue for all non-woven-backed wallpaper widths).
   **Mixing ratio for paintable non-woven wallpaper specifically: 4.5
   liters of water per package** — explicitly **not** 5 liters, per the
   manufacturer's own instructions, because paintable non-woven wallpaper
   needs a different (thicker) ratio than standard non-woven wallpaper.
3. **Glue-mixing technique**: pour glue powder into water in small
   portions at the bucket's edge while mixing with a hand/paddle mixer;
   let the mixed glue **rest ~10 minutes**, then remix once more before
   use. **Correct consistency check**: neither watery nor thick — it
   should flow off a roller in a steady sheet, not drip or clump.
4. **⚠️ Material-specific glue-application rule, the key mechanism of this
   video**: for **non-woven ("флизелиновые") wallpaper, glue is applied
   to the wall only**, not to the wallpaper sheet itself — **in contrast to
   vinyl wallpaper, where glue must be applied to both the wall and the
   back of the sheet**. Getting this backwards (double-gluing non-woven
   paper the way vinyl needs) is an avoidable technique mistake this video
   makes explicit.
5. **Glue direction and edge technique**: apply glue bottom-to-top on the
   wall to avoid drips running down and staining the lower wall/finished
   floor; use a small brush ("кисточка"/"клюшка") for the careful top and
   bottom edge strips, especially where the wallpaper borders a stretch-
   ceiling track — precision there avoids soiling the track.
6. **Panel-cutting and hanging allowance**: cut each wallpaper drop with
   **~5cm of excess material at both the top (ceiling side) and bottom
   (baseboard side)**; hang top-down, then squeeze out all air bubbles
   using a wide brush, roller, or plastic smoothing spatula before
   trimming the excess.
7. **⚠️ Cutting-tool technique for the trim cut**: use a wide (~40cm)
   metal smoothing blade plus a sharp utility knife (named blade brand:
   "Гольф," black blades) to trim the excess strip; **hold the knife
   blade parallel to the wall, not perpendicular to it**, and **never lift
   the knife off the surface mid-cut** — slide the smoothing blade along
   and keep cutting in one continuous motion rather than repositioning the
   blade with fresh knife entries, which risks a ragged/wandering cut line.
8. **Glue-residue cleanup**: wipe excess adhesive immediately with a damp
   "waffle-weave" cloth ("вафельная салфетка," sold in rolls at any
   building-materials market) — rinse the cloth frequently so residue on
   the cloth itself doesn't get smeared back onto the wallpaper surface,
   where it would later show as an unwanted glossy sheen once dry.
9. **Seam-placement planning rule**: plan the panel layout so that any
   seam/joint between adjacent drops lands **above a window or door
   opening** wherever possible — a genuinely useful positioning heuristic
   to make seams as visually inconspicuous as possible, applicable to
   both non-woven and vinyl wallpaper (both stretch slightly, per the
   narrator, which allows some seam-position flexibility during hanging).
10. **⚠️ Draft warning**: no air drafts should be present in the room
    during hanging/drying — a draft can seriously degrade adhesion
    quality, per the narrator's explicit closing caution.
