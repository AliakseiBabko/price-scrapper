## Design Concept — LRV as a Numeric Selection Criterion

- **⚠️ LRV (Light Reflectance Value) is used throughout as the quantitative handle on "how light is this shade," on a scale where higher = more light reflected.** This is the one thing in the source that this vault does not already have from a Russian-language practitioner: every other source in this batch discusses lightness qualitatively (светлота, «на тон светлее», «растяжка по светлоте»), while LRV gives it a published number that can be compared across shades before any physical sample exists. Values quoted, and their stated behavioural consequence:
  - **LRV ~80+** (Alabaster, 82) — reflects a lot of light without harshness; described as consistent across lighting conditions where many whites shift yellow, grey or blue.
  - **LRV ~66–76** (Natural Linen 66, Neutral Ground 70, Shoji White 74, Origami White 76) — the light-but-not-white band, used for whole-house schemes.
  - **LRV ~40–58** (Universal Khaki 40, Shiitake 51, Accessible Beige 58) — mid-tones that give walls presence without weight.
  - **LRV ~8–29** (Keystone Gray 29, Urbane Bronze 8) — deep neutrals for accent walls, built-ins, dens and exteriors.
- **⚠️ A concrete, checkable behavioural distinction between two categories of neutral** — the more useful half of this source, and independently consistent with Кузина's подтон warning (`n5ZBqdq0wH8`) and ARCHWOOD's orientation table (`AKxOtRhIcJY`):
  - Some neutrals are described as **stable across lighting** because they don't lean strongly yellow or grey (Universal Khaki, Alabaster, Accessible Beige are the named examples) — "it stays consistent, calm."
  - Others are described as **deliberately unstable, and chosen for it** — Shoji White is called out for reading warm and creamy in bright south-facing rooms and staying soft without going flat or grey in cool north-facing ones; Origami White is called "a chameleon." **The claim being made is that undertone stability under changing light is itself a selection axis, separate from the shade's lightness** — a room whose light shifts dramatically across the day is a reason to pick for stability, or to pick for shift deliberately.
- **Whole-house application**: the light greige band (Origami White named) is recommended for whole-house schemes specifically because it gives room-to-room flow while still letting each room read differently — the same conclusion as the cross-room coherence sources already on `Whole_Apartment_Coherence.md`, reached from a paint-selection direction.
