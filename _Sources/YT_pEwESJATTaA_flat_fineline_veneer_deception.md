## Materials — fine-line veneer («файн» / fineline), and why it is the industry's biggest mis-selling

- **⚠️ What it is, mechanically**: an **Italian technology, he dates it to roughly the 1990s.** Fast-growing, cheap, quickly-replaced timber (he describes it being cut in Africa) is brought in and assembled into a large block; the block is sliced into thin sheets, **and a print of an expensive species — his examples are ebony and rosewood — is applied.** So it is genuinely wood, "только тебе не сказали, какое оно."
- **⚠️ He is explicit that the technology itself is good, including on ecological grounds** — valuable slow-growing forests are not cut. **The objection is non-disclosure only**: "суть в том, что об этом не говорится по-честному."
- **⚠️ The overpayment he alleges: "разница в разы, в пять раз, в 10 раз может быть"** — a cheap species sold at the price of an expensive one. His analogy: a Mercedes badge on a car that is not a Mercedes, or a Land Rover badge on a Niva at Mercedes money.
- **⚠️ Who he blames, and it is not the factories: the incompetence of the people selling it.** A showroom manager for Italian furniture may have seen the piece only in a catalogue; **Italian makers like inventing names for simple things ("уникальный материал"); the disclosure exists but as a footnote you did not read.** **He says the complaint people level at furniture *manufacturers* is therefore partly unfair.**

### ⚠️ How to actually tell the three apart — the checkable core of the source

He shows three wood-textured samples of near-identical tone: **natural oak veneer, an oak imitation, and fine-line.**

- **⚠️ On a small sample of the same tone, fine-line and real veneer are "практически невозможно определить даже профессионалу."** He says so of himself and his trade. **So a small item's material claim cannot be verified by eye at all — it has to be verified on paper.**
- **⚠️ On a large area the tells are reliable, and they are all about uniformity:**
  - **Natural wood shows non-uniformity** — uneven tinting where it is stained, uneven figure, and possibly knots.
  - **Fine-line is entirely uniform across a large area** — that is the giveaway.
  - **ЛДСП is entirely uniform too, because the sheets are large and the print on each is identical.**
- **The practical consequence he states: "ключевое здесь — вам об этом должны рассказать."** Ask, and get it in writing; the eye will not settle it.

## Materials — ⚠️ natural wood cannot be colour-matched, and the expectation problem that follows

This is the most useful item for this project and it is a contract-level fact, not an aesthetic one.

- **Absolute non-uniformity is unachievable in real wood** — "на дереве настоящем идеальность абсолютную добиться нереально."
- **⚠️ Every experienced furniture company's contract carries a clause to this effect**: natural materials may vary in shade depending on the batch and on when they were painted.
- **⚠️ The consequence for phased buying, stated plainly: order a wardrobe with facades in a given tone, then decide later you need one more section — and no manufacturer working with natural timber will guarantee that facade matches the rest.** **This is a reason to order everything in one go, and it is independently corroborated for enamel by this channel's own facade-materials video** (see [[_Sources/YT_hAeIKfYMxbw_flat_kitchen_facade_materials|hAeIKfYMxbw]], where the same rule is stated for paint batches).
- **⚠️ The client case, and its diagnosis is the transferable insight.** A ~300 m² apartment, good design project, client explicitly wanted naturalness — "нам это ваше ДСП неинтересно, нам нужно настоящее дерево, шпон." Real solid-wood and veneer furniture was supplied. **The complaint: in low morning sun, a wall panel showed a slightly different veneer shade in one place — "не идеально… вы не умеете работать."**
  - **⚠️ He could not resolve it with GOSTs or arguments, and only understood the cause on site: the same flat contained a very expensive German kitchen, and he knew it was made of chipboard, plastic and imitation, with edge banding — and it was flawlessly uniform.** The clients read that as "конечно, это ж немцы," and read the real wood as defective workmanship.
  - **⚠️ The lesson to carry: an imitation material sets an expectation of perfection that a natural material cannot meet. A client who has lived with imitation may be dissatisfied by genuine timber, and the more expensive choice can read as the worse one.** His own line to clients: "если вы найдёте два идеальных дерева в лесу… мы сразу 50% скидка. Нету такого в природе."
