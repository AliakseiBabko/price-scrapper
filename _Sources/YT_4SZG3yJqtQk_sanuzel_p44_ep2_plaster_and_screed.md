## Priming aerated block — and two reasons, not one

- **Dust removal comes first, and the justification is the primer's own datasheet**: wide spatula, then **vacuum, working top-down** «потому что всё равно пыль сыпется вниз». «В грунтовке чёрным по белому прямо сказано: основание должно быть сухим, чистым и обеспыленным. Как лучше обеспылить, чем нежели пылесосом?» He also reads the vacuum by ear — the rustle in the hose tells him where dust is left.
- **What primer is, in his words**: «полимерные добавки, растворённые в воде… вода испаряется, полимерные добавки остаются, забивают поры и укрепляют основание.» He orders one with **elevated dry-matter/polymer content** specifically for aerated block.
- **⚠️ Two benefits, and the second is rarely stated**: (1) the next layer bonds better because the primer penetrates, strengthens the surface and **evens out absorbency**; (2) **the plaster mix itself stays workable longer** — «сама смесь будет дольше жить, и мне будет проще с этим работать». Priming as a working-time decision, not only an adhesion one.
- **Pigmented for coverage control** — and he names a second audience for that: «заказчик будет в курсе, потому что наглядно видно разницу». Corroborates the pigment point from [[_Sources/YT_z0hcMY5PYoc_sanuzel_pik_cabin_prep_works|the ПИК video]] and adds the client-verification angle.
- **Aerated block drinks it**: one bucket is not enough for this room, and he applies generously, «чуть ли не пропитывая сами стены».
- **⚠️ And he scopes his own claim**, which is worth recording as a habit: «сейчас я говорю именно про грунтовку газоблока — для других поверхностей и для других задач используются другие грунты и другой метод нанесения».
- **Wait at least 6 hours per the instructions.** He primes in the evening and leaves for the day.

## ⚠️ Setting beacons off a laser — the method, with the offset arithmetic

- **Marks on the straightedge, made visible with masking tape**: one **exactly at the edge** (used when pressing the beacon home), and a second **~7 mm beyond it — a 6 mm beacon plus ~1 mm of tolerance.**
- **Finding the minimum layer**: hold the straightedge against the wall where a beacon will sit, set the beam onto the far mark, and watch how the deviation runs up the wall; set to whichever end is tighter and mark beside the straightedge. Repeat at the other end of the wall, then **join the two marks with the beam** — that gives a true vertical plane offset from the wall by exactly straightedge + beacon.
- **⚠️ Record the plane before you lose it**: he marks where the beam falls **on the walls and additionally on the floor**.
- **Perpendicular by machine, not by measurement**: «уровень сам строит чёткий перпендикуляр под 90° по осям» — set one axis onto the mark already made and the second shows where 90° lands. Here it needed shifting **3 mm** closer to the wall, then verified by the beam touching the marks at both ends.
- **He built the walls square and still checks and plasters to the angle**: «стены я возводил под прямым углом, но тем не менее штукатурить всё равно придётся, и будем проверять угол с помощью уровня».
- **Three beacons on the wall with the door opening — check their coplanarity by laying a long straightedge diagonally across all three; it must touch at all three points.**

## ⚠️⚠️ The perimeter damper, now executed rather than argued

In the ПИК video he proposed separating the plaster from the structure «по аналогии с
пенным швом» and **explicitly hedged it as his own reasoning**. Here it is a named
material and a thickness:

> **A separator tape sold for metal profiles, ~3 mm thick, run around the floor and ceiling perimeter before the beacons go in, used as a damper.** «Помните, да, то, что мы стены ставили так, чтобы они не касались плиты перекрытия? Ну и, соответственно, я не хочу, чтобы и штукатурка также касалась.» **Cut off after plastering** — «уплотнительная лента везде срезана» — leaving the movement joint open. — Василий

## ⚠️ Plaster selection — two independent reasons and one general rule

**He chooses gypsum and pre-empts the obvious objection** («многие могут задать, почему не цементную»):

1. **⚠️ Part of the enclosure is the ventilation shaft, which is made of a gypsum material — «на неё ни в коем случае нельзя наносить цементные составы».** A panel-house-specific constraint that decides the plaster for the whole room, and this vault did not carry it.
2. **Aerated concrete is not strong enough to hold cement plaster.**

**His general rule, stated as a principle**: «каждый последующий наносимый слой должен быть чуть слабее, чем предыдущий — штукатурка должна быть слабее по марочной прочности, чем основание». Cement plaster he considers suitable only for **concrete, brick, expanded-clay-concrete block and similarly strong substrates**; and **a lightweight cement plaster for aerated block ends up "not much different in strength from gypsum anyway"**, so it buys nothing. This is the fuller version of the compatibility argument recorded from the ПИК video.

## Mixing and application

- **Whole bag at once, 17 L of water as stated on the bag, bucket with graduations.** **⚠️ «Кажется, будто бы густая и хочется добавить воды. Но на самом деле этого делать не надо — надо дальше продолжать мешать.»** Then **stand ~5 minutes and re-mix.** He washes the mixer after every batch — «этому уже несколько лет».
- **⚠️ Two straightedge types, named and distinguished — a tool fact this vault lacked**: an **H-profile («H-образное») rule for applying and levelling**, which «выступает в роли кельмы, только намного длиннее»; and a **trapezoidal («трапециевидное») rule for cutting back** plaster already applied — the same type used to press beacons in.
- Three application methods shown (throwing it on; loading the trowel and rubbing in; large random dabs then smoothing with a float), then the H-rule to spread and transfer.
- **⚠️ Then draw the rule from top to bottom, shaving the surface** — «чем толще слой, тем больше будет штукатурки срезаться».

## ⚠️⚠️ Pulling the beacons — the step that decides the final plane

**After roughly 1.5–2 hours, when the plaster no longer sticks but still dents under pressure**, he **removes the metal beacons** and cuts the wall back floor-to-ceiling with the long trapezoidal rule.

- **The stated reason is specific**: to remove **the bumps that form around the beacons**, since the beacon itself may be slightly out of true. So the beacon is a temporary reference, not part of the wall.
- **Technique: pull slightly diagonally so the rule does not drop into the groove.**
- **⚠️ Fill the beacon grooves immediately, while the plaster is still damp, and deliberately leave the fill standing slightly proud** — then cut that back once set. «Иначе может немножко усесть и будет ямка.» A named anti-shrinkage step for exactly the defect the repair itself would otherwise create.
- **Colour change is his cure indicator** — he points out the difference on camera between the first-applied and the just-filled areas.
- Then the damper tape is cut, **fresh tape stuck on**, and the wall continued.

**QC**: straightedge on the diagonal, level, and **the corners checked against 90° from the outside as well.** **⚠️ Plus one deliberate extra: the corner where the kitchen units will stand was kept square even though that wall was not plastered** — «я не знаю, будет ли она выравниваться впоследствии, но угол также сохранён, выведен». **Preserving a square corner for cabinetry that someone else will fit later.**

**And the door opening is cut only now, after plastering** — third statement of this across three videos, and here with the reason: «таким образом края получатся довольно-таки ровными и аккуратными».

## Floor — self-levelling compound over a 10–40 mm range

- **Grinding serves two purposes**: remove plaster spatter, **and open the concrete's pores** «чтобы сцепление со стяжкой было максимально хорошим». Diamond cup wheel; **~20 m²** of floor here.
- Then vacuum, then **the same primer as the walls, to cut absorbency — «для наливного пола это будет очень важно»**; damper tape around the whole perimeter.
- **⚠️ Why self-levelling rather than a sand-cement screed: the range.** ~**4 cm** needed at the far kitchen side by the window, ~**10 mm** in the other zone — one material has to cover the whole spread.

### ⚠️⚠️ The datum arithmetic, and it closes

**The reference is the existing threshold**, where the adjoining room's floor covering already lies — his job is to land flush with it later.

| Step | Figure |
| :--- | :--- |
| Required drop of screed top below the existing finished floor | **13 mm** — and he says what it is: **tile + tile adhesive** |
| Laser datum chosen so the target is a round number: beam → existing floor | **15.7 cm** (averaged, since that level itself varies slightly) |
| → beam → required screed top | **17.0 cm** |
| Example reading at one point: beam → bare slab | **18.3 cm** |
| → compound needed at that point | **13 mm** |

`arithmetic-exact`: 15.7 + 1.3 = 17.0, and 18.3 − 17.0 = 1.3. **The figures reconcile.** **The transferable method is the trick with the datum**: choose the laser height so that *target = a whole number*, «чтобы мне потом легче было считать уже с целыми числами», and set the target as *finished-floor level minus the tile build-up*.

- **Self-adhesive datum markers, trimmed to length, showing the pour top** — «чем больше, тем легче»; he sets many because the fall is uneven. Corroborates and extends the same technique in the ПИК video.
- **⚠️ Solo logistics, stated plainly**: **36 bags**, mixed **three bags per tub in two tubs alternately**, with pre-measured water waiting in a row of buckets and everything within reach, «поскольку я работаю один… работать нужно быстро». A real account of what pouring 20 m² alone actually requires.
- **Tools by layer thickness**: a **home-made squeegee/rakle** for the thick zone (4–5 cm), a **spiked roller** for the thin areas (10–15 mm), and **spiked shoes** to walk on the poured floor.
- **QC after set**: an almost-3 m straightedge, all in one plane, level checked throughout — **«конечно, есть небольшие перепады, куда без этого, но они вполне себе допустимы»**, which is an honest tolerance statement rather than a perfection claim.
