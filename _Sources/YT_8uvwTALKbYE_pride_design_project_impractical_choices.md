## Kitchen Appliances — Downdraft Hood

- **⚠️ A downdraft / in-worktop hood ("скрытая вытяжка в столешнице") is a materials decision, not only an aesthetic one.** Stated mechanism: steam, grease and moisture ("пар, жир, влага") travel very close to the facades and the working surface instead of being drawn up and away from them, so moisture- and heat-sensitive kitchen materials degrade over time. **Film-wrapped facades ("фасады в плёнке") are called out specifically** as the worst case.
- Stated selection rule: judge this hood type against **three** inputs together — appearance, the kitchen's facade/worktop materials, and how actively the household actually cooks. A conventional overhead hood is described as "намного надёжнее и проще в уходе" (more reliable and simpler to maintain) where cooking is heavy.

## Kitchen / Furniture — Milled Facades

- **⚠️ Fine-wave milled facades ("фрезерованные фасады в мелкую волну") — cleaning cost scales inversely with the size of the relief.** Stated rule: "чем меньше и глубже будет вот этот рельеф, тем сложнее будет уход" — the finer and deeper the fluting, the harder the maintenance, because dust, grease and splashes collect between the ribs and cannot practically be cleaned out.
- Stated placement rule: acceptable on a display cabinet or as pure decor; the speaker's position is specifically **not on kitchen facades**. Her own stated application: fluted facades on a living-room TV unit, and only on its lower portion, with the rest smooth — confining the texture to a low-splash zone.

## Furniture — Dining Tables

- **⚠️ Glass dining table — four stated daily-use costs against one real benefit.** Benefit: it visually lightens the space at any size, which is why it suits small rooms. Costs: (a) shows fingerprints, drips, crumbs and dish marks constantly, worse with small children; (b) needs near-continuous wiping to look tidy; (c) accumulates scratches that are **especially visible in raking/side light** ("при боковом свете"); (d) crockery set down on it rattles ("дребезжит"), which some people find unpleasant. Framed as a conscious trade-off, not a prohibition.
- **⚠️ Round table — the seat count at which it stops working.** Stated: genuinely comfortable for **2–4 people** (no corners, everyone faces each other, easier to walk around). At **6–8 people** the required diameter grows enough that (a) it consumes disproportionate floor area and (b) reaching the centre of the table requires standing up. Stated remedy for a large family: an **oval, or a rectangle with rounded corners** — "посадочных мест там будет столько же, но пространство при этом будет использоваться более рационально" (the same number of seats, but a more rational use of space).

## Design Concept — Mirrors

- **⚠️ A full-height mirror behind a dressing table has a functionally dead lower portion.** Stated mechanism: it is occluded by the table or a pouffe, and the seated user cannot see it anyway, while still collecting dust, streaks and eventually scratches. **Stated remedy: start the mirror at worktop level instead** — "эффект останется почти точно таким же, но ухаживать за ней будет в разы проще" (near-identical visual effect, far easier maintenance). Presented explicitly as the speaker's own opinion ("моё мнение").

## Flooring / Surfaces — Dark and Glossy Finishes

- **⚠️ Direct contradiction of the common "dark hides dirt" belief.** Stated: on graphite or near-black floors, dust, hair, pet fur, crumbs and **light-coloured streaks** are immediately visible — often worse than on white. Gloss compounds it, because reflected light makes footprints, drips and post-cleaning streaks *more* conspicuous, not less.
- Stated worst rooms for this: **прихожая (entrance hall), кухня (kitchen), ванная комната (bathroom)** — the three highest-traffic wet/dirty zones.
- **Stated remedy: a matte finish plus a "complex"/broken shade rather than a flat one** — named examples: a dark grey ("тёмный грей"), a light stone tone, or a natural-oak tone. The principle is that a compound undertone camouflages what a flat dark or a flat white cannot.

## Windows — Windowsill Drip Edge

- **⚠️ A windowsill without a "капинос" (drip edge / nosing) is a water-management defect, not a minimalist styling choice.** Stated mechanism: the small projecting lip at the front edge stops water running back **underneath** the sill and down onto the wall below. Without it, stains appear on the reveals/slopes ("откосы") and the finish begins to fail over time.
- **Stated triggers that make it decisive**: frequent condensation on the glazing, plants standing on the sill, or rain ingress through an open window. Stated decision procedure: establish how much water contact the sill will actually get **before** agreeing to delete the drip edge for appearance.

## Kitchen — Worktops and Backsplash

- **⚠️ Price does not buy durability in worktop materials — three materials, three distinct failure modes.** Stated: **marble** is acid-sensitive (lemon, wine, aggressive cleaning chemicals leave marks); **wood** fails under sustained moisture and needs its protective coating periodically renewed; **engineered/artificial stone ("искусственный камень") is not one material** — some variants scratch quickly, others tolerate high temperature poorly. Stated selection rule: choose against **cooking habits**, not appearance or price — "если вы много и часто готовите, то более практичный материал может оказаться намного удобнее дорогого, но натурального."
- **⚠️ Brick-look tile on a kitchen backsplash — the problem is the grout count, not the tile.** Stated mechanism: the grout ("затирка") next to the hob is what actually needs repeated scrubbing (grease, sauces), and many small joints are far harder to clean than a smooth surface. **Stated remedies, in order of preference: a large-format tile with minimal joints, or a single smooth slab.** If the brick effect is wanted anyway, confine it to areas with the least splash and grease contact.

## Surfaces / Living Room — TV Wall

- **⚠️ A porcelain-stoneware slab behind a TV — two stated problems, one optical and one access-related.** (a) A glossy slab throws reflections from windows and luminaires directly into the viewing position. (b) Sockets and cabling are commonly concealed **behind** the slab, so any later relocation or repair means getting back through the finish — "получить доступ к ним будет намного сложнее."
- **⚠️ Stated sequencing rule, and the strongest planning item in the source: the whole TV zone must be resolved as one package before the slab is mounted** — finish, lighting, furniture, sockets, and specifically the equipment positions. The stated reason is that these constraints are very hard to foresee individually ("бывает очень сложно предусмотреть по отдельности"); it is their interaction that fails.

## Lighting

- **⚠️ Ring chandelier ("люстра из колец") — one proportional and three practical objections.** Proportional: over a low ceiling, a large multi-tier ring fixture visually pulls the ceiling down and breaks the room's proportions. Practical: (a) powerful LED versions can be too bright, compounded when there are many rings; (b) the multi-ring structure is very hard to clean; (c) **integrated/built-in LEDs may not be individually repairable — a failure can force replacement of the entire fixture.**
- Stated pre-purchase checklist, four items beyond the design: **fixture size, suspension height, light output/brightness, and whether individual elements can be replaced.**
- **⚠️ Cove lighting behind curtains at the ceiling amplifies defects rather than hiding them.** Stated mechanism: grazing light along the ceiling immediately reveals any unevenness in the niche or the curtain track, and any unwanted pattern in the fabric. **If individual LED dots are visible, the effect reads cheap** ("эффект сразу станет намного дешевле") — the technique depends on a diffused, dot-free strip and a genuinely true plane to graze.
- **⚠️ Named compound mistake: repeating cove lighting everywhere** — curtains, ceiling, behind furniture, TV zone — until "интерьер просто превращается в какой-то набор светящихся линий" (the interior turns into a set of glowing lines). A restraint rule for accent lighting generally, not only for curtain coves.
- **⚠️ Serviceability requirement, stated as mandatory if the technique is used at all: provide access to both the LED strip and its power supply ("блок питания")** so either can later be replaced or repaired. This is a design-time decision, because the access has to be built into the niche.

## Plumbing / Fixtures — Mixers

- **⚠️ Ultra-minimalist mixers ("ультратонкие модели с маленьким рычагом") — three distinct geometric failure modes, each with its own cause.** (a) **Spout too low** → large pots and baking trays will not fit under it. (b) **Spout too short (insufficient reach / "вылет")** → the stream does not land in the centre of the bowl. (c) **Spout too high without a pull-out spray** → water splashes out of the bowl.
- Stated pre-purchase checklist: **height, reach ("вылет"), spout swivel, and handle usability** — not appearance alone. Stated priority, verbatim: "смеситель должен быть в первую очередь удобным, потом уже красивым" (a mixer must be convenient first and beautiful second).
- **⚠️ Stated mechanism for *why* designers specify awkward mixers** — a candid second-speaker exchange worth recording as a briefing-process insight, not just a fixture note: either (a) the client saw a specific mixer, liked its appearance and insisted, leaving the designer to fit it, or (b) the designer recommends the model that suits **their own** hand and habits to every client. Stated remedy: **inspect a mixer physically ("вживую") before committing**, because ergonomic fit is individual.

## Planning Rules — The Source's Own Synthesis

- **⚠️ The closing rule, which is the generalisation behind all thirteen items:** none of these solutions is bad in itself — "проблемы всегда начинаются тогда, когда красивую идею просто переносят из проекта в реальную квартиру" — the failure is transplanting an idea from a project into a real flat **without accounting for four specific variables: floor area, the household's own habits, maintenance burden, and technical details.** Stated evaluation test for any contested design decision: judge it not by the picture but by how convenient it will be to live with **in your specific case**.
