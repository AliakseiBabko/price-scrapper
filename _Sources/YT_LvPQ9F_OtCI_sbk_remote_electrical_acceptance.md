## Mistakes / Warnings — Remote Electrical Acceptance Checklist

- **⚠️ Two acceptance paths when a client can't inspect in person, ranked explicitly**: (1) **preferred** — hire an independent technical-supervision specialist to physically inspect and approve/reject before payment; (2) **fallback** — request a structured video report from the electricians themselves covering specific, named checkpoints (below). Framed as a real fallback, not equally good — the video itself demonstrates option (2) end-to-end as a real example.
- **⚠️ Video-report checkpoint 1 — cable-run routing**: request footage of the conduit ("гофра") runs as installed, including branch-offs toward future partition walls and wet rooms (bathroom/kitchen) specifically, since these are exactly the runs a client can't otherwise verify once walls close.
- **⚠️ Video-report checkpoint 2 — junction-box connection type, a real named defect to screen for**: request footage inside every junction box ("распаечная коробка") to confirm the *agreed* connection method was actually used — Wago-style connectors ("ваги"), solder, or welding — explicitly **not** "сопли мамонта" (a slang term for bare twisted-and-taped wire splices with no proper connector, a known unsafe/low-durability shortcut). The point: a client who agreed to a specific connection method in the contract has no way to verify it was honored without this specific footage, since a closed junction-box cover looks identical either way.
- **⚠️ Video-report checkpoint 3 — outlet-box (подрозетник) count and position, tape-measure-verified**: confirm the number of boxes installed per room matches the design project's point count, and — described as the ideal, not just adequate, version of this check — have the electricians use a tape measure on camera to show each box's position against its documented coordinates, not just a general "it's roughly there" pan of the wall.
- **⚠️ Video-report checkpoint 4 — open panel cover, visual assembly-quality check**: request footage of the panel with its cover removed; the video shows a side-by-side contrast of a disorganized, badly dressed panel build versus a clean, properly bundled/labeled one as the visual standard a client should be able to recognize even without electrical training.
- **⚠️ Video-report checkpoint 5 — functional outlet/lighting test, two interchangeable methods**: either a multimeter/tester showing 220V present at each point, or (called simpler and more visually convincing for a non-technical client) a simple test-lamp ("контрольная лампочка") lighting up when touched to the point.
- **⚠️ Video-report checkpoint 6 — breaker-by-breaker functional test requires two people on camera**: one person toggles each breaker in the panel while the second, elsewhere in the apartment, confirms on camera that exactly the correct lighting/outlet group responds — verifying each breaker actually controls its intended circuit, not just that "something" works when any breaker is flipped.
- **Worked real panel-circuit breakdown shown on camera** (this project's specific example, not a universal template): 32A breaker for the water heater; a 30mA differential/RCD breaker for the balcony; a separate group for lighting; a differential breaker for washing machine, dishwasher, and bathroom outlets; and room-by-room outlet groups — offered as a concrete illustration of what a reasonably subdivided panel looks like, consistent with (not a replacement for) this store's own existing circuit-count formula.
