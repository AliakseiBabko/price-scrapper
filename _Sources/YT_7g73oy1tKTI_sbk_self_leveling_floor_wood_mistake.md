## Materials — Cement-Based Floor Leveler Is Incompatible With a Wood Subfloor

- **⚠️ Named categorical rule**: standard finish-leveling compound (финишный ровнитель) formulated for cement- or gypsum-bound screeds must never be poured directly over a wood subfloor (deck boards, plywood, existing wood flooring) — this is stated as an absolute rule, not a case-by-case judgment call.
- **⚠️ Named mechanism**: a cement/gypsum-bound screed is a dense, rigid, dimensionally stable substrate that doesn't move; wood, by contrast, expands, contracts, and absorbs/releases moisture ("breathes") with humidity changes. Once a rigid cement-based leveler is poured over wood and the wood subsequently absorbs even a small amount of moisture and swells, the rigid leveler layer **cracks/detaches (described as "popping off") because it cannot flex with the substrate underneath it**.
- **Correct product**: wood subfloors require a **specialized wood-compatible leveler formulated to be elastic ("works like rubber")** specifically so it can flex with the wood's normal moisture-driven movement without cracking.
- **Practical buyer-facing mitigation stated directly**: if you observe your own crew pouring (or about to pour) a standard cement/gypsum-rated leveler over a wood subfloor, stop the work immediately, have that crew leave, and bring in a specialist who actually understands wood-substrate leveling — framed as a real risk of losing significant money if not caught before the pour cures.
