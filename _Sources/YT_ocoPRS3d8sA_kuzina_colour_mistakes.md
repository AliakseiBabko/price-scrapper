## Design Concept — Mistake 1: ignoring the combination rules (she only half-agrees)

- **⚠️ Following the rules is not a panacea: "можно пользоваться цветовым кругом… и всё равно получить плохой интерьер."**
- **⚠️ Colour is subjective, and her criterion is ownership, not correctness: "хороший интерьер — это тот, который нравится хозяину."** Not whether an outsider likes it and not whether the rules were obeyed. **The colours must be pleasant to the person living there.**
- **Any colour combination can be justified by some compositional logic** — which is why the rules cannot settle it.
- **Her worked series, all green-and-pink, is the evidence:**
  - A textbook **triad** with every wheel rule satisfied → mediocre. **It fails on distribution and brightness — and on something not colour at all: the furniture's size and placement do not match the room's dimensions or configuration.**
  - A correct **dyad** → *"неплохой, но ничего особенного."* Spoiled specifically by **a pale-blue armchair that does not belong to the range.**
  - Dusty green with fuchsia on a cushion → **works.**
  - Two strong examples: pink and green **flowing into one another and complementing each other**, with black and golden rattan alongside; and a bolder modern scheme with **pink as the lead colour plus mustard yellow and green** — *"собран хорошо и по цветам, и по формам."*
- **⚠️ The conclusion she repeats twice, opening and closing the section: the same wheel and the same colours produce opposite results. Following the rules is right, but departing from them is not automatically an error, and obeying them guarantees nothing.**

## Design Concept — Mistake 2: more than three colours (she partly supports it)

- **She agrees with the practical version: if you are not a designer and are doing it yourself, limit the number of colours, because the more colours the harder they are to balance.**
- **⚠️ But she cross-references her own 60/30/10 video (`GEBJkzYd874`, processed in this round) for the objection: you cannot count the colours in an interior.** Her worked example has **several different blues** — is the light-blue part one colour or several? — plus yellow, a yellowish-green that may be either, and orange. *"Конкретное количество цветов, которое использовано в этом интерьере, для меня загадка."*
- **⚠️ So the real problem is not the count but the distribution: "когда цветов много, то их нужно правильно распределять по пространству, и вот в этом как раз основной затык."** Limiting the palette is a way of dodging a hard problem, not a rule about beauty.
- **⚠️ And the reassurance that matters for a self-designed flat: a limited palette does not make an interior poorer or more monotonous.** Her evidence is *the same author* delivering a good result with one emerald plus white and black (and golden rattan) as with many colours. *"Всё зависит от дизайна."*

## Labor / Delivery Model — ⚠️ Mistake 3: choosing furniture to match the walls

**The sharpest sequencing rule in the source, and it inverts the order the site imposes.**

- **On site, walls are finished first and furniture arrives afterwards.**
- **⚠️ In *design* the order is the opposite: choose furniture, upholstery, finishes and rugs FIRST, and wall paint LAST.**
- **⚠️ Her reason is a supply-side one, and it is what makes the rule stick: "спектр вариантов краски для стен всегда будет больше, чем любой набор обивок."** Paint is the most freely matchable element in the room, so it is the one that should absorb the constraint. Choose the constrained things first.

## Design Concept — Mistake 4: zoning by colour, done badly

Scoped: zoning here means **dividing one large space into functional zones** — kitchen-living-dining, living-plus-study, bedroom-plus-workspace.

- **⚠️ It usually collapses into different wall colours plus one accent wall, and she advises against that** — because **dividing a wall into colour sections that line up with your zones is quite hard.**
- **On accent walls she is measured rather than absolute: "не очень в тренде… не могу сказать, что это прямо табу," but not fashionable either.** *(Consistent with the one-accent-wall rules this vault already holds from her and from Anuta Vlady.)*
- **⚠️ The worked failure, and the diagnosis is the useful part**: a yellow patch at the back of a space, closable behind curtains — **and you want to close them, because the yellow is supported nowhere else.** It would need a yellow sofa, or yellow accents, or yellow inside a cabinet. **Her verdict, which she flags as surprising herself: "лучше бы он просто был бежевым, чем бежевым с такой жёлтой зоной, которая не поддержана ничем."** **An unsupported colour zone is worse than no colour at all.**

## Design Concept — Mistake 5: colours that are too pure

- **The purest colours are the primaries — red, yellow, blue.** Mixing them with others, or adding white, grey or black, costs them their *звонкость* and purity. **⚠️ Scoped explicitly to mixing pigments and paints, not mixing light.**
- **⚠️ The rule: the purer the colour, the more obtrusive it is. Interiors usually work on complex shades, which are calmer and easier on the eye.**
- **Few styles accept pure primaries.** The exception she names is **Bauhaus and modernism imitations**: a neutral ground of white or calm grey, often with black mixed in, wood tones, **and blue, red and yellow as the accents.** Contemporary designers riff on this creditably.
- **⚠️ Using colours that bright in other styles is a very авторский, bold move and you can miss** — better to take colours slightly less bright.

## Design Concept — ⚠️ Mistake 6: making large areas bright, and the A3 sample trap

- **The scale rule: "чем больше площадь занимает цвет, тем больше он на нас влияет."** A lime cushion and a lime wall are not the same decision.
- **⚠️ So in living spaces, if you know there will be a lot of a colour, mute it from the start.**
- **⚠️ The paint-sample trap, which is directly actionable and belongs with this vault's existing sample-testing content**: paint is chosen from an **A3 выкрас**, and **A3 is not large relative to a room.** The impression from an A3 sheet and the impression of standing in the finished room are different — **and she says this is normal, not a failure of judgment: some designers repaint several times to reach the effect.** **⚠️ Her fix: buy the paint slightly less bright than the result you want.**
- **The worked pair**: fuchsia and green read fine earlier as decorative accents; as **a huge wall** the same fuchsia *"прямо давит на сетчатку"* and the interior fails. Against that, a scheme with sea-green and bright orange **where the wall planes stay neutral golden-beige** — calmer and easier to read.
- **⚠️ The counter-caution she attaches, so the advice is not one-directional: many people start with beige walls and finish with beige. The bright colours never appear at all, "что конечно тоже не хорошо."**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ Mistake 7: distributing colours wrongly — the bank-hierarchy metaphor

**The centrepiece of the source and the most transferable framework this channel has produced. It answers the question the 60/30/10 rule only pretends to.**

**The premise: colours compete for the viewer's attention. More area pulls more attention; greater brightness pulls more attention.**

**The structure — a simplified bank**: **board of directors → top managers → heads of departments → rank-and-file staff.**

**⚠️ Start with the board: the brightest or largest-area colours.** The questions to answer: which surfaces are there most of, which will matter most in the design; and of floor, walls and furniture — which colour is the brightest, which the most passive, which occupies the most area.

**The four failure modes, each with a worked interior:**

| Failure | What it looks like |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The whole bank is the board** | All colours equally bright and roughly equal in area |
| **Board plus a couple of rank-and-file, nothing between** | Her example: a strong scheme with only some pink cushions below it. **The gap is too large and the range is not stretched out** |
| **Board plus top managers only** | More logical, more harmonious — **but still odd** |
| **Only two tiers, evenly spread** | A bedroom with apple green distributed evenly throughout. Harmonious, does not fall apart, **but boring — there is no real difference between the colours** |

**⚠️ The success case, read tier by tier — this is the part worth copying:**

- **Board** — bright green armchairs and bright green curtains (what hits the eye first).
- **Top managers** — black wall panels.
- **Heads of departments** — the details: a pouffe in the foreground, a table, decorative cushions on the armchairs.
- **Rank-and-file** — the neutral parts, **occupying a fairly large area**: a neutral rug, a greyish-green wall colour, a greenish lampshade.

**⚠️ The extension — the outside contractor.** A bank with no in-house web designer hires one in. Likewise an interior can carry **episodic, more arbitrary colours that can later be swapped** — her example is a fuchsia cushion that could become a muted orange with nothing else changing. **But two constraints:**

- **"Приглашённый сотрудник не должен соревноваться за власть с советом директоров"** — and the mechanism is stated: **the more a colour differs from its surroundings, the more attention it pulls.**
- **⚠️ "Банки не приглашают подрядчиков просто с улицы."** They select — the contractor may specialise in bank sites, or have worked for that bank before. **So decor colours are freer, but not arbitrary; you cannot take any colour at all and expect it to work.**

**⚠️ And the proportion rule that falls out of the metaphor, which is its answer to 60/30/10: there are fewer bosses than staff, so do not divide the areas equally.** *"Не надо равномерно делить площади, которые занимают разные цвета. Начальников меньше, но при этом они ярче и контрастнее."*

**Her own summary of why the green-and-pink series came out so differently: "проблема именно в этом — в яркости и в количественных сочетаниях."**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ Mistake 8: trying to match colour exactly («цвет в цвет»)

- Her recurring character **"условный Алексей"** buys a kitchen and hunts for cushions that hit the facade colour exactly. **It is hard, and usually does not improve the design even if he succeeds.**
- **⚠️ What good practice looks like instead** (Florence Lopes): many similar-but-different blues. **"Не стоит задача сделать так, чтобы это был один-единственный синий, идеально правильный. Она даёт спектр, она даёт гамму" — and the interior is livelier for it.**
- **⚠️ The one case where exact matching does work, and why it is not reproducible**: dusty pink on wallpaper, lampshade and foreground cushion at near-identical colour — **but carried by an emphasised play of textures.** She calls it *"очень сложное дизайнерское решение, которое повторить без подготовки нереально."* **Ordinary repetition of one green or red across two objects works much worse.**
- **⚠️ The failure mode, and the diagnosis generalises past colour**: apple green on a bedspread and on a bag, near-identical. The interior reads **predictable and laboured** — you can see the search that rejected slightly warmer and cooler greens. **"Интерьер теряет ощущение расслабленности и спонтанности. Видно, что очень напрягались."** Effort that shows is a defect.

## Design Concept — Mistake 9: ignoring what the room is for

- **⚠️ She calls colour psychology "лже-наука"** in her own view — *(consistent with the Perspectives block this vault already holds on whether per-hue colour psychology is usable at all, where she is the sceptic against Kodolov)*.
- **The stereotypes she reports and then relativises**: warm colours for daytime and active zones (eating, receiving guests) — yellowish and orange; cool colours for private zones (bedrooms, studies, private bathrooms) — light blue, turquoise, lilac, greenish. **"Эти правила нарушаются с проше рядом"** — a pinkish bedroom instead of an olive one is not thereby bad.
- **⚠️ But purpose does bind in specific cases**, and these are physical rather than psychological:
  - **The fuchsia bedroom wall is especially bad in a bedroom** because such colours *сильно будоражат* — she cannot imagine sleeping there.
  - **⚠️ Where makeup is applied, over-bright colours throw colour reflexes onto the face.**
  - **⚠️ Where people concentrate, work or read, bright colours distract, and throw reflexes onto light surfaces, which is irritating.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ Mistake 10: choosing colours room by room — the musical metaphor

**The palette must be treated as a single whole.** Her analogy is a musical, and it is more precise than the usual "the flat should flow":

- Within one song, verses fit the choruses; **the songs are ordered into a plot**; all are stylised to a period; the characters are dressed to match and sing in one key; **a motif recurs from song to song — a reprise; and the cast is limited, so the same voices return.** That is what makes the work whole.
- **⚠️ The consequence for an interior, and it is more permissive than the rule it replaces: you need NOT repeat one colour room to room** — not everyone in a musical sings bass — **and you need not even keep one palette. It is enough that the colours have something in common: one saturation, or one warmth.**
- **Worked positive example — designer Wesley Moon**, a large project she rates highly. Pinkish, fuchsia and plum shades recur room to room; the living room is more restrained with more beiges, **but the beiges lean pink**; the owners' bedroom has a plum bedspread and a similar bed; the daughters' bedrooms are again pink-dominant with others added, **and the ground is always a neutral pinkish beige.**
- **⚠️ The subtlest observation in the source**: in the same property, her study and his study use similar shades **but feel entirely different** — hers airy, the pink reading slightly frivolous; his more serious and more contrasting, **and the pinks do not read girly there because they are a saturated dark plum.** **The same hue family carries opposite registers depending on saturation and contrast.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ Mistakes 11 and 12: memory, and colour names

**These two are a pair, both worked through her character Алексей, and both are directly actionable procedure.**

- **⚠️ Never choose colours from memory.** Алексей goes to choose bed upholstery **without taking the wallpaper with him**, remembering only that it was "some grey-beige." **Remembering a colour is hard.**
  - **⚠️ She runs a live experiment on the viewer to prove it** — memorise the palette shown from the Wesley Moon owners' bedroom, then a minute later identify it among **four deliberately similar options.** **The answer is number 4.** Her point: *"Прошла всего минута… А если бы прошёл день? А если бы прошла неделя? Выбирать цвета по памяти никогда не следует."*
- **⚠️ Never rely on a colour's name.** Алексей remembers his wallpaper was called «песочные», pictures beach sand somewhere — Bali, or maybe Crimea — then finds a paint swatch also called «Песочная» and assumes it will match.
  - **Everyone's idea of sand colour differs**, and sand itself varies enormously.
  - **⚠️ Her explanation of why the names are worthless is the memorable part: the people inventing them "придумывают их просто вот так, потому что им надо придумать по 50 названий в день," and sand inevitably features.** **"Названия лучше не читать."**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ Mistake 13: ignoring style, which she says is on no online list

**Colour and style are linked, and she flags this as her own addition to the received list.**

- **Beach house**: find ten beach-house interiors and they are all in one range — **creamy beiges, light blues, greenish and sea-green.** Very recognisable.
- **Modernism**: the **Mondrian palette** — black, white and the primaries, built on pure colours.
- **⚠️ Swap one style's native palette for another's and the style may simply stop reading.** Beach house is not only a palette — it also has **simple but slightly classically-inclined forms, and old wood** — **but that may not be enough: "если вы резко поменяете цвет, то стиль может уйти вслед за ним."**

## Design Concept — Mistake 14: fearing dark colours

- **The stereotype**: dark colours narrow a space and eat light and colour. **Sometimes true — "но далеко не все пространства нуждаются в том, чтобы их расширяли, и не все пространства должны быть светлыми."**
- **⚠️ Dark colours often read richer, dressier and more original than light ones. They lend mystery, they can dissolve boundaries, and they can give an interior depth.** Do not reject them out of hand. *(Directly supports the Perspectives block this vault already holds on whether a dark room should be painted light or dark, where she is on the against-lightening side.)*

## Design Concept — Mistake 15: following trends

**Her position is more permissive than the received one, and she says so:**

- **If a colour is fashionable and you also like it, take it.** She adds a genuinely practical argument: **you will be able to find wallpaper, cushions, upholstery, rugs and everything else in a fashionable colour — the market is full of it.**
- **⚠️ The condition: you must actually like it and be prepared to live with it for at least a couple of years.** Using a colour purely because it is trending is the error.
- **⚠️ And the fix if the fashion is the attraction — a callback to the bank metaphor: use it as an episodic accessory, "как приглашённый дизайнер сайтов в банк," so the items chosen in the fashionable colour are easy to replace.**

## Design Concept — Mistake 16: thinking it is only about colour

- **Sometimes an interior is bad not only, and not even mainly, because of its colour.** Change the colours to something acceptable and it improves only slightly, **because the form is not fixed and the style is not fixed.**
- Her example is a strange eclecticism that looks like **«сосиска под малиновым соусом»** — *"наверное, есть любители сосиски под малиновым соусом, и мы как раз нашли одного из них."*

## Rules / Heuristics — ⚠️ the closing methodological statement

**"Цвет воспринимается всегда относительно других цветов, которые его окружают."**

**And the consequence she draws for herself, which is a useful standard for this vault too**: she does not answer viewer questions of the form *"у меня будут зелёные стены — будут ли с ними сочетаться серые шторы?"* — **because "зелёный" and "серый" are too abstract to answer with. It requires far more nuance than naming two colours.**

## Mistakes / Warnings — condensed list

1. Treating the colour wheel as sufficient. 2. Counting colours instead of distributing them. 3. Choosing furniture to match already-painted walls. 4. A colour zone supported nowhere else. 5. Pure primaries outside Bauhaus/modernism. 6. Judging a wall colour from an A3 sample. 7. Equal areas and equal brightness — a bank of only directors. 8. Matching colour exactly across two objects. 9. Bright colour where makeup is applied or where people read. 10. Choosing each room's palette separately. 11. Choosing colour from memory. 12. Choosing colour by its name. 13. Changing a style's palette and expecting the style to survive. 14. Rejecting dark colours reflexively. 15. Taking a colour only because it is trending. 16. Expecting a colour change to fix a form-and-style problem.
