## Genuinely New Content

- **Clinker tile (клинкер) is a style-specific material, not general-purpose**: best suited to loft-style interiors (white/black/wood palette), not recommended broadly. Prep is identical to porcelain tile (plain flat plaster). **Real jointing technique**: ~1cm joint width maintained with foam spacers ("пенопластовые чопики"), filled with clinker-specific grout via a grout gun or a cut-corner piping bag, then finger-smoothed. **⚠️ Aesthetic rule flagged as dated if violated**: clinker must run genuinely wall-to-wall/edge-to-edge as a complete surface — stopping it abruptly mid-wall reads as an outdated 1990s look ("suddenly cut off"), not a modern finish.
- **Porcelain tile (керамогранит) recommended as a general-purpose material usable anywhere, not just wet zones** — a single accent wall in a living room is explicitly endorsed as stylish. **Recommended format: 60×120cm**, framed as the price/appearance sweet spot: a larger slab (mega-format) only removes ~3 extra horizontal joints from view while costing dramatically more once delivery and installation labor are counted — not worth the tradeoff, per the presenter's own stated preference. Available in dark grey, black, white, marble-look, and light grey; can run vertical or horizontal.
- **⚠️ Real DIY "concrete look" relief-texture technique using plain gypsum plaster, no specialized decorative-plaster product**: install a temporary track/guide profile on the wall, drag ordinary gypsum plaster along it to build a textured relief pattern, then remove the profile — yields a raw-concrete-look surface from standard plaster alone. Optionally painted, and (in a kitchen specifically) clear-lacquered afterward for washability.
- **Roller-nap recommendation for paint, contrary to a "smoother is better" assumption**: recommend a medium-nap roller over a minimal-nap one — a perfectly glass-smooth paint finish demands flawless underlying execution and shows every minor defect, while a slight roller texture better conceals both installer-skill limitations and lower-quality paint's own flaws.
- **Concealed/hidden door painted to match an adjoining painted wall will always show a slight texture mismatch**, even at an identical paint color — the door's smooth factory surface absorbs paint differently than the rougher plastered wall surface. Flagged as a real, unavoidable minor limitation worth setting expectations about with detail-sensitive clients in advance.
- **⚠️ Decorative plaster's spot-repairability advantage over paint, a genuinely new practical distinction for this store**: a scratch or scuff on decorative plaster can be self-touched-up by the resident with a small localized patch that blends in seamlessly; the same defect on a painted wall almost never allows a clean spot-touch-up — touching up a scratch on paint typically leaves a visible patch, forcing a full repaint from corner to corner of that wall. A concrete, practical reason to favor decorative plaster in higher-damage-risk areas.
- **Cross-room color-continuity design technique**: an accent material/color used in one space (e.g. an orange clinker wall) is deliberately echoed by a same-hued accent visible from an adjoining sightline (e.g. an orange-painted corridor wall visible from the room with the clinker) — everything else kept neutral (white/grey/black) so the one echoed accent reads as a deliberate visual thread between spaces, not a coincidence.
- **Mirror-wall substrate requirement varies by mounting method**: a glued mirror needs only a flat plastered wall; a mirror using specific mounting hardware needs a puttied (smoother) finish instead. Distinguishes a purely decorative accent mirror (this object's case) from the more familiar space-expanding mirror use.
- **Adjustable-opacity light-transmitting partition, a real space-perception technique**: a partition that can be selectively darkened/obscured lets one room alternate between an open, distant-sightline "larger space" feel and a fully enclosed, intimate feel — described as changing the room's perceived dimensions noticeably depending on the partition's current state.
