## Rules / Heuristics — Genuinely New Content

- **⚠️ Color temperature master rule**: never exceed 4000K anywhere residential; comfortable range 2700-4000K; **3000K is the universal default**; reserve neutral ~4000K for pure task zones (laundry, walk-in closet, storage, kitchen worktop task lighting).
- **⚠️ Same-scene fixtures must share one color temperature** — a 3000K ceiling fixture running simultaneously with a 4000K sconce reads as visibly mismatched/cheap even if each fixture alone is fine.
- **⚠️ Evening/decorative lighting temperature must match across the whole home**, not just within one room — a hallway at a different evening-light temperature than the bedroom/living room stands out the moment you move between rooms.
- **⚠️ Both over- and under-powering a fixture is a real, mechanism-explained mistake**: overly bright evening light measurably disrupts melatonin production/circadian rhythm (harder to fall asleep); underpowered light in a zone that needs brightness (entry, bathroom, closet) causes eye strain and fatigue.
- **⚠️ "Golden rule": every frequently-used room needs a minimum of 3 distinct lighting scenarios**, not 1-2 fixtures on a single switch group — the single most common lighting mistake per this source. Worked scenario sets given for bathroom (4: main/task, decorative/evening, mirror, night), kitchen-living (3 + separate worktop and dining-table task lighting), bedroom (3-4: main/task, evening/decorative, individual reading light, optional TV-viewing bias light), hallway (4: main, evening, mirror, navigation/night).
- **⚠️ Cinema-mode bias lighting, mechanism explained**: light the wall/zone around the TV (not the whole room) to reduce eye strain from repeatedly readjusting to the room-dark/screen-bright contrast — a small sconce, a lit niche, or a color-matching bias-lighting product all work; applies to both living-room and bedroom TVs.
- **⚠️ Bedroom motion-sensor night-light false-trigger caution**: a motion sensor near the bed fires constantly on incidental motion (a foot/arm hanging off the edge, a dropped blanket corner) — wire bedroom night lighting to a manual switch instead. Distinct from a hallway/corridor motion sensor, which doesn't share this failure mode.
- **⚠️ Bedside reading light should be directional, aimed precisely at the activity** (book/phone/laptop), ideally with adjustable brightness — the source's stated best implementation, not a generic ambient bedside lamp.
- **⚠️ No spot/track fixture directly above a reclining position — generalized across rooms**: extends the existing bathtub-reclining-glare and bedroom-central-pendant cautions already in this store to sofas and any spot/track (not just pendant) fixture over a bed; if a fixture must go there, use a narrow, carefully-aimed optic.
- **⚠️ Fixture buying criteria beyond color temperature**: brightness/lumens, beam angle, CRI, smart-home protocol compatibility (e.g. DALI), lens presence — check before buying, not from a catalog photo alone; a full lighting-designer calculation is close to mandatory for a business-plus/premium project, less necessary for economy/comfort tier.
- **⚠️ Mirror-light color temperature: 4000K specifically** (distinct from the page's general 2700-3000K default) — renders skin tone most accurately for grooming/makeup. Pendant fixtures work but are fragile; wall sconces or frame-integrated lights are preferred. Light must come from the front, not from behind the mirror or from a ceiling fixture positioned behind the user (both cases fail to light the face properly, the latter by casting the user's own head-shadow onto their face).
- **⚠️ Avoid a decorative/frosted-glass sconce at the mirror/sink position** — scatters light into small decorative shadows that work against the goal of evenly lighting the face; use a plain diffuser at this specific position.
- **⚠️ Never place a ceiling spot directly above a showerhead** — the fixture ends up behind/above the water stream and the showerhead's own body shadows the user standing beneath it; position a shower spot to the side instead.
- **⚠️ Kitchen worktop LED-strip placement, ranked**: facade-edge mounting (best — closest to the cabinet front, directly above the work zone, minimum shadow) → mid-cabinet mounting (worse) → recessed-near-backsplash mounting (worst, casts the most shadow). Fine to interrupt strip continuity at a range hood rather than sacrifice facade-edge position.
- **⚠️ Never light an upper mezzanine/antresol cabinet tier** — mounted too high, it glares into the eyes of anyone using the sofa/dining table below with no compensating benefit; light only the main/lower cabinet tier.
