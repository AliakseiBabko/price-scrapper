## Kitchen, Furniture/Built-ins, Materials, Mistakes/Warnings

- **⚠️ Narrow bottle/spice cabinet insert ("узкая секция под маслёнки/специи")** — critiqued with a cited (vaguely-sourced) storage-science claim: sunflower oil should be refrigerated, not stored at room temperature near the stove; spices can go in a regular upper cabinet — so this narrow, structurally-more-complex (specialized hardware, higher cost) insert is largely unused/wasted. Recommends a standard drawer instead — cheaper, same useful capacity.
- **⚠️ Lower cabinets, hinged doors vs. drawers — corroborates Round 9 drawer-preference content, with a genuinely new rental-vs-owned decision tier**: cheaper hinged-door cabinets for a rental/investment unit, drawers unconditionally for an owner-occupied home.
- Open dish-drying racks left on the countertop — wastes counter space, visually messy ("belongs in the cabinet above the sink"), commonly seen in short-term rentals specifically.
- **⚠️ Bar-height counter + full dining table combined in a small kitchen-living space — a specific real threshold**: recommends ONE for compact layouts; this combination genuinely works only once the combined kitchen-living area reaches roughly 50–60 m², where the dining table can be placed well clear of the kitchen counter zone.
- **⚠️ Kitchen islands in small apartments — a specific, non-obvious real infrastructure/plumbing constraint**: in an APARTMENT (unlike a private house), plumbing for an island sink or extraction/hood commonly needs to run to a floor waste stack at a slope, which typically requires RAISING the floor level in that zone — a real, concrete reason islands with plumbing are often impractical in apartments specifically, beyond simple square-footage. Recommends against installing an island purely for the aspirational idea of "cooking at an island" without this infrastructure reality in mind.
- **⚠️ Unused kitchen gadgets/appliances — a specific design-sequencing principle**: plan DEDICATED zones for specific appliances (coffee machine, kettle) at the design stage so the REAL remaining usable counter space is known upfront — concrete example given: a 1.2m run reduced to just 30cm of usable counter after appliances are added post-hoc. Ties to the individual-behavior-pattern/psychology framing recurring across this channel (cf. Round 9's archetype video).
- **⚠️ Particleboard (ДСП/chipboard) countertops with visible plastic corner-joint trim strips** — cheapest option, visually unattractive; functionally risky: if a silicone seal fails (which it eventually does), moisture swells the chipboard core, requiring full countertop replacement. Recommends avoiding chipboard countertops regardless of budget in favor of any solid/composite alternative.
- **⚠️ Backsplash rail/utensil-hanging systems ("рейлинги") — a nuanced, style-contextual take, distinct from Round 9's `ddRN-vIf3iI` flatter "avoid it" critique**: rails ARE contextually appropriate in certain styles (e.g. American/country-kitchen style) where they read as functional and stylistically coherent, but are genuinely wrong in minimalist, Japandi, or Art Deco contexts, where the visual-clutter/grease-buildup downside isn't offset by any stylistic payoff. The real principle is CONTEXT-DEPENDENCE, not a blanket rule — flagged as a qualification of the existing wiki entry.
- **⚠️ Open shelving/storage — restates the dust/grease-magnet critique (requires wiping down twice weekly) but gives a specific, narrow, genuinely novel exception**: legitimate only in a "parade kitchen" ("парадная кухня") — a real practice in some wealthy Russian/CIS households of maintaining a SEPARATE "black kitchen" ("чёрная кухня") where actual cooking happens (often staffed by a private chef), while the visible "parade" kitchen serves only a display/coffee-making function and is maintained by household staff.
