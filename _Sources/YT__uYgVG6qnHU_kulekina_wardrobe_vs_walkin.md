# Source Note - Ольга Кулекина: ⚠️⚠️ five named conditions under which a wardrobe beats a walk-in — the vault had the finding, not the decision rule (YouTube _uYgVG6qnHU)

## Evidence levels
(1) transcript text - (2) YouTube metadata - (3) contextual inference - (4) external validation (none)

**⚠️⚠️ Why this is worth a note despite low novelty per item: the vault already holds the CONCLUSION — a real case where two wardrobes beat one walk-in, and a measured ~2.5× space cost for equal capacity. What it did not hold is the set of conditions that PREDICT which way a given room goes.** This source is structured as exactly that, from a designer arguing against the fashionable option.

## ⚠️⚠️ 1. The area threshold — and the mechanism behind it

> ***«Если под гардеробную вы можете выделить только 3–4 м² или меньше, то отдельно стоящий платяной шкаф будет лучше.»***

**The mechanism she gives is the one that matters, because it is what the area figure is a proxy for:**

- **A walk-in must be WALKED INTO, so part of its floor is circulation and cannot hold anything** — *«часть площади уйдёт на проход, и общая ёмкость хранения вещей уменьшится»*.
- **⚠️ The partitions that create it consume flat area too** — a cost paid before any storage exists.
- **⚠️ And a counter-intuitive advantage for the wardrobe: you can stand FURTHER BACK from it** — *«расстояние до шкафа будет больше, чтобы посмотреть вещи»* — because the room it opens into is the viewing distance. **A walk-in supplies its own viewing distance out of its own floor.**

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ Corroborates the vault's measured ~2.5× figure with an explanation rather than another measurement**, and the explanation is what transfers to a different room shape. **Her instruction before deciding: measure the space and draw the layout** — *«обязательно сделайте замеры помещения и нарисуйте планировку»*.

## ⚠️⚠️ 2. Geometry can defeat the area test — the 80 cm rule

> ***«Даже при площади больше 4 м²… всё равно может быть неудобно, если это помещение, например, узкое вытянутое. Для комфортного использования такого помещения перед местами хранения нужно оставить проход не менее 80 см. И то, в некоторых случаях даже лучше больше, чтобы подальше отойти и видеть всю одежду целиком.»***

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ The genuinely new number, and the more useful of the two.** **Area is not the test; the aisle in front of the storage is.** A 5 m² walk-in that is long and narrow can fail where a 3.5 m² squarer one succeeds. **80 cm minimum aisle, more if you want to see a full outfit at once** — the second clause is the one that says *why* more is better, and it is the same "you need viewing distance" mechanism as §1.
>
> ⚠️ **This also gives the vault its first stated aisle figure for a walk-in.** Compare with [[_Sources/YT_etHFnxvLiyM_udesign_small_hallway|U.DESIGN's]] 70 cm minimum passage in a hallway, in this same batch — different rooms, different tasks (passing through vs. standing and choosing), and the 10 cm gap between them is consistent with that.

## ⚠️ 3. The cost comparison people get wrong

> ***«Общая стоимость строительства гардеробной комнаты, включая её наполнение, часто оказывается выше, чем у шкафа аналогичного объёма.»***

**Her point is about what gets counted:** the internal modules may well be cheap, but the walk-in also requires *«перегородки, закладные, гипсокартон, различные материалы, электрика, вентиляция»* — **construction, not just furniture.**

- ⚠️ **She hedges honestly: *«многое зависит от выбранного производителя»*, and premium Italian or German wardrobes can invert the comparison.**
- **⚠️ Her instruction: choose the model and the storage-system manufacturer FIRST, then cost it fully.**

> **→ ⚠️ The transferable point: a walk-in's price sits partly in the RENOVATION budget and partly in the FURNITURE budget, and comparing it against a wardrobe using only the furniture line understates it.** Electrics and ventilation in particular are easy to omit from a mental comparison. **No figures given, so nothing enters the vault under rule 2.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 4. Dust and allergies — the argument the vault did not have at all

**Walk-ins are usually built OPEN inside** — you enter and reach the clothes without opening further doors, *«что действительно целесообразно и удобно»*. **But open shelving means clothes sit in room dust**, particularly things stored a long time.

- **⚠️⚠️ Where there are allergies — hers or the children's — she says the closed wardrobe is the better object**, because the doors keep the clothes clean.
- **Partial mitigations she names, in order: garment covers (чехлы); fitting the walk-in with fronts, partly or fully; and —**
- **⚠️⚠️ the real fix: приточно-вытяжная ventilation in the walk-in, which she says *«решит проблему пыли практически полностью»*, and which she recommends for the whole flat, speaking as an owner of such a system.**

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ This is the item that most changes the decision, because it is the one nobody weighs in advance**, and it converts the wardrobe-vs-walk-in question into a **ventilation** question. **Directly relevant to this project: it ties a furniture choice to [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/HVAC_and_Ventilation|the ventilation design]], which is settled far earlier and far more expensively.** ⚠️ **Note her ventilation claim is experience plus advocacy, with no measurement offered.**

## ⚠️ 5. Resale and mobility — the wardrobe is an asset, the walk-in is not

**If the flat is temporary or a sale is likely, a freestanding wardrobe *«можно продать отдельно, даже если он дорогой, или забрать с собой в новое жильё»*. A walk-in is built-in and goes with the flat.**

- **⚠️ And the second-order point: a walk-in's internal configuration is usually made to ONE household's requirements and *«может не подойти новым жильцам»*** — so it may not even add the value its cost implies.
- She acknowledges many systems are adjustable (moving shelves, adding rails) **but says in some cases they are not.**

> **→ A finance argument the vault's storage pages did not carry. ⚠️ Recorded as her position; no resale figures, no market data.**
