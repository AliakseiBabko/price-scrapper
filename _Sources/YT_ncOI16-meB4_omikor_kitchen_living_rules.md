## Design Concept — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Combined kitchen-living-room zoning is gated by total room area,
  with concrete thresholds stated**: under ~20m² is too small to hold
  three distinct zones (kitchen, dining, living/TV) comfortably; ~10m²
  can support a kitchen + dining pairing but not a separate living zone
  as well; ~50m² gives real room to spread the three zones out; **~35m²
  is cited as a genuinely workable size for a family of four (two
  children)** — split with kitchen+dining on one side (typically the side
  nearer the entry) and the living/TV zone on the other.
- **Dining-table clearance rule, with a specific figure**: leave a
  minimum of **1.1m** clear on both sides of any dining table for chair
  pull-out and seating, **1.2m preferred** for genuine comfort — stated as
  a fixed rule regardless of table size.
- **Kitchen-zone footprint should scale with actual cooking intensity,
  not a fixed default**: an active/frequent cook needing a food
  processor, juicer, full oven, and large fridge justifies a larger
  kitchen footprint (potentially an L-shaped "corner" kitchen), leaving
  less room for dining; a light/occasional cook can work with a minimal
  footprint (a small sink, ~60cm of worktop, a 2-burner cooktop, a
  fridge), freeing more floor area for the dining zone instead. **General
  principle: size the kitchen zone against the household's actual cooking
  behavior, not the reverse (assuming dining/living space first and
  fitting the kitchen into whatever's left).**
- **Tall column-style cabinets are recommended as the most space-
  efficient storage option** in a combined kitchen-living zone, holding
  more volume than an equivalent footprint of standard base + wall
  cabinets — a concrete storage-density heuristic.
- **When a kitchen and living room form one continuous, visually open
  space, unify the design language across both** — kitchen cabinet
  facades should coordinate with the living-room furniture facades,
  either matching exactly or using different materials that visually
  complement each other; treating the kitchen as a stylistically separate
  zone from the living area is explicitly discouraged once the space is
  physically open.
- **Plan distinct lighting scenarios per zone within the same open
  space**: brighter, more functional task lighting over the kitchen work
  zone plus general ceiling light; a pendant/chandelier specifically over
  the dining table; dimmer, softer ambient lighting in the living zone —
  three deliberately different lighting characters coexisting in one
  physically continuous room.
- **Flooring-material zoning is area-dependent**: if the combined space
  is large enough, using different flooring per zone (e.g. tile in the
  kitchen, parquet/wood-look in the living area) is workable; if the
  space is small, use one flooring material throughout for visual
  continuity, generally favoring a wear-resistant option (ceramic/tile)
  as the safer default when unifying.
