## Mistakes / Warnings — 8 Comfort Principles

- **⚠️ Warm light in the 2700–3000K range is associated with sunset/relaxation/safety via an evolutionary framing**: he frames this as a historical/evolutionary transition (daylight → warm sunset light meant the end of the active day and the start of rest across "millions of years of evolution"), which is why warm light reads as relaxing/safe/domestic rather than a mere preference. **Extends this vault's existing 2700–4000K comfortable-range content (already recorded on `12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Lighting_Design.md`) — the Kelvin range overlaps, but the evolutionary-association mechanism/framing is new.**
- **⚠️ Natural materials (wood, stone/marble/terrazzo, cork, woven natural-fiber baskets/rugs) read as calming via a similar evolutionary "connection to nature" framing.** **Names Peter Zumthor's writing on atmosphere as an explicit reference**: atmosphere is the interaction of light, material, sound, temperature, scale, and duration of stay — a real, nameable citation worth preserving.
- **⚠️ Texture variety is necessary even within a minimalist palette — flat/textureless surfaces read as visually "empty"/fatiguing.** Recommends combining wood + stone + glass + metal; using limewash paint ("известковая краска," Ель Уош/L Wash) for a "living," unevenly textured wall finish; and **a distinct, nameable lighting technique — raking/directional light across a textured plaster or stone surface creates a visible light-and-shadow relief pattern** that reads as more expensive/handmade. Recorded as its own technique, distinct from the general layered-lighting principle.
- **Dappled/uneven light (forest-canopy-style light through leaves) reads as more "alive"/comfortable than flat, even lighting** — a genuinely new psychological rationale for why layered lighting works, attached to this vault's existing lighting-design content as an added mechanism rather than a duplicate of the fixture-count rule.
- **⚠️ Fire/flame named as the single most psychologically comforting light source** (evolutionary safety association with the campfire). Real fireplace groupings recommended in houses, tied to an actual usage scenario (evening reading, family gathering), not pure decor. In apartments where a full fireplace isn't feasible, recommends **grouped candles (ideally wood-wick, for the crackling sound) rather than a single candle**, for a similar effect.
- **⚠️ Prospect-refuge theory ("теория обзора и укрытия")** — the single most valuable new concept in this round. Humans are subconsciously most comfortable in a position with an open view ahead and a solid backing behind/beside them (a defensive/territorial instinct, paralleled to shade under a tree or a niche in rock vs. an open field). **Concrete applications**: (a) a desk should face the room's entrance with a solid wall/cabinet behind the chair, not the reverse — he cites his own office setup and the generic "executive office" convention (desk facing the door, a wall of status portraits behind); sitting with one's back to the door is stated to keep the mind in a low-grade subconscious tension (waiting for someone to enter/knock), a real if usually unnoticed "microstress" that affects concentration and a felt sense of control. (b) a living-room sofa should have a solid backing (a wall, a console, or a deliberate partition/screen when the sofa is used to zone an open-plan kitchen-living space) rather than floating with nothing behind it. **Worked example for an open kitchen-living layout (ЖК Шагал project)**: a rug clearly demarcating the living-zone territory under the sofa, combined with a lighter-lit zone for the seating area against a darker-lit surrounding fond — together reading as a sense of protection/defined territory even without a physical partition.
- **⚠️ Curved/organic forms (rounded sofas, oval tables, radiused walls/mirrors) read as calming/enclosing because nature has very few straight lines** (horizon lines are soft, hill silhouettes are rounded) — cites BNB Italia/Minotti as brands working in this "flowing, viscous geometry." **Sharp/rectilinear forms read as more energetic/dynamic/status-projecting** (cites Poliform/Molteni-adjacent brands for clean architectural graphics: glass, metal, strict proportions) but **carry a real injury-risk caveat, especially for small children running into corners.** A genuinely useful style-intent rule (curves for calm/rest, angles for energy/status) with a safety caveat attached.
