## Materials

- **Why prime a ceiling before finishing (post-demolition, before any
  stretch-ceiling or paint work)**: (1) binds accumulated dust so it
  doesn't later fall/show through a stretch-ceiling membrane; (2)
  prevents loose debris from pores/voids in the raw ceiling surface from
  shedding over time; (3) reduces the surface's paint absorbency (so a
  painted ceiling won't visibly "show through" a stretch-ceiling
  installed over it); (4) improves adhesion for any subsequent coating.
  `confirmed`, stated as a mandatory minimum step, not optional.
- **Concentrated primer dilution ratios — a single 10L canister of
  concentrate replaces carrying separate primers for each use case (1)**,
  per the named materials-company technologist:
  - **1:1** (concentrate:water) — adhesion primer for non-absorbent
    surfaces.
  - **1:3** — floor primer, sized specifically so a self-leveling
    compound spreads and bonds well afterward.
  - **1:5 to 1:6** — general-purpose strengthening primer (walls).
  - **1:7 to 1:8** — deep-penetration primer (ceilings, in this video's
    own use case).
  - **1:10 plus mixed into a compound** — replacing ~8-10% of a mix's
    water with concentrate turns the concentrate into a **latex additive**
    that measurably improves the properties of even inexpensive base
    materials.
  - **Stated tradeoff/caution**: a 10L concentrate canister is a
    meaningful cost outlay, and buying it for a small single-room job is
    explicitly called uneconomical — the concentrate will go unused and
    the money is effectively wasted; the concentrated-primer approach
    pays off primarily at whole-apartment/multi-room scale where multiple
    dilution ratios are actually needed across the project.
- **Ceiling-priming application method comparison — sprayer vs. roller,
  demonstrated side by side (1)**:
  - **Classical/roller method** (described as the outdated method
    professionals no longer use): requires two roller sizes (large,
    ~18mm nap, 25cm wide, plus a small roller) each with a telescoping
    extension pole, a corner roller (explicitly noted as unable to
    reach true corners the way a brush can — two ~50mm brushes are also
    needed for corners), a primer bucket (not a roller tray — ceiling
    primer consumption is too high for a shallow tray), plus PPE (shower
    cap, goggles, gloves, a rain poncho) and a stiff brush to knock loose
    debris off the ceiling before priming. Needs two people in practice.
  - **Sprayer method** (the source's recommended default): a 5-10L
    garden pump sprayer filled with the diluted primer. Demonstrated
    result: full ceiling coverage including corners and hard-to-reach
    spots in **~15 minutes**, versus longer with the roller method for
    comparable coverage — explicitly stated as faster than the
    roller method took for only a partial ceiling in the same video.
    Advantages stated: one person instead of two; less wasted primer
    (roller method drips/pools on the floor); every corner and
    hard-to-reach area gets reliably coated since a fine spray reaches
    where a brush/roller physically struggles to. **Explicit
    "don't undershoot" guidance**: apply primer liberally rather than
    sparingly — the surface will only absorb what it needs, and stopping
    once the surface visibly can't absorb more ("until it starts to
    pool") is the stated end-point, not a fixed coverage rate.
