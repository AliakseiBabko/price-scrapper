## ⚠️⚠️ 1. A parts schedule generated FROM the model geometry — and why it worked

The worked task is a toy (an image turned into a Lego-brick structure), but one requested output is not a toy: **a PDF breaking down piece count, colours, catalogue entries and the shape of each piece.** In his description of the result:

> *"It shows exactly how many individual pieces there are. Shows how many material colors, catalog entries. It even breaks down how many of each of these different colored blocks we have… It even breaks down the exact shape of each."*

**⚠️ The stated reason it is derivable is the part that transfers, and he says it plainly:** *"the way that we are able to understand how many individual pieces make up this model is by using Blender."*

> **→ THE SCHEDULE COMES FROM THE GEOMETRY BEING INDIVIDUATED, NOT FROM THE RENDER.** Each brick is a separate selectable object, so counting is a traversal. **This is the same mechanism as a quantity takeoff**, and it is the cheap half — count, group by type, group by material. **It corroborates the cost-attachment analysis already held in [[18_Digital_Toolchain/analysis/Quantity_Takeoff_and_Cost_Join|Quantity Takeoff and Cost Join]]: a countable schedule needs objects that exist separately and carry a type, and nothing more clever than that.**

**⚠️ And the limit is equally clear.** A brick count is exact by construction because bricks are discrete and identical. **Nothing here addresses the quantities that actually cost money on a renovation** — area, length, and volume of continuous materials, where waste, cut loss and coursing decide the number. **No such quantity appears anywhere in the video.**
