## Kids Room — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Long/narrow ("matchbox") room layout fix**: in a room roughly twice
  as long as it is wide, placing a bed along one long wall and a
  wardrobe along the opposite long wall (a common default) exaggerates
  the tunnel/train-car effect — the eye has nothing to stop it before
  reaching the window. **Fix applied**: the wardrobe was moved to the
  *short* wall containing the door (the only wall not already occupied
  by window or door swing), rather than a long wall, specifically to
  avoid making the room read even longer/narrower.
- **A composition principle borrowed from cinematography/photography,
  applied to furniture shape mixing**: a room dominated by rectangular
  furniture (bed, desks) benefits from at least one round element (a
  round rug, a round pouf/ottoman) — explicitly framed as a deliberate
  shape-variety composition rule, not incidental decor choice; the
  video argues the same layout with square/rectangular rug and pouf
  instead would read as visually duller or worse.
- **Furniture-only refresh technique for an existing bunk bed**: instead
  of buying new furniture, the existing father-built wooden bunk bed
  was repainted in two colors along a **diagonal split** (one bed
  "dipped" half in blue paint, half in yellow) — a low-cost visual
  refresh technique for reusing existing furniture rather than replacing
  it, paired with new textiles (quilted geometric-pattern blankets,
  color-matched pillows) to unify the room's palette without any
  construction.
- **Small anchor-image-driven palette technique**: the room's whole
  color scheme was built outward from a small set of existing accent-
  color images/artwork already on the wall (kept from before), rather
  than choosing paint/textile colors independently — a repeatable
  technique for a redesign that must preserve some existing elements.
- **Back-to-back desk pair with mixed mounting types**: one desk on
  telescoping (height-adjustable) legs that "grows with the child";
  a second desk specifically **wall-mounted and foldable**, sited on a
  path to a balcony door, folded flat in summer (when not needed) and
  left open in winter — its wall mounting also allows height adjustment
  over time. A genuinely space-efficient solution for a room too narrow
  for two full-size desks side by side.
- **Existing ceiling-height step (a prior drywall soffit/reveal) used as
  a natural mounting point for a light curtain divider** between the
  study and sleep zones — reusing an existing structural feature rather
  than adding new construction to hang a soft zone-divider; reported to
  make the room feel cozier/more "square" despite its narrow shape.
  Corroborates the general "curtain as a soft zone divider" technique
  already recorded elsewhere in this project's store, with a concrete
  mounting detail (using an existing ceiling step) not previously noted.
- **Pouf/ottoman doubling as a large toy-storage box**, paired with two
  additional under-bed storage drawers — consistent with, and adding a
