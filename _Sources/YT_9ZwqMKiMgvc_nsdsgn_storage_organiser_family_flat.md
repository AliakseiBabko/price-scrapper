# Source Note — NSDSGN with Ксения (организатор пространства), storage in a family flat

**A 35-minute walkthrough of a professional space organiser's own flat** — herself,
husband, an 8-year-old daughter and a dog — filmed to test the objection that the
host's own single-occupant order is easy to maintain. **Renovation done ~8 years
before filming, with a design project; she rebuilt most of the storage afterwards,
largely from IKEA parts.**

**Promotional ratio low, and unusually so for this channel.** Nothing is being sold
here; she repeatedly shows compromises and things she got wrong, and the closing
plug is brief. **⚠️ Two passages actively undercut the host's own business — both
recorded below.**

**No prices at all in this source.** Region level 2.

## ⚠️ The two claims that matter most, both against the host's interest

### 1. A design project does not produce good storage — it is a separate discipline

Stated by her, unchallenged by him:

> «У нас тоже был проект, но многие вещи, касаемые хранения, я уже переделала потом,
> когда стала вникать в эту тему. И, к сожалению, дизайнер этого не предусмотрел и
> вряд ли мог. Это не говорит о том, что плохой дизайнер. Просто специфика другая.
> Мы по-другому думаем, организаторы пространства.»

**«И вряд ли мог» is the load-bearing part** — not "our designer was bad" but "a
designer could not have." Her own гардеробная was drawn by the designer and rebuilt
by her afterwards. **This is the second source in this batch in which the host
publishes expert testimony limiting the designer's competence** (see
[[YT_ZKAcnh4kKfY_nsdsgn_lighting_colour_temperature_and_gear]], where two lighting
specialists and the host himself describe the designer as a generalist who must refer
out).

### 2. How to plan storage before you have lived somewhere

The host asks the right question — concrete walls, a project drawn before you move
in, «ты ещё не знаешь, куда ты будешь кидать свои трусы». Her answer:

- **Analyse your current home**: what is inconvenient, what is convenient, and
  **where the biggest mess actually accumulates.**
- **⚠️ The heuristic worth keeping: notice where you habitually throw a charger or a
  t-shirt, and then legitimise storage at that spot** — «подумайте, как организовать
  законно эту вещь в этом месте». Design for the behaviour rather than against it.
- **And the core principle: «Порядок зависит от того, удобно ли размещены вещи.»**
  Order is a function of placement, not of how many people live there.

## The verdict on the video's own question

- **Host: storage here is essentially identical to his single-occupant flat.** «По
  факту всё точно так же организовано, и у тебя везде порядок… получается, зрители
  были неправы.»
- **Her qualification, which is the more precise answer**: with more people you
  account for each member's needs and there are more things, **but the base
  principles hold — the scenarios are the same, there are simply more of them, and
  they intersect. «Нужно учитывать эти пересечения.»**
- **All the storage systems were bought gradually over several years**, so there is
  no single shopping list to copy.

## Kitchen storage

- **Placement follows the action sequence: pots in the lower drawer nearest the water
  source**, because filling with water is the first step when boiling.
- **Storage height follows who uses it**: everyday plates were moved down from an
  upper cabinet **because the daughter grew and began serving herself**.
- **⚠️ An honest disclosure of her own compromise**: oil and spices are stored above
  the hob, and she says this is undesirable — «есть риск, я уже один раз разбила
  плиту» — but she sees no alternative in this kitchen.
- Vertical dividers for lids; pans and baking forms under the oven in a vertical
  holder; **a deliberately shallow shelf beside the oven for baking trays**, so they
  do not obstruct anything.
- **Антресоли (top row) for genuinely rare items** — a mincer.
- A non-standard cabinet they could not get drawers for (shelves being inconvenient
  in a kitchen) holds **nesting containers from a single collection**, lids stored
  separately.
- **Pull-out bottom shelf for unopened stock and tinned reserves, plus a household
  protocol around it**: if something runs out, look here first; if it is not here,
  order it. **The rule, not the shelf, is what makes it work — and the whole family
  knows it.**
- **⚠️ «Ящик позора» — a designated shame drawer.** To keep the cabinets you open
  constantly presentable, allocate one drawer for everything ugly.
- **A whole kitchen drawer sacrificed to work equipment** — laptops, chargers,
  headphones — because she works at the kitchen table and has no desk. Nothing stays
  out on the table. Presented as the principle: organisation adapts to the family's
  actual habits even when the result is unconventional.
- **A low drawer with snacks the daughter is allowed to pack herself**, placed low
  deliberately to enable a child's autonomy.
- Corner cabinet: her simplest answer is **to put the sink there** and store cleaning
  chemicals and vegetables in the corner.
- An étagère in a spare corner for home exercise equipment, where she actually
  trains. **Stated as retrofit, not design: «мы организовывали пространство уже после
  ремонта. В новой квартире я себе это заранее запланирую.»**
- Designer's aside on a real coordination failure visible in the room: **three pendant
  lights hung where no table will fit** — kitchen and lighting positioned
  independently of each other.

## Wardrobe and clothing

- **⚠️ Shoes in a deep standard carcass stored two rows deep are staggered — one set
  back, one set forward and offset — so every pair is visible at a glance.** Solves
  losing shoes in the depth without shallower furniture.
- **Off-season shoes live in the bedroom wardrobe; current-season in the прихожая.**
- **Jewellery in a shallow pull-out shelf with inserts, giving a top-down view of
  everything at once.** She explicitly **rejects multi-drawer jewellery boxes** with
  latches and hooks. Her inserts are IKEA but she notes any similarly-sized inserts
  fit an ordinary drawer.
- Carcass depth **750 mm** on each side; his half, her half, and a shared section.
- **⚠️ A revision worth recording because it reverses standard advice**: the
  husband's t-shirts were stored **vertically in a drawer** — the usual organiser
  recommendation — and they concluded it was wrong for him, because t-shirts are his
  most-worn daily item. **They switched to hanging them all visible.** She does not
  iron: dries carefully, hangs immediately, and they are not creased.
- A small wardrobe forces **seasonal rotation** — summer clothing up on the antresol,
  sweaters below, swapped twice a year.

## ⚠️ Laundry and drying — a repeated, unrecoverable planning failure

**The strongest cautionary content in the source, because it is her own flat.**

- **They have no dryer. They did not allow for one during the renovation, and
  technical constraints now prevent adding one.** A drying rack stands permanently in
  the bedroom — she calls it «арт-объект» — drying bedding and all clothing, not just
  linen. «Некуда поставить больше. Это жизнь.»
- **The designer generalises it, and this is the rule to keep**: people do a design
  project, order an ordinary washing machine, **never think about drying at all**,
  and remember only after moving in — at which point the rack appears «в самом
  неожиданном месте».
- **→ Specify laundry equipment in the ТЗ (brief): washer, dryer, drying cabinet or
  washer-dryer. If there is none, plan where the rack goes.** He adds that clients
  frequently also ask for an ironing board, which also needs a place.
- Treadmill: he says they always become clothes racks; in her case she genuinely uses
  it — trainers and a tablet stand beside it — **but it was not planned, so it stands
  on a mat over a radiator and looks poor.**

## Bathroom — «вторая проблемная точка после прихожей»

- **Because the washing machine is there, all sportswear and sports bags settle
  there.** She added a separate plastic chest of drawers for sportswear, and dries
  sports clothing there constantly; the radiator is not enough.
- **⚠️ A real project precedent from his own practice: for a large family they built a
  separate санузел for the hockey-playing husband, with the brief "throw his kit
  here"** — shower directly over the laundry zone and a dedicated washing machine.
  **The generalisable point is his stated practice: when reviewing a design project
  he always asks about the жизненные сценарии.**
- **⚠️ Her own stated priority reverses the usual one: she would rather have a
  separate хозяйственная комната (utility room) with laundry and sports kit than a
  walk-in wardrobe**, and is willing to accept a wardrobe cupboard instead.
- **Her vanity unit is wrong and she says so: the drawers are both wide and deep.**
  «Для ванной такие делать не надо» — what is stored there is small categories, and
  that depth is not needed. She has already ordered a replacement.
- Storage method throughout: **containers and small trays that combine with each
  other.**
- **⚠️ Store underwear (at least pants) in the bathroom, not the wardrobe** — out of
  the shower, put on clean underwear, leave. Avoids crossing the flat undressed. She
  moved theirs from the wardrobes deliberately. Husband's: a box, he takes the top
  one.
- **Only two sets of towels** — one in use, one spare. Nothing surplus.
- Reserves and unopened packs stored **above**, separated from the daily-use drawer,
  so they do not obstruct it.
- A radiator on one wall was **cut off and removed to fit a storage cupboard.**
- She clears toothbrushes off the washbasin into a drawer; husband and daughter do
  not. On the common objection that enclosed brushes will mould: «не наблюдали
  такого».
- **⚠️ She rejects decanting shampoos and shower gels into matching bottles** — tried
  it, «это не для меня». A basket is easier and reads as tidier even though it does
  not hide anything. **Recorded because it contradicts the dominant social-media
  convention.**
- Perforated basket for the child's bath toys, so they ventilate and dry.
- Per the design project a large wicker laundry basket belonged here; she moved
  laundry out and put the sportswear chest in its place.
- **Dirty laundry sorted by colour at the point of undressing** — four containers:
  **dark, light, underwear, and a separate one for items needing special attention**
  (a bad stain). Husband's clothes are mostly dark, daughter's mostly light, so
  compliance is easy. **You can soak directly in these drawers.** «Мне стирать в
  тысячи раз удобнее.»

## Kids room

- **⚠️ The room is 9 m² and they call it their own mistake: «детские комнаты должны
  быть больше по площади».** Reasons given: many possessions, many hobbies, a
  шведская стенка, a period of putting up tents. They changed the bed to gain space
  underneath.
- **The designer names a real pattern: the older the child, the less parents invest in
  the room** — «она всё равно съедет через год, поэтому по остаточному принципу».
  Both push back: while children are small they genuinely need the space, because they
  play and invite friends.
- **Decluttering as a trained habit**: the daughter initiates it herself. Start with
  something obviously broken or clothing with a hole; it trains gradually.
- **⚠️ The child must do it themselves — with a real incident behind the rule.** The
  host's 15-year-old came home to find her grandmother had tidied her room while she
  was out; she could not find anything, and it ended in tears and a family row. «Если
  бы она сама всё это убирала, никакого скандала бы не было.»
- **Her professional rule, from age 2–3: involve the child. Space organisers never
  touch someone else's things — even a child's. Always ask, or leave the items in one
  part of the room and wait until the child is back from school to discuss. Never
  throw anything away.** Stated in explicit contrast to the common parental «пока
  ребёнок не видит, я это выкину».
- **But on design specifically, do not simply follow the child** — otherwise «чёрная
  комната с фиолетовым потолком».
- **A wardrobe configuration she recommends generally**: lower **drawers** for
  t-shirts and trousers; one rail for school clothes; a second rail for informal
  weekend clothes; **upper shelves for toys currently out of rotation**, deliberately
  out of easy reach.
- **⚠️ Pull-out lower drawers rather than one continuous minimalist façade** — «мы не
  открываем, а потом выдвигаем; мы выдвигаем сразу». One action instead of two. Plus
  **labelled** fronts, which in a younger child's room also helps them learn words.
- **Lay contents out so everything is visible when the drawer opens: take one t-shirt
  without destroying a stack.**
- Child's desk: a podcast with a child psychologist told him **white is the ideal
  work-surface colour for a child** — «но белый цвет — это до свидания». Their
  compromise is a plastic mat over the desk; not the first one, they do not wash
  clean, but better than the desk taking the damage.
- **⚠️ The inside face of the door as storage** — hooks for robes and home clothes.
  Not pretty, hidden while the door stands open, and she notes the daughter likes
  using it. «Не забываем про вертикальные поверхности.»

## Dividers — and an argument against fixing them

- **The more cells you create, the easier order is to maintain, because things stop
  mixing.** Dividers, small containers, or containers with dividers built in.
- **⚠️ She argues against built-in fixed dividers, which some organisers and
  furniture makers propose.** Reason: **life changes and the composition of what you
  own changes** — «мы эту комнату раз переделывали». **Use removable, repositionable
  dividers so the layout can follow the household rather than freeze it.**

## Прихожая — «самое больное место по хранению»

- **Shoe storage in the hallway is the single most widespread storage problem**, and
  their designer did not plan it — there was a permanent heap on the floor.
- **⚠️ A genuine counter-data-point on ЛДСП and moisture.** She built the shoe rack
  herself from sawn sheets of **the cheapest ЛДСП with the edges completely unsealed
  («кромка вообще не закрыта»)**, cut cheap mats for winter, and reports **nothing
  has happened to it — no swelling** — despite wet winter shoes. She was criticised
  online for doing it. **The designer's supporting aside: German premium washbasin
  brands are made of DSP too.** Recorded with its informality stated: one
  uninstrumented household observation, not a durability test — but it is direct
  field evidence against the blanket «ЛДСП всё» claim.
- Underfloor heating runs beneath where the wettest shoes go; «никаких с этим нет
  проблем, кроме эстетической стороны».
- **⚠️ She is categorically against шкафы-купе on principle**, even in a very narrow
  hallway, and wanted an open shelf instead.
- **A façade-opening error in her own hallway, which she volunteers**: the drawer
  should have been separate, and the door should have opened the other way — as built,
  the entrance door is on one side and the cupboard swings into it. Her conclusion:
  «нужно очень продумывать детально мебель, направление открывания фасадов».
  **This corroborates the independent kitchen-specific version of the same rule in
  [[YT_-6QKr4HerCQ_nsdsgn_kitchen_25_ordering_mistakes]].**
- Bags stored two rows deep; she notes one row would be better.
- **⚠️ Not enough drawers for hats and scarves, so she added a plastic chest of
  drawers and allocated one drawer per family member.** Offered as a general remedy:
  «если вам не хватает ящиков, всегда можно поставить пластиковый комод». She also
  removed a rail here and a shelf there — **ready-made wardrobes can be modernised
  rather than replaced.**
- A cupboard that reads visually as a continuation of the kitchen but **functionally
  belongs to the bathroom and hallway**: dog things, first-aid kit, travel first-aid
  kit, games, and reserves on the top shelf.
