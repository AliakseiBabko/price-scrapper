# Source Note — NSDSGN roundtable, "Как выбрать строителей и не пожалеть?"

**70-minute three-way roundtable between designers**, filmed at Курилов's studio. **The most useful participant is Сергей Аскорбин, who ran renovations as a прораб for years before opening a design studio** — so the panel contains an insider on the trade being discussed, and the two others operate in the expensive segment.

**Promotional ratio low.** No product is sold; the close is a mutual recommendation of the other two studios. **What must be flagged instead is the panel's own position in the market**: all three sell design services and two of them explicitly sell contractor selection as a service, so the recurring conclusion "trust your designer" is not disinterested. **It is recorded below with that noted, and with the counter-arguments the panel itself raises against designers.**

**Region level 1** — St Petersburg and Moscow both named in spoken content and their labour rates contrasted. Prices are **August 2024**, converted at **90.0251 RUB/USD** (trailing 6 months to 2024-08-15).

## ⚠️ Green flags — signals that a builder is competent, and the best one is behavioural

1. **He does not agree to anything straight off the bat**, and does not sell himself hard («мы всё вам сделаем по красоте»).
2. **He is always heavily booked.** Concrete: a plumber phoned three months ahead says the earliest is winter and «не факт» — «мне на моих объектах даже неустойку платят: если я не работаю, мне платят за это деньги». **«Любой хороший специалист — у него всегда очередь, 100%.»**
3. **⚠️ He asks to see the object. «Если ваш строитель даже не спрашивает "можно мне объект посмотреть" — это уже очень странно.»**
4. **⚠️ He composes the смета slowly and asks questions back.** «Он берёт проект, изучает, не спешит, не выдаёт размазанную смету через 2 дня. Он берёт паузу, считает и задаёт тебе вопросы: а что вот это? а вот здесь этого не хватает? а как это сделать? а этой информации в проекте нету — как же мне составить смету?» **The questions coming back are the diagnostic, not the estimate itself.**

**⚠️ Two proposed signals the panel itself disagrees about, recorded as contested rather than as rules:**

- **Social media absence.** The host calls it «красный флаг стопроцентный» and checks all accounts, wanting to see a family man rather than a professional portfolio — his stated reason being that a man with a wife and two children «не пропадёт с объекта, он его доделает до конца». **Both others reject it immediately** — «у меня вообще другой опыт»: one of their best and most engaged прорабы is unimaginable on social media at all. **Not a reliable signal.**
- **The prospective builder's car**, on the host's reasoning that either an expensive car proves he earned it on construction or a working van proves he works. He mentions throwing a прораб off a job for arriving with a driver in an Audi A8. **⚠️ And the panel flags the criterion itself as odd in the same breath — «меня пугают заказчики, которые судят дизайнера по его машине».** Recorded as one designer's heuristic with the objection attached.
- **Age** is raised and also contested: the host says he cannot work with builders 55–60+ because technology moves; the others push back that if he knows the technology and has a run-in team including younger members it is fine, «всё очень индивидуально», and note that there are designers doing the same thing for twenty years too.

## ⚠️ Site visits — the strongest vetting content, because one test is inverted

- **A shown object can be staged.** It may be a showcase object; they may tidy it for your visit; they may assemble a crew for the meeting; **or show you somebody else's object entirely.**
- **⚠️ The inverted test, which is the genuinely clever item in this source.** Дима observes that **he has never once had a brigade refuse to walk him onto a live object — while his own clients frequently refuse to let him show their flat to a prospective client**, and he respects that and shows photographs instead. **Conclusion: a builder who walks anyone onto any of his sites without asking the owner will do exactly the same with yours.** «Если ты вот здесь так поступаешь — значит, ты и со мной так же поступишь.» **A red flag derived from the builder's consent practice rather than from his work.**
- **Which stage to view: all of them, demolition through finish.** Demolition is not uninformative — it shows **the quality of the demolition and the «культура ремонта»**, the site culture. A firm running fifteen objects will have every stage available.

## ⚠️ Comparing estimates — the method, and the market friction it causes

**The method:**

- **Send the same design PROJECT to every brigade and ask each to produce its own смета** — better than sending one смета out for analysis. **The stated reason is that otherwise the quotes are not commensurable**: one costed one plaster and another a different one where the project did not specify; one costed these partitions and another different ones. Then collect and compare.
- **Named failure the method defends against: «есть прорабы, которые просто берут сумму, которая им комфортна, и размазывают её на смету».**
- **⚠️ The same object can be costed three different ways — as economy, as dearer, or as luxe — and the same builder can produce all three.** The variation lives in painting technology, cable-routing detail and engineering assembly: «можно сделать по-простому, можно сделать по-сложному».
- **⚠️ And a mechanism worth keeping: a company that genuinely does good work may deliberately UNDERSTATE the смета** so the client forms pleasant expectations — «они вроде делают хороший ремонт, и вот смета занижена».
- **The problem this leaves: «человек, для того чтобы разбираться в этой смете, должен быть экспертом».** The average client looking at 1.8M against 2.5M «не понимает вообще, что происходит».

**⚠️ The friction, which is a real and non-obvious planning constraint:**

- **Прорабы are increasingly unwilling to prepare a смета at all**, even for labour only. **Stated reason: the client wants to survey the whole market, the прораб knows he will spend three or four days estimating and then be told «мы другую выбрали».** One of them reports costing a смета with two прорабы for a client who then **took both and disappeared.**
- **→ So a client who shops many quotes may find the better contractors decline to bid.**
- **⚠️ The constructive resolution offered, and it is the actionable part: make estimating a separate paid service.** «Выведи это как отдельную услугу: составление сметы — вот столько времени, вот столько денег», under its own contract, and the person then sits down and does it thoughtfully.
- The host's counter to contractors' resentment: «эти подрядчики не встают на место клиента» — from the position of someone spending their own money you must check you are not being overcharged, and his own salespeople produce hundreds of unsuccessful quotes as ordinary process.
- **⚠️ And the argument against selecting on price alone, from the contractor's side: «если ты квалифицированный, твоя смета может быть выше средней. Он возьмёт её, пойдёт проанализирует, у всех будет дешевле — но если ты объективно чего-то стоишь, он вернётся к тебе независимо от цены.»**

## Company versus individual прораб

- **Сергей: «я для своих клиентов практически никогда не подбираю компании».** His reasoning: he knows how the internal hierarchy works, **it makes the process more expensive**, and it adds bureaucratic layers — he names it **«перекупство»**, and says it is often «просто перепродажа проекта».
- Companies can give guarantees and service, «но я таких компаний знаю мало».
- **He states the counter-risk honestly**: going direct to a прораб risks a low-qualification прораб — nominally a прораб, slightly cheaper, the client agrees, and the service layer simply is not there.
- **«Не всегда компания — это залог того, что ваш ремонт будет хорош.»**
- **⚠️ A vetting question this produces: how many objects is the person running concurrently?** The host asked several builders and **some answered «не больше пятнадцати»**; **Дима's own estimate is a maximum of three or four.** The panel qualifies it — it depends what the objects are, their area, design, materials and procurement — so fifteen may not be a lie for simple work, but the gap is large enough that the question is worth asking. *(Recorded as a divergence, not resolved.)*

## ⚠️ Технадзор — the panel's proposed answer, and its own failure modes

**The richest passage in the source, and unusually balanced: they advocate it and then dismantle it.**

**The case for it:**

- **The designer should NOT be the one interrogating сметы** — «это должен делать человек, который в этом разбирается».
- **Engage as early as possible**, ideally at the first stages, because he can «мосты построить или наоборот сжечь».
- What he actually evaluates: paint category, how the electrics are executed, what is and is not counted, waste.
- **Why not self-supervision: «если строитель контролирует сам себя, ты должен очень сильно доверять этому строителю».** Serious construction companies hire a separate технадзор for themselves as an internal audit.
- **The framing that makes it worth the money: it catches things while nothing is concealed.** «Хорошо, что это выяснилось на этапе, когда у тебя не зашито ничего. А когда у тебя не горит светильник — там уже хуже.»
- Where to find one: by recommendation; **технадзор is essentially the same builders — a competent person.**

**⚠️ The failure modes, all named by the panel:**

1. **Their own internal технадзор failed, and they say exactly why: «после первого полугодия они с парами уже друзья туда-сюда» — the supervisors and the crews became friendly and it stopped working.** A concrete organisational finding.
2. **⚠️ The market is bad: mostly ex-прорабы selling adversarialism rather than measurement.** Looking on Avito he found listings promising «выбью с ваших строителей все деньги, уничтожу их». Recruiting a genuinely good one was «огромная работа».
3. **⚠️ A worked case that is the best single item here.** A client-appointed технадзор told the client **all the electrics should be redone on скрутка (twisted joints) instead of Wago clamps.** The panel's rebuttal is technical: **under a thermal imager a скрутка has more contact area and therefore more heating — which is a minus, not an advantage.** And the incentive behind the "advice": **«поставить на клемму или сделать скрутку — это стоит 500 руб либо 1.000 руб за соединение»** (≈$6 vs ≈$10 per joint). He was eventually thrown off, but the site stalled meanwhile.
4. **«Технадзор может стройку просто не позволить доделать до конца из-за подобных глупостей.»**
5. **He names corruption as a reason to vet the технадзор himself**, and wrote into his own contract (when he was still a builder) that **any технадзор must be agreed with him first** — he meets the person and establishes **by what criteria they will inspect.**
6. **⚠️ A real capacity constraint: they are all busy — two to three months to replace one, and your object stands still meanwhile.** «Это жёсткая реальность.»

**⚠️ The governance fix, which is the reusable part:** any технадзор instruction must be formalised — **a технадзор journal (журнал), corresponding акты, and a written предписание** — and then a named party (технадзор, client, client's representative or designer) **takes responsibility for that decision in writing.** «Дай нам решение… и человек берёт на себя ответственность. Да ради Бога.»

**⚠️ And the criteria problem, which is the most transferable technical point: СНиП is too loose to be a quality standard.** «По СНиПу можно сдать любой объект, самые кривые» — **he cites 5 mm per metre, e.g. over the whole height of a tiled wall, and notes developers hand over on exactly that.** **→ "We comply with СНиП" is not a quality claim.** The alternatives he names as usable criteria are **технические карты** — from the manufacturer, and versions developed by builders within the market, «такой новый СНиП, обновлённый» — and **whatever the contract itself specifies.**

## ⚠️ Warranty — figures, a red-flag threshold, and a two-category split

- **Normal warranty: around 3 years.** A stated norm of **~2 years minimum**, and **«если меньше 2 лет — сразу до свидания, красный флаг».** Some builders write «договор актуален 1 год».
- **⚠️ The warranty should be split into two categories with written criteria, and this is the part clients misunderstand:**
  1. **A general ~2-year warranty on engineering** — electrics, plumbing.
  2. **Wear of materials from ordinary living, which is not a warranty matter but local restoration** — a touch-up with a brush, small patches, floor restoration.
- **⚠️ A modern-material example of category 2: matt paint — «салфеточка, и она уже блестит у тебя».** So matt finishes generate warranty disputes that are not warranty issues.
- Beyond the warranty period a good прораб still sends someone — a client writing five years later about furniture that has dried and cracked gets options offered, even though it is an operation error. **«Кто виноват, тот и платит.»**
- **⚠️ An incentive argument for the builder wanting his own технадзор: he carries the warranty.** «Если у тебя загорится скрутка — ты будешь в этом виноват.»
- **A service-subscription model he offers builders as a free idea**: a paid subscription to service — steady income, covers problems, and forces the warranty criteria to be split clearly.

## ⚠️ As-built documentation — a flat categorical finding and three methods

**«Я за всё своё время работы не сталкивался с реальной ситуацией, когда какой-либо исполнитель давал мне какую-либо исполнительную документацию. Никогда не было такого.»** Even where an electrical, plumbing or ventilation project exists, **there are always discrepancies and always additions**; the executor builds partly to the project, partly not, and leaves.

**The consequence, stated as the argument: five years later you call someone to service the ventilation or AC, hand them the project, and it does not match.** The person «разводит руками» and either cannot service the flat or spends enormous time and effort locating the fault.

**Three methods, in ascending order of reliability:**

1. **Photo-fixation by the builder.** The host treats it as what an adequate builder should do as standard and only writes it into the project on request. **Objection raised: a flat photograph carries no dimensions.** Answer: you mark the run with a pencil on site and then photograph it.
2. **⚠️ A 3D scan producing a point cloud — his own current practice, and the instruction that makes it cheap is to NOT build a model.** «Ты не строишь модель, ты просто облако точек оставляешь.» Hand it to the client or store it; open it later and everything is visible. **«Это стоит копейки.»** Worth it particularly in a large flat (200 m²+) where the runs are voluminous and cross each other. They note the scan will not capture gofra and cable, **but ventilation and AC ductwork with defined positions can be compared against the design.**
3. **⚠️ Or fall back on the installation rules themselves, which costs nothing: штробы must run vertically (ПУЭ), and there are rules for junction-box placement — with a stretch ceiling a box should sit no more than ~150 mm from the edge so local demolition can reach it.** Using those rules plus a detector you can locate services without a point cloud.

## Prices, August 2024

- **Their design project: 14,000 RUB/m² (≈$160/m²).**
- **Renovation contractors in their segment: 50,000–60,000 RUB/m² (≈$560–$670/m²)**, described as above average.
- **⚠️ A market phenomenon worth recording as such: at a construction/renovation forum near St Petersburg (Gatchina), builders were being coached to «просто продавайте за 60» — «вы никогда не сможете продавать за 60, пока не начнёте себе говорить, что вы продаёте за 60».** The host was shocked. **Price positioning being taught independent of capability is a direct reason to distrust a headline per-m² figure.**
- **⚠️ And the concrete differentiator he names for firms genuinely working at that level: two people arrive — «первый человек приезжает делает работу, второй смотреть, как он делает работу».** That is what the money buys.

## ⚠️ Why the trade has no quality floor — a structural explanation

- **There is effectively no vocational construction education available.** It exists but only in a few small towns. **In his entire career on site he met exactly one person with a formal trade qualification** — a woman with среднее профессиональное образование as a маляр.
- **That is why he abandoned the idea of franchising a renovation business: there is no qualifying factor for hiring.** «Я понял, что это невозможно, и даже забил эту идею.»
- **Counterpoint the designers raise honestly**: designers do have formal education and still have to be retrained from scratch — «приходят люди после института, они ничего не знают» — so **the decisive factor is experience either way.**
- A social framing he adds: plumbing and electrical work are seen as unprestigious, so people prefer «котлованы копать и заливать их» to building a complex process.

## Where to find builders, and the panel's own recommendation

Ranked as they give it:

1. **Social media, in the segment you need — but first be clear what renovation you actually need**, because complexity varies enormously.
2. **Find designers working in that segment and watch who they use** — the community around them.
3. Internal designer/builder chats — «тоже такая себе история».
4. **⚠️ Their stated best option: trust the designer — and then interrogate the recommendation anyway.** «Бро, ты с ним работал? Покажи объекты. Что это вообще за человек?» **Trust the party accountable for the result, but do the verification work.**

**Market note: ~90% of their clients now ask «у вас есть строители? Иначе нам не интересно»**, because they came for a finished product.

**⚠️ On "top builders", and this is a useful corrective to social proof.** Asked what the term means, they note that for many it signals bloggers, social media, a car, or media presence. Their own definitions: **«топовый строитель — тот, который мой вопрос решит и по пути лишних вопросов не задаст»**, and **«тот, который горит, для которого важен процесс и финальный результат — и это прямо видно в процессе».**

## Should the designer contract the builders directly? — asked and answered no

- Сергей has done it: he ran renovations as a design studio for years. **The problem inside your own project: the прораб blames the designer, the designer blames the прораб, the client is appalled, and sometimes the cause is a third party (material arrived damaged).**
- **His conclusion is a pricing one: «чем больше ответственность, чем больше риски, тем больше денег» — it would require a super-price and clients would refuse.** So they separated the businesses. **«Стройка — это совершенно другие деньги, совершенно другая ответственность, и нормы прибыли другие.»**
- **Globally it would be excellent for the client** — one accountable party handing over a finished object in two years to the drawing with an operation manual — **but the client must understand it costs more.**
- **⚠️ Market observation: construction firms often have a design department; he has never seen a design studio with a construction department, and has never seen interesting design come out of those construction-firm design departments.** Петрович named as a firm that tried it.

## Escalation when builders have caused real damage

- **For a large defect you stop reasoning about blame and bring in qualified experts — and it must not be the designer.** Either the технадзор, or the client engages **an inspection company with the appropriate attestations**, which issues a **заключение** naming the specific violations.
- **Then two paths: negotiate humanly, in which case the contractor also pays for the inspections; or, if he starts evading, go to court with the заключение.**
- Small defects (3,000–5,000 RUB, ≈$30–$60) are resolved day to day where people cooperate.
- **Large problems are usually resolved inside the designer–builder link, frequently without telling the client** — «наоборот, только всё испортишь».
- Relayed example of a severe case: a Moscow client's timber-frame house **froze — windows left open or heating off, pipes burst — rolling the renovation back six months**, with several million roubles of Italian tile being reordered and litigation under way.

## ⚠️ The case study — a flipping project, and the first stage was the diagnostic

Old fund, city centre, **~200 m²**, difficult object. The client had had flats done by Сергей before but this time went to a brigade **because it was a school friend.**

- The project had been running **over two years** and the client was exhausted.
- **He had paid them roughly 5,000,000 RUB (≈$56,000) including materials, effectively ahead of the work** — closing them out by stages but paying forward.
- Сергей came in as технадзор and found the work bad throughout.
- **⚠️ The trap: the contractors have no money, there is no way to recover it from them, and the client will not pay a second time for the same renovation.** Stalemate — he can neither move in nor sell, on a flat bought to flip.
- **And he will not expel them because they are friends** — «слишком добрый хороший человек, у которого не может в свою квартиру ни заехать, ни продать, ни перепродать».
- **⚠️ The lesson stated explicitly, and it is the most usable thing here: the very first stage was already diagnostic. «Стяжку залили через одно место… по сути по заливке стяжки можно было понять, что ребята не квалифицированы, и не допускать их дальше.» The screed pour is the first competence gate.**
- A designer was involved but **in another city**, said something was wrong, and «тянулось, тянулось, терпелось».

**⚠️ The generalisable dynamic the case illustrates: «все собираются на объекте и все друг на друга показывают» — furniture makers, builders, designer — with the client in the middle not knowing what to do.** The panel's answer is that if a client has reached that point, the process went wrong much earlier.

## Client involvement, and the root cause of builder–client conflict

- **He asks each client explicitly how involved they want to be**: shall we send you the технадзор reports, or just the прораб's report once the defects are already fixed?
- **His view: many clients want immersion and only make it worse for themselves** — «трепать нервы, а при этом ничего не меняется». Others genuinely gain from it: «я в жизни на многое теперь смотрю по-другому после нашего ремонта».
- **⚠️ Прорабы have started valuing themselves properly**, which he calls good; the old image was a downtrodden man to be squeezed. Clients tell him directly «я прораба буду каждый день в хвост и в гриву» — and he notes **a прораб who values himself will simply walk.**
- **⚠️ And the closing diagnosis, which is the best structural insight in the source: «чаще всего это возникает из-за того, что у него происходит несоответствие той сметы его ожиданиям».** The conflict originates in the estimate not matching the прораб's own expectation of the work. **→ «Всё проговаривать на берегу.»**

## Professional conduct rules the panel states about themselves

- Better to use a contractor you have already worked with — **but being all interlinked for years has minuses, and independent parties acting as противовесы is healthy.**
- **Сергей's own rule: «чтобы в этом кругу не краснеть — я веду игру абсолютно откровенно. Если это мой косяк, открыто об этом говоришь; если это чужой, ты не берёшь на себя ответственность за другого человека и тоже даёшь это явно понять.»**
- **And the limit of a recommendation: «мы не в ответе за каких-то других взрослых людей.»**
- **Red flag added at the close: being asked for a lot of money in advance.**
- **Потребительский терроризм is named as the reason Сергей left the construction side**, and as a red flag for the designer when a client arrives already in that mode.
