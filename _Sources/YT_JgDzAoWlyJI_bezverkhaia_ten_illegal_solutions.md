## Regulations / Permits / Approvals — the ten prohibitions (Russia)

1. **⚠️ Do not expand a kitchen at the expense of a bathroom/WC — "ни при каких случаях, даже чуть-чуть, даже на 10 сантиметров."** She flags the specific trap: buyers of «свободная планировка» new-builds assume no walls means no rules, do as they like, and end up with an apartment that is "рыночный неликвид."
2. **⚠️ Nor the reverse — do not expand a bathroom/WC at the expense of the kitchen.** **The reasoning she gives is the transferable part: although both are wet zones, the law treats it as worsening your own and your neighbours' housing conditions.** **Exception: the ground floor — see below.**
3. **⚠️ Do not expand a bathroom/WC at the expense of a living room** — commonly wanted in the old fund with its tiny WCs. **Stated rationale: it worsens the conditions of the neighbour below** — "а вдруг у вас потоп, а он прольётся им на кровать." **Not by a single centimetre.**
4. **⚠️ Do not expand a kitchen at the expense of a living room** — the common wish to open the kitchen into the living room. Illegal "никогда и ни при каких обстоятельствах."
5. **⚠️ A studio (open-plan) apartment is not permitted where there is gas.** With gas, **the kitchen must be separated from the rest of the flat by full interior doors with a tight closure (плотный притвор)** — sliding or hinged, single or double-leaf, the type does not matter, but they must be real doors that close tightly. **Rationale: in a gas leak, the doors are deemed to hold the gas out of the living rooms.** She is candid that these are **old, Soviet-era norms** and that doors are in practice kept open, so arguing with them is pointless — **the legal workaround is to renounce gas**: you may apply to have it disconnected, the pipe is cut and welded shut, and **you receive a certificate that the apartment is not gasified.** Then, **if the electrical capacity allows**, fit an electric hob and build the studio.
6. **⚠️ A kitchen niche (кухня-ниша) is legal, and she gives the exact conditions — this is the most useful permissive item.** A kitchen **may** be expanded onto, or relocated onto, non-living space — **including into a corridor** — subject to only two norms: **(a) its area must be not less than 5 m²**, and **(b) the extract must be connected to the kitchen ventilation system, not to the bathroom's vent duct — even if the bathroom's duct is closer.** **⚠️ Plus one planning constraint: no appliance — sink, refrigerator or hob — may extend beyond the corridor's own boundary as drawn on the БТИ plan.** She states her studio has done fully approved projects of exactly this kind and calls it "очень крутое решение для многих квартир."
7. **⚠️ Hydronic (water) underfloor heating is not permitted in an apartment — electric only.** Unlimited in a detached house. She notes it was popular in the 1990s and is now rarely requested.
8. **⚠️ Balconies and loggias may not be joined to the apartment** — doors or windows must remain, and they must be tight enough to separate the flat's warm contour from the balcony's cold one. **She observes this violation is often overlooked — but moving riser-fed heating radiators onto the balcony is not.** **A balcony may be heated by electric convectors, electric underfloor heating, or an infrared heated baseboard — never connected to the building's common heating system.**
9. **⚠️ Ventilation and plumbing shafts (вентканалы, сантехнические короба) may not be touched at all.** No reducing them, no cutting niches or shelves into them, no putting a WC installation inside them. **The reasoning is ownership, which is the part worth keeping: by the documents the shaft may be common building property and not part of your apartment at all**, so there is no right of intrusion regardless of how much dead space it appears to contain. She notes clients ask for this constantly, her own included.
10. **⚠️ The most serious violation: affecting load-bearing structures without an approved reinforcement project.** An opening in a load-bearing wall **is often possible, particularly on higher floors**, but the sequence is fixed: **commission a reinforcement project from a licensed organisation → have that project approved by the territorial authority → cut and reinforce the opening strictly per the project → keep all hidden-works certificates (акты скрытых работ) for the acceptance inspection.** Slow and expensive. **Her own cost-benefit verdict, worth recording because it argues against her own service line**: her studio does this routinely, but on large premium projects whose clients can afford it — **"если вы считаете, что в двухкомнатной панельке игра с проёмом в несущей стене стоит свеч, вы не правы. Лучше придумать какую-то другую планировку."**

## Regulations — the ground-floor exception, and the neighbour-below constraint

- **⚠️ The ground-floor exception, and note her precise definition**: "first floor" means **the first *residential* floor** — if there is a shop below you on the ground floor, then **you are effectively the second floor and the exception does not apply.** Where it does apply, **you may move a kitchen or bathroom onto living space, and expand your bathroom onto your own kitchen** — subject to the same remaining rules (not a load-bearing partition, no gas). **She notes people actively hunt for these high ground-floor residential units precisely because of the replanning freedom.**
- **⚠️ A constraint that depends on your neighbours' history and is easy to miss: if the flat below has already replanned — say expanded a bedroom onto a piece of corridor — you cannot put your wet zone over that same piece of corridor.** Her term for the resulting overlap is "криминальные пересечения." **So a wet-zone relocation must be checked against what the flat below actually did, not only against your own БТИ plan.** Her recommendation: consult a specialist replanning-approval organisation.

## Regulations — the enforcement consequences

- **⚠️ The resale mechanism is the one she treats as most damaging, and it is concrete.** Most flats, even resale, are sold through banks to mortgage buyers. **The bank sends a valuer who compares the БТИ plan against the actual apartment and records every discrepancy.** Discrepancies become **"красные линии" on the БТИ plan** — unapproved deviations — and **the bank will then not take the property**, leaving only a cash buyer, of whom there are now very few. Her framing: an unapproved replanning is you "наступаете на горло своей же будущей финансовой выгоде."
- **The state-side escalation, in order**: an initial fine for an individual, then a repeat fine, then enforcement by a bailiff (судебный пристав), **and ultimately a court order to restore the original boundaries — i.e. to undo a brand-new renovation.**
- **⚠️ The fine figure is NOT recorded here, because the auto-captions render it ambiguously ("22500 руб")** between plausible readings, and she describes it in the same breath as "смешной копеечный штраф" — a characterisation that fits a low-thousands figure and not a 22,500 one. **Recorded as: a small administrative fine for an individual, amount not reliably readable from this source.** Do not quote a number from this note.
- Her closing recommendation: pay for a consultation with a competent designer or a replanning specialist — available in any Russian city — rather than risk it.
