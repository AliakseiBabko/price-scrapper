This governs which of this page's existing heated-floor compatibility content applies to an apartment project specifically vs. a house.
2. **⚠️ Shadow-baseboard/profile vs. concealed-mount-baseboard compatibility distinction by installation method, new baseboard-vocabulary content for this page**: a **theневой profile/baseboard** ("теневой плинтус," a profile recessed into the wall, plastered/wallpapered over, that only partially or not-at-all covers the floor's perimeter deformation gap) is functionally different from a **concealed-mount baseboard** ("плинтус скрытого монтажа," which still fully covers the gap despite its hidden look) — the shadow-profile variant genuinely depends on which flooring sits against it. **Fully compatible** (glued installations, negligible-to-no real deformation gap): glued engineered board, glued parquet board, glued LVT quartz-vinyl, any tile. **Not recommended** (floating installations needing a real deformation gap): laminate, floating parquet-on-underlayment, and floating quartz-vinyl-on-underlayment — even though quartz-vinyl's own linear expansion is very minor, it's still grouped with the not-recommended floating category on this criterion specifically. Newer spring-loaded/sprung shadow-baseboard systems exist that claim to accommodate floating-floor expansion, but the source treats them as unproven over time and doesn't recommend relying on them yet.
3. **⚠️ Heated-floor-under-floating-materials nuance, extends this page's existing heated-floor-compatibility content**: some manufacturers do permit their laminate/floating-parquet/floating-quartz-vinyl products with a heated floor, but specifically only when the heating element itself is embedded *within the base* (screed or self-leveling layer) rather than installed directly beneath the floating floor's own underlayment — a real installation-method caveat distinct from a blanket yes/no compatibility answer.
4. **⚠️ Practitioner's own field-experience reversal on quartz-vinyl-plus-heated-floor marketing claims**: quartz-vinyl (both LVT and SPC/floating types) was originally marketed as having negligible linear expansion, making it heated-floor-safe. This company's own field experience across its projects instead shows most **floating** quartz-vinyl-on-underlayment floors installed over a heated floor do buckle/tent ("встают домиком") in practice, and whether the floor returns flat once cooled back down is unpredictable — sometimes it does, sometimes it doesn't. **Current company recommendation, as a direct result**: pair a heated floor confidently only with tile or **glued** LVT quartz-vinyl — floating quartz-vinyl is treated as a real, not just theoretical, heated-floor risk despite marketing claims to the contrary.
5. **Local-repairability framework by installation method, first structured version for this store**: floating installations (laminate, parquet-on-underlayment, quartz-vinyl-on-underlayment) are hard to locally repair — replacing one damaged board requires disassembling the floor back to whichever room edge is nearest, swapping the damaged piece, and reassembling, with a real risk of damaging the lock mechanism of adjacent boards during that process. Glued installations (any tile, glued parquet/engineered board, glued LVT) are easy to locally repair — an individual damaged piece can be removed and replaced without disturbing the rest of the floor. The source states repairability is personally one of his own top flooring-selection criteria.
6. **⚠️ Subfloor-prep cost/quality tiering across all 7 materials compared, extends this page's existing quartz-vinyl-substrate-sensitivity content with a cross-material comparison frame**: simplest/cheapest subfloor prep (plain level screed, no further correction required) — any tile, laminate, parquet-on-underlayment, quartz-vinyl-on-underlayment (5 of the 7 compared materials). More demanding prep — glued engineered board and glued parquet board (glued installation needs a genuinely good substrate). **Most demanding and critical subfloor prep of all 7 — glued LVT quartz-vinyl**: described as similar to "cut linoleum" laid down thin and soft, meaning it fully telegraphs every substrate imperfection, visible under raking side-light if the substrate isn't genuinely flat.
