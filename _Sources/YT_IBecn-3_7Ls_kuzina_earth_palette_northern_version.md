## Design Concept — ⚠️ the central claim, and it is a good one

**Her opening challenge**: who decided that *these* are the colours of nature? *"Если палитра земли — это палитра природы, то где васильки, где море, где северное сияние, где цветущие луга? Почему в неё попали глина, песок, камень, сухая трава, а всё остальное нет?"*

**The palette as named**: ochre, terracotta, sienna, sand, linen, olive, sage, tobacco, wood tones, stone colour.

**⚠️ Her answer: it is not the palette of nature in general but of a very specific *type* of nature.** The countries where it recurs — **the south-west USA, Morocco, Algeria, Egypt, some regions of Spain, certain districts of Greece** — are very different cultures **with similar landscapes: much sun, little water, the earth permanently visible because the vegetation does not cover it.** Stone, clay, dry grass and burnt soil are part of the everyday view.

**⚠️ The formulation worth keeping, because it makes the whole trend legible in one line:**

> **«По сути, палитра земли — это палитра ландшафтов с дефицитом воды. Чем меньше воды в ландшафте, тем чаще мы видим саму землю»** — and so the more visible ochre, terracotta, stone, dust and dry vegetation become.

## Materials — the pigment history, and why these colours read as "basic"

- **Earth pigments long predate any interior trend.** Before the names ochre, umber and sienna existed, **these were simply materials a person could pick up and grind by hand — clay, charcoal, chalk, a piece of stone.** The first drawings were made with what was literally underfoot, mixed with saliva or fat.
- **⚠️ Ochre is pigmented clay containing hematite, a mineral that neither washes out nor decomposes** — which is why it has survived on cave walls for thousands of years.
- **⚠️ Her explanation of why they read as primordial rather than as a colour choice: "Earth tones — это, по сути, цвета руки, держащей кусок земли."** They are perceived **not as colour but as material, texture, substance.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ transposing the palette to a northern climate

**The question she puts, and it is the one that matters for this project**: what happens when the palette moves to a different climate — **more forest, more grey sky, more cold light and damp; less terracotta, fewer burnt landscapes?**

- **⚠️ Her sharpest caveat, and it forecloses the obvious rebuttal: a burnt landscape alone does not produce an earthy palette.** Russia has burnt peat bogs too, *"но почему-то никто не мечтает оформить квартиру в эстетике торфяного пожара."* **So the appeal is not "dry earth" as such.**
- **Literal copying of the American south-west or North Africa therefore does not always work — but that does not make the earth palette unsuitable. There is a local version of it.**

**⚠️ The northern version, which she builds from painters rather than from swatches:**

- **Levitan painted exactly that sky** — not bright, **diffuse, as though the light passes *through* cloud rather than falling directly.**
- **Apollinary Vasnetsov's** nature is different again: **damp, forested, full of air and half-shadow.**
- **Photographs of Russian landscape in its ordinary state — not on a sunny tourist day — show the earth palette working differently: not exotic clay under a blazing sun, but a muted colour under cloud.**
- **⚠️ The operative shift, and this is the specification: more moss, more wood tones, more grey-green and more grey-blue.**
- She also quotes the band **Мельница** as another statement of the same palette — *"тёмные кудри лесистых холмов"* — and calls it *"весьма недурственная палитра леса, воды и северного света."*

## Design Concept — ⚠️ the conclusion, which generalises past this palette

- **"Никакой единственной природной палитры не существует."** There are different landscapes, different light and different climatic conditions, **and each culture answers differently the question of which colours seem natural.**
- **⚠️ So the question is not whether the earth palette suits you. It is which nature you want to bring into your home.**
- Her closing to viewers: when you hear the word "nature," which colour appears first?
