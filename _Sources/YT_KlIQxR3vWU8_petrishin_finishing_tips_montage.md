## Rules / Heuristics — New Detail

1. **Two-tone molding/cornice paint-blend technique, genuinely new
   decorative-painting method**: paint the molding white twice and let
   it fully dry, then apply the wall's own color over it, then wipe with
   a wet sponge — this lifts the wall color off the molding's raised/
   flat surfaces while leaving it inside the molding's recessed carved
   details, producing a deliberate two-tone blend ("something from the
   ceiling, something from the walls") for a unified-with-the-room look,
   rather than a flat single-color molding.
2. **Wallpaper-glue toxicity myth explicitly debunked by a practitioner**:
   popular claims that wallpaper adhesive fumes can cause real
   intoxication or hallucinations are called a myth — modern glue is
   stated not to cause genuine poisoning; at most, exposure might
   irritate sensory perception, not produce real toxic effects. A
   specific, named myth-debunking claim (practitioner's own assertion,
   not independently verified against a toxicology source, but specific
   enough to record with that caveat).
3. **Color-indicator wallpaper glue, a named product feature**: some
   adhesive brands include a color indicator (pink while wet, fading/
   changing as it dries) so an installer can visually confirm full cure
   before proceeding with the next step, rather than guessing by touch
   or time alone.
4. **Floor-level-transition planning must account for finish-covering
   thickness at the rough/leveling stage, with a stated direction
   asymmetry**: parquet, tile, marble, and PVC flooring all have
   different thicknesses, so the rough-floor leveling reference must be
   set knowing which finish covering goes where, planned from the very
   start of the leveling stage. **Going from a thicker to a thinner
   finish material is easy** (the rough level can simply be built up
   higher without issue); **going the other direction (needing the
   level to drop) is much harder** — it requires demolition/grinding of
   already-placed material, described as genuinely difficult work
   regardless of dust-control measures taken.
5. **Curved/organic-shape capability distinction between mosaic and
   rigid tile**: a client-requested custom shower stall in a deliberate
   curved "teardrop" shape was executed in mosaic specifically because
   rigid tile can't follow a smooth curved surface without either
   shrinking to an impractically small format or leaving visibly ugly
   cut edges at the curve — a genuine material-capability distinction
   for organic/curved bathroom geometry, not previously documented.
6. **⚠️ Luxury reference data point, out of this project's own budget
   scope but recorded per the price-comparability convention**: the
   practitioner's most expensive mosaic installation was a real
   24-karat-gold-leaf mosaic (gold leaf sandwiched between two glass
   layers, each box carrying a certificate card stating the gold karat)
   at **€5,500/m²**, for a private home pool project; a separate
   bathroom in the same project used a silver-leaf mosaic. Flagged
   explicitly as a luxury/pool-specific tier, not comparable to this
   project's own apartment-renovation budget.
7. **Common-area finish quality as an informal building-quality
   signal**: a practitioner heuristic — seeing sloppily cut tile with
   poor corner execution in a building's shared stairwell/landing areas
   is treated as a signal that low-quality materials and non-specialist
   installation are also likely inside individual units of that
   building, since a developer/management company that tolerates poor
   common-area work is unlikely to have held individual unit work to a
   higher standard.
