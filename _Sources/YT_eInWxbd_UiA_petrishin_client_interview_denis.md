## Rules / Heuristics — New Detail

1. **Plan ventilation ductwork before building interior walls, not
   after, in a free-plan renovation**: Denis states he'd advise
   thinking through ventilation routing *before* walls go up rather than
   after, specifically to avoid having to break into already-finished
   walls to retrofit ducting — routed up near the ceiling so it can be
   hidden behind a stretch ceiling or a drywall niche without dropping
   the ceiling height much.
2. **Integrating a protruding structural column into new partition
   walls, rather than boxing around it separately, to avoid two named
   risks**: the designer (Anastasia) resolved a developer-poured column
   protruding into the hallway by running the new partition wall flush
   with the column itself instead of either (a) building the wall out
   to match the column's full protrusion (which would create exterior
   corners at the joint that "could deform over time") or (b) shifting
   the wall inward (which risked landing too close to the sewage riser
   stack, with unclear clearance). The chosen solution also created
   space for a built-in closet niche as a side benefit and kept the
   hallway visually straight (the offset isn't noticeable until a door
   is opened).
3. **Designer responsiveness flagged as a real vetting criterion,
   contrasted directly against the construction crew's own
   communication discipline**: the client states that if the
   construction-side experience had been as poor as the *design*-phase
   communication ("сама не позвонила, не написала, не увидела, не
   поняла" — no calls, no messages, unresponsive), it "would have been
   much worse" — an explicit real-world data point that a company's
   construction-crew communication quality does not automatically
   predict its design-team's communication quality, worth checking
   separately when vetting a combined design+build company.
4. **Client self-verification of material pricing before signing**:
   Denis compared the company-supplied material price list against
   independent online prices before contracting and found them
   consistent with real market rates — sometimes 3-5% below, sometimes
   about 3% above, "on average, market rate" — a concrete client-side
   due-diligence habit and a specific plausible variance band for this
   kind of company-quoted-vs-market price check.
5. **Floor-level risk explicitly cited as the reason for over-investing
   in a leak-protection system**: living on the 7th floor with 6 floors
   below is stated directly as the reason to "invest more, buy more
   sensors" rather than accept a minimal leak-protection setup — an
   explicit floor-height-driven risk-budgeting rationale for this
   already-well-documented system.
