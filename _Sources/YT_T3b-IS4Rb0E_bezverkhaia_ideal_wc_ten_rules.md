## Regulations — Rule 1: wet-zone replanning limits (Russia)

- **Cannot expand a bathroom/WC at the expense of living rooms (e.g. bedrooms), or at the expense of the kitchen — "даже на 10 см."**
- **Can expand at the expense of a corridor, a dressing room, a storeroom, or anything designated non-living on the БТИ plan.**
- **⚠️ Cannot touch the ventilation shaft at all: it may not be reduced, may not have niches cut into it, and may not be deformed in any other way.** Same prohibition as `JgDzAoWlyJI` item 9.
- **⚠️ The liberalisation, stated slightly more broadly here than in `JgDzAoWlyJI`: an entrance to the apartment's sole bathroom may now be made from a bedroom *or from the kitchen* — previously forbidden.** She calls it new and highly relevant to small flats and studios. `single-account`, `unverified`, undated, no code cited; **and note this is the same channel as the other account, so not independent corroboration.**

## Plumbing — Rule 2: communications

- **⚠️ Site the toilet as close to the soil stack as possible — ideally with its back directly against it.**
- **⚠️ If that is impossible, a maximum of two 90° bends in the soil pipe. More than two and it will eventually block.**
- **⚠️ Sewer pipe fall: not less than 20 mm per metre (2 cm/m), ideally 30 mm/m.** Her instruction is to watch the builders actually hold it.
- **⚠️ Soil ("фекальная") pipe diameter is 100 mm, and 120–130 mm at joints** — plan how it is concealed or you get "уродливые короба." **The better solution: build the wall out and form niches or shelves above it**, rather than boxing the pipe.
- **⚠️ A hard constraint on concealing pipework: a brick wall can be lightly chased, but a monolithic reinforced-concrete wall must not be chased — it is illegal, and cutting the rebar weakens the load-bearing capacity.** Where pipes run along monolithic walls, **the wall must be built out instead.** Plan for the lost depth at design stage. **(Russian jurisdiction; and it corroborates `JgDzAoWlyJI` item 10's load-bearing-structure principle from the plumbing side.)**
- **⚠️ Concealed mixers have a bulky hidden body that also cannot go into a monolithic wall** — it must go into a plasterboard, foam-block or brick partition, or the wall must be built out. **⚠️ The procurement consequence is the actionable part: buy concealed mixers at the very start and hand the internal parts to the builders while walls are still being built and services roughed in.**
- **⚠️ Allocate space for the water manifold and provide hatch access to it.** What lives there: **two large manifold combs, possibly a water heater, mains filters, fine-filtration, and the leak-protection system** — normally co-located with the risers. **Access must reach every element, every valve.**
- **Soundproof the soil stack** — simply wrap it in dedicated soundproofing mats.

## Surfaces — Rule 3: tile layout

- **⚠️ Tile should not run floor-to-ceiling everywhere** — combine it with paint, and she says even with wallpaper. (Consistent with this round's `j-F2HmDkaqY` papyrus-substrate finding on splash-adjacent wallpaper.)
- **⚠️ "Главное правило джентльмена": whole tiles always in the visible positions, offcuts and small pieces hidden in the corners.**
- **⚠️ Set out from the centre, and top-down — because on entering you look at your own eye height (~1.70 m), so an offcut up at the ceiling is far more visible than one at the floor.** A genuinely non-obvious setting-out rationale.
- **⚠️ Floor and wall tile should ideally be the same format, or coordinating formats, so the joints align** — otherwise "выглядит люто колхозно."
- **⚠️ No plastic or metal external corner trims. Mitre the tile at 45° on every external corner** — and **"если ваш плиточник говорит, что это сложно, дорого, не нужно, найдите себе другого."** She states this is standard modern practice.
- **⚠️ Floor tile must be matte and non-slip.**
- **⚠️ Grout in wet zones must be epoxy only, or it grows mould.** **Corroborates her own Round 1 flooring source (epoxy grout for mosaic) and this vault's existing epoxy-grout content.**

## Plumbing — Rule 4: access hatches

- **⚠️ Hatch access is required to: the water manifold; the bath siphon; the basin siphon; and the shower slot drain.** Her reasoning for the siphons is that blockage is not a risk but a certainty — **"когда-нибудь он забьётся… через 5, через 10 лет это неизбежно случится."**
- **Modern slot drains have a removable grate**, which covers the revision requirement, but it still has to be considered.
- **⚠️ Hatch-to-tile coordination, and this is the detail that separates a good job from a poor one: the hatch edges must land on tile joints. Small-format tile must not be cut mid-module — the hatch should fully repeat the porcelain module.** She states any good tiler can do this and that all her projects are done this way.
- **⚠️ "Такого вот лючка сантехнику не хватит"** — the hatch must be genuinely large enough to reach everything, not a token opening.
- **Use quality modern push-open hatches finished for paint or tile.**

## Quantities / Measurements — Rule 5: ergonomic minima

**⚠️ The densest and most immediately usable block in either round. All figures as stated:**

| Dimension | Her figure |
| :--- | :--- |
| Soil stack or wall → front plane of the WC installation | **min 250 mm**; **200 mm** for ultra-thin installations, **never less** |
| Wall-hung WC bowl projection | **550 mm**; compact models **500 mm** |
| Clear space in front of the WC to a wall, door or any element | **min 550 mm** ("ещё больше полуметра") |
| Width of a WC containing only a toilet | **min 900 mm** |
| Same, where the WC sits in a niche between a vanity and a shower partition (more shoulder room) | **800 mm floor, no less** |
| Bath (the lying area) | **min 1700 × 700 mm**; **1600 mm** acceptable for shorter occupants |
| Shower | **min 900 × 900 mm**, or **1000 × 800 mm**; anything larger is better |
| Vanity/basin unit width | **min 650 mm** (she calls this uncomfortable — nothing can be set down); **min comfortable 800 mm** |
| Twin basins | only worth it in a bathroom of **10 m² or more** |

- **⚠️ Sit-in and half-sit baths are rejected outright** as "ужасное советское наследие" — uncomfortable and pointless. **Better to fit a shower and use the freed space for storage or the washing machine.**
- **⚠️ Twin basins get an explicit reality check, and it is a useful counter to Pinterest-driven briefs**: beautiful and stylish, and her own studio's projects use them, **but "по пальцам одной руки пересчитать, когда даже пара… одновременно чистит зубы."** In a standard-size bathroom, do not — you will regret not using the space for something more useful.
- **Free-standing baths: only for large bathrooms, better in a country house, as a second/third/fourth bathroom, and realistically only where someone else cleans.** **Semi-recessed (полувстраиваемая) is her stylish compromise.**

## Plumbing — Rule 6: shower and mixer positioning

- **Shower should be built-in ("в строгом исполнении").**
- **⚠️ The fall to the drain must be genuine and visible.** **And in an apartment there must be a threshold (бортик) between the main bathroom floor and the shower — a thresholdless sloped shower is technically impossible in an apartment**, in her account. **(Note: `jl3OSKoe0YU` from Round 1 lists a missing shower threshold as one of the two most common replanning violations, which is consistent.)**
- **⚠️ A real ergonomic failure to design out: position the shower mixer so that reaching to turn it on does not put you under the cold water.** She reports encountering this "сплошь и рядом" in projects, at friends' homes and at the gym. **Sometimes the answer is mounting the mixer to the side.**
- **⚠️ Two more mixer failures from practice**: a shower system installed so the client's head hits the head; and **a mixer on the bath rim positioned so you almost have to kneel on the rim to fill the bath.**
- **⚠️ Procurement rule: buy all mixers at the very start of the renovation and let them sit** — otherwise the outlets get set and the model is discontinued, forcing rework. **And send the chosen sanitaryware, or at minimum its technical cards/connection diagrams, to the workers early** so they rough in for what was actually bought.

## Plumbing — Rule 7: basin selection

- **⚠️ A surface-mounted (накладная) basin is "очень красиво и очень неудобно" without a cleaner.** **Her preferred options: a basin integrated into the countertop, or an under-mounted (glued from below) one — because you can wipe the countertop and sweep everything straight into the basin.**
- **Always provide storage under the basin — one or two drawers.**
- **Free-standing basins, pedestal monoliths and thin-legged consoles are "баловство из отелей и ресторанов," not viable for residential use.**

## Heating / HVAC — Rules 8 and 9

- **⚠️ Underfloor heating is always required in a bathroom.** **In an apartment it can only be electric; in a house, electric or hydronic.** **Corroborates this round's `JgDzAoWlyJI` item 7 and the existing Kruglov account on the flooring page — a third and fourth account of the apartment-electric-only rule.**
- **Good fall in the shower so water does not pool.**
- **⚠️ A towel warmer is required — and in an apartment she prefers electric, on the grounds that a hydronic one is "мало функционален"** (it only works when the system is running). In a house, either.
- **⚠️ An electric towel warmer can be recessed into the wall under tile or under paint, with hooks above the mats so towels dry against the wall** — she offers this specifically as the answer for **small bathrooms with no room for a conventional towel warmer.**
- **⚠️ Forced extract ventilation is mandatory — and the control rule is the additive part: the extract must switch from its own separate key, NOT together with the light and NOT on a motion sensor**, "иначе она вас дико задолбает."

## Furniture / Built-ins — Rule 10: storage, and the lifehacks

- **Plan plenty of storage for clutter**: a drawer, better two, under the basin; **shelves planned in advance in the washing zone**, so wire baskets do not get stuck to the wall afterwards.
- **Plan storage for household supplies** — mop, cleaning powders — at the point you are only just starting the renovation.
- **⚠️ A non-obvious placement lifehack she flags as such: put open shelves holding jars and bottles on the wall that contains the door, not on the wall facing you as you enter** — that way you see them least and they register least. Applies equally to children's drawings.
- **Provide a place for dirty laundry** — she notes people remember the washing machine and forget the laundry basket, then an ugly wicker basket appears in the middle of a laconic bathroom.
- **⚠️ Washing machine: ideally under the countertop, or give it a dedicated cabinet or full-height column covering washer, dryer and detergents — and failing that, move it out to the corridor.** (Relevant to this project's own `09_Laundry_Room`.)
- **A siphon under the basin means the bottom drawer needs a cut-out** — plan it. **If an open siphon is wanted, buy a decorative one deliberately** (chrome, brass, black, white).
- **Three closing lifehacks**: a **Bluetooth speaker in the bathroom ceiling**; **face lighting** at the mirror (consistent with her hallway source's shadowless-face-lighting rule); and **"как и на кухне, розеток не бывает много — их бывает только слишком мало."**
- **Choose decor at the end, matched to the actual colours** — she notes bathrooms where large sums went on porcelain, sanitaryware and smart toilets get spoiled by mismatched bottles and badly-coloured towels.
