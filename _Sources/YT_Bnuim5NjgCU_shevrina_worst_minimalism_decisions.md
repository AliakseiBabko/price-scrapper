## ⚠️ L- and U-Shaped Kitchens — Three Distinct Objections

Not a taste verdict; she gives three separable reasons:

1. **Perceptual** — the brain prefers simple geometry; complex shapes are harder work, and such kitchens visually weight and clutter an already small area.
2. **⚠️ Material joins fail at the corner.** A wood worktop joins badly at an internal corner, and **any directional decor — marble with pronounced veining — will not read as one continuous slab** once it turns the corner.
3. **⚠️ The blind corner can never be fully used**, even with a "magic corner," and using it is awkward — so in practice it becomes where rarely-used items go.

**⚠️ Her counter-claim on islands is the genuinely useful part, and it corrects a common rule of thumb: whether an island works depends on the flat's *configuration*, not its area.** She designed a **37 m²** flat that took an island comfortably, and says a **70 m²** flat can be one where an island doesn't work. **This is the same conclusion she reaches from the opposite direction in `9h2tAnm6rqA`**, where an island is argued to *lighten* a small kitchen by freeing the wall verticals.

**Her alternative to L/U**: tall floor-to-ceiling units along one wall, and lower (~90 cm) units perpendicular with wall cabinets above. Simpler geometry, same capacity.

## ⚠️ "Fifty Shades of Beige" — the Tone-Count Position

**Deliberately varying tones within one hue to avoid boredom produces bad taste, not minimalism.** Her named failure: lighter curtains, a darker sofa, a more saturated rug, all nominally beige. "Получится безвкусица," and the sense of lightness and air will not be there.

She is careful that this **does not** mean using one identical tone with identical properties either — monochrome interiors exist, but they achieve interest through *other* devices. **Her position is that in minimalism colour is chosen by different rules than "vary it so it isn't boring."**

> **This is the first of three statements of this position in Round 3** (also `9h2tAnm6rqA`'s "the smaller the flat, the fewer tones," and the two-wood-tones rule below), and **it conflicts directly with sources already in this vault** — Vittoria Selina's "stretch the beige across several lightnesses" and Wall Street's "three to five coffee shades together," both from the 2026-08-30 colour batch. **Routed as a Perspectives block, not merged.**

## ⚠️ Black Accessories — the Quantity, Not the Colour

The mistake is deciding "all accessories will be black" and then buying a black mixer, hook, toilet brush, paper holder and shelf. **There is no rule that accessories must share one colour** — they can mix (brown with black, brass with green, a chrome hook with black mixers) **provided each is a full participant in the interior's colour scheme.**

**Her mechanism for why this became common**: manufacturers now issue black as a *second base colour* alongside chrome, so the market is flooded with it, and people tired of chrome over-buy. **The result in a 4 m² bathroom someone wanted to feel light is a mass of small visual noise.**

**And a defence of chrome**: "ну что плохого в хроме?" Designers use it happily; it is not a dated finish. Consistent with the chrome defence in `QES02ExtmAg` (Round 2).

## ⚠️ Two Wood Tones — She Says One

"В современных интерьерах… вы не найдёте ни одного интерьера, где использовано больше даже одного оттенка дерева — если там два оттенка, то второй просто чёрный." **She explicitly names and rejects the commonly-repeated "up to three wood tones is fine" advice.**

Restated in `p5lXLETWI5s` (same round) against a real submitted interior, where she scopes it carefully to *architectural minimalism* and says she would never tell someone to demolish over it.

## Materials and Detailing

- **Wood + concrete is not inherently bad** — the failure is selection. People pick both textures too active: rustic wood plus heavily mottled concrete **in the wrong undertone**. She says it can be done well and shows examples, but that "коротко ответить, а как же надо, невозможно." Ties directly to the undertone analysis in `haM4H-b-bZM` (Round 2).
- **⚠️ Tile corner trims and edge profiles — rejected for the fourth time from this channel, now with new evidence.** She notes that **abroad, and even in Russian developers' own common-area finishes, everything is done flush**, and that she has repeatedly executed flush details on her own projects and they are **durable** — "живучие решения." This directly counters the objection that flush junctions are fragile, which is the usual argument for keeping trims.
- **Surface-mounted skirting**, and **⚠️ she pre-empts the standard dust objection against shadow gaps**: dust settles on a surface-mounted skirting too — **and there you have to wipe it by hand, smearing the wall, whereas a robot vacuum will get into a shadow gap and do it for you without touching the wall.** A neat inversion of the usual argument.
- **Prints** — minimalism doesn't use them. It avoids boredom instead through **texture: a different material, a different weave, a different degree of gloss or transparency.**
- **Photo wallpaper** — never looked good in her view; each period had its trendy variant, and none survived.

## ⚠️ Planning Rules — Storage for the Future, Not the Present

**The tenth item, and the most broadly applicable.** People assess their *current* possessions competently and organise storage for them — often decluttering well. **What they forget is the future**: a vacuum cleaner yet to be bought, a child, pets, a hobby returned to. Each brings a wave of new objects. **"У человека оказывается не минимализм, а шоу-рум мелких товаров на дому."**

**Her prescription: think proactively and over-provide** — "может быть, вы планируете когда-нибудь вернуться к лыжам… может быть, вы заберёте щеночка из приюта." **Directly relevant to this project**, which has two young children and therefore a predictable multi-year sequence of exactly this.
