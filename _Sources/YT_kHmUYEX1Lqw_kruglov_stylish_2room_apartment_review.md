## Genuinely New Content

**⚠️ Insufficient counter clearance for food prep in a compact one-wall kitchen**: too little space between sink/cooktop and cooktop/coffee-machine for comfortable cooking, diagnosed as a 4-burner cooktop being oversized for the footprint's realistic prep-space needs — acceptable here only because these specific clients don't cook much and rely on delivery. A concrete "verify clearance against your own cooking habits" caution.

**⚠️ Heated-floor thermostat mounted on the kitchen backsplash — a real placement mistake**: competes for scarce outlet space in the highest-outlet-demand zone, and its 2-3 hour warmth-lag makes a backsplash-adjacent manual toggle a poor UX match. Recommends wall placement plus a smart programmable thermostat on a twice-daily fixed schedule.

**Voice-controlled hands-free lighting/curtains, a concrete cooking-scenario justification**: "Alice, close the kitchen curtains" lets someone with dough/flour-covered hands adjust the room without touching a switch — a specific relatable use case for justifying smart-home voice control cost to a client, beyond generic convenience framing.

**TV cable-concealment niche, 15cm deep**: for a wall-mounted TV in a walkway zone, a 15cm-deep recessed niche lets the TV sit flush while fully hiding its cables.

**⚠️ Oversized sofa (~60% of living room floor area) — a real retrospective critique, plus a related outlet-planning miss**: the presenter's own stated view, sitting in the finished room, is that a smaller sofa (skipping the matching console/nightstand) would have served the same function; separately, no outlet was placed near/behind the sofa, so phone charging while seated needs an extension cord — worth checking outlet placement against actual seating layout.

**⚠️ Push-to-open ("tip-touch") furniture-front hardware, used throughout the apartment — a real, repeated tradeoff**: opens from the slightest touch (brushing past a nightstand, leaning near a wardrobe), a genuine annoyance households need to adjust to; framed as still preferable to the alternatives (a protruding handle catches on things; a cut-out finger-pull collects grime), not a design mistake to avoid outright.

**⚠️ Switch/outlet embedded inside a full mirror panel — a real installed mistake**: hard to locate blind at night, and the surrounding mirror smudges with fingerprints faster than elsewhere. Fix: stop the mirror short of the switch/outlet zone and cover that section with a matching non-reflective panel instead.

**⚠️ Gypsum wall-panel casting technique — pre-filled cavities at fixture locations**: a cast-in-place gypsum panel should have solid pre-filled cavities specifically where an intercom handset or switch/outlet faceplate will later mount, so the final surface reads as one continuous plane once hardware is installed — a planning step for the casting stage, not correctable afterward.

**⚠️ Concrete worked master-switch confusion scenario, extending this store's existing abstract caution**: a zone master switch (bedroom/walk-in/bathroom) plus a separate whole-apartment exit switch interact confusingly — one partner turns off the zone switch at bedtime, the other leaves without turning any bedroom light on and hits the whole-apartment exit switch, which turns on every light *except* the already-off bedroom zone, confusing whoever is woken by it. Fix stated directly: standardize on one single lighting-control philosophy apartment-wide rather than layering a zone master switch on top of a whole-apartment one.

**⚠️ Corner-drain vs. center-drain tub preference, a real forced tradeoff**: presenter states a personal dislike for center-positioned tub drain-overflow assemblies (vs. corner/end); one real project used a center-drain tub anyway because the mixer/drain-overflow plumbing had already been rough-in-committed to a center position before the tub-model preference was reconsidered — plan drain-position preference before plumbing rough-in if it matters.

**Deliberate wall clearance around a ventilation duct/box for future toilet-paper storage**: leaving space between a duct and adjacent wall specifically reserves room for a later shelf-and-door storage module, planned at design stage rather than retrofitted around the duct.
