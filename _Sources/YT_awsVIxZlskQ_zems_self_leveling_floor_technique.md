## Walls / Ceilings (Self-Leveling Floor) — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Room-zone partitioning before pour**: a large area must be divided into smaller "contours" (zones) using metal profile strips placed across doorways/openings before pouring — because self-leveling compound is fast-curing, and by the time the far side of a very large single pour is reached, the near side may have already begun setting, preventing a uniformly flat single surface. `confirmed`, mechanism-based rationale, not just a vague caution.
- **Edge tape (кромочная лента) installed around the full perimeter of each zone before pour**, adhered with contact adhesive — its function is explicitly stated as compensating for the screed's shrinkage/deformation during moisture loss while curing, i.e. preventing the screed from tearing itself apart as it dries. `confirmed`, genuinely new mechanism explanation for edge tape not previously recorded this specifically in this store.
- **Bonding primer (бетоноконтакт) rolled onto the base after edge-taping, before pour** — same bonding-primer material/purpose already recorded elsewhere in this store, restated here specifically for the self-leveling-floor stage.
- **"Find the highest point, then work down from it" leveling method — full technique, genuinely new and specific**:
  1. Use a self-leveling rotary laser level set to horizontal mode — it establishes one continuous, perfectly horizontal reference plane across the room. (Source explicitly contrasts this against inferior methods some installers use: running a level along metal strips by hand, or walking around trying to spot the high point by eye.)
  2. Measure with a tape from the laser's reference line down to the floor at multiple points around the room — the point with the *smallest* distance to the laser line is the room's highest point.
  3. Adjust the laser unit's height (via the tripod's height-adjustment screw) so its beam sits **exactly 1 meter above that highest point**.
  4. Fabricate a simple **metal template/gauge whose reference mark sits 995 mm from its bottom edge — deliberately 5 mm less than 1 meter.** Hold the template against the wall with its mark aligned to the laser line, and mark the wall 995 mm below the laser line, all the way around the room — this transfers the laser's 1-meter horizontal plane down to a target pour-fill line that sits a uniform 5 mm above the room's single highest floor point.
  5. Mix and pour self-leveling compound in each zone, spreading with a needle/spiked roller, filling to the marked line in each zone — since every zone's marks come from the same single reference plane, the finished zones join up perfectly level with each other.
  - `confirmed`, a fully specified, checkable technique — the 995 mm (1 m minus 5 mm) template-offset trick is a genuinely new, precise numeric method not previously recorded in this store for self-leveling floor work.
