# Source Note — NSDSGN, space organiser audits the designer's own flat

**33 minutes. The mirror image of [[YT_9ZwqMKiMgvc_nsdsgn_storage_organiser_family_flat]]** — there Ксения's flat was toured; here **she audits his**, room by room, and he explicitly left everything as it actually is: «я ничего не менял, ничего специально не готовил к съёмке».

**⚠️ Note the chronology: this is 2026-02-03, so it is the EARLIER of the two visits** — his flat first, hers seven weeks later. Round 1 processed the later one. Ксения is stated here as having organised space professionally for **6+ years**.

**Promotional ratio low.** The close plugs her blog (where she answers subscriber questions free every Sunday with links and article numbers) and his studio. **Nothing is sold, and much of the content is her contradicting products, him conceding his own mistakes on camera, and one genuine unresolved disagreement between them.**

**No prices at all.** He mentions in passing that their first breakout video, on kitchens, reached **~4 million views** — worth noting against Round 1's finding that view counts are noise on this channel: the storage format is the exception.

## ⚠️ Three causes of disorder — and the first reassigns the blame

1. **«Самая первая причина беспорядка — это не вы, а ваша неудобная мебель.»** Especially ready-made furniture, which is usually simply inconvenient: **«на этих полках никому непонятно, как соблюдать порядок. Поэтому получается хаос.»** **→ Mess is a furniture-design failure before it is a discipline failure, which relocates the fix to the specification stage.**
2. **The quantity of things, plus the absence of time** to work out what is needed and what is not — «на это действительно нужно много времени и сил».
3. **⚠️ Marketplace "storage lifehack" content — and she says it plainly, expecting to be attacked for it: «это обзорщики ВБ, мамочки, которые снимают типа классные лайфхаки для хранения, которые продаются на маркетплейсах. На самом деле это всё не работает, ребята. Чем проще, тем лучше.»** **A professional's direct anti-influencer position, and it is consistent with her rejection of decanting shampoos in Round 1.**

## ⚠️ Product verdicts — the format's real value, because she rates each one

**Endorsed:**

- **Rotating trays / turntables — but with a placement distinction.** On a countertop the rotation is «не очень обязательно»; **inside a cabinet a rotating stand to reach the back row is «очень хорошая рабочая история»**, cheap and works well in daily use. Named uses: perfumes, cosmetics, spices, oils.
- **Any trays and stands, on the general principle that they «ограничивают от разбегания вещей».**
- **⚠️ Her most-recommended single product, with dimensions: a container 45 cm deep, on wheels, with a handle, and with repositionable dividers.** «Супер универсальный, супербазовый» — usable in any room on any shelf, and **two fit perfectly into a kitchen drawer. «Я всегда в свои проекты просто десятками закупаю эти контейнеры.»**
- **⚠️ Her second: a spring-loaded expanding divider (раздвижной разделитель) on a tension strut.** Divides categories inside a drawer so they do not mix; **she says it works in literally every zone** — underwear, socks, bras, t-shirts, shorts, children's toys, and cloths stored vertically.
- **Modular trays (модульные лотки) for bathroom drawers — «самое лучшее решение».** Different sizes, sold in sets or singly, **so you assemble your own mosaic to your actual needs.** Transparent ones look weightless and may fit better around drawer locks. **«Совершенно бюджетная история.»**
- **An adhesive holder for a mop or any tube-shaped item** — stick it to the wall «и она висит у тебя, а не на полу лежит».
- **An adhesive cloth/towel holder inside a cabinet — her answer to the recurring "where do I dry wet cloths" question.**

**Rejected, with reasons:**

- **Hanging baskets («подвесные корзинки») — «это плохая история».**
- **Multi-level pyramids — «это уже колхоз».** A plain tray or stand achieves the point.
- **⚠️ The interlocking / notched dividers sold on marketplaces that assemble into any shape: rejected on function, not looks.** «Выглядит довольно странно, колхозно» — but the real objection is **«они не держатся, они разбалтываются, и свою функцию вообще никаким образом не выполняют».**
- **Dividers in a kitchen are largely unnecessary** — she recommends them for **bags** (so they do not fall over) and in the wardrobe.
- **A lid organiser he owns: «штука хорошая, но можно лучше».** Mechanism: **lids are all different sizes and variously domed, while that stand's compartments are all identical.** The better version has **sliding, repositionable dividers so you can customise to your own items** — and the same sliding stands **also hold pans, baking forms and boards vertically.**

## ⚠️ The container-sizing rule — the most transferable principle in the source

**«В нижнем ящике яркий пример, когда у нас мелкие категории, но большие контейнеры — и всё равно бардак.»**

**→ Match container size to CATEGORY size. Where categories are small — desk drawers, bathrooms — use small containers.** A large container holding small categories still produces mess, so buying "storage" without sizing it to the contents changes nothing.

## Kitchen — what she praises, and one real disagreement

- **The correct height logic, confirmed: what you use often goes LOW; less-used higher; rarest at the top.**
- **⚠️ She specifically praises plates high and cups low, and names the common failure: «обычно мебельщики, кухонщики меняют эту логику, и за чашками приходится прыгать» — especially for children.** Cups are used constantly, including by children, so they belong low.
- **⚠️ The dark corner base cabinet with SHELVES: «это проблема неудобной мебели», and she says everyone has one.** The governing principle for fixing it: **«нам нужно максимально постараться, чтобы эта полка стала похожа на ящик».** Methods: **pull-out shelves that fix with double-sided tape** (wooden or metal, sold ready-made), or **her container solution — two containers pushed into the corner and two in front, established by experiment.** Then reaching the corner means pulling out two containers rather than removing dozens of small items.
- **Under the sink**: a square bin with straight corners is praised; bags go into a smaller wheeled container, stackable. **⚠️ Her favourite tip: glue a small container inside the door beside the dishwasher for the tablets — scrape food into the sink, take a tablet, load.**
- Upper shelves: **two or three containers or baskets holding grains and teas**, and she further prefers **jars for grains, loose goods and teas** — more aesthetic and, she argues, more convenient.

**⚠️ A dimensional finding that is the best design item here: she normally does NOT recommend hinged (распашные) facades on 600 mm kitchen uppers «потому что они бьют в голову» — they hit you in the head. It does not happen in his kitchen because he made the countertop 100 mm DEEPER than standard.** **→ Extra countertop depth buys standing clearance from the upper doors.** *(Sits directly alongside Round 1's 300 mm hob clearance and 500–600 mm upper-cabinet clearance on [[YT_-6QKr4HerCQ_nsdsgn_kitchen_25_ordering_mistakes]] — three independent clearance mechanisms in one kitchen.)*

**⚠️ THE DISAGREEMENT, recorded as a Perspectives case because neither concedes:**

- He bought **Blum's own internal fitting** — a wooden tray behind a single facade, on a «поводок». Her assessment of the tray itself: **beautiful, and functionally «почти нет мёртвых зон» — no complaints.**
- **But she strongly dislikes hidden / internal drawers as a type: «я очень сильно не люблю и не рекомендую скрытые ящики».** Opening one facade reveals the tray, **but «добраться до второго ящика — это у нас большой труд».**
- **Her rule: «на кухнях я за удобство… рекомендую ящики делать обычными настоящими ящиками, каждый со своим фасадом».** Two reasons: **the extra body movements are a real daily cost if you cook often**, and **«а если оно сломалось?»**
- **He bought it deliberately as the native Blum solution and is «в целом очень доволен ей».** **→ Recorded as a genuine practitioner split: a fabricator-endorsed premium mechanism versus an organiser's movement-count objection.**

## Bathroom

- **⚠️ A specification detail he volunteers and it is worth keeping: he tells furniture makers about the trap cut-out — with a FLAT siphon a minimal cut-out is possible, whereas «мебельщики любят вот такой вырез», after which «там потом вообще ничего не поместится».** **→ Specify a flat siphon to preserve usable drawer volume.**
- **Pull-out drawers in the vanity: she confirms he is right to have them.**
- **Collect dirty laundry in the bathroom, sorted by colour at the point of undressing** — a small space is fine because **two or three containers fit.** «Мы же всё равно это сортируем перед стиркой.» **Direct corroboration of the same four-container rule from her own flat in Round 1.**
- **⚠️ Her addition to his bathroom: a shallow door-fronted cabinet beside the basin.** The reason is behavioural: brushes and products you need immediately to hand are awkward to take from a drawer **«потому что ты стоишь тут мокрый»**. It removes **«весь визуальный шум, который хранится на раковине»**. «Отличное решение.»

## ⚠️ Her professional tool case — worth recording because it reveals the method

Double-sided and ordinary tape, stationery elastics, **velcro cable ties for coiling wires**, **blank tags for labelling baskets you cannot stick to**, **a battery tester** — «проверяем, какие годные, какие негодные, чтобы избавляться от хламья» — the adhesive mop holder, and **two label printers**, one fully autonomous printing a narrow strip and one Bluetooth-app-driven printing a larger label.

**The battery tester is the telling item: decluttering needs a decision procedure, not willpower.** And the label printers make her Round 1 recommendation of labelled drawer fronts operationally cheap.

## Прихожая / entrance

- **⚠️ Her general recommendation, which she flags designers disagree with: bring drawers OUT from behind the facade — «чтобы они были не спрятаны за дверями, а были отдельно».** The reason is movement count: «вы пришли, открыли, бросили свои шапки» — **and especially with children, because «детям открыть два фасада ровно под 90°, выдвинуть ящик — это практически нереальная задача».** **She names the objection herself: «я знаю, что дизайнеры против». → A Perspectives case: façade continuity versus movements-per-use.**
- **Tile running UNDER the wardrobe with underfloor heating so shoes dry** — endorsed by both, with a nuance: he has underfloor heating outside the cupboard and she thinks that is sufficient, **preferring the bottom of the cupboard to be a DRAWER for shoe-care sprays instead**, which is more functional. His own added point: **making the bottom shelf pull-out loses a little more space beneath it — wasted volume.**
- **⚠️ Her standing recommendation: always keep at least a small OPEN space for just the shoes you have literally arrived in, left visible.** Combined with the underfloor heating there.
- **⚠️ A width check that is genuinely actionable: his shoe rack has «правильная ширина, комфортно встаёт три пары» — and she says watch for this, because «не бывают странных размеров… измерьте свои кроссовки, просто и посмотрите».**
- **⚠️ Open storage for a bag is important and usually missing: «обычно такие открытые места для хранения отсутствуют, хотя в прихожей это действительно нужно».** He throws his bag onto a shelf added after the renovation, which also holds keys and outdoor glasses behind a lip; he uses it constantly and «очень радуюсь».
- **Criticised: an open gap at the bottom where «в глубине неизвестно что — скорее всего, просто очень много пыли».**
- A transparent glass shelf is good in a walk-in for displaying accessories, **but here it produced an unattractive view and there is visibly not enough room for hats, gloves, caps and bags.**
- **⚠️ And her diagnosis of the whole hallway, which names the Round 1 method explicitly: «очень важно, что ты за собой проследил, где тебе неудобно. Это называется сценарий жизни. Когда ты наблюдаешь за собой, ты очень быстро начинаешь понимать, как перестроить свой быт.»** **Direct corroboration of the observe-your-current-behaviour intake method from Round 1.**

## ⚠️ Wardrobe — rail heights, and an ergonomic arithmetic failure

**The best dimensional content in the source, because she diagnoses a cause rather than stating a number.**

- His arrangement — **two rows of short hanging plus one row of long** — is right, and **«два ряда коротких штанг — хорошая рабочая история, практически для всех ростов доступна».**
- **⚠️ But the top rail is already uncomfortable for her at average height, and the cause is at the BOTTOM: he has a 150–180 mm set-back / plinth below, so all of that height was lost low down and pushed the upper rail out of reach.** «Мне уже как раз вот этих 15–18 см до верхней штанги не хватает.»
- **→ The rule: in a two-tier short-hanging arrangement, every millimetre spent below the lower rail comes off the reachability of the upper one.** And the bottom shelves in question **collect dust heavily and he stores nothing there** — so the height was spent for nothing.
- **⚠️ A second, distinct finding about a shelf ABOVE the rail: «вот эта полка на самом деле функционально не очень нужна», and she says her colleagues consistently agree. The mechanism is sightline: if you are taller you must bend down to see along the row you want — «она обзор перекрывает». Without it you would have a clean view down from your own height.** He defends it as a place to lay laundry before hanging, which he finds convenient — **recorded as his counter-use.**

## ⚠️ Vertical / file storage — and the scope limit the two videos together establish

- **Организаторы пространства recommend drawers over shelves, especially for underwear.** Within a chest of drawers the method is **вертикальное хранение — «складывание вещей по типу картотеки»**: fold each item into an envelope or roll it and stand it upright, **so opening the drawer gives an immediate full view and you take one item without disturbing the rest.** More relevant, she says, where a specific set or specific tights must be found.
- The failure it fixes, illustrated comically: men take the top pair of pants, wash them, return them to the top, and never wear the ones at the bottom — **the stack-rotation problem.**
- **He has a genuine realisation on camera and commits to redoing it.**
- **⚠️ AND THE SCOPE LIMIT, which only emerges from reading both videos together: Round 1 recorded this same household ABANDONING vertical storage for the husband's t-shirts** — because t-shirts were his most-worn daily item and hanging them all visible worked better. **So file storage is her general recommendation, and her own household documented one case where it lost to hanging, for the highest-frequency item.** Both belong on the page as a scoped rule rather than a universal one.
- **Labelling makes returning laundry thoughtless: he labelled socks, pants and shorts, so «я открываю, просто кидаю».** The top shelf he never uses is long-term storage.

## ⚠️ Velvet hangers — a three-year durability report

- **The advantage is real: nothing slips off them.**
- **⚠️ The cost, after three years with a long-haired cat: «это прямо вот ужасно» — everything gets covered, and with age the cheaper ones «начинают сыпаться» onto the shelves, which then need wiping.**
- **Verdict: recommended with the caveat — «с одной стороны рекомендую, с другой стороны надо думать вот об этом».**
- **Alternatives she names: plastic hangers; and «самые лучшие плечики — просто простые деревянные», though they take more space if you are economising — with a newer collection of THIN wooden hangers now available** (she names a Leroy Merlin range; **the brand string is ASR-garbled and recorded `uncertain`**).
- **Praise: putting containers, кофры or baskets on a shelf reads much tidier than loose items** — and wider кофры exist, so two cubes would lose little storage and look much better. The wire/mesh alternative he tried is «гаражная сельская история» and unsuitable for underwear.

## ⚠️ Trousers — a clear verdict and a named product

- **She «строго-настрого» recommends getting rid of the multi-tier trouser hanger: «это просто ужас».** Her objection is mechanical rather than aesthetic: **«вытянуть можно, но как ты запихиваешь это обратно?»** He agrees it looks bad.
- **Correct solutions: fold into drawers, or hang each pair on its own hanger. «Самое хорошее решение — вешать джинсы, брюки на плечики.»**
- **⚠️ Plus a named product with its mechanism: специальные брючные плечики, where only the protruding bar carries the garment, so «ты можешь просто перекинуть, даже не снимая со штанги».**

## Glasses — a second, milder disagreement

He fixed transparent cases to the end panel so all his glasses are visible (they previously lived under socks), conceding it adds visual clutter. **Her advice: store glasses in a drawer as well — «во-первых, они не пылятся», and you pull it out, see everything and take what you need.** **Recorded because it is the same drawer-over-display preference that drives her whole method.**
