## Furniture / Design Concept — What Makes a Chair Read Right

- **⚠️ The leg rule, sharpened past "no splayed legs" — the most useful item here.** Her objection is specifically to legs raked in **two** directions at once: "не только под углом назад, но ещё и вбок — назад было бы достаточно." **A backward rake alone is fine; adding a sideways splay is what breaks it** (and is what you kick). This is a real refinement of the blanket rule she states in `XGI6FS2ZdCc`, `GcTaKRQ3QC0` and `rRQ05JAoLtk`, and worth recording as the precise version.
- **⚠️ Legs must be visually anchored to something, not "growing from nowhere."** Her named failure: legs attached at arbitrary points, "растущими непонятно откуда, просто рандомно… ни к чему не привязаны и растут вникуда." **The leg rake should coincide with the back's angle**; there should be **соосные элементы** — coaxially aligned or at least ideationally linked parts. Without that the chair reads as "винегрет из деталей, которые не связаны друг с другом." Same principle as the "equal radii at every junction" rule in `rRQ05JAoLtk`, applied to a different axis.
- **Junction quality (узлы сопряжения деталей) is the tell.** She uses it repeatedly as the marker separating a considered chair from an unconsidered one — visible glue lines, unresolved corners where an armrest turns into a leg, an unshaped seat.
- **⚠️ Bulk versus room size**: massive chairs read disproportionate in a small room. Multiple viewers independently reported the same impression to her, which she notes.
- **A cheap light-wood chair betrays itself through construction**: to hit the price the seat is glued up from many small offcuts, which reads untidy and cheap in light timber — **the same model in black or a dark finish hides it** and becomes specifiable.
- **⚠️ "Детскость" — a childish note — disqualifies a chair from an adult dining zone.** She names this as a recurring characteristic of Russian furniture design generally (repeated from `GcTaKRQ3QC0`): fine in a kids' room, wrong in a strict interior.
- **⚠️ Saturated colours lower the perceived status of an interior and cheapen it** — stated here about chairs specifically. Corroborates the same practitioner's bright-accent-colour position in `XGI6FS2ZdCc`; `single-account`.

## Design Concept — Mid-Century as the Safe "Something Extra"

Her stated position on mixing anything into minimalism: **if you add one thing, add mid-century/Bauhaus notes.** Argument: those pieces have stayed current for decades and she has watched them not date over ~15 years. Named archetypes rather than SKUs: the Wassily chair, the Pierre Jeanneret chair and its many interpretations, CH-series chairs.

**⚠️ Attached caution worth keeping** — a period chair alone does not deliver the look; it needs supporting elements from the same period: period lighting, matching coffee tables, and **more glass with chromed metal**. A single vintage chair in an otherwise plain minimalist room reads as an orphan.

She also notes the market reality that one particular imported chair became the de-facto standard because it was the only comfortable good-looking one available, so essentially every Russian maker now produces a lookalike — meaning an equivalent is easy to source cheaply.
