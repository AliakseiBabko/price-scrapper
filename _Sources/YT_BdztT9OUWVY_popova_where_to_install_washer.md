## Appliance Types and Standard Dimensions — Genuinely New Content

- **Built-in vs. freestanding front-load washers, cost and footprint difference stated**: built-in (встраиваемая) models cost roughly **20-30% more** than freestanding models, but have a smaller footprint, a flat front panel, and can have a matching furniture facade hung directly onto them. Freestanding models are usually made with a protruding front panel instead. The source states she has never worked with top-loading machines, so this video's scope is explicitly front-load only.
- **Standard washer external dimensions**: 60cm wide × 60cm deep × 85cm tall for a standard-size model — a concrete baseline dimension this page didn't previously carry.
- **Door hinge/handle side convention**: most washer models have the door hinge on the left and the handle/controls on the right — a detail worth checking before placing a unit tight against a wall on one particular side, since it affects which side needs door-swing clearance.
- **Washing machines don't need a sloped drain line** (unlike a standard fixture drain) because they contain their own internal pump that actively pumps water out — a plumbing-mechanism detail distinct from, and complementary to, this project's existing dry-siphon-trap drain content (the trap prevents sewer-gas backflow; this fact is about slope, a separate plumbing-design question).

## Real Project Examples — Genuinely New Content

- **A 36m² one-bedroom apartment gained two separate utility spaces via reconfiguration**: a walk-in pantry/closet accessed from the hallway, and a separate clothing wardrobe/closet accessed from the living room — each fitted with its own washing machine. A real data point that a dedicated utility space for a washer is achievable even in a small apartment via replanning, not just in a large home.
- **Drain hose routed inside the floor screed during construction, reason stated**: embedding the drain-hose path in the screed keeps the hose itself from kinking/deforming over time, and — deliberately — leaves it replaceable later if needed, rather than being permanently sealed in.
- **Floor waterproofing plus waterproofing up the lower portion of the walls is required wherever a washer is installed in a dedicated utility space** — stated as a hard requirement for this placement, not optional.

## Bathroom Placement Sub-Options — Genuinely New Content, With Specific Clearances

- **Under-sink installation, two hard clearance numbers**: the washer's own depth must be **no more than 45cm**, because space is needed behind it (between the machine and the wall) for the drain and water-supply connections; the counter/sink height should be chosen to suit the household's own height (an ergonomic sizing note, not a fixed number). A custom countertop-plus-separate-sink installation is one implementation option; a purpose-made under-sink-washer sink unit is the other.
- **Beside-sink installation, a minimum combined footprint stated**: at least 90cm of width and 50cm of depth of free floor space is needed to fit both the washer and the sink side by side.
- **A real worked example with exact figures**: a washer-with-drying-function at 47cm depth, paired with a 60cm-deep plastic countertop and a natural-stone sink, with towel/storage shelving on the other side of the same run.
- **A full-height (floor-to-ceiling) niche built specifically to house the washer** allows either a dryer to be stacked directly on top, or — if no dryer — shelves above the washer for a dirty-laundry basket and household chemicals. A distinct planning option from a simple under-counter or beside-sink placement.
- **When a washer sits opposite (not beside) the sink/vanity, a purpose-made cabinet mounted above it (available at IKEA and other furniture retailers) avoids wasting the vertical space over the machine** — an off-the-shelf solution named for this specific layout gap. Recorded as a technique (a category of product, not a specific brand endorsement — IKEA named only as a source, not a recommendation of that retailer specifically).

## Hallway and Kitchen Placement — Genuinely New Content

- **Disguising a washer behind furniture-style cabinet doors ("imitation wardrobe") in a hallway** is described as a very commonly used placement, with two of the source's own real projects shown: a washer recessed into a niche with small shelves above it, the whole assembly hidden behind a matching furniture facade.
- **A real case of a washer physically located in an inconvenient kitchen corner but accessed from the hallway side instead of the kitchen** — the source's stated reasoning: that kitchen corner had little other practical use, so the workaround was to give it a hallway-facing access door rather than force a kitchen-facing installation; disguised as a shelving niche behind a facade, described as unnoticeable to a visitor.
- **Freestanding-in-kitchen-cabinet sizing rule, a specific number distinct from the general 45cm bathroom figure above**: to fit a freestanding (non-built-in) machine inside a kitchen cabinet, the source recommends choosing a model **no more than 50cm deep** and slightly shorter in height, plus ordering a wider cabinet with two doors to accommodate it. Built-in models are simpler here — they fit the standard 60cm width/depth footprint directly, with the furniture facade hung straight onto the machine's own flat front.
- **When a kitchen-hidden washer isn't disguised behind a matching facade at all, ordering the lower cabinet fronts in plain white keeps it visually consistent with the rest of the kitchen** — a stated fallback aesthetic rule for the (less common) case of a visibly exposed washer.
