## Materials / Mistakes-Warnings — Developer Screed Pull-Off Strength Test, a Quantified Failure Rate

- **⚠️ Named test and quantified failure rate**: this company tests every developer-poured screed for compressive and pull-off ("на отрыв") strength before agreeing to bond flooring adhesive to it — **in 98% of cases, the developer's own screed fails to meet the required standard**. Concrete numbers given: developer screed typically tests at **0.2-0.65 MPa** pull-off strength against a stated minimum requirement of **1 MPa** — i.e., "the developer's screed is almost always roughly five times worse than the standard." This project's own tested screed came in at **0.64 MPa cohesion strength** — still below the 1 MPa threshold and rejected/demolished on that basis, consistent with the general 98%-failure claim.
- **⚠️ Named consequence of skipping this test**: bonding a self-leveling compound, parquet/engineered board adhesive, or tile directly onto an under-strength developer screed risks the whole assembly (leveler, flooring, tile) delaminating/detaching after some period of use — framed as a real, not hypothetical, risk.
- **Stated rationale for full screed replacement over screed repair**: a fully replaced screed is described as guaranteed never to crack, never to "stand up" (buckle), and never to shed the flooring bonded to it — framed as the reliable fix once a developer screed fails testing, vs. attempting to reinforce or partially repair the existing one.

## Engineering / Systems — Heating-System Manifold Conversion, Extending the Existing Tee-vs-Manifold Argument to Heating

- **⚠️ Named system-design change, extends this store's existing manifold-vs-tee water-supply leak-detectability argument to the heating/radiator system specifically**: developer-installed heating is typically run on tee ("тройник") branch connections embedded in the floor/screed — described as a real leak risk multiplying with apartment size (potentially dozens of buried tee joints in a large apartment), with a real consequence (flooding one's own apartment or a neighbor's unit below). **Fix**: cut the developer's heating supply/return lines where they enter the apartment, route them into a dedicated manifold box/panel ("коллекторный щит"), then run individual dedicated pipe runs from that manifold to each radiator — eliminating all in-floor/in-screed joints entirely (every remaining joint is at the accessible manifold or at the radiator itself). This also enables a smartphone-connected leak-protection system at the manifold, controllable remotely (the speaker's own example: monitoring/controlling it "even sitting abroad") — explicitly not possible with a buried-tee system, since there's no single accessible point to instrument.

## Materials / Mistakes-Warnings — Soundproofing-Membrane Pour-Box Construction Technique

- **⚠️ Named two-layer membrane + plywood-formwork technique for a screed pour over soundproofing membrane ("Шуманет 100," used here as both sound- and water-proofing)**: (1) lay a first membrane layer, running it up the walls; (2) install partition-wall stud framing on top, which necessarily punctures the first membrane layer at each fastener — acknowledged directly as leaving the waterproofing "so-so" at that stage; (3) clad the stud framing with plywood sheathing set **significantly taller than the planned screed height**, fastened only at the very top edge (specifically so it can later be unscrewed and removed); (4) lay a **second** membrane layer over the completed framing, running it up onto the plywood facing and up all perimeter walls — this creates a fully sealed "tub"/basin with no punctures below the final screed pour line, since the second layer goes on *after* all stud-fastening punctures already happened in the first layer. **Named function of the plywood strips**: they double as the screed's required perimeter expansion-joint damper (so the screed pour doesn't bear directly against the walls) — once the screed cures, the plywood strips are unscrewed and pulled out, leaving a clean air gap between the stud framing and the cured screed edge, exactly the plywood's own thickness.
- **⚠️ Stated stakes for getting the "tub" waterproofing wrong**: named explicitly as scaling with the apartment's position — the speaker's example: an 18th-floor unit in an elite high-rise building risks tens to hundreds of millions of rubles in cumulative downstream flood liability if the seal fails, framed as a direct argument for the extra care in this technique (no currency conversion performed — illustrative order-of-magnitude claim, not a quoted price).

## Mistakes / Warnings — Door-Opening Height Uncertainty: a Sliding-Profile Technique

- **⚠️ Named technique and the coordination failure it prevents**: because final floor height isn't known until after the screed is poured, a door-opening-forming profile is **taped in place with adhesive tape rather than fastened**, at this pre-screed stage — deliberately left "slidable." **Named failure mode this avoids**: a drywall installer cladding the surrounding partition framing might otherwise assume the taped-in-place opening already reflects final dimensions and simply build to it — if the true finished floor level later comes in higher than assumed, the opening would be too short and the partition would have to be partially disassembled to fix it (vs. an opening that came in too tall, which is a minor fix — just add extra drywall). **Stated purpose of the tape-not-fasten convention**: it visibly signals to any installer working nearby that the opening is not yet dimensionally final, prompting them to check the design project or ask before building around it, rather than assuming it's already correct.
