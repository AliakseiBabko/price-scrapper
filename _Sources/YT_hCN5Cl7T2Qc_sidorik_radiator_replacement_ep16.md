## Plumbing

- **Developer-installed heating-distribution architecture in this new-build unit**: a shared distribution cabinet ("тепловой щит") in the common corridor serves all apartments on the floor — supply line (top, with ball valves and heat meters) and return line (bottom), allowing water to be shut off per-apartment for radiator replacement or emergencies. From the cabinet, two Ø25mm cross-linked-polyethylene ("сшитый полиэтилен"/PEX) pipes run into the apartment (supply in red corrugated sleeve, return in blue), stepping down to Ø18mm then Ø18→14mm branches to each radiator — sized down deliberately so water pressure is forced through every radiator across all rooms rather than short-circuiting the nearest one. `confirmed`, developer-installed, observed directly.
- **Named PEX pipe brand from this developer**: Kan (Польша/Poland-associated brand name as transcribed) — both the developer's original pipes and Sidorik's own replacement pipes are this brand, purchased at the same spec to match. Stated PEX pipe design life: 50 years; manufacturer material warranty: 10 years. `confirmed`, product-label claim.
- **Developer's original bottom-connection radiator hardware**: H-shaped Danfoss valve (nicknamed "бинокль"/"binoculars" by plumbers) plus copper chrome-plated Kan supply pipes, connected from below through the floor. `confirmed`, observed directly, no complaint about the hardware's reliability itself.
- **Mistake/Warning — bottom-through-floor radiator connection is a real functional and aesthetic problem, independent of hardware quality**: requires cutting into the finished floor covering for the pipe penetration (visually unattractive) and creates an ongoing cleaning obstruction once the floor is finished (pipes protruding from the floor interfere with routine cleaning). This **corroborates and reinforces** the existing store fact already on `12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Radiators_and_Convectors.md` (Kruglov/Ontario, "Radiator Connection Side" section) that bottom-through-floor connection is discouraged in favor of through-the-wall connection — now independently confirmed by a second, unrelated practitioner giving the same reasoning (floor-covering damage, cleaning interference). `confirmed`, `corroborates_existing`.
- **Radiator materials permitted for a standard central-heating apartment system, per this practitioner**: bimetallic, steel, or cast iron. **Aluminum radiators are explicitly not recommended** for this use case. This is consistent with (not contradicting) the existing comparison table on `Radiators_and_Convectors.md`, which already flags aluminum as poor for central-apartment heating due to pressure/corrosion/water-hammer vulnerability and better suited to private houses. `confirmed`, `corroborates_existing`.
- **Chosen replacement radiator, named product**: Royal Thermo Piano Forte bimetallic design radiator — fully steel manifold/collector, stated 25-year warranty, 30 atm working pressure, 45 atm test/pressure-test rating. His own building's heating-system operating pressure: 4 atm (a small fraction of the radiator's rated capacity, i.e. large safety margin). Available in black, grey, white; he wanted black but chose grey by household compromise. Paint brand named as "экзо нобель" (likely AkzoNobel — ASR-uncertain transcription, `ASR-uncertain`). Radiators ship factory-wrapped in film, with the manufacturer's own instruction not to remove the wrap until the renovation is fully finished (dust protection). `confirmed`, product-label/spec claim.
- **Radiator plumbing-accessory shopping list, itemized separately from the radiator itself** (not bundled with the radiator purchase): a matched-color installation bushing/adapter set, adjustable mounting brackets, a thermostatic head, and shutoff + thermostatic-regulating valves — all bought separately. Named valve brand: Danfoss. `confirmed`.
- **Danfoss valve mechanism detail, stated as a durability advantage**: these valves use a brass cone for sealing rather than rubber/elastomer sealing rings — no serviceable wear parts, and longer service life than equivalent valves that rely on O-ring seals. `confirmed`, product-mechanism claim, `single-account`.
- **Valve orientation mistake and correction**: he initially ordered side-entry ("боковые") shutoff/thermostatic valves by mistake, then had to swap for axial ("осевой") valves so the thermostatic head would point outward into the room rather than toward the wall. Flagged as a real ordering mistake worth warning others about when planning a radiator replacement parts list. `confirmed`, `Mistakes/Warnings`-relevant.
- **PPSU fitting installation rules — two specific rules stated as near-universal manufacturer guidance, not just this brand**: (1) **PPSU (ППСУ) fittings must never be sprayed/packed with expanding polyurethane mounting foam** — a common plumber shortcut to reduce plastering work by filling the wall chase with foam around the pipe — because mounting foam actively degrades/breaks down PPSU plastic. (2) **A pipe-fitting connection must never be left under mechanical stress/tension after installation** — stated as a rule that applies to essentially all fitting types (brass or PPSU), not PPSU-specific. `confirmed`, `Mistakes/Warnings`-relevant, source states this is standard across "probably all manufacturers."
- **PPSU vs. brass fitting reliability**: PPSU fittings are stated as not inferior to brass fittings in reliability, when installed correctly (i.e., when the two rules above are followed). `confirmed`, `single-account`.
- **Full PEX connection kit named**: PPSU fittings, brass crimp/press rings, copper installation tubes, pipe-bend fixing clips, and a nipple-with-ring piece for connecting pipe ends to the valves — all same brand (Kan) as the pipe itself, for compatibility. `confirmed`.
- **Comparative installation-difficulty claim**: installing and pressure-testing this cross-linked-polyethylene pipe/fitting system is significantly easier than welding polypropylene pipe — offered as a reason a first-timer (himself) could reasonably consider doing it solo next time, despite hiring plumbers for this first attempt. `single-account`, `unverified` (comparative claim, no controlled comparison shown).

## Quantities / Measurements

- **Radiator bracket load rule and this practitioner's deviation from it, with stated reasoning**: manufacturer instructions for a 12-section radiator specify 2 brackets (each individually rated to hold 70kg), but he used 3 brackets per radiator instead — because with side/axial plumbing connections, a radiator hung on only the top bracket (with the connection at that end) is effectively cantilevered with its far bottom corner unsupported and able to flex/wobble. His radiators after cutting to fit ranged 6-8 sections; every radiator got 3 brackets (one extra at bottom-center) regardless of section count. `confirmed`, technique/heuristic, checkable.
- **Center-to-center radiator spacing confirmed at 500mm** — matches the existing 500mm inter-axial standard already recorded on `Radiators_and_Convectors.md` from an independent source; his bracket spacing was set to match this same 500mm figure. `confirmed`, `corroborates_existing`.
- **Danfoss valve/thermostatic-head system pressure figures**: system operating pressure in his building measured at 4 atm (well under the radiator's own 30 atm working / 45 atm test rating cited above). `confirmed`.

## Mistakes / Warnings

- **Fastener-in-aerated/expanded-clay-concrete-block failure sequence, a genuinely useful trial-and-error progression**: (1) plastic wall dowels/anchors did not hold reliably in expanded-clay-concrete block ("керамзитобетон") — brackets crept/pulled loose and radiators began sagging after initial mounting; (2) tried filling the drilled holes with polyurethane sealant/adhesive before inserting the dowel, expecting it to bond and hold — this also failed to hold (the dowel could be pulled out by hand), costing him roughly half a day of rework; (3) switched to hot-melt/thermal glue ("термоклей") to bed the dowel in the hole (after re-cleaning the hole and slightly countersinking/coning it with a drill bit) — this held securely and installed quickly. He explicitly notes this is now enough separate hot-glue use-cases on this project that "a video of hot-glue life-hack tricks" could be made. `confirmed`, `single-account` result but a specific, checkable technique sequence. **Cross-reference note**: this project's own Round 4 batch separately includes a later video from this same channel titled "7 life hacks with HOT GLUE for your repair" (`zaW8XagX72U`) — worth checking whether that later video actually includes this exact dowel-fixing technique as one of its 7 tips, since this episode essentially predicts/foreshadows it.
- **Template-marking technique for repeatable radiator hole layout**: traced the radiator's own retail cardboard packaging onto a sheet, then precisely marked the mounting-hole positions on that template using a tape measure and laser level once — reused for every radiator instead of re-measuring each one from scratch. He explicitly suggests manufacturers should print such a template directly on the radiator's own packaging for exactly this purpose. `confirmed`, technique/heuristic, low-cost and broadly reusable.
- **Adjustable ("регулируемые") mounting brackets are recommended specifically because drilled anchor-hole position can only be so precise** (he estimates ±a couple millimeters achievable by hand-drilling) — an adjustable bracket's leveling bolt absorbs that imprecision by letting the radiator be raised/lowered/leveled after mounting, rather than requiring perfect hole placement. `confirmed`, technique/heuristic.
- **Chase-cutting (штроба) observation for expanded-clay-concrete block**: cuts and chisels easily with an angle grinder + rotary hammer, main downside is dust — he caught debris in a plastic bin placed underneath. All chases for this radiator run were cut and chiseled out in about 30 minutes. `confirmed`, `single-account`, minor.
