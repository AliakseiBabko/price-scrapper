## ⚠️ Kickback Schemes — Two, and They Are Different

### Scheme 1 — invoice padding (she calls it fraud)

**The mechanism**: a product has an open retail price. The designer asks the supplier to **add their own percentage to the invoice issued to the client** — she puts the markup at **15–20%, and says the size "зависит лишь от наглости дизайнера."** Supplier and designer issue the inflated invoice together, the client pays it, and the designer collects the padding.

**⚠️ Her stated client-side defence, and it is the single most actionable line in the source: never work with a designer on a scheme where you hand them money and they make all the purchases and payments through themselves. Payment should go from the client directly to the shop** — "у каждого своя зона ответственности." She gives the mirror instruction to designers: never route payments through yourself.

She states such a designer **can be sued**, and refers to recent large Russian court cases with significant sums. *(Russian legal context — not routed to `16_Legal_and_Regulations/`, which is Belarus-only.)*

### Scheme 2 — concealed discount split (not fraud, but concealment)

Retail prices are public and designers receive trade discounts. The client sees an invoice **below** retail and feels well served. **But the discount passed on is small — 5%, maybe 10% — while the designer keeps the rest.** The failure mode she describes is discovery: the client asks at another showroom and is offered 15%, or a manager lets slip that "ваш дизайнер забрал 20%, вам оставил 5, а скидка могла быть 25%."

**Her point is that the damage is relational rather than financial**: the client is usually willing to pay the designer properly — "ну почему нельзя было договориться на берегу?" — and what destroys the relationship is the concealment. "От таких тайных махинаций отношения портятся, если вообще не разрываются."

### ⚠️ The questions a client should ask — the practical output

At the **first meetings**, ask the designer:

1. **How will discounts be distributed — who takes how much, and how much reaches me?**
2. **What exactly is the payment scheme for design services and for комплектация (procurement)?** She says a designer must be able to give a very clear answer.
3. **And it must be written into the contract.**

Her preferred arrangement, stated to designers: **charge a proper fee (or a fixed monthly amount) so the incentive doesn't exist at all.**

## ⚠️ Competence Markers for Evaluating a Designer

- **A designer who publicly recommends specific paint article numbers as universally good is displaying incompetence.** Her reasoning: it means they painted **exactly one object** that colour and generalised from it — "чистой воды когнитивное искажение." **She includes her own past self** — "моё рыльце в пушку" — describing carrying a favourite warm white to a second and third object and getting "серовато-зеленоватый болотный" instead. She reports running repeated experiments buying extra sample pots specifically to test this, and says **there has not been a single case where the same article reproduced the result.** Conclusion: **"у дизайнера не может быть любимчиков."**
  - **This is the same position as `b5oeFxmaubI` (Round 1), reframed as a vetting criterion** — and it adds the experiments and the self-implication. Same channel, so a restatement, but a materially more useful framing.
  - Her attached warning about why publishing such advice is harmful: **"люди любят волшебные таблетки"** and someone will buy full tins for a whole flat sight-unseen and waste the money — then blame the designer.
- **⚠️ A designer should decline a project whose budget they cannot work with, rather than render unobtainable furniture and substitute downward during realisation.** Her description of the failure: filling a render with expensive Italian pieces that have no replicas even on Chinese sites, or drawing large-format seamless onyx for a client who cannot afford natural stone; then at realisation each item is swapped for something vaguely similar until the built result no longer resembles the image. **She calls this "плохой тон, если даже не обман."**
  - **Her practical remedy for designers who cannot yet estimate budgets: keep a running spreadsheet of every item used in the visualisation, with prices**, so you know what the render actually costs. She notes it is extra unpaid work early on but is how you become competent.

## Planning Rules — Legitimate Reasons a Result Diverges From the Render

She is careful to separate malpractice from ordinary causes, and these corroborate the Round 2 podcast (`KI2GvB0jzHs`) closely:

- **The client insists on their own fabricator, who cannot execute the specified material** — her example is a joinery shop that doesn't work with ceramic, which is the identical case described in the Round 2 podcast. **She says this is not the designer's fault, and that the designer's job is then to propose good alternatives.**
- **Supply disruption** — a named supplier ceased shipments after 2022 and the specified item has no direct substitute.
- **Currency-linked price rises** pushing an item out of budget between specification and purchase.
