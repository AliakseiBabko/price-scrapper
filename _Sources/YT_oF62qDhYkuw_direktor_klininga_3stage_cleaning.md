## Mistakes / Warnings — Why a Single Post-Renovation Cleaning Pass Fails

- **⚠️ Named as the top misconception among ~90% of clients**: that
  renovation cleaning can be done in one single pass right after work
  finishes. The source frames this as a real quality/cost tradeoff, not
  just an upsell — see the staged breakdown below.
- **Crew tidiness during construction directly drives final cleaning
  cost/difficulty**: the more carefully the renovation crew works
  (properly protecting surfaces, cleaning as they go), the simpler,
  faster, and cheaper the eventual professional cleaning becomes — framed
  as a direct lever the client can influence by vetting/instructing their
  contractor, not just something the cleaning crew absorbs afterward.

## Protective Technique During Construction (upstream of the cleaning stages themselves)

- **Window-frame protective film removal risk**: window frames often ship
  with a factory protective film; if construction work leaves this film
  on too long, UV/weather exposure makes it brittle and hard to peel,
  requiring mechanical scraping that can damage the frame itself. If the
  film is going to stay on through the renovation, cover it with an
  additional taped-down layer (masking tape/малярный скотч) instead of
  leaving the original film exposed and expecting it to peel cleanly
  later.
- **Floor protection during tiling specifically**: builders installing
  tile need the floor covered in multiple layers (cardboard + film, not
  film alone) — mortar/grout that dries directly on an unprotected floor
  requires long, labor-intensive scraping to remove later.
- **⚠️ Grout haze removal timing is critical, especially for epoxy grout**:
  after grouting, the installer must wipe the excess grout film off the
  tile immediately, before it sets. If left to dry — **especially with
  epoxy grout** — the haze "just smears and stays on the tile
  permanently." It can be partially removed afterward but a faint
  permanent residue mark remains regardless of later cleaning effort.
  This is a durable, checkable mechanism (not just "clean promptly"
  general advice) worth cross-referencing wherever this vault already
  covers epoxy grout selection.
- **Facade/exterior work window protection**: when a facade crew works
  from outside, either tape film over the windows or have them rinse
  spilled mortar/mix off the glass immediately — mix that dries on
  textured/patterned glass becomes effectively unremovable later (framed
  as a real, sometimes total, loss of the glass's finish).
- **⚠️ Real cautionary incident — paint solvent stripped a fireplace door's
  factory finish**: a metal fireplace door got paint splatter on it during
  a renovation; the cleaning crew warned the client that paint-removal
  solvent, while it would strip the splattered paint, would not
  distinguish between the contractor's paint and the door's own factory
  finish coating. The client accepted the risk ("let it happen"); the
  solvent removed both, leaving a visible worn patch/near-hole in the
  door's finish. **General lesson stated**: chemical paint removal on a
  factory-finished surface is a real, sometimes irreversible risk, not a
  routine safe operation — worth a client's informed consent before
  attempting, not just cleaning-crew judgment call.

## The Three-(Plus-One-)Stage Cleaning Sequence

1. **Stage 1 — after rough works, before finish works** (before
   wallpapering/flooring starts): full de-dusting of walls, floor, **and
   ceiling** — explicitly called out as commonly skipped. **Mechanism for
   why ceiling dust matters even under a stretch/suspended ceiling**: dust
   left above a stretch ceiling before it's installed doesn't disappear —
   it isn't sealed in airtight, and gradually escapes through gaps/vents
   over time, becoming a delayed, hard-to-trace dust source that keeps
   "reappearing" in an already-finished apartment months later. Tile
   already installed at this stage (e.g. bathroom) also gets washed of
   mortar/grout residue here.
2. **Stage 2 — after finish works, before furniture/fixture installation**
   (before cabinets, bathtub, shower cabin, kitchen units go in): a second
   full de-dusting pass, because dust settled on walls/tile during finish
   work would otherwise get trapped behind/under fixtures once they're
   mounted. **Concrete mechanism**: a shower cabin installed onto unwashed,
   dusty tile leaves that dust permanently sealed behind the cabin
   afterward — "only a finger fits" in the gap once installed, making it
   physically impossible to clean retroactively.
3. **Stage 3 — after furniture/fixtures are fully installed**, move-in
   ready: final full de-dusting and floor wash, the point at which the
   apartment is genuinely ready to move into.
4. **Informal fourth stage — post-move-in "light" clean** (not classified
   as renovation cleaning by the source): after furniture and belongings
   are moved in and movers have walked through, a light wet-mop/floor pass
   to remove move-in-day dirt — explicitly distinguished from construction
   dust, which should already be gone by this point if stages 1-3 were
   done properly.

## Practical Guidance — When One Combined Pass Is (and Isn't) Defensible

- **Budget/lower-cost renovation**: combining into a single cleaning pass
  (typically done at stage 2 or stage 3 only) is framed as a reasonable,
  defensible cost-saving choice.
- **Higher-end renovation/apartment**: attempting to save money by doing
  only one combined cleaning pass is explicitly called "not justified" —
  the full three-stage sequence is presented as necessary to reach a truly
  dust-free ("stopudovo," i.e. "100%, guaranteed") result free of
  construction dust/residue, which the source frames as proportionally
  more important to protect on a higher-value finish.
