## Doors / Trim — the two theses

- **⚠️ Thesis one, and it is a budget rule: on a small budget, choose neutral doors *or* make an art object of them — nothing in between.** A half-decorated door is the failure case.
- **⚠️ Thesis two: a door never plays the leading role.** There are techniques for decorating and complicating a door, **but do not make it the most beautiful object in the interior.** Her governing conditional: **"Если вы усложняете дверь — усложняйте и сам интерьер."**
- **⚠️ The reason a door cannot be the dominant, stated physically rather than aesthetically: the door is behind you once you have entered.** "Довольно странно было бы сидеть на диване и любоваться дверью." The dominant is normally a fireplace, a picture, or the view.
- **Her two analogies for the correct role**, both worth keeping because they set the ceiling on ambition:
  - **The background landscape in a Renaissance painting** — she cites Leonardo's backgrounds as fantastic and worth examining, **but you look at the figures first.** It adds quality and mood, sometimes carrying the subject's mood, **but is never the protagonist.**
  - **Theatre scenery** — it can be beautiful and complex and it creates the world the actors inhabit, **but it does not replace the actors.**

## Doors / Trim — why a complicated door commits you elsewhere

- **⚠️ A decorated door forces matching commitments across the whole flat, and this is the strongest practical argument in the source.** A door with veneer set on the diagonal plus routed slots requires **either parquet laid diagonally or wall battens supporting the slots** — it cannot simply be dropped into any interior.
- **⚠️ Her worst example: a door with hexagons — "самый трэш," and she found it easily on suppliers' sites.** You would have to **repeat hexagons in every room** — cushions, hexagonal tile, curtains — **or accept different doors on each side of the opening.** She notes the panels are also colour-picked out, which does not rescue them.
- **A cheaper but more complex door can be supported by furniture instead of by the whole scheme.** Her own project: a door divided into **unequal vertical strips** placed beside a bookcase divided into **unequal vertical sections**, with a decorative insert finished like the door. "Если заморочиться, поддержать сложную дверь можно."
- **A door can be a genuine art object only where the rest of the interior is equally bold** — her example is Dorothy Draper, with contrasting leaves and large door decor **inside an equally contrasting interior with confident colour combinations.**

## Doors / Trim — composition

- **⚠️ Doors and windows are part of the architecture, like mouldings.** So the proportions that matter are **between the doors and the room, and between doors, their casings, mouldings and windows.** Her example pairs large contrasting doors with large contrasting ceiling mouldings that support them.
- **Cased openings without leaves belong in the same category** and follow the same rules.
- **Dark doors work in light interiors provided there is another contrast present** — black-and-white patterned wallpaper, or a strongly contrasting geometric floor. **The door itself can carry the contrast internally: a black leaf with white architraves.**
- **⚠️ In symmetrical door arrangements, often only one of the pair is a real opening — the other is a false door**, with a differently finished wall behind it in the adjoining room. Placed purely for symmetry.
- **Repeat one motif across the door, the casegoods and other decorative elements.** Her restaurant example carries a diamond motif on a mirror, on glazed doors, and on cabinet fronts — **all read as "doors" though only some are.**
- **⚠️ Consider how a door relates to the *casegoods*, not only to walls and floors** — wardrobe sliding doors can be deliberately styled as an additional pair of doors.
- **Bright doors can mark the transition between rooms and simultaneously give the project coherence** — emerald doors relating to an emerald rug in one room and to turquoise chairs in the adjoining one. **Bi-fold ("книжка") doors are the mechanism choice where an open leaf would otherwise obstruct the perception of a room's shape** — her example is a round living room, where conventional leaves would stick out awkwardly when held open.
- **Kit Kemp example she rates highly**: the same fabric on the doors, on the living-room curtains and on the adjoining bedroom's walls, **with the doors opening 180° so one room opens toward the other.** Closed, from the bedroom side the doors simply read as wall.

## Doors / Trim — colour technique, and the edge question

- **⚠️ A two-colour door raises a question people get wrong: what colour is the edge? Her rule is procedural and easy to apply — stand in the room and pull the door toward you. The colour that then appears in your room is the colour that was on the *outside* face, and that is the colour to paint the edge.** Her worked example is a bathroom door, white on one side and pink on the other in the colour of the adjoining room, **where the edge should be pink.**
- **⚠️ Colour can appear in unexpected places: the extension jambs («доборы») and the architrave edges can be painted a contrasting colour unrelated to the leaf.** Her own project used warm grey-brown doors with **black доборы and black architrave edges, transitioning into a black skirting** — which "даёт интерьеру такую тонкую графику" **without introducing too much black.**
- **⚠️ A door can be treated as part of the wall rather than as its own element** — wall patterns continuing straight onto the leaf. **Especially effective in small rooms, and specifically useful where a door is badly placed**: painted the wall colour it "практически исчезает."
- **A subtler two-tone option**: panel surrounds picked out in white or a light tone while the leaf is another colour, with the white echoed by the architraves and by other elements — **"с одной стороны неброская, с другой стороны интересно разглядывать."**

## Doors / Trim — do they all have to match?

- **⚠️ No — and she gives her own hallway as the worked case: three different doors in one space.** A glazed entrance door, sliding doors, and a standard hinged door used elsewhere in the flat — **with three different architrave treatments: none on the entrance door, black on the sliding doors, grey on the interior door.** Decided deliberately by comparing viewpoints one at a time.
- **⚠️ But two constraints bound that freedom:**
  - **Doors relate to the building's exterior too.** In a house, the entrance door must suit the facade and the interior doors must suit the entrance door. **"Вопрос не в том, сколько у вас видов дверей, а как хорошо они связаны друг с другом."**
  - **⚠️ Hierarchy must be logical.** Large sliding glass doors to a bedroom while the living room gets a small ordinary interior door **is illogical — and she rates it a worse error than using several architrave types in one room.**
