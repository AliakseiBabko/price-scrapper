## The thesis, stated in his own words in 2021

- **The reframing that the whole channel later builds on: the enemy is not dirt, it is the CLEANING.** Material does not mostly fail from use — it fails from being repeatedly cleaned, so the design objective is to reduce the *number of cleaning events*, not to buy cleanable surfaces.
- **⚠️ THE CRITERION HE PROPOSES, WHICH IS THE USEFUL PART: choose materials by how VISIBLE dirt is on them, not by how clean they are.** A surface that shows every mark gets wiped constantly and wears out; a surface that hides marks is cleaned on a real schedule.
- **⚠️ AND THE ANALOGY HE USES, which is a good teaching device: car colour.** Everyone already knows that a black car and a white car both get equally dirty but *look* dirty at completely different rates — and that this, not the actual dirt, governs how often you wash it. He transfers that straight onto interior surfaces.
- **Gloss is the amplifier: minimise it.** Gloss shows dust, streaks, fingerprints and every touch; matte hides all four. This is the general rule; ⚠️ he qualifies it later for ultra-matte (below) and **reverses part of it in 2022** (see the reversal section).
- **⚠️ Pets are named as a contraindication to a low-maintenance interior** — stated plainly rather than hedged, and worth recording because he himself later lives with a cat (the 2023 kitchen source has a cat feeding zone), so this is a position he softened in practice.
- **⚠️ THE HONEST CAP, which is why this source is not a fanatic's list — «НЕ ВПАДАЙТЕ В КРАЙНОСТИ».** Every recommendation here is a trade against appearance, and he says so.
- **⚠️ AND THE ONE-LINE STRUCTURAL POINT THAT UNDERCUTS THE ENTIRE VIDEO, WHICH HE MAKES ANYWAY: «ЧЕМ МЕНЬШЕ КВАРТИРА, ТЕМ МЕНЬШЕ В НЕЙ ПРИДЁТСЯ УБИРАТЬСЯ.»** → **Floor area is the dominant variable in cleaning load and no material choice competes with it.** For a Minsk flat where area is fixed, that is a reason to treat the rest of this list as second-order optimisation.

## Ventilation as a durability decision — the 2021 original of the Round 4 "synthesis"

- **MVHR / mechanical supply ventilation is presented here as THE answer to dust**, on the ground that dust mostly arrives through open windows.
- **⚠️ AND THE MECHANISM IS STATED IN THE SAME BREATH: with mechanical ventilation you NEVER OPEN THE WINDOWS, and not opening the windows is what removes the dust source.** That is the full chain — ventilation → windows stay shut → less dust → fewer cleaning events → less wear — as one thought, in January 2021.
- ⚠️ **Route with the correction: the vault's `Breathers_vs_Mechanical_Ventilation` and `Fresh_Air_Ventilation_and_Ducting` pages hold this as a Round 4 finding and should carry the 2021 attribution instead.**

## Floors, edges and the dirt-trap inventory

- **Textured and relief floor finishes are named as maintenance liabilities** — texture holds dirt and resists wiping. (The vault dates *texture complicates maintenance* to a later source; ⚠️ it is here.)
- **⚠️⚠️ BEVELLED EDGES (ФАСКИ) ON LAMINATE AND ENGINEERED BOARD ARE A PERMANENT DIRT TRAP, AND THIS IS THE SHARPEST NEW ITEM IN THE SOURCE.** The chamfer at every board edge is a groove that collects dirt, cannot be reached by a mop, and is *built into the product* — so it is a purchasing decision, not a maintenance one. **A bevelled board multiplies the trap by the number of boards.**
  - → **⚠️ This is a checkable specification question to ask of any laminate or engineered board: is the edge square or chamfered?** The vault's `Flooring_Material_Selection` page has no bevel content at all.
- **Rugs and ковролин are argued against**, both as dust reservoirs.
  - ⚠️ **He supports this with a HEARSAY incident — an office where carpet was blamed for an allergic reaction that took three people out by ambulance. Recorded here as reported and FLAGGED UNVERIFIED: no company, no date, no diagnosis, and a mechanism (carpet → three simultaneous ambulance cases) that is not plausible on its face.** It should not be routed as a health finding; it is an example of him reaching for a dramatic anecdote, which is worth noting about the source itself.
- **The dust-trap inventory, as a list of details to avoid or detail differently:**
  - **Mouldings, cornices and applied decorative profiles** — every horizontal ledge is a dust shelf.
  - **⚠️ THE ЗАКАРНИЗНЫЙ СВЕС (the curtain-pelmet / cove overhang) IS SINGLED OUT, WITH A REMEDY: turn the cove so it faces DOWNWARD rather than presenting an upward-facing trough.** An upward-facing cove is an unreachable dust tray at ceiling height; a downward-facing one lights the same way and collects nothing.
  - **Skirting profile: the more relief, the more it holds.**
  - **⚠️ CONCEALED / SHADOW-GAP SKIRTING IS RECOMMENDED SPECIFICALLY ON CLEANING GROUNDS** — not, as it is usually argued, on aesthetic ones. → **A second, independent reason for a detail the vault currently holds as a style choice.** (⚠️ And in 2023 he continues the same shadow line into furniture — see the carcass-production source, where an inverted grip profile is aligned with the wall's shadow skirting.)

## Paint, plaster and the ultra-matte tension

- **Dark colours plus matte finish is the low-maintenance combination**, on the visibility criterion.
- **⚠️⚠️ LACQUERED DECORATIVE PLASTER IS CLAIMED USABLE IN A SHOWER ZONE AND AS A KITCHEN SPLASHBACK.** This is **bolder than anything the vault currently holds** — the vault's wet-zone content treats plaster as a dry-zone material — and ⚠️ **he narrows this claim himself in later sources.** Record it as his 2021 position with the later narrowing noted, and do **not** promote it to a recommendation.
- **⚠️ ULTRA-MATTE PAINT IS A CEILING PRODUCT, NOT A WALL PRODUCT** — it marks and cannot be washed, so it belongs where nothing touches it. **And he acknowledges the internal tension explicitly:** the whole video argues for matte, and here the most matte product available is ruled out for walls. → **The resolution is that "matte" is about *light behaviour* and "washable" is about *binder content*, and ultra-matte trades the second away for the first.** ⚠️ That resolution is **mine, not his** — he states the tension and leaves it.

## Fittings, radiators and appliances

- **⚠️ THREE-GENERATION RADIATOR HISTORY, ending in a maintenance recommendation: cast iron → panel/convector types → TUBULAR STEEL, which he recommends as ANTISTATIC and genuinely wipeable.** The argument against convector-type radiators is that the fins are an enclosed dust volume that cannot be cleaned at all.
  - → **⚠️ A radiator selection criterion the vault does not have: cleanability and static attraction, alongside the usual output and appearance.**
- **Chandelier and pendant shades that face UPWARD are dust bowls** — a shade open to the ceiling collects everything and is visible through the diffuser when it does. Downward-facing or enclosed shades instead.
- **⚠️ METAL DIFFUSERS AND SHADES, NOT PLASTIC** — plastic attracts and holds dust electrostatically; metal does not to the same degree.
- **⚠️ MATTE APPLIANCE FINISHES** over gloss and polished stainless, on the fingerprint criterion.
- **⚠️⚠️ THE WHITE HOB IS BANNED OUTRIGHT, FROM EXPERIENCE: a white hob looks unacceptable after TWO TO THREE MONTHS.** A specific, time-bounded verdict on a specific product — and see the reversal section, because it does not sit with what he says in 2022.
- **Gloss furniture fronts are criticised, with a client case: an ANTIQUED (patinated) MIRROR that PROFESSIONAL CLEANERS COULD NOT FULLY CLEAN.** → A finish whose defect is that its texture is *designed* to look dirty, so real dirt in it is unremovable.

## Storage, and the "close it" principle in its 2021 form

- **Closed storage over open, and GLAZED bookcases over open shelving** — books are named as a major dust collector.
- **⚠️ OPEN WARDROBE SYSTEMS ARE ACCEPTED, BUT ONLY BEHIND GLASS.** The look is preserved, the dust is not.
- **⚠️ KITCHEN BUILT TO THE CEILING, with a childhood memory as the argument: the greasy pans stored on top of the wall units.** Any gap above a kitchen unit becomes a horizontal grease-and-dust surface at head height. → Corroborates the vault's build-to-ceiling content with a maintenance reason rather than a storage one.
- **⚠️⚠️ FURNITURE ON LEGS, WITH AN EXPLICIT RANKING: WALL-HUNG > HIGH LEGS > LOW LEGS > PLINTH (цоколь).** The criterion is whether a vacuum or a mop head can pass underneath.
  - **⚠️ AND THE DETAIL THAT MAKES THE RANKING USABLE: it is not just clearance height but how far in from the edge the leg sits — a leg placed CLOSER TO THE CENTRE leaves the perimeter clear and is much easier to clean around than a leg at the corner.**
  - → **This is the origin of the vault's beds-and-furniture-on-legs content, and the leg-position refinement is new to the vault entirely.**

## ⚠️ Wet rooms — where the 2021 rules are the ones his own builders later broke

- **The wall-hung WC with a concealed cistern is recommended for the cleaning dividend** (open floor under the pan). The vault dates this to a later source; ⚠️ it is here.
- **⚠️ AND THE CONCEALED-CISTERN OBJECTION IS DEBUNKED IN 2021, with an appeal to trade experience: «СПРОСИТЕ У САНТЕХНИКОВ».** The fear that a built-in cistern is a maintenance trap is, on his account, not held by the people who actually service them.
- **Wall-hang the WC accessories too** (brush, holder) — anything standing on the floor is an obstacle to cleaning it.
- **⚠️ THE FREE-STANDING BATH IS REFUSED, ON ACCESS GROUNDS** — you have to clean all the way round it and behind it, in a space that is by definition too tight. (Vault currently dates this later.)
- **⚠️⚠️ TILE OVER THE BATH RIM WITH A MINIMAL GAP, AND SILICONE THE JOINT. THIS IS THE ONE TO ROUTE HARDEST.**
  - He publishes this rule in **January 2021**.
  - **⚠️ In his OWN flat, completed 2023–24, the builders used EPOXY GROUT at exactly this joint, and it LEAKED** (Round 5, `_nDCLhRUojE`).
  - → **⚠️⚠️ So the Round 5 leak is not a knowledge gap — it is a SUPERVISION FAILURE ON HIS OWN PUBLISHED RULE, three years after he published it.** That strengthens the Round 5 finding considerably and it is the single most instructive cross-source item in this round: **knowing the detail does not get it built, and the person most likely to catch it was the client.**
- **⚠️ UNDERMOUNTED BASINS over vessel/countertop basins** — a vessel basin has an exposed junction all the way round at splash height. (Vault dates this later; it is here. ⚠️ And note the 2023 kitchen source repeats the *undermounted* preference for the sink, so this is consistent across three sources.)
- **Concealed (in-wall) mixers reduce cleaning** — no base to collect limescale and no gap behind the spout.
- **⚠️ A HYGIENIC SHOWER PLUS A FLOOR DRAIN TURNS A BATHROOM INTO SOMETHING YOU CAN HOSE DOWN — with an honest feasibility caveat he supplies himself: it needs floor build-up for the drain fall, which is USUALLY IMPOSSIBLE IN A FLAT.** → Recorded as a house method with a stated flat-scale blocker, which is exactly how the vault should hold it. (⚠️ Round 5 has the same drain-fall constraint arising for a podium; same physical limit, different feature.)

## ⚠️ Central vacuum — explained in full, and then honestly scoped out

- **The build-up: 5–6 cm pipework buried in the structure, inlet sockets in the rooms, and the motor unit sited in a utility room or plant space** so the noise and the exhaust leave the living area.
- The benefits follow from that: no dust re-emission into the room, no machine to carry, no noise where you are cleaning.
- **⚠️⚠️ AND THEN HE SCOPES IT OUT HIMSELF, WHICH IS WHY THIS IS WORTH KEEPING: houses yes, small flats NO — «используйте РОБОТ».** Nowhere for the motor, no depth for the pipe runs, and the distances do not justify it.
  - → **Route as: understood, considered, and REJECTED for a flat by the practitioner who explained it — with a robot vacuum named as the flat-scale substitute.** That is a closed decision, not an open option, and recording it that way saves it being reopened.

## ⚠️⚠️ The 2021→2022 reversal on dark surfaces — record as a reversal, do not smooth it

- **2021 (this source): DARK hides dirt, WHITE shows everything. The white hob is banned after two to three months. Dark + matte is the recommended combination.**
- **2022 (`CN-Ab_g4CAI`, Round 5): DARK FACADES ARE THE MOST MARKING, regardless of gloss level.**
- **⚠️ These are not reconcilable as stated.** The most likely reconciliation is that the two claims are about **different soils on different surfaces** — *food and limescale* on a horizontal cooking surface (light shows them) versus *fingerprints, dust and skin oils* on a vertical touched surface (dark shows them) — so the real rule is **match the surface tone to the soil it will actually receive, per surface, rather than picking one tone for the whole flat.**
- **⚠️ THAT RECONCILIATION IS MINE, NOT HIS, AND MUST BE LABELLED AS SUCH ON THE WIKI PAGE.** He never addresses the contradiction; there is no source in which he acknowledges changing position. Record both claims with their dates and the reconciliation flagged as inference.
