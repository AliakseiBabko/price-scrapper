## Materials — the three substrates (основы), and what actually differs

- **ЛДСП / ДСП** — chipboard: wood shavings pressed and bonded with glue. **He corrects the common name: people say "ламинат"; the correct term is ЛДСП (ламинированная древесно-стружечная плита).** **Thickness 16 mm, European 18 mm.**
- **МДФ** — fine-dispersion fraction, timber ground to dust and bonded. **⚠️ Its one decisive advantage: it can be milled/routed, so a facade can take any shape.** **Thicknesses 16, 17, 19, 23 mm.**
- **⚠️ Массив (solid wood) — and his description punctures the word.** It is not a plank cut from a tree: it is a **мебельный щит** — small lamellae, **micro-tenon (микрошип) joints at their ends**, glued to each other and then calibrated and sanded. **"Она у тебя вот таких из маленьких склеенных палочек."** He calls «массив» "вообще сакральное слово у нас в мебельном мире."
- **⚠️ Which substrate is better is not answerable in the abstract, and he says so: once a facade is edge-banded and faced, "ты нигде в жизни не определишь, что там внутри — МДФ или ДСП, пока ты его не распилишь."** Both are good if the material is good quality. **The real difference is millability and thickness options.**

### ⚠️ His case against массив — a manufacturer arguing down the material buyers revere

Five distinct arguments, and this is the strongest anti-steering passage in the batch:

1. **"Массив — это не есть хорошо на самом деле."**
2. **Finding one made to proper joinery rules is hard — "мало кто делает это."**
3. **⚠️ Cost is species-driven, not "solid-wood"-driven, and he uses a retail comparison to make it concrete**: a solid-pine child's bed at a chain retailer for ~20,000 ₽ versus a solid-oak or teak kitchen at millions. **Pine is cheap, easy to work, can be painted without even sanding. Oak must be dried and is hard to work.** So "из массива" tells you almost nothing about cost or quality.
4. **⚠️ Cost against the alternative, stated as a ratio: solid wood starts around 3× the price of a veneered facade — "и видишь ты одну и ту же картинку."** Because you still apply a finish on top of it.
5. **⚠️ Stability, which is the technical argument: MDF with veneer on it is stable — it does not warp or crack. Improperly dried solid wood has a far higher probability of cracking.** Making solid wood properly costs more because there are many more production operations *and* the raw material is dearer.
6. **⚠️ The ecological claim is undercut too: glue is used in the micro-tenon joints and in veneering regardless — "он везде есть."**

### Formaldehyde emission

- **⚠️ Any laminate/ЛДСП may not legally be sold in the country below class E1**, and the good laminate they work with is E1 by default. Risk sits with uncertified material bought from unclear sources — "пилишь, от него запах идёт."
- **⚠️ His practical point: you never have bare chipboard in a home — it is edge-banded around the whole perimeter**, so the difference is not something you will perceive. "У меня тут кухня из массива, я там дома дышу лучше" is offered sarcastically.

## Materials — the facings, in ascending price order, each with its failure mode

1. **Melamine paper** — the cheapest. **Tell: hold it to the light and the shavings are visible through the paper.**
2. **⚠️ PVC film (плёнка) — and they refuse to work with it, with three stated reasons.** On MDF or chipboard; any colour, wood texture or plain; can be dear or cheap.
   - **Large roll-over at the edges («завалы»), which makes the gaps between facades look visually bigger.**
   - **The reverse face is a different colour** (e.g. white) — "это некрасиво."
   - **Poor film shows шагрень (orange-peel), and he reports pronounced pimpling on the edges even of expensive Italian film kitchens they imported.**
   - **⚠️ The paradox he draws out, and it is the most quotable item in the source: top Italian makers use film and so does Leroy Merlin — "в Леруа тебе об этом скажут, а дорогая итальянская фабрика тебе об этом не скажет, у них принцип продажи другой: посмотрите, какой дизайн классный."** **Most mass-market furniture facades are film, because it is fast and cheap.**
3. **Plastics (пластик)** — many producers, colours and textures; thermo-structured/hard-wearing ones named **TCC-плита, Cleaf**; available ribbed/relief, plain, with or without orange-peel, and super-matte. Tactilely varied — "плёнка как бумага," plastics are different.
   - **⚠️ A dear plastic costs about the same as veneer — and his verdict is to take the veneer: "если выбирать между ТСС-плитой и шпоном, я однозначно за шпон, потому что платишь ты столько же, но получаешь натуральный материал."**
   - **They do use plastic where it makes sense — Egger (ЛДСП with plastic on it), relatively inexpensive against Cleaf and Candela but good.**
   - **⚠️ A real, non-obvious risk with plastics: a decor can be discontinued.** Any facade can be damaged mechanically — children, a dropped pan — **and if the decor is out of production, replacement becomes your problem.** **This is one of his stated reasons for preferring enamel.**
4. **⚠️ Enamel (эмаль)** — his preferred route, with four advantages: **any colour ("2,000 in one RAL fan"); any milling; no edge banding, because the facade is painted in the mass; and it is restorable in most cases and interchangeable** — a damaged facade can be replaced.
5. **Complex/premium composite facades** — his showroom's example: **an aluminium frame with 5 mm quartz agglomerate bonded on, thin painted MDF as the reverse.** Expensive, looks and feels good on both faces. **He frames it as a question of legitimacy of spend, not necessity — "для тех людей, кто от этого кайфует, важна эстетика, важно тактильное ощущение, позволяет бюджет — почему нет."**
6. **Glass** — bonded to MDF, to chipboard, or to an aluminium frame. **⚠️ The reason for the aluminium frame is weight: a large glass-on-MDF facade becomes unliftable, whereas the hollow frame is light and practically does not warp.** Dearer than chipboard.

## Materials — ⚠️ the colour-matching rule, and it is an ordering rule not a taste one

**The single most operationally useful item in the source.**

- **⚠️ Matching a specific RAL between painted glass and a painted wall is very hard** — take the same RAL, paint the glass, and it will differ.
- **⚠️ Enamel between batches: "заказал партию краски, всё покрасил — окей. Купил через 2 дня такую же эмаль, взял такой же МДФ со склада, покрасил — тон уже отличается."** He calls it "прямо большая боль для многих," **worst in white.**
- **⚠️ Therefore: insist that everything is made in one go — "мы всегда настаиваем на том, что всё должно делаться единовременно."** He acknowledges the financial reality that people order in stages, and states the trade-off plainly: **if the budget allows, do not split it.**
- **This independently corroborates the same rule for natural veneer** from this channel's fine-line video ([[_Sources/YT_pEwESJATTaA_flat_fineline_veneer_deception|pEwESJATTaA]]) — **so the "order in one batch" rule holds for both painted and natural finishes, for different physical reasons.**

## Materials — veneer (шпон), technically described

- **How it is made**: the log is mounted in a machine, rotated against knives, and **unwound into thin strips — "миллиметр плюс-минус полтора," typically 1.2 mm.** **Thicker 2–3 mm veneer («палуба») exists and is significantly dearer.**
- **Species determines the veneer**: oak, rosewood, mahogany, ebony. **Oak veneer is the most widespread in furniture — predictable, attractive, and it tints well.**
- **⚠️ The base distinction from every imitation, and it is checkable: an ЛДСП sheet is 2800 × 2070 mm and every sheet carries an identical print** — "они на каждом разные делать не будут. Ты привёз себе шпон — навряд ли такой именно рисунок будет у кого-то." **Uniqueness of figure is the property you are buying.** (Complements the large-area uniformity tells in `pEwESJATTaA`.)
