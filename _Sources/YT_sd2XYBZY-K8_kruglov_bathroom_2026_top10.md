## Fixtures — Genuinely New Content

- **Wall-hung toilet installation-frame load ratings, not yet recorded on this page**: base/standard installation frames comfortably handle 150-200kg, with some rated as high as 250kg; reinforced frames go up to 400kg. **The critical installation requirement, per the source, isn't the frame's own rated capacity but its footing**: the frame's support feet/legs must rest on a rigid, solid base — specifically the concrete floor slab — never on top of self-leveling screed and never on tile, both of which the source implies aren't rigid enough to be a legitimate structural bearing surface for the frame's own feet. Also notes the flush mechanism is fully serviceable/replaceable through the access panel behind the flush button, without opening the wall or the boxing — framed as a reason not to worry about future repairability despite the concealed installation. `single-account`, `unverified`.
- **Tankless (проточный) water heater — pro/con detail beyond the store's existing placement-flexibility note**: three stated advantages over a storage-tank unit — (1) much smaller footprint, avoiding the need for a large tank inside the plumbing box/santekhkorob; (2) effectively unlimited hot water, relevant specifically for a household of more than two people or anyone who showers for a long time — no risk of someone running out of hot water mid-shower; (3) while instantaneous draw is higher than a tank unit's, there is zero standby/idle electricity consumption while the household is at work or asleep, unlike a storage tank which keeps a reservoir hot continuously. `single-account`, `unverified`.
- **Valve-controlled (вентильное управление) hygienic shower — a distinct sub-type not yet distinguished on this page**: the source explicitly separates this from a "standard" hygienic shower and states the mechanism reason it's safer: with a standard unit, the water supply is controlled by the main mixer, and it's very common for a user not to bother closing the mixer before re-hanging the spray head, leading to floods. A valve-controlled unit physically can't have its spray head returned to its holster/dock until the user closes the valve — removing the human-forgetfulness failure mode entirely rather than just warning against it. Extends (doesn't contradict) this store's existing "always pair a hygienic shower with a check valve + leak-protection system" guidance — this is a different, complementary mitigation (device-level rather than system-level). `single-account`, `unverified`.
