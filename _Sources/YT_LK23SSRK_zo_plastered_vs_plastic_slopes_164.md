## Windows / Slopes — Plastic vs. Plastered: Refined Decision Rule

- **Core framing reversal, stated explicitly by the source**: "plastic slopes are the premium-class marker, plastered is the economy-class marker" — inverted from common assumption. Reasoning matches this store's existing recorded mechanism (`yt_AGjYrwqilNA`): even a top-tier paint job on plastered slopes will yellow over time, and while frame-edge cracking and plane cracking can each individually be avoided with care, paint yellowing on plastered slopes cannot be avoided at all. Independent-source corroboration of this store's existing "plastered slopes always yellow" claim.
- **Premium-look plastic slope technique, genuinely new for this store**: to make a plastic slope visually read as plastered/premium rather than the cheaper-looking standard plastic install, omit connector profiles and corner trim entirely — just fit the sandwich panel directly, using a guide strip only to true the panel relative to the frame, then trim the panel's own edge cleanly and finish by skimming/puttying it so wallpaper can be applied right up to the corner (no visible plastic trim strip at all). Panel thickness in the worked example: 8mm. `confirmed`, distinct from this store's existing snap-together-trim installation method (`yt_nb3L-k69yx8`) — this is an alternative *finish style* for the same underlying sandwich-panel material, not a competing installation method.
- **Caution attached to the trim-free finish**: regardless of whether a slope is plastic or plastered, a hard door impact against the wall can cause wallpaper to delaminate near the slope edge — the trim-free premium finish does not eliminate this risk, it's a cosmetic choice, not a durability upgrade.
- **Bay/oriel window (эркерное окно) exception — plastered slopes are the explicitly correct choice here, not a compromise**: on a non-rectangular (angled/broken-plane) window opening, a plastic slope's snap-together trim system produces large, clearly visible seams at each angled joint — tolerable on a sill (occupants are already used to seeing a sill's connector profile there, and it yellows more slowly at that location) but conspicuous and worsening-with-yellowing if used on the vertical/head slopes of the same opening. On this geometry, the source states the usual crack/yellowing downsides of plastered slopes are explicitly the lesser problem compared to visible, yellowing trim seams — reverse the material recommendation specifically for this window shape.
- **Worked confirmation on the source's own project**: three of four rooms on this object use plastic slopes per the general recommendation above; the one room shown behind the speaker, confirmed on-camera by exposed drywall cross-section, uses genuine plastered slopes specifically because that window is bay/oriel-shaped.
