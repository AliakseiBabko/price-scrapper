## Walls / Ceilings — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Laser-projector layout method for a new partition wall**: mark the room's floor corners, place a self-leveling laser unit at each marked point, and the device projects the room's exact footprint onto the ceiling and vertical reference lines onto the existing walls simultaneously — source states this full layout step takes about 3 minutes, versus a traditional plumb-bob/tape approach. `confirmed`, single-account technique/tool description.
- **Temporary metal-profile guide frame set along the chalk-line/laser marks before laying block**, so the mason lays foam blocks directly against the guide profiles without needing to re-check each course with a level/tape — only the mid-span needs checking against a stretched string line. Source states the guide-frame setup itself took ~12 minutes and estimates it saves roughly 3 hours versus laying without it. `confirmed` (technique), the specific time-saving multiplier is `single-account`/`unverified`.
- **Surface prep before laying block: vacuum the base surface (not sweep), then apply a deep-penetration bonding primer with a garden pump sprayer** — source explicitly warns that laying block onto a dusty/unprimed surface will cause the new wall to detach later ("гарантированно отстоятся"); demonstrated to take ~1.5 minutes vacuuming + ~2 minutes priming. `confirmed`, a specific and checkable step-skip failure mode.
- **Adhesive-mortar consistency rule**: mixed in a 10-liter bucket, thick enough that a mixer paddle doesn't sink/fall under its own weight when let go, but fluid enough that excess mortar sheds cleanly off the paddle — then let it slake ~5 minutes and remix once before use. `confirmed`, concrete workable-consistency test.
- **Block-laying technique**: spread adhesive with a notched trowel in enough quantity that it visibly squeezes out both sides once the block is seated; place the block on the prepped base and press/slide it down to firm contact (not drop it), then push it with one decisive motion against the previous block in the row — stated to produce a reliably strong, non-shrinking joint. `confirmed`.
- **Running-bond coursing rule**: every course must be offset from the one below (each even-numbered course starts with a half-block) — standard masonry-bond principle, restated specifically for foam block. `confirmed`.
- **Top joint (wall-to-ceiling) treatment — a named common failure and its fix**: packing the gap between the last course and the ceiling with adhesive mortar via trowel is described as the top-joint method that "stresses most masons" and that "ordinary builders" instead badly seal with foam-backer-rod plus smeared plaster — both approaches leave the joint prone to eventual separation and poor sound isolation. **The source's own recommended method**: apply professional expanding **polyurethane foam** (not adhesive) along the full top perimeter, one continuous bead on each face of the block (two beads total forming a closed loop), applied **only after the masonry has cured 48 hours** (foam applied to fresh, still-curing masonry will expand and deform it). A single unbroken bead around the entire top perimeter is stated as important — even a small gap in the foam bead is described as significantly worse for the wall's sound isolation. Result claimed: better soundproofing and stronger adhesion than mortar-packed joints, done in ~4 minutes. `confirmed`, specific mechanism and explicit before/after failure-mode contrast — genuinely new technique detail for this store (foam vs. mortar for the top masonry-to-ceiling joint, with the 48-hour cure-before-foaming timing rule).

## Doors / Trim — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Door-opening layout dimensions for a 70 cm-wide door in a 10 cm-thick foam-block wall**: mark the opening height at 2.07 m; mark the opening's inner edge 21 cm from the outer wall corner (leaving an 11 cm-thick return/reveal of block on the inside), then measure 78 cm further across for the clear rough-opening width. The stated reasoning for the 11 cm return: it lets the door-frame casing (наличник) install without needing to be cut/notched, while keeping at least 3 cm of clearance between the casing and the corner for a clean, proportionate look. The 78 cm clear width is stated as the amount needed to comfortably install a 70 cm-wide door leaf. `confirmed`, concrete and checkable dimensional rule — a specific worked framing-to-door-width relationship not previously recorded this precisely in this store.
- **Reinforcing lintel technique over a foam-block door/window opening**: cut a rebated slot into the top course of block on both sides of the opening, insert two rebar rods sized to extend ~10 cm past each side of the opening into the slots, spread the rods slightly off-center, and fix them in place with mounting/shim wedges — the rods are later permanently locked in during the plastering pass, when the mason fills the remaining gaps with fast-setting compound. `confirmed`, concrete technique for both door and window openings alike (same method reused for the window opening in this video).
- **Opening cut-out technique**: rather than measuring and cutting the door/window opening perimeter in block ahead of time, lay full block across the opening area during construction, then cut the opening out afterward in one pass with a reciprocating saw, guided by a laser plane/level projected vertically onto the marked cut line. `confirmed`, sequencing/technique detail.
