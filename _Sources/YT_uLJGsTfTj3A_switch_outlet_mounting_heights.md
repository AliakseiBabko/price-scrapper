## Durable Facts

- **Switch height is deliberately height-independent** (the video's central, counterintuitive claim): despite intuition, an adult's hand-drop height (distance from floor to relaxed hanging hand) is claimed to be roughly constant across a wide range of adult heights (demonstrated with people from 150 cm to 185 cm). Based on this, the company sets **all switches at a fixed 90 cm from finished floor** in every project, regardless of occupant height. Demonstrated live with 4 people (150–185 cm) each blindly locating the switch on the first try. `unverified` — single-account practitioner claim illustrated by an informal, non-blinded demo (the same switch was tested repeatedly in front of camera), not a controlled or independently reviewed study.
- **Switch-to-door-opening spacing**: switches are placed with **15 cm from the edge of the door opening/casing to the center of the switch**. Reasoning given: closer spacing (e.g., 10 cm) causes the switch to visually/physically conflict with the door casing/trim once installed; 15 cm was presented as the point that avoids this while still being close enough to reach without looking. `unverified`, single-account design standard, not a code citation.
- **Four-category framework for whether an electrical point's height should track occupant height**:
  1. Points that must NOT be adjusted for height, fixed instead at a fixed ergonomic constant — switches (90 cm, see above).
  2. Points that indirectly depend on height via furniture height — e.g. kitchen countertop-level outlets: if the countertop is raised above standard height, outlets above it are raised by the same delta.
  3. Points that are independent of the occupant's height and instead follow standard furniture dimensions — e.g. bedside-table outlets, headboard reading-light cable outlets, TV outlets, vacuum-charging outlets — kept at fixed heights regardless of who lives there, specifically so that non-standard designer furniture (e.g. floor-mounted nightstands) doesn't force reduced-height outlets that would be unreachable from a normal bed.
  4. Points that genuinely should be adjusted to the specific occupant's height — video-intercom monitors and underfloor-heating thermostats/regulators, because these should sit at eye level, and eye level varies materially with height (demonstrated between a 150 cm and a taller person). `unverified`, single-account.
- **Anti-pattern called out explicitly**: mounting a video-intercom monitor or underfloor-heating thermostat too high or too low (not at eye level) makes it awkward to read/operate; conversely, adjusting switch height to try to match an individual occupant's height is called an unnecessary and incorrect practice given the hand-drop-height claim above.
