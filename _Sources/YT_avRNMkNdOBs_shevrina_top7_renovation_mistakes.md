## Mistakes / Warnings — Seven Named Failure Modes, Each With a Mechanism

- **⚠️ 1. Painting walls and cabinet/kitchen fronts to the same colour code produces a visible разнотон — and this is backed by their own controlled on-site experiment.** People assume that picking one NCS or RAL number and using it for both guarantees a match; it does not. **Their experiment**: on a site where the walls were already painted to a specific NCS article, they had their joinery shop make a perforated radiator panel to *exactly the same NCS number* — and got a visible mismatch, with the panel reading noticeably pinker than the wall.
  - **Stated mechanism, three parts**: (a) the paint formulations differ — what goes on MDF is not what goes on a wall, so the same colour recipe yields a different result; (b) the substrate differs (MDF vs. plaster); (c) the application method differs entirely.
  - **⚠️ The fix is counter-intuitive and is the most useful thing in this video: to get a tone-on-tone match, deliberately choose *different* shades for the wall and the front.** That is how they achieved the matched result they show.
  - **General rule attached**: always do выкрасы *and* commission a front/facade sample — don't skip the sample stage.
  - This is a **third, independent statement** of the do-выкрасы rule already corroborated four ways in the 2026-08-30 colour batch, but the wall-vs-front разнотон mechanism itself is **new to this vault**.
- **⚠️ 2. Never bridge laminate to walls (or to another floor material) with a cork expansion strip or sealant.** She notes she gets consistent hostility for saying this, and that **it is written in laminate manufacturers' own technical sheets.** Stated mechanism: laminate shrinks and expands substantially, and **the failure may not appear in the first year** — someone who reports success after a month or two of living in the flat is not evidence. "Чем больше площадь, на которую вы положите этот герметик, тем хуже"; a single small threshold strip under a door is survivable, a large run is not. Honour the technological expansion gap.
- **⚠️ 3. Push-to-open (Tip-on) and cut-in finger pulls are the wrong hardware on frequently-used fronts.** Push-to-open looks like the height of clean design and is cheap, which is why people over-apply it. **Scope it to rarely-opened cabinets only** — her examples: антресоли, or a cabinet over the WC installation holding spare toilet paper. **Never on a kitchen**, where fronts are opened many times a day, nor on clothes or cosmetics storage.
  - **Cut-in (routed) finger pulls are also named a poor choice**: very small finger contact area, the hand slips repeatedly, the fingers have to go in edge-on with little room, and it damages manicured nails; slipping actually hurts.
  - **Her recommended alternative for frequently-used fronts: pull on the front itself** — the whole front edge as the grip.
- **⚠️ 4. Never hide an air conditioner behind a perforated panel** — the strongest mechanism in the video. "Ни один кондиционерщик вам не скажет, что это грамотное решение."
  - **Primary mechanism: the AC's temperature sensor is on the unit, inside the enclosure.** Even with generously sized slots, only part of the cooled air escapes into the room, so the air inside the cabinet is always colder. Set the unit to 18 °C — its minimum — and you get 18 °C *inside the cabinet* while the room sits at, say, 25 °C, and you are still hot.
  - **Secondary mechanism: condensation.** The temperature differential across the panel produces condensate on both the unit body and the panel, and **mould follows within about a year to a year and a half of use.** The eventual outcome is that the owner removes the panel anyway.
  - She notes even a furniture maker will not endorse it, and advises checking with both the joiner and the AC installer before committing.
- **⚠️ 5. Door swings that lengthen the walking trajectory.** Named alongside a short basin spout as her "top two favourite mistakes." **The specific insight: work out which direction the door is actually approached from most often, which for a guest WC, a household/laundry block or a wardrobe is usually *not* from the entrance hall** — it is from the kitchen or living room. The handle should fall on the line of travel, not force you to take two extra steps, grab the handle, step aside to clear the swinging leaf, and then walk in. **Rehearse the usage scenarios on the plan, and decide the light-switch position in the same pass.**
- **⚠️ 6. Grey tile, especially in a dirty zone, reads as a permanently dirty floor.** She observes that ~90% of the grey tile on the market is an unhelpful shade for the interiors most people actually want, and that grey rarely suits them in the first place. **The effect is strongest with concrete-look porcelain and worst when the tile is mottled/spotted** — the brain, seeing an entrance hall where dirty shoes stand, reads the pattern as dirt rather than as a texture. The resulting urge to re-mop a clean floor every time you pass is the real cost.
- **⚠️ 7. Don't glue porcelain tile on top of an existing finish.** It leaves an ugly exposed edge, and **neither a metal edge profile nor plastering over it is a fix.** **The correct approach is to leave a recess in the plaster in advance** so the tile can be set flush with the adjacent finish (e.g. paint), meeting it in one plane through a П-profile. **The same applies to mirrors.**
  - Attached general position, worth quoting: a designer interior is not one with red accents, a yellow sofa or a designer pendant — "это интерьер, который как раз-таки продуман до мелочей." Consequence: this has to be raised with the builder **at the rough-plastering stage**, not later.
