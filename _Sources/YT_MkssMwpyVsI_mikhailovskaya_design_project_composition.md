## Labor / Delivery Model — ⚠️ three tiers of design project

**The framing this vault has been missing.** Кузина's `H43m5bn7W8k` gives the *stages* of a design project (measurements → layout → styling → render → drawings); **this gives the tiers and the deliverables.**

### 1. Эскизный проект — the sketch project

- **Visualisation as a sketch, explicitly WITHOUT reference to reality**: no real materials, no real objects, no real lighting. **A free rendering of the idea.**
- **⚠️ When it is appropriate, and both cases are about *risk* rather than about budget:**
  - **The client is not yet sure of you, or is not sure of the style** — *"предоставлять полный пакет чертежей непонятно кому"* is unwise, so you show your vision on a minimal package without spending much of your time or much of the client's money.
  - **⚠️ Or the client is highly self-confident and can select and cost everything alone — "от вас ему нужна только ваша идея, ваше видение его интерьера."** She adds honestly: **"не много таких случаев."**

### 2. Стандартный пакет — the standard package

- **The minimum necessary drawing set to actually realise the project.**
- **Visualisation now WITH reference to reality** — real materials, furniture, lighting, appliances, sanitaryware. **"Не просто какая-то эскизная визуализация, а визуализация, которая показывает будущий интерьер."**
- **⚠️ More drawings, plus obligatory SPECIFICATIONS of materials, furniture and equipment — and maximum counting of the necessary elements: counts of light fittings, counts of switches, and so on.**

### 3. Полный проект — the full project

- **For a demanding client wanting maximum detail and maximum costing.**
- Visualisation with reference to reality, specifications, **additional drawings**, and **⚠️ 3D views of the room, of furniture, and of individual contentious elements or ones needing special attention.**
- **⚠️ Her named examples of what earns a 3D view, and this is the useful part: the junction between furniture and a wall, or a special panel** — **shown specifically "для того чтобы не было допущено там мебельщиками или строителями каких-то ошибок."** **A 3D view is a defect-prevention device aimed at the trades, not a sales image aimed at the client.**

## Labor / Delivery Model — ⚠️ the five rules worth demanding by name

**These are the items a client would not know to ask for, and each has a stated reason.**

### 1. ⚠️ Tile setting-out is the designer's job, not the tiler's

**On the floor plan, if the covering is tile you must indicate the STARTING POINT of laying and show the dimensions** — her example is tile centred on the corridor.

> **"Это входит в обязанности дизайнера — показать, как эту плитку разложить."**

**And it appears twice**: also on the bathroom elevations — **"на развертках мы указываем раскладку плитки обязательно, потому что по вашим разверточкам будет плиточник раскладывать вашу плитку."** **The tiler works from the elevations, so the layout has to be on them.** She also counts the tile there — including a separate count for the kitchen backsplash.

### 2. ⚠️ Underfloor heating — area per zone, and three exclusions

Her project carries **two kinds — electric and from a gas boiler — distinguished by colour on the plan.**

- **⚠️ State the AREA of each specific underfloor-heating zone, explicitly so that purchasing is easier.**
- **⚠️ No underfloor heating under furniture; none under appliances; and an offset from the walls, shown on the drawing.**
- **⚠️ And agree it with the client** — she names this as a step, not an assumption.

*(This is the plan-side counterpart to the Кузина Round 5 finding that underfloor cable cannot be cut on site: if the zones are not dimensioned at design stage, the over-order that follows has nowhere to go.)*

### 3. ⚠️ Separate black-and-white electrical elevations — her distinctive practice

**She says she has not seen other designers do this, and offers it with an argument rather than as preference.**

**Separate elevations showing ONLY sockets, switches, electro-outlets and cable channel, in black and white**, for every wall of every room including the bathroom and WC, with dimensions.

> **⚠️ Her reason: "многие розетки у нас находятся за мебелью, электровывод можно упустить, который там в потолке находится. Если мы делаем отдельно такие развертки, мы этого не упустим."**

**On a coloured elevation carrying furniture, an outlet is legible but easy to overlook. On a bare one it is the only thing there.**

### 4. ⚠️ Label every electrical outlet — including the ones no switch controls

**On the switch-binding plan she labels every electro-outlet, including those not connected to any switch** — for instance one energised by a door opening.

Her worked label: *"электровывод, высота такая-то, подсветка шкафа, выключается на открывание двери."* And where a sconce switches on the fitting itself: *"бра выключается на приборе."*

> **⚠️ Her reason is self-checking, not documentation: "часто можно сделать ошибки в этих планах, и для того чтобы себя перепроверить, лучше все выводы взять себе за принцип подписывать — независимо от того, подключены они к какому-то выключателю либо выключаются на приборе."**
>
> **An unlabelled outlet is ambiguous between "deliberately unswitched" and "you forgot to connect it."** Labelling removes the ambiguity **and reduces questions back to the designer.**

### 5. ⚠️ Elevations should label the mechanism, not just the object

On colour elevations she labels **which drawer is push-to-open, where a handle is** — and **where she has not drawn a handle, she labels "opens by handle."** Also **"opens lift-up,"** the extractor position, **which chipboard, which mouldings, which wall finish where, the backlighting (dashed and labelled), the skirting, and the plinth material.**

**⚠️ And she places 3D views on the elevations wherever possible**, plus **drawings of contentious junctions** — *"чтобы было понятно при изготовлении мебели, как что работает"* — including a section where something is set back.

## Labor / Delivery Model — a detail that lands on an earlier finding

**⚠️ Her worked project goes from a 2,800 mm ceiling height on the survey plan to 2,650 mm on the post-replanning plan, because the floor level rose after levelling** — and she shows this as a normal thing to record on the drawing.

**This is exactly the arithmetic Кузина's `JOBm37_9iDg` identified as the highest-consequence rough-stage error** (a miscalculated finished-floor level making fixtures unusable; a WC set so high the feet do not reach). **Михайловская's practice is the control for it: the finished-floor change is carried explicitly from one plan to the next, so the number that later governs fixture heights is written down rather than assumed.** **Two independent channels, one making the error visible and the other showing the documentation that prevents it.**

## Mistakes / Warnings

- Accepting a "design project" without establishing which of the three tiers it is.
- A drawing package with no tile setting-out point — the tiler will then decide it.
- Underfloor heating drawn without zone areas, wall offsets, or the furniture and appliance exclusions.
- Electrical outlets shown only on coloured elevations, where furniture hides them.
- Unlabelled outlets, which cannot be distinguished from forgotten ones.
- **⚠️ Her own self-criticism, offered as advice: on her drawing the hatching overlaps the labels. "Либо делайте штриховку светлее, либо подписи немножко смещайте… не делайте так, как я."**
