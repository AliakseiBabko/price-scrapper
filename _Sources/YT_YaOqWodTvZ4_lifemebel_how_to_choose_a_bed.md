## ⚠️⚠️ 1. THE CONTRADICTION — clearance around the bed

**The vault currently carries ARMOS's figure: *passages 50 cm or more* around the bed. LifeMebel states a materially larger number, and adds a second one the vault does not have at all.**

> ***«Расстояние от края кровати до стены или шкафа должно быть не менее 70 сантиметров. Если предполагается наличие рабочей зоны, то расстояние от края кровати до рабочего стола должно быть не менее одного метра.»***

| Constraint | ARMOS (in the vault) | **LifeMebel (this source)** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Bed edge → wall **or wardrobe** | **50 cm** | **⚠️⚠️ not less than 70 cm** |
| Bed edge → a desk | *(silent)* | **⚠️⚠️ not less than 1 m** |

> **→ These are not one claim with noise on it — 70 is 40% more than 50, and in a small bedroom that difference decides whether the bed fits at all.** Neither source shows its working. ⚠️ **Unresolved; recorded as a disagreement, not averaged away.** A plausible reconciliation is that 50 cm is a squeeze-past minimum and 70 cm a comfortable-use figure — **but that is my reading, not either source's.**
>
> ⚠️ **Note the desk figure is the more useful of the two here**, because it is the only clearance in the vault that governs a bed against *working* furniture rather than against a wall, and the vault's own layout work puts a desk in a bedroom.

## ⚠️⚠️ 2. The width test that needs no tape measure

> ***«Оптимальной шириной считается та кровать, когда человек может легко запрокинуть руки за голову и не задеть соседа.»***

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ The best-formed thing in the source.** The vault already has ARMOS's arithmetic rule (20 cm from each sleeper to the edge or to their partner). This is the same constraint **expressed as an action you can perform in the showroom**, which is the form that survives contact with a shop floor. **Keep both: the arithmetic sizes the purchase, the arm test checks it.**
>
> He gives height the same treatment — *«высота должна позволять человеку легко ложиться и спускаться с кровати»* — which is the weakest version of the 90°-knee rule in the vault, but it is at least body-referenced.

## 3. Size classes, and the length rule

| Class | Sleeping-surface width |
| :--- | :--- |
| Односпальная | **70–120 cm** |
| Полутораспальная | **120–160 cm** |
| Двуспальная | **160 cm and up** |

- **⚠️ Length must exceed the sleeper's height by AT LEAST 10 cm, *«а лучше покупать кровати с ещё большим запасом»*.** The vault did not have this stated as a rule.
- **⚠️ And the caveat that matters in a small room: size on the classification is SLEEPING-surface width, not the bed's footprint** — *«ориентироваться нужно не только на ширину спального места, но и на габаритные размеры кровати»*. **A soft bed with a thick upholstered surround can be two size classes apart on those two numbers.**

## ⚠️ 4. Bases — and the one that is sold separately

Three named: **настил** (a plain sheet of plywood or ЛДСП), **металлическое основание**, and **ортопедическое основание**.

- **⚠️⚠️ His definition of the orthopaedic base is specific and checkable: *«конструкция состоит из ламелей, держателей и рамы; ламели имеют слегка изогнутую форму, благодаря чему нагрузка на решётку полностью распределяется по основанию»*. Rated load *«до 150 килограмм»* per sleeping place.** **→ Curved, not flat — that is the inspectable distinction between a real orthopaedic base and slats in a frame.**
- **⚠️⚠️ And the commercial point, stated outright: *«стоит учитывать, что ортопедическое основание приобретается отдельно»*.** **→ A budgeting trap: someone comparing two bed prices may be comparing one with a base against one without.** This sits alongside the vault's existing lamellae-gap rule (7–8 cm) from a different angle — that rule says what a good base *is*; this says **you may not be buying one at all.**
- Lift mechanism described as lifting *with* the mattress and latching at the top, freeing the wardrobe or chest of drawers. **Consistent with [[_Sources/YT_nybZA1MkUrs_netzaru_bed_buying_advice|NetzaRu]], who goes much further on what to inspect on one.**

## ⚠️ 5. Materials and upholstery — DATED, and one item reads against the vault

Materials, in his order of preference: **натуральное дерево** (most valuable — durable, ages well, low-maintenance) → **МДФ** (tolerates temperature and humidity swings, *«демократичные»* prices) → **ЛДСП** (econom segment) → **металл**, which he places socially rather than technically: loft or provence interiors, a dacha, or a child's room.

**Upholstery, and this is where the 2020 date bites:**

- Soft beds *«на пике популярности уже несколько лет»*; **рогожка *«добавляет уюта»*, and велюр is named as *«самый популярный тренд»*.**
- **⚠️ Soft headboards: comfortable to lean on without a pillow, and *«препятствуют возникновению потёртостей на стене»*** — a small, real, non-obvious benefit. **He also notes headboard-less models suit a small room because they save depth.**
- **⚠️⚠️ BUT: the vault carries TWO independent sources (Шеврина; ARMOS) saying рогожка is exactly wrong with pets, because interlacing fibres catch claws** — and this batch adds **two more** ([[_Sources/YT_SwOVFr5YRP8_yakobi_upholstery_fabric_choice|Якоби]], [[_Sources/YT_1UGUjGmZPNA_pinskdrev_sofa_fabric_guide|Пинскдрев]]). **LifeMebel names no pet caveat at all.**

> **→ Do not read this as a fifth opinion on one side of a four-to-one split. He is answering a different question — what looks current — and is silent on the one (claws) where the other four agree.** His велюр recommendation is consistent with all four.
