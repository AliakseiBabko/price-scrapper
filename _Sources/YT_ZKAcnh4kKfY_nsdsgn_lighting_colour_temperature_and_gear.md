## ⚠️ Colour temperature — the reasoning, which this vault did not have

Before this source, `colour temperature` returned **zero** matches across the vault
and `Kelvin` only six. This is the gap it fills, and the value is the *rule*, not the
numbers.

1. **Living interiors: warm spectrum — 3000 K, sometimes 3500 K, and 2700–3000 K in
   some cases.** The host's own flat is 2700–3000 K throughout, with **4000 K over the
   worktop**.
2. **Latitude argument**: at this latitude the dominant daylight from the sky is
   bluer, so **warmer artificial light suits people here**. In southern countries
   (Dubai, Thailand named) interiors are deliberately cooled — «у них уют достигается
   наоборот холодом» — which the host finds actively uncomfortable.
3. **Height rule («гистограмма»): the higher the luminaire, the cooler it may be;
   the closer to the eyes, the warmer it should be.**
4. **Biodynamics**: perception of colour temperature is tied at a genetic level to
   the sun's daily arc. Warm light supports rest; **cooler light makes a person more
   capable of work — «чем холоднее, тем мы больше работаем»** — which is why offices
   run 4000 K and above.
5. **⚠️ The Kruithof curve is the actual scientific basis, and it gives a usable
   rule.** (ASR renders it «кривая Крит хофа»; the referent is the
   Kruithof curve.) It relates luminous flux intensity to colour temperature:
   - **The brighter the light, the cooler it should be. The more dimmed, the warmer.**
   - Stated against office illuminance norms — **500, 600, 700, up to 1000 lux** →
     colour temperature must rise; drop to **200, 300, 150, 100 lux** → colour
     temperature must fall (warmer).
   - **Applied consequence, and it answers the question the host actually asked:** a
     cooking/work zone is light-intensive, so **4000 K is the comfortable choice
     there** — not an arbitrary convention. Domestic lighting generally is *not*
     bright, which is why the rest of the home is warm.
6. **Third, independent reason for 4000 K over food preparation: under bright yellow
   light cooked food looks unappetising** — «она будет жёлтая такая, ужасно».

## Equipment selection — what to look at beyond price, colour and shape

The stated checklist: **optics and optics quality, luminous flux, colour-rendering
index, whether it pulsates, whether it is controllable, what heatsink (радиатор),
and what power supply (блок питания)**.

### Secondary optics (вторичная оптика)

- Definition given: what forms the beam — diffusers (maximally scattering) and
  **lenses focusing to 10°, 20°, 30°, 60°**.
- **⚠️ The beam angle must be written down everywhere — on the luminaire, in the
  commercial offer, and in the project.** He names it as a characteristic buyers
  should demand and check.
- **Cheap optics smear and distort light, produce aberrations, and significantly
  degrade colour rendering.** So optics quality is not only about beam control —
  it directly costs you the CRI you paid for.
- CRI explained for viewers as how faithfully artificial light renders colour, and
  tied to how well you see your home and the people in it. Illustrated by a
  restaurant where he could not read the menu without a phone torch.

### ⚠️ Power supplies — the most checkable rule in the source

- **Recommended: sealed, IP-rated metal units.** Claimed service life is
  ASR-garbled (transcript reads «35 лет», which is implausible for a driver and is
  recorded as `uncertain`); the **verifiable claim he immediately substitutes is his
  own: one has been running in his home for 10 years with no failure.**
- **Avoid: the perforated/mesh units, especially any with a fan.** These fail
  **reliably within about a year to eighteen months**.
- **Warranty on the cheap units is typically one year — and they fail at about a year
  to two.** «Замена, трата нервов, трата денег, трата сил.»
- **He offers this as a method, not just a fact**: whichever element of a lighting
  system you take, it can be decomposed the same way. Worked example of a quote that
  is «twice as cheap» for kitchen-backsplash LED: the cheap version has **low-CRI
  tape instead of high-CRI, and no aluminium profile**.

### ⚠️ Dimming is the single biggest failure point in lighting

- «Всё самое больное со светом всегда было связано с диммированием.»
- **Mechanism**: buy a tape, later decide to dim it, and **an ordinary power supply
  can produce strong flicker (мерцание)**. Whether it flickers depends on the quality
  of both the equipment **and the dimmer**.
- **Operational rule: the moment anything in the scheme must be dimmable, treat it as
  a warning flag** and specify for it deliberately from the start.

## Chinese light and replicas

- **«Китай Китаю рознь.»** No objection to Chinese manufacture as such — phones and
  cars are Chinese; almost any product on the world market contains a Chinese
  component. Choosing quality Chinese solutions intelligently is correct; expecting
  a miracle from an AliExpress luminaire is not.
- European light is frequently Chinese-made anyway: he visited the Chinese lighting
  city (ASR-garbled place name) where a Spanish factory whose fixture hangs in his own
  home keeps an official showroom, alongside a «улица светильников» where the world's
  lighting trade gathers.
- **⚠️ Replica price ladder, and it is a striking data point.** The image you see
  online is, **in 99% of cases, the original manufacturer's own photograph**, scraped
  and reused identically everywhere. The same apparent model then lists at
  **180,000 → 70,000 → 60,000 → 50,000 → 40,000 → 30,000 → 20,000 → 15,000 RUB**
  (≈$1,900 down to ≈$160) — roughly a **12× spread behind one photograph**.
- Three honest paths he sets out: save and buy the same picture cheaply **and then do
  not complain** about shade, tint or paint quality; find something within budget in a
  **different** form factor; or consult someone experienced first — some items are
  «ведро с гайками» but perfectly acceptable at 3 m height where nobody will see.
- **⚠️ The procurement risk of buying direct is the loss of the inspection step.**
  «AliExpress пришёл, ты его принял на объекте — ты с этим уже ничего не сделаешь.» A
  bureau or supplier opens, checks and **rejects (отбраковка)** items that miss a
  quality standard. That rejection step is what direct purchase removes.

## ⚠️ Integrated-LED chandeliers are disposable — with a service case

**The distinction is drawn first, and it matters**: **технический свет** — spots,
projectors, linear and profile systems — is LED and is *better* than lamp-based
equipment, precisely because it is assembled from replaceable parts (typical chips,
replaceable drivers) and can be repaired.

**Integrated-LED chandeliers are the opposite**, and he says he cannot stand them:
- «Чем она проще, тем скорее выйдет из строя», and they are **not amenable to
  resuscitation**.
- **The service case, which is the mechanism**: a client's chandelier bought about
  two years earlier («хороший срок для супер-эконома») burnt out. Opened in their own
  service area, the driver turned out to be a **bespoke L-shaped unit made to fit that
  specific niche**, produced by nobody in the world except that chandelier's own
  manufacturer — who replied that the model **and its driver had been discontinued as
  unsuccessful**. Nothing can be done; the chandelier is thrown away.
- **⚠️ The worst case is the one worth designing against: a chandelier that fitted the
  design perfectly, or was architecturally fitted into specific dimensions or a
  niche.** Then the recovery path for the whole composite solution is severe.
- **Rule: for chandeliers, buy either genuinely high-quality equipment, or fixtures
  with replaceable lamps and replaceable elements.**
- Also named as an irritant: the LED chandelier that **cycles cold → neutral → warm
  on successive flicks of the wall switch**, encountered in a hotel.

## Lighting budget

- **⚠️ Asked directly what lighting costs for a 100–120 m² three-room flat, and
  whether there is a percentage of the construction budget, both specialists say they
  genuinely do not have that data** — clients, designers and architects never disclose
  the construction budget to them. The host believes such a percentage exists; they
  agree to look into it after recording. **Recorded as an honest refusal to invent a
  figure, and as an open question.**
- **One concrete data point: a евродвушка/евротрёшка done in a "super-economy"
  variant for ~350,000 RUB (≈$3,700) total, covering light AND electrics together.**
  He frames this as roughly the floor. **⚠️ Note the scope — the figure bundles two
  trades, so it is not comparable with a lighting-only budget.**
- Chandeliers «от 10.000 руб (≈$110) до бесконечности».
- **⚠️ Maslow-pyramid rule for lighting decisions, and it is genuinely useful
  planning advice: work bottom-up.** Base needs first — being able to see where you
  are going, checking on children at night, «в Питере в ноябре». **Marketing pulls
  clients to the top of the pyramid** — «я хочу управление с голосового помощника» —
  before the base need is solved. «Сначала реши базовую потребность… а потом уже
  подумай, как это на голосовой помощник завести.»

## Cost of a late question, and inter-trade practice

- **⚠️ A concrete asymmetry, and the best single illustration in the batch**: a
  question was asked only **after the countertop had been installed**. Inserting the
  countertop took seconds; **the rework took a month**, with a serious dispute.
- **Advice to designers from the specialists**: analyse and pass everything on to the
  client *and* to adjacent trades (прорабы, electricians). Their complaint is that
  until the album is handed over, everything on site behaves as if classified.
- **Bring the client to meetings, and keep a shared chat containing every participant
  including whoever actually decides.** Reason given: speaking only to the designer
  leaves it uncertain whether and how the information reaches the client.
- **⚠️ The designer-as-generalist model, stated by the specialists and endorsed by
  the designer himself.** «Дизайнер — это ключ от всех дверей, но с очень
  ограниченным функционалом. Он везде всё знает, но глубинно не разбирается…
  решение финальное не за ним.» Depth *and currency* of information can only come
  from a narrow specialist, because the market changes constantly. Medicine analogy:
  the designer is the therapist who makes a primary diagnosis and refers on —
  and «терапевт отправляет более узкому специалисту, чтобы исключить свою ошибку».

## Client segmentation (context, lightly extracted)

Three client types with different pains: **commercial** (understands lighting design
is tied to revenue), **flat**, and **house** (adds exterior/landscape). The recurring
domestic pain is not knowing what anything should cost and suspecting the specialist
of upselling. **Exterior lighting carries a genuinely different risk profile** —
snow, dogs, lawnmowers, ground movement, badly laid cable, and electric shock — so
the responsibility boundary is much harder there than indoors, where «можно
переделать, можно потерпеть».
