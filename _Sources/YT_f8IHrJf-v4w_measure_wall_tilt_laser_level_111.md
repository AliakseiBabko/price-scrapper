## Quantities / Measurements

- **Laser-line-level wall-tilt measurement technique (1)** — measures a
  wall's out-of-plumb deviation in centimeters without a laser distance
  meter, using only a self-leveling laser line projector ("осепостроитель"):
  1. Place the laser ~3 m from the wall, enable its vertical-plane laser
     line, and aim it **roughly perpendicular** to the wall (does not need
     to be exact — the leveler self-corrects to true vertical regardless
     of the laser's own angle to the wall, since the unit auto-levels
     internally).
  2. Mark two points where the vertical line crosses the wall: one as low
     as possible, one as high as reachable.
  3. Move the laser to the **side**, along the wall, roughly 3 m away from
     its first position, and re-aim it so the vertical line lands exactly
     on the **lower** mark.
  4. Read the offset between the laser line and the **upper** mark — the
     offset distance in centimeters **equals** the wall's tilt in
     centimeters over that measured height, by direct geometric
     correspondence (explicitly framed by the source as "pure geometry,"
     not an approximation): if the beam misses the upper mark by 3 cm, the
     wall is out of plumb by 3 cm over that span.
  - **Direction reading**: if the beam lands to one side of the upper mark
    when the laser has been moved toward the viewer's original position,
    that side indicates the wall leans away from the viewer at the top;
    landing to the other side indicates the wall leans toward the viewer.
    (Transcript is ASR-garbled on the exact left/right mapping — treat the
    *existence* and magnitude method as `confirmed`, the specific
    left/right-to-lean-direction mapping as `unclear`/needs re-verification
    against the source video directly before being relied on.)
  - **Why a simple physical level is described as insufficient for this
    purpose**: a standard (even high-quality/branded) bubble level held
    against the wall gives an imprecise read for this specific "how much
    is this wall out of plumb, in cm" question; a laser distance meter
    held to a level would be accurate but isn't something field crews
    typically carry to a first survey visit, whereas the laser line
    projector already is standard survey kit.
