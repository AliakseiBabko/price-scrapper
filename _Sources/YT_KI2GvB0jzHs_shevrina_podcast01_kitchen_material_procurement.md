## Cost Drivers / Planning Rules — Why a Visualisation and the Built Result Differ

She addresses head-on a complaint designers get, and gives two concrete causes rather than defending the profession:

- **⚠️ Time. Up to about a year and a half can pass between the design project and joinery realisation** — and specified materials sell out or are discontinued in that window. Her live instance: suppliers ceased shipments to Russia, remaining stock was bought out quickly, and they were sourcing alternatives at the time of recording.
- **⚠️ The client sees the real slab.** Once you get to procurement and visit showrooms, factories and warehouses, the client comes too — and may fall for a different stone. She says this happens on essentially every project to some degree, and on this one the client preferred a much more active veining than the calmer stone specified. **Her position: let them.** "Главное, чтобы клиенту было комфортно жить" — an island is an accent point anyway, so a more active stone there is not a problem, as long as the client has seen the whole slab and understands the render will change.

## Materials — Kitchen Surfaces

- **Thin large-format ceramic can be fixed to MDF fronts.** She gives the thickness as **3–5 mm** here. **⚠️ Note the discrepancy: in `z4G-ocStu9o` (same channel) she gives "up to 6 mm" for the same technique.** Both are the same order and neither is load-bearing on its own; recorded as ~3–6 mm with the inconsistency flagged rather than silently averaged.
- **Why ceramic is used on worktops**: heat-tolerant (you can put hot pans down) and **absorbs no stains at all**.
- **⚠️ Acrylic (solid surface) worktop drawback, with her design position attached**: white acrylic stains — red wine absorbs if not wiped promptly, hot items leave a yellow mark. It *is* restorable by sanding and polishing, and she acknowledges that. **But she will not specify a material whose outcome depends on the owner being diligent**: "чаще люди ленятся… лучше предусмотреть материал понадёжнее, не рассчитывать на то, что человек будет готов шлифовать." A clean statement of a design principle — specify for the realistic owner, not the ideal one.
- **Island cost technique**: ceramic on the top *and* wrapped down the visible side, front and back panels; **ordinary MDF fronts on the hidden inner side, because nobody sees them** — "можно сократить бюджет."
- **Microcement can go on cabinet fronts**, and there is a real execution choice (below).
- She is **explicitly unsure whether microcement resists kitchen grease and heat** on a worktop — "я вот вам сразу заявляю, тут нужны эксперименты." Recorded as an open question, not a fact.

## Planning Rules — Sample and Sequencing Discipline

- **⚠️ Facade colour samples must be real MDF-in-enamel front samples, not paint on paper.** "Нельзя делать на фасады выкрасы чисто на листочке… МДФ другой материал, эмаль это другой материал, это не просто краской покрасить." **This is the practical remedy for the разнотон finding in `avRNMkNdOBs`** — that video proved wall and front differ at one code; this one says what to do instead.
- **⚠️ Cost of a front sample: about 3,000 RUB (≈$50)** from a joinery shop — cheap enough to order as many as the budget allows and compare them on site. *(5b: `USD/RUB` trailing 6-month mean before the 2023-03-18 publish date, 2022-09-20..2023-03-18, 121 samples, 66.1279 RUB/USD; rounded to the nearest 10.)* **Region unresolved at level 1**, so treat the absolute figure with the standard comparability caveat — the useful part is that it is a small, per-unit, repeatable cost.
- **⚠️ Sequencing rule: let the less flexible material constrain the more flexible one.** They went to look at acrylic and quartz slabs *first*, found the stone, and then **eliminated the too-light NCS enamel shades** and made front samples only in the ones that suited the stone. Stone comes in fixed slabs; enamel can be tinted to anything — so the enamel adapts, not the stone. (The shortlist ended up within two or three tones of each other.)

## Cost Drivers — Procurement Constraints That Change the Design

- **⚠️ Your joinery shop's capabilities are a hard constraint on material choice, and this killed a specified material on this project.** The client insisted on a particular joinery shop (another city, personal connection, large discount, and genuinely good service) that **does not work with ceramic** — so ceramic was dropped from both the island and the fronts entirely. **Her stated market pattern: finding a ceramic-capable joinery shop is easy in Moscow and hard in other cities**; she has clients elsewhere who canvassed local shops and got refusals. **Actionable: confirm the fabricator can execute the material before specifying it.**
- **Thin quartz exists and can go on fronts**, but there is not much of it — and the quartz they wanted on design grounds was too thick for fronts.
- **⚠️ Microcement on fronts — two execution routes, and the choice is forced by context.** Either (a) the joinery shop applies it at their factory and delivers finished fronts, or (b) you order **raw MDF fronts "подготовленные под микроцемент"** and have the **same applicator who does your walls** coat the fronts on site. **Route (b) is required whenever a wall in the same room is also in microcement** — you cannot send a wall to a factory, so factory-finished fronts would be a different batch, manner and composition, and would not match.
- **⚠️ Material-availability risk scales with how common the decor is.** Popular decors — concrete-look, microcement-look, marble-look — have many producers, so "даже если половина производителей… уедут из России, у вас всё ещё останется половина." **A rare decor such as onyx has no substitute: if it runs out, "пиши пропала, придётся вообще другой дизайн придумывать."** Her mitigation for a rare material: **track stock levels, and reserve, buy out and warehouse it in advance.**
- Worked instance: Porcelanosa stopped supplying in early 2022 while they needed bathroom tile immediately; they substituted Atlas Concorde. The master bathroom ended up nearly identical to the render, the guest bathroom did not — and she prefers the substitute's texture.
