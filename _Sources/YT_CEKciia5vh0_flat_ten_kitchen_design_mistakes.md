## Quantities / Measurements — the dimensional core

**⚠️ Work surface (mistake 2), and he defines the term, which matters because "work surface" is used loosely elsewhere:**

- **⚠️ Minimum ~1,000 mm of work surface**, defined as **the distance from the hob to the sink** — or from the hob to a tall unit, depending on the kitchen's configuration. Below that "это будет неудобно," especially for people who cook actively.
- **⚠️ Depth: the multi-year trend is away from 600 mm toward 700 or even 750 mm.** Where the backsplash zone is functional (a rail system) it can be deeper still.
- **⚠️ But there is a ceiling, and he gives it directly: "больше 800 я бы не делал"** — deeper and you simply cannot reach across it, which is its own problem.

**⚠️ Sink selection (mistake 3):**

- **For a 600 mm cabinet, choose the largest sink the cabinet allows** in both width and depth. **A 400 mm bowl is too small for active cooking; 550 mm is acceptable; larger is better.**
- **⚠️ A drainer wing occupies roughly 250 mm — and it shortens the work surface by that much**, so it trades directly against the 1,000 mm minimum above.
- **⚠️ Under-mounted (подклейка) looks better. Surface-mounted (накладные) — "нет."**

**⚠️ Countertop thickness (mistake 4):** quartz agglomerate is generally **20–30 mm**; at 30 mm it starts to look heavy. **A chamfer/bevel on the front edge makes a thick top read thinner while staying structurally thick and reliable.**

**⚠️ Plinth height (mistake 9) — three standards and a real appliance constraint:**

- **Standards: 100, 120, 150 mm.**
- **150 mm has no practical purpose in most cases.** The one justification: **where the design uses tall skirting boards and the skirting meets the kitchen, matching the plinth height avoids cutting it and reads as one line.**
- **Otherwise 100 mm is much more aesthetic. In some cases they use 50 mm — more aesthetic again, with one catch.**
- **⚠️ The catch is a real specification trap: not every built-in dishwasher will fit a 50 mm plinth.** Models that will are limited — he names **Smeg** now, **Siemens** formerly — and **they cost around 30–40% more than a standard dishwasher** ("не чуть, прилично"). **So a 50 mm plinth is an appliance-budget decision, not just a styling one.**

**⚠️ Under-cabinet lighting position (mistake 6):**

- **Place the LED profile roughly at the mid-depth of the wall units.**
- **For tall units, recess it 100 mm from the front of the carcass.**
- **⚠️ Putting it in the corner makes it decorative only — it washes the backsplash and makes the backsplash and countertop look unevenly lit.** The lighting has to work as task light while cooking, not only as decoration.

## Design Concept — colour and texture (mistake 1)

- **⚠️ His limit: a kitchen reads laconic with a maximum of two colours, plus a third as the countertop** (accent or not). The failure case he describes: base units one colour, tall units a second, wall units a third, countertop a fourth — "для современного дизайна это уже перебор."
- **⚠️ And a permission that is as useful as the limit: do not try to match everything.** Appliances, handles and profiles have their own colours and shades; **internal hardware, drawers and pull-out mechanisms will never be one tone and need not be** — "не нужно и сложно." Full monochrome "будет выглядеть как раз скучно." **The two-colour rule applies to base colours, facades and countertops only.**
- **⚠️ The matching criterion is warmth, not hue: "они должны быть подобраны по теплоте оттенков."** Warm wood needs warm stone; a mismatch in warmth produces dissonance. His own showroom example — tinted natural veneer plus a light marble-effect quartz agglomerate, on a 7 m island.

## Cost Drivers — ⚠️ mistake 4, the allocation rule, stated against his own interest

- **The mistake: good facades with a cheap countertop.** Expensive veneer or enamel facades under a plastic countertop "просто убьёшь весь дизайн. Это 100%."
- **⚠️ The correction, and he flags the conflict himself: "так нам как будто бы, как компании мебельной, в теории выгоднее продать кухню подороже."** But when a client wants to optimise cost, **they insist on the reverse: simpler facades — ЛДСП, laminate, a plastic — with a well-chosen colour, and a stone countertop. "Это будет намного лучше."**
- **This is the same allocation logic as the channel's 42 m² case** ([[_Sources/YT_-wYdhssCHlI_flat_42m2_ldsp_case|-wYdhssCHlI]]) **and it corroborates Безверхая's "economise on what is replaceable" principle from a different trade's perspective** — here applied within a single object rather than across a renovation.

## Furniture / Built-ins — hardware (mistake 7), with a failure mechanism

- **What he means by фурнитура: the runners a drawer rides on, and the hinges that open a swing door.**
- **⚠️ Cheap hardware cannot be adjusted** — you cannot set a facade in all planes with the correct gaps — **so the aesthetic result is unobtainable regardless of anything else.**
- **⚠️ The failure mechanism, and it is the item this vault did not hold: a cheap hinge with cruciform mounting plates fixed on self-tappers gradually destroys the carcass. You notice nothing for years, then the door stops working properly and "у тебя уже корпус весь в труху превратился вместе с креплениями петель."** **The repair is then not a hinge but a side panel — and for a central section, dismantling half the kitchen.**
- **Replacing cheap hardware later is expensive, and "никто не любит за такую работу браться."**
- **⚠️ A concrete budget instruction for corner mechanisms**: do not buy a cheap corner basket at **5,700–9,000 ₽ (2026-01) ≈ $70–$110** (USD equivalent, 12-month trailing mean to 2026-01-01, 83.2108 RUB/USD; comparability aid) — the stainless rusts, it works loose on cheap runners, the door hangs crooked. **"Купи нормальную поставь, либо просто поставь полку. Это просто полка будет намного лучше, чем вот эта вот дешёвая."**
- **LeMans is named as the optimal corner solution** — petal-shaped shelves that swing out of a corner unit, using the full corner volume — **if bought at proper quality.**

## Planning Rules — the island (mistake 8) and the backsplash (mistake 5)

- **⚠️ "Остров ради острова"**: an island is genuinely convenient but needs enough space. Forcing one into a small kitchen-living room "просто для того, чтобы он был" is wrong; **in most such cases a kitchen plus a kitchen table is the more logical pairing.**
- **⚠️ A second test he applies: if the island has neither a hob nor a sink built into it, it is also an island for its own sake** — it just occupies space.
- **⚠️ And a constructional one: islands are often designed so you cannot actually sit at them — no leg room.** So an island needs (a) enough space and (b) a functionally resolved structure.
- **Terminology worth keeping: «фартук» is the domestic word; in the interior trade it is «стеновая панель».**
- **⚠️ Return "ears" (загибы) on the wall panel look unprofessional** — without side pieces the wall material simply shows, which is fine. A sink hard against a wall is not always convenient; move it away where possible, and if you cannot, the return is "можно пережить с точки зрения сценария эксплуатации."
- **⚠️ Wall-panel installation order is conditional, and there is no universal answer — this is the sequencing item to carry**: **if the kitchen has a closed contour (base units, wall units and two tall units), the wall panel is installed BEFORE the kitchen. If the contour is open (tall units at top and bottom only), the wall panel goes in AFTER the kitchen is installed.**
- **Colour: in ~90% of kitchens the wall panel matches the countertop's colour and material.**

## Mistakes / Warnings — his own kitchen, as a worked failure

- **⚠️ A first-person case that is a clean lesson in specifying two components together**: he bought an expensive German mixer with a dual outlet and filtered water — "очень удобная штука" — and then, at the end of the renovation with budgets overrunning, economised on the sink from a chain retailer.
- **The failure: the stream lands directly on the drain valve and sprays across the whole kitchen.**
- **⚠️ Why it was never fixed: the sink is bonded into a quartz-agglomerate countertop and is a non-standard size — so correcting it means dismantling part of the kitchen, removing the countertop, un-bonding the sink and cutting a new opening. "Дорого, долго, больно."** **They lived with it for five years.**
- **The transferable rule: mixer and sink geometry must be checked as a pair before either is bought, and a bonded sink in a stone top is effectively permanent.**

## Planning Rules — mistake 10, and the honest form of the advice

- **His "main mistake" is designing the kitchen yourself**, on the ground that a person does this two or three times in a lifetime, so experience is insufficient, and habits are not necessarily good ones — "то, к чему вы привыкли, не всегда хорошо."
- **⚠️ But the form of the recommendation is better than a pitch, and is what makes it usable: bring your own project and ask to have it audited.** He contrasts two responses you may get — "окей, за деньги, давай сделаем, как ты хочешь" versus "у тебя здесь такие-то ошибки, давайте оптимизируем" — **and says to choose the second.** Ask directly: "посмотрите, пожалуйста, всё ли здесь окей? Может быть, вы что-то рекомендовать могли бы?"
- **⚠️ And a caution against the copying instinct: "не всегда правильная позиция смотреть, что где сделано у других. У каждого человека свой сценарий эксплуатации кухонной мебели."** Consistent with this vault's existing scenario-driven planning content.
