## Materials — preconditions before any wood goes in

- **⚠️ Two non-negotiables, stated as the frame for everything else: the bathroom must have good ventilation, and the wood must be impregnated.**
- **⚠️ Distance from wet zones is a *planning*-stage decision, not a finishing one.** Wetter zones are those immediately by the basin, shower or bath, where water constantly lands; drier zones are walls furthest from any mixer. **But she is explicit that dry zones still suffer from condensate** — "сухие" is relative, not safe.
- **Species selection follows from which zone the item sits in**, because species differ in **how much moisture they absorb and how much they deform.**

## Materials — species, ranked (common species only, not exotics)

- **⚠️ Oak («дуб») is best, and the reason is chemical, not just density: it contains tannin, a natural antiseptic that inhibits fungal growth.** It also deforms less than beech.
- **⚠️ Beech («бук») — popular for furniture and specifically should not go in a bathroom.** Named failure modes: it will either **bow and open along its glue lines, or crack.**
- **Ash («ясень»)** — harder than some oak species (she notes baseball bats are made from it), usually lighter in colour than oak. **Its drawback is appearance, not performance: it can be blotchy («пёстрый»), which reads badly, so ash is often toned or painted.**
- **Elm / karagach («карагач», «вяз»)** — bright, contrasting, slightly exotic figure; commonly used for single-slab worktops following the trunk's own shape. **Rarely seen in bathrooms** — the reason emerges later under slab warping.
- **Larch («лиственница») — cheaper than oak or ash**, and works with a quality impregnation, specifically a moisture-resistant anti-rot impregnation («влагостойкая пропитка против гниения»).
- **Any species can technically go in, including birch and softwoods — but soft species rot faster than hard ones.**
- **⚠️ Birch («берёза») — a scoped exception worth recording.** It **cracks quickly, which is a defect everywhere except a wabi-sabi bathroom, where it is a feature.** No pronounced texture, so it reads calm and monolithic. **Needs good impregnation and careful maintenance — "если у вас не ваби, то не надо так."**

## Materials — the two deformation mechanisms, and what follows from each

**Mechanism 1 — general moisture/temperature movement.**
- **Doors and drawer fronts warp, and doors warp faster because they have more area.** They stop closing properly and the distortion is visible by eye. (She notes wryly that this does not annoy everyone.)
- **⚠️ Therefore: never fix a large flat wooden surface rigidly. Always leave gaps — room for movement — especially in a bathroom, where wood "меняется сильно и постоянно."**
- **⚠️ Worked failure she shows: joinery built tight around a washing machine.** Two problems at once — the shelf has nowhere to expand, **and the machine's vibration will rapidly loosen a structure clamped against it.** The better version leaves space around the machine "куда им трястись."
- **Shelves must also be left free to move**, not fixed hard.
- **⚠️ Shelves close to water are a doubtful idea for a maintenance reason, not a material one**: wood does better dry, so it has to be wiped — **and a worktop is easy to wipe while a shelf means unloading everything, wiping, and putting it all back.** So put shelves further from the wet zone, or on the other side of a wall.
- **⚠️ Doors/fronts do better as a framed («рамочная») construction of separate elements than as one solid panel** — her example uses glass infill.

**Mechanism 2 — cupping along the growth rings.**
- **A single large piece of wood bows along its annual rings even if it was well dried to begin with.** The same applies to a wide board.
- Such a piece with a natural edge is a **slab («слэб»)** — **which she notes is currently out of fashion anyway**, especially combined with epoxy.
- **⚠️ The remedy is narrow lamellae: the smaller the pieces a glued panel («щит») is built from, the less it deforms. Choose lamellae 5–9 cm wide.**

## Materials — finishes, where she is most emphatic

- **⚠️ Oil, not lacquer. This is the central prescription of the source.**
- **Why lacquer fails, with the mechanism**: it forms a **film** that protects only while it remains hermetic. **Any crack lets moisture in — and it cannot evaporate back out.** That is where rot starts. **Even the most expensive or industrial lacquer performs worse than oil.**
- **Yacht lacquer is not an exception** despite being made for prolonged water exposure — moisture still gets under it, **and it yellows the timber unattractively**, producing a look she compares to a faded Soviet doll.
- **Enamel has the same failure**: scratches form easily and moisture enters through them.
- **Stain («морилка») needs a further coating over it, usually lacquer** — and because stain does not penetrate deeply, **any scratch or chip is quite visible.**
- **⚠️ Why oil works, stated as a mechanism rather than a preference**: it soaks into the pores and, applied correctly, **the wood takes up as much as it can, so there is no room left for moisture.** And crucially, **if moisture does get in, nothing prevents it evaporating back out.** Oiled items last considerably longer.
- **Oiled furniture is also easy to repair** — sand the damaged spot and re-oil. **The condition is not to delay and not to leave wood unprotected.**

## Materials — what is worth making from wood, and what is not

- **⚠️ Wooden basins — she rejects them, in unusually strong terms**: "я не считаю, что они стильные, и, на мой взгляд, они даже хуже, чем садовые гномики." A glossy one is clearly heavily lacquered and its lifespan is unknown. **An uncoated wooden basin lasts a couple of weeks and turns slimy "как деревяшка в реке."** Her rule for sanitaryware: **"лучше не искать приключений — быть как все. Выбирайте керамику."**
- **Wooden baths**: also "любители экстремальной экзотики." The surface shows the individual pieces it is built from — they are very rarely one solid piece — so it reads variegated, closer to a cheap glued panel. **Her wry framing is actually a real observation about register: it is a good technique if you want to spend at luxury level while lowering the ostentation** (she tells the two-New-Russians-and-a-tie joke to make the point).
- **⚠️ Worktops and vanity units: choose oak, treat it thoroughly with impregnation, and wipe water off as soon as it lands.** This is her recommended application.
- **Consoles survive with proper care.**
- **⚠️ Barn board («амбарная доска») — a specific caution.** It is reclaimed from something dismantled and deliberately barely processed, or it loses its charm. **But it is often so warped that even a purpose-cut recess for the basin base does not save it.** Old barn board can crumble and carries deep cracks and holes where moisture, dust and dirt collect **and cannot be cleaned out.** Her framing is the general principle: **"если вы покупаете предмет с историей, то вы покупаете и всё, что к этой истории прилагается."**
  - **Two clean alternatives**: take a flat surface with no patina at all, or **imitate the aged look** — the cracks are then shallower, freshly coated, and easier to maintain.
- **⚠️ Small wooden items tolerate humidity considerably better than large ones.** Mirror frames, ladders, stools, short shelves keep their appearance well in wet rooms. Care is the same as a worktop: wipe them so they are not wet, and **do not let them stand in a puddle.**

## Mistakes / Warnings

- **Beech in a bathroom.**
- **Any lacquer, enamel or stain-plus-lacquer finish** where oil was available.
- **Rigidly fixing any large wooden surface**, and building joinery tight against a washing machine.
- **Wooden shelves close to the bath** — a maintenance trap rather than a material failure.
- **Wooden basins**, and uncoated wood in contact with water at all.
- **Solid wide boards and slabs** where narrow lamellae would have been stable.
- **Barn board with deep cracks** near a basin.
