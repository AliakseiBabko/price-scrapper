## Cost Drivers — her five drivers of premium cost

1. **Finish-material tier.** Her illustrations: chain-retailer wallpaper → a mid-priced factory → hand-painted silk at the top; standard 30×30 to 60×60 tile → large-format porcelain slabs. **⚠️ The mechanism she names for large-format porcelain is threefold and it is the extractable part: production is much more expensive, *and* installation requires special tools, special materials and specialist crews, *and* the visual gain is fewer joints — "целая стена является одним слэбом."** Also: cheap tile's printed stone pattern is visibly pixelated where large-format is not.
2. **Furnishing/contents tier** — furniture, lighting, decor. **⚠️ Her stated components of expensive furniture, useful as a checklist**: the **design itself** (named designers behind long-established factories); **upholstery fabric** (more wear-resistant, more pleasant to touch, better pattern or texture, more natural); and **construction quality** — the fine legs on Italian or Scandinavian chairs that are nonetheless very strong. **⚠️ She explicitly notes country of origin is no longer the signal** — named Russian factories are not much cheaper than Italian or Danish. **On replicas: a 3,000-RUB designer-lookalike chair from a marketplace is "как поддельный Louis Vuitton в переходе метро."**
3. **⚠️ Workmanship and concealed engineering.** Her visible markers of premium work, worth keeping as an acceptance checklist: floor laid perfectly with perfect board joints; **porcelain mitred at external corners**; all grout joints equal and cleanly finished with no bulges or hollows; walls under paint "гладкие, как яичная скорлупа," with corners sharp enough to cut yourself on; neat mouldings, shadow profiles and baseboards; doors sitting "как влитые". **⚠️ Her economic explanation for why such trades cost multiples is a real mechanism: a tiler laying tile at 20,000 RUB/m² (≈$220/m², same basis) carries the breakage-replacement risk on that material** — working with expensive, demanding materials requires a qualification and is priced accordingly. **On the engineering side she names specifics**: GOST-compliant cable of the correct cross-section for the right load point, **REHAU pipe rather than polypropylene**, reliable valves, filtration systems, water-hammer protection, and leak protection with many sensors. **Shadow baseboard costs materially more than ordinary purely because the work is slower and more painstaking.**
4. **Individualisation.** Budget housing looks broadly alike; premium is unique because the client's own habits drive it. **⚠️ The concrete instances she names are the useful part — each is a survey question that changes a layout**: whether and what the client cooks (drives kitchen composition beyond "standard hob, oven, dishwasher"); bathroom configuration; whether guests come or the client is an introvert; **which staff enter the home — cleaner, nanny, workers — and how those processes flow.** Her marker of success: a new client saying they dislike none of her existing interiors, which she treats as a compliment because it means each was individual.
5. **Process, and the two supervision roles.** **⚠️ Premium delivery requires both авторский надзор (the project author's own supervision) and технический надзор (an independent expert checking construction quality) — and she states her own studio maintains an internal independent technical supervision function that checks its own builders.** The full sequence she names: design project → construction, with author's supervision in parallel → technical supervision engaged → engineering specialists (ventilation, low-voltage) integrated → scheduled procurement so materials arrive on time → acceptance → cleaning → installations → snagging → decoration.

## Cost Drivers — the budget-allocation principle

- **⚠️ Her stated trap, and it is the opposite of what a premium studio is expected to say: "даже если у вас очень много денег… всё самое хорошее ≠ самое дорогое."** Find the price/quality balance, decide what is genuinely a priority, and **be deliberate about what merely needs to be done decently and then left alone.**
- **⚠️ Two worked allocation examples**: a wooden floor that is real wood but the simplest kind, **paired with a "wow" kitchen**; or the whole accent on the living room with other rooms simpler and **wardrobes built from LDSP because the client does not care about them.**
- **Her framing: there are no genuinely unlimited budgets — there is always a frame, and the work is maximising quality inside it.** Directly relevant to this project's own tier-allocation reasoning.

## Planning Rules — where full design management is NOT worth buying

**⚠️ The most valuable item in the source, because it argues against her own service:**

- **A simple, low-budget renovation does not need comprehensive project management.** Her reasoning follows from her own five drivers: **a simple interior has simpler technical solutions** (painted walls, laminate), **no complex engineering, and no expensive heavy-to-install materials** — "с таким заказчик в целом может справиться самостоятельно."
- **⚠️ But she draws the line precisely: "придумать, сделать дизайн — скорее нет, а вот разобраться с ремонтом такого уровня сможет любая ремонтная бригада."** The designer's value at that level is **the design and the drawings, not the management.**
- **What her comfort-segment studio therefore delivers instead**, and it is a useful description of a minimum viable design engagement: **a detailed set of drawings any builders can work from, plus a guide to self-managing the project — what to buy when, how to supervise the builders, and what to watch for.** Aimed at flats of ~40–80 m² on a small budget.
- **Her closing advice**: match ambitions to the property and the wallet, and do it well *for your own situation and budget*.
