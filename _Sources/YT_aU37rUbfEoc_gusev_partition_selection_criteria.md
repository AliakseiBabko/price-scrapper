## Materials — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Two profile material families**: a wood-look profile (described as
  effectively an MDF/particleboard-core profile with a thin strip of solid
  wood inset in the middle purely to resist warping — not solid wood
  despite the "wooden profile" name) and an aluminum profile. Aluminum is
  now the more popular choice.
- **⚠️ Economy vs. premium aluminum profile — a real quality distinction
  independent of "aluminum" as a category label**: an economy aluminum
  profile is visually bulky/wide but thin-walled; a premium-class profile
  looks thin/minimal from the edge but is actually thick-walled
  (anodized) — the thick-walled premium profile is stated to have a
  materially longer service life than the thin-walled economy one, even
  though the economy version can look "more substantial" at a glance.
  This is a genuinely new material-quality axis not yet recorded on
  [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Sliding_Partition_Mechanisms|Sliding Partition Mechanisms]],
  which covers mounting/hardware mechanism detail but not profile-wall
  thickness as a durability signal.
- **Paint/finish quality — anodizing is the stated top tier**: anodizing
  is a chemical-etching process (the aluminum profile is submerged in an
  acid bath, causing it to oxidize) that yields the premium color/finish
  options (bronze, gold, and others) and is framed as the top achievable
  paint-quality tier. Contrasted directly against low-end competitors
  described as painting cheap Chinese aluminum with makeshift equipment
  and selling it far under any price a genuinely quality partition could
  be made for.
- **Glass type — tempered vs. triplex, both framed as acceptable, plain
  (non-safety) glass is not**: tempered glass ("закалённое") is required
  at minimum — if it breaks, it shatters into small, non-hazardous
  granules rather than sharp shards, explicitly framed as important with
  small children in the home (a dropped toy or bumped chair is cited as a
  realistic real-world impact scenario). Triplex (laminated glass, two
  glass layers bonded with an interlayer film, the same construction used
  in automotive windshields) is the alternative — if it breaks, the glass
  stays adhered to the film in a cracked "spiderweb" pattern rather than
  falling apart. This is a more specific two-way glass-type breakdown than
  this store's existing general "thick laminated/tempered glass helps
  sound insulation" note from `YT_xnLiADJF5lw` — worth cross-referencing,
  not duplicating.
- **Mechanism quality — Italian vs. Chinese, a stated but unquantified
  reliability claim**: the large majority of the market uses Chinese
  sliding mechanisms, which this source states leads to a wobbly/loose-
  feeling partition over time; Italian mechanisms are named as the
  higher-quality alternative, described as noticeably smoother and softer
  in motion. `single-account`, `unverified` — no specific brand, failure
  mode, or comparative data given beyond this general claim, distinct from
  the DIY-hobbyist floor-pin/mechanism detail already recorded elsewhere
  on the deep-mechanism page.
- **Buying-channel advice — prefer a multi-brand showroom over a single-
  manufacturer showroom**: a showroom tied to one manufacturer has a
  structural incentive to push that manufacturer's product regardless of
