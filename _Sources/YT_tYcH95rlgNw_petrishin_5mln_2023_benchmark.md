## Rules / Heuristics — New Techniques and a Real QC Mistake

4. **Hexagonal-tile-to-quartz-vinyl transition, with a named expansion
   joint**: hexagonal porcelain tile in the entry (chosen specifically
   so wet shoes can be safely set down without damaging the floor)
   meets quartz-vinyl flooring through a **cork compensator/expansion
   strip** at the transition line.
5. **Thin (6mm) porcelain tile laid at 45° in hexagonal format, an
   explicitly named difficulty**: the tiler had real difficulty
   achieving a clean 45° hexagonal layout specifically because the
   porcelain tile was unusually thin (6mm, not standard thickness) —
   flagged directly as a harder execution than the same pattern in a
   thicker tile.
6. **⚠️ Grout-staining risk on light tile even with ordinary
   (non-epoxy) grout**: after grouting a light-colored tile backsplash
   with black cement-based grout (not two-component epoxy), wiping off
   excess grout revealed the light tile had absorbed dark pigment from
   the grout — an unexpected staining risk that the crew hadn't
   assumed was possible with ordinary cement grout specifically (epoxy
   grout is more commonly flagged for this risk). A stain-removal
   product was found and applied as an urgent fix. Worth flagging as a
   real risk to test/sample before committing to a dark-grout/light-
   tile combination, not just with dark epoxy grout.
7. **Client-sourced custom DIY accent-wall tile panel, an unusual
   sourcing technique**: the client found and ordered a modular
   decorative wall-tile/panel product via an Instagram vendor —
   individually-sized tile elements shippable in three thickness tiers,
   freely combinable/oriented by the buyer to build a custom accent
   wall pattern, then installed it himself. A concrete example of a
   client sourcing and installing a decorative feature independently of
   the general contractor, worth knowing as an option for a DIY-inclined
   client on a self-managed or hybrid project.
8. **Utility-cabinet aesthetic access hatch, painted to blend**: the
   entry-hall electrical/low-voltage utility cabinet sits behind a
   painted "invisible hatch" (люк-невидимка) rather than a visible
   access panel — consistent with, and a concrete named-product example
   of, this project's existing "non-sagging, purpose-built access
   hatch" guidance for utility/collector nodes.
9. **Real material-logistics hassle example**: running short one piece
   of a specific metal balcony slat mid-installation required a
   dedicated trip to a wholesale market (not sold in retail stores),
   only reachable during weekday working hours — the foreman had to
   personally request time off and coordinate building access to make
   the run. A concrete illustration of why buffer stock/over-ordering on
   a distinctive material can save real schedule time.
