## Materials / Mistakes-Warnings — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Zemskov's four-channel taxonomy for where flooring is sold, with a stated recommendation ranking**:
  1. **Market-stall vendors** — per Zemskov, now nearly extinct, existing mainly to catch buyers who wrongly assume an informal market stall is cheaper than a real retailer; his claim is a stall vendor is just a reseller/middleman and is actually *more* expensive, not less.
  2. **Online-only stores with no physical presence** (no warehouse, no warranty service, no installers of their own) — Zemskov's warning: their lower prices reflect having no overhead or accountability; his stated real risk is that such a store can simply rebrand under a new name once a bad batch/complaint volume becomes a liability, leaving no recourse for affected buyers. Recommends avoiding these regardless of price.
  3. **Specialized flooring retailers** (stores that deal specifically in flooring, not general hardware) — Zemskov's stated pros: typically the highest prices and (his claim) the most aggressive in-store upselling of whatever they need to move from inventory, **but** they employ their own installers, offer a combined product-plus-installation warranty, and — his central claim — have no incentive to sell a spec-downgraded ("левак") product, since their business survives entirely on reputation/repeat business (including his own company's recurring bulk purchases) and a single bad batch getting out would risk that reputation. **This is Zemskov's own stated recommended channel**, and he names his own "Zems Baza" (zemsbaza.ru) vetted-supplier directory (four flooring-specific listings, each specializing in a different product category — quartz vinyl, engineered board, laminate, etc.) as where he sources these recommendations — flagged as self-promotional, not adopted as a neutral store-level recommendation on its own.
  4. **Large chain hypermarkets** — Zemskov's central warning (the video's main content): flooring sold here can look, per the packaging, brand, and product name, **identical** to the same product sold at a specialized retailer, while priced roughly 1.5-2× cheaper — and per Zemskov this isn't a real bargain but a **manufacturer-produced "special series" for that specific chain**, made under the same packaging/branding but to a deliberately lower spec (his cited example: a wear-class rating of 31, or "31.5," where the standard-channel product is rated class 33 — a real, checkable, but easy-to-miss difference, since Zemskov states the downgrade is sometimes not even disclosed on the packaging and is only detectable by close side-by-side spec comparison). Real cited consequences of the downgraded product, per Zemskov: laminate that swells from as little as two drops of water; quartz vinyl that develops a strong odor even without underfloor heating, and wears through within a described short time under normal foot traffic with a small amount of dirt; engineered board that warps at a mere 1.2% humidity swing.
- **Zemskov's stated real-world consequence of falling for this**: buyers who bought the cheaper hypermarket version, then discovered the defect, end up paying more overall than if they'd bought the full-spec product initially — they still have to remove furniture, pull baseboards, discard the failed flooring, and install a proper replacement, on top of the original purchase cost.
- **Practical buying rule distilled from this source**: when a flooring product at a chain hypermarket is priced dramatically below the same-branded product at a specialized retailer, treat the price gap itself as the signal to check for a "special series"/spec-downgrade — compare the packaging's technical specs (wear class rating, water-resistance rating) line by line rather than assuming identical branding means identical product.
