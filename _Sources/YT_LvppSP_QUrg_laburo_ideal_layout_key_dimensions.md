## Quantities / Measurements — Durable Facts & Rules

**General circulation:**
- **Corridor width: 90cm absolute minimum; 110-120cm preferred for a
  long corridor** (comfortable to walk).
- **Interior door width standard: 70cm minimum**, used for bathrooms/WCs
  where this is "critically important"; 80-90cm preferred where space
  allows; wider still ("no limit," practitioner's words) for larger
  homes. **⚠️ Explicit past-mistake caution**: a 60cm door was previously
  specified for wet rooms in this firm's own past projects and is now
  called categorically wrong — a standard washing machine is often
  slightly wider than the resulting ~60cm-minus-frame clear opening and
  may not physically fit through.

**Living room:**
- Sofa placement: leave at least 5-10cm gap from a wall even when a
  sofa reads as "against the wall" (its own bulk/softness needs the
  clearance); a walk-behind path (sofa positioned centrally with
  circulation behind it) should be ≥70cm; distance between two sofas or
  a sofa and a coffee table can be as tight as 30-40cm since that's a
  feet-only path, not a full-body one.
- **⚠️ TV-viewing distance, a third independent flat-figure data point**:
  2.5m to 4m from the eye-level point on the sofa back to the screen —
  a third source giving a flat-meter range, alongside the two already
  noted on `Living_Room_Layout_and_Furniture_Sizing.md` ("some sources
  cite 3-3.5m flat" vs. this page's own preferred 4×-diagonal rule of
  thumb). Also corroborates the existing mounting-height caution: don't
  mount the TV too high, size/position it so a seated viewer doesn't
  have to tilt their head up.
- Curtain-line allowance: budget 20-25cm of wall depth for curtain
  hardware/fabric thickness in front of any window — don't draw a sofa
  or other furniture flush to a window wall without this allowance
  (applies beyond just the living room).

**Kitchen:**
- **Counter/backsplash height standard: 90cm** (average build) —
  explicitly adjustable up to 95-100cm for a taller household, framed as
  personalization rather than a fixed rule. **A different flat figure
  than the Zlobin-sourced domestic-standard range of 80-85cm already on
  file** (`03_Kitchen/Kitchen_Furniture.md` Cabinet Dimensioning
  Standards) — two independent practitioner figures for the same
  "domestic standard" concept, worth citing both since they don't fully
  agree (LA BURO's 90cm sits at the *top* of Zlobin's 80-85cm domestic
  range, closer to Zlobin's *international* 90-93cm figure) — a real,
  if modest, discrepancy between two Russian-market sources.
- Kitchen with an island: minimum 90cm walk clearance around the island
  for cabinet doors to open comfortably; keep the gap between the main
  counter run and an island ≤120cm (especially if the island holds a
  sink/cooktop) to avoid an extra step while cooking; for a G/U-shaped
  kitchen with an island, keep ≥90cm clearance between fridge and
  island too.
- Standard counter depth 60cm; add 10-20cm extra depth specifically for
  an island counter, especially one with a sink or cooktop (splash
  containment).

**Dining:**
- Table-to-wall/furniture clearance: 90cm minimum, 100-110cm preferred.
- Standard seat width allowance 60cm per diner — wider for upholstered
  "half-chair"/"half-armchair" seating (a current trend, per this
  source); check chair + table-leg clearance together so chairs can
  actually push in under the table.

**Bedroom:**
- Bed-side walk clearance: 65cm minimum each side; increase to 70-80cm
  on the side with an adjacent wardrobe (door-swing/use clearance).
  Curtain-line allowance (20-25cm) applies here too. Foot-of-bed
  clearance can shrink to 50-55cm.
- **⚠️ Bed-frame sizing caution**: mattress width/length is *not* the
  bed's overall footprint — add ~5cm per side for the frame, and up to
  20-25cm more if the bed has a large/bulky headboard.

**Storage (wardrobes, closets, dressers):**
- **Swing-door wardrobe depth: 60-65cm; sliding-door wardrobe: 70cm**
  (sliding needs the extra depth for the door-track mechanism itself) —
  corroborates the clothing-zone 60cm figure already on file at
  `14_Furniture/analysis/Wardrobe_Vocabulary_and_Sizing.md`, and adds the
  swing-vs-sliding distinction that page didn't previously carry.
  Shelving/dresser units: 45-50cm depth — deeper makes the back row
  genuinely hard to reach/use.
  Single-wall walk-in wardrobe: 130cm minimum room width; double-sided
  (wardrobes on both walls) walk-in: 80cm minimum aisle between them.

**Bathroom:**
- **⚠️ Minimum walk-in shower stall: 90×90cm**; if one side must be
  narrower than 80cm, compensate by lengthening the other side to
  ≥120cm rather than shrinking both dimensions. Shower door: standard
  opens outward; an inward-opening shower door needs the stall itself
  to be ≥130cm on its long side to leave room to enter and swing the
  door.
- **Double-sink vanity needs ≥140cm of vanity length** to use both
  sinks comfortably at once.
- Standard sink height 85cm, adjustable to 90cm for a taller household;
  shower-head height: manufacturer-recommended 210cm, adjustable to
  220-230cm for a taller household.
- **⚠️ Toilet-between-two-walls clearance: 85-90cm minimum width**, front
  clearance (back wall to the leading edge of a usable path) ≥120cm
  preferred.
- **⚠️ Washing-machine niche/cabinet sizing**: standard machines are
  60×60cm — plan the niche/cabinet at **65×65cm minimum**, not
  exactly 60×60cm, for real-world clearance margin.
