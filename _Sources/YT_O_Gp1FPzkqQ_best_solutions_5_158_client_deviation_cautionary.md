## Durable Facts

- **Coordinate entrance-door and interior-door trim material at the point of purchase, not later** — entrance-door and interior-door hardware are typically different manufacturers, so their casing/reveal materials can mismatch at the end of a project unless the same finish is deliberately specified for both up front. Genuinely new cross-vendor coordination rule.
- **Real regret case: a client cut a designed built-in countertop (meant to conceal a front-loading washing machine) to save ≈15,000 RUB**, and would now pay ≈50,000 RUB to retrofit it after the fact — a concrete "small savings during construction, large cost to fix after" data point reinforcing (with real numbers) this store's general design-integrity-vs-cost-cutting theme.
- **Every entryway needs a dedicated makeup-mirror lighting zone**, separate from a general full-length mirror — a full-length mirror alone isn't adequately lit for checking grooming/makeup before leaving. Extends this store's existing mirror-lighting-color-match content with a placement/provisioning requirement.
- **Real installer-error case: a heated towel rail was mounted off the tile-row centerline because the crew didn't read the design documentation**, which explicitly specified centering — cited as leverage a client can use (a hired designer's documented spec gives the client the right to demand a redo at the installer's cost when it's ignored).
- **Video door intercom is a mandatory feature of any well-executed renovation** — stated rationale: so a resident never needs to check a peephole or lean out a window to identify a visitor.
- **Floor-drain slope-direction rule when the drain (трап) is offset from the wall**: the floor must slope toward the drain from *both* directions around it, not a single uniform slope — a single uniform slope leaves a low point away from the drain where water pools, causing mold/mildew. A specific, checkable installation-quality error the practitioner reports seeing repeatedly.
- **Budget lifehack: hide network/server equipment in a cheap small wall-mounted box ("birdhouse"-style) as an interim solution** if a proper built-in server-closet cabinet isn't affordable yet — a real client-suggested, low-cost workaround, explicitly credited to the client on camera.
- **Underfloor-heating thermostats for a bathroom/WC must be mounted outside the room, at a comfortable operating height** — corroborates this store's existing `yt_uwXBHuWPUIo` visibility-first placement rule.
- **Window-adjacent workstation, extended rationale**: beyond this store's existing windowsill-desk-plus-blackout-blinds rule, this source adds a distinct mechanism — a convector's warm airflow blown into a seated person's face (when a desk sits directly at the window) dries the eyes and accelerates eyesight strain, in addition to the already-recorded condensation and constant-daylight concerns. Only build a window-desk as a last resort.
- **Windowsill-desk height must be lowered from standard windowsill height to match a standard chair** (a standard-height windowsill is too tall to work at comfortably; a bar-stool alternative causes leg fatigue), **and electrical points must be planned specifically for that lowered position** — real regret case: a client cancelled the planned lowered-desk millwork mid-project to save money but never told the designer, so the pre-planned outlets remained fixed at the position meant for the now-cancelled lowered desk, ending up visibly misplaced relative to the simplified windowsill that was actually built.
- **Custom convector grille ordering (Moscow)**: numerous companies offer bespoke convector-grille fabrication — color, width, flange/return-edge profile, and cutout pattern (florals, geometric, etc.) matched to different design styles. Low-detail sourcing note, no brand named.
- **Frameless glass shower-divider cleaning burden, real data point**: a client spent ≈1.5-2 hours cleaning fingerprint/water streaks off one frameless glass panel on move-in day, including improvising a toothbrush-wrapped-in-cloth tool to reach an inaccessible corner seam. **A genuinely new real maintenance-time data point** for the glass-vs-curtain shower-divider trade-off (glass = design impact + high cleaning burden; curtain = low maintenance + reduced design impact) — the client's own conclusion on camera was regret at not choosing the lower-maintenance option knowing this in advance.
- **Multi-room passage/hallway switch topology, worked example**: a hallway connecting an entry, kids' room, and a shared living/kitchen space needs (at minimum) three separate switches so a person moving through never has to backtrack to turn off a light behind them — extends this store's existing two-way-switch content into an explicit ≥3-switch chained-passage topology with a full worked walkthrough.
- **Never add a pass-through switch immediately outside a bathroom/WC that already has its own two switches (main + duty light)** — a third switch there is unintuitive (flipped behind the person as they leave) and risks confusing the switch group; residents simply use one of the bathroom's own two switches instead. Concrete switch-group-design nuance, avoiding over-provisioning near a WC.
- **Duty/night light must exist inside the bathroom/WC itself, not only in the connecting hallway** — two common mistakes flagged: omitting a duty light entirely, or only lighting the hallway/passage and forgetting the bathroom needs its own, forcing a resident to use the blinding main bathroom light at night. **Correct implementation**: cover all rooms needing duty lighting on one shared switch. Extends this store's existing duty/night-light content with the WC-specific gap.
- **Track-lighting (шинопровод) minimum busbar width for a room's main light source: 38mm.** Most designers/architects reportedly spec 20-25mm rails, which can only carry weaker fixtures inadequate as a room's primary light source — resulting in an attractive ceiling that still leaves the room under-lit. **Genuinely new, concrete numeric spec** — first track-lighting content in this store.
- **Multi-zone (studio-style) room lighting/switch plan, worked example**: a combined kitchen/bedroom/office space needs a separate switch per functional zone (kitchen zone, bed/desk zone) so using one zone at night doesn't require walking to another zone's switch; at the bed itself, one central-light switch reachable from bed plus independently-switched bedside lamps on each side — corroborates and restates this store's existing bedside dual-switch content with a fuller worked multi-zone example.
- **Avoid a single light fixture with 3 fixed cycled color-temperature presets (cool/neutral/warm via repeated clicking)** — every use requires cycling through unwanted tones to reach the desired one; prefer a single-tone, single-mode fixture with a straightforward on/off switch instead. Concrete product-avoidance recommendation.
- **Real cautionary case: unplanned late loggia join without consulting the designer causes compounding physical-junction defects.** Client's design initially kept the loggia unjoined (explicitly declined at design stage); months later, decided independently to join it without re-engaging the company. Consequences, all attributed to the lack of 3D pre-modeling: (1) kitchen cabinetry meets the new opening-trim edge with zero gap (should have been offset ~40mm to leave room for a 40mm wallpaper strip so cabinetry doesn't crash directly into the trim); (2) the loggia-side countertop had to be recut **three times** — first cut too narrow (only 3mm overhang) not accounting for the loggia wall-cladding thickness, second cut still too narrow after forgetting the ~12mm opening-trim thickness, and a further redo still needed. **Concrete numeric rule from the company's stated "knowledge base"**: a countertop must overhang the adjoining wall/trim assembly by a **minimum of 40mm** to correctly seat trim up to ~20mm thick without the countertop edge appearing to float/hang unsupported. **Genuinely new, specific numeric rule for this store's loggia-join and countertop-fabrication content**, and a strong real-world argument for 3D pre-modeling before any loggia join, planned or late-added.
- **Bathroom leak-sensor count tied to floor-level choice, refined**: a bathroom floor lowered relative to adjacent rooms (code-compliant, permit-friendly) needs at least one leak sensor; a **level (flush) floor needs two** leak sensors, since there's no natural low point guaranteeing a single sensor catches a leak. Extends this store's existing leak-sensor-placement content with a concrete sensor-count rule tied to floor-level choice.
- **Laminate-under-underfloor-heating buckling risk (this store's existing ~50/50 "lottery" heuristic), refined**: even in a small apartment, laminate should still be split into separate zones/loops per room rather than run as one continuous loop, to reduce (not eliminate) buckling risk — regardless of apartment size, and especially when an adjoining bathroom floor is flush-level (added moisture/heat interaction). Quartz-vinyl remains safe to lay in one continuous loop.
- **Curtain-pelmet channel + shadow-gap stretch-ceiling combination technique**: older technique wraps stretch-ceiling fabric fully to the wall then plasters/patches a curtain-rod recess afterward (leaves a visible seam) — modern technique uses a specialized recessed curtain-rod pelmet channel that the stretch-ceiling fabric deliberately stops short of, simultaneously concealing the curtain rod *and* preserving a shadow-gap "floating ceiling" effect that would otherwise be mutually exclusive with curtain concealment. **Genuinely new detailed ceiling-finishing technique.**
- **Vet a stretch-ceiling contractor by whether they leave access hatches near track-lighting transformers/drivers/control units** — these components periodically fail (not lifetime-rated) regardless of price tier; a contractor who conceals everything with no access panel forces full stretch-ceiling demolition and reinstallation for every future repair (described as effectively a recurring "subscription fee"). Concrete, checkable vetting/red-flag heuristic.
- **Joining a loggia with flush (level) flooring to the main apartment usually requires raising the loggia floor 10-15cm**, achievable in an estimated ~90% of apartments — but occasionally the *whole apartment's* floor must be raised instead (when the loggia can't be raised enough on its own), a real cited cost of "several hundred thousand rubles" just for the extra screed material/delivery, and a practical downside: the finished floor can end up higher than the building's own shared-corridor entry threshold, forcing a step *down* leaving the unit. Extends this store's existing loggia-floor-level content with a real cost/consequence example.
- **Sliding-door (шкаф-купе) wardrobes are cheaper than framed/corpus wardrobes of the same footprint** — no back/top/bottom panel fabrication needed since the surrounding wall/niche does that structural job (a cost the client would pay anyway for wall finishing regardless of wardrobe type) — plus more space-efficient internal use and generally more reliable hardware than corpus alternatives. Always plan a purpose-built ceiling box above a corpus wardrobe if that type is chosen instead.
- **Temporary baseboard technique with pre-planned future cut zones**: since clients routinely run out of budget and buy furniture much later than the renovation finishes, install temporary baseboard everywhere (even walls that will eventually be covered by furniture) so the apartment doesn't look unfinished for the intervening year or two — but **pre-mark/plan the exact points where a later furniture-install crew will need to cut the baseboard to fit cabinetry**, so that crew cuts cleanly at the planned relief point instead of tearing the baseboard (and the wallpaper glued over its edge) at an arbitrary spot. Genuinely new technique combining two known facts (temp baseboard default; furniture arrives late) into a specific pre-marking step not previously recorded.
- **Buy spare finish-electrical wall-plate frames when purchasing finish electrical** — real anecdote: a frame was bought same-day (30 minutes before filming) because the original supply ran out after one was crushed/stepped on during install; restates this store's general "buy hardware reserve" purchasing heuristic with a memorable concrete case.
- **Plan TV-wall outlet position specifically behind the actual planned TV location**, not just "somewhere on the TV wall" — a real mistake shown where outlets had no relation to the eventual TV position, forcing visible cable "spaghetti" on day one of use. Extends this store's existing TV-cable-channel-provisioning content with an outlet-positioning specificity requirement.
- **Kids-room door swing exception, extended**: an unusually wide door swing (~180°, vs. the ~95° default already recorded elsewhere in this store) is deliberate when a large wardrobe will occupy the space a normal door stop would otherwise use — visible in advance from the wardrobe's ceiling box. **Wardrobe interior lighting must always be planned in advance** — flagged as frequently forgotten by designers.
- **Fresh-air supply unit ("breather") wall-placement trade-off, two real options presented (brand-agnostic)**: (1) surface-mounted near a window — attractive/tech-forward appearance, but conflicts with curtain operation, forcing curtains to be shifted well away from the window and wasting floor space; (2) recessed into a built technical niche — avoids the curtain conflict and floor-space loss, at the cost of always-visible exposed niche corners that look slightly unfinished (a plastic corner trim there looks worse, so the niche is simply accepted as permanently curtain-covered). Presented as the lesser of two evils versus losing usable floor space. Genuinely new, concrete installation-trade-off content (distinct from this store's existing brand-preference/full-system-vs-breather content).
