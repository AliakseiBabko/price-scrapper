## Plumbing — Real Two-Bathroom Rough-In Walkthrough

1. **Riser insulation, brand-named**: both hot and cold risers wrapped
   in color-coded Energoflex-type foam pipe insulation immediately
   distinguishing hot/cold/towel-warmer-feed lines at a glance.
2. **⚠️ Leak-protection system trigger scenarios, stated directly**: the
   under-sink sensor is framed around three realistic real-world
   triggers — a running tap left unattended (e.g. answering a phone
   call), a child playing with the tap and leaving it on, or a sink's
   overflow drain being unable to keep up with an open tap — any of
   these causes water to rise past the sink's own overflow, at which
   point the sensor cuts **both** hot and cold supply completely. A
   separate control unit/sensor-panel install and live demo are promised
   for a later episode (not in this video).
3. **Check-valve mechanism, restated with a specific real-world cause**:
   installed to prevent hot water being force-pushed backward into the
   cold-water line — most commonly caused by a hygienic-shower/bidet
   mixer.
4. **⚠️ Fine-filter/reducer material distinction by water temperature,
   brand-named**: Honeywell fine filter + pressure reducer used on both
   lines, but **the cold-water unit has a glass bowl/bulb housing while
   the manufacturer specifically recommends a metal (iron) housing for
   the hot-water unit** — implicitly because glass isn't rated for
   sustained hot-water temperature exposure the way metal is.
5. **Geberit installation-frame detail**: a wall-hung-toilet frame
   ("инсталляция," brand Geberit) had its mounting angle-bracket
   pre-installed and fixed into the wall ahead of the frame itself,
   specifically for mounting reliability.
6. **Concealed-mixer hygienic-shower plumbing**: a concealed
   ("скрытый") mixer valve pre-mixes hot/cold water internally in the
   wall before it reaches the hygienic-shower outlet — the visible
   shower fixture itself receives already-mixed water, not separate
   hot/cold lines.
7. **Second bathroom variant**: same component stack, plus a
   water-fed heated towel rail ("полотенцесушитель") on this unit's own
   riser branch.
8. **⚠️ Real cable-under-straightedge screed-QC demonstration mechanism
   — genuinely new, not previously recorded on this vault**: to
   demonstrate why beacon-setting precision matters, the practitioner
   places a piece of insulated electrical wire (~2.5mm cross-section)
   under a straightedge laid across the screed and presses down,
   visibly showing how even a 2-3mm beacon-height error would create a
   rocking/flexing gap under a future rigid floor covering (laminate,
   parquet, solid board) — the real mechanism behind "an uneven screed
   causes floor creaking," made physically visible rather than just
   asserted.
9. **Real screed-evenness QC result on this object**: a 3-meter
   straightedge check across multiple rooms found deviation under 1mm,
   credited directly to the crew (named: Андрей, Виталик, Володя) taking
   real time and care at the beacon-setting stage.
10. **⚠️ Hand-poured (non-mechanized) screed room-sequencing risk,
    generalized as a common industry mistake**: pouring screed room by
    room with hand-mixed material (rather than a mechanized continuous
    pour) commonly produces a level mismatch at the room-to-room/
    corridor threshold, since each room is finished independently —
    this object's execution was praised as an exception (seamless
    transitions, no gap), but the underlying risk is stated as common
    industry practice generally, not just a hypothetical.
