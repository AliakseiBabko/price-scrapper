## Lighting — the central mechanism, and it explains several existing vault rules

- **⚠️ A bright unshielded source in the field of view makes the eye adapt down, and you stop seeing the room.** A bare lamp has no optics and emits over roughly **350°**; when it enters your vision the eye darkens its own response, "и ты уже не видишь пространство." **His conclusion: "ни одна лампочка за тридцать рублей может убить дизайн за несколько миллионов."**
- **⚠️ His named pet hate is the bare-bulb glass globe («шар»)** — he concedes the form factor is beautiful and fits any interior, **but the moment the bulb is lit in the evening "ты не видишь шары, ты видишь горящую лампочку"**, and every decision about wallpaper, curtains and sofa becomes invisible. This is the general mechanism applied to the most fashionable fixture.
- **⚠️ "Люстра — не прибор освещения."** A chandelier, surface-mounted sconce or decorative pendant is **first of all a decorative element that also glows.** Treating it as the room's lighting device is "большая ошибка."
- **⚠️ And the specific compounding error: designers build the entire lighting scheme around the chandelier — "колоссальная ошибка."** The mechanism: **a chandelier flattens the design.** "У тебя разные цвета, разные паттерны на стенах — врубила люстру, всё одинаково, свет равномерно спустился везде. **Люстра — убийца интерьера.**"
- **⚠️ So the less light a chandelier gives, the better** — and he says so in direct answer to Кузина asking whether it is legitimate to hang a chandelier that barely lights. **"Люстра не должна светить. Люстра должна выглядеть круто, красиво и уместно в интерьере."** *(She reaches the same conclusion independently in `Dd_pUAvzqew`, from a designer's premises rather than an engineer's — worth recording as convergence.)*
- **⚠️ "Чем меньше света, тем лучше. Не надо много света в квартире."** Stated flatly, against the intuition that more is better.

## Lighting — colour temperature, with a physiological mechanism

- **⚠️ Why colour temperatures must not be mixed within one room, and this is the mechanism the vault's existing rule has been missing.** "По физиологии, когда ты видишь разную цветовую температуру в одном помещении... **фокус внимания раздваивается**" — you stop reading it as a single space. A well-designed room works because it is executed in one aesthetic; **mixing temperatures "разбивает пространство на две части."**
  - **Scoped exception: where there is a genuine functional split.** His example — a workout corner with a punchbag under cold light and a separate calm zone under warm — is a scenario distinction, not a mistake.
- **⚠️ Cold light aids focus, physiologically** — which is why offices use it. He accepts a large open-plan office legitimately using cold light so productivity-paid staff stay alert, **and adds that it should ideally be warmed towards evening, though most companies do not bother.**
- **His own domestic preference is 2700 K**, and the exchange records a real household disagreement (Кузина's husband wants bright and cold, she wants dark and warm) as a common client situation rather than an edge case.
- **⚠️ Tunable-white is now baseline, and this is the practical answer to that disagreement.** Three or four years before recording, a factory told him such a thing did not exist, then quoted **3× list price**; by 2022 **it can be done on a single fixture as standard.** Some fixtures **shift colour temperature as they dim** — dimming down goes warmer, giving a more intimate atmosphere.
- **⚠️ CRI («коэффициент цветопередачи») should be above 95** — "желательно, чтобы ты выглядел так, как ты по-настоящему выглядишь."
- **The complete set of objective fixture parameters, per him: colour temperature, luminous flux, secondary optics, and CRI — plus form factor.** His point in listing them is that **there is no other axis on which one fixture can be claimed to "light better" than another** — a useful filter against marketing claims.

## Lighting — scenarios, deflated

- **⚠️ He deflates the scenario concept for ordinary flats.** "Принцип здравого смысла." In a 45 m² apartment there cannot meaningfully be scenarios — **"это называется выключатель."**
- **Scenarios earn their cost where there is a real task**: a two-level apartment that hosts events; a client who reads in one specific corner — **and then the designer's job is to know where he reads and where his hand falls to switch it.** His framing of the research required is unusually blunt: how many live there, does a partner visit, how many children.
- **⚠️ For an ordinary apartment he reduces it to two**: a **weekday scenario** (four of six lamps on) and an **evening/duty scenario** for reaching the toilet at night.
- **⚠️ Night lighting, concrete recommendation: a strip near the bed, necessarily in an aluminium profile so heat is dissipated and the LED does not degrade.** *(Corroborates existing vault content on LED thermal management from a second, unrelated channel.)*
- **A night scenario should use several very weak sources**, specifically so you do not fully wake when getting a glass of water.
- **Navigational light**, borrowed from his landscape-lighting practice — so a person knows where to go in the evening — transfers indoors.
- **The hotel observation, as a cheap proof**: hotels have almost no fixtures, only table and floor lamps; throw a towel over a shade and you have an excellent scenario.
- **⚠️ Kids' room lighting: "все про это забывают."** Children pick at things, draw, and look at them from very close range, **so genuine illuminance in the work zones specifically matters for their eyes.**

## Lighting — layering and sequence

- **Functional layer first, accents second.** "Самое важное в освещении — чтобы был слой функционального освещения, чтоб тебе было просто комфортно и всё видно, и дальше ты уже работаешь акцентами" — **and only where there is actually something to accent.**
- **Named default for that layer: a recessed downlight with 60° optics** — "мы его почти всегда используем." Accents come from track systems, other recessed types, or pendants.
- **⚠️ Lighting follows the plan; it is never the organising idea.** "Дизайн первичен, всегда. Всегда. Свет — это просто макияж." Asked whether an interior can be built around its lighting, he rejects it outright. **The layout tells you which accents you want and where.**
- **Track systems have a specific non-aesthetic advantage: they are universal and removable** — you can unclip the fittings and take them when you move. He raises this in the context of Russian businesses not planning long-term (his own 150-person employer moved four times in five years).
- **Presence sensors («датчик присутствия», which he distinguishes from a motion sensor) dim fixtures by up to 80%** and save substantial electricity; combined with daylight sensors they also adjust temperature and brightness through the day. **Expensive up front.**

## Cost Drivers / Advertising — the tier-steering finding, which is the most valuable non-technical item

- **⚠️ Showrooms cannot serve a real project, and the reason is inventory, not taste.** A showroom's task **"не впарить — задача сгрузить склад, распродать остаток."** If ten fixture types exist and they hold three, the whole project gets built from those three. **"Шоу-румы закрыты своим ассортиментом."**
- **⚠️ And the structural reason stock can never match a project**: one downlight model exists in **10–15 variants** — different optics (60°, 50°, asymmetric), different colour temperatures. **"У тебя на базе одного светильника может быть 15 разных моделей, а моделей этих 30."** Nobody can hold that. **What is held in stock is the basic functional layer only**, so anything beyond it is a special order by definition. His analogy: a fixture is like a dress — different optics for different jobs.
- **⚠️ How lighting factories capture design studios — a direct conflict-of-interest account.** Factories approach studios offering to produce the lighting plan **free**: "присылайте нам свои проекты, мы вам вообще бесплатно всё нарисуем, и вы будете нашими." **He claims 9 of 10 studios do not place the fixtures themselves** — they send the interior drawing to the factory and drop the returned layout into their album. His verdict: "там нет души." **Кузина pushes back that she places them herself** and is then told there are too many. **This belongs with this vault's existing tier-steering material.**
- **He states he is not affiliated with any factory** and will tell a client which of his own designed fixtures are worth buying and which are mediocre.
- **⚠️ Buying rule with a clean boundary: never buy cheap pendant or surface-mounted fixtures, because they are permanently in your field of view. Recessed fixtures can be bought cheaply.** Кузина immediately presses him on CRI below 95, and **he concedes good cheap ones exist** — the rule is about visibility, not about quality tiers as such.
- **⚠️ On Chinese fixtures — a nuance worth keeping.** "В Китае есть любой свет — абсолютно стрёмный, ужасный... и абсолютно шикарный." The Chinese seller's task is to sell and it does not matter what. **The reason Russian buyers fear Chinese lighting is historical: early importers deliberately brought the very cheapest tier, marked it up ~5×, and sold it here.** There is nothing inherently wrong with Chinese product, and production has largely moved there regardless.
- **⚠️ The honest admission underneath his own sales case: between a genuinely good fixture and a mediocre one the visible difference is about 15–20%**, and without trained visual experience you cannot tell. He offers this as the reason to hire a specialist — but it is equally a reason not to over-invest, and is recorded as such.

## Labor / Delivery Model — vetting and scope

- **⚠️ A designer is not obliged to know lighting engineering.** Stated twice. It is a separate professional competence («светотехническая»), and light design is essentially not taught — he took a светотехника faculty course and learned the rest by making expensive mistakes on employers' projects. **When a designer takes responsibility for lighting anyway, they can wreck their own space.**
- **His definition, which doubles as the quality bar: "светодизайн — это отсутствие косяков в расстановке светильников."**
- **⚠️ How to vet a lighting designer, per him**: not by pretty portfolio images but by **how many sites and clients they have actually completed**; ask **what tools they use**, and **who specifically will work on the project** (he names his own team structure: project manager, light designer/engineer, procurement).
- **The "express project" service he sells**: a ~2.5-hour joint session producing a small album with fixture layout, fixture types, **specific brands and article numbers, power, colour temperature and secondary optics, plus a budget estimate** — explicitly so the client can then instruct a ceiling contractor precisely. **A price figure is stated but the captions garble it beyond recovery — see Confidence notes.**
- **He reports clients who commissioned lighting subsequently asking him for furniture advice**, which he takes as evidence that visual judgement transfers across disciplines.

## Mistakes / Warnings

- **⚠️ His own most expensive mistake, told against himself.** Expensive German asymmetric-optic fixtures recessed into a ceiling and aimed sideways, producing a crisp wall-graze with the source invisible — a museum effect. **The client did not mention the wall would be marble.** Marble is glossy and reflects, so the fixture's own reflection appears in the wall and the entire effect is destroyed — **at roughly €300 per fixture, the most expensive fittings in the apartment.** His lesson: "такому не научишься, такое можно только понаошибаться." **Generalises to: check every surface a directional fixture will wash for gloss before specifying it.**
- **Building the lighting scheme around a chandelier.**
- **Bare-bulb fixtures in the field of view.**
- **Mixing colour temperatures in one room without a functional reason.**
- **Buying cheap pendants.**
