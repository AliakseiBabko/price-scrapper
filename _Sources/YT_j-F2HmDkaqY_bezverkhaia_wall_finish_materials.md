## Materials — why cheap paint is usually not cheaper (four mechanisms)

Her framing question is the common one: why buy expensive paint when a 10-litre bucket of the cheap stuff exists. The supplier answers with four distinct failure modes, which is the most reusable structure in the video:

1. **⚠️ Hiding power (укрывная способность) — the cost argument that reverses the saving.** Budget paint's real coverage does not match the manufacturer's stated consumption: **instead of two buckets you bring four to site.** Paints using expensive dispersions have materially higher hiding power, so **"на круг" you likely do not save at all.**
2. **⚠️ Washability, with the actual mechanism: open versus closed pore.** Mass-market **matte** paints are easily soiled because they have an **open pore** and cannot be properly washed. Good mid- and premium-segment paints are wear-resistant **because their pore is closed, even in a matte finish** — so they can be fully washed. The failure case is repainting a few months later where children or daily life has marked the wall.
3. **⚠️ Colour rendition when tinted.** Tinting a complex colour — his example is a classic **"французский серый"** — on a good manufacturer's base yields a deep shade that reads differently under different light. Tint the same colour into cheap raw material and you get **"больничный серый"** — a flat, unpleasant grey. **The colour you chose is a property of the base, not only of the formula.**
4. **Durability in service** generally, framed as not having to redo the finish.

## Materials — the paint sheen ladder, mapped to rooms

The single most actionable block in the video, because it ties gloss percentage to a use decision:

| Paint type | Sheen | Where, and why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ceiling paint** | maximum matte | **Scatters light and hides minor substrate defects** left from ceiling prep — nothing shows in raking light. **Not wear-resistant**, which is acceptable: "мы обычные потолки не драим." |
| **Velvety matte (бархатисто-матовая)** | **~3%** | **His default recommendation for walls.** Wear-resistant, **washable with a sponge and mild solutions**, behaves well in service. |
| **Velvety matte / eggshell** | **~5%** | **⚠️ His specific recommendation for children's rooms** — a child marks the wall, it is wiped with a cloth, and **the paint film itself is not altered**, so no repainting. Visually still reads as matte: she remarks on camera that at 5% the sheen is "вообще практически незаметно." |
| **Satin** | **15–20%** | Strongest in service, **but highlights every defect left by a poor painter.** |

**⚠️ The governing trade-off, stated plainly: the higher the sheen, the more durable *and* the more it exposes substrate and workmanship errors.** Mid-range velvety matte is the recommended compromise for walls.

## Materials — wall preparation before paint, and what skipping it costs

- Her framing of the common fear: paint needs three coats of filler, fibreglass mesh (стеклохолст), a final levelling pass, and a genuinely competent painter — all of it expensive.
- **⚠️ His answer is a tolerance question, not a technique shortcut**: you *can* simplify, the way you can cook off-recipe — you will get an inedible dish. **"Идеальной красоты вы добьётесь только соблюдая технологии."**
- **⚠️ The usable middle he concedes**: if you accept walls that are not perfectly flat, **you can paint over without levelling, and with a good colour it will still look decent.** Recorded because it is a rare explicit statement that the low-prep route is legitimate at a lower tolerance rather than simply wrong.

## Materials — printed panels and modern wallpaper substrates

- **⚠️ Digitally-printed seamless panels are the supplier's most-requested product**, driven by the accent-wall trend — people now decorate **one wall** rather than a whole room.
- **The mechanism that makes them different from roll wallpaper**: roll wallpaper shows **seams** if hung badly; a printed panel is produced **without joins** for that specific wall.
- **A low-quality source image is not necessarily disqualifying**: rather than upscaling pixels, their artist **redraws the subject digitally** and it is printed at high detail.
- **⚠️ Substrate options, and one has a real property worth keeping — «папирус»**: printing can go on standard **non-woven (флизелин)**, a **sand-textured** substrate, or **папирус**, which looks like textile from a distance but is **polyester**. It is **maximally wear-resistant and can take direct water contact**, while still gluing easily because its backing is non-woven.
- **⚠️ The wet-room boundary they actually draw**: for **direct water contact** (the shower itself) they still recommend classical tile. But **in a humid room where splashes reach the wall — his example is beside a basin — this material is unaffected.** That is the useful line: splash-zone-adjacent yes, shower enclosure no.
- **Premium natural-material wallcoverings** — bamboo, water-lily, palm leaf, largely handmade, Belgian producers named as among the most popular — are positioned as comparable in value to art objects.
- **⚠️ Her own designer recommendation, and she flags it as her opinion inserted into his pitch**: **do not wrap a whole apartment in expensive wallcovering.** Paint neutrally, then make **one accent** in something like this — it reads expensive and nobody else will have it.
- **A market-taste observation, recorded without her framing of it**: heavy gilt-damask wallpaper is characterised as an outdated preference now mostly requested by older buyers.

## Materials — moulded decor (лепнина): gypsum vs polyurethane vs duropolymer

- **Gypsum is the most expensive material and looks materially different** from polyurethane or duropolymer. **⚠️ Its installation is also always expensive because it is handwork** — the cost is in two places, not one.
- **Polyurethane and duropolymer are the budget materials**, installable by a general-purpose tradesman, and cheaper across the board.
- **⚠️ The mass-market failure mode, and she confirms it from her own tenancy**: cheap foam-type moulding looks fine at handover and then **dries out and degrades month by month**. Her rented flat's mouldings shrank visibly over her tenancy. **"Из этого рыхлого пенопласта лучше никогда не покупать."**
- **⚠️ The real mechanism, and the fix — this is the most valuable technical item in the video: polyurethane shrinks (усыхает), which causes splits at the joints.** Good producers therefore **make a joining adhesive out of the same material the moulding itself is made from**, so the joint moves with the profile and does not tear open. **Buy the matching same-material joint adhesive, not generic glue.**
- **Density claim for quality polymer profiles**: some are **comparable in density to oak**, hence hard to damage with furniture legs or a vacuum cleaner — which is the argument for using them as **baseboards and mouldings in high-traffic locations**. Also described as moisture-resistant enough to use in a shower. `single-account`, sponsor-adjacent — the density comparison is the supplier's claim about his own range.
- **Her studio's stated practice**: gypsum, or duropolymer/polyurethane — never the chain-retailer foam type.

## Materials — decorative plaster

- Positioned against the dated glossy "венецианка": modern decorative plasters include a **matte suede effect (эффект замши)** — smooth to the touch, suited to Japandi and contemporary interiors.
- **⚠️ Natural lime (натуральная известь), and the distinction is the extractable part**: still quarried and imported from Italian quarries. **In Russia lime is usually sold with acrylic added; theirs is natural**, which is why it is **plastic to work and maximally ecological**, and can produce loft finishes, stone effects (травертин, марморино) and the currently popular slightly-uneven clay-like surfaces.
- **⚠️ Application requires a decorator, not a generalist** — "чтобы он был декоратором и в принципе понимал, что такое кельма и шпатель." Their own materials are formulated to tolerate the all-rounder who paints walls, lays floors and does plumbing, but **a qualification is still required**; with lime or suede types, someone who has worked with decorative plaster before will manage, and video instructions and technical documentation exist.

## Materials — wood, laminate and MDF as a wall finish

She presents this fifth category herself, without the supplier.

- **⚠️ Board, laminate and anything imitating wood must be an accent only.** You cannot line a whole room's perimeter in board — **"если у вас конечно не яхта."** Best used on **one accent wall**: behind the sofa in a living room, or behind a bed's headboard.
- **⚠️ If you make that accent, the tone on the floor and on the wall should match, and ideally the texture too** — best of all, use literally the same material.
- **Styles it suits**: eco, Scandinavian (where wall accents and вагонка are native), loft. **In other styles it reads as less appropriate** — those want smooth veneered or MDF panels instead.
- **⚠️ Budget route she names explicitly**: in the economy segment or a rental, **you can decorate a wall with laminate** — paint everything a neutral colour and pick out the sofa or headboard wall this way.
- **MDF panels** are among the oldest classical wall treatments — descended from British solid-oak panelling, which is now expensive. In classic and modern-classic interiors MDF gives classical **филёнки** of any shape, carving, or in contemporary styles plain flat panels.
