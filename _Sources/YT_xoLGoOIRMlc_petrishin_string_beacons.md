## Walls / Ceilings — String-Beacon ("Струнные маяки") Installation Method

1. **Core mechanism**: a small corner bracket ("уголок") is screwed to
   the wall near each end of a wall run (self-tapping screw, ~75mm, plus
   an "eared" dowel/anchor chosen specifically so it won't tear out of a
   foam-block substrate). A length of galvanized tie wire ("оцинкованная
   проволока," the same wire used for tying rebar, bought at a
   construction-materials market — thinner insulated electrical wire was
   named as a usable substitute in a pinch) is wound tightly around a
   bolt at each bracket and tensioned like a guitar string, producing a
   dead-straight reference line along the wall without a physical strip
   beacon.
2. **Fine positional adjustment via tapered wedges**: the bracket's
   stand-off distance from the wall corner (~10cm in this demo) is
   fine-tuned using thin tapered plastic wedges/shims stacked behind it —
   a thicker or thinner wedge shifts the string's offset from the wall by
   as little as ~1mm, letting the crew dial in the exact deviation the
   wall's own geometry requires at each end.
3. **Real measured wall-deviation example**: on this real wall, the
   practitioner measured the resulting plaster-layer thickness at several
   points along the same run and found it varying roughly between
   ~1-1.5cm and ~3.5-4cm — attributed to the developer's own construction-
   stage deviation, not a plastering error, and used to explain why the
   string method's per-point adjustability matters.
4. **Two strings squared to each other via string/plumb cross-checks**,
   then a fixed, whole-run offset is corrected uniformly by inserting a
   shim of the exact needed thickness (a 3mm-thick shim to correct a
   measured 6mm deviation, in this demo) — corroborates and extends this
   page's existing whole-apartment/whole-wall squaring principle (Round
   3) with a concrete string-specific correction mechanism.
5. **Mid-span partial-set anchoring technique**: once the string is
   tensioned, the crew dabs a small amount of plaster mix at the string's
   mid-span point (rather than plaster-dab points every ~1.2m the way a
   rigid beacon needs) and waits **~40 minutes** for that dab to firm up
   (not fully cure) before continuing — keeps the string from bouncing/
   sagging while the rest of the wall is plastered. One string run in
   this demo spanned close to 7m off a single mid-span anchor.
6. **⚠️ Reinforcement-mesh numeric threshold, more specific than this
   page's existing general corner-mesh rule**: if the plaster layer at
   any point along a run will exceed **7cm** thickness, a metal
   reinforcement mesh must be installed before that section is plastered
   — plaster alone isn't considered self-supporting past that thickness.
7. **Scope limitation, stated explicitly by the practitioner himself**:
   this method is not presented as universally superior to rigid beacons
   — short wall segments and door-opening returns still use traditional
   short metal strip beacons (set both horizontally and vertically),
   since there isn't enough wall length to make tensioning a string worth
   the setup; the string method is described as best suited to long,
   straight wall runs.
8. **Laser-level self-verification practice, with a real cautionary
   anecdote**: recommends periodically re-checking a laser level against
   a plumb line/string, because a laser can silently drift out of
   calibration (e.g. from being dropped) and continue reporting confident
   but wrong readings with no visible warning sign. Cites a real incident
   where an intern accidentally dropped a laser level on-site and the
   resulting miscalibration went undetected for a period, producing
   crooked work that was initially blamed on the plastering crew rather
   than the tool.
9. **Site-management/communication risk specific to this method**: a
   taut string strung across a wall visually resembles a piece of
   discarded rope or a hung bag to someone unfamiliar with the technique
   — client site visits or building inspectors arriving while the crew is
   away have reportedly pulled the strings down, mistaking them for
   debris, requiring the crew to re-set and re-tension the line. Not a
   risk that applies to a rigid beacon set in plaster.
10. **⚠️ ASR-uncertain cost figures, not converted to USD (2018 source,
    transcript numbers unclear)**: metal strip beacons quoted at roughly
    40-60 rubles each, with a typical apartment needing on the order of
    40 of them (≈2,400 rubles total material cost for the traditional
    method this video is being compared against); a separate remark that
    minimizing average plaster thickness by ~5mm across roughly 300m² of
    wall area was called "significant" for material cost, with a
    specific ruble figure that didn't parse cleanly from the ASR
    transcript — flagged uncertain rather than quoted as a hard number.
11. **Speed claim**: setting up both string lines shown in this demo took
    about 15 minutes; the practitioner separately estimated that laying
    out this method for a full room takes on the order of 3 hours.
