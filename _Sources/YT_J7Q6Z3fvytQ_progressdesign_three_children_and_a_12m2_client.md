⚠️⚠️ **The thirteenth and fourteenth Progress Design videos in this vault; nothing here is independent corroboration of the other twelve.** **Both were taken for specific open questions rather than for general wall-bed content, and both hit.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 1. THE EFFORT QUESTION, FROM A USER WHO OPERATES BOTH SIZES DAILY

**This is the closest thing the vault has to a controlled comparison, and it comes from a repeat customer.** The household bought a wall bed for the ADULTS' bedroom, used it for a year, and came back for a children's room with three beds. Asked whether they really fold it daily rather than leaving it down:

> ***«Каждый день. Более того, я ПО НЕСКОЛЬКО РАЗ могу это делать. Разложил, в комфорте посмотрел футбол… Ну это вообще не сложно.»***

**And then, comparing his own double against the children's 90 cm beds:**

> ***«Та у нас двуспальная история, она достаточно ТЯЖЁЛАЯ, что, в принципе, не вызывает дискомфорта. Я посмотрел вот эти — они очень ЛЁГКИЕ. То есть это ПАЛЬЧИКОМ просто.»***

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ THE VAULT'S WIDTH-VERSUS-EFFORT FINDING NOW HAS A SOURCE WHO HAS BOTH IN ONE HOME.** It previously rested on inference across households — a 7-year-old managing a 90 cm bed here, an adult squatting to work a double there. **This is one person, same mechanism brand, same day: the double is *«достаточно тяжёлая»* but acceptable; the 90 cm is *«пальчиком»*.**
>
> **→ ⚠️⚠️ FOR THIS PROJECT'S OPEN KIDS-ROOM QUESTION that is the most decision-relevant sentence in the batch**, because the children's beds under consideration are exactly 90 cm. **It does NOT answer the 3-year-old question** — his children are *«почти взрослые»* — **but it removes bed WEIGHT as the obstacle and narrows the remaining doubt to reach, habit and supervision.**
>
> ⚠️ **He is a satisfied repeat customer filmed by the seller. The revealed preference — buying a second time after a year — carries more than the words.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 2. A HOUSEHOLD THAT REPLACED A BUNK BED WITH WALL BEDS

**Asked what was wrong with the children's room before:**

> ***«Она стремилась к ХАОСУ. Здесь была ДВУХЪЯРУСНАЯ КРОВАТЬ, здесь ещё одна кровать, диван-кровать, и просто куча игрушек, всего напичкано — и здесь постоянный был хаос. А сейчас это стало и лучше, и эргономичнее.»***

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ A THIRD ARRIVAL AT THE BUNK REJECTION, and the first from a household that actually HAD one and removed it.** The vault's other two are a maker rejecting it on footprint and a household rejecting it on permanent visibility; **this one lived with a bunk plus an extra bed plus a sofa bed in one room and describes the result as chaos.**
>
> **→ Relevant to this project as corroboration only — the bunk decision is already taken (2026-09-16, owner). Recorded because it is the strongest version of the argument and it arrived after the decision, not before it.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 3. THE POWER-CUT ANSWER — an open question this vault flagged twice

**A client reviewing his own 12 m² room, unaccompanied by the maker, listing what convinced him:**

> ***«Есть специальный на случай АВАРИЙНОГО ОТКЛЮЧЕНИЯ СВЕТА хомуток, такой ремешок, за который ты дёргаешь — и кровать опускается.»***

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ THIS ANSWERS THE QUESTION `o6_HwxwZau4` WAS PICKED TO ANSWER AND COULD NOT, because that video turned out to be 29 bytes of music.** **An automatic wall bed has a dedicated manual release — a pull-strap — for a power failure.**
>
> **→ It also completes the specification rule recorded from the voice-control fault**: voice is the least reliable layer, a button is the fallback, **and a mechanical pull-strap is the fallback to the button.** **Three levels, and the bottom one needs no electricity at all.** **→ Worth confirming by name with any supplier, since it is the difference between a power cut being an inconvenience and being a household without a bed.**
>
> ⚠️ **One client's description of one maker's product. It establishes that such a release EXISTS and is worth asking for — not that every automatic bed has one.**

## ⚠️ 4. The 12 m² room — what actually fitted, from the person who paid for it

**A client's own account, having researched competitors before choosing.** Into 12 m²: a full storage system (*«у нас даже вещей нету, чтобы распихать по всем этим ящичкам»*), a sofa-and-TV living zone, **a pull-out table** that turns the room into somewhere to lay on a meal, a **140 cm** wall bed, **a built-in ironing board**, and a **work surface fitted into an awkward corner at the windowsill, held to a minimum 50 cm depth** on non-standard wall geometry.

- **⚠️ His stated reason for choosing this maker over competitors is a specific detail**: *«мне не хотелось, чтобы вот эти подушки приходилось постоянно снимать и куда-то ставить»* — **the cushions retract into the sofa body automatically.** **A purchase decided by one daily-use irritation, which is a useful reminder of what actually drives a furniture choice.**
- **⚠️ The sofa detaches for deep cleaning underneath** — *«при необходимости совершенно спокойно снимается, чтобы под диваном провести генеральную уборку»*. ⚠️ **Relevant to the two-person cleaning requirement recorded from FREE_DOM: at least one maker makes the sofa removable rather than requiring the bed to be held.**
- **Bedding stays strapped in place** — *«вам не надо убирать бельё, вам не надо убирать подушки… застёгиваете ремнями»*.
- **⚠️ A THIRD arrival at the shin-guard point**, in a user's own words: soft edges *«чтобы не расшибить себе ноги ночью»*.
- **⚠️ The ironing board again** — independently corroborating [[_Sources/YT_ztubNCfTyhg_kachanova_seven_transformer_households|Качанова's households]] and [[_Sources/YT_cZNP6TaUyCU_freedom_wallbed_installation_and_two_layouts|FREE_DOM]]: the recovered space near a wall bed is where an ironing board ends up, often as a built-in.

## ⚠️ 5. The three-child room, and details worth carrying

- **Three sleeping places, all 90 × 200**, chosen deliberately so that *«через пару лет, когда дети вырастут, они всё также смогут с комфортом здесь спать»* — **sized for the children they will become, not the children they are.**
- **Son on one side, two daughters on the other, with a zoning partition planned** — ⚠️ the same boundary answer as the shelving-unit divider recorded from the sibling batch.
- **High ceilings used deliberately as vertical storage**, including a client-requested niche for **a stepladder and a vertical vacuum cleaner** — ⚠️ a reminder that the awkward tall objects need a named home or they end up in a hallway.
- **All three manual; a few seconds each; LED strip inside with a touch dimmer; a socket at every bed; side shelves for each child.**
- **Soft bolsters around the whole perimeter**, with headboard and mechanism covers **in the same fabric as the sofa** so the composition reads as one piece.
- **⚠️ And the benefit the parent names first is not space but tidying**: *«не нужно заправлять кровать — для ленивых история. Быстро собрал, быстро положил, кровать убрана. Это гениально.»* **→ The made bed folds away made. That is a daily-habit benefit, and it is the one this household volunteered ahead of floor area.**
