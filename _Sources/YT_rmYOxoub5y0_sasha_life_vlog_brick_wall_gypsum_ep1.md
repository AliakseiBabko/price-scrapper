## Walls / Ceilings — Durable Facts & Rules (faux brick wall, gypsum plaster technique — episode 1, core method)

- **Faux exposed-brick wall finish, built from gypsum plaster over a
  hand-drawn grid, not from any brick-pattern stencil/roller/premade
  panel product.** Full method as demonstrated:
  1. Prime the wall with a deep-penetration primer ("грунтовка глубокого
     проникновения") and let it dry fully.
  2. **Lay out the brick grid by hand**: horizontal lines spaced **7.5 cm**
     apart across the whole wall (stated brick width 6.5 cm + ~1 cm
     allowance for the "mortar joint" line/tape width = 7.5 cm).
  3. Vertical divisions: brick length target **25 cm** (+1 cm joint
     allowance = 26 cm), with a **13 cm half-offset** (26 ÷ 2) between
     rows — a running-bond (staggered) brick pattern, not stacked/aligned
     columns.
  4. **Prime the wall a second time**, over the drawn grid — stated
     purpose: to fix/seal the pencil-drawn lines so they don't smear once
     wet plaster is applied.
  5. **Apply adhesive tape along every drawn grid line** — specifically
     12 mm-wide stationery/office tape ("канцелярская лента"), sourced
     from a stationery shop, not a hardware store — masking off what will
     become the recessed "mortar joint" lines.
  6. **Apply gypsum plaster over the taped grid** — the source
     specifically researched online beforehand and found either gypsum
     plaster or ceramic-tile adhesive ("клей для плитки") work for this
     technique; she used gypsum plaster.
  7. **The core technique insight, stated explicitly and repeated**: apply
     the plaster **deliberately unevenly, not smoothly** — "the less even
     you apply it, the more beautiful it looks" ("чем неровнее вы её
     нанесёте, тем красивее будет смотреться") — an uneven, textured
     surface reads as a convincing 3D brick texture; a smooth, leveled
     application (the instinct of someone trained to plaster evenly) works
     *against* the effect. This directly contradicts normal plastering
     technique/training and is the single most important non-obvious
     detail in the method.
  8. **Remove the tape while the plaster is still wet** (not after drying)
     to reveal clean, recessed mortar-joint lines, then go over sharp
     corners/edges with a damp cloth to soften them.
  9. Let the finished wall dry for a couple of days before priming again
     and painting.
- **Real time/mess cost, stated candidly, not glossed over**: the grid-
  drawing stage took a full day on its own (nothing else was finished that
  day); the plastering stage was described as "very dirty work — more
  cleanup afterward than the actual work itself," done over roughly 2
  working days total for this one wall (drawing day 1, plaster application
  day 2). A genuine budget/time-planning data point for anyone considering
  this technique themselves, not a polished "quick and easy" framing.
- **First-time result was subjectively very positive** ("I didn't expect
  this result, I love it") — stated intent to continue the same technique
  across the rest of the corridor in later episodes of this series.
