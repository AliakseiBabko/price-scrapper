## Mistakes / Warnings — Sound & Sleep Foundation

- **Soundproof the headboard wall if it borders a neighbor** — any method
  works (frame-based, frameless, or decorative soft acoustic panels), the
  point is to do *something*. `corroborates_existing` (already covered in
  depth in [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Soundproofing|Soundproofing]]).
- **Never install a sliding door between the bedroom and any shared/common
  space** (corridor, living room) — sliding doors have the worst sound
  insulation of any door type, and a bedroom needs that isolation. A
  sliding door into a **pass-through walk-in closet** behind the bedroom is
  fine (not a "common" space).
- **Noisy climate equipment kills sleep quality** — a noisy AC indoor unit,
  noisy breather/fresh-air unit, or noisy humidifier will prevent good
  sleep regardless of everything else. **Numeric thresholds, stated
  directly: ideal device noise in a bedroom is up to 25 dB; up to 30 dB is
  still comfortable; anything above 30 dB becomes noticeably irritating.**
  Select bedroom-specific climate devices against these thresholds, not
  general-purpose specs.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Climate & Air

- **AC required in the bedroom for climate comfort** — speaker's own
  experience in both Vladivostok and Moscow: doesn't consider a bedroom
  livable in summer without one.
- **AC indoor-unit placement rule**: airflow must never hit the bed
  directly (legs, face) — this is a stated illness/cold risk from repeated
  direct airflow exposure during sleep. Mount the unit either to the side
  of the bed's own axis, or entirely outside that zone.
- **A fresh-air breather (бризер) without a proper full-capacity humidifier
  will make winter air uncomfortably dry** — cold intake air holds far less
  moisture than room air, so a breather's constant fresh-air intake in
  winter dries the room faster than a closed window would. **A basic humidifier
  can keep pace with a merely-open window, but a breather specifically
  needs a higher-capacity, "serious" humidifier** to compensate — an
  explicit product-capacity distinction, not just "add a humidifier."
  Speaker's own general recommendation: he'd install a breather (or
  equivalent forced-fresh-air system) in "95% of apartments" in the
  country, regardless of this humidity tradeoff.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Light & Lighting

- **Three-scenario lighting plan required, not one fixture on one switch**:
  (1) base/main light (chandelier or equivalent), (2) decorative light
  (cornice/backlighting, an accent panel), (3) individual/task light (a
  bedside sconce or equivalent) — a single light source trying to serve
  all three purposes (ambient, decorative, and reading) is called out as a
  concrete usability failure, not just an aesthetic one.
- **Never mount linear/track lighting directly above the sleeping
  position** — install it along the walking path beside the bed instead,
  so it lights the way rather than shining into a sleeper's face,
  including on waking. `corroborates_existing` (this store's existing
  no-spotlight/track-over-bed caution, [[00_Master/Bedroom_Design_Principles|Bedroom Design Principles]]).
- **Bedroom lighting color temperature: 2700K-3000K is the comfort
  range** for rest — a specific numeric range not previously on this
  store's bedroom content.
- **Each occupant should be able to independently switch both main and
  dimmed/decorative light from their own side of the bed** — recommends
  three two-gang (double-rocker) switches to cover this, rather than one
  shared switch for the whole room.
- **Never hang a glass pendant/globe sconce directly over a nightstand at
  close range** — a first-hand account of repeatedly (~20 times) bumping
  a ~30,000 RUB glass-globe pendant hung too close over a nightstand in a
  rented apartment; it never broke but the risk is described as an
  unnecessary "delayed-action mine." Recommends any other bedside fixture
  type instead.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Light Blocking

- **Blackout curtains or blackout roller shades are close to mandatory**
  — speaker's own preference for sleeping/waking in full darkness, with a
  concrete regional data point: sunrise in Moscow in summer is around
  4:00 AM. **Ideal implementation: blackout curtains plus a motorized
  drive integrated with smart-home**, framed as a cheap upgrade relative
  to the comfort payoff (curtains auto-open on an alarm schedule).

## Mistakes / Warnings — Bedroom Electronics/Tech

- **A TV in the bedroom is explicitly framed as the speaker's own
  minority/contrarian opinion** — he states most of his own clients now
  request no bedroom TV, and acknowledges the mainstream reasoning (a
  bedroom is for sleep/rest, and the "real" TV with good picture/sound
  lives in the living room) — but personally still wants one in his own
  home. Tag as a stated personal preference, not a technical
  recommendation either way.
- **Plan bedroom outlets for the full device set up front, not
  extension cords added later**: bedside charging outlets (explicitly
  recommended even though "many people skip this"), plus dedicated
  outlets for a robot vacuum charging dock, a fresh-air breather, a
  humidifier, a string/decorative light, and a TV if one is planned.
- **A smart-speaker/voice-assistant device (Alice/Яндекс Алиса) is
  recommended in every room including the bedroom** — cited uses: weather,
  time, whole-apartment single-source music playback, and voice control
  of motorized curtains as an alternative to the alarm-schedule automation
  above. Speaker's own stated daily-use habit, tag as a stated preference
  with a named product (regional voice-assistant brand), not a universal
  technical rule.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Materials, Color & Visual Noise

- **Avoid extremely bright/saturated colors (yellow, red, green) in a
  bedroom** — stated reasoning: these colors are psychologically
  stimulating to the nervous system and make falling asleep harder.
- **Avoid glossy/reflective finishes in a bedroom** (glossy cabinet
  fronts, glossy stretch ceilings) — anything that produces glare/
  reflections works against the room's calm function; reserve gloss for
  elsewhere (his own example: a car), keep bedroom surfaces matte.
- **Textile presence (not just soft-goods styling) is functionally
  important, not merely decorative**: fabric decorative panels or a
  fabric headboard, curtains, or a rug — any combination — contribute two
  distinct effects beyond visual coziness: they measurably reduce
  echo/reverberation in the room, an acoustic-comfort detail distinct
  from wall soundproofing.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Storage & Order

- **Open storage systems (open shelving, open wardrobes, non-faced
  dressers) create visual clutter/disorder in a bedroom specifically** —
  framed as incompatible with the room's calm function even when tidy.
  `corroborates_existing` (this store's existing closed-storage-preferred
  rule, so far recorded for the entryway — extends the same underlying
  principle to the bedroom).
- **A nightstand is treated as a near-mandatory item**, even against the
  counter-argument that a phone could charge in another room — speaker's
  own stated practical reasoning: he does get called and needs the phone
  reachable, and needs a defined surface (book, phone, laptop) rather than
  ad hoc floor/bed clutter.
- **A dedicated spot for end-of-day clothing (not a chair)** — a
  floor-standing valet stand, or a bedside bench, is recommended
  specifically so clothes aren't dumped on a chair (his own admitted
  current habit, called out as neither tidy nor attractive). Framed as
  more often a male household member's habit/need than female.

## Numeric Data / Quantities

- **Bed-to-rug clearance formula**: the rug's near edge should sit **at
  least 50cm from the bed, ideally 60-70cm** — a rug that only reaches
  ~10cm from the bed means a sleeper's heel touches rug while the rest of
  the foot lands on bare flooring on the first step out of bed, undermining
  both the tactile "expensive renovation" feel and genuine comfort. New,
  more specific figure than this store's existing bed-clearance formulas
  (which cover bed-to-wall/furniture clearance, not bed-to-rug edge).

## Mistakes / Warnings — Sleep Comfort & Furniture

- **Core "basic sleep comfort" formula, stated directly**: a quality
  mattress + quality pillow + quality duvet/blanket — the room can be
  visually perfect and still leave the occupant chronically uncomfortable
  if any one of these three is mismatched to the person.
- **A vanity/makeup table in the bedroom is flagged as a common source of
  friction, not endorsed by default**: two stated problems — (1) staggered-
  schedule couples (one wakes earlier/later) clash over the table's own
  bright dedicated lighting disturbing the other; (2) the table tends to
  accumulate visible clutter (cosmetics, tools), undermining room order.
  Recommends relocating it to a walk-in closet or another space where
  possible; acknowledges some floor plans genuinely have nowhere else to
  put it, in which case it stays in the bedroom by necessity.
