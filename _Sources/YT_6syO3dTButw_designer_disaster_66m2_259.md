## Rules / Heuristics — Genuinely New Techniques

- **A wall-mounted mirror should be raised roughly 15cm off its mounting surface, not installed flush** — leaves room to add electrical points/backlighting later, even if not wired at initial install. Extends this vault's existing "always include a lit mirror" guidance with a specific installation-clearance figure.
- **Protecting an unavoidable exposed exterior corner with furniture placement (e.g. a pouf/ottoman positioned directly against it) is a legitimate alternative to an architectural fix (arch/portal detail)** when the architectural fix would require breaking up otherwise-good cabinetry — a specific technique for balancing corner protection against layout disruption.
- **An ultra-thin countertop material (as thin as 9mm) exists specifically to resolve a height conflict between a tall appliance (e.g. a maxi-height dishwasher) and a shorter user's ergonomic counter-height needs** — using a standard-thickness countertop in this situation would push the whole counter uncomfortably high for a shorter user; the thin material keeps the counter at a workable height while still clearing the appliance.
- **Cooking-zone task lighting should be diffuse, not sharp directional spotlighting** — mechanism: any directional light source, however bright, casts a hand/head/body shadow directly onto the work surface as the cook leans in, risking both reduced visibility and unnoticed contamination (hair, debris) falling into food unseen. **General rule: prefer a diffused light source over the primary cooking/prep zone specifically to avoid this shadow-casting failure mode.**
- **⚠️ Magnetic (floor-mounted, flush) door stops have a real hardware-durability tradeoff against traditional mechanical stops, worth stating explicitly to a client rather than assuming they're a strict upgrade.** They look cleaner (no visible bump/protrusion), but releasing the door from the magnetic catch requires enough force that it visibly strains/bends the door's own handle hardware over repeated use — this practitioner's own experience: expect to repair or replace the affected door hardware roughly every 6-12 months when using magnetic stops. **Still worth using in a genuinely tight space where a traditional protruding stop would create a real injury risk** (e.g. striking fingers in a narrow WC) — a situational exception, not a blanket preference either way.
- **If building a home sauna, place a dedicated drinking-water point immediately adjacent to or inside the sauna zone** — a real, practical recommendation from an owner with actual extended home-sauna use experience: long sauna sessions create frequent thirst, and having to leave (wrapped in a towel) to fetch water repeatedly is a genuine usability gap most designs don't anticipate.

## Rules / Heuristics — Smart-Home / Automation Features Worth Knowing

- **A dashboard "red frame"/alert overlay that visually flags any device or zone left in an unexpected "on" state** (a sauna heater left running, a light left on in an empty room, water left running) is a genuinely useful smart-home safety feature — catches exactly the kind of oversight a manual walk-through misses.
- **Dynamic/circadian lighting — color temperature shifting through the day to match outdoor natural light (roughly 2,700K warm in the evening to 6,000K cool at midday)** — a real, mechanism-explained feature aimed at supporting circadian rhythm (easier waking, easier winding down at night), not just an aesthetic gimmick.
- **A wired smart-home control system is preferred over a WiFi/cloud-dependent wireless one for reliability** — a wired system responds instantly and locally; a cloud-dependent wireless system's responsiveness depends on internet connectivity and the manufacturer's own server availability, a real point of failure outside the homeowner's control.
- **A door-release/intercom button placed in the main living ("clean") zone, not only at the entry, avoids forcing residents to walk to the entry through a "dirty" zone just to buzz someone in** — a genuinely useful convenience upgrade to standard intercom placement.
- **Presence-based automatic "away mode"**: all lighting/devices (except refrigerators) can be set to shut off automatically once every registered household phone leaves the home WiFi network — a real automation upgrade over a manual master switch, removing the need to remember to trigger it on the way out.
