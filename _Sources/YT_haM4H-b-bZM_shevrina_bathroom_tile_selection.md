## Cost Drivers — The Large-Slab Logistics Trap (the standout item)

**⚠️ A 3 m ceramic slab does not fit in a lift and has to be carried up the stairs by hand — she puts that at 30,000–50,000 RUB (≈$330–$550).** Delivery costs more too, since the truck and the loading/unloading are a different class of job from panels under a metre.

**⚠️ Her fix, which is the actionable part: have the slab cut down at the factory before delivery**, to pieces of about a metre, and **align the cuts to real lines in the design** — where cabinet-front divisions fall, where the mirror begins, where the basin or the WC installation ends — so the joints read as deliberate (соосно) rather than as random seams. You keep the wide-format look and drop both the hand-carry and the oversize delivery.

## Materials — Format Rules

- **⚠️ Mid formats are the problem, and 20×40 is named as the worst.** "Что-то среднее и не очень эстетичное." 30×60 is named alongside it. **Go either large or genuinely small; the middle is where tile looks cheap.**
- **⚠️ Marble and marble-look: from 120×45 or 120×60 upward, never a mid format.** Restates the rule from `QES02ExtmAg` and `XGI6FS2ZdCc` (same channel) and pins the lower bound more precisely at 120×45.
- **⚠️ The small classic format is fully current and is not a trend** — "ещё 100 лет назад выглядели актуально." A safe choice at the opposite end from wide format.
- **⚠️ Marble must be matte, not polished — with a stated reason: "в природе не встречаются полированные материалы, мрамор в природе матовый."** Gloss cheapens it badly, and worst of all on wide-format marble. She reports still seeing polished marble used on living-room and hallway *floors*.
- **⚠️ The one exception where gloss works: the small classic format** — "это единственная плитка, в которой идёт глянец," where it actually looks better than matte.

## Materials — The "Dirty Tile" Effect, With Causes and a Showroom Test

Her long-running theme, now with a mechanism. **Concrete-look tile reads as dirt for three reasons:**

1. **Too mottled/пёстрая.**
2. **Too dark.**
3. **⚠️ Wrong undertone — a cold one especially — and the effect intensifies when a cold-toned tile is combined with saturated warm wood.**

Plus a fourth, physical cause: **many such tiles are printed with darkened edges**, so every unit's border is visible on the wall, which clutters an already small bathroom instead of reading as one continuous concrete surface.

**⚠️ Her showroom test, which is concrete and cheap: lay two or three tiles of the same collection side by side** and check (a) whether they have darkened edges and (b) whether a continuous texture is actually achievable with them.

**Replacements she gives**: the same concrete-look but calmer, lighter and warmer; or go fully dark — graphite or black works; or switch to a quiet **slate** or **microcement** texture, both of which she calls stone patterns that have been current for decades and will stay so.

## Materials — Relief and Pattern

- **⚠️ Relief types to avoid**: representational reliefs (plant motifs are the most commonly bought and look the most artificial); overly specific or complex geometric reliefs (recently trendy, now dated); and **wave reliefs** — rejected on cleaning grounds as well as aesthetic ones, and she notes that cleaning is what deterred most buyers.
- **⚠️ The timeless relief is the slat/rib — метр or ритм.** She dates the motif to Asia and Japan "ещё до XV века." *(Note: in `QES02ExtmAg` she dates рейки to the 13th century. Both are loose and neither is load-bearing; recorded as "long-standing" rather than as a date.)*
- **Patterned tile: all of it is trend-bound.** Either it was trendy and is now dated, or it is trendy now and will likely be dated in about five years. Asked which patterned tile she would specify for a долговечный interior: **"никакую."**
- **"Пол на стене"** — floor-format planks run up a wall — is dated. It arose because wide-format wood-look wasn't available and people wanted wood where water hits. **Replacement: wide-format wood-look ceramic** (see the logistics fix above for the cost objection).

## Design Concept

- **⚠️ Splitting a wall horizontally into two different tiles is NOT an anti-trend** — she raises it specifically to defend it. It's commonly used in a small bathroom to balance a tall, narrow, box-like room, and it still works; the failure was the *old* execution (floral or dolphin borders, decorative кантики), not the device. Pick two currently-good tiles and it reads well, in large bathrooms too.
