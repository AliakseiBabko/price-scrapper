## Mistakes / Warnings, Quantities / Measurements, and Planning Rules

- **⚠️ Sink-to-cooktop distance, refining/extending Round 1's work-triangle content**: explicitly states **80% of all kitchen actions happen in the zone between the sink and the cooktop**, and this distance should be minimum **80-90cm**. Treated as a refinement/elaboration of Round 1's work-triangle figures (`LU35znr1jVM`: sink-to-stove ≈1m) rather than a contradiction — gives an explicit minimum floor and the "80% of actions" framing; cross-reference both.
- **⚠️ Cabinet mechanism placement — a real, specific, checkable rule**: avoid push-to-open mechanisms on LOWER cabinets (trigger accidentally from a knee/hip bump while working nearby) and avoid lift-up/flip-up mechanisms on UPPER cabinets (short users can't reach to close them without a step stool; heavy fronts wear out the lift hardware faster — cites a specific client anecdote of replacing lift-up hardware five times). **Correct approach**: push-to-open only on UPPER cabinets (used less often, avoids visible fingerprint smudges accumulating on lower fronts that get touched constantly); everywhere else (lower cabinets) use integrated pulls or visible/graspable handles instead of push-to-open.
- **⚠️ Sink setback from the counter's front edge**: recommends setting the sink back **5-10cm from the front edge of the counter** rather than flush with it, so you don't have to lean/reach forward while washing dishes — reduces back/neck strain during extended dishwashing.
- **⚠️ Oven and microwave heights — refining/nuancing Round 1's kitchen-appliance-height content, not flatly agreeing**: oven should be at **waist height** (not chest height as some sources suggest) for safe/comfortable access to hot trays without overreaching; microwave should be positioned **slightly below eye level** (not above it) to avoid the burn/spill risk of pulling a hot bowl down from above head height. **Cross-check against Round 1's `LU35znr1jVM` content**, which recommended a microwave stacked with a built-in wall oven, microwave's lower edge at roughly chest height — these may describe genuinely different configurations (a stacked oven+microwave column vs. a separate microwave placement) rather than a true contradiction. Recorded both, flagged for the routing pass to decide whether a Perspectives block or just a clarifying cross-reference is warranted.
- **⚠️ Countertop height — a specific, individualized formula, distinct from Round 1's flat 86cm/92-95cm figures**: measure your elbow's bent-arm crease height, then SUBTRACT 15cm — that is the correct individual countertop height. Even a 3-5cm deviation from this calculated height causes measurable discomfort, especially for tall users who end up bumping their forehead reaching for the sink or compensating with spinal curvature. A genuinely more precise, individualized method than Round 1's flat height ranges — cross-reference both, don't pick one as "more correct."
- **Avoid placing an oven directly beside a window with curtains** — grease/steam splatter stains curtain fabric quickly, heat exposure can yellow or deform the fabric over time, and there's a real fire risk from fabric near a hot oven.
- **Empty gap above upper cabinets reaching the ceiling** — restates Round 1's `LU35znr1jVM` content on this exact mistake; cross-reference only, not duplicated.
- **⚠️ Under-cabinet task lighting installation detail — a specific new technique**: mount the LED strip/fixture **5-10cm forward from the wall, near the front edge of the cabinet bottom**, rather than tucked flush against the back wall — light aimed straight down at the counter reads much brighter/more useful than light that hits the backsplash/wall first. Recommends narrow LED strips or linear fixtures with a built-in matte/frosted diffuser for soft, shadow-free light, and a color temperature around **3500-4500K neutral white** — a range, distinct from and slightly wider than Round 1's single 4000K figure; a refinement, not a contradiction.
- **⚠️ Dedicated electrical circuits required for high-draw appliances** (cooktop, oven specifically) — cannot share standard household wiring circuits, which will overheat; requires separate dedicated lines run to separate breakers in the panel.
- **⚠️ A full outlet-count planning table — genuinely valuable, specific, directly actionable content**: an appliance-by-appliance count for kitchen outlets/stubs — fridge (1), oven (1), dishwasher (1, behind the appliance), microwave (1), kettle (1, at the work zone), coffee machine (1, near the kettle), blender + toaster (2 SEPARATE outlets, specifically to avoid unplugging one to use the other), multicooker/food processor (1 spare), hood + cabinet lighting (2, but these are low-voltage stubs/leads not standard outlets), phone charging (1, wherever the phone tends to be kept). **Totals to roughly 10-11 outlets/stubs for an average kitchen; 12-14 for a large kitchen with an island or bar counter; 6-8 minimum for a compact kitchen.** States the general **designer/electrician convention is 8-12 outlets** for comfortable, safe kitchen use, and warns that under-provisioning leads to constant extension-cord use and later costly rewiring. Recommends a voltage-surge-protection relay or a quality surge-protected power strip for kitchen electronics generally, specifically flagging induction-cooktop and microwave control modules as electronically vulnerable to voltage spikes.
- **⚠️ Backsplash installation sequencing — a genuinely useful, specific rule**: install and level the cabinet carcasses FIRST, verify their exact dimensions and front-face overhang, and only THEN install the backsplash tile/panel to fit flush against the finished cabinet run. Installing the backsplash before the cabinets forces force-fitting tile edges around cabinet variations later, filling gaps with a thick silicone bead (looks unfinished) and risks damaging the backsplash material (tile, porcelain, marble) during the subsequent cabinet-mounting process.
- **⚠️ Small-appliance outlet clearance and positioning around the cooktop and sink — a specific safety-distance rule**: outlets for small countertop appliances (kettle, toaster, blender) should sit roughly **10-20cm below/away from the cooktop's bottom edge** so cords don't dangle across a hot surface and the counter stays easy to wipe. Explicitly recommends never placing an outlet directly behind/opposite the cooktop or directly behind the sink — maintain a horizontal offset of roughly **20cm from the sink edge** and **15-20cm from the cooktop** to avoid water/hot-oil splash reaching the outlet.
- **Hidden storage/appliance "garage" for small countertop appliances** (blender, kettle, multicooker) — a dedicated cabinet nook or a pull-out shelf under the counter keeps these off the visible countertop, reducing clutter and the need to move things around during meal prep.
