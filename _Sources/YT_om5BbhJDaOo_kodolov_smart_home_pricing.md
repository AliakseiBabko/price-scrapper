## Planning Rules / Regulations — New Facts

- **⚠️ Real pricing structure, three tiers**: a basic "taste-test" starter
  kit (one smart bulb + a small hub/server) from **≈10,000 RUB**, with
  individual smart bulbs priced ≈600-1,400 RUB each — explicitly framed
  as a low-commitment way to test whether you'll actually use a smart
  home before investing further. A genuinely multi-functional system
  ("feel like Tony Stark") starts around **≈500,000 RUB** and scales
  upward with no real ceiling. **Cost-delta framing relative to a
  classic (non-smart) electrical build-out of the same project: +3%
  premium just from smart-capable wiring/topology alone** (the smart-
  ready wiring topology can actually save a little on cable in some
  cases), but **+20-35% total premium once the full smart-device/
  controller/programming layer is added**, varying by object and by
  which functions the client actually selects.
- **⚠️ Must be planned from the start of the renovation, not retrofitted
  after rough electrical**: a smart home has to be designed alongside
  (not after) the electrical design project, and its own installation/
  commissioning work runs in parallel with the whole renovation timeline
  (the smart-home crew's final commissioning happens around the same
  time as final furniture placement) — unlike ordinary lighting, which
  can just be "turned on" once wired, a smart system needs a real
  commissioning/setup phase.
- **Wired vs. wireless (Wi-Fi) control backbone tradeoff**: wired control
  signals are described as fast and reliable; Wi-Fi is faster/easier to
  install but less stable — a real practical consequence given
  (a Wi-Fi outage means repeatedly re-issuing voice commands that don't
  register).
- **Core system architecture, three components**: a controller (routes
  information and issues commands, aggregates sensor data), sensors
  (e.g. leak/motion detectors), and actuators (relays/devices that
  execute the commanded action) — worked example given: a motion sensor
  signal reaches the controller over a twisted-pair connection, the
  controller matches it against a stored scenario, then signals a relay
  to turn on the light.
- **Four smart-home control methods, in the source's own ranked
  preference**: physical push-button switches (a "hatted"/pressable
  design specifically, not a toggle), a phone app, and — the stated
  favorite — a voice assistant (Alice/Siri). **Scenario-based control is
  called the single most valuable smart-home feature**: instead of
  hitting many individual switches, one voice command or button press
  triggers a whole lighting/scene scenario (e.g. distinct
  morning/day/evening light levels).
- **⚠️ Cross-brand device-compatibility caution, a real buying rule**:
  if self-installing, check which communication protocol devices
  actually speak to each other in, not just the brand name — mixing
  many different brands (Yandex, Xiaomi, Google, Redmond, etc.) into one
  system is described as genuinely difficult to get working reliably.
  **Explicit recommendation: hire a specialized smart-home integration
  company rather than self-assembling**, specifically because a
  correctly-integrated system is described as reliably excellent, while
  a self-assembled one is the source of most negative "smart homes are
  annoying" sentiment (attributed to well-intentioned DIY assembly done
  without protocol-compatibility knowledge).
- **Smart-lock detail, with a real safety/reliability caveat**: unlocks
  by phone, fingerprint, code, or card; **all smart locks run on battery
  power, not mains** — a low-battery warning beeps well before failure,
  and an externally-accessible 9V "crown" battery terminal lets you
  power the lock briefly from outside to get in if the internal battery
  fully dies. Losing a physical key is framed as a real recurring
  problem specifically with children, motivating the fingerprint/code/
  card options as more loss-proof than a physical key (with a
  fingerprint-loss joke aside, i.e. not literally a design factor).
- **Motion-sensor use cases, real examples given**: garage lighting
  (auto-on entering, auto-off once the car has left) and — a genuinely
  concrete anecdote — a client whose cat wouldn't use the litter box
  without light, solved with a motion-triggered bathroom light rather
  than leaving a light on permanently.
- **Smart kettle/coffee-maker real functional benefit beyond
  convenience**: some smart kettles hold a *specific target temperature*
  (e.g. lower temperature specifically for white/green tea, since
  full-boil water is stated to scorch delicate tea leaves and destroy
  aroma) rather than just full boil — a genuine functional advantage
  over a standard kettle, not just automation for its own sake. Some
  models also refuse to heat with no water present, flagged as a
  child-safety benefit.
- **⚠️ Motorized curtains/blinds — a real physical threshold where manual
  operation stops being practical, not just a convenience upgrade**:
  above roughly **3-3.5m ceiling height**, manual pull-cords/rods
  genuinely stop working even with pull-rod attachments — motorization
  becomes the only practical option, not merely nicer. Heavy curtains
  are also flagged as prone to *literal mechanical damage* from repeated
  manual pulling at the fastening points over time, independent of
  ceiling height — a durability, not just convenience, argument for
  motorization on heavy fabric.
- **LED strip color preference, real installed-base data point**: per
  the source's own installation experience, the strong majority of
  clients end up choosing a simple warm-white dimmable strip over full
  RGB — RGB is described as mainly useful for deliberate accent/attention
  effects (their own stated example: exhibition-booth lighting in Moscow
  and the Emirates to draw visitor attention), not as a typical
  residential choice.
- **⚠️ CO2 sensor — a real (anonymized) cautionary anecdote about an
  unintended consequence**: a CO2 sensor is marketed for air-quality/
  ventilation triggering (elevated CO2 correlates with impaired
  cognitive function in a crowded room), but one client's app flagged an
  unexpected CO2 spike in the bedroom while the client himself was away
  — leading him to conclude infidelity had occurred at home. Recorded as
  a real, if unusual, illustration that continuous environmental sensing
  data can have consequences well beyond its intended HVAC-automation
  purpose — worth being aware of, not a technical rule.
- **Smart-plug functional uses beyond energy-saving**: making an
  otherwise "dumb" lamp app/voice-controllable by controlling power to it
  rather than the device itself; and a named real case — a commercial
  tenant's radiant floor heating kept being left running by staff after
  hours, fixed by a temperature-threshold automation (e.g. auto-off once
  the floor reaches ~23°C) rather than relying on staff compliance.
- **⚠️ Leak/gas-shutoff sensors — explicit "basic safety, not optional"
  framing, from a first-hand account of a real flood**: the source
  personally experienced two real water-pipe-burst floods (one his own
  fault, one not, both his responsibility as the party managing the
  renovation) *before* leak sensors were standard practice for his
  company; leak sensors physically close a shutoff valve automatically
  on contact with water, faster than a person could physically get home
  after being called. A parallel gas-sensor-triggered automatic shutoff
  is described as standard for elderly-occupant households specifically
  (forgotten unlit gas burners named as the risk case).
- **House/villa-specific additions beyond an apartment's scope** (flagged
  as house-specific, not this project's own apartment context): exterior
  motion-triggered lighting; heated storm drains/steps/walkways
  (freeze/ice-safety, explicitly recommending storm-drain heat-tracing
  always be included to prevent burst pipes from ice damming); perimeter
  security/alarm systems; backup/uninterruptible power for whole-house
  outages (common in Russian private-house areas per the source, with an
  automatic SMS alert on power loss); automatic gate control (including
  a fire-truck-access use case: gates can auto-open on a fire-alarm
  trigger even if no one is home); roof anti-icing/heat-trace systems
  (framed as effectively impossible to manage manually and genuinely
  safety-relevant — falling ice/snow load risk) — **with an explicit
  warning against DIY roof heat-tracing**: a real Moscow residential
  fire (~400m² roof) was attributed to an improperly self-installed roof
  heating system, used as the reasoning for "trust professionals for
  this specific system."
- **Design-process sequencing point, corroborating this vault's existing
  "plan smart-home alongside electrical, not after" rule from other
  sources**: a smart-home system must be professionally designed before
  or alongside the electrical rough-in design project, not decided
  during/after construction.
