## Planning Rules — the eight layout types she argues against

Each comes with a stated mechanism, which is why this is worth extracting rather than treating as an opinion list.

1. **⚠️ Pass-through rooms (проходные комнаты) — and she gives a usable numeric rule.** The modern form is a kitchen-living room that serves as the entrance to the WC, to one bedroom, to another bedroom and to the corridor: "не гостиная, где ты спокойно отдыхаешь, а чёрт знает что," and worse still if a sofa bed there is someone's sleeping place. **Her rule: a living room should have an entrance to at most one other room besides the corridor.** **The permitted case: a living room opening into a master block** (master bedroom plus its own bathroom) in a three-room flat is normal. **The failure case: a living room opening into the master bedroom *and* the first *and* second children's rooms — it becomes "сплошной перекрёсток."**
2. **⚠️ "Вагон" rooms — long and narrow with the window at one end.** Consequence without replanning: **part of the room is dark and part is light, and furniture ends up along one wall**, leaving a passage she puts at **40 cm** — "там даже самая худая задница застрянет." **With replanning they work well** — she reports her studio zoning exactly these rooms in Stalinka projects, splitting off a separate bedroom, dressing room and pantry. **The verdict is therefore conditional on whether replanning is in scope**, which is the actionable part. She notes this type is currently very common.
3. **Few windows, or a large apartment with windows on one side only.** The far half becomes "царство сплошной тьмы" with no direct light even on a sunny day. Her framing is climatic: half the year is winter and effectively twilight even by day, so accepting a layout that removes daylight from most rooms compounds an existing deficit. **Many developers do this.**
4. **⚠️ Curved/oval external contour, and this is the most cost-specific item.** Four separate consequences: **any cut of any floor material along the curve looks bad**; **convectors for semicircular windows cost 1.5–2× more**, and **anything non-standard ("не квадратно-гнездовое") costs substantially more** generally; **the panoramic window "сжирает полкомнаты"**, leaving furniture either centred or along only two walls. **Her conclusion is a competence threshold, not a prohibition**: such layouts demand a genuinely professional ergonomics-and-planning workup, and without paying for one "сами вы, как обычный обыватель, с такой планировкой не справитесь."
5. **⚠️ Long, narrow, useless balconies — the Soviet panel «балкон-вагон».** Long but so narrow nothing fits: you can place something at each end, **and the ~4 m middle stretch is unusable simply for lack of depth.** **Second consequence, and it is a same-channel corroboration**: such balconies materially reduce light reaching the room, because light is lost first at the balcony glazing, then at the solid lower parapet block, then at the room's own window — **"в жилых комнатах постоянно полумрак."** **Her buying-side point: you pay for that useless run when you buy the apartment, never use it, and get a darker room as well.**
6. **⚠️ Acute-angled or sector-shaped rooms — she calls this the worst of all.** Four mechanisms: the acute corners **cannot be used functionally** (no sofa, bed or cabinet fits); left empty they still **"подсознательно нас триггерят"** because sharp spaces read as uncomfortable or even dangerous rather than restful; **any non-rectangular furniture made to square them off costs substantially more**, being a bespoke item needing templates and per-shelf cutting; and **floor or tile runs against an angled wall produce sliver cuts that "выглядят как щепки."** **⚠️ The squaring-up cost, stated as a number: building plasterboard constructions to straighten the walls consumes 5–7% of usable floor area.** Her tolerance: one odd-angled storeroom is fine; **acute-angled living rooms are a reason to walk away.**
7. **⚠️ Almost all walls load-bearing — typical of panel houses.** Room and corridor boundaries are fixed. **The one common exception she names: cutting an opening in the wall between kitchen and living room in П-series houses (П44 and similar) — and even that needs separate approval.** Approving work on load-bearing structures is "долго, дорого," requires special projects and territorial/inspection sign-off. **Verdict: if you want individualised housing, an all-load-bearing apartment is not for you.**
8. **⚠️ Large corridors — and here she deliberately dissents from the consensus.** She notes most designers, architects and almost all laypeople treat a 25 m² corridor in a 70 m² flat as waste. **Her position: if the walls are not load-bearing, a large corridor is a major replanning opportunity**, because **RU norms permit expanding bathrooms and kitchens and relocating wet points onto non-living space, i.e. corridors.** Uses she names: separate dressing rooms, a laundry room, a WC-plus-basin where a dog's paws get washed, "крутой хозблок." **⚠️ The dissent is explicitly conditional on intent**: if you are buying a whitebox and commissioning a design project, large corridors are an asset; if you want to move into a finished flat as-is, they are wasted area.

## Planning Rules — her positive criteria for a good layout

Stated as a compact checklist at the end, and worth keeping as the constructive counterpart to the eight failures:

- **Close to square in overall form** ("квадратно-гнездовая"). If rectangular, **all corners 90°, or at minimum all but one.**
- **Windows on as many sides as possible — ideally three, at least two.**
- **Not many load-bearing walls**, and an adequate original layout needing only minor correction.
- **Her resale framing**: such housing "всегда будет в цене" — liveable, worth an investment-grade renovation, and resellable at a margin.
