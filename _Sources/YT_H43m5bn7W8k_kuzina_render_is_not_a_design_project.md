## Labor / Delivery Model — what has to happen before a render can exist

She treats the render as one stage inside a longer chain, and the value here is the chain itself.

1. **Measurements («замеры»).** **⚠️ "Клиентов, которые в состоянии сами снять досконально замеры своей территории, меньшинство"** — most clients cannot survey their own space thoroughly. If you skip the designer you can hire a measurer for this alone.
2. **Layout («планировка»).** **⚠️ Approaching a render without a layout is pointless**, and her reasoning is concrete: to make an image at all you must already know where the sofa stands, **where guests take their shoes off**, what happens in the kids' room, the configuration around the cot. Those are planning decisions, not rendering decisions.
3. **Stylistic direction** — the designer progressively establishes which style, which colours, and **which finishes suit your budget.**

## Cost Drivers — the point she makes hardest

- **⚠️ "Мы работаем над проектом не с целью нарисовать визуализацию, а с целью его воплотить."** The aim is realisation, not a picture.
- **⚠️ The consequence for budgeting: the cost of implementation must be visible from the very first stages.** From the planning and concept moment onward, the designer must already have a rough idea of the available budget for furniture, lighting, wallpaper and so on. **A concept developed without that number attached is a concept that may not be buildable.**
- **If you skip the styling stage and go direct to a visualiser, you must specify exhaustively yourself**: that sofa, from that company, in that colour, in velvet, its overall dimensions, its height, and its position in the room.
- **⚠️ And the observation that undercuts the DIY route**: even clients who insist they can picture their interior precisely **change their vision during the process** — the wishes get corrected as work proceeds. Specifying exhaustively up front assumes a stability she says does not hold in practice.

## Labor / Delivery Model — the render stage itself

- **Many designers do not produce 3D themselves**, handing it to specialists working in 3ds Max.
- **⚠️ Practically no designer gets a usable visualisation on the first pass**, and she says she checked this with her own visualiser and with colleagues. Once the space is seen in three dimensions, things not accounted for at the planning or concept stage want correcting.
  - **⚠️ The consequence for anyone going direct: you must make those corrections yourself, and that needs a trained eye. Otherwise the visualiser's first output is also your final one.**
- **⚠️ Her analogy for why working with a visualiser directly is harder than it looks**: a designer is an *interface* — like Windows showing you windows and buttons instead of raw binary. **A visualiser takes a brief and returns "task completed," and nothing else happens.** No interpretation, no pushback, no iteration unless you drive it.

## Labor / Delivery Model — the project does not end at the render

- **⚠️ After visualisation come the detailed drawings**, which are the actual instructions to builders: what sequence of actions realises the project. Her worked example: *"here is this profile, two layers of plasterboard over it, so that is this thickness, therefore this dimension."*
- **⚠️ Drawings sometimes correct the visualisation, and she frames this as normal rather than a failure**: dimensions can shift at the drawing stage. **The overall concept and the colours do not change; furniture or room dimensions may.** Useful for setting client expectations — a render is not a contract.

## Labor / Delivery Model — when a render is genuinely unnecessary

- **Projects without 3D exist**, either to save money or because the client wants specific items from specific shops, where visualising is slower and more expensive.
- **⚠️ The honest trade she names: skip the render and the nuances that would have been polished during the 3D-correction pass surface on site instead.** They do not disappear; they move to the most expensive place to discover them.
- **⚠️ But she concedes a legitimate alternative**: many designers who constantly deliver realisations have a trained enough eye that **a good collage suffices** — a flatter, simpler image the designer makes and edits themselves, approves with the client, and sends straight to site.
- **Her conclusion, against her own most saleable deliverable: "сказать, что 3D визуализация — это непременный атрибут того, что у вас будет происходить на объекте, нельзя."** It is pleasant to look at, and **easier to reach agreement with a client on** — but it is not a precondition for a good outcome on site.
