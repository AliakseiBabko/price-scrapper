## Case 1 — Bedroom/Study Dual-Purpose Room (relevant to `06_Small_Bedroom`)

- **Video-call ("Zoom") background design should match the room's
  existing fixed materials and reflect the occupant's profession**: the
  source chose laminated MDF wall panels (with a metal trim strip to
  fill panel-width gaps, avoiding on-site cutting) toned to match the
  room's existing floor and a built-in tall cabinet, for a mother's
  remote-work video-call backdrop — deliberately "serious"-reading
  given her stated conservative profession (investment/law).
- **When trimming laminated wall panels to fit a wall height, cut only
  the top row, not the bottom** — keeps the factory-laminated edge
  intact at the bottom, where an exposed raw MDF edge would be exposed
  to floor-washing moisture and could swell.
- **Reduce competing pattern/color sources to one unifying accent
  rather than removing all pattern**: a room combining a blue Roman
  blind, a multicolor throw, and a high-contrast black/white bedspread
  was fixed by replacing just the bedspread with a solid blue matching
  the blind — not by stripping every pattern from the room.
- **DIY headboard-from-matching-panel technique**: build a custom
  headboard from the same finish/material as an existing built-in
  cabinet already in the room (matching a known supplier article/color
  code), extended visually to the doorway trim, specifically to unify
  disconnected-feeling furniture into one composition; **set headboard
  height by aligning it to the bottom edge of a nearby wall outlet**
  (a concrete, repeatable sizing reference rather than an arbitrary
  height).
- **⚠️ Physical furniture-placement mock-up technique, height included
  not just floor footprint**: when a floor-plan drawing leaves genuine
  doubt about whether a furniture placement will feel workable in
  practice, mock it up physically with boxes or similar props at the
  *actual height* the furniture will occupy, not just its floor
  outline taped down — clearance problems near head/shoulder height
  (e.g. beside a staircase or walkway) won't show up in a floor-only
  mock-up.
- **⚠️ Bed-under-window placement objection explicitly challenged**: the
  common objections (harder window-cleaning, draft risk when opened)
  are argued as overstated in practice — window cleaning happens only a
  few times a year, and where AC is present, a too-warm room is more
  likely to get the AC turned on than the window opened. Presented as
  the source's own lived experience (personally lived with this exact
  bed placement for several years, no issues), not an independently
  verified universal claim.
- **A large headboard unit with integrated shelving removes the need
  for separate bedside tables** — the shelving built into the headboard
  itself absorbs that storage role.
- **Double-layered curtains (a second curtain row) around a bed
  positioned at a window create a cozier, partially-enclosed "alcove"
  feel** — presented as one of two valid options (the alternative,
  single-layer curtains, reads airier/more open); an explicit stylistic
  trade-off, not a universal recommendation either way.
- **Preserve one small deliberate personalization element for a room's
  actual (non-paying) occupant even within an otherwise coordinated
  adult-led redesign** — here, a small contrasting black/white graphic-
  print cushion and a personally-requested picture were kept/added as
  the room's one individuality accent, rather than fully overriding the
  actual occupant's own stated preference.
- **A slim folding guest mattress (stored in a closet or under a lift-
  bed) can replace a bulky sleeper armchair while preserving the same
  overnight-guest sleeping capacity** and freeing meaningfully more
  daily floor space than the chair occupied.

## Case 2 — Kitchen-Living Room With Wallpaper (relevant to `04_Living_and_Dining_Room` / general furniture arrangement)

- **⚠️ A large gap between a high-mounted wall TV and a low-profile TV
  console reads as visually "ugly"/disharmonious** — when a TV is
  mounted high, use a taller console table (with a shallower ~30cm
  depth, vs. a standard TV cabinet's greater depth) instead of a
  standard low TV cabinet, to close that visual gap.
- **Same physical height-inclusive furniture mock-up technique as Case
  1**, applied here to test whether a console table would obstruct a
  stairway walkway — floor-only clearance looked fine on the drawing,
  but the source flags real doubt about whether the table's actual
  height/depth would still work in practice near the stairs, and
  recommends the physical mock-up test rather than trusting the
  drawing alone.
- **Swapping two armchairs for two loveseats/settees near a tight
  walkway can, counter-intuitively, free up more usable circulation
  space than the smaller chairs did** — a specific worked example
  achieving just over 1m of clear stairway-access width by placing one
  loveseat flush to the stairway and the second against the wall with a
  10cm curtain allowance, while also adding more lounging capacity than
  the original armchairs offered.
- **Roman blinds as the fallback window treatment when adjacent kitchen
  cabinetry rules out full-length curtains**, with the blind's trim
  color matched to the room's existing wallpaper accent and the kitchen
  finish for visual cohesion; curtain/blind rods mounted noticeably
  higher than the window opening itself to visually elongate the
  window.
- **Size-by-visible-reference technique for unlabeled decor pieces**:
  when a client's photo doesn't give exact dimensions for existing
  decor (here, a set of bird pictures), infer the missing size from
  another object already visible in the same photo at a known/
  comparable scale (here: a picture already resting on top of kitchen
  cabinetry) rather than guessing.
- **A shallow-depth (~40cm) storage cabinet remains functionally usable
  even in a tight-clearance spot** (here, under a staircase) as long as
  the drawers can still physically open within the available approach
  space — a real cost/practicality trade-off worth accepting rather
  than ruling the location out entirely.
