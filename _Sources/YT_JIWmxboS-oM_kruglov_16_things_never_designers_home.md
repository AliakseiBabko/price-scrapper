## Genuinely New Content

**Faucet/mixer finish — new topic, no prior page coverage found**:
avoid black, plain chrome, or white mixer finishes — all three show
water spots, fingerprints, and (for chrome specifically) surface
corrosion over time, in the presenter's stated experience. Prefer
blackened/gunmetal-type steel or **matte chrome** specifically (a
"contradictory-sounding" name for a genuine finish category) — both
resist showing marks far better, though even blackened steel isn't
fully immune on some models.

**Toilet color — new specific reasoning**: prefer a plain white toilet
with a white lid over a colored unit (gray/brown/etc.) — if a component
part (most often the seat/lid) later breaks, sourcing a color-matched
replacement becomes genuinely difficult; white parts are universally
available. Black toilet+lid sets are acceptable as a deliberate
alternative for those who want it.

**Standalone bidet fixture (not just the toilet-bidet-function
question) — a distinct point from this page's existing "bidet function
redundant if you have a hygienic shower" content**: installing a fully
separate bidet basin (its own supply/drain plumbing, its own floor
footprint) is called an irrational use of a bathroom's limited area —
this is a space/plumbing-cost argument against the *fixture* itself,
not the toilet-vs-hygienic-shower functional-redundancy argument
already on `Fixtures_Mixers_and_Sinks.md`. Framed as a genuine personal-
habit exception: someone already used to a standalone bidet may keep
wanting one regardless.

**⚠️ Freestanding tub — restates this channel's own Round 1 content
(`YT_9dfEdjOewng`) almost exactly (avoid using one as the sole shower
source, especially enclosed on 3 sides), but surfaces a genuine
same-channel contradiction worth flagging explicitly**: this video

(also Kruglov/Ontario, source `YT_Tyl0yPQkO5g`, processed earlier the
same day as this batch) lists a **freestanding bathtub as the explicit

**Push-to-open ("тип-он") mechanism — a refinement of this channel's
own existing "avoid entirely" position on `03_Kitchen/Kitchen_Furniture.md`**:
this video is less absolute — a small, localized use (e.g. a single
attic-level cabinet or one chest of drawers) is called acceptable; the
"avoid entirely" framing is specifically for using the mechanism across
*all* of a household's furniture. **New specific failure detail**:
knee/hip-height-mounted push-to-open mechanisms specifically suffer
frequent false triggers (incidental contact opening the facade
unintentionally) — sharper than this page's existing general "leaning
against it opens it" caution.

**Windowsill material — two new material options beyond this vault's
existing four-type list (plastic/tile/artificial-acrylic-stone/wood) on
`13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/analysis/Windows_Slope_Finishing.md`**:
**large-format porcelain tile** and **quartz agglomerate** are both
named as premium windowsill-material alternatives to plastic, alongside
the existing acrylic-stone option — plastic is called something no
designer would keep in their own home regardless of budget.

**White built-in kitchen appliances — new topic for this vault's
kitchen-appliance content**: avoid white built-in ovens/microwaves/
cooktops — far more limited model selection across every brand (black
is available from essentially every manufacturer at every price tier,
making replacement/matching easy), and **white shades genuinely differ
between manufacturers, and even between different appliance types from
the *same* manufacturer** (an oven and a built-in microwave from one
brand, made in different factories, can have visibly different
whites) — a real color-mismatch risk unique to white that black doesn't
share. Secondary aesthetic note: mismatched control-panel display
colors (e.g. red digital clock on one unit, blue on another) reads as a
minor but real inconsistency.
