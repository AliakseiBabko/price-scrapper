## Mistakes / Warnings — the five flooring categories

- **⚠️ Glossy tile / polished marble / glossy porcelain**: shows every scratch, water spot, footprint, and dust mark almost instantly, especially in high-traffic zones with children/pets — ages visibly within roughly **2 years**. Also **slippery when wet**, a practical safety issue beyond the aesthetic one. **Fix: matte or textured finish** — hides minor soiling/wear far better, reads as more current, and (his claim) can deliver the same premium marble/stone-look aesthetic without the glossy downside; if a glossy material is specifically wanted, prefer a matte-finish version of the same stone/porcelain rather than abandoning the material category.
- **⚠️ Cold grey laminate/vinyl — the "2010s budget-flip" look**: a 2010s-era trend for fast-turnaround new-build/rental/budget renovations, especially cold light-grey tones with a blue/concrete undertone. Now reads as cheap/economy-segment by association **regardless of the material's actual quality** — and reads visually cold, particularly in low-sunlight climates. **⚠️ Named trend-report figure (attribution not independently verifiable — treat as `unverified`)**: **46% of buyers now prefer warm natural wood tones vs. only 10% preferring grey.** Real-estate-agent-sourced anecdote (unnamed acquaintances): apartments with grey floors reportedly generate less buyer interest. **Fix for an already-grey floor**: warm rugs, wood furniture, natural-texture textiles/decor to counteract the cold read. **For new floors**: light-to-medium warm wood tones — oak, walnut, maple.
- **⚠️ Concrete / microcement floors (raw screed or decorative polished-concrete finish)**: cold underfoot without underfloor heating, even in summer. Porous — water/tea/oil/marker stains sink in and become effectively permanent, or require costly professional restoration. Develops micro-cracks over time; surface dulls, can pit/crumble at edges even when well-sealed. **Requires an extremely flat substrate** — any unevenness, crack, or level change is visible after finishing. **Full process is multi-stage and specialist-dependent**: rough + fine grinding, chemical impregnation, micro-crack filling, protective top coat — genuinely more expensive than it appears, and **needs periodic reapplication of the protective sealant**, an ongoing cost most buyers don't anticipate.
- **⚠️ Terrazzo (cement/polymer composite with large stone/marble/glass aggregate)**: fine as a small accent — entryway, small bathroom — but at large scale (especially with a bold, high-contrast aggregate pattern) becomes the dominant visual element in the room, forcing every other material/furniture/decor choice to defer to the floor's pattern or risk visual chaos. **Owners report fatigue with a bold large-scale terrazzo floor within roughly 2–3 years** — and unlike a wall colour, a floor "can't just be repainted" once the owner tires of it. Recommendation: use only as a **localised accent**, never across a large primary living area.
- **⚠️ Bathroom floor tile with excessive small-format joints/grout lines**: a mosaic or small-format tile pattern looks distinctive initially, but the high volume of grout collects grime fastest in a bathroom's high-moisture, high-contaminant environment (water, soap, shampoo residue, pet hair). **White grout discolours within months; dark grout looks visibly "dirty" even freshly cleaned** — described as not fully solved even by epoxy grout. **Fix: large-format matte tile with minimal joints** — modern stone-look porcelain slabs, cut for minimal seams, visually expand a small bathroom while genuinely reducing grime-collection area versus small-format tile.
