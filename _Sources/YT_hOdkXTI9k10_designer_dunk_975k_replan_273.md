## Planning Rules — Wet-Zone Placement & Real Full-Apartment Replan

- **Wet-zone (bathroom/kitchen) placement should be driven by proximity to existing plumbing stacks/risers first, never by aesthetic preference (e.g. "feng shui")** — the critiqued designer placed the primary ("master") bathroom based on stated feng-shui/window-orientation reasoning, with no working stack nearby. Consequence: no floor drain existed near the intended fixture positions, forcing (as the designer's own proposed fix) raising the screed by 25cm across that zone and installing a sump/macerator pump (sololift) to force drainage sideways to a distant stack — the client's decisive reason for firing the designer was specifically this combination (a very loud sump pump positioned directly adjacent to the bedroom).
- **When a unit has no living space below it (top floor or over a non-residential space), any wet zone can genuinely go anywhere — but "can go anywhere" doesn't mean placement should ignore stack proximity anyway**, since stack-independent wet-zone placement still forces expensive drainage workarounds (raised screed, pump) even when there's no code violation risk from doing so.
- **A "master" (en-suite) bathroom accessible only from the primary bedroom, and a fully separate kids' bathroom, are treated as distinct planning requirements with different fixture lists** — a worked full technical brief for a real project: master bath needs a large shower stall (not a tub), toilet, ≥80cm-wide sink, large towel warmer; kids' bath needs a ≥1.7m tub, double-basin sink (≥60cm per child), and its own toilet.
- **Full-apartment layout replan sequencing, demonstrated step by step**: lock wet zones (bathrooms) to available stacks first; then place fixed-requirement rooms (bedrooms with strict area/fixture needs); then work outward to flexible-use spaces (hallway, storage) last, checking each room against the full technical brief before moving to the next — matches this store's existing hard-vs-flexible-dimension planning framework, applied here at full-apartment scale across many rooms in one worked example.
- **Real technical-brief items recorded as a worked full checklist for a family with two children**: separate his/her wardrobes in the primary bedroom; a home-office nook with document storage in the bedroom; a floor mirror for trying on clothes; a kids' room with bunk beds, a shared 80cm-per-child wardrobe, two separate desk/study nooks, ≥3 shelving sections for toys/books, and a 2m-wide "sports corner" (two Swedish walls, one per child); an entry zone with a quick-access mudroom, coat closet, and makeup zone; a laundry/utility zone; a separate work-from-home nook outside the bedroom and kids' room, near a dedicated storage zone; a sports-equipment closet; a large seasonal-storage walk-in; a household-supplies closet; an adult home-gym corner (treadmill + exercise bike); and a ~30m² kitchen-living combo with a large cooking zone, built-in fridge/oven/microwave column, a table seating 6, an L-shaped sofa ≥3m, a ≥65" TV with TV stand, and open shelving for décor.
