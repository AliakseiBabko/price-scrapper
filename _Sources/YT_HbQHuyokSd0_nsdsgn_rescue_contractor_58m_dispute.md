## ⚠️⚠️ The failure taxonomy — six named, costed failures

### 1. Door frames installed before the finished floor — 1.5 million ₽

- **1.5 million ₽ (≈$19,600) buried in wrongly-installed door frames and concealed-door pockets (пеналы).**
- **⚠️ THE RULE HE STATES: «ДВЕРНЫЕ КОРОБКИ СТОИТ УСТАНАВЛИВАТЬ ТОЛЬКО ПОСЛЕ УКЛАДКИ НАПОЛЬНОГО ЧИСТОВОГО ПОКРЫТИЯ.»**
- **⚠️⚠️ THIS IS IN TENSION WITH A FINDING THE VAULT ALREADY HOLDS** — that concealed-door pockets are a **rough-stage** element, built into the partition before plastering. Both cannot be literally true of the same component.
- **⚠️ THE RECONCILIATION I OFFER, EXPLICITLY AS A HYPOTHESIS AND NOT AS A FINDING: the two claims are about different objects on the same order line. The POCKET (пенал) is a rough-stage carcass and must go in before plastering; the FRAME/LINING (коробка) and the leaf are finishing items and must go in after the floor. So the sequencing rule is "ORDER early, INSTALL late", and the failure mode is treating one purchase as one site operation.**
  - ⚠️ **Route this as a flagged open question on the door pages, tagged as needing a third source to resolve. Do not present it as settled.** Neither source addresses the other's case.
- **⚠️ AND THE LIABILITY POINT, which is the reusable half: «НОЛЬ ДАЮТ СТРОИТЕЛИ.»** The finished floor level (the zero datum) is set by the builders, so if the frames are set to a wrong or provisional zero, the responsibility sits with whoever issued it — and the cost lands on the client regardless.

### 2. A collapsed playroom ceiling — 500,000 ₽, from one missing tool

- **A plasterboard ceiling in a playroom FELL.** Remediation 500,000 ₽ (≈$6,500).
- **⚠️⚠️ ROOT CAUSE, AND IT IS EXCELLENT BECAUSE IT IS SO SMALL: there was NO PLASTERBOARD LIFTER (подъёмник) on site, so the boards were held up and the clips PRESSED BY HAND — and hand pressure does not seat the clip, so under a load of about 30 kg/m² they progressively LET GO.**
- → **A ceiling failure caused by the absence of a piece of lifting equipment, not by a material, a design or a calculation. The clip either seats or it does not, and a person holding a board overhead cannot seat it.**
- **⚠️ This is a checkable site observation available to a non-expert client: is there a board lifter on site when the ceiling goes up?** That is a far more useful test than reading a specification, and it belongs on the ceiling pages as an inspection item.

### 3. The garage conversion — a rescue with an honest ceiling on the outcome

- A garage converted to a habitable room, in which: **the window frame was the WRONG SIZE**, the timber was **not dry**, and there was **no insulation to the facade and none to the I-beam**.
- His prediction for it as found: **it will twist and the glazing will burst.**
- His actual intervention: **screw-fix and insulate what could be reached — «А ДАЛЬШЕ УЖЕ КАК ПОВЕЗЁТ».**
- → **⚠️ Worth recording precisely because the rescue verdict is "and after that, luck". A remediation on top of an unsound build buys probability, not a fix — which is the argument for catching it during the build rather than after.**

### 4. The 4-million-rouble smart-home panel that caught fire

- **A KNX installation with a panel costing about 4 million ₽ (≈$52,100).**
- **⚠️ His own electrical laboratory found 57 VIOLATIONS in it.**
- **⚠️⚠️ AND THE PANEL CAUGHT FIRE WHEN AN EV CHARGER WAS LOAD-TESTED** — i.e. it failed at the first realistic sustained load, which is exactly the load a modern house adds.
- **Rebuilding it to the design the client had ALREADY PAID FOR: a further 300,000–400,000 ₽ (≈$3,900–5,200).**
- → **⚠️ The transferable points: (a) an expensive bill of materials is not an installation quality signal — the components were premium and the workmanship was not; (b) a distribution board is the one element where third-party testing is both cheap and decisive, and an independent electrical laboratory is a purchasable service; (c) load-test with the actual worst-case load, because a panel that passes at idle tells you nothing.**
- This routes to `12_Engineering_and_Systems` electrical pages and to the smart-home page, and it is the strongest counterweight the vault has to the channel's generally positive smart-home content.

### 5. Blackmail, a self-awarded salary, and paid-for work never done

- **⚠️ THE BLACKMAIL, VERBATIM IN SUBSTANCE: «ЕСЛИ ВЫ МНЕ СЕЙЧАС НЕ ЗАПЛАТИТЕ, У МЕНЯ ОТСЮДА УЙДЁТ БРИГАДА.»** → The crew's departure used as leverage for an unscheduled payment. **The mechanism only works if the client has no alternative crew and no retained sum — which is what the control measures below are for.**
- **⚠️ A 3-million-of-10-million self-allocated "salary" (≈$39,100 of ≈$130,000)** — 30% of a tranche taken by the contractor as his own remuneration, not agreed as such.
- **Paid-for waste removal never performed:** four skips' worth still standing on the first floor, with **rooms physically inaccessible** as a result. → **A trivially verifiable deliverable that went unverified for the whole build, which is the tell about how absent the client was.**

### 6. ⚠️⚠️ Materials bought THROUGH the contractor, then held hostage

- On dismissal, the contractor retained materials the client had paid for: **European lighting, finish sanitaryware, smart-home controllers and servers.**
- **⚠️ THE STRUCTURAL LESSON, WHICH IS THE MOST DIRECTLY ACTIONABLE THING IN THE SOURCE: what matters is WHOSE NAME IS ON THE SUPPLY CONTRACT.** Materials procured *through* a contractor are, in a dispute, in the contractor's possession and often in his name — so they become leverage precisely at the moment the relationship fails.
- → **Buy long-lead and high-value equipment on the client's own contract, delivered to the client, even when the contractor offers to handle it.** This is a decision made at procurement time and irreversible afterwards.
- ⚠️ Note this cuts against the convenience argument the vault records elsewhere for letting a single contractor handle комплектация. **Route as a genuine trade-off: contractor-procurement buys coordination and costs you custody.**

## ⚠️ Technical supervision — the control mechanism, with named deliverables

- **His recommendation: engage INDEPENDENT TECHNICAL SUPERVISION (независимый технический надзор) for the WHOLE build — «ДАЖЕ ЕСЛИ ПОДРЯДЧИК — ДРУГ».**
- **⚠️ THREE NAMED DELIVERABLES, which is what makes this routable rather than generic advice:**
  1. **Checking the estimate document** (смета) — before signature, as a document.
  2. **Checking executed works** against what was billed.
  3. **RECORDING OF CONCEALED WORKS** (фиксация скрытых работ) — the photographic/documentary record of everything that is about to be buried.
- **A practical route to finding one: search «НЕЗАВИСИМАЯ ТЕХНИЧЕСКАЯ ЭКСПЕРТИЗА».**
- **⚠️ AND THE TIMING POINT: «НЕТ ЛУЧШЕГО ВРЕМЕНИ, КОГДА СОСКАКИВАТЬ»** — there is no good moment to walk away from a failing contractor, so the decision is always made under sunk cost, which is why the control has to be in place from the start rather than deployed as a rescue.
- ⚠️ This is a substantial addition to `Technical_Supervision.md`, and the deliverables list is the first *itemised* version of that role in the vault.

## ⚠️ The victim profile, stated plainly

Four attributes, offered as the pattern that makes a client vulnerable:

1. **Wealthy** — the sums are large enough that individual overpayments do not register.
2. **Accustomed to being served** — used to delegating and to things simply being handled.
3. **Emotionally invested** in the project.
4. **⚠️ ABSENT.**

- → **⚠️ The fourth is the operative one, and the other three explain it. Every failure in this source is one a present, mildly sceptical client would have caught: skips in a room, no lifter on site, frames set before the floor.** This belongs on the contract/supervision pages as the mechanism, not as a character sketch.

## ⚠️ As-built documentation that documents nothing

- **CCTV outlets that DID NOT EXIST**, despite paid-for as-built (исполнительная) documentation showing them.
- **⚠️ THE FOREMAN'S ANSWER WHEN ASKED WHY: «Я ТАК РЕШИЛ.»**
- → **As-built documentation is only worth what its verification was worth. A drawing produced by the party being checked is not a check** — which is exactly deliverable (3) above, and why it has to be someone else's job.

## ⚠️⚠️ Access hatches made only to the filters — and the incentive analysis behind it

- **AC/ventilation access hatches were provided only to the FILTERS**, not to the serviceable equipment behind them.
- **⚠️ The previous crew's stated reasoning: «НУ, ЕСЛИ СЛОМАЕТСЯ, ТО СЛОМАЕТСЯ, БУДУТ ЛОМАТЬ ПОТОЛОК.»**
- **⚠️⚠️ AND THE DESIGNER'S INCENTIVE ANALYSIS, WHICH IS THE BEST GENERAL IDEA IN THE SOURCE: A CONTRACTOR'S PLANNING HORIZON ENDS AT HIS WARRANTY PERIOD.** Anything that fails after the warranty is, from his side, not a cost — so serviceability, which by definition pays off later, is systematically under-provided, and no amount of goodwill fixes a horizon mismatch.
- → **This is a *general* explanation for a whole class of defects the vault records individually** (unreachable valves, buried junction boxes, no hatch to a pump). Route it as the mechanism on the access/serviceability content, and pair it with the Round 4 **serviceability** definition, which it now supplies a reason for.
- ⚠️ **And it argues for a specific client action: serviceability items must be specified and checked by the client or the supervisor, because the contractor's interests do not reach them.**
