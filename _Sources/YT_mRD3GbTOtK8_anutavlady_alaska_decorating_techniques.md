## Case — Kitchen-Living-Dining Decor Refinement, Alaska

- **Roman-blind mounting-height alignment rule**: when a room's curtain
  rods are mounted at ceiling height but an existing Roman blind hangs
  lower on the same or an adjacent window, raise the Roman blind's mount
  to the same height — makes the window read taller and the whole
  window-treatment composition more harmonious, rather than leaving
  window treatments at inconsistent heights in the same sightline.
- **Match window treatment across visually-adjacent zones**: two windows
  serving connected living/dining zones (one fully dressed with
  curtains, one left bare) were treated identically (same curtain style
  added to both) for visual consistency within one continuous sightline.
- **⚠️ One-accent-wall rule (general design principle, clearly stated)**:
  a room should have only **one** accent wall — creating two identical
  accent treatments in the same room cancels the accent function itself
  ("an accent is something there's little of, something that stands
  out — if you make two identical ones, they stop being accents").
  Explicitly stated as a reason **not** to mirror an existing accent
  wall's treatment on an opposite blank wall, even though that would
  have been the simplest option.
- **Framed-print gallery technique, two vertical rows flanking a
  window**: six matching baguette/passe-partout frames from a market
  retailer, filled with free downloadable art (curated by theme/color
  palette on a source found via Pinterest), arranged as two vertical
  columns — chosen over a single accent-wall treatment specifically
  because that wall couldn't have a second accent wall (see rule above).
- **Battery-powered/rechargeable picture lights as a no-rewiring
  workaround specifically for an insulated stick-frame exterior wall**:
  in a house built with insulated frame construction (common in a cold
  climate like Alaska), fishing new wire through an exterior wall for
  picture lighting is impractical — a cordless, rechargeable picture
  light avoids the issue entirely. Extends this batch's earlier
  accumulator-light workaround (`DvQTBiItLpY`'s illuminated mirror) to a
  new context (art lighting on an insulated exterior wall, not just "no
  nearby outlet").
- **Furniture-isolation fix via echoed color/accessories**: an
  otherwise-fine leather recliner chair was reading as visually
  disconnected from the rest of the room; adding nearby accessories
  (a larger vase, warm rust/orange tones already present elsewhere, e.g.
  in the framed prints) in colors that echo the chair's own tones
  re-integrates it into the room's overall composition, without moving
  or replacing the chair itself.
- **Decor-object-to-surface proportion rule, restated for a vase**: a
  small ceramic vase looked disproportionately small on a large dining
  table; sized up to a larger vase in a warmer tone — the same
  proportion-matching principle this batch has already recorded for
  pendant-light shades (`fkoUNvB9LV8`) and picture-block sizing
  (`DvQTBiItLpY`), now applied to a tabletop decor object.
- **⚠️ "French mattress" reusable sofa-protection technique, full
  method**: a thin quilted throw (synthetic-batting-filled, similar to a
  French "matelassé" mattress pad), machine-washable and quick-drying,
  laid over a light-colored sofa specifically to protect the upholstery
  from dogs jumping on it — presented as the source's own real solution
  (she made hers herself, with decorative tassels for a more traditional
  look) rather than re-upholstering the sofa or relying on a fixed
  slipcover. Can be made reversible/double-sided and swapped seasonally;
  the source notes it's simple enough to DIY or have made by any tailor/
  atelier, and that a Russian buyer could source an equivalent through a
  named retailer (Laredoute, commercial mention).
- **Glass pendant-shade dust-visibility problem, restated** — corroborates
  this batch's existing glass-shade caution (`uwGxjv9O1kk`); the source
  explicitly recommends a matte/non-glass shade instead whenever the
  household doesn't want to dust fixtures frequently.
- **Faux-stone pendant shade chosen for dual reason**: an artificial-stone-
  finish pendant shade was chosen over a glass one specifically because
  it (a) matches a natural-materials-driven design scheme and (b) avoids
  the glass dust-visibility problem — one fixture choice solving a style
  goal and a maintenance goal simultaneously.
- **⚠️ Functional-only open-kitchen-counter styling rule**: only display
  kitchen items that are actually used regularly on open counters;
  remove purely decorative-only objects. When many functional items must
  stay visible, group them onto trays, boards, or stands rather than
  leaving them scattered — this is what prevents an open counter with
  many objects from reading as cluttered, not reducing the object count
  itself.
- **Book-cover-wrapping technique to mute a bookshelf's visual noise**:
  wrap display books in plain paper covers matching the room's palette,
  rather than removing/hiding them, to calm an overly colorful spine
  arrangement while keeping the shelf's actual books visible/available.
- **Pet-item color-coordination**: swapping grey wicker pet-toy baskets
  for warm-beige woven ones is presented as a small, easy palette-
  consistency fix for an otherwise-neutral room.
- **⚠️ Mantel/shelf-styling constraint under a wall-mounted TV + soundbar**:
  no decor items can be placed directly on or immediately around a
  shelf/mantel that also holds a mounted TV and soundbar — physically
  interferes with sound path and screen sightline. The fix used here:
  add a substantial wood mantel shelf *below* the TV/soundbar zone as
  the actual decor surface, leaving the TV/soundbar's own zone clear.
- **Composition-bridging technique using matched framed art**: two
  visually separate zones (a bookshelf wall and a TV/mantel wall) were
  tied into one cohesive composition by adding a matching pair of framed
  prints positioned to visually connect them — using repeated art as
  deliberate "connective tissue" between two otherwise-disconnected
  decorated zones.
- **Floor-plant-near-rug watering caution**: placing a floor-standing
  houseplant on or near a rug carries a real risk of water spilling onto
  the rug during watering — a practical maintenance caution to weigh
  when choosing a floor-plant location, not a reason to avoid floor
  plants altogether.
