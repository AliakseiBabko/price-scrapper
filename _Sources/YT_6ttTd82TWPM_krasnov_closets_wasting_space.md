## Rules / Heuristics — storage organisation principles

- **⚠️ Storage should be distributed by functional zone, not centralised in one room.** His core diagnostic claim: household mess is rarely caused by laziness — it's caused by not planning storage locations in advance. Named failure pattern: outerwear/coats stored in a bedroom wardrobe while the entryway sits empty (just a console and mirror) purely because the entryway wasn't planned for storage at the outset — even when entryway floor area would allow it. **Decide the storage "brief" first** (what actually needs storing, and how the household actually lives) before laying out storage — a rarely-visited second/holiday home needs far less storage than a full-time family home; a family with children needs meaningfully more, and needs it deliberately zoned rather than assumed.
- **A proper master-suite flow is the ideal, but is often a real compromise even for the practitioner's own home**: bathe → dress in an adjoining walk-in closet → exit already dressed. His own flat, despite its size, lacks a true master-suite adjacency — he must walk the length of the apartment between the shower and the walk-in closet, which he states he would design differently if planning the layout from scratch. Recorded as an honest admission that even a design professional's own home is a planning compromise, not a showcase of the ideal.
- **Design storage around actual personal routines/habits, not a textbook layout** — his stated method with clients: ask directly how they currently get dressed/get ready, rather than assuming a fixed "correct" storage-zone template (e.g. a vanity/dressing table doesn't have to live in the walk-in closet just because a textbook master-suite diagram puts it there) — the final layout should match how the specific household actually behaves.
- **⚠️ Entryway shoe storage + heated floor combination**: hide shoes behind closed cabinet doors (not an open shelf/rack — described as visually chaotic with mismatched shoe shapes/sizes). **Combine with underfloor heating across the entryway/hallway zone**, including running the heated floor **under a "floating" (wall-bracket-mounted) shoe cabinet** so shoes stored beneath sit on a warm floor rather than a cold one — addresses the specific Russian-climate problem of shoes arriving wet/cold/snow-covered for most of the year. Requires detailed coordination with the cabinet manufacturer at design stage (bracket engineering, floor-heating layout) rather than an afterthought.
- **⚠️ Avoid coloured plastic storage bins/containers as visible storage** — flagged as one of the specific "interior killers" that cheapen a space, even inside a closed walk-in closet the household itself sees regularly. **Prefer woven/natural-fibre baskets** — described as a more tactile, higher-end-reading material even where the storage itself is fully hidden from guests.
- **⚠️ Custom/bespoke storage furniture uses genuinely more of the available volume than stock/modular furniture**: stock furniture is built to standard dimensions and therefore wastes vertical/floor-to-ceiling volume and can't be tailored to an irregular room shape; custom storage is built to use every available centimetre including full ceiling height. Costs more upfront but is framed as paying off through materially more usable storage volume over the long run — not treated as a luxury-only argument, but a functional one.
- **⚠️ Kitchens without upper cabinets — a scale-dependent tradeoff, not a universal trend to copy**: a no-upper-cabinets kitchen only makes functional sense in large houses that have a separate working/back kitchen (a staffed, catering-style prep kitchen handling the actual cooking, with its own additional fridge) — the visible "showcase" kitchen without upper storage works there because upper-cabinet storage capacity is displaced onto that second kitchen. **For an ordinary single kitchen, removing upper cabinets is a functional storage mistake regardless of the current showroom/Pinterest trend** — he explicitly invites viewers to "live a while with a kitchen with no upper cabinets" before defending the trend in the comments.
- **Storage cost/quality framing, closing point**: bespoke/made-to-order storage furniture costs more than stock positions but uses every centimetre of available volume and can be customised drawer-by-drawer/shelf-by-shelf — his stated general recommendation for anyone able to afford the difference, on long-run functional grounds rather than aesthetics alone.
