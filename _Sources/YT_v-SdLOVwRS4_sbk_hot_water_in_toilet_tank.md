## Plumbing — Hygienic-Shower Hot/Cold Backflow, a Real Named Defect Mechanism

- **⚠️ Named root cause, stated as accounting for ~90% of cases of this symptom**: a hygienic shower/bidet spray ("гигиенический душ") fixture, in many models, allows hot water supply pressure to overpower ("передавить") the cold supply line internally — hot water pushes backward into the cold-water side at the point where the fixture mixes hot and cold. Where the plumbing uses a manifold ("коллектор") distribution system, this backpressure propagates upstream through the shared cold-water manifold to **every cold-water consumer fed from that manifold** — the video's own real example: a dishwasher, a kitchen sink, and a toilet's concealed-tank installation ("инсталляция") all began receiving warm/hot water at their supposedly cold-only supply line, traced back to a single hygienic-shower fixture.
- **⚠️ Fix: install check (non-return) valves on both the hot and cold supply lines feeding the hygienic shower fixture specifically**, not elsewhere in the system — a check valve permits flow in one direction only, so installing one on each of the shower's own hot and cold feeds prevents hot water from being pushed backward into the cold line (and vice versa), keeping every other cold-water consumer genuinely cold and every hot-water consumer genuinely hot.
- **⚠️ Retrofit difficulty depends entirely on plumbing topology — manifold vs. tee-branch ("тройниковая") distribution**: on a manifold-distribution system, this fix is straightforward — cut into the two supply lines near the manifold and install the check valves there, a simple, quick task. **On a tee-branch distribution system, the check valve must instead be installed directly on the pipe run feeding the hygienic shower itself** — and that specific pipe run is typically embedded/concealed inside the wall, so retrofitting requires demolishing the bathroom floor, removing tile, and potentially disassembling built cabinetry/boxing around the pipe run to reach it — described directly as both highly labor-intensive and likely expensive compared to the manifold-system fix.
- **Preventive design rule, stated directly as the video's own bottom-line takeaway**: install check valves on both hot and cold supply lines to a hygienic shower fixture **at initial installation time**, regardless of distribution topology, specifically to avoid this defect ever surfacing — framed as cheap insurance against a fix that can become a major, disruptive retrofit later (especially under tee-branch distribution).
- **Diagnostic note for the symptom with no hygienic shower or water heater present**: if warm/hot water still appears at a fixture that should be cold-only, and there's no hygienic shower or in-unit water heater to blame, the speaker's own suggested first step is to check with neighbors on the same shared riser — implying the same backflow mechanism can originate from a neighboring unit's fixture onto a shared supply riser, not necessarily from within the affected unit's own plumbing. `single-account`, offered as a practical troubleshooting starting point rather than a fully explained mechanism in this transcript.
