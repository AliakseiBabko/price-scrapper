### Living Room #1
- **⚠️ Dining table positioned directly beside/behind a sofa in a compact room — a well-argued ergonomic critique, genuinely new**: crumbs/spills from the table land directly on the sofa; sitting on the sofa with your back to the dining area creates a subconscious "something behind me" discomfort, and hot drinks/spills from the dining side can reach the sofa occupant. Recommends either a taller/more structured sofa back for a genuine physical barrier, or increasing table-sofa distance.
- **⚠️ Overly "matchy" identical furniture reads like a showroom — a specific critique, useful counterpoint to symmetry-focused content elsewhere**: matching sofas, matching poufs, identical texture reads like a showroom rather than a lived-in home. Recommends deliberate eclecticism — an armchair of different form/material (wood or metal texture) to break uniformity and add depth.
- Art without any real point/meaning ("just to fill wall space") critiqued as adding nothing — recommends personal-story art instead (restates existing personalization content, applied concretely).

### Living Room #2
- **⚠️ Excessive track-lighting fixture COUNT, distinct from gloss/mismatched-temperature critiques already logged — this is about sheer quantity**: especially damaging in a low-ceilinged room where the fixture count visually lowers perceived ceiling height. Specific fix bundle: use diffuse-light-emitting fixture types instead of pure point sources, add floor/table lamps for softer ambient fill, or if track lighting must be kept, **paint the fixture housing/track the SAME color as the ceiling so they visually disappear** — a specific fixture-to-ceiling color-matching technique, distinct from (though related to) Round 6's wall-color-to-ceiling technique (that was wall-color-to-ceiling; this is fixture-color-to-ceiling).

### Kitchen #1
- **⚠️ Furniture-leg mismatch — a specific styling detail, genuinely new**: table and chair legs of different THICKNESS but the same black-metal finish create visual "geometric overload"/competing lines rather than cohesion. - **⚠️ Corridor-rhythm lighting in a kitchen, distinct from the fixture-COUNT critique above**: excessive point/spot lighting fixture RHYTHM more suited to a long corridor than a kitchen — a "borrowed corridor lighting rhythm doesn't suit a kitchen" critique (this is about spacing/rhythm pattern, not sheer number).

### Kitchen #2
- **⚠️ Excessive single-color saturation, a specific critique with a memorable analogy**: green across chairs + cabinet fronts + lower island columns simultaneously. Krasnov's analogy: "как будто человек надел слишком много украшений — каждая вроде бы красиво, но вместе это перебор." Recommends varying texture/shade within the same color family rather than flat repetition, and using a more restrained quantity of the accent color overall.
- **⚠️ No-upper-cabinets kitchen — black-vs-white ceiling-fixture mechanism, genuinely new**: black ceiling track fixtures combined with a bold copper range hood both visually "eat" the room's vertical space, making a no-upper-cabinet kitchen read heavy rather than light despite the intended airiness. Recommends WHITE track fixtures instead of black specifically in a no-upper-cabinet kitchen (where the ceiling needs to read light to compensate for the missing storage volume above), and a white range hood instead of a statement copper one.
- **⚠️ Marble-look porcelain "fatigue" — a pointed meta-critique about the whole design industry, genuinely new as a stated observation**: an explicit callout that marble-look material has been used SO repeatedly across so many reviewed reference images that Krasnov explicitly asks viewers to consider other stone textures — his own stated fatigue with an over-relied-upon material trend, distinct from a single-image critique.

### Kitchen #3
- **⚠️ Kitchen island cooktop placement — a well-reasoned safety/social critique, genuinely valuable, worth prominent entry**: a cooktop positioned on the DINING-facing side of a kitchen island (facing guests seated at the island) puts hot splatter/steam/oil directly within arm's reach of seated guests — real risk of splashing a guest with hot oil, plus general discomfort of being "cooked in front of" even without incident, and specifically flags this as dangerous with children present. Recommended fix: position the cooktop on the FAR side of the island (away from the seating/guest-facing side), keeping the near/guest-facing side for safe seating — a specific, concrete, safety-relevant kitchen-island layout rule.

### Bedroom #2
- **⚠️ Textured "architectural concrete" accent wall — a nuanced critique of a once-trendy material**: acknowledges the material genuinely adds depth/texture, but flags it as a now-dated trend (popular a few years ago, now reading as a dated attempt at freshness).
