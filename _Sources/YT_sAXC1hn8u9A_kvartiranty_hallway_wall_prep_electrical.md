## Design Concept — Durable Facts & Rules (long/narrow-hallway zoning)

- **A long, narrow, poorly-lit corridor was deliberately split into two
  functionally and visually distinct zones** rather than treated as one
  continuous "tunnel" — Zone 1 (adjacent to the living room's doorway)
  is designed to visually read as an *extension* of that room; Zone 2
  (further down, toward the exit/other rooms) is designed to read as the
  corridor/transition zone proper. **Directly extends this project's
  existing long-hallway proportion-control content** (Anuta Vlady's
  color-blocking/line-orientation techniques, already in
  `01_Entrance/analysis/Layout_and_Zoning.md`) with a *furniture-and-
  lighting-composition*-based zoning method, a genuinely different
  mechanism from color/line proportion tricks.
- **A specific, dimensioned zone-1 composition, stated as a design rule
  with worked reasoning**: centered directly opposite the living-room
  doorway — a large mirror (centered on the doorway's own midline), a
  console/chest of drawers below it (**160-180 cm long, 100 cm tall**),
  two table lamps on the console (requiring **two low-height wall outlets**
  flanking the mirror), and two wall sconces (бра) either side of the
  mirror wired to a switch near the entry door. **The whole composition is
  centered on the room's doorway and made symmetric**: the width available
  on one side (constrained by an existing wall/opening near the entry
  door) is measured, then the *same distance* is marked off on the other
  side of the doorway — this symmetric offset simultaneously determines
  both the console-zone width and the width of a wardrobe to be built
  into the space beyond it. **A directly reusable measurement method** for
  centering an entry-facing furniture composition symmetrically around a
  doorway when the two sides of the hallway aren't naturally equal.
- **The zone boundary is reinforced with a color change, not just a
  volume/furniture change**: the wardrobe and ceiling in the "corridor"
  zone are deliberately given a different color from the "room-extension"
  zone specifically so the two zones read as distinct even where there's
  no physical wall between them — a concrete implementation of the
  "double down on the zone break, don't rely on furniture alone" principle.
- **A wall-mounted picture/artwork with dedicated LED backlighting was
  placed opposite a bathroom door specifically to add general corridor
  illumination** in an under-lit spot, not purely for decoration — the
  backlighting is explicitly framed as doing double duty (art accent +
  functional corridor light).
- **A low cabinet placed in a niche near the WC**, with its own lamp,
  needing a dedicated wall outlet at that spot — another small
  niche-lighting data point for a hallway/corridor with an adjacent WC.

## Electrical — Durable Facts & Rules (real wiring/wall-prep technique)

- **Locating a concealed junction box behind plaster**: needing power for
  a new light fixture with no visible nearby source, the source tapped
  the wall near the ceiling above an existing switch and listened for a
  hollow sound at several points — this located a **plastered-over,
  concealed junction box** at the wall/ceiling seam. **A directly reusable
  diagnostic technique**: knock-testing along a wall/ceiling seam near an
  existing switch/outlet to find a concealed junction box before assuming
  new wiring must run from the breaker panel.
- **Opening and extending from a found junction box**: broke open the
  plastered-over gap at the wall/ceiling seam with a hammer, drilled a
  short vertical channel (штроба) from the ceiling down to the new wire's
  exit point using a hammer drill with a masonry bit, threaded the new
  wire through this channel from the junction box, then filled the channel
  with **Rotband** (a gypsum-based patching/plastering compound) that was
  **past its stated shelf life ("просроченный")** — the source's own
  economical-reuse choice, treating expired setting compound as still
  usable for a non-critical, purely-cosmetic channel fill (not a
  structural or load-bearing application). A genuine, checkable
  materials-reuse tip for a low-stakes patching task.
- **Multi-gang switch sequencing matches the walking direction through the
  space**: a 3-button switch panel by the entry door is wired so button 1
  controls the living room, button 2 the hallway's own light, and button 3
  the picture backlight — deliberately ordered to match the sequence a
  person encounters walking from the entry door inward. A directly
  reusable multi-gang-switch layout principle for a corridor with several
  independently-lit zones in a row.
- **A wall with no existing switches/outlets** (the side opposite the one
  worked on) had its new-outlet/sconce-wiring decision explicitly
  deferred — wire ends were capped/insulated for later completion rather
  than forcing a decision immediately; new sconces on that side were
  temporarily wired directly to the entry-door switch with bare bulbs
  (no shades yet) as an interim state, described as "too bright" without
  shades — an honest account of an intentionally incomplete interim state
  rather than a finished result.
- **Laminate-plank wall cladding inside a niche narrower than the plank
  length**: no need to join planks end-to-end in rows when the niche width
  is less than one plank's length — simplifies this wall-cladding
  technique specifically for narrow niches (cross-references the
  channel's own separate, more detailed laminate-wall-cladding video, not
  itself part of this batch).
