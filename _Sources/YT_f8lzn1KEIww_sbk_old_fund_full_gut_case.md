## Mistakes / Warnings — Old-Fund Structural/Systems Red Flags Found on Real Sites

- **⚠️ Through-riser wall penetration to a neighboring unit, a real named defect found on-site**: at the point where a plumbing stack passes through, an actual through-hole exists straight into the neighboring apartment — demonstrated on camera as a real, checkable structural/privacy/moisture-transfer risk specific to old-fund construction quality, not a hypothetical.
- **⚠️ Multi-strand soft aluminum wiring at an outlet, a named visible red flag for old-fund apartment buyers**: aluminum wiring of this era is stated to already be past its safe service life by design, making it unsafe by default rather than merely outdated — visually identifiable at any exposed outlet during a viewing, before purchase.
- **⚠️ Lath-and-plaster ("дранка") partition construction as a named structural weak point**: original old-fund partitions in the buildings shown were built from "whatever was on hand" by period construction standards — contrasted directly with the aerated-concrete (газобетон) block partitions the company rebuilds them with, which the speaker frames as a genuinely stronger, more durable replacement, not a like-for-like cosmetic redo.
- **⚠️ Gas pipe boxed inside a wall, a code-violation red flag with a named remediation requirement**: a gas supply pipe found fully enclosed inside a wall cavity (behind a covering box) is called out as improper — gas pipe must remain either fully visible/exposed or concealed only behind an easily removable furniture-type construction, specifically so it stays inspectable and serviceable. Any re-welding/rerouting of gas pipe must be performed by licensed gas-utility technicians ("газовщики") according to a formally developed engineering project, not by a general contractor — directly parallel to (and independently corroborating, in a different trade) this store's existing `AGqrxEtyrdI` finding that load-bearing-wall modification in old-fund buildings requires its own formal engineering project and signed-off acceptance.
- **Rotten/crumbling original screed and windowsill substrate found under stripped finishes**: removing an old wooden windowsill board revealed the underlying concrete fill was also in poor condition — both stripped out and rebuilt in brick with new sills, described as a common finding once old-fund finishes are opened up, not a one-off surprise.

## Engineering / Systems — Old-Fund-Specific Utility Workarounds

- **⚠️ Gas water heater ("газовая колонка") as the standard hot-water source in old-fund apartments lacking central hot water, paired with a collector (коллектор) cold-water distribution system**: cold water is routed to a wall-mounted gas water heater (installed behind the kitchen wall in this case), heated there, and returned through the plumbing as the apartment's actual hot-water loop — the collector/manifold distribution system installed at the entry point serves this same loop once heated, same manifold architecture as this store's already-well-documented modern collector-node content, but specifically adapted to the old-fund reality of no piped-in central hot water.
- **A second, distinct instance of expanded-clay-under-screed floor buildup for old-fund floor-height correction, independently corroborating `AGqrxEtyrdI`'s technique with a different stated rationale**: this apartment's original floor was wood-on-joists ("на лагах") at nearly 25cm total thickness; once demolished, a thick expanded-clay-concrete (керамзитобетон) screed is poured to restore floor height up to the level needed to clear the door threshold properly — feasible here specifically because the building underwent a prior capital repair with a monolithic (not old wood-joist) structural floor slab underneath, which can bear the added screed weight. **Distinguishing detail from `AGqrxEtyrdI`**: that case's rationale was avoiding excess dead load on an old slab (using expanded clay specifically *because* it's lightweight); this case's rationale is reaching a target floor height where the underlying structure can already bear a full-depth screed pour — two different old-fund floor-buildup scenarios using the same general material, not the same specific mechanism.
- **⚠️ Sewer stack replacement routed under the floor specifically because of the deep available screed thickness** — only possible in this apartment because the thick expanded-clay screed buildup (described above) provided enough depth to bury a re-routed sewer line beneath the finished floor, rather than needing to bulk out along a wall or ceiling.
- **A pragmatic tool-substitution anecdote for cutting a chase ("штроба"), recorded as a real site-management observation, not a recommended technique**: when the intended chase-cutting tool was unavailable/broken on a given day, the crew cut the chase using a concrete core (hole-saw) bit instead, on the stated principle that crews must "find a way" to keep the schedule moving with whatever tool is on hand, as long as the end result meets spec — recorded as a realistic site-management fact about tool substitution under real conditions, not endorsed as a best-practice method.
