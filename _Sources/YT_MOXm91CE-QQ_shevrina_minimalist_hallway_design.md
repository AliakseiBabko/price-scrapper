## Entrance / Doors — A Three-Level Ladder for Lightening the Entrance Door

Her framing: an entrance door is structurally busy and carries far more visible hardware than an interior door — handle, thumbturn, keyholes, spyhole. Three escalating solutions:

1. **Simplest.** A plain flat MDF overlay panel — **no filenki, no wood texture** — with the frame painted to match, and **ideally the whole assembly in the wall colour**. Then match the hardware finish to what the interior doors will use. "Вроде бы дверь как у всех, но… выглядит и ощущается куда легче."
2. **⚠️ Contrasting finish — but only if it runs to the ceiling.** If you give the entrance door a different finish (wood, say), **carry the same material above the door up to the ceiling**, or the door reads as "какой-то заплаткой на стене" — a patch. **Either the whole wall gets one finish, or the finish is door-width but full-height.** She notes other designers use the same "плаcтырь/заплатка" language for this failure.
3. **⚠️ Perfectionist: a second, concealed *interior* door as a second contour inside the entrance door.** Her argument against relying on a concealed entrance door alone: **"у них скрыты лишь петли"** — the handles, thumbturns and spyholes remain, and the hardware range for entrance doors is neither as large nor as attractive as for interior doors. She says she can barely remember the last time they installed an ordinary entrance door, and that they now do this "объект за объектом."
   - Same psychological rationale as in Round 1's `z4G-ocStu9o`: the entrance is often visible from a seating position, and looking at a front door is subtly uncomfortable; an interior door reads as another room. **This note and `z4G-ocStu9o` are the same channel, so this is a restatement, not corroboration** — but it adds the hardware argument and the "hidden hinges only hide the hinges" point.
   - She flags that designing a second door into an entrance opening has many nuances (deferred to her course).

## Storage — The Raised Entrance Wardrobe

**⚠️ Their near-universal technique: lift the entrance-hall wardrobe off the floor by 30–40 cm** (she has gone as low as **15 cm** minimum). She calls it "10 в одном" and the reasons are cumulative:

- Whatever is pushed underneath is **invisible from the rest of the flat** unless you light the recess deliberately.
- **Low shoes live there** — out of sight, and out of the circulation path.
- **A robot-vacuum dock fits there** — a genuinely common problem with nowhere else to go.
- **Underfloor heating can be run under the wardrobe** so wet and dirty shoes dry in the recess.
- **A pouffe can be pushed under it** when there is no room for one otherwise.

**Two caveats she attaches:**

- **⚠️ A wall-hung cabinet may not carry the load through its back panel** — depends on the cabinet's size and what goes in it. **Discuss with the joinery shop; don't plan this unaided.**
- **Choose the pouffe first** (or decide its height), so the clearance actually fits it.

## Engineering — Panels and Manifolds in the Entrance Zone

- **An electrical panel is comparatively easy** — the cabling into it is thin, and they usually house it in the wardrobe.
- **⚠️ Heating manifolds ("гребёнки") are the hard case, and the raised-wardrobe trick does not solve them.** The manifolds carry thick plumbing runs that cannot be buried in plaster, and **even with a surface-mounted manifold behind a wardrobe raised 30–40 cm, the pipes feeding it stay visible.** Plan for this early.
- **Heating manifolds should be recessed into the wall.** If the wall is load-bearing, build it out.
- **⚠️ Alternative used on a recent project: pass through the wall into the adjacent room (e.g. the bathroom) and reinforce the resulting opening.** Worth it **where you cannot afford to lose ~12 cm** of circulation width or storage depth to a built-out wall.

## Design Concept — Small Details

- **Switches can be recessed into the entry storage niche** (the one holding keys and bags) — invisible, but findable by feel on the way in. Sockets can go inside the wardrobe; **switches cannot**, since you won't open a cabinet door to turn the light on in the dark.
- **A video intercom has to stay visible at chest-to-face height**; slim part-recessed models exist. She questions whether one is needed at all now, since door codes get sent to guests and couriers.
