## Materials — Tile vs. Porcelain Stoneware (durable facts)

1. **Ceramic tile vs. porcelain stoneware ("керамогранит") composition
   and durability difference**: ceramic tile is fired clay with a glaze
   coating — lighter, thinner, doesn't come in large formats (cracks at
   scale) — largely a 15-years-ago material now. Porcelain stoneware
   adds strength-boosting components to clay/sand, uses more intense
   pressing and firing for a super-dense structure — thicker, heavier,
   stronger, and available in large formats. **General recommendation:
   choose porcelain stoneware over ceramic tile** when given the choice.
   Minimum stated tile-category service life: **20 years**, framed as the
   reason material/format choice deserves real attention (unlike
   wallpaper/paint/flooring, tile essentially can't be "redone" cheaply
   later).
2. **⚠️ Russian-vs-imported porcelain market-shift claim**: Italy, Spain,
   and Turkey led 15 years ago and still lead on design/pattern
   reputation, but domestic Russian manufacturers have since bought the
   same production equipment/pigments as European factories and adopted
   the same manufacturing technology — framed as now a genuine quality
   competitor, not a budget compromise. **Practical guidance: buy
   domestic porcelain stoneware for quality-for-money; only look to
   Italy/Spain/Turkey if domestic doesn't satisfy** — flagged as the
   company's own market-facing opinion (self-interested framing possible
   given this is a Russian renovation company), tagged accordingly.

## Materials — Format and Layout (Rules / Heuristics)

3. **Three basic tile formats — rectangle, square, plank ("доска",
   wood-imitating)**: floors take square or plank; walls take square or
   rectangle; plank format on a wall should be used only as an
   accent/decor on one wall, not the whole room (looks odd applied
   everywhere).
4. **⚠️ Tile size should be chosen by the designer/project planner, not
   the client directly** — sizing needs to account for the room's real
   dimension grid and cut-piece waste layout to look right; picking size
   purely on preference risks a poor-looking cut-piece pattern.
5. **Avoid large-format floor tile in small rooms** — excess cut-off
   waste at the room's edges looks poor; move to a smaller format
   instead when the room doesn't support a large one.
6. **Numeric format-size reference ladder**: floor baseline **60×60cm**;
   larger rooms can use **75×75cm or 90×90cm**; **90×120cm** needs a
   large, expensive dedicated tile cutter that only narrow-specialist
   installers own, and they charge noticeably more for this format;
   smaller rooms should drop to **45×45cm or 40×40cm**. Wall tile: **60×60
   or 60×30cm**, called a universal size specifically because it aligns
   well with standard inspection-hatch mounting — a different wall-tile
   format requires the design project to separately plan the hatch's
   opening geometry and the plumbing behind it.

## Bathroom — Fixture-Fit Risk on Textured Tile (routed 07_Bathroom)

7. **⚠️ Textured/patterned porcelain ("фактурная плитка" — waves,
   hexagons, fish-scale, stripes) creates a real fixture-flush-mounting
   problem, named explicitly as a design-project oversight**: any
   fixture mounted against a textured surface — a socket/switch cover
   plate, a mixer's own decorative escutcheon/reflector cap, a toothbrush-
   holder mount — won't sit flush against the wavy surface, leaving a
   gap that can't be closed with silicone; achieving a clean fit needs
   near-jewelry-level precision fitting work by the installer.
   Practitioner's own observation: designers "often don't account for
   this" when specifying decorative textured tile in a design project —
   worth checking for explicitly if a design project specifies textured
   tile anywhere a fixture will be mounted.

## Materials — Quality Factors (durable facts)

8. **Five stated quality factors for tile/porcelain**: caliber
   (dimensional tolerance), edge type, surface type, pattern, and
   color-depth (through-body vs. surface-only).
9. **Caliber ("калибр") defined and explained**: even within one box,
   individual pieces vary by a few millimeters from each other — higher
   quality tile has a caliber tolerance closer to zero. A **cut-edge
   ("обрезной") type lets the installer trim each piece to a precise
   size on-site**, the practical way to achieve a premium, perfectly-even
   result despite caliber variance in the raw product.
10. **Surface-type taxonomy and placement rule**: matte, glossy, satin
    (semi-matte/semi-gloss), structured (textured), and lappato (part
    matte/part gloss on the same piece). **Floor: matte or structured
    only, never glossy** (glossy is slippery and dulls/wears over time).
    **Walls: glossy is good** — its reflective effect visually enlarges
    the room. **Lappato is not recommended for a small room** — looks
    unflattering there.
11. **Pattern/design leadership note**: Italy's continued design/pattern
    reputation is stated as the reason it remains a premium choice for
    buyers who specifically want a particular aesthetic; modern porcelain
    can imitate concrete, marble, marble-chip terrazzo, metal, wood, or
    be a flat solid color.
12. **⚠️ Coloring-method durability rule, floor-specific**: porcelain is
    colored either surface-only or through the full body ("по всей
    массе"). **Surface-colored tile on a floor will visibly wear through
    over time — use fully-through-colored tile on floors.** Walls can use
    either coloring method safely (no comparable foot-traffic wear).

## Materials — Buying Cautions (Mistakes / Warnings)

13. **Two buying-scam cautions**: don't buy a discontinued production
    line even at a good price — remaining stock may be pulled from
    different warehouses/production batches and mismatch in caliber or
    even color. Don't pre-order foreign tile that doesn't exist in stock
    yet against a prepayment — the source states in "90% of cases" a
    promised ~2-month production time stretches to 6 months to a year.
