# Source Note - Пинскдрев: ⚠️⚠️ a Belarusian manufacturer, and the FOURTH arrival at the pet-claw fabric finding — this time with the construction that fixes it (YouTube 1UGUjGmZPNA)

## ⚠️⚠️ Read this note's limits first

**This is a sales briefing to dealers about next month's collection.** Most of it is model names, colour-trend narrative and which fabric suits which sofa in their own catalogue — **all discarded.** ⚠️ **Its `fact_yield` is low relative to its 17-minute length, and the promotional ratio is the highest of anything processed this batch.**

**Two things justify keeping it:**

1. **⚠️⚠️ It is a BELARUSIAN manufacturer.** The vault's furniture sourcing is overwhelmingly Russian and Ukrainian; a Belarusian voice is rare and is the jurisdiction that actually matters for this project. ⚠️ **Note carefully: it yields no price and no regulation, so it does NOT discharge the standing need for Belarusian pricing — it is a market-context data point only.**
2. **⚠️⚠️ The claw finding arrives here from the manufacturing side**, stated as a reason for a construction choice rather than as advice — which is the more informative form.

## ⚠️⚠️ 1. THE FOURTH ARRIVAL — and the first to say what the FIX is

Describing a fabric called Stella, the speaker explains its dense backing:

> ***«Такая подложка нужна для того, чтобы вот этот ворс весь держался очень плотно, чтобы когти с животного не выдирали этот ворс — потому что если бы это был обычный шенилл, то структура была бы нестабильная.»***

And separately, on velour:

> ***«Особенно мы их рекомендуем тем людям, которые дома держат домашних животных, потому что в плане плотности структуры велюр — лидер.»***

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ This is the mechanism from the other end.** The vault's three existing sources say *which fabrics fail against claws*. **Пинскдрев says what the manufacturer does about it: anchor the pile with a dense backing (дублирование / подложка) so a claw cannot pull it out.** Consequence for a buyer:
>
> - **⚠️⚠️ The question to ask is not only "what fibre" but "is it дублированная"** — backed or not. **The vault had no purchasing question for this before.**
> - **Velour is named as the pet-owner leader on structural density** — ⚠️ **which is the fourth independent source to put velour on the safe side**, with Шеврина, ARMOS and [[_Sources/YT_SwOVFr5YRP8_yakobi_upholstery_fabric_choice|Якоби]]. **Four sources, four incentive positions, same answer.**
> - **Plain unbacked chenille is stated by a manufacturer to be structurally unstable under claws** — so the fashionable category is not automatically the safe one.

## ⚠️ 2. The chenille shift, and velours engineered to look like chenille

- **The stated market movement: *«сейчас наступила эра шенилла»***, displacing the velours that dominated their earlier collections. The claimed advantages are **tactile feel and VOLUME** — a bulky weave upholsters a curved sofa *«мягкая, пушистая, нет лишних складок, каких-то морщинок»*.
- **⚠️⚠️ The buyer-relevant part: some velours in the range are deliberately made to READ as chenille** — *«мода на шениллы есть, и велюры очень успешно мимикрируют под шениллы»*, explicitly *«для того, чтобы ткань была модная, но при этом практичная»*, and *«неспециалист даже не скажет, что это велюр»*.

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ Directly actionable, and it cuts the right way for this project.** If velour is the pet-durable choice ([[14_Furniture/analysis/Upholstery_Fabric_Selection|four sources]]) and chenille is what looks current, **a mimicking velour resolves the conflict rather than trading one off against the other — and you cannot identify it by eye, so it has to be asked for by name.**

## ⚠️ 3. Density as the property that removes the need for backing

On a bouclé (Sage): *«здесь такая высокая плотность, что в этой ткани дублированный слой не нужен… плотное букле, без всяких промежутков, без всяких просветов»*.

> **→ ⚠️ The general rule behind §1, stated by accident: what protects the pile is DENSITY — achieved either by weaving it tight or by bonding a backing on.** Two routes, one property. **This is a better inspection heuristic than a fibre name: hold it to the light and look for gaps.**
>
> ⚠️ **Caveat this against Шеврина, who is in the vault warning specifically about БУКЛЕ and claws.** They are not necessarily in conflict — she is describing loose bouclé, he a bouclé chosen for having no gaps — **but the vault should not read as if "bouclé is fine" follows from this.**

## 4. Light colours, and soil resistance

- Chenille "Grace" is claimed to have **dirt-repellent behaviour from the weave structure itself** rather than a treatment, with white washing and cleaning acceptably — and it is said to have performed on their showroom podiums.
- ⚠️ **Recorded with the promotional discount applied. It is a vendor claim about a vendor product with no test cited**, and it sits directly against [[_Sources/YT_SwOVFr5YRP8_yakobi_upholstery_fabric_choice|Якоби's]] finding that soil-resistance sold as a fabric property is usually a coating that expires. **Якоби is the better-positioned source on that question. Do not carry the Grace claim to the wiki as a finding.**

## ⚠️ 5. Fabric "groups" — how a Belarusian manufacturer actually prices upholstery

Fabrics are referenced throughout by **group number** (18th, 19th, 20th, 22nd), with a lower group meaning cheaper: a velour praised as *«всего лишь восемнадцатая группа»* suitable for *«любые недорогие бюджетные диваны»*, a bouclé found *«по двадцатой группе»* where the previous equivalent was *«несколько дороговато»*.

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ A structural fact about buying a sofa here, and the vault did not have it: the price is set by the FABRIC GROUP applied to a model, not by the model alone — and the collections are stated to be universal across models (*«практически без ограничений»*).** **Consequence: the same sofa quoted twice at different prices may differ only in group, and the group is negotiable in a way the frame is not.** ⚠️ **No actual money is named anywhere, so no figure enters the vault under rule 2.**

## 6. One design opinion, recorded as opinion

On materials imitating other materials (plaster-effect and metallic velours of two years prior): *«когда один материал начинает имитировать другой, то происходит какая-то путаница… люди стараются вернуться к обычным простым вещам»* — wood should read as wood, metal as metal.

> ⚠️ **Note the internal tension with §2, where a velour imitating a chenille is presented as a virtue.** The distinction implied is imitation across a material CLASS versus within one. **Recorded as the speaker's aesthetic position, attributed, not adopted.**
