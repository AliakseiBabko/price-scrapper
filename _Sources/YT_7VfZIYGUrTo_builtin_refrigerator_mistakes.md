## Kitchen Appliances — Built-In Refrigerator

- **Standard vs. non-standard built-in width**: speaker states a "standard" built-in refrigerator is never large; what counts as a large built-in is specifically a **non-standard** built-in unit, and gives **750 mm width** as the figure for the large unit shown in this video. `single-account`, presented as clarifying a common point of confusion/debate ("finally settling the old argument that a built-in fridge can't be large").
- **Freezer-in-unit strongly recommended over separate/no freezer**: if choosing between a fridge with an integrated freezer compartment vs. one without (fresh-zone only), always choose the version **with** the freezer — company's own practical experience is that "everyone always needs a freezer," and clients who skip it end up regretting it. Cites a real project where the client chose a fridge without a freezer and the company had to source and fit an additional freezer module afterward. `single-account`, but backed by a specific cited real-project consequence.
- **Standalone ice generator requires a dedicated water line**: a separate ice-generator unit is convenient (for chilled drinks/juice) but does **not** make ice from air — it needs its own water supply connection, and this is a commonly forgotten requirement when planning the rough plumbing for a kitchen with this feature. `single-account`, concrete and checkable.
- **Refrigerator module interior-panel color must be ordered pre-finished, never repainted after fabrication**: every kitchen module has a facade panel (usually colored to match the kitchen) and an interior/reverse panel (standard factory finish is usually white) — if a client wants the interior panel to match a custom facade color, it must be **special-ordered pre-colored on all sides from the factory**, not painted after the fact. Company's own real case: modules were built with standard white interior panels, then repainted blue post-fabrication to match — the paint later peeled, requiring a full module rebuild. This is the source's most concrete, arithmetic-checkable mistake (a real rework cost, even though the rework's own cost isn't separately quantified).
- **Touch-open/sensor-front refrigerator panels always show fingerprints**: regardless of finish (matte, glossy, "soft-touch") a touch-to-open refrigerator facade will show finger/palm marks — this is presented as an unavoidable physical property of that opening mechanism, not a specific-brand defect. **Mitigation rule**: always pair a touch-open unit with a milled pull handle ("фрезерованная ручка") on the surrounding cabinetry so a user can open cabinetry with dirty/greasy hands without touching the refrigerator's own facade directly. `single-account`, but a specific, checkable design-mitigation pairing, not just a complaint.
