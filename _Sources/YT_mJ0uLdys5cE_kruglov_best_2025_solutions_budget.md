## Genuinely New Content

**⚠️ Electrical panel module-count sizing rule by apartment size — new,
concrete, quotable rule not previously on this store's panel-design
content**: studio/1-bedroom apartments need a minimum **12-module**
panel; 2-3-room apartments need a minimum **24-module** panel; 3-4-room
apartments need a minimum **36-module** panel (framed as three 12-module
DIN rows). Named explicitly as a budget-tier *minimum*, not a luxury
spec — the actual cable run length depends on the number of *circuits*
planned in the panel, not the raw count of outlets/switches in the
apartment. Also restates (not new): only copper conductors and
**ВВГнГ-type cable** are code-compliant per current ПУЭ rules, aluminum
wiring must be fully removed on any secondary-market gut renovation.

**⚠️ Demolition of an existing built-in sanitary cabin (сантехкабина) on
a secondary-market apartment — new topic for this store's demolition
content**: recommends full removal rather than reuse, for two stated
reasons — (1) the cabin material eats usable floor/ceiling/wall space
that a from-scratch block or partition build wouldn't, and (2) the
material itself is described as "resonant" (звонкий) with real elastic
give, meaning **it should never be chased horizontally** (a horizontal
groove cut for pipe/cable routing) since that removes structural
strength, and should not be overloaded with heavy tile without care.
Recommended replacement: build a new wet-zone enclosure from blocks or
another solid material from scratch rather than working around the old
cabin.

**⚠️ Cheap-tier lighting — a real same-channel disagreement against this
project's own `Budget_Tiers_Cheap_Optimal_Premium.md` Lighting section,
not a corroboration**: this video's cheap-tier lighting pick is
**surface-mounted or recessed point downlights, or a chandelier** —
**explicitly excluding LED strips, track lighting, and any backlighting
at this tier** ("никаких линий светодиодных, подсветок, трековых
светильников мы не используем"). This is the **opposite** of that
page's existing cheap-tier pick (from a different Kruglov/Ontario video,
`dBn4nhn8d9c`): track lighting or surface-mounted spotlights,
**explicitly preferred over recessed fixtures**, on the stated grounds
that recessed lighting is "already leaving the trend." Both positions
come from the same channel; recorded as an open, unresolved disagreement
on the Budget Tiers page rather than merged or silently favoring either
side.

**⚠️ Baseboard — a third source reinforcing one side of this project's
existing flagged PVC-baseboard disagreement**: this video's cheap-tier
baseboard pick is **factory-finished (pre-painted or film-wrapped) MDF
baseboard**, not PVC — no PVC baseboard is mentioned anywhere in this
video's cheap-tier package. This aligns with (does not newly establish)
the "avoid PVC, prefer MDF/polyurethane" side of the existing flagged
disagreement on `Budget_Tiers_Cheap_Optimal_Premium.md` (previously
2 Kruglov/Ontario sources on record: `Tyl0yPQkO5g` favoring PVC at the
cheap tier, `dBn4nhn8d9c` favoring MDF/polyurethane and avoiding PVC) —
worth recording as the split now standing at 2-vs-1 in favor of the
MDF/avoid-PVC position, though still not treated as resolving the
disagreement (a vote count isn't the same as new technical evidence).

**⚠️ Furniture — a new Budget Tiers page section, this page's first
furniture-specific tier content**: at the cheap tier, this source
specifies **fully ready-made, serial/modular furniture only** — modular
kitchen units (assemblable from a catalog/marketplace, no custom
cabinetry), ready-made wardrobes/cabinets/dressers — explicitly ruling
out custom-fabricated kitchens or built-in wardrobe systems at this
tier as a real cost driver to cut first.

**Plumbing rough-in — leak-protection system explicitly framed as
skippable at the cheap tier, distinct from a cost-cutting recommendation**:
this source states that with quality pipes, fittings, and a manifold
node, a leak-protection system can be omitted entirely at the budget
tier "not for the sake of saving money" but because the risk is already
well-controlled by using quality components — check valves are
similarly framed as optional/discretionary. A water-hammer-protection
device, by contrast, is called mandatory regardless of tier.
