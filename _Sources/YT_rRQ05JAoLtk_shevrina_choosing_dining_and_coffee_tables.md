## Furniture / Quantities — The Table Taxonomy, With Dimensions

**⚠️ Her central point: "журнальный" and "кофейный" are two functionally different objects that people conflate, and the failure mode is a low table that is simultaneously big *and* tall.**

| Type | Height | Top size | Mobility | What it is actually for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Журнальный** (low/coffee table) | **≤ 30–35 cm** | Large — ~100 cm on the long side is normal | Largely static, sits in front of the sofa | Magazines, remotes, sweets, a fruit bowl; board games played sitting on the floor around it. **Not** for repeatedly setting down a mug |
| **Кофейный / приставной** (side table) | **~50 cm and above** | Small — small diameter or footprint | Mobile, moved to where it's needed | Actually drinking tea/coffee and eating. Can be placed on **both** sides of a sofa — her example is a couple each wanting their own |

- **⚠️ The named failure: a table that is both large (100 cm) and tall.** It clutters, and the effect is worst in a small living room. She reports low tables routinely ending up **taller than the sofa seat** — "ещё чуть-чуть, и он уже будет выше сидушки дивана. Зачем такой?" On one model she says being just **15 cm lower** would have fixed it.
- **⚠️ The pairing convention**: use two together, and differentiate them — **one bigger and lower, one smaller and higher**. A "set" of two similar tables defeats the purpose, since the point is to cover both functions. Her worked sizing: a **32 cm high × 90 cm diameter** low table plus a **45 cm high** small one.
- **⚠️ Co-scale rule against the sofa** — a real proportional constraint, not taste: a small-diameter side table suits a sofa of roughly **180–190 cm**; against a **2,400–2,500 mm** sofa the same table reads out of scale.
- **A `приставной` should be able to slide its base under the sofa**, so the top can be pulled in close over your lap.
- **⚠️ Tables with a massive base are held to the stricter end of the height rule — 30 cm, 35 cm maximum** — otherwise they read heavyweight. Worst case named: a small living room that *also* has low ceilings.

## Design Concept — Why a Piece of Furniture Reads Beautiful

**⚠️ Her strongest general claim, and the reason this source clears the bar despite the brand-survey framing**: an attractive piece is never accidental — "он не может быть красивым ни с того ни с сего." The causes she names are checkable against any piece in a showroom:

- **Соосность** — coaxial alignment of elements.
- **Repetition** of a form at different scales.
- **Deliberate scale relationships between details** — or a deliberate contrast instead.
- **⚠️ Equal corner radii at every junction** of the piece. She names unequal radii within a single item as a marker of unconsidered design.

**⚠️ Contrast of forms is the specific lever for visual weight**: a **thick leg or base under a thin tabletop reads light**; when top and legs are the same thickness the piece reads massive and clutters the room. She works this through named examples in both directions and calls one table's success "именно благодаря контрасту этих форм — толстый, тонкий," noting it stays visually light *despite* being large.

## Mistakes / Warnings

- **⚠️ Splayed legs ("в растопырку"), and worse when they extend past the tabletop edge** — "как будто стол вам пытается подставить подножку." You strike your toes on them approaching the table. She argues they can't even be defended on aesthetic grounds, since there is no beauty in it either. Restates the same rule from `XGI6FS2ZdCc` (same channel).
- **⚠️ Legs that taper toward the floor** — rejected outright as "совершенно бесполезный элемент, лишняя работа, которая ведёт к дисгармонии" when a cylindrical leg would do. Their studio never specifies them.
- **⚠️ A tabletop finished in a different shade from its base** reads "наигранно, искусственно и не архитектурно — как будто просто какой-то пластик наклеили."
- **A trend-fabric base dates the piece**: one table is described as fine in principle but supplied in bouclé, which is a trend; in a plainer non-trend fabric the same table would stay current much longer.

## Market Observation (contextual, not a technique)

She states the Russian market for tables, low tables and chairs is **markedly weaker in design terms than the upholstered-furniture market**, and characterises the problem in three parts: pieces too "designerly"/bright to be livable; an absence of simple base-level designs one can rely on; and heavy repetition of a few templates (round top on three or four legs).
