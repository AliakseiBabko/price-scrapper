## Lighting — Fixture Type Taxonomy (new content)

- **Point/spot fixtures** split into recessed (built into ceiling or wall) and surface-mounted; surface-mounted further splits into **static** (fixed aim) and **adjustable/aimable** (has a mechanism to change beam angle/direction). [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Track lighting** uses a rail with magnetic connectors that fixtures attach to; the rail itself comes in three mounting types — recessed into wall/ceiling, surface-mounted, or suspended/pendant. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Light panels** (flat diffused-light panels, associated with Armstrong/cassette-panel ceiling systems) are a distinct fixture type from a chandelier even though some marketplace listings market a light panel under the "chandelier" (люстра) name — both give diffused light and serve a similar function, but should be tracked as separate product categories when specifying. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **⚠️ LED strip mounting has a real failure-history lesson**: roughly a decade ago, LED strips were commonly glued directly to a wall or ceiling and would detach/fall over time, requiring repeated re-installation. **Current standard practice is to mount the strip inside a metal profile channel** (recessed or surface-mounted, with or without a diffuser lens) so the strip's adhesive backing bonds to the metal profile rather than failing on the wall/ceiling surface directly. `single-account`, but a specific, checkable technique claim. [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]
- **Linear LED fixtures** are a related but distinct product: an aluminum profile housing that comes pre-assembled with both the LED strip and its transformer/driver together, and mounts the same three ways as track lighting (surface, recessed, suspended). [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]

## Lighting — Function-Based Categories (new framing, corroborates existing scenario guidance)

The source frames lighting purpose as four functional categories rather than per-room scenario counts (compare to the existing "minimum three scenarios per room" framework from `dJMsXYUyh7A`) — treat as a complementary lens, not a contradiction:

- **Base/main light**: even, shadow-minimizing, soft light for everyday use (cleaning, general evening presence in a room). Best candidates: a chandelier or light panel (softest, most even distribution); recessed/surface point fixtures work too only if they have soft/diffused beam control, not a hard narrow beam.
- **Accent light**: narrow-purpose light for one or two specific uses only (a kitchen task light, an illuminated bathroom mirror, art/decor/panel highlighting) — explicitly contrasted with a chandelier's "do anything" generality. Best candidates: directional/aimable point fixtures, track fixtures focused on one zone, sconces, or a narrow-beam LED strip run.
- **Background/ambient light**: soft, unfocused light whose purpose is atmosphere rather than task visibility — the light source itself often isn't visible, only its glow (e.g. an LED strip inside a decorative ceiling cornice/"karnab", or furniture-integrated lighting inside kitchen cabinets that doesn't meaningfully add task illumination but adds ambiance).
- **Decorative light**: a comparatively rare category whose light itself forms a visual pattern (e.g. crystal chandeliers casting a patterned shadow/light play on walls/ceiling), plus neon signage/fixtures whose primary purpose is visual decoration rather than illumination.
- **Navigational light** (new label for content that corroborates the existing whole-apartment night-light-circuit guidance): activated at night specifically to move safely between rooms/along a staircase without turning on full lighting — implemented via motion-sensor-triggered fixtures, a single always-dim fixture left on overnight in one zone (e.g. bathroom), or a manual single-switch "night mode" covering the whole home. **Explicit rule: if the home has a staircase, it must have navigational lighting — no exception stated ("100%").** [source: [[_Sources/YT_xikuzV80GP4_kruglov_best_lighting_options|xikuzV80GP4]]]

## Rooms / Zones — Bathroom (WC)

- **⚠️ Mirror light must shine horizontally toward the face, not down from above the mirror and not by lighting the tile behind the mirror.** Lighting the tile around/behind the mirror rather than the mirror/face directly is called out as an ineffective, commonly-seen mistake; overhead lighting casts shadows on the face.
