## Genuinely New Content (generalizable principles only)

- **Door-swing rule: a door should open from the smaller room into the
  larger adjoining space, not the reverse** — stated explicitly as the
  "honest" rule the architect follows (a direct response to viewer
  comments on the Part 1 video asking why doors are drawn opening
  inward): a small guest-WC door swings out into the larger entry hall,
  not into the cramped WC itself. This is a general spatial-efficiency
  principle (the larger room can always absorb the swing arc without
  conflict; the smaller room usually can't) independent of house vs.
  apartment context — not previously captured on this vault's Bathroom/WC
  door-swing content.
- **Separating the toilet behind its own partition within a large ensuite
  bathroom, with an explicit two-part rationale**: (1) hygiene — the
  stated principle is that the toilet should sit as far as practical from
  the fixtures used for washing the face/body, and enclosing it behind a
  partition achieves that separation even within one combined room; (2)
  **it enables two people to use the ensuite bathroom simultaneously**
  (one at the toilet, one at the sink/shower), explicitly tied to a
  double/king-size bed serving two occupants. A concrete design principle
  for a combined-bathroom layout independent of the specific house — worth
  weighing for any ensuite bathroom design that isn't already split into
  separate WC and bathroom rooms.
- **A translucent (frosted/light-diffusing) partition around a
  screened-off toilet is only worth doing if the санузел itself has a
  window** — the source flags this specifically: the point of a
  translucent partition is to let daylight reach the screened-off toilet
  area rather than leaving it fully dark, so it only makes sense paired
  with an actual light source in the room. A general fixture-planning
  caveat, not house-specific.
- **"Blind"/frosted double-glazing that reads as a normal window from the
  exterior facade but doesn't transmit light into the room** — raised as
  a solved technique (per a knowledgeable commenter's correction on the
  Part 1 video) for a санузел window whose *facade appearance* matters
  (matching window rhythm/proportion from outside) but whose interior
  needs full privacy/opacity, rather than defaulting to a frosted-but-
  still-translucent glass or omitting the window entirely. Generalizable
  glazing technique, not tied to this house.
