## Rules / Heuristics — the five numbered tips

### 1. Bath vs. shower — decide, or install both if space allows

- **If floor area allows, install both a bathtub and a separate shower enclosure in the same room** — his own home has this; he and his wife rarely bathe but their children use the tub with a jacuzzi/hydromassage function, while a separate shower serves daily washing. If only one fixture fits, a bathtub can substitute for a shower (step in, shower inside it), but a shower cannot substitute for a bath.
- **Bathtub material tradeoffs**: **acrylic** — warm, tactile-pleasant, light, but soft and scratches under abrasive cleaning. **Cast iron** — very quiet (fill noise damped), holds heat well, extremely heavy, costs more than acrylic. **Cast/engineered marble (литьевой мрамор)** — visually striking ("вау"-effect), very durable, very heavy, most expensive of the three.
- **Freestanding tubs**: genuinely worth it as a design centrepiece ("точка притяжения") **only where floor area allows** (his figures: 8, 10, up to 15 m²) **and** a separate shower is available nearby for daily use — since a freestanding tub in a hotel-style room is often more decorative than heavily used day-to-day.

### 2. Toilet installation — go wall-hung, and get the height right

- **⚠️ Prefer a wall-hung toilet on a concealed cistern/frame** over floor-standing: visually lighter ("парящая история"), and in his career he reports never hearing of a properly-installed concealed frame failing (falling off the wall, leaking) — provided hidden works aren't cut on cost. **A correctly installed installation frame lasts a minimum of ~20 years.**
- **⚠️ Optimal toilet-bowl height from finished floor: 40–42 cm** (matching the existing vault figure — corroboration from a second, independent channel). He flags **46 cm and above as too tall** ("это уже тумач"), estimating he encounters over-tall installations in roughly 20% of restaurants/cafes/public venues he visits, sometimes forcing an improvised step or footrest — a design/planning failure, not a fixable-after-the-fact issue.

### 3. Sinks — default to an inset vanity sink; avoid small round vessel bowls

- **Default recommendation: an inset sink filling the full width of a wall-hung vanity** — sufficient for handwashing, teeth-cleaning, makeup removal, and the safest choice against sizing mistakes.
- **⚠️ Avoid small-diameter round vessel/overmount sinks**: (a) often too small a bowl diameter for comfortable use; (b) a common installer error is hanging the vanity cabinet at the "correct" reference height without accounting for the extra height added by a raised bowl sitting on top — leaving the user reaching awkwardly upward to wash hands.
- **⚠️ Faucet spout reach must match the raised-bowl geometry**: with concealed/in-wall plumbing (fixed wall-mounted spout position), a vessel bowl can end up with the spout almost touching the rim, making hand-washing impractical — a real case cited from an acquaintance's home.
- **Faucet spout "throw" (вынос) must match basin depth**: depending on how deep/shallow the basin is, the water stream can land centred or too close to one edge — misalignment causes splashing over the basin edge. Needs to be checked/adjusted so the stream lands centred.
- Figured vessel basins with a wide, deep bowl are usable but read as a "restaurant/wow-effect" choice rather than a practical daily-home fixture (no cosmetics/toiletries storage nearby) — acceptable as a deliberate design statement, not the safe default.
- **When placing/selecting a sink, the whole bathroom's plumbing context must be checked together**: sewage-stack slope requirements (gravity flow), hot/cold supply routing, and whether the installation is concealed or exposed — sink choice isn't independent of these.

### 4. Sanitaryware colour — no universally "correct" answer, but real cost and maintenance tradeoffs

- **Chrome**: cheapest, most time-tested, safest default. Widely claimed to show fewer fingerprints than black.
- **⚠️ Contrarian claim on his own experience**: he states black fixtures actually show **fewer** toothpaste/residue marks than chrome in his own observation — explicitly disagreeing with the common claim that chrome hides marks better.
- **⚠️ Cheap/no-name black fixtures (e.g. unbranded Chinese product) visibly peel/degrade within 1–1.5 years** — black and colour finishes are only viable from a quality manufacturer.
- **Coloured fixtures (including black, dark-graphite toilet bowls) cost roughly double a white/chrome equivalent** — a legitimate budget-optimisation lever: choosing classic white/chrome is not a compromise, it delivers identical function at lower cost.

### 5. Shower enclosures — plan the slope and drain position before construction

- **Always install a proper built shower enclosure** (not a prefab cabin) except for a dacha/summer-house context.
- **⚠️ Drain/trap position must be planned against the existing fan (фановая) pipe location and achievable slope** — placing the drain too far from the shower area forces raising the shower floor/threshold to achieve the needed fall; **a 25 cm platform rise is called explicitly too high/impractical** ("это уже тяжело").
- **Drain placement recommendation**: place the drain/trap **near the wall** where the fan-pipe routing allows, since a wall-adjacent linear drain only needs a single-direction slope. With **mosaic** tile, drain position matters less (small units cut trivially for a multi-directional slope); with **large-format porcelain tile**, a centre drain forces expensive/difficult cutting into four sloped planes — a wall/linear drain avoids this.
