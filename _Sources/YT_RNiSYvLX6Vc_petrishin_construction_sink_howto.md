## Construction Sink — Build Instructions and a Tile-Adhesion Defect Mechanism

1. **Problem statement without a construction sink**: the typical
   jobsite water-access workaround is a hose connected to the toilet —
   not practical for washing hands or tools. Tools that get genuinely
   dirty (spatulas/trowels, buckets, paint rollers) are often simply
   thrown away and rebought **at the client's expense**, because
   washing them via the toilet-hose setup is inconvenient enough that
   workers skip it rather than attempt it.
2. **⚠️ Genuinely new defect mechanism — tile bonds to its own
   cutting-slurry residue, not to the wall**: when a tiler wet-cuts tile
   on a saw, the tile's cut/reverse face gets coated in dirty cutting
   slurry. Without a convenient sink, tilers rarely bother walking to a
   toilet to rinse this off — the slurry is left to dry, forming an
   invisible dust layer on the tile's back face. **Adhesive is then
   applied directly to this dirt layer**, so the tile effectively bonds
   to its own dried cutting residue rather than to the wall — producing
   a later hollow-sounding ("бухтеть"/knock-test-failing) tile.
   **Explicit reframing of a common installer complaint**: tilers who
   later blame "a bad/defective batch of adhesive" for hollow tile are,
   per this source, often actually experiencing this residue-bonding
   failure instead — a real root-cause correction, not just a new tip.
   A construction sink lets the tiler rinse the cut face immediately,
   removing this failure mode.
3. **Real time/cost investment stated directly**: a few hours of build
   time and a few thousand RUB in materials produces a working
   construction sink.
4. **Step-by-step build instructions**: build a wooden frame sized to
   fit a plastic basin/tub. **Recommended sink height: 80cm.** Cut a
   shelf from **12mm plywood** to mount the mixer/faucet — the same
   shelf is angled to create the basin's own drainage slope toward the
   drain. Cut the mixer-mounting hole with a **32mm diameter** hole
   saw/bit. Cut the drain/siphon hole with a **45mm diameter** hole
   saw. Can be run either as a simple basin with a bucket underneath
   collecting waste water, or plumbed with a real supply-and-drain
   connection.
5. **Practical uses beyond the tile mechanism above**: washing hands/
   face after work, even washing hair; washing any tool (trowels,
   buckets, rollers) that would otherwise be discarded rather than
   cleaned.
6. **Long-term personal account**: the presenter states he has used a
   construction sink on every single object for several years since
   first building one, and considers it indispensable enough to
   actively recommend to every viewer. `single-account`.
