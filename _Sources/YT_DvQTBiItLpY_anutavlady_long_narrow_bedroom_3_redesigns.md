## Case — Long/Narrow Bedroom, 3 Redesign Variants

- **⚠️ Bedroom mirror placement rule (decorative, not wardrobe-door)**:
  place a decorative mirror on the wall *behind* the headboard, not
  facing the bed from elsewhere in the room — achieves the desired
  visual effect without disturbing the person lying on the bed. Distinct
  from, and complementary to, this project's existing wardrobe-mirror-
  facing-into-closet rule (`Bedroom_Design_Principles.md`) — this rule
  covers a freestanding/hung decorative mirror, not a wardrobe door.
- **Photo-row proportion rule for a long wall**: a horizontal row of
  framed photos along a long wall visually emphasizes the room's already
  poor (too-elongated) proportions. Arranging the same photos in a
  *vertical* block concentrated behind the headboard instead visually
  "cuts" the long wall, makes the room read as less long, and makes the
  ceiling read as slightly taller. **Refinement**: sizing the photo block
  to match the headboard's own width (using 6 photos rather than 4, if
  needed to reach that width) improves the composition further.
- **Accent-wall composition rule — avoid a centered material/color
  split**: when an accent wall combines two materials or colors (e.g.
  wall panels + wallpaper), splitting them exactly at the bed's center
  line reads poorly; an asymmetric split (roughly 1/3-2/3, offset to one
  side) reads as a more dynamic, intentional composition. Demonstrated
  across multiple reference-photo critiques in the same video.
- **Match a reference accent-wall's material/color family to the room's
  *existing* furniture finish before adopting it** — a wood-toned panel
  treatment that looks good in a reference photo was rejected for this
  specific room because the room's actual furniture is white laminate
  (ЛДСП); the same treatment would suit a room with cappuccino/grey-toned
  furniture instead. A material-compatibility check, not a universal
  material ranking.
- **⚠️ Wardrobe depth vs. hanging-clothes function**: a **40cm-deep**
  wardrobe forces hanging rods to be mounted frontally (parallel to the
  door) rather than perpendicular, which only works well for a
  shelves-only wardrobe — **hanging clothes on a rod needs the fuller
  ~60cm depth** to hang perpendicular to the door. Flagged in this
  source as the room's single biggest problem ("главная больная точка
  интерьера"). Candidate for cross-reference into
  `14_Furniture/Wardrobes_and_Storage.md` if not already covered there.
- **Low dresser/console height rule for a walkway-adjacent room**: keep a
  dresser/console at or below roughly outstretched-arm-fingertip height
  — a taller piece of furniture makes a room feel cramped and impedes
  free movement even at an identical footprint to a shorter piece.
  Framed as the reason a nearly 2m-wide, 40cm-deep low dresser doesn't
  make a narrow walkway feel tight, where a taller piece of the same
  footprint would.
- **Foldable wall-mounted clothes-hanger racks** (складные вешалки-
  трансформеры), mounted above a dresser, as compact supplementary
  clothes storage that folds flat against the wall when not in active
  use — a real product-category solution for "no-renovation" storage
  expansion.
- **Illuminated mirror as a no-renovation lighting fix**: a plug-in
  (cord + existing switch type, sensor/touch options available)
  illuminated mirror can add functional lighting to a dim area without
  any electrical work, so long as it can be run off an existing outlet
  via its own cord.
- **⚠️ Under-bed rug caution specific to a storage-drawer bed**: a bed
  base built as a solid box with pull-out drawers (not legs) should
  **not** have a rug placed partially underneath it — uneven support
  (part of the box on rug, part on bare floor) can skew the drawer
  mechanism over time, and cleaning under it becomes difficult. Place
  the rug beside the bed instead (e.g. in the aisle between bed and
  wardrobe) rather than under it. This is a distinct caution from this
  store's existing bed-to-rug *clearance* formula (Kruglov,
  `Bedroom_Design_Principles.md`) — this one is about a rug's placement
  relative to a specific bed-base construction type, not clearance
  distance.
- **Built-in pop-up/flush outlets mounted directly on or beside a
  headboard's own surface**, functioning as an ordinary extension cord
  wired into the headboard rather than nightstand-mounted outlets —
  sometimes sold with a cord/plug included, sometimes requiring one
  purchased separately. Presented as a more convenient bedside-power
  solution than under-nightstand outlets.
- **Cordless, dimmable, rechargeable bedside lamps** as a way to avoid
  bedside cord-management entirely, independent of nightstand outlet
  placement.
- **⚠️ Structural bracing rule for a reoriented/freestanding wardrobe**:
  a wardrobe unit repositioned away from its original wall-anchored
  placement (here, turned 90° with its doors removed, used more like
  open shelving) is **unsafe without anchoring** — connecting/bracing it
  structurally to an adjacent desk unit is presented as the practical
  fix when neither piece is separately fixed to the wall or floor.
- **Reference-image analysis methodology**: when using a reference photo,
  deliberately analyze its proportion and composition, not just its
  color/material palette — presented as a teachable skill (the source's
  own course dedicates an early lesson specifically to this), and as the
  reason casual reference-collecting often fails to translate into a
  workable design.
