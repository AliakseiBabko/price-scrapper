## Design Concept

- Standard color-wheel vocabulary: primary/secondary/tertiary hues; tints (base + white), tones (base + grey) — genuinely useful as a formal framework even though implicit in earlier content.
- **⚠️ Two genuinely new named color schemes, additions to the existing analogous/complementary/triad systems from Round 4's `vM7inxJZ-6U`**:
  1. **Monochromatic scheme** — using different tints/tones/shades of ONE single hue for a cohesive, visually enlarging effect (minimal color variety reduces visual fragmentation).
  2. **"Accented analogous" scheme** — take an analogous group (e.g., blue+green) and add ONE complementary accent color (opposite the group's central hue on the wheel, e.g., red-orange) as a bold accent while the analogous group stays the cohesive base.
- **⚠️ Gray-undertone contrast mechanism, a worked example extending the existing "match warm with warm, cool with cool" rule**: warm grey placed next to cool grey reads "dirty"/muddy, while warm grey next to warm tones reads cohesive and cozy.
- **⚠️ Saturation-context principle, distinct from hue/temperature matching, genuinely new**: a saturated/pure color's perceived aggressiveness depends on its saturation level RELATIVE TO its neighboring colors, not just the hue itself — his worked example: a saturated red next to soft pastels reads aggressive/overwhelming, but the SAME red next to deep earthy tones reads calm/harmonious. A distinct AXIS from warm/cool matching (this is about saturation/intensity matching specifically).
- **⚠️ Saturation-matching rule with a bathroom worked example, a specific, actionable, genuinely new principle**: mixing muted/earthy colors with pure/highly-saturated colors in the SAME room creates a "dirty"/mismatched read because their saturation levels don't match, independent of hue. His worked example: a bright, pure sky-blue wall paired with an earthy-beige countertop reads as if the countertop is "dirty" against the vivid blue, even though the hues themselves aren't clashing. Recommends keeping saturation levels consistent throughout a palette: either mostly muted tones with a FEW pure accents, or all similarly-saturated tones together.
- **⚠️ 60/30/10 rule restated and endorsed AGAIN — SEE CROSS-ROUND TRACKING**: explicitly restated as "60% доминирующий цвет... 30% дополнительный... 10% акцент," described here as "приём, который используют практически все дизайнеры" (the technique nearly all designers use). This is Krasnov's SECOND explicit endorsement across two different videos (after Round 5's `vM7inxJZ-6U`), strengthening his side of the existing Кузина Perspectives disagreement on `17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Color_Harmony_and_Combination_Rules.md`. - **⚠️ "80/20 warm-cool balance" rule, distinct from and explicitly reconciled with 60/30/10 in the same breath**: separately from the 60/30/10 role-based ratio, recommends an 80/20 split specifically for warm-vs-cool color balance — for a cozy/warm-leaning interior, ~80% of colors should be warm-toned with ~20% cool accents to balance; for a cool/airy-leaning interior, invert to ~80% cool with ~20% warm accents. His own explicit clarification that this doesn't contradict 60/30/10 since the two rules operate on different axes (60/30/10 = role-proportion: dominant/secondary/accent; 80/20 = temperature-proportion: warm/cool) — "противоречие между правилом 60/30/10 и правилом 80/20 тут нет никакого... мы объективно можем вписаться в оба этих правила."
