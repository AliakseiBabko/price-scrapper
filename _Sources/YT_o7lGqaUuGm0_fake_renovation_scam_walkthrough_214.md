## Mistakes / Warnings — Fraudulent "Move-In-Ready" Renovation, Full QC Walkthrough

- **⚠️ Fabricated ("fake") underfloor-heating installation — a genuine consumer-fraud finding, not just a QC defect.** Clients specifically asked about underfloor heating before purchase (tile floors were too cold for them to walk on barefoot) and were shown three functioning-looking thermostats, each labeled for a different zone. After purchase, activating the thermostats caused sparking/an electrical fault. Investigation found: the thermostats received line power but produced no output, and their output wiring was simply short-circuited together with no heating cable at all — confirmed with a cable detector across the whole tiled area (nothing found). When the sellers were contacted, their own suggested "fix" was to install ordinary wall outlets in place of the fake thermostats. **Treat any claimed underfloor-heating system in a pre-renovated unit as unverified until independently confirmed** (visual thermostat + verbal claim is not evidence) — this is the single most severe consumer-fraud finding in this Category 5 trial and a genuinely new caution for this store.
- **Domestic water heater sealed permanently behind a later-built wall, with no service access** — the water heater was mounted first, then a partition wall was framed and tiled around it afterward, permanently blocking access. Once it failed, repair or replacement required demolishing the finished bathroom wall/tile down to the studs. Compounding defect: even after removal, no dedicated electrical circuit exists to power a tankless replacement in that location. **General rule extracted**: any water heater or similarly serviceable appliance must be planned with real future access in mind — mounting it, then walling around it, is a checkable planning-sequence failure.
- **Toilet seat height, an explicit numeric ergonomic standard, genuinely new for this store**: **the seat itself should sit 42cm above the finished floor; the bowl rim without a seat, 40cm (400mm)** — the defect found was an unusually tall installation that left a shorter-statured user's feet not reaching the floor while seated, a real daily-use discomfort.
- **AC pre-wiring claims must be verified for completeness, not just the presence of a power cable** — sellers claimed the unit was "ready for AC, just hang the units," but only a 220V power cable existed at each intended location; no refrigerant line conduits and no dedicated circuit breaker were present (the cable was simply spliced onto an existing general-purpose outlet circuit) — AC units need a dedicated breaker, and refrigerant lines require breaking into the finished wall to add after the fact, defeating the "ready to go" claim entirely.
- **A dishwasher and sink sharing one continuous, undivided counter opening are both hard to use — a genuinely awkward two-basin/appliance interaction**, since a typical use pattern (scrape food waste into a bin, then load the dishwasher) needs both accessible at once without one blocking the other; a poor layout here forces working around the conflict on every use.
- **Kitchen cabinet doors reaching only partway to the ceiling leave an open dust-collecting gap with exposed wiring** — described as a real recurring cleaning burden and, for an allergy sufer specifically, a genuine health irritant (repeated coughing/sneezing while trying to clean the gap).
- **A curtain niche must be checked against the actual curtain type before finishing** — a decorative ceiling recess built specifically to house curtain hardware was placed in the same plane as the windowsill, meaning drapes hung there would simply fall onto the sill rather than hanging freely — the niche was structurally built but functionally useless, forcing an exposed surface-mounted curtain rod as the only working option.
- **A radiator cover/decorative grille is expected at Comfort-class and above** — an open (uncovered) radiator was flagged as visually and functionally substandard for the renovation tier being sold.
- **Pendant/track lighting in a bathroom/kitchen should be checked for consistent color temperature across all fixtures** — a real defect found: three installed spotlights of the same fixture type showed three visibly different color temperatures.
- **Never buy window trim/reveal strips made from secondary (recycled) plastic** — installers left the original window-frame protective nail-strip in place and puttied/plastered over it instead of removing it first, then filled the resulting gap with a secondary-plastic infill strip; that strip visibly yellows within about 2-3 years and can never be restored to white, unlike the frame's own primary-plastic material. Corroborates and extends this store's existing primary-vs-secondary-plastic yellowing caution (previously recorded for sill end-caps) to window trim strips specifically.
- **A concealed/soft-close AC condensate or plumbing service-access panel must actually reach the components it's meant to service** — a real defect found: a sliding access panel only opened in one direction, and even fully open still left the actual shutoff/service point reachable only by removing the sink itself — a "service access" panel that doesn't functionally provide service access.
- **A hand-held shower needs an adjustable-height rod, not a fixed wall bracket** — a fixed-height showerhead mount forces every user regardless of height into the same (often awkward) position; a defect found left the fixed height too low for comfortable head-washing for a taller user.
- **A hydronic (water-fed) towel warmer showing active corrosion/rust staining at its wall bracket is a real, not cosmetic, defect** — the unit in this case had already begun leaking and had to be permanently capped off (converted to non-functional decorative-only), losing all drying/warming function since no electric alternative was installed.
- **Sink/basin width should be chosen from the standard range (60-100cm) to match the room's actual available wall width — never let a rigid room dimension force an undersized or awkwardly-fit basin.** Plan the room dimension around the intended fixture, not the reverse.
- **Washing-machine niche width must be set from the machine's actual dimensions during design, with the opening's own construction referenced to it** — a defect found showed a visibly mismatched niche width relative to the installed machine, an avoidable planning-sequence error (same "plan around the fixture" principle as the sink rule above).
- **Recessed/point ceiling lighting frequently sourced from unreliable low-cost fixtures burns out at a visibly high rate** — a real observed pattern: fixtures replaced roughly monthly, with a third of a given room's fixtures already dead again within a month of the last replacement — framed as a fixture-quality issue, not an installation-technique issue, since the replacement fixtures fail at the same rate as the originals.
- **AC pre-wiring, floor threshold hardware, and other "hidden" fixings should use concealed (not open-fastener) fixing systems by default at Comfort-class and above** — repeated defects found: transition thresholds fixed with visible exposed screws (when concealed-fixing threshold hardware has existed for years), and window-sill trim held by open screws instead of a concealed clip system — both flagged as outdated, avoidable choices.
- **A "ready to move in" claim for a purchased renovated unit should be independently verified room by room, fixture by fixture, before treating the renovation as sunk value** — the video's own closing framing: buying an already-renovated unit to save time backfired financially (a net 4.5M RUB loss once resale-comparable pricing plus the cost of a full teardown-and-redo are counted), and the source's explicit advice to viewers is to always start any renovation (including a "pre-renovated" purchase) from an independent, verified project rather than trusting a seller's claims about existing work.
