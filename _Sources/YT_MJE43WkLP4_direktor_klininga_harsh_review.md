## Rules / Heuristics — QC Checklist of Commonly-Missed Spots

- **⚠️ Kitchen facade protective film leaves an adhesive residue layer that
  must be actively washed, not just peeled**: cabinet fronts look clean
  and smooth once the film is off, but the leftover adhesive rapidly
  attracts dust/dirt that won't release again until properly washed —
  "wiping and polishing" alone is described as an incomplete job that
  looks done under casual inspection.
- **⚠️ Backsplash/apron grout residue is often invisible under ambient
  light and only shows under bright/accent lighting** — the presenter
  demonstrates a spot that looks clean with room lights on but reveals
  dust and un-wiped grout haze once the under-cabinet accent lighting is
  switched on. **General QC technique**: check surfaces under strong/
  direct lighting before declaring them clean, not just casual ambient
  light.
- **Hidden/hard-to-reach spots named as commonly skipped**: the underside/
  back cavity of kitchen drawers (looks clean when the drawer is checked
  normally, but the presenter finds significant dust once reaching
  inside/behind); the interior of radiator fin gaps (needs a steam
  cleaner with a narrow specialized brush attachment to actually reach —
  **⚠️ named corner-cutting fraud risk**: some cleaners fake this step by
  spraying and wiping only the visible front surface, leaving the true
  interior untouched while it visually "looks cleaned"); the underside of
  windowsills; door/cabinet handle interior surfaces (grip side, rarely
  wiped).
- **⚠️ Bathtub screen (экран ванной) removal is a genuine skill/liability
  gap**: whether a fitted bathtub access screen can be safely detached to
  clean behind it isn't always something a general cleaner has the
  expertise to judge — the source states he isn't confident it can be
  removed without risk on this specific job, framing the real options as
  calling a specialist, spending extra (possibly substantial) time
  figuring it out safely, or leaving it to the client to decide/accept.
  A genuine limitation worth knowing when scoping post-renovation
  cleaning, not resolved as "always removable."
- **⚠️ Door/window-frame top edges (above the sash, not just the visible
  window itself) commonly hold a heavy layer of construction dust/cement
  residue that's rarely checked** — applies to every interior door, not
  just windows. **Explicit method**: use a ladder and a vacuum with a
  brush nozzle to remove it; **wiping construction dust with a wet cloth
  on wood/dusty surfaces is called "madness"** — it smears rather than
  removes, and the correct method is dry vacuuming.
- **Window rubber gasket/seal conceals dust that's invisible without
  physically pulling the seal aside to check** — a spot that passes
  casual visual inspection but fails once the seal is manually checked.
- **⚠️ Never let the renovation crew wash windows themselves**: crews may
  use a flat putty knife/scraper to knock dried paint splatter off glass,
  which scratches the glass permanently — professional glass cleaning of
  dried paint should be done by cleaners, not construction workers
  improvising with a metal tool.
- **Interior-only window washing on a sealed/non-opening high sash leaves
  faint streaks visible only at certain lighting angles** — a real,
  accepted service-tier limitation: a genuinely streak-free result on a
  sealed pane requires exterior access (e.g. a rope-access window
  washer), which standard interior post-renovation cleaning doesn't
  include by default; flagged as a known limitation rather than a defect
  to chase indefinitely.

## Dust Removal ("обеспыливание") Mechanism, Cross-Referencing Stage-1 Logic

- **Dry de-dusting is a hard rule**: no water — walls and ceiling first
  (vacuum or a specialized dry tool), floors last. Directly reinforces
  `YT_oF62qDhYkuw`'s stage-1 ceiling-then-walls sequencing.
- **⚠️ Genuine mechanism for why the multi-stage process is more reliable
  than a single-pass clean, distinct from `YT_oF62qDhYkuw`'s framing**:
  during any de-dusting pass, disturbed dust visibly resettles around the
  apartment (chandeliers, door tops, walls) within the same session — in
  a staged process this is caught by the *next* stage's own de-dusting
  pass; in a single-pass ("за один заход") cleaning there is no such
  safety net, so any dust that resettles after the pass finishes simply
  stays. This is a causal argument for staging (a self-correcting QC
  loop), not just "more thoroughness."
- **Air humidifier run on-site during cleaning as an active dust-control
  technique**: raising ambient air humidity makes airborne dust heavier
  so it settles instead of continuing to circulate/resettle on just-
  cleaned surfaces — an active, not passive, part of the process.

## Protective-Technique Notes (extends `YT_oF62qDhYkuw`'s protection content)

- **Correct door-handle taping order**: cover the handle in plastic film
  first, then apply masking tape over the film — taping directly onto a
  bare handle (as this crew did) leaves adhesive residue that's much
  harder to remove afterward.
- **Floor transition thresholds are a commonly-missed protection point**:
  crews carrying construction-debris bags across a threshold strip
  repeatedly scuff/damage it if it isn't taped/covered like the rest of
  the floor — an easy-to-forget protection point distinct from the main
  floor surface.

## Mistakes / Warnings

- **⚠️ Real self-critical incident, acid-based cleaner damaging a
  fixture**: a crew member spilled/left an acid-based cleaning product
  near a faucet/showerhead fitting; combined with hot water exposure it
  damaged the fixture's finish. The team's own stated response: replace
  the damaged item and own the mistake rather than hide it — cited as
  their internal policy, not hypothetical. Reinforces this vault's
  existing chemical-damage caution from `YT_oF62qDhYkuw`'s fireplace-door
  incident with a second, independent example from the same channel.
- **Floor cleaning quality can't always be confirmed in a single attempt**:
  mopping, waiting for the floor to dry, then re-inspecting is described
  as sometimes needing more than one round to reach full quality — a
  concrete reason the source gives for preferring the staged process over
  a single rushed pass.
- **Repeated re-inspection of already-"finished" rooms is standard
  practice, not paranoia**: the presenter walks back through
  previously-marked-clean rooms multiple times during the session and
  still finds missed spots (a window streak, dust on a shelf) — framed as
  a normal, expected part of doing the job properly rather than a sign of
  the crew's incompetence.
