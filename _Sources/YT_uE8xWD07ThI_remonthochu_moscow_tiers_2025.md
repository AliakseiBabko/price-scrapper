## ⚠️⚠️ 3. The breakdown by element — and a spec distinction worth more than the prices

**Средний-plus specification, Moscow 2025, RUB per m² (rough + finish):**

| Element | Stated | USD/m² |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Floor** | **10,000–15,000** | **≈ $120–$190** |
| **Walls** | **15,000–30,000** | **≈ $190–$370** |
| **Ceiling** | **10,000–20,000**, up to 30,000 | **≈ $120–$250**, up to ≈ $370 |

**⚠️⚠️ WHY WALLS SPAN 2× — the operations differ, and he lists them:**

| Wall for **WALLPAPER** | Wall for **PAINT** |
| :--- | :--- |
| plaster → base filler → several primer coats between layers → sand → hang | **all of the above, PLUS fibreglass lining (стеклохолст), PLUS three coats of finishing filler, PLUS sanding** — to a surface *«близкую к А4 формату бумаги»*, i.e. near-perfect |

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ This is the most useful thing on the page: it explains the paint premium as a list of ADDED OPERATIONS rather than as a taste surcharge, and it makes the quote checkable — if a contractor prices paint-ready walls at wallpaper rates, ask which of those operations is missing.**

**⚠️⚠️ AND A LABOUR PRINCIPLE: the same work costs MORE on a ceiling than on a wall.** A plasterboard ceiling beats stretch on cost because it must also be filled and sanded — *«все операции, которые мы делали на стене, их придётся также делать и на потолке, но в неудобном положении, смотря вверх»*. **⚠️ He also notes stretch ceilings are no longer cheap once hidden profile, cornice lighting and shadow profile are added.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 4. What actually drives the price — and he ranks them

1. **Material cost.** (A single engineered board at ~3,000 RUB against a 2 m² pack of quartz-vinyl at ~5,000 — ⚠️ illustrative figures, not quotes.)
2. **⚠️⚠️ *«Самый главный критерий ценника — это СЛОЖНОСТЬ РАБОТЫ.»*** Cheap material laid in a complex way costs more — **his example: laminate in herringbone AND glued down. Doable, but it carries risk (moisture, swelling), so you pay for the risk as well as the time.**
3. **⚠️⚠️ Access and logistics, which he calls non-obvious.** Distance from Moscow, or difficulty of access — **his example is Moscow City, where raising materials means queuing for the lift and can take a whole day or two in parts.** Obstacles → time → money.

> **→ ⚠️ Item 2 inverts the intuitive model: complexity outranks material. And item 3 is a cost driver this vault's delivery-model material does not name — worth carrying, because it is assessable before any quote by looking at the building.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 5. Three cost-saving measures — and the second one argues against the obvious

1. **⚠️⚠️ A FIXED ESTIMATE, which is downstream of a FINISHED design project.** If you proceed stage by stage while the designer keeps drawing, *«100% вы не знаете, какая будет цена впереди»*. **A fixed price requires a worked-through, accepted, properly planned design project from the start.**
   **→ ⚠️⚠️ THE SAME CLAIM AS THE DELIVERABLE-SET WORK, ARRIVING FROM THE MONEY SIDE.** [[00_Master/Planning_Project_Deliverable_Set|Planning Project Deliverable Set]] argues about what an album contains; **this says the album's COMPLETENESS is the precondition for a fixed price at all. That is the strongest argument in the vault for finishing the design set before starting.**
2. **⚠️⚠️ He WARNS AGAINST the cheapest-tender reflex.** Not every contractor is conscientious, and cheaper does not mean equal. **The failure mode he describes: a low entry price, extras piled on, the estimate grows, he fails the brief, you dismiss him, a competent contractor arrives at higher default rates, declares it must be redone, and you pay for the rework** — *«как снежный ком накапливаются проблемы»*. **Choose a competent contractor at the outset instead.**
3. **⚠️ A QUALITY ROUGH STAGE.** *«Обои можно переклеить, краску перекрасить, шпаклёвку перешпаклевать… но основа должна быть очень хорошо сделана.»* Control quality early so nothing is redone at finish. **Corroborates this vault's existing rough-stage emphasis.**

> ⚠️⚠️ **AN UNFULFILLED PROMISE, recorded because it bears on how much weight the video carries: he opens by promising a lifehack that saves "up to 30% of your budget", and the three measures above are never quantified. The 30% figure is not substantiated anywhere in the video and is NOT carried.**
