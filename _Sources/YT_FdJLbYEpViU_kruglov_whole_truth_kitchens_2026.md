## New facts — Facades

1. **Acrylic facade, a distinct category from this store's existing plastic-on-MDF tier
   content**: LDSP or MDF substrate coated with an acrylic layer (effectively a thick,
   glossy plastic finish) — extremely high-gloss ("grand piano" gloss) is the most common
   form, though matte/soft-touch acrylic variants exist. Moisture/UV/temperature-stable,
   scratch-resistant even in gloss finish. **Same fingerprint-visibility rule as elsewhere**:
   gloss shows fingerprints heavily, matte/soft-touch barely shows them.
2. **Stone veneer (каменный шпон) facade** — same MDF-substrate-plus-edge-banding
   construction as the existing wood-veneer content, but with a thin stone veneer layer
   instead of wood. Heavier than wood veneer, requiring reinforced premium hardware and
   carcass; flagged as a genuinely rare, high-cost, high-complexity choice, used in very few
   real kitchens.
3. **⚠️ "Феникс" (Phoenix) facade material, more fully specified than this round's
   `A1mvvVObK5M` coating mention**: manufactured by **Arpa Industrial (Italy)**, described as
   a next-generation HPL laminate — ultra-matte with a Soft Touch surface. **Self-repair
   mechanism**: a small scratch can be heat-buffed out on-site with a cloth-covered iron,
   without removing the facade or sending it to a workshop — described as a genuine
   industry first for a plastic-family facade material. No visible fingerprints even
   without a scratch. Water-resistant, chemical-cleaner-tolerant. Con: no gloss finish
   option exists, and no relief/milled profile is possible (flat facades only).
4. **Solid wood (массив) facade** — classic-style only, made from a single continuous
   piece of properly dried/prepared wood. Fully restorable/repairable (sand out scratches
   and dents). **Cost scales geometrically with wood species rarity/cost.** **Requires
   active home climate control** (humidity and temperature) — without it, the facades (and
   any accompanying solid-wood flooring/furniture in the same home) can warp, developing
   gaps and misalignment.
5. **Facade-mixing technique**: combine different facade materials within one kitchen run
   by cost/style zone (e.g. acrylic on upper cabinets, veneer on lower) — a real option on
   a custom-built (not modular/prefab) kitchen order; not available on a supersegment-economy
   LDSP-with-cheap-film kitchen.
6. **⚠️ Edge-banding (кромка) three-tier classification, more detailed than this store's
   existing edge-banding notes**: **economy — 0.4mm PVC edge, hand-applied (no factory
   process), seam not watertight, vulnerable to peeling with cheap adhesive**; **comfort —
   1-2mm, factory-applied, seam still visible but properly sealed**; **premium — PUR-glue
   bonded, seam nearly invisible, fully watertight for the life of the kitchen, resistant to
   temperature-driven adhesive failure.**
7. **Facade summary ranking (source's own synthesis)**: most dirt/fingerprint-prone = any
   gloss finish (film, plastic, acrylic, or enamel); most dirt-absorbing = natural wood
   veneer; least dimensionally stable (warps/deforms with use) = solid wood; most
   wear-resistant = plastic, enamel, film, LDSP, and Phoenix (roughly tied); source's own
   aesthetic favorite = natural wood veneer.

## New facts — Carcass (Корпус)

11. **⚠️ Formaldehyde emission class by tier**: economy-tier LDSP carcass boards meet
    class **E1** (at the edge of the legal safety threshold); comfort/premium boards from
    named brands (Egger, Kronospan) achieve **E0.5 or E0** (meaningfully lower emissions).
12. **"Moisture-resistant LDSP" flagged as more marketing than real benefit for carcass
    material — an independent corroboration of this round's own `e3bHUlP0PMY` claim about
    the same "moisture-resistant" label.** This source adds the specific failure mechanism:
    the real vulnerability is the edge/seam, not the LDSP body itself — if the edge banding
    fails or isn't sealed, both standard and "moisture-resistant" LDSP swell at essentially
    the same rate.
13. **New carcass material: Kronospan laminated MDF board** — combines LDSP-like surface
    properties with MDF-level structural strength; a newer market entrant, priced above
    standard LDSP. **Carcass board thickness tiers: 16mm (economy-only), 18mm (meaningfully
    stronger, comfort/premium), 25mm (rare, premium-only exception).**
14. **Carcass QC differences by tier**: economy-tier carcasses sometimes skip edge banding
    entirely on hidden/rear-facing joints (a real moisture-ingress risk if water reaches
    that unprotected edge) and show more manufacturing defects in shelf-pin/assembly holes;
    comfort/premium always edge-band every joint (1-2mm) at the factory and show
    meaningfully fewer defects. **Milling (фрезеровка) for concealed wiring/lighting
    channels is absent at the economy tier, present at comfort/premium.**
15. **Carcass color convention**: economy-tier carcass interior is white or light-grey only;
    comfort/premium carcass color is matched to (or coordinated with) the facade color — a
    detail the source frames as immediately signaling a kitchen's price tier the moment a
    door is opened.
16. **Back-panel (задняя стенка) material tier ladder**: economy — 3mm HDF/fiberboard,
    nailed on; comfort — 4-5mm HDF, grooved into the side panels for added rigidity;
    premium — same as comfort, occasionally LDSP (flagged by the source as overkill for
    this application).

## New facts — Countertops (with a concrete price comparison)

25. **⚠️ Economy tier (LDSP) detail, extending this store's existing DSP+HPL budget entry**:
    moisture-resistant LDSP core, **28-38mm thick**, HPL-laminated, edges either
    fold-wrapped in the same laminate or PVC-edge-banded. Average lifespan **5-7 years**
    (up to 10-15 years with careful use); cannot support an undermount sink (edge banding at
    the cutout is the vulnerable point); visible seams are managed by sealant (weakest),
    a metal/plastic trim profile, or a factory mitred "euro-cut" joint (**best method,
    used on corner runs**).
26. **⚠️ Comfort tier (acrylic solid surface) detail**: mineral filler + acrylic resin
    composite; any shape/curve/radius achievable, including a fully integrated
    same-material sink with zero visible seam; non-porous (no water/coffee/wine staining);
    highly repairable by a specialist (professional sand-and-polish, not DIY); low heat
    resistance (a hot pan will still damage it); scratches relatively easily but is easily
    repaired — **source's own maintenance tip: a light professional sand-and-polish every
    few years keeps it looking new.**
27. **⚠️ Premium tier (quartz agglomerate) detail — extends this store's existing 2026-trend
    quartz-agglomerate content**: 90-95% natural quartz + polymer binder + pigment; slabs
    typically **20mm or 30mm** (rare thin 12mm option); second only to diamond in scratch
    hardness; genuinely heat-resistant (hot pans/pots directly on the surface); non-porous,
    very easy to clean; realistic functional lifespan **10-30 years** ("you'll get bored of
    it before it fails"). Cons: heavy and brittle under bending stress (needs a fully
    supported substrate); **the slab seam is never fully hideable** — always visible; very
    difficult to repair a chip (live with it, or replace the whole slab).

    source either doesn't know about or considers not real integration), but the flat
    contradiction is recorded rather than silently resolved.
29. **Natural stone countertop detail (granite/marble/travertine/slate)**: granite —
    strong but porous (wine/coffee stains can be permanent); marble — even more porous
    (lemon juice stains can be permanent); travertine and slate — more exotic, harder to
    fabricate/install; **all natural stone needs periodic re-sealing/impregnation** to
    manage porosity; framed as chosen more for aesthetics/status than durability, and
    used in relatively few real kitchens for that reason.
30. **Compact-plate/HPL self-supporting countertop panel** — corroborates this store's
    existing "compact-laminate" countertop content (Round 8) with additional detail: no
    LDSP/MDF substrate at all (the HPL laminate itself is structurally self-supporting),
    **10-12mm thick**, sold and jointed like natural stone slabs; con: priced comparable to
    natural stone/acrylic despite being fundamentally a plastic product.
31. **Natural wood countertop** — rare in current kitchens; requires constant
    oiling/sealing, absorbs moisture, scratches easily; framed as decorative rather than
    genuinely functional.
32. **Out-of-league/niche materials**: stainless steel (commercial/restaurant kitchens);
    glass (very costly, shows every mark); tile — historically messy grout lines, but
    **modern large-format porcelain slab now makes a genuine tile countertop feasible**
    (cannot support an integrated sink; any damage requires full-slab replacement; joints
    filled with two-component epoxy resin, rarely cement grout).

    (≈$7,400) total**, from changing one material choice.
34. **Countertop tier leaders (source's own explicit picks)**: economy = LDSP; comfort =
    acrylic with an integrated same-material sink; premium = quartz agglomerate.

## New facts — Backsplash (Фартук)

35. **Partial-height backsplash trend, with a technique detail**: rather than tiling/glazing
    the full wall height between counter and upper cabinets, a **20-30cm (sometimes 15cm)
    partial-height upstand** — often in the same material as the countertop itself (e.g. a
    20cm quartz-agglomerate upstand) — protects the countertop/wall joint while leaving the
    wall above free for any finish material, since that zone gets dirty far less often.
36. **⚠️ Backsplash material tier ladder, first structured version for this store**:
    **tile/porcelain** (mosaic including glass, subway/kabanchik, standard formats, or
    modern large-format porcelain up to 320x240cm) — fully waterproof, repairable if
    matching stock remains, huge style range; con: visible grout/seam lines (filled with
    epoxy, rarely cement). **Same-material-as-countertop** (matches the countertop
    material, e.g. acrylic or quartz agglomerate) — monolithic look, fully hermetic seam;
    con: roughly doubles the material cost for that zone. **Tempered glass** (with printed
    graphic) — called outdated/rarely used now; requires all outlet/switch/fixture cutouts
    to be pre-planned and factory-cut with zero tolerance for error; shows
    fingerprints/grease heavily; fragile (a crack means full replacement); not actually
    cheaper than tile. **MDF-based** (plastic/veneer/enamel-coated) — wide color range,
    good moisture resistance; a plastic-coated MDF backsplash can deform from cooktop heat
    — pair with a small tempered-glass shield directly behind the cooktop in that case.
    **Plain plastic** — cheapest, poor heat resistance, easily damaged, can discolor over
    time. **Combination/partial-shield** — tempered glass only behind the cooktop/sink,
    cheaper wall finish (moisture-resistant paint/wallpaper) elsewhere — a budget technique,
    rarely used in higher-end kitchens. **No backsplash at all** — acceptable only for a
    kitchen that isn't actually used for cooking.
37. **⚠️ Real installation-sequencing cost multiplier**: tiling/glazing the backsplash
    during the rough-in phase (before the kitchen cabinetry is installed) is roughly
    **2-3x cheaper** than tiling after the kitchen is already installed — the latter carries
    real damage risk to the installed countertop/facade/sink/mixer/cooktop plus added
    protective-covering labor, both of which get priced into the job.
38. **Backsplash tier leaders (source's own explicit picks)**: super-economy = none or
    plain plastic; economy = MDF panels; comfort = large-format porcelain tile; premium =
    same-material-as-countertop backsplash.

## New facts — Sink & Mixer

42. **⚠️ Sink-mounting three-tier framework, extending this round's own rimmed-sink caution
    (`A1mvvVObK5M`) with the full comparison**: **top-mount/rimmed (drop-in)** — cheapest,
    widest material/style range, fits any countertop, but least modern and hardest to keep
    clean at the rim. **Undermount (glued from below, sometimes with added mechanical
    fixings)** — modern, easiest to sweep debris straight into the basin, but **only
    compatible with a countertop material that doesn't need edge-banding at the cutout**
    (rules out LDSP and other edge-banded materials; works with quartz/acrylic/composite).
    **Fully integrated (monolithic sink-and-countertop, typically acrylic)** — easiest to
    clean, best aesthetics, highest cost.
43. **Sink shape preference reinforced across a third independent source**: round sinks
    called the clear "outsider" (can't properly wash a baking tray/rack); rectangular or
    square is the only recommended shape — corroborates Round 8's `2I77xJIeRwM` budget-sink
    guidance (already recorded), now independently reinforced by this source too.
44. **⚠️ Mixer spray-mode taxonomy, more detailed than this store's existing mixer content**:
    **aerator** (mixes air into the stream, reduces water use); **jet/stream** (denser,
    higher-pressure flow for scrubbing heavily soiled items); **spray** (rinsing
    plates/produce); **mist/fog** (for delicate produce like berries, avoids bruising);
    **pulse** (alternating drip/jet, a super-premium-tier feature). A flexible pull-out
    spout is called "the one correct type" for a kitchen sink mixer. A built-in
    drinking-water outlet on the main mixer itself (not a separate dedicated tap) is called
    the ideal configuration.
45. **⚠️ Food-waste disposer price, converted**: **≈30,000-40,000 RUB (≈$370-490)** —
    the source's own stated price range and explicit personal recommendation to always
    install one if the budget allows.
46. **Sink dual-basin/1.5-basin configuration option** — noted as a real configuration
    choice (one larger + one smaller basin) alongside the standard single-basin sink.
