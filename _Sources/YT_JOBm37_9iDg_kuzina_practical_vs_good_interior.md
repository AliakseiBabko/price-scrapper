## Materials — ⚠️ A. Practical means easy to clean

### Fingerprints, and a worked product case

**Exclude super-glossy finishes if you want a super-practical interior.** Her case is unusually concrete and inverts the obvious assumption:

- A client bought **Geberit Sigma** flush plates. **Some carry a special anti-fingerprint coating** — one was **black**, where fingerprints should show most, **but the material means you press it and nothing remains.**
- **⚠️ Another plate of the same Geberit Sigma model went to the guest WC but was light/silvery. She expected fewer marks. It showed far more — one press and it needs wiping with a cloth.**

> **⚠️ The transferable finding: the coating decides fingerprint visibility, not the colour.** A dark surface with the right treatment beats a light one without it — which is the opposite of what most people assume when specifying.

### Joints, profiles and corners

- **Minimum number of joints.** **Large-format tile cleans better than small format — especially a kitchen backsplash. The smaller the format the more joints, the harder to clean.**
- **⚠️ No profiles or projections that collect dust** — especially **panelling («филёнки») on door leaves or on furniture doors.** Also **wide, relieved skirtings: a profiled skirting means wiping it constantly, "удовольствие не из больших."**
- **⚠️ Internal corners should not be at 90°.** Her worked example is a sink: **very contemporary models have a crisp contour meeting the walls at exactly 90°, and that corner is very hard to wash. A smoother transition is materially more practical.** A rule that generalises to any wet-zone internal corner.

### Built-in furniture, and horizontal surfaces

- **⚠️ Built-in furniture is practical: floor-to-ceiling cupboards leave no under-gap to reach into — "мышкам жить негде."** **And treat the top the same way: either drop the ceiling over the cupboard, or take the cupboard up with only the minimum gap needed to install it.** Then it is nearly sealed and dust collects neither above nor below.
- **⚠️ Few horizontal surfaces — and the ones people forget are overhead.** A plafond or shade **open only at the top (uplighting) is excellent at collecting dead flies**; beautiful for raising the visual ceiling, not practical.
- **⚠️ Cove/cornice backlighting: you build a niche for the LED strip, "там никто никогда не убирается — это самое лучшее место для тараканов." But it is beautiful.** She is candid that she has specified it and clients are happy — *"но с точки зрения практичности как-то не очень."*
  - `[restatement of position, new mechanism]` — **this is her SECOND distinct objection to cove lighting, and it is not the one already in the vault.** Her recorded objection is that a hollow cornice needs ≥5 cm and therefore lights the very junction it should hide; Kruglov's is dust and insects becoming visible *through* a stretch membrane. **This one is simply that the channel is unreachable and nobody ever cleans it.** Three mechanisms, three sources, one detail.

### ⚠️ If you cannot avoid cleaning, clean less often

- **Choose colours closest to the colour of dirt.**
- **Matte surfaces need wiping less often than glossy ones.**
- **⚠️ The more small-scale ornament a surface carries, the less visible dirt is.** For corridor tile: **choose a dust colour, or a tile with a bright small ornament — visually it will read less dirty.**
- **⚠️ An honest observation about the gap between portfolio and life: almost all her clients want open shelves in the project, because they decorate beautifully and photograph well — "но никто не хочет" live with them. Everyone wants closed shelves behind doors so the dust needn't be wiped.**
- **Textiles**: dirt-repellent impregnated fabrics, mostly wanted for sofas, plus anti-claw fabrics. **⚠️ Curtains with dust-repellent impregnation also exist — but it does not last: after dry cleaning the impregnation is gone. At least the first year you needn't clean them.**

## Materials — ⚠️ B. Practical means easy to renew

### The worktop comparison, which is genuinely balanced

| | **Acrylic (Corian-type)** | **Quartz** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Restoration** | **⚠️ Easily restored — can be re-polished. Chips are the colour of the worktop through, so they can be filled** | **⚠️ Any chip is visible and CANNOT be restored — only full replacement** |
| **Hot dishes** | **Some acrylics mark from hot cookware** | **Does not** |

**Her verdict is deliberately not a verdict: "что из этого практичнее, я не знаю — но реставрировать можно акриловые камни."**

### Replaceability of parts

- **⚠️ Parquet joint types decide replaceability: tongue-and-groove, a click lock, or a plain square edge. A board with a plain square edge is easier to lift out and swap if you ever need to replace one.**
- **⚠️ Track lighting is the easy case: bored of the warm light and want cooler or brighter, or bored of the fitting's form — pull the fitting out of the track and put another in.** *(Corroborates the removability advantage already recorded on the lighting page from Реньжин, from the renewal angle rather than the moving-house angle.)* `[partial restatement]`

## Labor / Delivery Model — ⚠️ C. Practical means convenient to use

### The finished-floor-level error, which is the most consequential item here

- **Stairs**: she learned in practice that a poured or turning staircase is a difficult job. **If the builders are not experienced or not expensive, the staircase may need re-pouring several times — because the FIRST and LAST tread must come out at the right height, which requires correctly calculating the finished floor level on each storey.**
- **⚠️ And the general case, which applies to a flat with no stairs at all: a miscalculated finished-floor height makes an interior inconvenient to use.** Her worked example is **WC height** — a pan set so high the user's feet do not comfortably reach the floor. **"Ошибка там в 3–4 сантиметра реально делает интерьер адски неудобным."**
- **⚠️ And the diagnosis is the important half: it is not that the builders did not know the correct WC height above the floor. It is that the finished-floor height was calculated wrongly.** **A rough-stage arithmetic error that only becomes visible, and unfixable, at fit-out.**

### Individual ergonomic constraints

- **⚠️ On her most recent project they chose a bed specifically because the client had a history of knocking their feet on the previous one** — its legs were not recessed far enough from the outer perimeter, so bare feet catch them. **They chose a bed with the base recessed as deeply as possible.** **A reminder that some practicality constraints are personal history, not general rules.**

### ⚠️ "Интерьер, в котором всё работает" — and the acceptance point

Her worked example is a restaurant washroom (Tanuki) she admired in detail: **plain small black tile, all the decor on the doors, frosted-glass inserts printed with samurai from films, and the glass appearing backlit though it was not — purely from correctly distributed ceiling light.**

**Beautiful right up until she could not get the latch into its keeper, and changed cubicle.**

> **⚠️ Her point is about acceptance, and it belongs in this vault's snagging material: at the end of a renovation, do you accept work like that or not — and when something breaks, do you fix it or pretend everything is fine?** The design was excellent and the room was unusable.

### Things that make an interior convenient

- **Closers («доводчики») on doors and furniture doors** — the leaf is opened or closed softly for you.
- **⚠️ Functional kitchen internals let you fit far more into a kitchen of the same size — "если вы оплачиваете вот эту крутую Blum начинку, это действительно очень практично, но это может стоить в ДВА РАЗА дороже, чем та же кухня с более простой начинкой."** **A 2× multiplier on the same kitchen for premium internal fittings.** `single-account`, `unverified`, 2020.
- **⚠️ Charging points everywhere** — at the table, in the bedroom; **and a frequent client request is a socket beside the WC for a charger.**
- **⚠️ Smart functions without a full smart home**: intercoms that pass the signal to your phone; **smart leak sensors that alert your phone while you are out — "гораздо безопаснее, чем не иметь датчик от протечек или чем иметь обычный датчик."** `[connects to — and strengthens — the leak-sensor timing argument from `UnCjxyDtWG0`, processed in this same round: notification matters because the shutoff is not instantaneous.]`
- **⚠️ Dimmers — and she dissents, which is worth recording because this vault's lighting page recommends them.** *"Я лично думаю, что диммером скорее неудобно пользоваться"* — you must turn or hold it every time, **"стоишь как дурак и три секунды ждёшь, когда люстра дойдёт до нужного уровня освещения."** **Her qualification: dimmers exist with a separate dimmer control and a separate switch key, which is easier to live with.** And full control may genuinely suit someone — dimmed overhead light while watching TV is not a bad thing.

## Materials — ⚠️ D. Practical means damage does not show

- **⚠️ Materials coloured through the body («крашеные в массе»).** **Porcelain: some have a dark surface layer over a light body, so every chip shows a white shard. Others are the same colour throughout and a chip does not read. This matters most for dark colours.**
- **Parquet**: a natural-colour parquet means scratches are the colour of the wood. **Her own worked contrast: she recently laid natural-oak parquet under clear lacquer, where a scratch is exactly the parquet's own colour — while at home she has WHITE parquet where every scratch shows, "потому что дерево само по себе не белое, белый только верхний слой."**
- **⚠️ Sanitaryware: when choosing non-white sanitaryware, establish whether it is coloured through or only on the surface.** With a black WC **the coating may start to come away if quality is poor, or a chip will leave a white mark on a black pan.** She notes she has never actually had chips on a WC in practice.
- **⚠️ Harder material ages less.** **Chipboard against MDF: paint chipboard and within a year it is obvious what it was; paint MDF and it can pass for solid** — though it depends on paint quality, and MDF quality varies too. **Timber hardness varies similarly, and she orders them roughly: birch is not hard; beech is soft, which is why it is used for carving; oak is medium; palisander is the hardest — "отсюда цена, естественно."** **The denser the material, the fewer scratches it takes.**
- **⚠️ Non-natural materials age less.** **Synthetics crease and age far less than cotton or silk.** **Veneered fronts behave more capriciously than painted ones, especially in a kitchen — veneer can hold grease stains that would wipe off a painted front easily.**
- **⚠️ But there is a third category: materials that age *beautifully*.** **Natural timber on a floor ages well, unlike laminate — even very expensive laminate.**
- **⚠️ And the most elegant point in the source: more practical still are materials that are imperfect to begin with.** Rustic timber already distressed; a floor that looks as though a saw went over it. **Styles that presume this imperfection — loft, to a degree Provence, and boho — get ageing for free, because the ageing is what the style is made of.**

## Design Concept — E. Practical means ordered

- **The more you have thought through, the better it will look.** Kitchen: **ugly sponges and dispensers on view — the dispenser can go under the worktop, sponges hidden beside the sink; a kettle, coffee machine or other utensils that must live on the worktop can be hidden behind their own door.**
- **Hallway: in small flats there is often not enough shoe storage and shoes end up over the floor.** One option is stacking them on rails — **the shelf is open so it needs wiping, so it is not practical, but at least it is ordered.**

## Rules / Heuristics — ⚠️ F. Where it is genuinely unclear which choice is more practical

**A short section, and an honest one — she poses these as open questions rather than answering them:**

- **Free space versus an extra cupboard.** Lots of free space is ergonomic and you do not bang into corners; an extra cupboard means more order and fewer scattered things. **Which is more practical?**
- **⚠️ Glossy tile is easier to wash, but matte shows dirt less.** Which is more practical?
- **Multifunctional furniture** — pull-out sofas, sofa-beds — **gives the interior more space, so practical in that sense; but sleeping every day on a sofa-bed is "адски неудобно."**
- **⚠️ Furniture without legs collects no dust beneath it. Furniture on high legs lets a robot vacuum get under and clean.** **"Это ещё зависит от того, какой образ жизни у вас, какие у вас привычки."**

## Rules / Heuristics — ⚠️ G. The thesis, and where pure practicality leads

> **"Практичный интерьер — не синоним хорошего интерьера."**

| **Practical** | **Good** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Laminate** — unaffected by temperature or humidity change; **better still without a bevel, so nothing gets into the groove and it reads like linoleum** | **Parquet** — but oiled parquet that ages beautifully needs its oil renewed periodically |
| **Eco-leather or leather on a sofa** — **⚠️ though a couple of her clients chose leather for practicality and reported sliding off it and finding it cold** | **Fabric that is pleasant to sit on** — velvet, better with impregnation; a light plain fabric is beautiful and not practical |
| **A chair with maximum timber and minimum upholstery** — wipe it and move on; a child spilling juice does no harm | **Soft upholstery** — comfortable to sit on |
| **No rug at all** — many clients refuse one as a dust collector | **Rugs**, currently fashionable and beautiful — **and high pile is "адски непрактично"** `[partial restatement — `NenrhBgNxGI` in this round gives the rug sizing rules; this is the maintenance side]` |
| **Bare windows** — nothing to take down and dry-clean; fine with a good view and no need for blackout | **Curtains** |
| **Light wall paint** | **Bright or dark walls** — **⚠️ a scratch reveals the white base under the paint, so bright and dark walls are impractical** |
| **Stretch ceilings** — **if the neighbours flood you it does not come down on your head, and you can wipe it** | **⚠️ But it is not beautiful, especially where it reads as film, and such ceilings "не дышат"** |
| — | **⚠️ Wallpaper in a bathroom: "я не знаю, как дизайнеры делают обои в санузле — это просто адски непрактично."** Her read: the wallpaper goes up, a beautiful photograph is taken, and afterwards the room is best not used — kept to open the door and show guests |

**⚠️ Her summary of the endpoint, which is the most quotable passage from this channel:**

> **"Обычно практично всё то, что выглядит скучно"** — beige on which dust does not show, smooth, without decor, without panelling, preferably without veneer. **So you end up, if money is short, with "интерьер в стиле Ашан" — everything practical and very easy to wash. Or, if the budget allows, "интерьер в стиле павильонов Мис ван дер Роэ" — bare space, no tables, a couple of armchairs uncomfortable to sit in but leather so they wipe clean, no curtains to change, no rugs.**

**⚠️ And her conclusion, which is a position rather than a shrug: "практичность — это важно, но возможно, какие-то компромиссы в пользу хорошего интерьера."** Not that it should be inconvenient — **but at least bright walls, bright accents, and a rug.**
