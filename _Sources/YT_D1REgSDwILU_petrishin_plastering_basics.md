## Walls / Ceilings — Substrate Prep and Priming ("грунтовка") by Material

1. **Substrate cleanup before plastering**: strip all wall surfaces
   ("зачистить до основания") down to base material — concrete
   ("бетон"), aerated concrete block ("газобетон"), foam/silicate block
   ("пеноблок"), or brick — most relevant to secondary/existing housing
   ("вторичное жильё") or white-box handover. Concrete specifically is
   ground with an angle grinder + diamond cup wheel to roughen the
   surface for better bond. All exterior-wall corner joints are opened,
   cleaned, foam-filled, and reinforcing mesh laid over them to prevent
   drafts and crack formation; the same treatment (open, clean,
   foam-fill, mesh) is applied at any seam where two different wall
   materials meet (e.g. concrete meeting brick) — corroborates the
   existing material-transition-joint mesh technique already in this
   page (Sidorik, ep.11) with an independent second source.
2. **Primer-by-substrate matrix, with the mechanism stated explicitly**:
   concrete gets a standard deep-penetration primer first, then
   betonokontakt ("бетоноконтакт") over it. Gas block ("газоблок") and
   foam/silicate block ("пеноблок") get a film-forming primer
   ("плёночная грунтовка") instead of standard deep-penetration primer —
   **explicit reason given**: these materials are highly porous
   ("как губка") and will wick moisture out of the plaster too fast for
   it to cure evenly if a standard absorbent primer is used, causing the
   plaster to dry unevenly and crack; the film primer seals the pores so
   the plaster dries slowly and evenly instead. Brick and drywall
   ("кирпич", "гипсокартон") get standard deep-penetration primer.
   This corroborates and sharpens the mechanism behind the existing
   absorbency-tier framework already in this page (Sidorik's primer FAQ)
   with a second source independently naming the same porous-substrate
   failure mode.
3. **Betonokontakt described as functioning like double-sided tape**
   ("эффект двухстороннего скотча") bonding plaster to concrete — three
   named failure conditions from the speaker's own direct experience:
   (a) applying betonokontakt onto un-primed, dusty bare concrete risks
   it peeling off in a sheet later, since residual dust prevents real
   adhesion to the substrate; (b) plastering over betonokontakt before it
   has fully dried risks later plaster detachment; (c) even correctly
   primed, dried betonokontakt that then sits exposed to construction
   dust (e.g. from wall-chasing or screed work happening later in the
   same room) before plaster goes on top can fail the same way — dust
   settling on the cured betonokontakt undermines the bond once
   plastered over. **Practical rule distilled**: prime before
   betonokontakt, let betonokontakt fully dry, and keep it protected from
   dust until plaster is applied.
4. **Deformation seam for building settling, distinct from the
   screed-perimeter expansion joint already in this page**: any
   multi-unit building continues to settle under its own weight as
   residents renovate (pour screeds, move in furniture, etc.), putting
   stress on wall surfaces; a special damping tape/strip
   ("демферный шов") is laid along the top edge of the plaster layer
   to absorb this settling pressure and prevent the plaster itself from
   cracking. Framed as a step that's easy to skip and easy to regret.

## Walls / Ceilings — Plaster Type Selection and Drying

5. **Cement vs. gypsum plaster in wet zones — company's own current
   practice, corroborating the existing Kruglov/Ontario tradeoff already
   on this page**: the traditional rule was cement plaster in wet zones,
   gypsum in dry zones; the speaker states manufacturers now claim gypsum
   plaster can be used anywhere, including wet zones, provided it is
   waterproofed afterward — and states this is now standard practice on
   this company's own projects, including the object shown in this
   video, "holding up fine." Framed as the company's own current choice,
   not a universal recommendation independent of proper waterproofing.

## Walls / Ceilings — Acceptance/QC Checklist with Numeric Tolerances

6. **Verification checklist with concrete tolerance figures**: check wall
   parallelism first (measure room width at two different points along
   its length — should match); then hold a straightedge ("правило")
   against each wall vertically, horizontally, and diagonally (especially
   at corners) — **gap tolerance: no more than 2mm**, and no
   "see-saw"/rocking humps; **plumb/level tolerance: max 1mm of
   deviation per 1m of wall height/length** (explicit worked example: a
   5m wall may deviate up to 5mm, no more); door openings/portals are
   flagged as the most common place defects are found. Laser aimed into
   each corner should track the corner line exactly. Door jamb
   ("откос") thickness must match on both sides. Wall-to-ceiling and
   wall-to-floor junctions are checked visually for sharp, bump-free
   lines. Finally, a carpenter's square checks every corner in every
   room for true 90°.
