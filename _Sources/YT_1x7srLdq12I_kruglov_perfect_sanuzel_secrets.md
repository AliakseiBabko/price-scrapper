## Genuinely New Content

- **Moscow water-shutoff regulatory context (level 1 — city named directly)**: per Kruglov, Moscow law limits scheduled hot-water shutoffs to once per year, capped at two weeks, and this is described as strictly enforced/predictable (residents can know the shutoff date in advance and prepare). Offered as the reason a storage-tank water heater can be a fully adequate choice in Moscow specifically, versus a tankless unit being the only option for someone unwilling to tolerate even a planned, known-in-advance annual outage.
- **Storage-tank vs. tankless water heater — a temperature-ceiling nuance not previously recorded**: a storage-tank unit heats its full reservoir to a genuinely high temperature (preferred by someone who likes very hot showers); a tankless (проточный) unit heats water inline as it flows through, reaching a real but comparatively lower maximum temperature, regardless of flow rate — i.e. even at low flow, a tankless unit's ceiling temperature is lower than a tank unit's, not just "eventually runs cold" at high flow. This is additional to (not a restatement of) this store's existing tank-vs-tankless capacity/placement/standby-power comparison in [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Structure_and_Framing]].
- **Standalone clothes dryer explicitly recommended as a comfort upgrade, when budget/space allow** — framed by the source as directly comparable to a dishwasher: technically optional (line-drying/drying racks still work), but a genuine quality-of-life improvement once tried, especially versus drying racks placed on a cold balcony/loggia or scattered around the apartment.
- **Underfloor heating failure-mode specifics, genuinely new detail for this store**: per Kruglov, a reputable heating-mat brand (he names Термотех/Thermo) warranties the mat itself, and in his practical experience the mat rarely fails when installed and used correctly. **The actual common failure point is the temperature sensor or a cheap thermostat unit** — he singles out a roughly 800 RUB marketplace thermostat as an example of a unit that fails quickly ("they burn out immediately"). **A separate installation-error failure mode**: tile-setters can physically damage the heating mat's own insulation during tile-adhesive application, causing the heating element to burn out. **Sensor lifespan and repairability**: a correctly installed sensor from a quality thermostat can last 20-30 years; if it does fail, it's normally a simple swap with no need to open the tile — **except** when the original installer embedded the sensor's conduit incorrectly (e.g. concreted over with no accessible sleeve), in which case a sensorless thermostat (from Thermo and other manufacturers) is a real fallback — these units make control decisions from internal logic/ambient air temperature instead of a floor-embedded sensor, avoiding the need to ever access the embedded sensor at all.
- **A concrete heated-floor scheduling example, corroborating and adding a specific worked schedule to this store's existing scheduling guidance**: the source's own bathroom thermostat is programmed for two daily heating windows, 6:00-9:00 and 16:00-20:00, timed so the floor is already warm by the time it's used in the morning and evening, while the tile's own heat retention keeps it comfortable between active heating periods — framed as a meaningful electricity saving versus continuous operation, with the suggestion that narrowing the daily active window further (to 4-6 hours/day total) saves even more.
