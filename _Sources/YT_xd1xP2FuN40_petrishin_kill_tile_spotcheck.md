## Tile — Acceptance/QC & Technique (new items only)

1. **Bathtub-protection reminder**: if a bathtub is already installed
   before tiling starts, ask the tiler in advance to cover/protect it —
   a dropped hammer or tile fragment during tiling can chip the tub's
   enamel finish.
2. **Layout starting-point and centerline technique**: start tiling from
   the most visually prominent wall (the one facing you on entering the
   room) for the best visual payoff. Mark a vertical centerline dividing
   that wall in half so tile radiates symmetrically from a central
   seam; if this produces small edge scraps ("огрызки") on either side,
   instead mark the tile's own centerline and align it to the wall's
   centerline so both edges get equal, larger pieces. **General rule: no
   installed fragment should be smaller than half a full tile anywhere**
   — the tiler or the design project should determine layout direction
   with this in mind before work starts.
3. **Two distinct tile-detachment mechanisms, more specific than this
   page's existing "why tile falls off" content**: (a) using adhesive
   itself to level an uneven substrate leaves a wall that "wants" to
   return to its original curved shape over time, gradually pulling the
   tile away from the base even with otherwise-adequate adhesive
   contact; (b) applying tile adhesive directly onto bare **gypsum
   plaster** (instead of over a waterproofing membrane, in a wet zone)
   can fail because the adhesive's bond strength exceeds the gypsum
   layer's own internal cohesive strength — the gypsum layer itself
   tears away from the wall, taking the tile with it, rather than the
   adhesive-to-substrate bond failing.
4. **Internal shower-corner grout-washout mechanism, extending this
   page's existing silicone-at-internal-corners rule with a stated
   "why" specific to that corner**: the internal corner in a shower is
   often the tightest/thinnest grout joint in the room, purely because
   of how the cut tiles meet there — grout doesn't fill a joint that
   thin well, and it washes out over time under direct water exposure,
   which is the specific reason silicone (not grout) belongs there.
5. **SVP tile-leveling-clip system, mechanism and honest limitation**:
   these clips, set between tile joints, prevent lippage caused by
   adhesive settling/shrinkage overnight between tiles laid on different
   days (a tile set today can end up slightly proud of one set
   yesterday once its adhesive fully seats). **Not a panacea** — result
   still depends heavily on installer skill — but meaningfully reduces
   the fatigue and inconsistency of manually tapping every tile level by
   hand; a suction-cup handle tool is the easier alternative to hand-
   tapping for the same leveling task.
6. **Grout-finish QC check, with an inspection technique**: grout lines
   should not be concave/scalloped, and tile edges should not be visibly
   poking through the grout — inspect with a flashlight held at a
   raking angle across the surface.
7. **Fixture-penetration hole technique, with a named bad-practice
   shortcut**: cut pipe/valve penetrations through tile with a hole
   saw/core bit for a clean round hole. Some installers instead drill or
   angle-grind an irregular (even rectangular) opening and rely on the
   fixture's own decorative cover plate to hide the mess — a properly
   equipped, competent tiler always cuts a clean round hole instead.
   Silicone-seal the penetration before final placement to keep water
   from tracking under the tile at that point; pre-mark and pre-cut the
   hole position precisely so the tile doesn't need to be drilled
   through again once already set (drilling an already-adhered tile
   risks cracking it).
8. **Company internal QC-agreement practice**: a formal written quality
   checklist is reviewed and agreed with the tiler at the hiring/
   interview stage before work starts; if finished work doesn't meet it,
   the foreman brings the checklist back to the tiler and requires
   rework, citing the tiler's own prior agreement to that standard as
   the basis (not a unilateral after-the-fact demand).
9. **Acceptance-stage electrical check as part of tile acceptance**:
   check switches, outlets, and light fixtures behind/around the new
   tile for pinched or hidden wiring damage — a tiler may not notice a
   thin wire getting covered or compressed during installation, so this
   is worth checking specifically at tile handover, not assumed to be
   the electrician's problem alone.
10. **Floor-tile protection through the rest of the renovation**: keep
    finished floor tile covered with rags plus hardboard/fiberboard
    sheeting for the remainder of the project, removing it only for
    inspection and re-covering immediately after — protects the
    finished work from damage by later trades.
11. **Cost-risk-based labor-pricing rationale**: installation labor cost
    scales with the material's own cost/risk, not purely with labor
    time — a real named example: a single large-format porcelain slab
    can cost 40,000-50,000 RUB, so a tiler's mistake (a cracked slab)
    is a real material loss, meaning installing that tile can't
    reasonably cost the same per unit as installing 2,000 RUB/m² tile.
