## Partition-Wall Material Comparison — Composition, Sound Insulation, Pricing

1. **Material/thickness taxonomy shown**: drywall (ГКЛ) — two layers of
   construction paper around a hardened gypsum-paste core with fillers,
   12.5mm per sheet, **double layer recommended for a strong wall**;
   solid tongue-and-groove gypsum block (ПГП) — gypsum binder plus
   plasticizing/hydrophobic additives, tongue-and-groove edges for
   interlock, 80mm shown; foam/aerated-concrete block (газоблок) —
   cement + quartz sand + a gas-forming additive, 100mm shown; solid
   brick — fired clay (fired at 1000°C), 120mm shown. **⚠️ Explicit
   structural caution**: not every floor slab can support a full brick
   masonry partition — load-bearing capacity must be checked before
   choosing brick.
2. **Sound-insulation index (dB) comparison, a real measured/cited
   figure per material — genuinely new numeric data for this store**:
   double-layer drywall with sound-insulating infill between the two
   sheets averages **53 dB** insulation (worked example: an 80dB source
   behind the wall is heard at only 27dB through it); 100mm foam block
   averages **46 dB**; solid tongue-and-groove gypsum block averages
   **43 dB**; solid brick laid in a half-brick bond averages **47 dB**.
   **Conclusion stated directly: drywall is the best sound-absorber of
   the four, a genuinely counter-intuitive result** (lighter material
   outperforming heavier masonry on sound isolation) — the company
   states this is their own preferred material specifically for that
   reason.
3. **Real material-only unit pricing, gathered from an actual store and
   an actual building-materials market (not company list pricing)** —
   material only, excludes studs/fasteners/sound-insulation infill/
   labor:
   - Drywall 12.5mm: store 255 RUB, market 266 RUB, average **260.50
     RUB/sheet** (one sheet = 3m²) → **≈87 RUB/m² for one layer**,
     **≈174 RUB/m² for the recommended double layer**.
   - Solid tongue-and-groove gypsum block: store 171 RUB, online-store
     reference 177 RUB (not on display at the physical store), average
     **174 RUB/unit**, 3 units/m² → **≈522 RUB/m²**.
   - Foam/aerated-concrete block, 100mm: store 78 RUB, market 58 RUB,
     average **68 RUB/unit**, 5-6 units/m² (6 used for the total) →
     **≈408 RUB/m²**.
   - Solid brick: store 15 RUB, market 13 RUB, average **14 RUB/unit**,
     52 bricks/m² at half-brick-thickness laying → **≈728 RUB/m²**.
   - At this small per-m² scale (roughly $1-11/m² material-only, all
     under $15/m² material cost even at the priciest option), a USD
     conversion collapses into the same nearest-$10 rounding bucket for
     every material and would misleadingly erase the real relative
     difference the RUB figures show — the RUB figures above are the
     primary comparison; treat them as the real data point.
   - **Cheapest overall including all supporting materials** (per a
     downloads-only reference table the video points to but doesn't
     read aloud — fasteners, guide profiles, sound-insulation infill,
     etc.) was **solid tongue-and-groove gypsum block**. Material
     takeoff assumed finishing with thick, non-glossy fleece/vinyl
     wallpaper; thin/light wallpaper would need extra plastering/
     skim-coat cost not included in this comparison.
4. **Real phoned labor-only quotes** (excludes plastering/puttying/
   finishing): foam block came out **cheapest for labor** among the
   three quoted; brick half-brick-bond labor quotes ranged 500-600 RUB
   (unit unspecified in the ASR, likely per m²); tongue-and-groove
   quotes varied noticeably between contractors ("не все одинаковы").
5. **Tool-competency vetting heuristic**: if a "master" arrives to build
   partitions with only a plain hand hacksaw, question whether they're
   really a professional; a reciprocating saw ("сабельная пила") is a
   real professional tool (not cheap) and its presence is a positive
   competence signal. Foam/aerated-concrete block is typically cut with
   masonry chisels/bits or an angle grinder with a diamond blade (very
   dusty) rather than a hand saw.
6. **Informal comparative speed/durability test** (explicitly
   disclaimed as "not 100% objective, could be done in a lab" — treat
   as illustrative, not a controlled result): foam block was the
   fastest to cut in their test (~11 seconds/unit). A dropped-weight
   test from 2.4m onto mockup partitions found double-layer drywall
   (simulating the real double-layer + stud spacing at 60cm) surprisingly
   resilient — the presenter explicitly states surprise at drywall's
   result, having expected tongue-and-groove to perform better. **If
   reinforced with mortar bonding, brick-in-bond is stated as the
   strongest of the four overall.**
7. **⚠️ Drywall backing-block ("закладные") requirement**: if building
   drywall partitions, embed backing blocks at the design stage for any
   heavy furniture/kitchen-cabinet/sink mounting planned for that wall —
   skipping this creates major installation problems later. **A design
   project (with exact furniture dimensions/placement) is recommended
   specifically to make this coordination possible up front**, for both
   the homeowner and the installer.
8. **Company's own stated position and a forward-looking preference
   shift**: on most Petrishin-Stroi objects, foam/aerated-concrete block
   is the default choice, described as the "golden middle" across
   strength, sound insulation, and total cost (material + labor)
   criteria. **After this comparison, however, the company states it
   personally plans to start adopting double-layer drywall with a
   mineral-wool infill for its own future work**, specifically because
   of the sound-insulation result — an explicit, source-disclosed shift
   in the company's own default recommendation, not a universal claim.
