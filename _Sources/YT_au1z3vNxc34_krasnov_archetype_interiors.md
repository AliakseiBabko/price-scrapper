## Design Concept, Planning Rules

- **⚠️ Core reframing**: an archetype is not a decorating-style choice but an innate, fixed perceptual/behavioral pattern a person is born with — the interior either supports or conflicts with this pattern. A person can live in an objectively "correct," expensive, well-executed interior and still feel a persistent, hard-to-articulate irritation if it conflicts with their archetype.
- **⚠️ Four archetypes, each with specific planning-stage implications** (the core content of the video):
  1. **Structure/Control** — psyche seeks to minimize unpredictability/visual noise (each extra object is cognitive load). Design implications: clean geometry, minimized variability, HIDDEN and GENEROUSLY-SIZED storage calculated with margin at the planning stage — must happen at layout/rough-construction stage, not addable later without losing floor area or "breaking the logic" of the interior.
  2. **⚠️ Environment/Sensation** — the opposite: needs the space FILLED with sensory richness (tactility, light, depth, layered materials). A person of this archetype placed into an objectively minimal interior will experience it as EMPTY and will start "cluttering" it — not a mistake or bad taste, but an unconscious attempt to restore the sensory density their psyche requires. Requires a MINIMUM of 4-5 distinct lighting scenes (main, evening, accent, local, decorative) — separate circuit groups and scene-control wiring must be planned at the ELECTRICAL design stage.
  3. **⚠️ Status/Level** — reads a space through quality markers (material choice, proportion, joint precision, colour depth, material weight). Quotable reframing: *"уровень — это не равностоимость, это согласованность"* ("level is not about cost-equivalence, it's about consistency/coherence") — an expensive interior with inconsistent execution logic reads as weak, while a simpler but precisely-executed interior reads as higher-level. For this archetype, ANY imprecision (a visible joint gap, a misaligned detail) disproportionately breaks the intended effect.
  4. **Movement/Freedom** — needs the space to NOT lock them into a fixed script; poorly tolerates built-in, fixed, "sealed" compositions and will rearrange/break them over time. Design implications decided at the PLANNING stage: fewer load-bearing/permanent partitions, more transformable zones, flexible furniture — if not designed in from the start, the space remains more rigid than this person can tolerate, and cannot be fixed after the fact.
- **⚠️ Combination archetypes require balanced zones within a single project**: Structure+Environment = zones of deliberately DIFFERENT density within the same home; Status+Freedom = the solution moves toward transformable/mobile furniture executed to a high quality standard, rather than fixed high-end built-ins.
- **⚠️ Central process claim, Krasnov's own explicitly-stated recent methodology shift**: in roughly the past year, he has started treating "behavioral pattern"/archetype diagnosis as a foundational part of his own studio's client brief process, BEFORE any reference-image/style discussion — good design starts with "diagnosis," not references, explicitly compared to a doctor's diagnostic process.
- **⚠️ Stated practical consequence**: an archetype mismatch cannot be fixed cosmetically (new curtains, new paint colour, added decor) because the mismatch is baked into decisions made at the planning/electrical/storage-depth/partition stage — a "diagnose personality-type needs before finalizing layout/electrical/storage plans" principle, directly relevant to any renovation's own planning process.
