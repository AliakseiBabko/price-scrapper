## ⚠️⚠️ 1. NEW — sliding partitions, and the condition that makes them work

Her fourth technique, and the vault has partitions only as an installed case study, not as a decision.

**The two situations she says require real separation rather than visual marking:**

1. **Active cooking — so noise and process do not reach the living zone.**
2. **⚠️ Overnight guests needing an enclosed room.**

**⚠️⚠️ THE CONDITION, and this is the part that makes it a finding rather than a suggestion:**

> ***«Только при этом надо предусмотреть ещё отдельный вход на кухню — тогда раздвижная перегородка будет работать так, как надо.»***

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ A sliding partition across a kitchen-living is USELESS unless the kitchen has a second door.** Otherwise closing it either traps the cook inside or seals the kitchen off from the flat. **This is a plan-level requirement that must be settled at layout stage, long before anyone is choosing a partition** — and it is exactly the sort of dependency that is discovered too late. **Directly checkable against this project's own plans.**

**Her notes on current systems, as spec expectations:** top track only, **no floor threshold**; smooth running with soft-close; visually light rather than the bulky constructions of the past.

- ⚠️ **She also says partitions are sometimes *«просто необходимо для согласования»* — needed to get a layout APPROVED.** ⚠️⚠️ **NOT ROUTED and NOT generalised: she names no jurisdiction, and a permitting claim from an unidentified country is precisely what standing rule 4 exists to stop.** Recorded here only, as a prompt that the approval angle exists.

## ⚠️ 2. NEW — open shelving units as a see-through divider

Her fifth technique: a **стеллаж на просвет**, accessible from both sides.

- **Divides without blocking light or reducing the apparent volume** — *«вы по-прежнему видите помещение целиком, но зоны начинают считаться отдельно»*.
- **Doubles as display storage** — books, vases, objects.
- **⚠️ The failure mode she names: overloading it.** *«Важно не перегружать стеллаж, ибо он не превратился в визуальный шум… чтобы он не стал стеной, чтобы на просвет вы видели другое помещение.»*

> **→ ⚠️ The caveat is the finding: an open shelving divider that gets full stops being an open divider and becomes a wall you paid extra for.** **Its zoning function depends on how you live with it, not on how it is specified** — which distinguishes it from every other technique on the list. ⚠️ **She cites Milan exhibition practice, not a project of her own.**

## 3. Corroboration only — recorded, not re-routed

- **Furniture as the divider**: sofa back to the kitchen; the dining table defining the dining zone; island or peninsula as the boundary; consoles, chests and low cabinets as light dividers. **Already on the page.**
- **⚠️ Lighting**: her framing that light *«редко работает в одиночку… чаще оно усиливает уже существующее зонирование, как в театре»* is a better statement of a principle the vault already holds. Bright directional light over work areas; soft diffuse light in the living zone **that must not fight the TV screen**; accent light over the dining table to collect the composition. **Control of zones separately, designed in at drawing stage.**
- **Flooring**: moisture-resistant tile, керамогранит or кварцвинил in the cooking zone; **⚠️ natural parquet or engineered board called impractical there — *«такие материалы могут прийти в негодность достаточно быстро»*** if the kitchen is used often; warmer coverings in the living zone. **⚠️ Her aside that the living-zone covering can be one that does not require underfloor heating is a useful negative constraint** — but it is a passing remark with no reasoning attached.
- **Rugs** to gather the living or dining furniture into one composition; she notes rugs under dining tables are common *«за океаном»* and defends the practice.
