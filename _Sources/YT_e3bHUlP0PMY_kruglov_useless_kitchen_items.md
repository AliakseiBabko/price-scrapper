## New facts

1. **Avoid motorized/electric-drive facade mechanisms** (lift/pop-up powered fronts) —
   expensive to install, expensive to maintain, and need repair/replacement service calls
   when they fail; doesn't pay for itself functionally.
2. **Avoid push-to-open (touch-latch, "тип он"/push-to-open) facade mechanisms entirely**,
   cabinetry and freestanding furniture (dressers, wardrobes) alike — a well-known failure
   mode: leaning on the facade (e.g. reaching for a glass of water) triggers an unwanted
   spontaneous opening. Prefer ordinary external handles or a milled finger-pull (profile)
   groove instead.
3. **Avoid motion/proximity-sensor task lighting under cabinets** — unreliable and prone to
   false triggers; the source's own installed unit sits above a kettle and fires every time
   steam rises past the sensor. **Recommended alternative: an ordinary physical switch, or
   at most a named "smart mechanical switch" (Hype Pro brand) paired with a relay** — an
   explicitly flagged non-sponsored brand mention, more stable than a proximity sensor.
4. **Avoid a cutlery drawer nested behind a large lower facade** (i.e. reachable only by
   first pulling out the whole oversized bottom drawer/facade in front of it) — the outer
   drawer is typically loaded with heavy pans/lids, so retrieving a spoon means hauling out
   the entire heavy load first; these nested mechanisms also develop play/rattle
   ("люфт") over time. Distinct from, and adds a mechanism to, this store's existing
   nested-drawer caution already on `Kitchen_Furniture.md`.
5. **Avoid standard-size plastic/rubberized cutlery-and-dish divider inserts** — fixed
   standard dimensions rarely fit real dishware well; **prefer simple added shelving plus
   loose containers instead.**
6. **Avoid internal drawer lighting (a light that turns on when a drawer is opened)** — a
   pure added cost with no real benefit once adequate ambient/task lighting already covers
   the cabinet run (per this store's existing task-lighting content).
7. **⚠️ "Magic corner" (волшебный уголок) mechanisms are a false economy — sometimes priced
   as high as the rest of the furniture carcass combined for what is mechanically just a
   set of hinges and metal rods.** Simpler corner swing-out systems are cheaper but still
   develop play/backlash and wear out relatively quickly. **Recommendation: skip a
   dedicated corner storage mechanism entirely** and instead use that corner for a trash
   bin plus a reverse-osmosis water-filter system (and a food-waste grinder if it still
   fits) — a concrete corner-use alternative distinct from this store's existing
   "technical column" corner-avoidance technique on `Kitchen_Furniture.md` (that technique
   is for a *structurally* unusable corner; this is a cost/reliability argument against
   corner storage hardware even in a usable corner).
8. **Avoid retractable/pop-up backsplash-mounted outlets** (sliding out of upper or lower
   cabinets, or lifting like retro pop-up headlights) on the main countertop run — grease
   and grime jam the mechanism over time and it eventually fails, leaving that section of
   the kitchen without working outlets. **Use ordinary fixed backsplash outlets instead.**
   **Exception: a kitchen island** — with no backsplash to mount a fixed outlet on, a
   concealed/pop-up or magnetic side-mounted outlet is the accepted solution there, since
   no ordinary-fixed-outlet alternative exists for an island. (Corroborates/extends this
   store's existing island-embedded-outlet caution on `Kitchen_Furniture.md`, which already
   flags pop-up outlets on an island as failure-prone from food/liquid ingress — this source
   adds the *backsplash* context as the case to actively avoid, vs. the island as the one
   accepted exception.)
9. **⚠️ Carcass material recommendation: standard LDSP (ЛДСП) is sufficient — MDF carcass
   material is unnecessary premium spend.** **"Moisture-resistant" (влагостойкая) LDSP is
   flagged as a low-value upsell**: paying extra for the "moisture-resistant" label doesn't
   meaningfully extend the carcass's real service life. **What actually determines carcass
   longevity is the fabricating company's own workmanship** (accurate cutting, correct edge
   banding, ideally PUR-glue-bonded edges) using a quality base LDSP board (**Egger** named
   as an example of a quality LDSP brand) — not the board's own "moisture-resistant" or
   material-type label.
10. **Avoid black sinks and black mixers/faucets specifically for the Russian domestic-water
    context** — Russian tap water commonly carries mineral content (iron, etc.) that leaves
    visible mineral-deposit residue once the fixture air-dries; a black finish only looks
    clean immediately after hand-drying, and reads as visibly dirty the rest of the time.
    Recommend a different (non-black) sink/mixer color for this reason.
11. **Avoid hanging rail/rod (рейлинг) storage systems** mounted under upper cabinets or on
    the backsplash for the main kitchen — called visually cluttered/an "eyesore" regardless
    of how neatly items are hung. **Exception: a genuinely separate "working kitchen"**
    (a second, staff/cook-operated kitchen distinct from the main household kitchen, common
    in larger homes with domestic help) — there, a stainless-steel rail/rod system is a
    legitimate practical tool, since that space is a working tool rather than a showpiece.
12. **Facade color/finish avoid-list, three named categories, same underlying mechanism
    (visible dirt/fingerprints) regardless of material**: very dark facades, very light/white
    facades, and glossy facades of any color — all three read as dirty faster than a
    mid-tone matte alternative. (Extends, doesn't duplicate, this store's existing
    small-kitchen monochrome-facade formula on `Furniture_Facade_Materials.md`, which is
    about color/handle *harmony*, not this specific dirt-visibility mechanism.)
13. **Pull-out/extending cutting boards and pull-out countertop extensions — a
    never-actually-seen-installed feature per the source's own direct experience**,
    flagged as impractical for a normal kitchen; conceivably justifiable only in an
    extremely space-constrained "field kitchen"-style installation with no room for even a
    small standalone table.
14. **A built-in trash-bin cutout in the countertop itself (with a hinged lid, waste
    scraped directly through the counter surface) — flagged as a feature the source has
    never understood the appeal of; not recommended.**
