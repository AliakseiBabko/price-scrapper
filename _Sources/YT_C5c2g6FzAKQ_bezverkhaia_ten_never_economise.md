## The ten items

1. **⚠️ Cheap builders — and the argument is specifically about engineering, not aesthetics.** Three levels of consequence: they **do not know correct technology** (she describes the failure mode as someone who once watched a friend do painting work and repeats that); they **cannot perceive quality** — "они даже не понимают, что такое хорошо положить плитку, потому что они никогда не видели" perfect 45°-mitred corners and perfectly even joints, so their own standard becomes the definition of good; **and worst, they do not understand engineering** — plumbing, electrical, heating and low-voltage distribution. **⚠️ The safety consequence she names concretely: incorrect connections in a socket or a badly assembled consumer unit means breakers tripping constantly and, at worst, a short circuit.**
2. **⚠️ Basic soundproofing — and her method is a room-adjacency audit, which is the reusable part.** **Establish which of your rooms and zones adjoin which of the neighbours' rooms and zones**, then treat only the pairs that matter. **Her worked contrast: if your kitchen adjoins their kitchen, soundproofing is pointless — "вы же в кухне не спите." If your bedroom adjoins their bedroom, treat that wall.** **⚠️ Also treat the bedroom ceiling** — her case is a children's bedroom directly above, with children awake and throwing toys at 4 a.m. **She states Russian building norms now require floor soundproofing.** **⚠️ And a myth debunked: gluing cork to a wall does not make it quieter — only a properly designed assembly built to norms works.**
3. **⚠️ Heating — the new-build default and its fix.** **Russian new-builds are by default plumbed with radiators connected in series (последовательная система), which means reducing one radiator's output with a thermostat reduces every other radiator too.** **The fix: convert to a radial/manifold connection (лучевое подключение) where each radiator runs separately from a manifold cabinet and can be regulated independently.** **⚠️ For an older building on a riser (стояк) system: fit an "американка" union valve at each radiator** so it can be isolated at will — **and she frames this as a safety measure too: if the radiator leaks, close the unions, remove and replace it.** **Corroborates existing vault content on radial heating distribution and isolation valves.**
4. **⚠️ Rough/hidden materials, because they are the basis of everything above them.** Named failure modes: **uncertified dry mixes, plaster in particular — the plaster begins separating from the wall, voids form, and cracks follow the voids**; **non-GOST cable — no guarantee the wiring lasts**; likewise the components of heating, water supply and all other engineering.
5. **⚠️ Finish materials that are hard to change later.** Her stated case — a client economised on tile, then disliked it because it was poor, and the whole floor had to be relaid. **The rule is the priority principle above, applied: tile and board are the wrong place to save; furniture, lighting and decor are the right place.**
6. **⚠️ Windows — framed as heat *and* quiet, with the detail in the reveals.** Street noise comes through the window, so buy quality glazing units — **but also: the window must be properly foamed with no voids, and the reveals themselves should be made soundproofed (шумоизоляционные откосы)**, which is what actually makes a bedroom quiet.
7. **⚠️ Entrance door — security and, less obviously, quiet.** Security: steel, several levels of protection, **locking pins on all three sides rather than one**, quality locks. **⚠️ The acoustic half is the additive part: stairwell noise enters through the entrance door** — neighbours' doors banging, dogs, children, rubbish going down the chute, the lift clanking — and a cheap lightweight door provides no isolation. **Soundproofed reveals here too.**
8. **⚠️ Rough plumbing — everything hidden from view**: pipes, valves, manifold cabinets, connectors. **The failure consequence she states is the escalating one: your neighbours below, and their neighbours below, swimming in water from your manifold cabinet.** Use only certified materials; REHAU named as an example.
9. **⚠️ Finish plumbing — but only the parts with a concealed element, and she draws the line precisely.** A basin mixer is easy to replace, so it is not the priority. **A wall-mounted mixer that appears to "grow out of the wall" has a substantial concealed body behind it — that is where not to economise.** **⚠️ Her strongest case is the wall-hung toilet: the installation frame and concealed cistern are buried in the wall, so if the cistern leaks the wall must be broken open to reach it.** Buy quality concealed components only.
10. **⚠️ The kitchen — and the reason is that a wrong one cannot be salvaged.** "Купить кухонный гарнитур, потом собрать, выкинуть на помойку и заплатить ещё раз — это не экономия." **Her instruction is to study your own cooking habits before ordering — how much you cook, what, and how** — and she lists appliance options that follow from specific habits: **a steam-combi oven where there are children; a built-in vacuum sealer if you do a lot of batch preparation; a built-in wok/asian panel; a built-in grill for steaks.**
