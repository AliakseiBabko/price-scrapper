## Mistakes / Warnings — Living Room

- **⚠️ Minimum walkway width in main traffic zones: 75–90 cm.** Stated directly as the figure to keep furniture arrangement from reading cramped and to allow free circulation between pieces.
- **⚠️ Conversational seating distance should not exceed 2–3 m between seats** for comfortable conversation without raised voices or awkward turning/leaning to maintain eye contact.
- **Combine varied furniture types (sofa + armchairs + poufs) rather than one rigid matching set** — creates a flexible space adaptable across different use-cases (quiet family evening vs. hosting guests).
- **⚠️ Coffee/side tables must be reachable without leaning or effort** — size/height should be proportional to the seating height; compact, mobile models are named as a practical option that can be repositioned per guest count.
- **Declutter filter**: identify items that have lost function/relevance but are kept anyway (old magazines, decor that's lost its appeal, furniture serving no function); ask directly whether an item is still used or brings joy — a recognizable KonMari-adjacent framing, attributed here as his own stated heuristic, not implied original.
- **Avoid impulse furniture/decor purchases disconnected from an overall plan** — buying an appealing piece in-store that then clashes at home. **Fix: define palette, materials, and textures FIRST** (evaluate what's already owned, pick a wood tone/metal/fabric direction), then select new pieces to match.
- **⚠️ Symmetry is not mandatory.** Strict mirror-image furniture placement ("what's on the left is on the right") reads as predictable/impersonal, especially outside classical/formal styles. **Asymmetric pairing** (one sofa + a pair of differently-styled armchairs/an ottoman rather than a matched sofa pair) reads as more dynamic and individual, provided pieces stay coherent via shared color palette/material rather than matched form.
- **Avoid literal/on-the-nose theme decor** (his example: an all-nautical room with anchors, all-blue palette, beach signage everywhere) — reads overloaded and unsophisticated. **Fix: evoke a theme through palette/texture** (sea-inspired blues/turquoise/sandy tones, natural wood/linen/cotton) rather than literal props; small thematic accents (a couple of cushions, one accessory) are fine in moderation.
- **⚠️ Vary tone/saturation of a single chosen color across different elements, rather than one flat repeated shade** — a single static hue used everywhere reads flat/monotonous; play with different tones/saturations of the same base color across cushions, throws, rugs, and art for a "deeper, more professional" result.
- **Balance warm natural materials (wood, woven natural-fiber baskets, linen/cotton textiles, plants/fresh flowers) against cold materials (metal, glass, mirrors)** — cold materials alone read "chic" but sterile; natural materials/plants soften the room, add perceived warmth, and (his aside) genuinely improve air quality. Brief mention here; fuller mechanism/evolutionary framing is on `ufKHek_TU30` — cross-reference, don't duplicate.
- **⚠️ Rug sizing rule: the rug should be large enough that all seating-zone furniture sits on it, or at minimum the FRONT legs of every piece rest on the rug.** A too-small rug makes furniture look scattered/disconnected; his framing is explicitly perceptual ("the brain reads this as harmonious and complete"). **This exact rule recurs in `vqDhBjDJAgA` below — same rule, first introduced here, cross-reference from there rather than duplicating.**
- **⚠️ Floor-length curtains ("floor to ceiling") vs. short curtains**: short curtains visually cut the room, making walls read lower than they are; floor-to-ceiling curtains create one continuous vertical line, reinforcing height/wholeness. Where short curtains are chosen for a practical reason (pets, small children), **prefer roller/roman/venetian blinds instead** — stylish and don't obstruct movement. **Heavy/quality fabric drapes better and holds a fold**; light fabric drapes poorly and won't fall cleanly to the floor.
- **⚠️ Artwork hanging height: center of the piece at roughly eye level, ≈150 cm from the floor.** Hanging too high is named as the most common art-placement mistake.
- **Artwork scale should match wall scale** — small art on a large wall reads lost/disproportionate; use large pieces, or a balanced multi-piece composition (itself following symmetry/balance principles) on big walls.
- **Match art style to room style**: abstract/minimalist work for modern rooms; traditional framed pieces with recognizable subjects for classical rooms.
