## Source Metadata

A real, single-project on-site case (a completed project revisited ~1
year later) — a load-bearing wall on one side of a doorway and a hot/cold
riser utility box on the other left only ~80cm clearance where ≥90cm was
needed for a 60cm door (70cm rough opening + 2×10cm door-casing/baseboard
returns). No pricing, no client interview, minimal self-reference.

## Plumbing

- **Standard rough-opening/return-clearance math restated with a
  concrete real-project worked example**: a 60cm door needs a 70cm rough
  opening; add 10cm returns on each side (needed for a correct door-
  casing-to-baseboard junction and to keep a relief-textured wallpaper's
  visible edge from landing awkwardly at the corner) → **minimum 90cm
  total clearance needed between two fixed obstructions** (here: a
  load-bearing wall and a plumbing utility box) to fit a 60cm door
  correctly on both sides — only 80cm was available pre-plaster.
- **Two concrete techniques used together to recover the missing ~10cm,
  summarized by the source as two general-purpose rules**:
  1. **"Never hesitate to slightly recess pipes into a wall"** — the
     riser pipe was recessed into a rebuilt utility-box wall by ~5cm,
     directly recovering clearance.
  2. **"Never hesitate to relocate shutoff-valve access panels
     (экраны) if needed"** — the original design had visible shutoff-
     valve access screens/panels on the risers, positioned such that
     opening them near the door corner would be physically awkward
     (blocked by the door swing) and visually poor when closed. The
     valves were relocated onto a straight run of pipe with the access
     panel removed from that awkward corner position entirely, freeing
     both the physical clearance the panel's own housing consumed and
     the swing-clearance problem.
