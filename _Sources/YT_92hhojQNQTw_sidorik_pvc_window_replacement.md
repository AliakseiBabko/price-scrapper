## Rooms / Zones: Flooring, Walls / Ceilings — Pre-Demolition Prep for Window Replacement

- **Slope ("откос") demolition technique, done *before* window
  installation day specifically to avoid double handling later**: cut the
  perimeter first with a wall chaser (штроборез), then carefully knock
  off the plaster layer with a hammer drill (perforator) — done in that
  order to control cracking/spalling, though even this careful sequence
  still left a few small delaminated patches.
- **Developer ("застройщик") plaster reliability warning**: it can look
  solidly bonded but delaminate under any real physical force — the
  speaker explicitly regrets keeping developer plaster rather than fully
  demolishing and re-plastering, because further putty work downstream
  risks the plaster lifting off unexpectedly.
- **Large window/sliding-door units must be demolished by at least two
  people.**

## Rooms / Zones — Opening Resizing When Converting an Elevated Sill to Floor-Level

- **Technique for narrowing/lowering a window opening** (replacing a
  window that previously sat on a raised block sill with a new
  floor-level unit): glue extruded polystyrene (XPS) foam board in two
  layers plus foam sealant to build up the sill; for rigidity at the
  frame's tie-in point (needed here because the new unit is a sliding
  door), core-drill through the insulation to the structural lintel beam
  at the opening's center, cut a rigid strip through the middle of the
  second XPS layer at the same width as the frame, then coat the whole
  assembly with gypsum plaster. Result: insulation preserved on both
  sides of a rigid central tie-in strip — avoids a cold thermal bridge to
  an unheated balcony while still giving the sliding frame the rigidity
  it needs (a plain XPS-only fill would flex/vibrate under a sliding
  door's use).

## Mistakes / Warnings — Planning Failure: Oversized Window Logistics

- **A genuine developer-planning failure, worth flagging for anyone
  planning to enlarge/replace windows later**: the building's elevator
  and stairwell were not sized to accommodate the eventual passage of the
  largest replacement window/glazing units — the biggest frames fit
  through neither. **Practical lesson: measure elevator, stairwell, and
  balcony access dimensions *before* ordering oversized replacement
  windows**, since retrofitting large frames into an existing building
  envelope may require workarounds bordering on impossible.
- **Failed and successful large-frame logistics attempts, in order
  tried**: (1) squeezing the frame through the stairwell — required
  masking-taping the frame first, then pushing it through with
  millimeter clearance, risking scratches to both frame and stairwell
  walls; (2) hoisting via ropes through the shared balcony — worked for
  most frames but was heavier/harder than it looked; (3) a
  lift/cherry-picker truck — failed because a neighboring building
  blocked reach; (4) roof access for a rope-lift — denied by building
  management. **Final resolution for the one frame none of these methods
  could move**: order it built as a 2-piece (split) construction instead
  of one oversized single-piece frame — same final appearance, installed
  in two connected sections.

## Rules / Heuristics — Frame Fixing Method

- **Two frame-fixing methods, compared and ranked**: (1) anchor bolts
  driven directly through the frame — common but **not ideal**, because
  it perforates the frame itself; (2) mounting plates ("монтажные
  пластины") screwed to the wall with concrete screws (2 screws per
  plate) — **the more correct method**, avoids frame perforation
  entirely. This corroborates and is consistent with the existing
  "Fastening Method: Direct by Default, Distributed Plates for Soft
  Materials" section already on

## Mistakes / Warnings — Protective Film Removal Timing

- **A named, very common installation mistake**: leaving the exterior
  protective film on the frame during/after installation. If left in
  place and exposed to sun, the film bonds permanently to the profile and
  becomes extremely difficult to remove — requires grinding it off with
  a sander. **Rule: remove the protective film from the exterior face
  before setting the frame, every time.**

## Materials / Technique — Foam-Joint and Vapor-Barrier Sequencing

- **PSUL tape (compressed foam pre-compressed expanding tape)** applied
  to the frame's wall-contact face before setting the frame — protects
  the foam/mounting joint from weather while remaining vapor-permeable
  (lets interior moisture escape outward). **Must not protrude past the
  "quarter" (четверть, the masonry reveal)** — it should stay pressed
  flush against it, not bulge outward.
- **Foam-joint dampening step**: the joint is sprayed with water from a
  spray bottle immediately before applying foam sealant — water
  increases adhesion and speeds foam curing.
- **A genuinely reusable sequencing trick for downstream slope
  finishing, explicitly flagged as a common finisher headache**: don't
  apply the interior vapor-barrier tape onto fresh foam immediately —
  expanding foam commonly domes/bulges under a vapor tape applied too
  soon, forcing finishers to trim the bulge and the tape off together,
  leaving no vapor barrier at all in that spot. **Better technique**: let
  the foam expand and firm up slightly first, press the bulge down flush
  with the frame using a finger, and only then apply the vapor-barrier
  tape — leaves it intact and undisturbed through the slope-finishing
  stage. The speaker specifically instructed his installation crew to
  follow this sequencing.

## Rules / Heuristics — Leveling and Fit Checks

- **Leveling technique**: shims/leveling plates ("рихтовочные
  пластины") of varying thickness combined as needed under the frame,
  checked with a bubble level on both vertical and horizontal axes, with
  no deformation in the jambs.
- **A defect that's only visible once the windowsill goes in, worth
  catching at the leveling stage**: slope width must be equal on both
  sides of the frame — an asymmetric slope width becomes visibly obvious
  once the windowsill is installed.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Glass Handling

- **Large insulated glass units (double/triple glazing) must be
  installed using suction cups** — handling a large unit without them
  risks chipping/breaking the glass, which is also a real safety hazard
  given the size and weight involved.

## Planning Rules — Crew Sequencing

- Installers typically work in pairs to speed the process; the whole
  job must be completed quickly since an apartment cannot practically be
  left without functioning windows overnight or for an extended period.
