## Design Concept — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Three classic kitchen-living layout patterns, with an explicit
  designer preference among them**: (a) dining table centered in the
  room with a straight or L-shaped kitchen, sofa positioned separately;
  (b) a bar counter instead of a dining table, suited to young
  households without children who don't need full-family seating; (c) a
  large kitchen work surface with the dining table placed next to the
  sofa — **flagged by this designer as the least preferred of the three**,
  because it mixes the post-meal relaxation zone with the dining table's
  physical footprint (wanting to lie down on the sofa right after eating,
  with a table still in front of it).
- **Real applied zoning example (20m², near-entry U-shaped kitchen)**:
  kitchen placed along the entry-adjacent wall (U-shaped with an attached
  table); soft-seating group (sofa, coffee table, rug, armchair), dining
  zone, and cooking zone kept as separate functional areas within one
  open room. **Floor-material zoning matches the functional zoning**
  exactly — porcelain tile in the kitchen meets engineered wood in the
  living zone at a defined transition line — a real applied instance of
  the flooring-zoning principle already recorded from `YT_ncOI16-meB4`
  (Omikor) and `YT_TnYExlfWw9c` (Diskill).
- **Pull-out/extendable countertop-as-table technique**: a 2m quartz-
  agglomerate countertop that slides out to add seating capacity — 1.4m
  of fixed usable worktop expands to a full 2m plus an additional
  end-seat when extended, fitting 3 people. **Worktop width trade-off,
  concretely reasoned**: chosen at 80cm rather than an ideally-preferred
  90cm-100cm, specifically to preserve walkway clearance for opening the
  adjacent refrigerator door while a chair is pulled up to the counter —
  a genuinely checkable clearance-vs-comfort trade-off with real numbers
  on both sides.
- **Ceiling-height cabinetry ("кухня в потолок") is now a standard
  design move for a current-generation designed kitchen** — a continuous
  cornice/valance is run around the kitchen's full perimeter specifically
  to visually unify base cabinets (60cm depth) and upper cabinets (35cm
  depth) into one contour despite their differing depths.
- **Corner dead-zone resolved with a functional rear-facing appliance**,
  not a "magic corner" mechanism or hidden shelving (the two default
  corner-cabinet options this source explicitly calls out as the usual
  fallback): a second, rear-accessed freezer compartment was fitted
  behind the main refrigerator specifically to make the corner's second
  face genuinely usable rather than dead storage.
- **Quartz-agglomerate countertop material chosen specifically to solve
  a two-corner seam problem**: this room's kitchen has two internal
  corners requiring countertop seams; agglomerate's available panel size
  let the fabricator route both seams into visually inconspicuous
  locations — a material-selection reason tied directly to this room's
  specific corner geometry, not a generic material preference.
- **Kitchen "eye-catching" design formula, stated as a repeatable
  recipe**: an accent backsplash in large-format stone-look porcelain
  tile; facades combining walnut veneer with painted MDF matched to the
  wall color; cornices painted the same wall color to read as one
  composition with the facades; flooring combining engineered wood with
  several porcelain-tile variants unified by a shared color undertone
  across all the materials.
- **Appliance-stack placement note**: coffee machine mounted at
  approximately eye level for convenient pouring, with the oven placed
  beneath it; when a microwave replaces the coffee machine in this same
  stack, the oven typically sits slightly higher with the microwave above
  it instead — both configurations workable, framed as a matter of which
  appliance the household actually uses more.
