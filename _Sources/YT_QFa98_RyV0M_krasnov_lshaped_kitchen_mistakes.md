## Kitchen, Mistakes / Warnings

- **⚠️ Corner dishwasher placement — a two-layer failure mode**: a dishwasher tucked into the inside corner of an L-run can fail to open fully because its door strikes the adjacent perpendicular cabinet front; the fix ("умные люди") is a spacer/setback panel so the opening front clears the neighbouring run. **The deeper, more commonly missed problem**: even with that fix, the sink and dish-rack must ALSO be planned around the dishwasher's corner position so loading/unloading doesn't require standing in the corner — otherwise this is one of the worst possible dishwasher placements ("самое-самое неудачное").
- **⚠️ Conflicting cabinet-door swing radii at an L-junction**: adjacent fronts meeting at a corner can physically collide when opened together ("конфликт открывания фасадов") — must be resolved at the planning/furniture-design stage (draw door-swing radii on the plan), not discovered after installation, since living with it means re-cutting/redoing the finished cabinetry.
- **⚠️ Track/spot ceiling lighting vs. cabinet-door clearance — a specific design-coordination failure, explicitly generalized beyond kitchens**: a track/spot fixture protruding ~10cm from the ceiling can be struck by an opening upper-cabinet door if the lighting plan was overlaid on the furniture plan without cross-checking door-swing clearance — described as a real coordination gap (two plans drawn independently, without door-swing radii marked on the furniture layout). **He explicitly states the same failure mode recurs in entryways/hallways and anywhere there's a ceiling-mounted object near an opening door, not just kitchens** — record as a general design-coordination caution.
- **Corner sink placement critiqued as inherently awkward**: requires reaching further into the corner to use, and typically conflicts with the sink-stove-fridge work-triangle principle since a corner position rarely sits efficiently between the other two points ("там по-другому просто не выстроить это").
- **Dishes stored far from the dishwasher** forces walking back and forth across the kitchen between load point (sink) and unload point (dish rack/cabinet) — extends this vault's existing kitchen-ergonomics content to the L-shaped-kitchen case specifically.
- **⚠️ Freestanding oven-column horizontal placement — distinct from this vault's existing oven-mounting-HEIGHT content (a different axis: horizontal proximity to counter, not vertical height)**: an oven column placed far from the main counter run forces walking extra distance with a hot tray — framed as a real safety issue, not just convenience ("это просто небезопасно"). Correct placement: an oven column adjacent/flush to the main countertop run so a hot tray can be set down immediately on adjacent open counter. A coffee machine, by contrast, can sit further away since it doesn't carry the same hot-tray urgency.
- **Work triangle, applied concretely to the L-shaped-kitchen failure mode**: fridge → sink (wash) → prep → stove (cook), the real cooking sequence he describes; scattering the three points far apart across an L-run is both potentially unrealizable given plumbing/structural constraints and simply exhausting daily — restates/extends this vault's existing work-triangle content, tied specifically to the L-shape case.
- **A first-person disclosure**: Krasnov states he personally prefers the visual look of linear/straight-run kitchens («линейные варианты... более ровные, такие лаконичные») over L-shaped ones, yet chose an L-shaped kitchen for his OWN home specifically because it's more functional for his actual cooking habits — an explicit real-world example of prioritizing functional fit over pure aesthetic preference. He and his wife ("мы с супругой") both understood what they visually preferred vs. what they'd actually use.
- **Final framing**: none of these L-shaped-kitchen mistakes are about budget ("никак не связан с деньгами, вообще никак") — all are pure planning/ergonomics issues, independent of spend.
