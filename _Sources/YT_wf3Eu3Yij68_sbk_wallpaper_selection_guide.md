## Materials — Wallpaper Base/Coating Taxonomy and Selection Logic

- **Two base types are the primary classification, everything else is secondary**: non-woven ("флизелиновая основа") vs. paper-based ("бумажная основа") — this is the foundational distinction a buyer needs before considering coating/finish type. Coating/finish types layered on top include acrylic, vinyl, liquid wallpaper ("жидкие обои"), fiberglass wallpaper ("стеклообои"), natural-material wallpaper, paintable wallpaper, frescoes (custom individual sizing), and pictorial/mural wallpaper (also individually produced, fresco-like).
- **⚠️ Price-tier coherence rule, a named consistency requirement between wallpaper price, wall prep quality, and installer skill**: an economy-segment wallpaper (~300 RUB/roll, ≈$5 at this video's trailing-6-month rate) glued by an average-quality prep/installer produces an acceptable average result — but buying a premium roll (the video's own example: a Versace-branded roll at ~15,000 RUB, ≈$230) and installing it over the same average prep with the same installer skill level will **not** produce a premium result, because the substrate prep quality and installer skill are the actual limiting factors, not the material cost alone. Stated rule: premium wallpaper requires premium substrate prep **and** premium (well-paid, qualified) installers together — the three must scale together, not just the material choice.
- **Thinner/lighter-colored plain wallpaper needs near-perfect substrate prep**; textured/patterned vinyl wallpaper (thick vinyl top layer) can visually mask minor prep imperfections, but still requires genuinely competent prep underneath, not a shortcut.
- **⚠️ Old-wallpaper-over-old-wallpaper substrate risk, a named material-compatibility mechanism**: modern non-woven/acrylic-coated wallpaper does not stretch the way older paper wallpaper did — old paper wallpaper could visibly conform to minor wall unevenness during application, but a thin modern non-woven roll applied over walls only previously covered with old wallpaper (i.e., "the wall has always been fine, why redo it") will show visible seam/surface defects, because modern prep standards and modern wallpaper's non-stretch behavior don't tolerate the same substrate imperfections old paper wallpaper absorbed.
- **Named buyer-installer coordination failure mode**: agreeing with an installer on a specific wallpaper type/article number before prep begins matters because prep requirements differ by wallpaper type (e.g., agreeing on thick vinyl, then showing up with thin photo-print wallpaper) — a mismatch forces either refused installation or costly substrate rework, plus wasted material cost. Recommendation: communicate the exact wallpaper type/article, or provide a design project/technical brief, before prep work starts.
- **Self-installation viability by tier**: cheap paper wallpaper (~200 RUB/roll, ≈$3) remains genuinely DIY-installable the old-fashioned way (glue directly, similar to historic newspaper-backing technique). Mid-tier-and-above wallpaper (the video's example: a dense flizelin "brendovaya mina" roll at high per-roll cost) is explicitly recommended to be installed only by a professional installer, not the buyer — cited failure modes: wrong glue selection, incorrect glue dilution, glue squeezed out and smeared into visible seams — with a real named risk that a single roll of this tier (transcript states a per-roll price the speaker calls "quite expensive," recorded here as ASR-uncertain given no punctuation/likely digit-transcription noise in the source audio) can be ruined by an inexperienced installer, at real material-cost risk.

## Quantities / Measurements — Correct Wallpaper-Quantity Calculation Method

- **⚠️ Named buyer mistake: calculating wallpaper quantity from room floor area, not wall perimeter** — a standard roll contains 10 linear meters of usable length before trim loss, but the correct calculation is **perimeter of the walls × ceiling height**, not room floor area, because floor-area-based estimates systematically under-order. Worked example given: a 10m roll at a 2.5–3m ceiling height yields 3 usable strips per roll after trim allowance — store staff routinely recalculate a customer's own area-based or online-calculator-based figure by perimeter/height before finalizing an order.
- **⚠️ Two named measurement mistakes specific to private houses with staircases**: (1) stairwell/stair-flight wall area is frequently omitted or miscalculated in a quantity estimate; (2) a private house with a floor-to-floor height exceeding ~10m (i.e., taller than one full roll's usable length) cannot be papered from a single standard roll without a horizontal seam, which will almost always be visible — the stated workarounds are either choosing a wallpaper pattern that visually mimics/masks a decorative border at the seam line, or replacing wallpaper with an actual decorative treatment (e.g., molding/trim) at that height instead.

## Rules / Heuristics — Returns Policy, Three Distinct Categories

- **⚠️ Three-tier wallpaper return policy, a named planning constraint**: (1) standard in-stock rolls — 30-day return window, unpackaged-but-labeled/undamaged condition required, full refund at point of sale; (2) special-order (non-stock) positions — 7-day return window, a materially shorter window that buyers must plan around; (3) individually manufactured items (the video's example: a custom-sized fresco from a named manufacturer, pre-paid, produced to the buyer's own dimensions) — **not returnable at all**, since the product is made specifically for that buyer's space. **Stated practical planning rule**: don't buy wallpaper far in advance of the installation date — buy as close to the install date as practical, so there's still time to identify and return excess/wrong rolls within whichever window applies, rather than discovering the return window has lapsed.

## Materials — Real Price-Driver Mechanisms Within the Same Nominal Product Category

- **⚠️ Named, checkable price drivers for wallpaper within one coating category**, not just "brand markup": for non-woven plain/paintable ("гладкий малярный флизелин") wallpaper, *density* is the direct driver — a denser roll better hides minor substrate imperfections/micro-cracks and tolerates less-perfect prep, and costs more; for vinyl wallpaper, *width* (e.g., 70cm vs. 53cm roll), *tisnenie v registr* ("тиснение в регистр" — raised/embossed relief precisely registered to the printed pattern, each element individually contoured, vs. flat printing) and *added surface effects* (glitter, pearlescent finish) each independently add cost; a named-designer/brand license (the video's example: Versace-branded product) adds cost on top of all the above, independent of the physical material difference.
