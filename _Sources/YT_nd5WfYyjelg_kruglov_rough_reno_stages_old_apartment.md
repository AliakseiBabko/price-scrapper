## Planning Rules / Sequencing — Genuinely New Content

- **A concrete secondary-apartment debris-volume data point, a second
  metric alongside this vault's existing bags/trips figure**: a
  70-80 m² secondary-market apartment undergoing full ("капитальный")
  renovation requires roughly **8 separate 8m³ containers** for
  demolition debris — a different unit (containers, not bags/trips)
  than `Renovation_Sequence.md`'s existing "≈300 bags, 18-20 trips per
  100 m²" figure, so not directly comparable, but a useful second
  reference point for planning container logistics specifically.
- **⚠️ A named partition-wall construction technique not previously
  documented in this store**: this company's own preferred material
  (100mm aerated/gas-block) is built up **row by row with a mandated
  drying wait between each row** — a full partition cannot be built in
  one day — set on a CSP (ЦСП, cement-particle board) footing strip.
  **Before mortar work begins, the crew first dry-stacks a single test
  row along the entire planned partition layout** to visually confirm
  room proportions/furniture clearances match the design intent, only
  proceeding to real ("mokraya"/wet) construction after this check.
  **Metal embeds ("закладные") placed during this stage are primed
  against rust** before being sealed in.
- **⚠️ A genuinely new plastering-geometry technique, distinct from a
  blanket "everything must be 90°" rule**: strict 90° corners are
  reserved for rooms where it functionally matters for later cabinetry
  fit (walk-in closet, kitchen, bathroom) — elsewhere (e.g. a plain wall
  that will only carry curtains), forcing 90° is explicitly called
  counterproductive. **A specific window-reveal technique**: since a
  window unit is frequently installed slightly out of square relative to
  its rough opening, plastering should prioritize **equal-width window
  reveals on both sides** over strict wall perpendicularity at that
  specific wall — visually mismatched reveal widths are a more
  noticeable defect than a slightly non-square wall, and mismatched
  reveals also cause windowsill-fit problems.
- **A cost/labor-saving construction-sequencing note**: a wall segment
  that will be covered by frame-based soundproofing doesn't need
  plastering underneath at all — skip the plaster material and labor
  cost on that specific wall face entirely, since the frame system
  covers the raw surface.
- **⚠️ A sharper mechanistic detail behind this channel's existing
  electrical-after-plastering rule** (already recorded in
  `Renovation_Sequence.md`, restated here — not re-extracted as new on
  its own): in panel buildings specifically, chasing cable grooves after
  plastering means the chase cuts mostly through the new plaster layer
  and only minimally into the structural panel wall — important because
  panel-building walls contain load-bearing rebar that legally/
  practically cannot be cut, a specific reason (not just "the geometry
  is more accurate") this vault hadn't recorded before.
- **⚠️ A concrete pre-purchase timing checklist for concealed/built-in
  fixtures needed at the rough-plumbing stage**: a hydronic (water-
  heated) towel warmer, any concealed/built-in mixers, and a hygienic-
  shower valve must all be purchased and on-site **before** rough
  plumbing is routed, since the installer needs to embed their internal
  components at that stage — purchasing these only at the finish stage
  is too late. Radiators intended for the heating layout are needed at
  this same stage for the same reason.
- **⚠️ Door/window purchase-lead-time figures, previously unrecorded in
  this store**: an entry door typically has a **~1-month** production
  lead time (install planned for right after demolition or during rough
  works, wrapped/sealed to protect it through screed work); replacement
  windows typically need **~2 weeks** lead time (installed either right
  after demolition or after rough works are complete — either timing is
  fine, since windows tolerate being present during dirty work if
  protected).
- **⚠️ A stretch-ceiling installation-order nuance conditional on
  ceiling *type*, not previously documented in this store**: for a
  standard (non-shadow-gap) stretch ceiling, install the perimeter
  profile first, then finish/paint/wallpaper the walls, and only stretch
  the fabric membrane itself **last** — this order minimizes the risk of
  ceiling installers accidentally damaging already-finished walls
  (a stray hand-mark, a ladder scrape, a profile catching a corner).
  **This reversed order is explicitly not possible for a shadow-gap/
  floating-profile ceiling**, since painting/papering the wall cleanly
  around an already-installed shadow-gap profile isn't practical —
  shadow-gap ceilings must follow a different sequencing.
- **⚠️ Kitchen backsplash tile timing/cost mechanism, extending (not
  duplicating) this vault's existing large-format-tile-specific
  backsplash-sequencing note**: installing backsplash tile during the
  main tiling phase (using the kitchen design plan's own precise
  dimensions to tile the exact future-cabinetry zone) is cheaper and
  more precise than tiling after cabinets are installed — the
  after-cabinets approach requires transporting the client's own tile
  off-site for cutting (or an installer cutting on-site with an
  extension cord in a stairwell, awkward for both installer and
  neighbors) — a cost/precision mechanism applying to *any* backsplash
  tile, not just large-format slabs specifically as the existing note
  frames it.
- **A consolidated client-vs-contractor purchasing-responsibility
  breakdown across the whole sequence** (new as an organized checklist,
  even where individual items restate existing content): contractor
  typically sources rough materials, tile adhesive/putty/primer/
  fiberglass mesh, ceiling material, and finish flooring material
  (whichever the client selected); the client is responsible for
  sourcing the entry door, windows, all concealed/built-in plumbing
  fixtures (towel warmer, concealed mixers, hygienic shower, radiators),
  the tile itself, and finish-stage plumbing/electrical fixtures
  (outlets, faceplates, showerheads, faucets).
