## Genuinely New Content

- **Corner-angle consistency check, a new pre-hanging QC step**: measure the room's internal corner angle at both the top and bottom of the wall — it doesn't need to be exactly 90°, but the top and bottom angles must match each other. A mismatch (e.g. 90° at the top, 85° at the bottom) forces a wedge ("carrot") trim cut at the corner during hanging, which can visibly shift a patterned wallpaper's repeat right at the corner.
- **Substrate-prep tier keyed to wallpaper thickness, not a flat standard**: named prep classes K2/K3/K4 (progressively finer/smoother); some wallpaper needs only K2, while super-thin wallpaper needs full paint-grade prep — zero visible shadow/texture under raking side-light. **Sequencing rule**: decide the wallpaper first, then prep to match it — not the reverse.
- **Wet-test technique to determine a specific wallpaper batch's expansion behavior, independent of its label**: soak a sample and observe whether it expands in width, length, both, or neither. The installer's stated point: labels like "vinyl on fleece" or "fleece on fleece" don't reliably predict this — every batch should be tested, and the hanging technique adjusted to the result.
- **Primer's absorption-evening mechanism spelled out**: beyond substrate strengthening, primer regulates how evenly the wall absorbs adhesive; an unevenly primed wall has patches of higher absorption where glue dries faster than the rest of the same wall — this differential drying is given as a direct bubble/peeling cause, not just "poor prep" generically.
- **Primer application lifehack and cure time**: apply primer with side/raking light and curtains closed/windows covered, to visually confirm even coverage across the wall. Each primer coat needs a full 4-6 hours to dry before the next step.
- **Laser-level vertical line treated as mandatory professional equipment, with a named consequence for skipping it**: hanging the first (or second) sheet even slightly off-vertical compounds over a 5-7m wall run into a large drift by the far corner, forcing a wedge ("carrot") trim cut at that corner — flagged as a clear quality-shortfall signal if an installer doesn't carry a laser level.
- **Tool restriction for smoothing, explicitly banning older methods**: only a rubber roller and a plastic spatula are used to smooth hung wallpaper — no waffle-weave towels or rags (a common technique as recently as ~10 years ago, per the speaker).
- **Excess-glue removal direction rule**: any excess glue squeezed out during hanging should be pushed in the direction of hanging progress (into not-yet-papered wall), never toward or across a seam.
- **A second, distinct mechanism for why internal corners are always cut/butt-jointed rather than wrapped**: building settlement causes the two intersecting wall planes to move slightly independently over time, and a single sheet wrapped around the corner will visibly accordion-wrinkle as a result — a different failure mechanism from this store's existing "wrapping traps air bubbles" reasoning (Петришин-Строй, `VcrYHkDgb0o`), not a duplicate of it.
- **Glue-selection nuance and a named product**: retail labels ("glue for vinyl wallpaper," "glue for fleece wallpaper," etc.) often don't correspond to genuinely different formulas — correct glue choice and mixing consistency should be tuned to the specific wallpaper's own tested expansion behavior (see wet-test above), not assumed from the label. Recommended glue brand: **Син Морал (Sinmoral)**, used by this company's own top installers, with dry glue optionally blended in for specific wallpaper types.
- **Dry-glue mixing order rule**: pour the dry powder into cold water (not water into powder) to avoid clumping; mix, rest 3-5 minutes, then remix. Applying it: usually glue goes on the wall, but if a specific wallpaper swells when wetted, pre-moisten the wallpaper itself (lightly wet or a thin pre-applied glue layer) before hanging onto the already-glued wall — an exception clause to the general wall-vs-paper glue-placement rule this store already has from a different source (`ot7qPVf7XVU`).
- **Room climate and a bubble-repair technique**: optimal hanging/curing temperature 18-23°C, avoid drafts and any humidity source removal (open windows, dehumidifying equipment) during and after hanging — rapid humidity loss is cited as a bubble/peeling cause. **New repair technique for an existing bubble**: inject glue into the bubble with an ordinary medical syringe and needle, then smooth flat — a specific, previously undocumented fix for this store.
