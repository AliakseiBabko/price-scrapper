## Durable Facts

- **"Alternative layouts" planning concept**: a good design project should plan a family's electrical/room layout not just for present-day needs but for a **5, 10, and 15-year horizon**, because household composition and needs change substantially over that period (e.g., a couple with a toddler becoming a family with two children of different ages/handedness). Practical technique demonstrated: the designer should explicitly ask the client "how long do you plan to live in this apartment?" early in the process, since a short-hold ("flip in 3-5 years") vs. long-hold ("staying long-term") answer changes what's worth planning for. `unverified`, single-account business-development framing, but a reasonable planning heuristic.
- **Handedness consideration**: desk/workspace lighting and desk placement should account for a child's (or future child's) handedness — a right-handed person's desk light should fall from the left (so the writing hand doesn't cast a shadow over what's being written), and vice versa for left-handed; cited as a reason to plan two desks (one lit for each handedness) in a shared kids' room when handedness of a not-yet-born second child is unknown. `unverified`, single-account, but a concrete and independently-plausible ergonomic rule.
- **Hidden/reserve outlets behind wallpaper for future use**: outlets and a data (twisted-pair) drop can be run and terminated behind finished wallpaper (not cut through, left concealed) at a wall the client currently doesn't want a TV on, specifically so a future owner/occupant (potentially 15-20 years later) can cut the wallpaper and use them without any wall opening work — described as a deliberate "resale bonus" feature. This is a cheap variant of the general "reserve/future-use outlet" pattern already noted in the companion room-walkthrough source (`iBPJimtw7k0`).
- **Window-slope outlet for seasonal decorations + motion-sensor night light dual-use**: an outlet installed in a window-slope/reveal (откос), specifically for plugging in holiday string lights, doubling as a mount point for small motion-activated night lights for safe nighttime bathroom trips without needing a main light. Cross-references the general window-slope-outlet rationale covered independently and in more depth in the companion video `P7_rUkk8clU`.
- **Smart-lock-triggered scene automation as a "master switch" alternative**: instead of installing one large physical master switch/breaker at the entry to kill non-essential circuits (fridge excluded) when leaving, a smart door lock that recognizes who is entering/leaving can trigger the same effect via automation scenes (all sockets off, appliances paused, etc.) — described as functionally equivalent to a physical master switch but reconfigurable later without any additional wiring work, whereas changing what a physical master breaker covers would require re-wiring. `unverified`, single-account/client's own stated opinion (the speaker explicitly frames himself as personally skeptical of most smart-home systems' practical value, singling out only the entry-scenario and dimming/voice-lighting-control use cases as genuinely useful).
- **Voice-controlled dimming**: the hub demonstrated supports voice commands for percentage-based dimming of lighting circuits (e.g., "set to 25%") — noted as functional and reasonably accurate, offered as one of few smart-home features the speaker considers genuinely useful rather than novelty.
