## Design Concept — Two-Rule Color-Combination Method

- **⚠️ First rule, the "releasing" one ("освобождающее правило")**: there are no forbidden hue pairs — any color can be combined with any other ("можно сочетать любой цвет"). Explicitly rejects the article-genre premise that green "doesn't go with" red or blue with yellow. Hue selection should therefore be driven by personal preference or by intended psychological effect, **not** by a permitted-pairs lookup. Worked counter-example: a green-and-blue stairwell, a pair the presenter says most people assume clashes, shown working.
- **⚠️ The operative rule — match on lightness and/or saturation, not on hue**: two colors read as harmonious when they are **equivalent in lightness ("светлота"), in saturation ("насыщенность"), or in both**. This is the substantive mechanism the video is built on, and it is a different axis from the hue-wheel schemes the other theory sources in this batch use.
  - **Lightness** is defined operationally as what changes when white or black is added to a pigment (red → pink → white; red → maroon → black).
  - **Saturation** is defined as what changes when gray is added (red → progressively duller → gray).
- **Worked example on a paint fan deck ("цветовой веер")**: a soft peach and a soft lavender taken from *the same row* of the deck are equal in lightness and saturation and pair well; the same peach against a dark saturated blue-violet from a different row fails — the peach reads as "a wall yellowed with age" and the blue-violet reads "practically black," giving an oppressive mood. Stated as a graded risk, not a binary: **the further you move across rows, the more you risk** — which makes the fan deck itself a usable physical proxy for the rule.
- **Practical tooling note**: professional designer paint collections contain complex non-trivial interior shades but need real experience to combine; **a cheap paint fan deck from a paint retailer** is recommended as the beginner's working instrument instead, and can be asked for at the counter just to handle before buying.
- **Two failure modes named from real photos**: curtains too saturated for the rest of the room (the first thing the eye lands on, reads as wanting to be removed); and a yellow introduced to "liven up" a room that is too intense for that space and reads as distracting and irritating rather than cheerful.
