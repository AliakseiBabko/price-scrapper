## Plumbing — Installation Frame Selection: Brand-Agnostic Principles

- **⚠️ Mounting-point count should scale with the wall material behind the frame, not be fixed at a default**: for a poured-concrete load-bearing wall, 2 rear anchor points are described as sufficient; for a softer material (aerated concrete/cinder block), more anchor points are recommended, because these materials hold anchors less securely — a real installation-safety principle independent of any specific frame brand.
- **Frame depth is a real use-case variable, not just a size option**: standard-depth frames (~15cm) suit ordinary in-wall installation against a real wall; slim-depth frames (~8cm) exist specifically for stud/false-wall ("фальш-стена") builds where saving depth behind the fixture matters. **⚠️ Regional/building-type caveat stated directly**: the slim-depth variant's connection method (routing into the floor or straight back into the wall) is only practical in private/detached construction — in a multi-story apartment building, the sewer stack's fixed location generally rules it out. A useful building-type filter independent of brand.
- **⚠️ A concrete, checkable cost-saving trick, not brand-specific in principle**: air-conditioner condensate is not permitted (per SanPiN-type code, as stated) to drain directly into the sewer — it normally requires a dedicated siphon (cited at ≈10,000 RUB). The trick demonstrated: route the AC's condensate line into the installation frame's tank through its existing technical knockout hole instead of buying a separate siphon — satisfies the same code requirement without the extra siphon cost. Generalizable to any frame with a spare technical opening, not just this brand's.
- **Rimless ("безободковый") toilets are now the standard hygiene recommendation over rimmed ("ободковый") toilets** — a rim traps dirt the glaze can't reach during manufacturing. **⚠️ Named tradeoff and fix**: rimless toilets can splash water onto the floor on flush if flow isn't tuned to the bowl; the fix (either from the toilet manufacturer or the frame manufacturer) is a set of interchangeable flow-restrictor rings fitted inside the flush outlet to widen or narrow the water column — a DIY-installable fix, no plumber required. Genuinely useful, brand-agnostic mechanism.
- **A children's-bathroom-specific product exists: a height-adjustable installation ("toilet terminal")** allowing the toilet height to be lowered while children are small and raised later as they grow, without redoing the surrounding tile/finish — a real design option worth knowing exists, independent of which brand supplies it.
- **⚠️ Design-trend conflict named**: a floating shelf/lighting feature built above a standard-height installation frame (a current design trend) can push the frame's own maintenance/manifold access point too high to service without a stepladder; a low-profile or height-adjustable frame variant keeps that access point reachable even with the shelf added. A real coordination point for a bathroom designed with this kind of feature, regardless of brand.
