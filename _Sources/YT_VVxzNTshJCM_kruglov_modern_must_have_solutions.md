## Genuinely New Content

- **⚠️ Master-switch second-household-member usability caveat, extending
  this store's existing master-switch content**: because the master
  switch works by fully cutting power to the apartment's general
  lighting circuit, a household member who stays home/sleeps in after
  another member leaves for work (and switches off the master light on
  the way out) loses light access entirely until they themselves walk to
  the entry door and reactivate the master switch — a real, easily
  overlooked usability tradeoff in a multi-person household with staggered
  schedules, not present on this page's existing master-switch content.
- **Smart-kettle skepticism, a new practical counterpoint to this
  store's existing smart-kettle feature content**: remotely starting a
  kettle from a phone sounds convenient, but if the kettle has no water
  in it, the household still has to physically walk over and fill it
  before the remote-start does anything useful — and modern kettles
  already heat water in roughly 30-40 seconds regardless, meaningfully
  narrowing the real-world benefit of the smart-start feature. Framed as
  general advice: prioritize smart-home tools that actually solve a
  problem for your specific household, not just because a device
  category exists.
- **Passthrough-switch cost framing, a minor new figure**: a passthrough/
  crossover switch setup costs roughly **2,000-4,000 RUB** more than a
  standard switch at a given location — framed as clearly worth paying
  for the daily comfort of not needing to backtrack to a switch, a
  slightly different framing from this round's own `kkE25HmFciU`
  cognitive-load caution (that entry warns against overusing passthrough
  switching broadly; this one argues the marginal cost is worth it at
  the specific locations where it's genuinely useful — complementary,
  not conflicting).
