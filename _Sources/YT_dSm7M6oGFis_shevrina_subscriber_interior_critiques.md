## Design Concept — Named Mistakes With Correctives

- **⚠️ Pendants and sconces hung at different heights — she calls it the most frequent mistake she sees.** "Я не знаю, откуда пошла такая мода — это и не было стильно тогда, и не стильно сейчас." Applies to dining zones, bedside pendants and wall sconces alike. **The same error with staggered shelves**: reads visually overloaded rather than playful.
- **⚠️ Bath alcove wider than the bath — the mistake is building out only ONE side.** The usual move is a stub wall on one side with storage niches for bottles; she says that reads as "какая-то недоработка." Two correctives:
  1. **Build out both sides** — shallower, so no bottle niches, but compensate with **a horizontal shelf running almost the full length of the bath's long side**. Then **drop the ceiling over the bath zone and paint it the same colour**, strengthening the "box" effect.
  2. **A tiled podium at bath height (60 cm)**, deliberately made *larger* than the bath so there are tiled margins at **both** ends, with the bath centred. She notes this reads as intended especially with small-format tile, which is what most real examples of the detail use.
- **⚠️ Paint all three walls of a bath alcove one colour, not just one accent wall** — so the colour "окутывает" the bath. She praises this where a submitter had done it.
- **⚠️ Storage niches for bottles should be thin and elegant, not wide and massive** — a common proportional error, and she contrasts two submitted bathrooms directly on this point.
- **⚠️ Shelf proportion corrective, with a dimension**: for shelving that reads too wide, **add a central vertical divider and make it thick — about 2 cm** — to assert the vertical, then **reduce the number of horizontal divisions and make them thinner** (metal, for instance) so they recede. Function is unchanged, appearance improves.
- **⚠️ Square floor drains ("трапы") — avoid, and never place one at a random point in the shower.** Her mechanism is a tiling one: **the floor has to fall to the drain, so the tile gets cut a great deal, including diagonal cuts, which looks bad.** Renders mislead because they show smooth uncut tile. A concrete, checkable objection that a visualisation cannot reveal.
- **⚠️ Knotty, high-contrast wood-look tile is wrong in minimalism** — it generates visual noise and steals attention from a genuine accent (in the submitted case, an accent wall she liked). **It works only on horizontal surfaces such as floors; on walls choose a calmer grain without knots and without strong veining.** Refines her grey-tile/knotty-wood pairing point from `avRNMkNdOBs` and `haM4H-b-bZM`.
- **⚠️ Random single-element colour or material insertions** — one wood-fronted door among white fronts, or a random blue patch on an otherwise white wall. Her position: don't, unless you know why that specific element is being distinguished. Consistent with the accent-must-be-justified rule already on record from Anuta Vlady in the colour batch.
- **Extend a material to the full height where it defines a zone**: in one submitted bathroom she would carry the wood fronts over the WC installation as well, in wood-look panels, so the whole WC zone is wood floor-to-ceiling rather than wood cabinets above a differently-finished installation.
