## New facts

1. **Worked no-upper-cabinets case, extending this store's existing trend content**: a real
   kitchen with no upper cabinets uses (a) a tall vertical fridge column, (b) a separately
   relocated, concealed freezer built under the countertop (distinct compartment, not the
   fridge's own freezer drawer), (c) a combined oven+microwave vertical column, and (d) a
   separate full-height storage pantry column — a concrete example of the storage
   relocation this store's existing no-upper-cabinets trend content (Round 8) says is
   required, not optional.
2. **Hidden sponge-holder mount**: a sponge holder/clip mounted to the *underside/front
   face* of the sink lip itself (not left loose on the counter) — keeps the sponge out of
   sight while still within reach, reinforcing a "visually clean" countertop.
3. **⚠️ Kitchen backsplash is a poor location for a heated-floor thermostat control** —
   the backsplash is this kitchen's highest-demand real estate for outlets/appliances, and
   a heated floor has a real 2-3 hour thermal lag before it's felt anyway, so instant
   backsplash access provides no practical benefit. **Recommendation: mount the heated-floor
   thermostat on a wall away from the backsplash instead**, and use the thermostat's
   twice-daily schedule-programming capability (heat scheduled ahead of actual use) rather
   than relying on immediate manual access.
4. **⚠️ Artificial-stone/acrylic countertop wet-zone material-selection caution**: not every
   artificial-stone material is suitable for a genuinely wet zone — some show permanent
   water marks/staining that only professional polishing can remove. **A higher price does
   not guarantee wet-zone suitability** — consult a countertop specialist on which specific
   material is rated for the intended zone before ordering.
5. **Backsplash-tile-after-countertop installation sequencing rule**: install and level the
   countertop first, then fit the tile backsplash down onto it (not tile-first,
   countertop-after) — produces a cleaner, less-staining, longer-lasting, and more
   repairable joint at the countertop/backsplash seam.
6. **Real-project defect critique: undersized prep clearance from an oversized cooktop
   choice** — a 4-burner cooktop installed for a young, actively-cooking household left too
   little counter space on both sides (sink-to-cooktop gap and cooktop-to-coffee-machine
   gap), with no room left for occasional-use appliances (grill, waffle iron). Recommends
   matching cooktop size to actual household cooking volume, not defaulting to the largest
   available cooktop.
7. **Compact extending/transforming table recommended over a large fixed 4-chair set for a
   2-person household in a small kitchen** — the specific counter-case observed: a heavy
   (~50kg) metal-and-tile-base table that's functionally immovable day-to-day (too heavy for

8. **Voice-controlled curtains via smart home, alongside voice-controlled lighting** — a
