## Walls / Ceilings — Durable Facts & Rules (faux brick — painting/finishing stage, delta vs. episode 1)

- **Two-coat (in practice, closer to three-coat by this point) painting
  process for the faux-brick plaster texture**, using a thicker, more
  expensive acrylic interior paint on the second attempt after a thinner/
  cheaper paint (used on the first coat) failed to fully hide surface
  imperfections. **Direct causal comparison stated by the source**: the
  thicker paint "lies down much better and covers all the gaps that the
  previous [cheaper] paint left showing" — a real, checkable
  price-vs-coverage tradeoff for this specific rough-plaster-texture
  painting application, not a general paint-quality claim.
- **Painting technique/sequence for the brick texture, stated explicitly**:
  (1) go over the recessed mortar-joint lines/seams with a brush first;
  (2) then go over the brick faces themselves with a **dry roller** (no
  additional paint loaded) — the dry roller pass removes visible brush
  strokes and leaves a soft, uneven texture the source likens to
  "meringue"/"zephyr" (a soft, cloud-like marshmallow texture) rather than
  a flat painted surface. This is the specific mechanism that finishes the
  3D brick illusion from episode 1's plastering stage — the plaster
  creates the rough shape, this brush-then-dry-roller sequence is what
  makes the *paint* read as brick-like rather than as a flat painted wall.
- **A real, stated failure mode for this painting technique**: losing
  track of which individual "brick" has already been painted is easy if
  you get distracted mid-wall, since the technique requires painting
  brick-by-brick rather than in broad strokes — a concrete, checkable QC
  risk (missed/incompletely-painted bricks) specific to this method.
- **A smaller roller (than the one already owned) was deliberately bought
  for repainting the brick texture specifically**, on the reasoning that a
  smaller roller is easier to control brick-by-brick than a larger one —
  a practical tool-sizing tip for anyone replicating this technique.
