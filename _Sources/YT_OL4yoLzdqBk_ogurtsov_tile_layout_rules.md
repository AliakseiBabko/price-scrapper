## ⚠️⚠️ 1. THE FINDING THAT CONNECTS TO EVERYTHING ELSE THIS BATCH — fixtures are positioned by the tile grid

> **Place mixers and electrical points in the MIDDLE of a tile or on a JOINT. During design you can move BOTH the tile grid and the fitting.**
>
> **And the reason is not only symmetry:** ***«Отверстие на краю плитки хрен сделаешь, не обломив кусочек.»***

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ INDEPENDENTLY CORROBORATED IN THIS SAME BATCH by [[_Sources/YT_0c-QhBDQMWE_shemchuk_technical_design_album_series|Шемчук]], who positions a bathroom extract fan so its centre lands exactly on a tile joint, and chases the duct to suit.** **Two unrelated practitioners, one principle: the tile setting-out is an INPUT to the services drawings, not a decoration applied afterwards.**
>
> **→ The scheduling consequence, which is the actionable part: the tile layout has to be resolved BEFORE the electrical and plumbing first fix, because it moves socket, mixer, flush-plate and fan positions.**

**⚠️ And the prerequisite for the tile layout itself:** *«Невозможно просчитать плитку, если не определились с планировкой стен, проёмов, расстановкой мебели и техники, потому что из этого исходит расположение точек электрики и воды, а также видимые части плитки.»* **→ Third source this batch for "furnish first, then everything else."**

**⚠️ RELIEF TILE has a related trap: check whether electrical points, water points, the flush plate or ventilation land on relief tile**, because a flat plate over a relief surface leaves ugly gaps behind it.

## ⚠️⚠️ 2. Cuts — lay from the CENTRE, and change the room if you have to

> **Laying from the edge is *«игра в русскую рулетку»*: the final cut may land over half a tile (fine) or as an ugly sliver.**

- **⚠️ The applied reason, not just perfectionism: a very narrow final piece is hard to cut cleanly, and if the wall is out of true it looks dreadful.**
- **⚠️⚠️ Therefore do NOT lay tile vertically — it multiplies the chance of narrow slivers.**
- **⚠️ Lay from the CENTRE, and choose whether a tile's CENTRE or a JOINT lands on the room's centreline — the correct choice is whichever leaves the edge pieces WIDER than half a tile.**
- **⚠️⚠️ AND IF BOTH OPTIONS LAND NEAR HALF A TILE: artificially REDUCE the room — add plaster, or build the wall differently while still at design stage — so the layout comes out in whole tiles.** **→ Changing the room to suit the tile. A striking inversion, and only available if the layout is done early.**
- **⚠️ One place you cannot win: a concealed access hatch needs ~60 cm and services usually hug a wall in a narrow WC. Accept the cut there.**
- Also avoid narrow cuts at floor and ceiling openings and at the bath screen, and **make adjacent cuts equal.**
- **⚠️⚠️ ONLY VISIBLE AREAS MATTER** — what happens under the bath or behind a cabinet is irrelevant, so plan the joints from the visible parts. **Corroborates Шемчук's floor-laying arrows, which hide the narrow cut under the kitchen.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 3. The laying ORDER, which is not the obvious one

> **The critical thing is that the boundary between the FIRST and SECOND row is level — *«если этого не сделать, то дальше вся плитка будет плясать»*.**
>
> **Starting with the floor is risky: you can damage it by dropping something, and even protected you would need certainty the floor is perfectly level.**

**⚠️⚠️ SO THE USUAL ORDER IS:**

1. **Fix a temporary profile by laser level.**
2. **Lay from the SECOND row upward.**
3. **Then lay the FLOOR.**
4. **Then lay the FIRST row last.** Cuts may be needed — *«бояться нужно не их, а ситуации, при которой останется щель»*.

**⚠️ And a companion rule: if one wall is smooth tile and another relief, lay the SMOOTH one first so the relief butts to it without gaps.**

## ⚠️ 4. Joints, and an expansion joint the vault did not have

- Tile cannot be laid without joints — they exist so tiles do not butt and crack under thermal movement.
- **⚠️⚠️ An EXPANSION JOINT (деформационный шов) of about 10 mm is required every 6–10 m** on larger continuous runs — offices, retail, or simply a hallway and living room tiled as one contour. **Ugly, but otherwise tiles crack or tent (*«встать домиком»*).** ⚠️ **He says "по нормам" without citing one; no jurisdiction given, so treat the interval as trade practice rather than a code figure.**
- Normal joints **1–3 mm**, set with spacers. **⚠️ A 1 mm joint looks best ONLY with rectified tile or careful selection — if the tile size varies, the joints visibly wander, and compensating by varying joint width is not worth attempting.**
- **⚠️ Mixing manufacturers is riskier still: even two rectified tiles can differ in actual size.**
- **⚠️⚠️ A tile-to-laminate junction needs the HEIGHT difference compensated — e.g. by a stepped screed — or you get threshold bumps.** **→ Independently corroborates [[_Sources/YT_0c-QhBDQMWE_shemchuk_technical_design_album_series|Шемчук's]] screed detail giving a step so different floor finishes meet under the door leaf at one level. Two sources, same detail.**

## ⚠️ 5. Where tile belongs — using the ELECTRICAL zones as the moisture guide

> **⚠️⚠️ He proposes reading the bathroom's ELECTRICAL SAFETY ZONES as the guide for where tile is mandatory, "потому что они как раз учитывают возможные брызги".**
>
> - **Zones 0–1: only fully waterproof materials — tile.**
> - **Zone 3, and cautiously zone 2: special paints, microcement, even non-paper wallpaper.**
> - **Floors: besides porcelain — poured floors, and (his own "кто-то удивится") linoleum and quartz-vinyl.**

> **→ ⚠️ A neat transfer: the electrical zoning already encodes where water reaches, so it can be reused as a finishes-zoning map.** ⚠️ **Zone numbering is jurisdiction-specific and he names no standard — the IDEA transfers, the zone boundaries must be taken from whatever code applies here.**
>
> He also warns the opposite extreme — tiling a whole bathroom perimeter can make it read as a technical room.

## ⚠️ 6. Buying — the part most likely to cost money

- **⚠️⚠️ Buy a solid surplus.** Tile breaks during and after the works, and a services replacement may force you to break tile deliberately. **Do not count on buying more later: another batch differs in shade, or the collection is discontinued.** Keep at least a box after completion. **⚠️ Unopened boxes can usually be returned within two weeks.**
- **⚠️ Decide quickly and buy about two months ahead.** Tile disappears; you prepare everything around a chosen tile and it runs out; a six-month wait for a new batch is not viable and re-planning mid-works rarely goes well. **If you have nowhere to store it, ask the shop — they often will, for a modest fee.**
- **⚠️ Pay for delivery to the floor so the SUPPLIER carries the breakage risk.** *«Везде, где только можно, оставляйте риски на других.»*
- **⚠️⚠️ CHECK BATCH, CALIBRE AND TONE ON EVERY PACK.** Under the guise of a discount — or without one — you may be given leftovers from different warehouses, which differ in shade and even size.

## ⚠️⚠️ 7. Vetting the tiler — and a single screening question

- Not every tiler has good core bits for porcelain, a saw for large formats, or experience with modern materials. **⚠️ Confirm the tiler can handle the project's level BEFORE buying materials — and if tiler and material cannot be married, decide which to replace at that stage.**
- **⚠️⚠️ THE SCREENING QUESTION: ask whether they will use a tile levelling system (СВП).** *«Хороший вопрос, чтобы отсеять уж совсем некомпетентных кадров.»*
- **⚠️ Epoxy grout: not all tilers will work with it** — the proportions cannot be eyeballed, it needs particular tools, and it can ruin tile. **On mosaic it costs far more because the joint area is much greater. Check the chosen tile is compatible, since epoxy may not wipe off — test in the least visible spot first.**
- **⚠️ Do not use contrasting grout without reason**: too dark reads as a gap, too light looks odd on a floor once dirt sits in the joints. **Without a designer, choose grout close to the tile colour.**

## ⚠️ 8. Patterns, prep, and external corners

- **Diagonal laying hides uneven walls**; diagonal and offset are design devices needing justification since they raise labour and cost. **Default to straight.**
- **⚠️ Small tile plus even smaller mosaic produces visual noise** — use fine-joint small tile as contrast rather than over a whole surface; with large tile it can work.
- **Before laying**: confirm tiles are one size and adjust if needed; **separate different stamps into stacks so they are not laid adjacent; if batches differ in shade, put them on different walls**; protect any coating from grout and adhesive.
- **⚠️ External corners, three options**: a **45° mitre** (most attractive, difficult, expensive — ⚠️ **and some executions leave a sharp, fragile, injury-prone arris**); a **decorative profile** (visible strip, but well protected and much cheaper); or **overlap** (simplest, edges visible, acceptable with through-coloured porcelain).
- **During laying**: no voids beneath tiles (crack risk); wash epoxy grout off immediately; handle rectified tile without a chamfer carefully; **avoid adjacent identical patterns by drawing from the different stacks and rotating.**

## ⚠️ 9. Underfloor heating under tile

- All UFH types can go under tile, and **where you walk on tile constantly it is not merely permitted but necessary** — tile is cold, otherwise you end up managing with rugs.
- **⚠️ A current fashion: UFH in the WALL instead of a towel rail, drying on hooks — but arrange it so the bathroom side is tile and the other side has no furniture or adjoining elements.** ⚠️ `single-account`, no detail on build-up.
