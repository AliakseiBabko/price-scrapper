# Source Note — NSDSGN podcast with builder Руслан, "15 важных вопросов о ремонте"

**72-minute podcast. The guest, not the host, carries the content** — Руслан is a
contractor with 30+ years in construction, ~20 in business-plus/premium отделка and
in **старый фонд** (old-fund) reconstruction, restoration and «снятие с аварийности».
He speaks as a general contractor, not as this studio's employee.

**Region is level 1**: he names **Санкт-Петербург** and **Москва** directly in
spoken content and contrasts their price levels explicitly. Prices below are
**December 2023**, converted at **92.8875 RUB/USD** (trailing 6 months to
2023-12-15, `currency_converter.py`).

**Delivery model: Mixed / Ambiguous, deliberately.** He quotes market rates for
brigades and for individual hires he does *not* sell, alongside his own
turnkey/генподряд segment — and he flags his own figures as approximate and
explicitly **not an offer** («не надо это воспринимать как оферту»).

**Promotional ratio low, and this is worth stating precisely**: the host opens by
saying «не привязываюсь к тебе даже, мы не занимаемся рекламой, мы разбираемся
именно в стройке». The one self-interested passage is the general-contracting

## Renovation cost tiers, St Petersburg, December 2023

**⚠️ These are per-m² orientation figures for construction work only.** He states
the exclusions himself: no classical-style work, no decorative plaster, no
ventilation, no balcony insulation.

1. **~10,000 RUB/m² (≈$110/m²) — the floor, and he says below it is impossible.**
   Low-qualified «дешёвая отделка» by one or two individuals hired by the hour, with
   no brigade and no прораб. Roughly «муж на час» / Профи.ру territory.
2. **~20,000 RUB/m² (≈$220/m²) — a good brigade with a бригадир.**
   **⚠️ The important qualification, and it is a checkable competence test:** such
   brigades **do not know what a теневой профиль or a двери скрытого монтажа is** —
   «они говорят, что знают. Нет. Они могут сказать: а нафиг вам надо, а давайте
   поменяем». Good brigades genuinely working at 20,000+ have **three-year waiting
   lists**.
3. **~20,000–25,000–30,000 RUB/m² (≈$220–$270–$320/m²) — his stated minimum
   average level** for a modern renovation built to a design project.
4. **~70,000 RUB/m² (≈$750/m²) — the premium end**, quoted against the 20,000
   figure as the 3.5× question clients actually ask: «ну это же стоит 20.000 за
   метр, почему разница в три раза?»
5. **Moscow: 90,000 / 100,000 / 150,000 RUB/m² (≈$970 / ≈$1,100 / ≈$1,600 per m²)**
   — a structurally higher floor than St Petersburg. He attributes part of it to
   genuine service certainty and part openly to **пафос** — status signalling about
   what the renovation cost. Quoted line from a client with flats in both cities:
   «в Москве деньгами никого не удивишь».

### Why the 3.5× gap exists — the mechanism, not the assertion

- **Economy**: the master counts square metres per day and optimises for speed, not
  quality. He **skips technological waiting times** — primer, then putty applied
  immediately, fast sanding, wallpaper on top. Result: **microcracks within a year
  or sooner**, and surfaces that are not quite flat.
- **Premium**: a different малярка «пирог» — reinforcement, **расшивка** of
  plasterboard seams, additional painter's tapes, more layers, and mechanical
  application (the tooling itself costs money). Plus somebody is paid to come and
  check the work.
- **Rationality runs both ways**: a super-renovation in cheap property is pointless,
  and an economy renovation inside very expensive walls is equally odd. He resists
  the host's «ремонт с мебелью должен стоить столько, сколько стоит квартира»
  heuristic as too crude to compare across markets.

### ⚠️ Why cheap anecdotal per-m² figures circulate — and are true for the speaker

A genuinely useful explanation of a recurring data-quality trap. A beginner
contractor **has no price list**, so he copies a смета off some company's website of
unknown age. Being decent, he finishes the job at zero margin or at a loss, and then
reports honestly: «я сделал себе ремонт за 7.000» (≈$80/m²). **That figure is true
for him and false as a market rate.** A real price is: cost of organising the work +
company markup + expenses + taxes.

He also gives the failure mechanism for the cheapest quote: client wants 10,000/m² ×
50 m² = 500,000 RUB; the worker takes ~200,000 as an advance, burns it, finds three
weeks of unpaid work still ahead, and leaves — «ключи в почтовом ящике» — for a
neighbour offering 15,000/m². **The cheapest quote is the one most likely to be
abandoned mid-job**, and the arithmetic shows why.

## Estimates (смета) — what a good one looks like

1. **A four-line смета is the red flag** («сантехника столько-то тысяч, электрика
   столько-то»). It guarantees допы and «а мы вам это не считали» arguments.
2. **Concrete example of the granularity required**, and it is a plumbing spec:
   a **коллекторный узел** can be with or without leak sensors («датчики от
   протечек»), with or without filters; the разводка can be **тройниковая** or
   **лучевая**. If it is written down, the contractor cannot later say it was not
   quoted.
3. **State explicitly what is NOT included.**
4. **⚠️ Door installation should be excluded from the builder's смета and left to
   the manufacturer's own fitters** — mechanism given: they unpack, find a chip, and
   resolve it themselves fast, with responsibility and warranty staying on the door
   maker. If the builder unpacks it, the manufacturer says «вы там их пока
   открывали, наверное, вы и поцарапали».
5. **Waste removal (вынос/вывоз мусора) belongs in the смета as an approximate
   range** — the exact quantity is never calculable in advance.
6. **With a concrete design project and a смета against it, the total should neither
   grow nor shrink.** Normal drift is **~10%**; keep a **10–15% buffer**. The stated
   cause is real and specific: **design-project measurements are taken at rough
   stage, and walls lose thickness after plastering, so areas move.** Chasing 3–4%
   more accuracy is not worth the days it costs.
7. A detailed смета also lets lines move painlessly into and out of акты and
   допсоглашения when the client changes something.

## Materials procurement and price volatility

- They buy черновые материалы as a service for a **stated percentage**, and handle
  **рекламация** with suppliers themselves (damp plasterboard, transport damage —
  return claim, refund, re-purchase).
- **⚠️ Never lock a year-long contract to prices as at the day of signing.** Real
  observed series on an **OSB sheet: 800 → 1,600 → 2,400 → 3,600 RUB** — roughly 4×
  over about fifteen days, screenshotted day by day by a colleague. **Year not
  stated for this series, so it is deliberately left unconverted.**
- **Their response to that volatility is a substitution, and it is the useful part**:
  they had been lining old-fund ceilings with OSB *because it was cheap* (to stop
  dust falling from the floors above); once OSB passed plasterboard in price they
  switched to plasterboard. The function, not the material, was the requirement.
- **Method**: take an advance for materials, refresh the actual price list **every
  3–6 months**, buy at today's price, report by receipts.

## Contractor selection and vetting

- **Signals to check**: how long the social accounts have existed; whether comments
  look genuine or fake; visit two or more live objects; ask for a client's phone
  number; meet in person.
- **⚠️ Crew churn is visible on site and is a real signal** — «приходишь на объект,
  там посмотришь, они друг друга и не знают толком».
- **The core problem he names: a specialist's quality cannot be assessed in the
  moment.** «Это будет понятно через полгода, когда этот человек уже куда-то уедет,
  и у тебя что-то прорвёт.» His conclusion is that only long-horizon references
  («я живу несколько лет в этом интерьере») or somebody who will answer for the
  subcontractor actually work.
- Real failure case relayed from a management company: an organisation-supplied
  master **sold the client's materials to her neighbours, slept in her flat, and
  moonlighted on other jobs**, stretching her schedule. He notes the counter-view
  — on those rates it is genuinely hard to survive, so they improvise.
- Moscow-specific pattern: recommendation chains ending in unqualified labour — a
  colleague's «замок на Новой Риге» where the plastering was done by someone found
  on Avito who had never held plaster.
- **Escrow is coming but does not exist yet**: he knows of a bank-plus-app pilot
  where the client sees funds in an electronic cell and release happens against an
  electronically signed акт. Named nothing. **Record as an anticipated mechanism,
  not an available one.**
- **On advances**: an advance is the client's money held by the contractor; it
  becomes the contractor's only as акты are signed against completed work.

## What never to economise on

- **Fundamentals, and the builders themselves.**
- **Старый фонд: never economise on surveys/обследование.** A superficial inspection
  is «бомба замедленного действия».
- **Engineering**: good taps and all shut-off valves («запорная арматура»), good
  pipes, and never a loose/рыхлая **стяжка**.
- **The electrical panel — автоматы and wiring.**
- **⚠️ The rule stated as a trade-off, which is what makes it usable: «лучше не
  купить шкаф, чем сэкономить на тех же автоматах в электрощите и проводке.»**
  Skip a piece of furniture rather than downgrade the concealed engineering.
- **Cost × durability, not cost**: «поменяешь пять раз кран текущий каждые полгода —
  потратишь те же деньги, может даже больше». A cheap material multiplied by its
  service life is often not the cheaper option.

## Schedule, client behaviour and site discipline

- **Deadline-driven renovation is dangerous** — rushing to 1 September or
  31 December. Prefer one overall object deadline, movement in stages, and stated
  causes for delay.
- **Duration: ~1 year for an average 100 m²+**, dominated by supply lead times.
  **⚠️ Some finish materials can only be chosen after a stage completes** (measure
  what actually resulted, then decide). Clients resist this and want to buy early;
  he says that is where the pain repeatedly comes from.
- **The main pain in the premium segment is not deadlines — it is mid-project design
  changes.** «Слушайте, а я здесь не хочу кухню.» Clients return from holiday having
  seen something in Italy. He notes **«аппетит приходит во время стройки»** is normal
  and expected: clients state a budget, then knowingly raise it.
- **⚠️ Anxious daily client presence is counter-productive, with a stated mechanism**:
  processes have a duration, and intervening mid-way makes a normal state look
  wrong. **Better to arrive at the end of a stage (e.g. all malyarka) and list every
  defect at once.** Changing things daily extends the schedule for the same end
  result — and masters start optimising for the client's reaction instead of for
  correctness. He extends this to the firm's own director not disrupting the прораб.

## Roles: client / designer / builder

- **Паритетный договор, equal parties** — each came for something different (money,
  a renovation, realised creative work).
- **⚠️ The builder's incentive to substitute a cheaper method, named plainly by the
  builder himself**: same money, three times less time, plus «дизайнер вообще ничего
  не понимает» to the client. **The client cannot tell the difference; friends notice
  later.** He calls working that way «утопия» for anyone with a repeat-business model.
- Recorded as a positive worked example: on a shared object the builder proposed a
  more modern ceiling execution, the designer rejected it on design grounds, the
  builder reworked it, and both agreed — **appearance unchanged, execution more
  durable.** The point being that he raised it instead of silently substituting.
- **⚠️ Авторский надзор is necessary, and the reason given is decision-making, not
  supervision.** Construction is «живой организм»; only the project's author may
  change the concept. Concrete example: a shelf specced at **250 mm because the
  client's specific shampoo bottles are that height** — 2 cm lower and it fails.
  When dimensions move after plastering, somebody has to decide.
- **⚠️ The failure mode of авторский контроль is the designer teaching the builder.**
  «Дизайнер не должен никого учить, строители должны быть с руками.» He names
  **теневой профиль** specifically — «я столько на нём копий сломал, многие впервые
  его делают». **This is the second independent statement in this batch that shadow-
  gap detailing is where crews are least competent** (see
  [[YT_gTgUJaWKobM_nsdsgn_stretch_ceiling_shadow_gap]]).
- Designer's own claimed value: knowing ~95% of the materials on the market, and
  extracting from the client a requirement they cannot articulate.

## Генподряд — threshold and mechanism

- **⚠️ Threshold stated: around 300 m², or a two-storey flat.** Below that
  («однушка в Кудрово») it is pointless. At that scale separate brigades and
  independent subcontractors «работают очень плохо».
- **Definition**: one legal entity holds the whole scope; every смежник on the
  project is contractually under it; it accepts their work, sequences who comes when
  — «как дирижёр оркестра» — bears responsibility, and takes a percentage for that
  control.
- **Why it works, mechanically**: an established team passes information along the
  chain, resolves breakage internally instead of blame-shifting, and knows who is
  capable of what. Without it, «никто ни за что не отвечает, каждый сам за себя» and
  the client stands between two trades adjudicating fault.
- **Sequencing needs an owner**: someone rushes ahead when they should pause for
  electrics and then ventilation.
- **⚠️ Client-supplied subcontractors are a named risk category.** Industrial
  ventilation fitters who are the client's acquaintances may be able to build complex
  ventilation but **have never worked in a delicate finished environment**. Observed
  directly: they were **cutting a ventilation duct on the kitchen island** at final
  finish stage. «Люди по профилю могут хорошо делать свою работу, но не умеют её
  делать в определённых условиях.»
- **Supplier stability over price**: he dropped a mirror supplier who won tenders on
  price and then re-did the work at their own cost every single time — «я не понимаю,
  на что они жили». Changing people constantly is worse than supporting a known
  weakness.
- ⚠️ **This section is the one place his own commercial interest aligns with the
  advice** — he sells exactly this scheme and says «я стараюсь на все большие объекты
  проталкивать именно такую схему». The mechanism is recorded; the recommendation is
  tagged as tier-steering.
