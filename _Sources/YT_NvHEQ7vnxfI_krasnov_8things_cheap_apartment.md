## Mistakes / Warnings — the eight items

- **⚠️ Hardware finish durability**: matte finishes — brass («латунь»), bronze, nickel — "стареют красиво и не теряют внешнего вида," versus glossy/"rhinestone"-finish hardware ("сияющие стразы, золотое глянцевое покрытие") which loses its sheen within a couple of months, scratches, and (for gold-look plastic) visibly reveals itself as fake metal. Applies to door handles, kitchen cabinet handles, bathroom hardware. If real gold/brass is wanted, buy the real material rather than a glossy imitation.
- **Entryway pouf-under-console is a Pinterest staple that fails in daily use**: pulling the pouf out from under the console every time to sit down is inconvenient enough that it migrates around the room instead of returning to its spot. **Fix**: give the pouf its own dedicated spot beside the console, not underneath it, if it will genuinely be used daily.
- **⚠️ Flooring material transitions**: mixing laminate/tile/quartz-vinyl room-to-room and joining them with wide aluminium thresholds reads as "renovated in pieces," even with expensive individual materials. **Prefer a single continuous flooring material across the whole flat.** Where a material change is technically unavoidable, prefer thin, unobtrusive transitions: a profile matching the floor colour, or brass/stainless-steel strips if the concept calls for it, or (his favourite) **no visible profile at all — a cork compensator strip.**
- **⚠️ Mirror-to-sink gap**: a wall mirror mounted flush against a sink looks clean in photos but collects water-spot and soap residue within days from normal handwashing/face-washing splash, requiring near-daily wiping. **Fix: leave a 15–20 cm gap between mirror and sink** — also adds a sense of depth/space. He notes the alternative "Pinterest-photo" version of this only really works with daily household cleaning staff, which he treats as a legitimate but different life-pattern choice, not a universal fix.
- **⚠️ Faux-material tells, three named**: (1) tile imitating stone/wood/marble with a visibly repeating pattern (e.g. only 2-3 unique tile faces) or an unnaturally small format reads as fake immediately; (2) laminate/quartz-vinyl **without a bevel edge** risks reading as linoleum, since there's no surface texture break between planks; (3) an LDSP kitchen countertop imitating stone/marble is exposed by touch — it feels warm, where stone reads cool. His own studio's practice: use these substitute materials only in low-visibility or heavy-wear zones, and only genuinely good-quality modern versions (stated to sometimes outperform the natural material on durability/maintenance) — never as a blanket ban on synthetic materials.
- **Built-in microwave/oven column height**: mounting the appliance block level with the upper cabinets (eye height or above) forces near-arm's-length reaching for hot dishes — a burn/spill risk, worse for shorter users. **Recommended height: roughly chest level or slightly below**, so the whole household can use it safely and comfortably.
- **Bathroom glass shelves**: visually light and currently popular, but show water spots, soap residue, and handprints almost immediately — requires either daily cleaning or living with a look of neglect. **Alternative: shelves in tile matching the wall, or quality-coated metal** — hold their clean appearance longer with less maintenance.
- **⚠️ Switch/outlet plate colour**: standard white plastic switches/outlets clash visibly against dark, complex, or saturated wall colours/finishes and read as an unfinished detail. **Match the plate colour to the wall (or tile) colour** where the palette allows (e.g. dark-grey wall → dark-grey/graphite plate; beige wall → beige/cream plate) to make the fixture visually disappear. **Contrasting plates (e.g. black on a light wall) can work as a deliberate accent** — his own studio uses this — but the shade must match the room's palette precisely; even a small mismatch reads as an error rather than a choice, so only use a contrast when confident in the exact tone.

## Family Requirements / Preferences

- **His explicit design philosophy on cleaning-dependent finishes (mirror/sink gap, glass shelves)**: "любое проектное решение... должно делаться под конкретного человека, под его сценарии жизни." A finish requiring daily upkeep (glass shelves, flush mirror) is not wrong in itself — it's wrong for a household without daily cleaning help, and right for one that has it.
