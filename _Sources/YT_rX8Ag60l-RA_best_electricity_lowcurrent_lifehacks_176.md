## Electrical / Switches, Sockets, Cables

- **Electrical/low-current-point centering must account for finished door-casing width, not the raw opening**: installers commonly center outlets/switches on a wall segment measured before door casing (наличник, typically 5-7cm wide) is installed — once casing is added, the true visual center shifts, leaving the point permanently off-center relative to the finished opening. Rule: offset electrical points in advance so they land centered on the *finished* (post-casing) wall segment. `confirmed`, described as "the single dumbest mistake" made by installers/designers who only plan around the room as it exists in front of them at rough-in time.
- **Dedicated low-current distribution cabinet, separate from the main electrical panel**: rather than leaving twisted-pair/ethernet and TV-coax cabling terminated in an exposed, dust-collecting tangle inside a closet ("the octopus"), build a small dedicated cabinet with its own cover — sized so a router can sit inside without degrading WiFi signal. `confirmed`, concrete equipment/practice recommendation.
- **Combined electrical+low-current "technical column" technique**: rather than recessing the whole panel assembly into a non-structural wall, or building an entire closet shallower (e.g. only 40cm deep) just to avoid one small protrusion, dedicate a single narrow closet section (~110mm deep column) purely as a technical bay for both panels, giving the rest of the closet its full functional depth (50-60cm) — invisible once the closet doors are closed. `confirmed`, addresses a stated common designer/installer hesitation.
- **Pass-through opening between a low-current distribution box and an adjacent closet shelf/section**, plus a nearby outlet — lets a client later place a network storage device (NAS) on a closet shelf and route its ethernet/power cleanly through the pass-through rather than running exposed cable across the closet. A concrete example of anticipating a plausible future client need at rough-in stage.

## Furniture / Built-ins

- **P-44T-series ventilation shaft in the entry hall — a specific fix for a common Moscow-region panel-building defect**: the protruding shaft narrows the hallway and creates an exposed exterior corner prone to impact damage. Fix: measure the shaft's depth, then extend the adjacent wall flush with it (minimum ~50cm), and box in a matching ceiling soffit above — enabling a **built-in** (not free-standing/corpus) wardrobe to install flush against it. Corroborates this store's existing built-in-vs-corpus-wardrobe cost/practicality rule with a specific P-44T-series application: a built-in wardrobe only requires paying for the front/end panel finish (the shaft-adjacent wall is already being finished regardless), while a free-standing corpus wardrobe additionally requires its own top/bottom/back/side panels.
