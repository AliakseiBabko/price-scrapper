## Tile/Grout Selection — New Items

1. **Shopping tip: bring a small tile sample when choosing grout** — a
   piece small enough to fit in a pocket/bag makes color-matching against
   a manufacturer's grout swatches far easier and more reliable than
   comparing from memory or a photo.
2. **Color-matching design rule, monolithic vs. modular look**: grout
   matched to the tile's own base color reads as a monolithic, seamless
   surface (recommended for larger-format tile where that's the goal);
   grout in a contrasting, darker shade instead emphasizes the tile
   module/joint pattern and size — a deliberate choice, not a default.
3. **⚠️ White/light cement grout on floors — a real, photographically
   demonstrated discoloration mechanism, the video's own headline
   warning**: even a quality two-component (epoxy) white grout on a
   floor visibly discolors toward a "coffee with milk" tone within about
   a month of normal use — but **only along actual foot-traffic paths**,
   not uniformly across the whole floor. Demonstrated directly on his
   own kitchen floor (light-brown, not even white, grout) — the
   traffic-path area is visibly darker than the same grout just outside
   the walked path. The lighter the grout's base color, the more visible
   this effect becomes; stated as the reason he does not recommend white
   floor grout at all, regardless of manufacturer or grade.
4. **Grout surface texture varies meaningfully by manufacturer/brand,
   worth checking before buying, tagged as a personal brand comparison**:
   some brands (Ceresit, per his own preference) have a smooth, fine
   finish with no visible coarse aggregate; others (a competitor,
   transcribed as "Litokol" or similar) have a visibly grainier/sandier
   texture. Personal preference, not a universal quality ranking —
   commercial brand mention.
5. **Buying convenience: some manufacturers sell matching-color silicone
   sealant alongside their grout color swatches** (named: Ceresit) —
   lets a buyer color-match the corner sealant to the chosen grout shade
   in the same shopping trip, rather than guessing separately.
