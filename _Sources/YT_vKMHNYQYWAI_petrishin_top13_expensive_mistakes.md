## Flooring — Semi-Dry Screed Quality Requirements, a Real Conversion Story

1. **A real personal conversion story with an explicit quality-ingredient
   checklist**: the speaker states he was originally skeptical of
   semi-dry mechanized screed after finding a developer-poured example
   that crumbled under a fingernail scratch on a real object — he
   describes ripping it out and pouring classic wet screed instead at the
   time. After later working with competent screed subcontractors, he
   concluded semi-dry mechanized screed is actually the best
   speed/cost option, **provided it includes**: fiber additive
   ("фибра"), a perimeter deformation joint ("демфер"), the correct
   cement grade and quantity, and correctly graded sand. Framed as a
   real practitioner reversal, not a first-time recommendation.

## Walls / Ceilings — Paint-Prep and Reinforcement Detail

2. **"Жираф" pole-sander-with-light grinding pass before filler
   application**: grinding plaster with a light-equipped extension-pole
   sander ("жираф") before applying filler removes bumps and low spots
   first — skipping this means the filler layer just telegraphs every
   existing surface irregularity, making later correction much harder.
3. **Fiberglass wallpaper-backing mesh ("стеклохолст") crack-hiding
   mechanism, corroborating an existing cross-channel technique with a
   new reason**: this store already has a stekloholst-in-the-prep-stack
   technique from RemProektMD (Moldova); this source adds an explicit
   mechanism — as a building continues its normal micro-settlement,
   hairline cracks will eventually appear in a paint-only wall finish,
   and stekloholst bridges/hides them. States that crews skipping it can
   underbid by roughly 30%, at the cost of this crack protection —
   corroboration-with-extension, not pure duplication.

## Bathroom — Tub/Tile Sequencing, Load-Testing, and Shower-Stall Material Spec

4. **Tub-to-tile install sequence with a specific silicone-joint width**:
   install and fully secure the tub first (e.g. on a mounting rail/
   channel), seal it to the wall around the full perimeter, *then* lay
   tile, then apply only a thin (~2mm) silicone finishing joint at the
   tub-tile line — contrasted with the common shortcut of leaning tile
   directly against an unsealed tub with a fat silicone bead, which the
   source says visibly blackens with mold much sooner (weeks, not
   months) simply because there's more silicone surface for mold to grow
   on.
5. **Acrylic-tub load-testing requirement before finalizing the seal —
   a genuinely new mechanism**: after sealing an acrylic tub to the wall
   but before finishing the joint, the tub must be loaded to
   approximately its real in-use weight (filled with water, or weighted
   with roughly 8 bags of tile adhesive as a stand-in) — an unloaded
   acrylic tub can sag once actually filled with water and a person's
   weight later, breaking the silicone seal at the tile line even if the
   installation looked perfect when new. Load first, then finish the
   seal, then remove the load.
6. **Shower-stall ("душевая кабина в строительном исполнении") material
   spec — described as this company's single largest warranty/
   reclamation cost source**: cement-based grout used in a wet shower
   zone instead of the required two-component (epoxy) grout is named as
   the top warranty-cost driver, corroborating this store's existing
   cement-vs-epoxy grout distinction with a real business-cost
   consequence. **Separately, the shower pan/base ("поддон") itself
   should be built from masonry (brick/cement, monolithic) — never
   framed from drywall or foam block** — plus careful perimeter
   waterproofing with tape and compatible materials. **Suggested lower-
   risk alternative**: a prefabricated cast shower-pan unit with a
   built-in siphon opening, paired with proper perimeter sealing, to
   reduce the risk of flooding the downstairs neighbor. Verification
   method recommended: visit the site during the pan-construction stage,
   or request photo/video documentation of the waterproofing layers.

## Electrical — Panel Handover and Labeling Discipline

7. **Electrical panel must be fully wired/terminated before handover of
   the rough-electrical stage, not deferred** — a named real practitioner
   complaint: some crews leave wiring routed to outlet boxes but the
   breaker panel itself unterminated, promising to "finish it in 30
   minutes later." **Breaker labels should be requested and applied
   immediately at the moment of payment for the electrical stage**, not
   left for later — otherwise, by the time the apartment is ready to
   occupy, nobody remembers which breaker controls which circuit.

## Walls / Ceilings — Drywall Screw and Joint-Tape QC

8. **Drywall screw-depth control, and a paper-vs-mesh joint-tape
   preference**: a screw head should sit slightly recessed but must not
   tear through the paper facing — a torn-paper screw hole isn't visible
   from outside but holds the board poorly and risks a crack or the
   board detaching later; check drywall ceilings up close (from a
   stepladder) for intact paper at every screw. **Seam taping**: this
   source states paper joint tape works properly for drywall seams,
   while mesh tape does not — instructed to insist on paper tape if a
   crew proposes mesh. `single-account`, stated as this practitioner's
   own technology preference — not independently cross-checked against
   this store's other drywall sources.
