## Bathroom / WC

- **Bathtub-recess resizing rule**: when a developer's wet-zone footprint is oversized, narrow it (moving the dividing wall inward) to reach one of three standard tub-recess widths — **1.60m, 1.70m, or 1.80m**. Field judgment given: 1.60m is uncomfortably small, **1.70m is this practitioner's stated optimum**, 1.80m suits taller occupants.
- **Tub-length comfort mechanism**: a too-long tub leaves a shorter occupant without foot purchase against the far end — while reclining, they slide down toward the drain instead of bracing; matters both sitting/showering and fully reclined/submerged.
- **Critical tub-installation tiling rule (cross-references and reinforces this store's existing rule)**: never leave a flat, tiled horizontal ledge/shoulder around an undersized tub set into an oversized recess — moisture, soap scum, and grime collect there and grout deteriorates and falls out over time. The tub must be sized to match its recess so tile can overlap the tub's own rim directly, with no tiled flat shoulder anywhere around it.

## Kitchen

- **Kitchen electrical rough-in sequencing rule, when the specific kitchen manufacturer/design isn't chosen yet**: (1) form the room's rough geometry first; (2) bring in that specific kitchen company's own on-site measurer once selected; (3) sign the kitchen contract; (4) obtain written/signed technical requirements from that company specifying exact electrical-point and water/drain stub-out positions; (5) only then finalize kitchen electrical rough-in. Reasoning: every kitchen manufacturer/installer uses its own internal placement conventions with no universal standard — guessing risks a redo. `confirmed`, explicit step order given.

## Walls / Ceilings — Radiator Niche Insulation

- **Never add extra insulation or a heat-reflective panel behind a radiator niche recessed into an exterior wall**, and don't thin the wall further there. **Explained mechanism**: for a uniform wall of any thickness, the interior/exterior temperature gradient reaches equilibrium roughly at the wall's midpoint; insulating or reflecting behind a radiator recess blocks the room's own warm air from reaching and warming that thinned wall section from the inside — the opposite of the intuitive "add insulation to protect the thin spot" instinct, and it makes that spot colder / condensation-prone instead. A wall thinned ~15cm by a radiator niche is still stated to remain warmer overall than most modern panel-building exterior walls without any special treatment — just plaster and drywall-clad it normally. `confirmed`, genuinely new mechanism explanation for this store, distinct from (and more specific than) any prior radiator-niche content already recorded.

## Plumbing

- **Leak-protection shutoff-valve sequencing rule**: the electromechanical shutoff valve (paired with floor-level leak sensors) must be the very first component after each riser's own first shutoff valve — installed **before** any other fitting, including a coarse (grubber) pre-filter. Reasoning: any downstream fitting/filter/hose that leaks stays protected only if the shutoff sits upstream of it; placed later in the chain, everything ahead of it remains an unprotected leak-risk zone regardless of the sensor system's presence. One device per riser (hot + cold = 2 for a standard single-riser-pair apartment). Extends this store's existing leak-sensor content with an explicit installation-order rule not previously recorded.
- **Always replace developer-supplied stock ("штатные") plumbing manifolds/collectors with new ones** — developer-installed fixtures are described as the cheapest available grade, with the presenter stating essentially every client who skipped replacing them has called back within 1-2 years reporting a leak/flood. `single-account`, strongly stated practitioner experience.
- **Insulate every rough cold/hot supply pipe run in foam sleeve ("флекс"), regardless of manufacturer** — prevents surface condensation forming and dripping.

## Windows / Slopes

- **Widening a window/light opening on a non-load-bearing exterior wall is recommended by default wherever practical** — measurably brightens the room with no real functional downside, per this practitioner. Two hard limits stated: (1) minimum **10cm return/pier** must remain on each side of the widened opening (needed to form the slope/reveal); (2) if extending the opening upward, leave a minimum **20cm** clearance to the ceiling/stretch-ceiling plane, or the finished slope and ceiling plane will visually crowd each other once the stretch ceiling is installed.
- **A commonly-cited fire-safety "protected strip" beside a window is characterized as providing minimal real protection in practice** (this practitioner's own assessment) — recommends keeping a functional fire extinguisher on the loggia as the actually-effective measure instead, rather than treating the strip's width as a meaningful safety constraint on opening widening. `single-account`, an explicit opinion on a safety topic — flag accordingly, don't adopt as a neutral safety-code fact.
- **When merging a loggia into a room, do not remove the low windowsill-height ledge/nook or relocate the radiator that sat beneath the old window** — stated design-experience caution: occupants consistently end up wanting a small ledge/surface there regardless (a place to set things down), so a fully open floor-to-ceiling result usually ends up "re-acquiring" informal clutter in that spot anyway; leaving the ledge (built as a proper counter/shelf) avoids that outcome while still gaining the light/opening benefit.
