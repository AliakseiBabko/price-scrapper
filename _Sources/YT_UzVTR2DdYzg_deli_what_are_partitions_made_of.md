## Materials — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Three profile-material families named**: metal (aluminum or steel/
  iron), wood-MDF, and combined — this company specifically uses a
  **combined profile**.
- **⚠️ Combined-profile internal construction, a genuinely detailed
  cross-section not recorded elsewhere on this page**: a **finger-jointed/
  glued solid-wood core** (explicitly *not* a single piece of wood — a
  knot-free beam glued together from smaller wood pieces, "сращенный
  брусок") provides rigidity, then the wood core is **faced/wrapped in
  MDF** (medium-density fiberboard, dry-pressed wood fiber) which acts as
  a cushioning/stabilizing outer layer specifically to help the finished
  profile hold its geometry over time (resist warping) — a materially
  more detailed construction rationale than the generic "MDF profile"
  descriptions recorded from other sources.
- **Finished profile dimensions**: cross-section **60×38mm**; maximum
  single-piece profile length **2.8m** (max single-leaf height ≈2.75m).
  For taller openings, the profile can be spliced/joined ("сращивание")
  to reach heights up to **5m** — cited against a real completed project
  with a leaf height of **3.7m** (`ASR-uncertain`: transcript literally
  reads "37 метров," almost certainly a mistranscription of "3.7 метра"
  given the immediately preceding 5m maximum and normal residential
  ceiling heights — treated as 3.7m here, flag if independently
  re-checked against the source video).
- **Two external profile coating/finish options**: a wood-textured film
  ("эко-шпон"/eco-veneer, various colors), or enamel paint (matte or
  glossy, various colors) — a concise two-way finish taxonomy distinct
  from (and more concise than) the veneer-species detail already recorded
  from UNION Doors Furniture's wood-veneer material section.
- **Glass surface-area share and strength claim**: glass makes up roughly **85% of a
  partition's total surface area** — stated explicitly as the reason
  glass-choice quality matters disproportionately to overall satisfaction
  with the finished partition. Tempered safety glass is used, described as
  **"6 times stronger than ordinary glass."** **⚠️ Same-channel numeric
  inconsistency**: the sibling `YT_Z_NUmsCIwWA` video from this exact same
  channel states tempered glass is "7 times stronger than ordinary glass"
  — a minor but real same-channel inconsistency in the specific multiplier
  used, worth flagging rather than silently picking one; both are
  `single-account`/`unverified` marketing-style strength claims with no
  stated test method.
- **Glass finish options**: clear, frosted/matte, painted/lacquered
  ("крашеное") glass, and mirrors.
- **⚠️ Frosted vs. clear glass — a stated selection principle, not just an
  aesthetic choice**: frosted glass is the most commonly used option in
  this company's own projects specifically because it passes up to **85%
  of natural light** while still occluding a direct view through it —
  functions as light-transmission-without-visibility. Clear glass is used
  specifically for the opposite goal: physically separating two spaces
  while preserving visual connection between them — the source names the
  **exact same kitchen-living zoning use case** already recorded from the
  sibling `YT_Z_NUmsCIwWA` case study (cook watching children play in the
  other room while cooking odors stay contained) — here stated as a
  general company-wide design principle rather than one specific project,
  a genuine reinforcement of that earlier case's generalizability.
- **Adjustable-light-blocking options, restated as a general menu**: add
  decorative curtains behind clear glass (can be drawn for full light
  isolation when needed), or choose an inherently opaque option instead
  (painted/lacquered glass, a mirror insert, or a fully blind/opaque
  panel) — consistent with, and a slightly more systematic restatement
  of, the same options already recorded from the sibling case-study video.
