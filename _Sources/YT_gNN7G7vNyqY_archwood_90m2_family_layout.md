## Planning Rules — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Brief-first methodology**: before any layout sketch, gather a full
  household-usage brief (who lives here, appliance habits, hobbies,
  routines) so the plan isn't a generic/abstract layout but fits the
  specific family — explicitly framed as preventing "surprises during
  renovation" once the plan goes to an engineering company for
  perepланировка (replanning) approval. All furniture dimensions
  (folded/unfolded sofa, extended dining table, etc.) should be locked in
  before submitting for approval, not adjusted after.
- **Prepare two structurally different layout options in parallel when a
  family is undecided on a real trade-off** — here, one variant kept the
  children in one combined room, a second split them into two separate
  kids' rooms plus a dedicated adult bedroom, developed side by side
  until the client made a final call late in the process.
- **A "growing" kids' room brief note**: a child approaching school age
  needs the design to anticipate a full desk (laptop/printer capability)
  replacing a toddler-sized table/chair set within roughly 1-2 years —
  brief this explicitly rather than designing only for the child's
  current furniture.
- **Two full bathrooms sell better even when the household doesn't need
  the second one for daily use** — a real-estate/resale consideration
  the architect raised directly to the client as a reason not to simply
  merge/eliminate a second bathroom, even for a family with "a standard
  amount of belongings" and no heavy daily use case for it.
- **Dedicated storage/pantry room for bulky items** (here: two bicycles)
  should be planned explicitly when no other storage location exists for
  them — treated as its own brief requirement, not an afterthought.
- **Structural rough-in constraint carried through every layout variant**:
  two load-bearing columns/pylons partially project into the combined
  kitchen-living space, and a utility revision hatch (ревизионный люк)
  must remain accessible in every version — this ruled out at least one
  otherwise-attractive kitchen layout (see below) because base cabinets
  couldn't be placed along the wall containing the hatch.
- **Kitchen-island layouts can cost more usable floor area than they're
  worth in a mid-size (~90m²) 3-room apartment** — an island variant was
  explicitly tried first, then dropped in favor of a standard linear
  kitchen + freestanding dining table once the island was judged to
  constrain the room's overall usable area too much.
- **A kitchen layout that faces the sink/prep zone into a dark interior
  niche (away from the window) was rejected specifically for the cook's
  comfort** — "unpleasant to stand at a dark sink unable to see out the
  window" — a livability rejection reason distinct from any code/
  structural constraint.
- **Loggia-to-living-space merge, permitting-relevant restrictions
  (spoken directly, general Russian practice, not location-confirmed)**:
  when a loggia is declared a non-residential space (e.g. "кабинет"/
  office) at the permitting stage, sliding glass partition systems
  (rather than a solid wall) must be used at the boundary; no household
  appliances may be shown/declared there; a heated-floor element can be
  installed but should not be declared during the approval filing.
- **Minimum kitchen-niche area for permitting when relocating a kitchen
  into a former hallway/corridor zone**: the speaker states a kitchen
  niche must be at least 6.5 m² to remain classified within the
  apartment's "common/public zone" for approval purposes, when the
  kitchen is being moved out of its original declared location.
- **TV-placement sightline rule for an open kitchen-living-dining plan**:
  place the television so it's visible simultaneously from the loggia
  seating area, the kitchen island/counter, and the dining table — a
  single shared media zone serving every regular use-position in the
  open-plan area, not just the sofa.
- **Semi-transparent slat partition as an entry "dirty zone" divider**:
  for a family with young children tracking sand/dirt in from a play
  area, use a light/daylight-permeable wood-slat construction (rather
  than a solid wall) to visually and psychologically demarcate a
  tiled/washable entry zone (here ~90cm × 150cm) without blocking
  daylight from reaching the entry — explicitly distinguished from a
  solid opaque partition, which the speaker frames as unnecessary and
  worse for daylight.
