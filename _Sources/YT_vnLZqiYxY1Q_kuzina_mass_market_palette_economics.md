## Cost Drivers — ⚠️ the thesis

## Cost Drivers — what the mass market actually offers

- **A person shopping without a designer — for a kitchen, a wardrobe, a sofa — meets the same scenario every time: beige, or grey, or, for the brave, bright red, bright green, "синий как маркер" or "жёлтый как детский пластилин."**
- **In Hoff, Leroy Merlin and comparable mass-market segments most standard fronts are white, grey or wood, plus a few open accent colours.**
- **⚠️ With a figure: per kitchen-trend research she cites, white kitchens have been the mass buyer's most popular choice for years — in various years about 40–45% chose white fronts, followed by greys and wood tones.** *(Research house name ASR-destroyed — see Confidence.)*

## Cost Drivers — ⚠️ the buyer's logic, steelmanned

**This is the most valuable passage in the source, and it is notable because it comes from the practitioner who elsewhere attacks the neutral default hardest.**

**Someone assembling an interior themselves is primarily REDUCING RISK.** They think not only about beauty but about how it will live together later:

- **Will the colour pall?**
- **Will a new sofa suit it?**
- **What happens if the curtains change in five years?**

**⚠️ "Нейтральная палитра кажется безопасной именно потому, что её сложнее испортить. Она легче собирается по частям и меньше зависит от идеального совпадения оттенков."**

> **⚠️ This is the strongest statement of the *pro*-neutral case anywhere in this vault, and it comes from the source that has stated the anti-neutral case four times.** It makes that Perspectives block symmetrical, and it identifies the actual variable: **a neutral palette is the correct answer for an interior assembled incrementally by a non-expert, which is a different question from whether it is the correct answer aesthetically.** `[reframes rather than restates]`

## Cost Drivers — ⚠️ why the shop shows you the opposite

- **A fully neutral display attracts no attention.** Walking through a mall, flipping a catalogue, passing a window — **a white or beige front will not stop you. "Яркий цвет нужен для того, чтобы остановить взгляд."**
- **She names the mechanism: the attention economy.** Merchandising and consumer-psychology research shows **highly saturated colours are read faster and stand out more against competitors**, and she cites a marketing researcher for the claim that **up to 90% of a first impression of a product is bound up with colour.** *(Name ASR-destroyed.)*
- **Bright colour produces an emotional spike so the stand or window is remembered.** Her analogy: **red lipstick in cosmetics advertising — not necessarily the biggest-selling everyday product, but necessary for the manufacturer to capture attention.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ who actually buys the fluorescent option, and why

**"Нельзя сказать, что фломастерные цвета нужны только для экспозиции. Кто-то их всё же покупает, иначе бы их просто не производили."** Partly because people buy what is displayed — **but not only.**

- **⚠️ The real mechanism, and it is a good piece of consumer psychology: the person feels that neutral is too faceless, but cannot assemble a complex palette themselves. So the most accessible way to add colour appears — take one element and make it loud.** A blue sofa, a bright green armchair, an accent wall. **The palette stays simple; they will not or financially cannot strain to assemble complex shades together; so they choose one emotional marker.**
- **⚠️ "Такие покупатели более чувствительны к личности в интерьере. Они ищут решения, которые были бы живыми и небанальными."** The impulse is right. **But mass market offers only two personalisation tools: an accent colour, or decor.**

## Labor / Delivery Model — ⚠️ why complex colour is only sold by designers

- **Complex colouristics are sold by designers — "но не все, а из более дорогих." Designers are interested in the Total Look, in how it all assembles together.**
- **⚠️ Shopping centres do not sell complex colouristics, and her structural explanation is the sharpest part of the source: "её продавать неудобно."**
  - **Hard to show quickly.**
  - **Hard to explain if the salesperson on the floor is not a colour expert.**
  - **⚠️ And it requires accounting for colours belonging to other products the company does not sell.** *"Для них это время и усилия впустую."*

> **The retailer is not withholding complex colour out of conservatism. It is unsellable in their format** — which is why no amount of consumer demand fixes it.

## Design Concept — ⚠️ the showroom-to-flat failure

- **"Эффектный в шоуруме цвет не всегда хорошо живёт в реальном пространстве."**
- **⚠️ Her worked example, and it is the memorable line: the bracing green kitchen arrives in an ordinary Moscow flat with winter light, and suddenly "свежий зелёный выглядит как пластиковая папка для документов."**
- **She attributes the underlying principle to Josef Albers: "цвет почти никогда не воспринимается таким, какой он есть на самом деле." The same shade can change completely next to another material, under evening light, or on a large surface.** `[restatement — the relativity principle is already in the vault from Round 4's `ocoPRS3d8sA` and `yU2zKPpDghM`. **The attribution to Albers is new**, and worth keeping, since the vault held the claim without a source.]`

## Cost Drivers — ⚠️ the missing middle, and the cost figure that is the punchline

**"Между безопасным нейтралом и фломастерным акцентным цветом часто отсутствует промежуточный сегмент сложных оттенков."** No warm smoky greys, no olives, no garnets, no wines.

**⚠️ Why the gap exists — and it connects directly to the sample-testing material this vault holds:**

- **Complex shades sell worse in a fast-choice format.**
- **They rarely produce an instant effect and DO NOT REVEAL THEMSELVES IN A SMALL SAMPLE.**
- **⚠️ "На стенде они выглядят грязными или какими-то пыльными и вообще немножечко странноватыми."**
- **"Сложная палитра начинает работать только внутри интерьера целиком."**

*(This is the retail-economics consequence of the A3-sample and ignoring-accidental-context findings from Round 4's `pFCVO7AuSMw`: a shade that needs a large sample and a whole room to read cannot survive a shop stand — so the market stops stocking it.)*

**⚠️ Why expensive projects therefore look different, and it is NOT a taste claim:**

> **"Нет такой логики, что чем больше у человека денег, тем лучше он чувствует цвет. Просто у них есть доступ к другим инструментам."**

**The tools that access buys**: non-standard sprayed finishes; **testing samples directly on the object**; selecting complex enamel shades; repainting vintage furniture; reupholstering chairs and armchairs. **"И внезапно оказывается, что да, белый может быть льняным, а зелёный может быть изумрудным, а красный может быть винным."**

### ⚠️ The cost delta — the single most actionable figure in the source

> **"Самое ироничное, что технически это не всегда огромная разница в бюджете. Нестандартный цвет эмали увеличивает стоимость фасада на 10, максимум 20%."**

**A non-standard enamel colour adds 10–20% to the cost of a facade.** `single-account`, `unverified`, Russian market, June 2026.

**⚠️ And the conclusion she draws from it, which is the source's real argument:**

> **"Люди думают, что дизайнерский интерьер — это обязательно дорого. Хотя иногда разница начинается с того, что один человек выбирал между шестью готовыми фасадами, а другой — между сотней сложных оттенков."**

**The gap between a mass-market and a designed palette is a choice-set gap before it is a budget gap.**

## Mistakes / Warnings

- Reading the mass-market palette as evidence of what looks good, rather than of what displays and sells well.
- Choosing a saturated accent colour from a showroom without testing it in the room's own light.
- Assuming a designed palette requires a materially larger budget, when the facade delta may be 10–20%.
- Judging a complex shade from a small sample on a stand — the format is what makes it look dirty.
