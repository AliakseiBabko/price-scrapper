## Genuinely New Content (generalizable to any gas appliance / wet-technical-room, not the boiler room specifically)

- **Coaxial vs. vertical flue — a real efficiency tradeoff relevant to any
  gas-fired water heater/boiler, not just a house's dedicated room**: a
  coaxial flue (draws combustion air from outside through the same
  penetration the exhaust uses) is technologically simple and avoids
  cutting a second wall/roof penetration, but **draws combustion air from
  outside rather than from inside the building — explicitly stated as
  worse for equipment efficiency/operation** than a vertical flue drawing
  indoor air. Worth knowing when specifying any gas water heater or
  boiler, including a smaller individual unit that might serve an
  apartment. [source: this transcript]
- **Electrical panel should not be located in the same room as a gas
  boiler / hot-water-preparation equipment — general humidity-avoidance
  principle, not gas-specific**: the source's own stated reasoning is
  general, not boiler-specific — a boiler/water-treatment room runs
  elevated humidity, and electrical panels are vulnerable to damp
  conditions; cites a broader design convention against placing an
  electrical panel near any wet room (bathroom, water-treatment/water-
  prep space). **This adds a general "panel away from wet/humid rooms"
  rule not previously captured on this vault's Electrical pages** (which
  cover wet-room circuit protection — RCD/differential breakers — but not
  a rule about panel *location* avoiding humid rooms specifically).
- **Combustion/ventilation makeup-air gap under a door, 0.2m² minimum
  (worked example: ~2cm gap under a 1m-wide door)** — corroborates this
  vault's existing "never block the makeup-air path under a door" theme
  (already documented for a bathroom exhaust fan in
  [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Doors|Bathroom: Doors]]) with a second,
  independent mechanism: a fuel-burning appliance's combustion air supply
  is just as dependent on an unobstructed door gap as an exhaust fan's
  makeup air is — the same underlying principle (don't seal a door if
  something on the other side needs airflow through the gap) applies to a
  wider category of technical-room appliances than previously recorded.
