## Mistakes / Warnings, Materials

- **Glass pendant light fixtures** — collect dust/grease, lose transparency, sometimes trap dead insects visibly, and are awkward to clean (must remove the shade/globe, access and wipe the interior). Recommends open-bulb or metal-shade fixtures for low-maintenance alternatives, or if glass is wanted, prefer MATTE or textured/fluted glass (shows marks less than clear glossy glass) and avoid closed/complex-shaped glass forms.
- **Glass coffee/side tables** — fingerprints, dust, and cup-ring marks show immediately and constantly, even with daily wiping the surface rarely looks as clean as in a showroom. **⚠️ A real safety point, not just an aesthetic one**: tempered glass still has internal stress points and CAN crack from a sharp/localized impact or from placing something heavy on it in the wrong spot — even rounded-corner glass tables carry breakage risk from an impact (a pet jumping up, an inadvertent heavy object placed down), and sharp corners specifically are a hazard in homes with small children. Recommends reserving glass tables for low-traffic/formal areas rather than everyday family-use rooms.
- **⚠️ White kitchen/bathroom sinks — material practicality ranking, genuinely useful and specific**: white sinks stain from water deposits/grease/dark-dish residue within 1-2 days of use, and scratch even from ordinary plates/cutlery contact. If choosing a white sink anyway, recommends a metal grid/rack placed in the basin bottom (explicitly called an "old Soviet-era" solution) to protect the surface from direct scratching and partially hide staining until cleaned. **Material ranking for practicality**: ceramic sinks are the most practical (easiest to clean, doesn't absorb stains, highly heat-resistant); quartz-composite second-best (denser/less porous, more stain-resistant and durable than plain acrylic-composite, visually reads "expensive"); natural white marble is decorative-only — acid-sensitive (etches from citrus/vinegar contact), absorbs stains quickly, needs delicate/frequent care, not recommended for actual daily kitchen use.
- **No-upper-cabinet kitchens — restates/extends Round 1's `6ttTd82TWPM` critique of this trend**, adding only what's genuinely new: specific storage categories that become hard to place without upper cabinets — mugs, glasses, spices, medications, thermoses/travel mugs, spare jars. Recommends deep pull-out DRAWERS rather than swinging cabinet doors if going without uppers (drawers show full contents at a glance), but explicitly notes even good drawers leave SOME categories (glasses, a first-aid kit, small loose items like straws/lid organizers) genuinely hard to store well without upper cabinets — a storage plan by category is required, not just "add drawers" as a blanket fix. Explicitly warns that skipping upper cabinets tends to push clutter onto the countertop/open shelves/corners over time, undermining the clean minimalist look the choice was originally made for.
- **Open shelving — restates the dust/grease critique (cross-reference, don't duplicate)**, adds the specific moderation rule: fine for items washed/used often (mugs, plates, jars in active rotation) since they get handled/cleaned regularly anyway; risky for pure decor items that just sit and collect dust — if used for decor, commit to wiping at least weekly.
- **⚠️ Open-plan living/kitchen layouts — a stated PERSONAL preference, tag as his opinion not a universal rule**: lists real downsides (loss of privacy — kitchen mess visible from anywhere in the space; sound/smell/activity bleed between zones constantly; harder to find quiet for reading/work/rest; requires the whole space to be tidy essentially all the time, which he calls unrealistic especially with kids or a demanding job). He states he personally prefers closed floor plans for these reasons, framing it explicitly as his own preference rather than a technical mistake to avoid.
- **⚠️ Black faucets/fixtures — SEE THE CONTRADICTION NOTE BELOW, this is one of the two videos reinforcing the anti-black-finish position** — water spots/limescale especially bad with hard water, requires frequent dry-wiping to maintain the matte look. Recommends neutral finishes instead: classic chrome, brass, or stainless steel.
- **Glossy kitchen cabinet fronts — restates the existing critique (cross-reference, don't duplicate)** — explicitly calls the trend "already over" among most designers, states the reflective/space-enlarging benefit doesn't really apply to DARK glossy fronts specifically (no visible lightening/enlarging effect from dark gloss). Recommends matte or semi-matte fronts, ideally with a slight texture or pattern.
- **⚠️ Small-format mosaic tile — restates/extends Round 1's bathroom grout-line-grime finding specifically for kitchen backsplash/floor use too, not just bathroom**: many grout lines collect grease/dust/moisture especially in wet zones, cement grout darkens and is hard to clean thoroughly in volume. Recommends using mosaic ONLY as a small accent (backsplash strip, niche, small floor zone) rather than a large surface, and specifically recommends **EPOXY grout** (more expensive, harder to apply, but doesn't absorb dirt and holds its clean appearance far longer than cement grout) whenever mosaic is used at all.
