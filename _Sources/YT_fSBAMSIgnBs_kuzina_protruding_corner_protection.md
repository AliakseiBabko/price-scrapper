## Design Concept — the problem being solved

- **Why the corner exists in the first place**: choosing an apartment means trading off location, area, transport and schools, and "идеального сочетания таких факторов просто не найти" — so rooms end up with irregular geometry as a residual. She names the common shapes: **a room shaped like a Г («пистолетиком»)**, and Г-, S- or Z-shaped corridors.
- **She classifies it as a defect, not a feature**: "это ошибка архитектуры, с которой нужно бороться."
- **The specific failure mode**: an inward-pointing corner gets dirty and abrades over time — you brush it with clothing, catch it with elbows — making it "довольно непрактичная часть интерьера." Note the abrasion is what drives every option below; the corner is a *wear* problem before it is an aesthetic one.

## Materials — cladding and overlay options, ranked worst to best

**1. Plastic or metal corner protectors («уголки») — her worst option, unconditionally.** "Это просто ужасно," acceptable only in econom-grade work. **She names the real reason they appear: builders use them to avoid having to get the corner straight.** She specifically mocks the idea that matching the protector to the wallpaper colour rescues it — "какого бы цвета ни был уголок, это всё равно самый худший вариант."

**2. Decorative brick with a ragged/torn edge — second worst.** She has "ни разу не видел, чтобы такие кирпичики хорошо выглядели на стене." Worst against a plain painted wall, worse still against wallpaper, worst of all against patterned/floral wallpaper.
- **The one version that works is not a corner treatment at all**: clad the *entire* wall in slate. She names **Ацтека/Aztec** as having slate tile with **dedicated corner elements that wrap the corner**. The ragged edge is what fails, so it only works as a whole-wall treatment.
- **⚠️ And it inverts the problem in a narrow corridor**: instead of the corner wearing, "вы будете изнашиваться сами" — you catch on the rough slate.

**3. Corner pilaster («пилястра») — viable in a classical scheme.** "Не все пилястры это такая уж пошлятина."
- **Material determines whether it actually solves the wear problem**: gypsum, polyurethane, or joinery. **Joinery is the most abrasion-resistant** — you wipe it with a cloth, "примерно как угол шкафа" — but is non-standard, harder to source, harder to design, harder to build and more expensive. **A polyurethane pilaster abrades at about the same rate as the bare corner**, so it buys appearance, not durability.
- **⚠️ The renewal argument, which is the strongest case for a pilaster and applies regardless of material**: with plain wallpaper and no pilaster, refreshing the worn corner means stripping and re-hanging wallpaper across **both** walls. With a pilaster, you repaint the pilaster and leave the wallpaper alone.
- **⚠️ Detail that is easy to miss: the pilaster must stand on a plinth block («каблучок») for the baseboard to run into.** Stated as a general rule — always resolve the junction between the baseboard and whatever element sits on the corner.

**4. Wall panels (joinery).** Listed as a further option in the same family; no separate reasoning given.

**5. Porcelain / large-format tile inserts at the corner.** Uses the tile system's own corner trims, and **the metal tile-edge trim is acceptable here** — "это совсем не то же самое, что поставить просто металлический уголок на углу." The distinction is that the trim belongs to a tile system rather than being applied to a painted wall.

**6. Liquid wallpaper («жидкие обои») — restorable, but she doesn't like it.** Its genuine advantage: the specific worn patch can be repaired and comes back "почти как новое." Her objection is that it is "не очень интерьерный вариант" — better suited to offices, and a dubious finish material in her view.

**7. Paintable wallpaper — she warns against relying on the obvious repair.** Do not count on simply repainting the worn area: **even with leftover paint from the original batch, the touched-up patch will be visible.**

## Planning Rules — constructive options that change the geometry rather than clad it

**8. Chamfer the corner («снять фаску»)** — take the 90° down to a shallower angle, which abrades less than a sharp corner.

**9. Round the corner (a radius)** — softer than a chamfer, but with two conditions:
- **It must be justified within the scheme.** Radii draw attention; a single isolated radius reads as an accident, so there should be others elsewhere in the interior.
- **⚠️ It changes the adjacent room's layout, not just this one.** A corresponding curve appears on the other side of the wall and has to be dealt with there — "ещё не факт, что это получится сделать."
- She cites a worked example she rates highly: a curved wall finished in **MDF strips with painted edges**.

**10. Niches with shelves cut into the wall** — breaks one long corner into short segments, each of which abrades less. **The mechanism she gives is behavioural, not geometric**: "психологически вы будете бояться снести что-то с этих полок," so you keep your distance without thinking about it.

**11. Place an obstacle in front of the corner** — a plant stand, for instance. You brush the obstacle instead of the corner, or subconsciously steer around the whole zone. **She states its limit plainly: it doesn't work with many children or dogs** (dogs eat the plants).

**12. Build a column beside the corner** — suits classical and neoclassical schemes; **not viable in a narrow corridor.** **Her historical analogy is the mechanism**: the corner guard stones of old Moscow, set at building corners so carriages would strike the stone rather than the building.

**13. ⚠️ Remove the inward corner entirely by dividing the room — her own default.** "Я в своих проектах обычно стараюсь делать именно так."
- Instead of keeping a pistol-shaped room, split it into two.
- **The method is a cased opening, sized deliberately wide**: "не такой проём, который размером с дверь, а большой широкий, размером с то пространство, которое у вас есть" — you give up only the width the architrave needs.
- **Two stated benefits beyond losing the corner**: it lets you **change wall colour between the two parts** while the whole remains visually connected and fully open; and it "исправляет огрехи той архитектуры, которая вам досталась."

## Mistakes / Warnings

- **Accepting a builder's plastic/metal corner protector as a finish.** It is there to avoid making the corner straight.
- **Cladding only the corner in ragged-edge decorative brick or slate** — it reads badly against every adjacent finish, and in a narrow corridor it transfers the abrasion to you.
- **Specifying a polyurethane pilaster expecting durability** — it wears at roughly the corner's own rate.
- **Forgetting the baseboard junction** at whatever element lands on the corner (hence the pilaster's plinth block).
- **Planning to touch up paintable wallpaper in the worn patch** — the repair shows even with the original paint.
- **Adding a single radius with no other curves in the scheme**, and not checking what the curve does to the room on the other side of the wall.
