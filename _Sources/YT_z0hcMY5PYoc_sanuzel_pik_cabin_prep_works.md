## ⚠️ The drainage arithmetic — the strongest single item in the video

He wants the sewer entry as low as possible because a shower tray is planned:
«каждый сантиметр на счету». **The whole calculation is stated, and it closes:**

| Step | Figure |
| :--- | :--- |
| Top of the existing socket above the floor slab, untouched | **just over 12 cm** |
| Shower position away from the stack | **3 m** |
| Fall required over that run (at the 3 cm/m he uses elsewhere) | **9 cm** |
| → Tray height if nothing is done | **"больше 21 см" — 12 + 9, and he calls it многовато** |
| Gained by pressing the stack pipe down at the per-floor compensator | **5 cm** |
| **Final tray height achieved** | **"чуть больше 16 см"** |

`arithmetic-exact`: 12 + 9 = 21, and 21 − 5 = 16. **The stated figures reconcile
exactly**, which is what makes this usable as a worked example rather than an
anecdote.

**The technique that bought the 5 cm is specific and reusable**: every floor's
stack carries a compensator, and he **pressed down on the pipe to drop the socket
below its as-found position**. He then reworked the second stack the same way,
lowering it until the tee came out at the height a **wall-hung WC** needs.

> **⚠️ And note the honest limit:** «ниже уже некуда» — he says he ran out of
> room, and 16 cm is what a shower tray costs in this flat. It is not a claim
> that a flush tray is achievable.

## Blockwork, and a movement joint most sources skip

- **Cut back excess plaster and screed first** so the new blocks bear **directly on the structural wall and floor slab**, not on a finish layer.
- **Grind off mortar splashes across the floor** before the levelling screed — they defeat adhesion. His own process correction, recorded against himself: he normally grinds only the future wall lines first and the main area later, did the lot up front here, and had to redo it because the floor was dirtied again during the work.
- **Fast-setting compound for the first course**, so the wall can continue the same day rather than waiting on drying. The first course «задаёт всё направление» and is set with a laser plane builder.
- **⚠️ Each course is tied to the concrete wall with a length of stud** («шпилька») set into a pre-drilled hole and bedded into the block joint — **and no adhesive is applied between the wall and the blocks.** A deliberate gap is left, later **filled with foam**.
  > The mechanism, stated: the foam **bonds the masonry to the wall while remaining a damper layer** for movement, because the two materials expand differently with temperature. — Василий
- **The plaster coat is given the same treatment** — a damper separating it from the walls around the perimeter, «по аналогии с пенным швом». ⚠️ He flags this one as his own reasoning rather than received practice: «мне кажется, это логичной операцией… ничего не будет давить ни на стены, ни на штукатурку, соответственно и трещины не появятся». Carry it as a practitioner's rationale, not a rule.

## Plaster on aerated block

- **A dedicated aerated-block primer**, distinguished from ordinary primer by **a higher polymer content** for strongly absorbent substrates. The colour is just an added pigment for coverage control. Stated purpose: stop the plaster **losing its water too fast**, which «приведёт к порче материала».
- **Gypsum plaster is the right choice on aerated block** because **its grade strength does not exceed the block's strength**, so adhesion is maximal — a materials-compatibility argument rather than a preference.
- **⚠️ But gypsum plaster under tile must be treated with acrylic waterproofing afterwards** — stated flatly and referred out to his other videos.
- **Top-floor detail**: with only the roof above, he temporarily dismantled the vent duct to plaster behind it and **fitted a ventilation valve** so the stack ventilation was neither interrupted nor filled with debris. On any other floor he says he would simply have added an extra plaster beacon instead.
- **Door openings are cut to the design size after plastering** — «мне так удобнее и позиционирование точнее».

## Floor build-up

- Underfloor heating **cable bought as mats but stripped off the mesh** and bonded with **thermal adhesive**, because laying the mesh is awkward.
- **Panel construction has significant level differences** — thin self-levelling screed over the cable, with **datum markers («реперные маячки») set by laser and trimmed to height**: the top of the marker shows the pour level and reads back where you over- or under-poured. He says he uses them constantly.
- **The technical project is drawn after levelling, from measured dimensions**, and drives the material take-off and the work itself. Corroborates his modelling method in [[_Sources/YT_M8EyOCrm0tw_sanuzel_water_inlet_unit_assembly|the П-44Т inlet-node video]] and is the subject of his own separate technical-project video.
- **Pipe lengths are cut from the project** so long pipes aren't bought and wasted — a small, real material-cost point.

## Water supply

- **Italian silicone sealing tape ("Facot")** — **this is the follow-up verdict on the tape he was trialling for the first time seven months earlier**: «мне она нравится, работать легко и очищается намного быстрее». Same-channel product follow-through, which is rare enough to be worth citing as such.
- **Stainless steel press-fit tube** again for the node interconnections. **Practical clash detail: the filter bowls had to come off to get the press tool in**, then went back on.
- Distribution in **PEX («сшитый полиэтилен») with a sliding sleeve** («надвижная гильза»), **16 mm, and 20 mm for the shower system**.
- **⚠️ Pressure test at 10 atm with a test pump, and he says why working pressure is not a test**: «конечно, при рабочем давлении это не проверка». **The test found two leaks — a plug and a threaded joint.** He rebuilt the section, replaced the plug and re-ran it clean. Second video in a row where he publishes his own failed joints.

## Framing and sheathing

- **Structural aluminium profile (Russian-made) instead of standard drywall profile** for the false wall and the new partition between bath and WC, and he demonstrates its rigidity on camera against ordinary GK profile.
- **The WC installation frame is bracketed into that profile through a drilling template**, which **holds the frame in the same plane as the carcass and multiplies its fixing points** — and in that case **the frame's own supplied fasteners can be omitted**.
- **Access hatch leaves fabricated from the same profile** using dedicated inserts — **two hatches: one to service the water-preparation node, one for storage.**
- **Sheathed in acoustic gypsum board** (Knauf Sapphire — dense, heavy, fire- and moisture-resistant), cut with a **circular saw rather than a knife for accuracy**, service holes cut with a hole saw.
- **⚠️ Two layers of board, and the trigger is tile format**: «зашивать буду на два слоя, так рекомендуется делать, если планируется плитка больших размеров» — here **1200 × 600 mm** tile.

## Shower tray — the method, and a near-miss

**His standard build-up, «поддоны я почти всегда делаю именно таким подходом»:**

1. Bulk the height out first in **block or brick**.
2. Then a **fast-setting screed** to level.
3. Then the finishing screed carrying the **fall («разуклонка»)** — perimeter held one level **above** the drain, with a sloped "envelope" («конверт») inside it.

**Stated reason for the fast-setting materials**: they let waterproofing and tile follow **within about a day**, compressing the wet-work critical path.

- **Formwork from a piece of plasterboard wrapped in tape so nothing sticks**, propped with brick. Struck after 2 days, over a weekend.
- **⚠️ The formwork began to spread under the weight of the screed on camera** — «я не сразу увидел… чуть не встрял на полную переделку, ведь состав реально быстро схватывается». He had to prop it in a hurry. **The speed that makes the method attractive is also what makes a formwork failure unrecoverable**, and he shows the contrast in one shot: where he had just finished it was still workable, where he started it was already stone.
- **Beacons set on screw heads with clamps («клеймеры»)**: screws driven to height, the clamp bent around the beacon and pushed onto the screw head, pulling the two together — a fast way to set beacons and pour immediately.
- The fall was completed **in one day** using a fast-setting Litokol product.
- **The drain is built into the wall** («трап встроенный в стену») — he calls it an interesting solution and expects it to look good; no performance claim made.

## Site protection

**⚠️ Tape choice is a named, non-obvious risk.** The entrance door had already been
replaced, and rather than accept liability for damaging it he masked it — **with
PVC tape, explicitly not paper masking tape**. His stated criteria: holds for a
long time, **removes without problems**, is unbothered by damp, and mortar does
not stick to it. «Если кто-то из вас защищал двери и окна бумажным скотчем, в
конце ремонта сталкивались с неприятным сюрпризом — имейте в виду, скотч имеет
значение.» He says he spent a long time finding a good one.
