## Design Concept — ⚠️ what colour block actually is

**She starts by rejecting the standard definition.** Wikipedia defines colour blocking as **combining complementary colours — those opposite on the wheel — for interesting optical effects.** **⚠️ "Это немного странно, потому что сочетать можно любые цвета, не только дополнительные."**

**⚠️ Her own definition, which is the usable one — three parameters, all of which must hold:**

1. **Large colour patches are combined.**
2. **There is contrast BY COLOUR.**
3. **⚠️ Plane matters — "плоскость важнее объёма."**

**On the second**: Itten distinguishes seven colour contrasts, and **contrast by colour — literally colour-against-colour — is the simplest: a comparison of several different pigments, e.g. green and orange, which the eye easily identifies as different colours. Two different colours put side by side. That is colour block.**

## Design Concept — origins, and why the received story is probably wrong

**Two versions, and she argues against the popular one.**

**The less common version — the impressionists and post-impressionists** first explored what optical effect could be achieved by laying complementary colours side by side on a canvas; she names **Georges Seurat** and pointillism. **⚠️ Her analogy: it is like colour on a monitor — one pixel is one colour, one brushstroke is one pure colour, and they mix in the eye.** **But she immediately undercuts it: the impressionists are the second half of the 19th century, while planes flooded with colour "как детская раскраска" plus colour contrast appear far earlier — in icon painting, in medieval stained glass, and in the decorative arts of many peoples.**

**The common version — colour block came to interiors from the fashion catwalk**: Mondrian used colour blocking first in painting; Mondrian inspired **Yves Saint Laurent**; and through YSL it entered contemporary interiors.

- **Mondrian** — Dutch, first half of the 20th century, inventor of geometric abstraction (neoplasticism). **Abstract compositions of rectangles and squares filled with local tones of the base colours — red, yellow, blue — separated by a black contour.** **⚠️ And a detail that explains the parallel: he held that light should be flat.**
- **⚠️ The idea underneath, which she explains rather than name-drops**: a painter can depict something resembling reality — draw a tree, and it becomes the world-tree, a symbol of world order. **Mondrian asks why, when you can draw simply a vertical and a horizontal and convey the whole picture of the world. For him the squares rest on the idea that the world can be expressed by two basic concepts: the horizontal as the force-line of the earth, and the vertical originating in the centre of the sun.**

**⚠️ Her counter-argument, and it is a genuine piece of reasoning rather than trivia:**

- **Even granting Mondrian is the father of colour blocking, that does not establish that the technique reached *interiors* via fashion.**
- **Mondrian belonged to De Stijl alongside Theo van Doesburg and Gerrit Rietveld** — **"роль Ритвельда в истории архитектуры вполне сопоставима с ролью Сен-Лорана в мире моды."**
- **Van Doesburg** — painter, typographer, poet and architect — **wanted to teach at the Bauhaus and moved to Weimar in 1922 specifically for it. He met director Walter Gropius, who was impressed but did not hire him. He stayed living near the Bauhaus and influenced its students anyway.**
- **Both van Doesburg and Rietveld have interiors using colour blocking.**
- **⚠️ Her preferred lineage: "Мондриан повлиял на Баухаус, и оттуда color blocking пришёл в интерьеры домов — это было бы вернее, чем говорить, что он пришёл из мира моды."**
- **⚠️ But her honest conclusion is that no lineage is needed: the idea of filling a plane with colour "просто витала в воздухе."** **Malevich** developed his own abstract style on geometric elements filled with flat colour, **also visited the Bauhaus and knew Gropius**; **El Lissitzky's** work follows the same principle and **also influenced Gropius and the Bauhaus.** **And other modernists used it in interiors directly — Le Corbusier and Charlotte Perriand**, with the same three parameters.
- **⚠️ Her best worked historical example: Alvar Aalto, 1929, a hospital interior.** He wanted a more humane and comfortable atmosphere in the common areas by means of saturated colour — **hence the yellow rubberised floor in the corridor and on the stairs, intended to recall the sun, with the other primaries, red and blue, in the dining hall.**

## Design Concept — the two revivals, and why now

- **⚠️ The 1960s–70s revival was not the same technique.** **Flat planes and right angles disappeared; strange outlines appeared — rainbow-like, several colours folding into one trajectory; and new furniture forms meant primary-colour upholstery read nothing like it had at the Bauhaus.** **"Сейчас мода на 70-е, именно поэтому color blocking снова вызывает интерес."**
- **The 1980s symbol was the Memphis group**, and she parallels them with 1960s pop — **both wanted to slap public taste**, and both contain colour block. **Memphis adds specific forms, often the circle and semicircle**, and in contemporary readings **pastel tones "как будто бы у детских конфеток или леденцов."** Memphis remains very popular — *"если интерьер у вас выглядит как пряничный домик, то это про них."*
- **⚠️ And an acute observation about what Memphis interiors do to space: note the absence of volume, "как будто бы этот интерьер просто распечатали на белом листочке на принтере, потом раскрасили цветными фломастерами." They are very fused — they do not separate the plane of the walls from the plane of the ceiling, the furniture, textile and decor as distinct elements. "Такое ощущение, что ты находишься внутри картины."**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ strengths and weaknesses, stated plainly

**Strengths:**

- **It easily changes proportions.**
- **The interiors come out bold and joyful.**
- **It is easy and quick to execute.**
- **⚠️ It easily creates historical references — get the colours right and you can nod to the 1920s, 60s, 70s or 80s fairly simply.**

**Weaknesses:**

- **⚠️ It takes all the attention — "он как бы ослепляет зрителя и отвлекает от любых других приёмов," so in many interiors colour block is the only technique everything rests on.**
- **⚠️ Positive though such interiors are, they are hard to call refined: there is no place for small detail, because colour block works with large volumes and large brushstrokes.**
- **⚠️ It often fragments an interior — "буквально разрывает его на части," and the interior starts to look garish.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ the two theoretical rules

**Scale of the patch:**

> **⚠️ "Лучше использовать три ярких пятна, три цвета, чем восемь цветов в мелких элементах."** The colour and the patch must read easily — **elements of one colour are grouped TOGETHER.** **"Здесь нет задачи размазать или как-то распределить цвета по интерьеру."**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **⚠️ This is a scoped REVERSAL of a rule this vault holds, and it must be recorded as such rather than filed alongside it.** The vault carries «поддержка цвета» from `yU2zKPpDghM` — distribute an accent colour to other parts of the room — and Round 5's `uRK4OPR4dak` qualifies it further (support with varied shades, not identical repeats). **Colour block does the opposite on purpose: consolidate one colour into one large readable patch and do not distribute it at all.** **The two are not in conflict; they are different techniques with opposite rules, and mixing them yields neither.**

**Contrast:**

- **The more contrasting the colours, the stronger their influence.**
- **⚠️ The strongest colour contrast comes from the primaries. Intensity — and therefore contrast — declines as a colour moves away from the centre of the wheel: orange, violet and green give less colour contrast than the primaries.**
- **Check the specific shades — whether they get along.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ a staged adoption path

**"Столкновение цветов — это очень яркий приём. Лучше не экспериментировать сразу со всем имеющимся объёмом, а сначала прислушаться к себе" — whether you would actually be content to live with and look at these blocks every day.**

**Her recommended order: start with transit zones — a picture and curtains; then bathrooms; then gradually bedrooms. "Если вас не устроит колорблок в коридоре, то вы не будете делать его в жилой комнате."**

- **⚠️ And an explicit warning against the obvious cheap test: "не советую начинать с мелких предметов."** Buy a couple of bright vases or cushions, like them, and conclude you are ready for colour blocking — **and you will be wrong, "потому что однотонные цвета в больших масштабах производят совершенно другой эффект."** **A bathroom curtain works as a starting point if it is large enough.**
- **⚠️ And: do not start with a children's room. "Я вообще не очень понимаю, почему считается, что color block — это детский приём. Если он не устраивает вас в гостиной, вряд ли он устроит ваших детей в их комнате."**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ coloured outlines, the low-commitment version

**"Цветные обводки — это небольшой, но действенный приём, более интересный, чем однотонные подушки."** Not yet colour blocking, but close. Where to put them:

- **Inside a door opening, or along the contour of doors.**
- **Coloured skirtings.**
- **Contrasting picture frames, or mounts.**
- **Coloured window frames.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ flat painted shapes on walls: when it fails, and four cases where it works

**She is blunt about the failure mode.** Painting walls in flat squares **"может выглядеть по-настоящему убого"** — as though people were trying out different colours but tried very hard to make a square, **or as though they ran out of money for a full bucket of paint and bought up all the shop's leftovers.** Usually what she dislikes is the colour combination and **the combination of their scales.** And there are **wild neon versions "которые очень хочется развидеть" — kitsch in the bad sense, "не смешно, а просто как-то брезгливо."**

**⚠️ The four cases where flat wall painting is justified:**

1. **A very carefully-worked composition — and it is especially good in spaces of unusual shape**, where a roof slope or broken walls participate in the whole.
2. **Complex ones that read more like wall painting — they work as a large abstract picture.**
3. **⚠️ Where the colour block is only part of the wall's decor and forms a single whole with a picture or other wall decor — "картина как бы выплёскивается из рамы и расширяется на всю стену."**
4. **Dividing a wall into two parts without forming geometric figures.** `[the one restatement — she cross-references her own wall-division video, held in this vault as `lptrnXyf0as`]` **She particularly likes a thin graphic stripe, and skirtings and architraves chosen to enter the scheme correctly. With a vertical division, asymmetry works well.**

**Two further placement notes:**

- **⚠️ One variant is continuing a colour patch over onto the ceiling, or from one wall onto another — and the same principle applies to doors, and along walls and built-in furniture.**
- **⚠️ A specific failure worth quoting: rectangles painted on walls that ALSO have mouldings read as cheap. "Получается такая стереоскопическая модель: один глаз видит молдинги, другой глаз видит цветовые пятна — и кое-где они совпадают, кое-где идут параллельно, а кое-где наслаиваются друг на друга."**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ where else it can go, and how to hold it together

- **Colour block is associated with an accent wall, but can be applied anywhere — on the floor, on the ceiling, on large upholstered furniture, on case furniture, and on curtains.**
- **⚠️ Choose ONE dominant colour** — her example is red — **and use the rest as supplementary.**
- **Bright colour blocks as furniture against neutral walls works.** In her example, white walls, **and she draws attention to the black outline: "как чёрный держит этот интерьер вместе, как складывает его воедино."**
- **⚠️ Dilute bright patches with more neutral ones — "тогда интерьер меньше похож на коробку с карандашами и больше похож на дом."**
- **⚠️ And the principle behind the black outline, attributed to Itten**: *(attribution garbled in the ASR and recorded as probable)* — **when each colour is separated from the others by white or black stripes, the individual character of each colour becomes more pronounced and their mutual influence decreases.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ the test that distinguishes colour block from merely bright walls

**Her best worked comparison, and it is what makes the three-parameter definition operational.** Two interiors, both with bright emerald walls:

| | **Interior 1** | **Interior 2** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Large colour patches?** | Yes | Yes |
| **⚠️ Contrast by colour?** | **NO — bright green combined with bright metal. No pronounced colour contrast** | **YES — bright green walls, YELLOW floors, bright BLUE chairs** |
| **Plane over volume?** | Less so | **⚠️ Reads flatter; light-and-shade matters less for it** |
| **Verdict** | Bright plain walls | **Colour blocking** |

**Her framing of the second: the combination "направлено на то, чтобы мы сложили эти цвета вместе и смотрели, как они воздействуют на нас вместе."**

- **⚠️ And a related case: plain walls can still be colour block if they collide with the colour of the FLOOR** — her example also has bright wall paintings in the orange zone. **⚠️ Through an opening you can play the difference of colour schemes between adjoining rooms attractively.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ where colour block stops and wall painting begins

**She runs a gradient of four examples, which is a useful boundary test:**

1. **Plain walls** — you simply enjoy and compare the colours. **Colour block.**
2. **A composition in which a picture participates.** Still colour block.
3. **Stripes appear** — now closer to abstract painting.
4. **Recognisable forms — mountains, sky, clouds. "Это уже точно не color block, это уже точно роспись."**

**⚠️ The rule that falls out: "если вы делаете не квадраты, то есть риск заиграться формой. Color block — это больше про цвет, формы должны быть простыми и беспредметными."** And: **not too many colours; the patches must be large and easily read; it is about colour, not form.**

*(Her final example is flat painting over mouldings in Memphis style, where the room is treated as a set of planes to draw on. She calls that an artwork rather than an interior — "важно здесь не функция, а то, что мы находимся именно внутри произведения" — and points to her deconstructivism video.)*

## Rules / Heuristics — her three closing points

1. **Start small, with what is easy to redo.**
2. **You can work with more than walls.**
3. **The scale of the patch matters, and composition matters generally.**

**"Не используйте колор блок, если вы не уверены, что вам это нужно — не все хотят каждый вечер возвращаться в коробку с карандашами. Но это весело."**
