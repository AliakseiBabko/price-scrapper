## Planning Rules — Durable Facts & Rules (Design-Process Methodology)

- **Staged design-project workflow, in order**: (1) client brief
  (household members, pets, color/style preferences); (2) a shared
  group chat with the assigned designer, a design-team lead, and the
  client (optionally including the client's own contractor specifically
  for technical Q&A, but not as a permanent chat member — reasoned as
  too disruptive to a contractor's own separate active job chats); (3)
  floor-plan/layout stage; (4) a "concept collage" stage; (5) full
  photorealistic visualization + technical drawings; (6) production
  (site supervision, procurement/furniture sourcing).
- **Floor-plan iteration convention**: a designer typically produces
  2-5 initial layout variants based on the apartment's area and window
  count, testing room-size trade-offs and household-scenario variations
  (e.g. "what if guests come over"); client feedback typically
  converges to a final approved layout within **~2-4 rounds**, with the
  whole approval stage taking **~1-2 weeks**.
- **⚠️ Explicit rejection of taping/drawing wall outlines on the floor
  to "feel" a layout before committing** — argued to give no genuine
  spatial sense (no real walls, no furniture, no true clearance
  experience) and to be a low-value, time-wasting exercise; already-
  standardized clearance/opening-width norms (corridor width, door
  clearance) used by trained designers are the reliable substitute for
  this ad hoc physical test.
- **"Concept collage" staging rationale**: build a rough moodboard-
  style collage (photos of finished interiors, furniture, materials)
  before committing to a full photorealistic 3D visualization —
  revising an already-completed render is extremely costly/time-
  consuming, so the cheap collage step confirms client and designer are
  picturing the same direction *before* that expensive step, avoiding
  a costly rework cycle later.
- **⚠️ Visualization's functional role beyond aesthetics — a real
  miscommunication-reduction mechanism, not just a "picture for the
  client"**: without a rendered visualization, client, contractor, and
  on-site workers each tend to imagine a different finished result from
  drawings alone, causing repeated on-site confusion, redone work, and
  real time/cost loss for both the contractor and the client. Workers
  reportedly understand and retain a rendered image far more reliably
  than a technical drawing alone.
- **Three distinct construction-documentation types, each serving a
  different communication role**: чертежи (precise plan-view drawings
  — e.g. exact outlet distance from a wall); развертки (wall-elevation
  "unrolled" views, used to check where multiple surfaces/fixtures
  align on a single wall); and the photorealistic visualization itself
  — treated as complementary, not redundant, documentation layers.
- **⚠️ Refusal to provide free on-site cost estimates from an unfinished
  apartment, with a stated mechanism-based reason**: a rough-shell
  apartment is functionally near-identical ("a clone") to every other
  same-area unit until a finished design project confirms actual
  engineering specs — electrical panel/metering system choice and
  flooring choice specifically can vary in cost by "tens of times"
  (an order of magnitude) depending on the final spec, making an
  on-site verbal quote for an unspecified apartment effectively a quote
  for something not yet real ("виртуального"). Framed as a practice
  only unqualified operators ("шарлатаны") still do.
- **⚠️ Candid practitioner self-admission of an early-career pricing
  bias**: in his early career (when he had no completed portfolio
  objects), he would informally price a job partly based on a
  prospective client's visible wealth signals (their clothing) —
  offered explicitly as a past, now-abandoned practice, contrasted with
  his current standardized project-based pricing.
- **Stated real full design-project timeline: ~2-3 months total**, from
  brief through finished drawings and visualization, split roughly
  evenly between the designer's own active working time (~1-2 months)
  and the client's own review/approval turnaround time (~1-2 months) —
  offered as a direct rebuttal to client requests for a 2-week design
  project.
- **Client-site-visit access gated by financial commitment**: site
  tours for prospective clients are now offered only to those who have
  already paid for a design project (or equivalent commitment) —
  reasoned as both a seriousness filter and a practical necessity once
  the company had enough active objects that unlimited free tours
  would take staff time away from paid work; contrasted with his
  earlier-career practice of unlimited free tours when he had few or no
  objects to show.
