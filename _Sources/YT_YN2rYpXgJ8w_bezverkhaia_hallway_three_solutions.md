## Hallway — the three-zone split

Her first solution is zoning, against the failure mode of "всё свалено в одну кучу." The value here is that each zone comes with a stated reason, not just a list:

- **Zone 1 — outerwear.** Jackets, coats, headwear, scarves. **⚠️ Explicitly not "один какой-то несчастный хилый крючок" but a system** — panels with many hooks, or rails. **The stated reasons are three, and two are about the garments rather than the tidiness**: every family member *and guests* get their own place; **coats do not pile on top of each other, so they ventilate better; and they do not crease.**
- **Zone 2 — footwear**, and the scope matters: **all the shoes, not only this season's.** Forms she names: an open low shelf or bench for shoes in current use, plus a closed cabinet, chest or drawered bench for rarely-used pairs. **The stated payoff: the floor stays clear, holding only the pairs actually in rotation.**
- **Zone 3 — accessories**, i.e. the things always taken when leaving: keys, wallet, sunglasses, masks, gloves. **A small shallow shelf in plain sight**, plus trays, baskets or dishes for sorting, **and a hook for a bag or backpack.** **⚠️ The stated payoff is the morning-panic case**: no searching, no "did I take it?" re-checking — she frames the return as saved nerves and time rather than saved space.

## Hallway — vertical storage

Against the constraint she names directly — "нехватка места, особенно в маленьких прихожих. А наши квартиры, к сожалению, в основном маленькие."

- **⚠️ The principle: do not consume valuable floor area; use the vertical surfaces.**
- **Perforated panels (перфопанели)** taking hooks, small shelves and baskets — **her argument for them is reconfigurability**: the layout can be changed indefinitely as needs change, which she calls the most flexible option, suited to contemporary and Scandinavian interiors.
- **Over-door hanging organisers** — she scopes these to small accessories, shoe-care products and gloves specifically.
- **⚠️ Ceiling-height shelves for seasonal items** in boxes or baskets — "один раз в сезон залезли, поменяли." Recorded because the access frequency is what justifies the height, and that reasoning is the reusable part.
- **The stated effect: it frees the floor visually**, which is the point in a small hallway, as distinct from merely adding capacity.

## Hallway — mirror plus lighting, treated as one decision

She calls this the most important detail in a hallway, and the pairing is the substance:

- **⚠️ The mirror must be large enough to see yourself full-length, shoes to head.** Two functions, and she names both: checking appearance, **and visually enlarging what is usually a cramped hallway** — a small hallway with a large mirror always reads roomier and brighter.
- **⚠️ Reject a single central ceiling fixture.** The mechanism: **lighting from directly above casts deep, unflattering shadows on the face.**
- **Her prescribed combination**: **perimeter downlights across the ceiling for even ambient light, plus a wall sconce or fitting beside or above the mirror.**
- **⚠️ The framing worth keeping is the reference she gives it: shadowless facial lighting, "как у профессиональных гримёров"** — the dressing-room standard, where you see yourself accurately.
- Her stated payoff is behavioural rather than technical: you leave the house without unpleasant surprises once you reach daylight.
