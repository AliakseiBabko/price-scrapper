## Family Requirements / Preferences — A Reusable Client-Intake Tool

- **⚠️ Their questionnaire technique, the most directly reusable item in the source.** Rather than assuming practicality is the default priority, they ask the client to score it:
  1. **Every family member individually rates themselves on an aesthetics-vs-practicality scale** (1 to 10), because household members differ.
  2. **The client lists the zones in descending priority for practicality, and separately in descending priority for aesthetics.** Worked example: "the kitchen must be maximally hard-wearing," while in the bedroom a stone headboard or hard bed edges are acceptable because "главное, чтобы было красивенько."
- **⚠️ Their own finding from running this: roughly half their clients, or more, are *not* practicality-maximalists.** (Explicitly an impression, not a counted statistic.) Their conclusion: "мы никогда не полагаем в своей работе практичность как истину в последней инстанции."
- Worked consequences: for a practicality-leaning client's study, fit everything needed into the cabinet and don't labour over an elegant front-division; for an aesthetics-leaning client's make-up area, choose the beautiful chair over the softer, more comfortable one — knowingly, because the questionnaire already established that trade.

## Planning Rules — The Psychological Framing

- **⚠️ No 100%-practical material exists, and a designer should not pretend otherwise.** "Не нужно обманывать заказчиков, что существуют такие идеальные материалы… единороги." Every material ages: walls get scuffed and need washing, parquet dents under heavy furniture, laminate moves, quartz-vinyl is plastic.
- **⚠️ People over-weight low-probability events and buy permanent ugliness to insure against them.** Named examples: fitting a stretch ceiling against a hypothetical flood; cladding the entrance-door reveals in MDF against a hypothetical neighbour carrying a piano through. The result is that you look at the ugly mitigation every day for a decade and the event never comes. The framing they offer against this: **wabi-sabi** ("ничто не вечно, ничто не закончено, ничто не совершенно" — the philosophy, not its visual clichés), **kintsugi** (a repair is highlighted in contrasting gold rather than hidden, because the crack is part of the object's history), and **shikata ga nai** (don't agonise over what you cannot influence).

## Materials / Mistakes — The Practicality Audit

**Underrated as practical (their position):**

- **⚠️ Shadow-gap profile at the floor/wall junction ("теневой профиль"), with a real number.** The standard objection — that the gap fills with dust you can never reach — they call a myth. **A robot vacuum's side brushes clear it; and a standard mop with its cloth is 10–15 mm high, so a 20 mm shadow profile clears a mop comfortably** even if you prefer a bulky cloth. They "твёрдо настаивают" that this is the most practical floor/wall junction available.
- **⚠️ And the inverse: a surface-mounted skirting (накладной плинтус) is the *least* practical solution** — the protruding top face accumulates a thick dust layer, plastic attracts it, and weekly wiping smears it up the wall and can abrade a low-durability paint.
- **⚠️ A useful third distinction: they consider a *concealed/flush* plinth impractical too.** MDF in enamel chips exactly like a wall, you will not match the touch-up colour, and you mop against it anyway — "по сути это такая же стена." So their position is **shadow gap yes, flush MDF plinth no** — a finer split than the general enthusiasm for "hidden plinths" elsewhere.
- **Microcement is fine in wet rooms and even public spaces**, provided the substrate preparation is right — and **preparation differs between floor and wall**, so it must be discussed with the applicator specifically.
- **⚠️ Worktop with no overhang, flush with the fronts — and they ran an experiment to settle the objection.** Cabinetmakers routinely object that a 2 cm overhang protects the fronts from spilled water. **They poured a large quantity of water onto an overhanging worktop and it wrapped around the overhang and ran straight down the fronts anyway** — so the protective argument fails. Their additional objections: an overhang reads like a depth error, and **you strike your hand on it every time you grab a handle**, which during active cooking is not merely impractical but painful. Their aim is a worktop flush with the fronts.

**Overrated / rejected (their position):**

- **⚠️ Window reveals: just paint them.** A reveal is not a traffic zone, so ordinary paint is fine — pick a higher wash-durability class if the window is often open and there is no supply ventilation. Plastic overlays are "уродливые," don't materially help, and intrude visually.
- **⚠️ Entrance-door reveals: paint too**, in a higher wear class. Their argument is asymmetric-repair: **damaged MDF has to be replaced; damaged paint is touched up.** If a client insists on something tougher: **microcement, and specifically the coarse-fraction kind** — they tried to trim a slight excess at a corner with a knife on site and could not, because it sets that hard. (Fine-fraction microcement is the paint-like one.) They also name a newer material: **slaked lime with marble chip.** If MDF is genuinely wanted, use a **thin frame** around the reveal rather than wrapping the whole corner, optionally standing a couple of millimetres proud, in the wall colour or in veneer or stone.
- **⚠️ Tile corner trims (уголки) are never justified.** A properly mitred 45° porcelain corner with matching grout is no weaker; if you take a hammer to it the trim chips too. **And in a wet zone the trim collects mould, and eventually peels off.** Corroborates the same practitioner's position in `avRNMkNdOBs` and `XGI6FS2ZdCc`.
- **⚠️ Stretch ceilings: never — and their flood argument is that the flood argument fails.** A contemporary interior has many light penetrations through the membrane, so water finds its way through anyway. Their practical objection is different and more concrete: **the membrane is installed before the last fit-out trades arrive, and one careless movement by a furniture fitter puts a hole in it**, after which you are patching or replacing the whole sheet. Cats, likewise. See routing — this is a genuine disagreement with other sources in this vault.
