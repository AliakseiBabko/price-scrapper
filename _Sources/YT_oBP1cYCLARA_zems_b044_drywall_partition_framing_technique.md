## Walls / Ceilings

- **Double-track ("сдвоенная направляющая") vertical profile choice for
  a load-bearing/high-traffic partition, explicit cost/rigidity
  tradeoff**: chosen over a single profile (called "weak as a noodle" for
  a tall commercial partition exposed to impact on both sides) and over a
  fully doubled/back-to-back profile setup (rejected as slower and more
  expensive for the client with no meaningful rigidity gain over the
  simpler double-track approach for this use case). `single-account`,
  practical tradeoff reasoning, not universally validated.
- **Template-based marking for large batches of identical cuts** (used
  here for ~1,100 crosspieces at 593mm each): rather than measuring each
  piece individually with a tape measure (stated as impractically slow at
  volume) or a full physical template/jig (called too fussy to set up),
  the crew pre-marked one reference profile precisely once, then butted
  a stack of profiles against a wall/stop and transferred the single
  mark across all of them at once before cutting — a genuinely reusable
  time-saving technique for any project needing many identical framing
  cuts, not just this commercial scale.
- **Chalk-line point-transfer marking for repeated spacing across a long
  run**: rather than measuring and leveling each stud/crosspiece position
  individually, mark reference points with a tape measure at coarse
  intervals (here: every 20m) then transfer/snap those points across the
  intervening studs with a chalk line — faster and avoids repeated
  measuring-tool error accumulation over a long run.
- **Fastening tool choice: gas-actuated (powder-actuated-style) fastening
  tool over a hammer drill (corded or cordless)** for track-to-floor/
  ceiling fastening at volume — stated as faster than either drill option
  for this scale of work. `single-account`, tool-preference claim, not
  independently benchmarked here.
