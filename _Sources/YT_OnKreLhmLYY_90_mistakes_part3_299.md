## Rules / Heuristics — Standout New Findings

- **⚠️⚠️ Major finding — thicker/"improved" door weatherstripping can break a magnetic door lock, and doesn't actually improve soundproofing anyway.** Documented mechanism: magnetic door locks are commonly sold alongside marketed "improved" (denser/thicker) weatherstripping seals for better soundproofing — but installing the denser seal causes the door to physically bounce off it before the magnetic latch can fully engage, breaking the lock's function. **And the marketing claim itself doesn't hold up**: these "improved" seals don't meaningfully improve soundproofing at all, because the real sound leak path is the gap *under* the door (not around the frame) — a denser perimeter seal doesn't address that gap. **Practical guidance: don't pair a magnetic door lock with an "upgraded" weatherstripping product without verifying compatibility, and don't expect frame-perimeter weatherstripping alone to meaningfully improve door soundproofing** — the under-door gap is the actual leak path to address (see this store's existing door-rebate/threshold soundproofing content).
- **⚠️ Walk-in-closet-style open storage vs. simple wardrobe, quantified space-efficiency comparison**: a documented case shows a bedroom's open walk-in-style storage area consumed **roughly 2.5× more floor area** than an equivalent-capacity simple wardrobe would have needed, while storing the *same total amount of clothing* — and additionally created an exposed, unprotected external corner that takes repeated wear/damage from normal use. A concrete quantified data point for this store's existing wardrobe-vs-walk-in tradeoff content.
- **⚠️ Textured decorative wall finish + raking-light (side-angled) fixture interaction caution**: a decorative textured wall plaster finish, combined with a light fixture that casts raking/side light (e.g. a directional sconce), visually amplifies every texture irregularity in the wall — reading as if "the crew didn't finish the job properly" even when the texture itself is intentional and well-executed. **When texture and a nearby light fixture will coexist, choose a fixture type that produces diffuse light rather than raking/side light**, specifically to avoid this effect.
- **⚠️ Diagonal tile-transition justification caution**: a tile pattern that shifts to a diagonal orientation at a room transition (e.g. entering a kitchen) needs a real functional or design justification — documented case: a diagonal shift with no apparent purpose, producing extra cuts, material waste, and no real visual payoff. **General rule: don't default to a diagonal transition without a specific reason — it costs more in cuts/waste and needs to earn that cost.**
- **⚠️ Glossy kitchen windowsill: three compounding reasons this is a bad choice specifically in a kitchen**: (1) small appliances (a coffee machine, an electric kettle) are likely to end up placed on a kitchen windowsill — especially if outlets are located in the window reveal, actively inviting this — and will scratch a glossy finish over time; (2) a glossy finish visibly shows every installation imperfection via reflection, more than a matte finish would; (3) pairing a glossy windowsill with a matte window frame of the same color looks worse than either alone (mismatched sheen reads as an installation defect even when both are intentional choices). Extends this store's general glossy-surface cautions to this specific application.
- **⚠️ "Butt-first design" anti-pattern, explicitly named**: a real documented case where a designer built a symmetric decorative wall composition (light fixtures placed to frame a painted accent stripe) *before* confirming where a functionally-necessary piece of furniture (a wardrobe) would actually go — once the wardrobe was installed where it was actually needed, it blocked the intended symmetry and covered the accent stripe entirely, and the decorative composition was wasted effort. **General planning-order principle, worth naming explicitly: confirm and place functionally-necessary furniture first, decorative/symmetric wall compositions second** — never the reverse.
- **⚠️ TV outlet pre-placement rule, reinforced with a concrete failure mode and fix**: if the electrical outlet isn't placed directly behind where a wall-mounted TV bracket will sit, the result is visible loose cabling ("spaghetti wires") running to a nearby outlet instead. **Fix if many devices need to connect to the TV**: use a cable channel/raceway connecting the two outlet locations rather than leaving wires exposed.
- **A stated (self-reported, `unverified`) health-exposure argument against painted/drywall ceilings**: beyond this store's already-recorded reasons (slow, messy, expensive, cracks/yellows/fades, total loss on a flood), the source claims drywall + putty + primer + paint together contain roughly **10-100× more chemical content** than a stretch-ceiling fabric's own material — presented without a cited source or methodology; record as an attributed practitioner claim, not a verified fact.
