## Design Concept — the rule as she states it, before attacking it

She sets it out fairly and in more operational detail than the vault currently holds, which is worth keeping independently of the critique.

- **The rule: 60% main colour, 30% second colour, 10% accent colour**, to balance an interior's palette.
- **⚠️ What each share maps to in practice** — the part the bare formula omits:
  - **60% — walls** (the largest surface), **or large objects: a sofa, large casework, kitchen units, built-in wardrobes.** Very often a neutral range — white, beige, light grey.
  - **30% — smaller furniture, an accent armchair, bed linen and other textile, and painted doors.**
  - **10% — decor: cushions, pictures, lampshades — and mouldings.**
- **⚠️ One colour used at different lightnesses and in different shades counts as ONE colour.** She demonstrates on a neutral ground whose objects differ in lightness, and on a pink that shifts shade across the room — including peach chairs in an adjoining room continuing the same pink range.
- **The neutral need not be the 60.** It can be the second colour, with 10% still reserved for the brightest accents.
- **It applies to monochrome interiors too** — her example is white/cream walls, brown-grey kitchen, black accents in the same 60/30/10 ratio.
- **⚠️ The documented way to deviate: keep the 60/30/10 and add a further accent colour also at 10%, so the total is "как бы 110".** Recorded because it is the rule's own escape hatch, and it is the first sign the formula does not close.

## Design Concept — the rebuttal

**⚠️ Her opening line, and she immediately walks it back, which is characteristic**: *"Я хотела сказать, что 60/30/10 — это самое тупое правило в дизайне интерьера, о каком я только слышала, но поняла, что это не так"* — because while making her eclecticism video she met a rule that **two mixed styles should be in an 80/20 ratio**. *"Почему так? Я не знаю. Ну, чтобы было."* **So the competition for stupidest rule is open.** *(Worth noting: that 80/20 style-mixing figure is itself a claim this vault does not currently hold, and she reports it only to dismiss it.)*

Her actual objections, in order:

1. **⚠️ Interiors exist with one colour, two colours, or any number, and the count does not make them good or bad.** She concedes the real point underneath — **limiting the palette is a correct approach if you are designing for yourself** — but asks why *three* specifically, **and states there is no evidence that successful three-colour interiors outnumber successful interiors with any other count.**
2. **⚠️ The number of colours depends on the style, so a single number cannot be right for all of them.** Minimalism can run on one colour or two and be fine; **for maximalism three is too few.**
3. **⚠️ The formulation is undefined — "what counts as a colour?" is never answered, and this is her sharpest objection.**
   - On one reading there are only **three primaries — red, yellow, blue** — and every other colour derives from them, so **however you contort an interior it always comes out at three colours.**
   - And it is unclear whether the neutral range counts. **Her diagnosis of how the rule survives contact with reality: "нейтральные то считаются, то не считаются за цвет — в зависимости от того, набирается у автора три цвета или нет."** The classification is fitted to the answer.
4. **⚠️ Her demolition of the rule's own illustrative images, which is the most concrete part.** Taking the very interiors used to demonstrate 60/30/10 and counting what is actually in them:
   - The first also has **a white ceiling, white window shutters, a large black-and-white photograph or black artwork covering a significant wall area, gold light fittings and golden parquet.**
   - The second also has **white marble on a table (a significant area), a white ceiling, a black accent chandelier, black window framing, and black within the artwork** — and **from other angles the white and black are more prominent still.**
   - **Her conclusion: "совершенно непонятно, как можно считать пропорцию в таком помещении."** You cannot compute the ratio, so the rule cannot be checked, so it cannot be wrong — which is the objection.
5. **Why this ratio and not another?** No reason is offered for why 60/30/10 should be better than **two colours at 70/30**, or four colours in some other proportion. Her counter-example is an interior at roughly **30/30/30** which she calls not amazing but hard to call unsuccessful.
6. **⚠️ It fails outright where there is a pronounced pattern occupying more than a small decorative cushion.** A patterned surface cannot be assigned to one of the three shares.

## Design Concept — what she says people are actually doing instead

- **⚠️ The technique that is genuinely widespread is much simpler: "нейтральная база плюс один цвет."** In the interior she analyses she counts **one** colour — pink — with black adding tonal variety while remaining part of the neutral palette.
- **What it buys**: it keeps an interior from falling apart, and **it works particularly well where the forms are unusual or the interior is rich in texture.**
- **⚠️ And the cost, stated against her own recommendation: it also produces the most banal design imaginable** — colour on the chairs or the sofa, some art on the wall, *"а всё остальное такое беленькое, серенькое, бежевенькое и нейтральненькое."* **This is the same convergence-on-generic complaint she makes in `gwa1wsTYYLE` and `E6nYBdz9NhM`; it is now stated three times from three different starting points and should be treated as a settled position of hers rather than three observations.**

## Design Concept — the grain of truth, and the replacement rule

**⚠️ She does not leave the rule demolished without offering the real version, and this is the most valuable content in the source.**

**The correct approach considers not only which colours are in the interior but their quantitative proportion and how evenly they are distributed** — and **the proportion is not abstract: it depends on which specific colours are involved.**

Her source is **Johannes Itten, *The Art of Colour*** — the part beyond the familiar wheel, which she says people stop at. Itten discusses quantitative proportion, and credits **Goethe** with having already assigned numeric lightness values to the colours.

- Take the **six main colours — three primary and three secondary.** **At equal saturation they differ in lightness**, which she demonstrates by converting the wheel to greyscale: **yellow is the lightest, violet the darkest.**
- **Goethe's lightness numbers**: the lighter the colour, the higher the value. **Yellow = 9, violet = 3, green = 6, red = 6.**
- **Balance comes from relating those numbers.**
- **Saturation interacts with area**: reduce red's saturation while leaving green as it is, and **you need more red to reach balance**; reduce it further and you need more again. *(This part is internally consistent and matches the standard account.)*

> [!WARNING]
> ### ⚠️ Her worked examples invert the standard formulation — flagged, not silently reproduced
>
> **What she says**: yellow 9 against violet 3 gives 3:1, so **"нам нужно взять три части жёлтого и одну часть фиолетового."** Similarly yellow 9 against green 6 gives **three parts yellow to two parts green.**
>
> **⚠️ Itten's harmonious areas are *inversely* proportional to the light values**, not directly. With yellow at 9 and violet at 3, the harmonious *areas* are **3 parts yellow to 9 parts violet** — i.e. **more violet than yellow**, which is also what the eye expects, since a small amount of a light brilliant colour balances a large amount of a dark one. Her yellow/green example inverts the same way.
>
> **Her red-against-green example (6 and 6, therefore 1:1) is unaffected**, because an inverted 1:1 is still 1:1 — which is probably why the error was not caught.
>
> **This is recorded as her claim with the discrepancy flagged, per this vault's attribute-as-opinion rule — but it must not be used as a working formula without checking Itten directly.** The *principle* she is importing (balance relates lightness to area, and depends on which colours) is sound and is the useful part; the direction of the ratio in her two worked examples is not. **`⚠️ verify before use`.**

**Her own honest qualifier, which limits how far any of this travels**: nobody sits with a calculator working out proportions, **because perception is also affected by illuminance, by texture and fаktura of the material, and because perception differs between people.** **"Если вы хотите достичь идеального баланса соотношения цветов, то это не так легко, как использовать правило 60/30/10."** — which is, on her own account, the reason the simpler rule spread.

## Rules / Heuristics — the general lesson, and the best-remembered part

**⚠️ The Ильяхов analogy, which generalises far past colour and is worth carrying into how this vault treats *any* numeric rule.** Some copywriter invented that every product should have **7.2 advantages**, and now every copywriter, whatever the product, forces the count to 7.2 — **padding a product that has three, cutting one that has ten** — *"и получается полная фигня."*

**"Нужно думать сначала о смысле и только потом о формуле. Формулой можно пользоваться как вспомогательным инструментом"** — but first make the thing good.

**Her closing position**: use 60/30/10 if you want to, that is your right, **but the main thing is a logically constructed and beautiful interior, not hitting the ratio.** *"Об этом правиле можно благополучно забыть."*

## Mistakes / Warnings

- Treating 60/30/10 as a check that can be passed or failed — it cannot be computed on a real room, as her own worked count of the rule's illustration shows.
- Counting neutrals as colours or not depending on whether you need three.
- Applying the rule where a pronounced pattern occupies real area.
- Applying a single colour-count to every style.
- **Using her Itten-derived ratios in the direction she states them — see the warning above.**
