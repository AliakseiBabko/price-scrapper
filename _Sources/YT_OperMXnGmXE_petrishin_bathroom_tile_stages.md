## Bathroom Tile-Laying Stages

1. **Pre-tiling squareness verification, concrete method**: mark points
   15cm from each wall along a shared parallel line, then use a laser
   level to check that every marked point lines up with no deviation,
   both horizontally and vertically — confirms the walls are parallel
   and flat *before* tiling starts, not assumed.
2. **Priming and cure-time sequencing**: once flatness is confirmed,
   prime the walls (roller application) and let the primer dry a full
   24 hours before adhesive/tiling for reliable bonding.
3. **Wall-before-floor sequencing, with a stated reason**: start tiling
   from the walls, not the floor — tiling the floor first means
   later wall-tiling adhesive drips have to be cleaned off an already-
   finished floor. **Leave the first floor row unglued** while the
   walls are tiled, then return to lay it last, once the wall tile's
   bottom edge is known — this produces a cleaner base joint fit than
   tiling the floor to a guessed height first.
4. **Second-row height calculated from a decorative accent row, worked
   method**: use cardboard under the floor to simulate the adhesive
   layer's thickness, level, and mark the floor tile's finished top
   height on the wall. Where a decorative accent tile row is planned
   above a sink/vanity, measure the vanity cabinet's height from the
   (marked) floor-tile level, transfer that height to the wall to find
   the decorative row's own bottom edge, then use one tile's width
   (plus a 2mm joint allowance) to count backward and find the top edge
   of the first full row below it. A temporary support batten/ledger
   ("опорный профиль"), propped with gypsum-board scraps, is set at
   that calculated point and checked with a level before tiling begins.
5. **Tile-width-dependent centering rule — extends this page's existing
   "classic layout" centering technique**: find the wall's midpoint and
   the tile's own midpoint, then align them — but whether to center on
   the tile's *center* or its *edge* depends on the tile's own width.
   Worked examples given directly: with a 400×200mm tile, centering
   tile-center-to-wall-center produces edge pieces larger than half a
   tile on both sides (the good, intended outcome). With a 500×200mm
   tile, the *same* center-to-center alignment instead produces edge
   strips *smaller* than half a tile (the bad outcome this page's
   existing classic-layout rule is meant to avoid) — for that size, the
   fix is to start the layout from the tile's *edge* at the wall's
   center line instead of the tile's center, which restores large
   (>half-tile) edge pieces. **Practical implication: the classic-layout
   centering rule needs a tile-size-specific check, not a single fixed
   alignment method, to reliably avoid narrow edge slivers.**
6. **Pre-installation tile-batch consistency check**: before installing,
   verify the manufacturer's own lot/batch sticker on every tile box —
   grade/sort, tone, and production date must all match across boxes —
   and check individual tiles for flatness before laying, catching
   shade/batch mismatches before they're grouted in place.
7. **Adhesive application mechanics**: mix adhesive per the package
   instructions; apply to the wall first with a flat trowel, pressing
   firmly to seat it against the substrate, then comb it to a uniform
   ridge height with a 6mm notched trowel; press the tile in firmly —
   **the compressed adhesive layer under the tile should not exceed
   3mm thick**. Plastic spacer wedges level and seat the tile; 2mm
   intermediate spacer crosses set the joint width (a separate
   self-leveling-clip system exists but wasn't detailed — flagged by
   the presenter as "a topic for another video").
8. **Fixture-cutout technique**: measure and transfer the faucet/mixer
   fitting's location onto the tile, overlay a prepared template, then
   drill with a diamond core bit sized to the fitting.
9. **Edge-cut symmetry rule**: when a row ends in a cut piece, keep the
   cut width equal on both the left and right ends of the row for
   visual symmetry, rather than cutting one full-width piece at one end
   only.
10. **Cure-and-cleanup close-out**: after 24 hours, remove the
    intermediate spacer crosses and the temporary support batten; also
    clean excess adhesive out of the grout joints before grouting —
    stated as an easily-forgotten step.
