## Walls / Ceilings — Wall-Prep-for-Paint Staged Sequence

1. **Full staged sequence for walls destined for paint, distinct from
   this page's existing wallpaper-oriented sequencing**: (1) full
   substrate float/skim ("затирка") using either a hand pole or a
   long-reach "жираф" sander, whichever the installer prefers; (2)
   **raking-light bump check before sanding** — the installer shines a
   portable work lamp across the wall at a grazing angle specifically to
   find remaining bumps/waves before the sanding pass, so the sanding
   pass removes exactly what the light reveals rather than guessing; (3)
   sanding; (4) priming ("грунтовка") — removes surface dust and
   promotes bonding/tack for the next material; (5) chase-filling
   ("заделка штроб"); (6) base filler pass — either "Пуфас" or "Унифлот"
   (see item 2 below); (7) for a **paint-destined wall specifically**,
   two coats of "LR" finishing putty (a putty this speaker says is
   normally a wallpaper-finish product, applied here as an intermediate
   layer before the paint-specific final steps); (8) **fiberglass
   wallpaper-backing mesh ("стеклохолст") glued across the *entire
   apartment***, not just paint-destined rooms; (9) final "Шитрок"
   (Sheetrock-brand) finish putty coat; (10) full sanding to true flatness;
   (11) handover. **Distinguishes this from the existing page's
   wallpaper-only sequencing** — LR putty is described as normally a
   wallpaper-finish product, repurposed here as an intermediate step
   specifically because the wall is going under paint, not wallpaper.

2. **"Пуфас" vs. "Унифлот" base-filler comparison, cost/workability
   tradeoff**: Пуфас is slightly cheaper, but sandier/coarser in texture
   and sheds more — needs an extra troweling/rubbing-down pass after
   application so it doesn't later flake or separate from the wall once
   the next material goes on. Унифлот costs slightly more but self-bonds
   better ("as if already primed") and lays flatter on its own, giving a
   modest labor-speed edge — the installer's own assessment is that the
   time difference between the two is minor overall and depends more on
   job volume than on which filler is chosen.

3. **Fiberglass backing mesh ("стеклохолст") glued across the entire
   apartment as a reinforcement layer before the final finish-putty
   coat** — applied uniformly regardless of whether a given room is
   destined for paint or wallpaper, distinct from the LR-putty step which
   is paint-specific.

## Planning Rules — Timeline and Cure/Dry Discipline

4. **Wall-prep-for-paint timeline, small area**: full wall-prep stage
   (through the fiberglass-backing/final-putty/sanding steps) takes
   roughly **2-3 weeks** even for a small area (this room's own wall
   area); the actual painting pass itself afterward is only **1-2 days**.
   Painting itself is described as not especially time-consuming, but
   effort-intensive in a different way — avoiding drips/runs and getting
   color/tone right (client-dependent) takes real attention even though
   it's fast.
5. **24-hour per-stage cure rule, and its compounding effect on a small
   job**: every material/stage needs roughly 24 hours to cure/crystallize
   before the next step, regardless of how little actual work time that
   stage took. **Named example: a balcony-only job** — even if the
   painter spends only about an hour of actual work on one stage for a
   small balcony, the next visit still has to wait a full 24 hours for
   that material to cure before continuing; on a full-apartment job the
   installer instead spends a full day working room-to-room, returning to
   each room the next day once its 24-hour cure window has passed and
   working forward around the apartment in a cycle. Approximate cure
   times vary per material (stated as roughly a day each, but fiberglass
   backing glue, Унифлот, and plaster each cure on their own distinct
   timelines) but "about a day" is used as the practical planning
   default.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Acceptance Checklist and Cross-Trade Risk

6. **Most common installer-error location for paint-prep specifically:
   edges/corners, not the open wall field** — a rushed or less-practiced
   installer may not fully carry the finish layer all the way to an edge
   (e.g. at a door/window reveal), leaving a short, uneven strip; getting
   this right needs a "trained hand" ("рука должна быть набита"),
   distinct from this page's existing plastering-QC content, which
   flagged door openings as the main defect zone for *plaster* rather
   than paint-prep finish coats specifically.
7. **Chase-line ("штроба") visibility as a recurring finish-coat defect**:
   filled chases can still show as a visible groove after the first
   finish-putty pass (even after the base-filler pass); the installer may
   need **two, sometimes three, coats** specifically to eliminate visible
   chase lines and small craters, followed by full sanding to bring the
   wall to true flatness.
8. **⚠️ Cross-trade sequencing risk, named explicitly by the host on
   walking the job site**: a window/door-frame replacement crew working
   *after* plastering but *before* painting can break already-finished
   door/window reveals ("откосы") — the painter then has to redo that
   section as fresh plastering work before he can even start his own
   finish-coat sequence, adding real time/cost that a client scheduling
   trades without coordination wouldn't anticipate.
9. **Practitioner's own quality-distinguishing criterion — explicitly a
   work-ethic judgment, not a technical checklist item**: asked how to
   tell a good finisher from a bad one, the speaker declines to name a
   technical tell ("I haven't worked with bad ones") and instead frames it
   as attitude toward the work — someone who wants to do it well will
   take the extra time needed even if a step isn't going smoothly,
   rather than rushing to collect payment.
