## Mistakes / Warnings — Door Casing vs. Shower-Pan Clearance

- **⚠️ Named clearance conflict: a classic door casing installed at its normal position can overlap a shower pan's footprint by as much as ~6cm in a tight bathroom**, forcing an installer to cut the bottom of the casing off flush with the floor — described as producing an ugly, visibly truncated "stub" ("огрызок") rather than a full casing profile. - **⚠️ Premium-segment fix, a real applied technique**: before final installation, obtain a sample door frame and sample casing piece, dry-fit the frame at its intended depth (pushed as deep into the rough opening as it can go), then have the tile setter cut the shower-wall tile reveal/jamb specifically to meet that exact frame position ("тютелька в тютельку" — precisely, no gap) — so that when the real door frame is installed later, it simply slides into a reveal already sized to fit around it with no cutting, and the casing installs at full, uncut profile on the outer face. Described explicitly as a solution requiring above-average tile-setter skill and added time/cost, not a trivial swap.
