## Additional Named Rules (Beyond the Table)

- **Substrate requirement, brick specifically**: brick partitions need a
  solid floor-slab substrate — cannot be built over a wood-joist floor
  or over a floor screed alone.
- **Foam block install prep**: requires a leveled mortar bedding course
  first, plus junction detailing where the partition meets an existing
  monolithic wall; plastered both sides regardless.
- **Gypsum pazogreb plate install prep**: also set on a leveled mortar
  bedding course; whether plastering is needed depends on the specific
  room's required flatness/geometry — often skippable.
- **Drywall install prep**: can be built on virtually any substrate
  (including a wood-joist floor or bare screed) with no special
  substrate prep — the most substrate-flexible of the five.
- **Drywall fastener strategy**: since drywall itself has no fixed pull-
  strength figure, plan ahead — either aim for the steel stud directly,
  or (better) pre-install a plywood/OSB blocking panel behind the
  drywall skin at any point a TV mount, wall cabinet, or wall-bar/
  pull-up bar is planned, before the wall is closed up.
- **Brick's popular "durability + sound insulation" reputation is
  explicitly challenged by this source**: brick's own airborne-sound
  figure (47 dB) is *worse* than both drywall (49 dB) and silicate
  pazogreb (50 dB) at comparable thickness — the presenter states this
  directly to counter what he calls a common but incorrect client/
  designer assumption.
- **Silicate pazogreb plate named as the presenter's own preferred
  material** — best thickness-to-soundproofing ratio of the five, tied
  for thinnest, only mid-pack on cost — but flagged with its own real
  weakness (weakest fastener pull-out rating of the five, 40 kg/m²).
