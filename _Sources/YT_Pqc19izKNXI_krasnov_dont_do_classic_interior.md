## Design Concept, Mistakes / Warnings

- **⚠️ Wall-sconce mounting height above a bed's headboard — a specific, new numeric bedroom-lighting standard**: correct height is **20-30cm above the headboard**; the reviewed real listing had sconces at roughly 50cm, flagged as too high. Distinct from this vault's existing bathroom-mirror-sconce content — this is bedroom-headboard-specific.
- **⚠️ Recessed downlights on a heavily relief-moulded ceiling — a specific mechanism critique, genuinely new**: recessed spotlights that wash light across a ceiling already dense with 3D moulding relief accentuate the moulding's texture, making an already-heavy ceiling read even more oppressive/"dome-like" ("давящее ощущение купола"). A flatter/softer light treatment would have softened it instead. A specific claim about how recessed-lighting choice must account for a textured/relief ceiling surface, not just room-level lux targets.
- **Overloaded surfaces / lack of a single accent**: every surface (walls, ceiling, floor, furniture) competing for attention with no dominant focal point — fragments the room into competing parts rather than one composition.
- **Monotonous beige-brown palette with no contrast**: the whole room "sinking" into one tone, reading heavy/suffocating, nothing for the eye to catch onto; compounded by glossy wall/ceiling surfaces amplifying the closed-in feeling.
- **⚠️ Mixing genuinely disparate historical/decorative registers in one room** — this specific listing combined an Art Nouveau doorway (echoing Alphonse Mucha), a floor pattern resembling a backgammon board, and a Greek-motif glass cabinet, plus a plain modern bar stool "from a different era entirely." Diagnosing WHY a specific real room fails (competing decorative "languages" with no unifying logic), distinct from generic "pick one style" advice.
- **⚠️ Over-matching finishes — a specific critique of too much exact matching, distinct from mismatching critiques**: identical wall panels and cabinet-door panels in a bedroom fuse furniture and walls into one visual mass, making the room read as "a box with no depth" and erasing any sense of zoning between furniture and architecture.
- **Heavy ceiling moulding paired with completely flat, undecorated walls** — a vertical/horizontal compositional imbalance; decorative intensity should be reasonably proportional between ceiling and wall planes rather than concentrated entirely on the ceiling.
- **⚠️ Columns installed inside an ordinary residential apartment (not a palace or museum) flagged as inherently disproportionate at that scale** — "жилое пространство, а не дворец культуры или не Эрмитаж." Echoes the same "misapplied monumental architectural element at residential scale" pattern already logged for arches (Round 3's `gbOoH1oV4Hk`) — cross-reference as the same underlying pattern applied to columns.
- **Absence of layered lighting scenarios, restated**: a single central chandelier plus hrusталь (crystal) and heavy fabrics reads "90s new-Russian" rather than elegant — cross-reference existing layered-lighting content, not new.
- **⚠️ Overly heavy drapery blocking natural daylight**: a real listing had two windows' worth of daylight potential undercut by overly heavy/thick curtains.
- **⚠️ Braid/passementerie trim edging on curtains/upholstery («отделка тесьмой», quoted Russian term) flagged as reading dated rather than elegant** — a specific named textile-detailing critique, genuinely new for this vault.
- **A decorative deer/stag figurine placed between two windows with no thematic connection to the room** flagged as reading arbitrary/random — recommends either removing it or replacing it with something coherent, e.g. a painting.
- **Furniture-style clash within one room**: a heavy formal classic dining set alongside a plain Scandinavian-style sofa, plus a rough "country"-textured rug — reads as "a random assortment" rather than deliberate eclecticism.
- **⚠️ A sofa positioned back-to the dining zone — a nuanced point about HOW furniture-based zoning is executed, not whether it's valid**: furniture CAN be used to zone a room deliberately, but here the specific positioning reads as cutting the room into two disconnected halves ("мебелью можно зонировать пространство, но тут он просто его разрезает буквально") rather than a soft zoning gesture.
- **A picture frame with no stylistic relationship to anything else in the room** — his point is the FRAME itself (not just the artwork) needs to be a deliberate choice, matching or intentionally accenting the room's palette/style; he has no criticism of the painting itself.
- **⚠️ A cluster of small-WC/toilet-room over-decoration critiques, genuinely new for this vault's likely-thin WC-specific coverage**: a crystal chandelier in a small WC flagged as excessive for the room's scale; a heavily gilded, ornately-moulded mirror with candle-holder details flagged as too "dressed up" for a small utilitarian room; framed engravings in gilt frames flagged as belonging in a living room, not stacked in quantity in a small WC; heavy decorative moulding trim in a narrow WC specifically flagged as visually elongating the room further, worsening a "train-car"/corridor-like feel. **Explicitly flags this specific WC's total lack of storage (no shelves, no cabinet) as a real functional gap independent of any style choice** — a genuinely new WC-storage-planning point.
- **Final positive-guidance summary (his own recommended fixes, worth recording as his stated methodology)**: classic style done well uses moulding/columns/trim as RESTRAINED ACCENTS paired with modern pieces, not maximalist quantity; classic doesn't require an all-beige palette — pair a neutral base with deep accent colors (navy, deep green, complex grey-beige); a single central chandelier alone is "starting the game 5-0 down" (his own phrase) — add sconces, floor lamps, hidden cove lighting; genuine classic luxury comes from material QUALITY/authenticity (real wood, stone, textile, refined metal finishes) rather than sheer gold/marble quantity; final framing — luxury is about a genuinely comfortable-to-live-in space, not a demonstration showroom.
