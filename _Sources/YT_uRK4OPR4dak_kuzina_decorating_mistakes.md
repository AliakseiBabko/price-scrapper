## Design Concept — ⚠️ Mistake 1: everything matched too well

**Her cooking analogy, which is the framing for the whole source**: a good dish needs something sweet, something salty, something sour, a little pepper, **in proportion.** Too much sugar and it cloys; too much pepper and it is inedible. **⚠️ "Слишком много однородных предметов делают интерьер нежилым, невкусным, без изюминки."**

- **⚠️ The fix, and the constraint on it is the useful half: add bold accessories or a large artwork that differs in STYLE from the rest — but it must still relate to the interior in some other dimension.** Either **the same colour range** while the style differs, or **a similar form.** That is what stops it reading as alien.
- **Worked before/after**: a dull cream classic scheme, into which she puts **a chandelier and chairs with rounded forms specifically so they agree with the existing pieces**, plus a little ornament. **Only a few elements changed, and the interior stops looking so evenly measured.**
- **On a neutral base you can thread coloured elements onto it** — she adds a bright chandelier over a table, unexpected and not a close style match, **and supports it with other details.** She notes this is a common decorator's move.
- **⚠️ But then a negative example running the identical logic, and this is the sharpest item in the source.** A neutral scheme with yellow elements added — **"их оттенок везде одинаковый, и он с такой педантичностью, я бы сказала занудством, повторён"** — so the yellow does not rescue it. **Better to take several different shades that agree with each other.**
  - **⚠️ This is a real qualification of the «поддержка цвета» rule this vault holds from `yU2zKPpDghM`: colour support needs *variation*, not repetition. Supporting an accent with identical instances of itself is a distinct failure mode from not supporting it at all.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ Mistake 2: proportion

- **Three failure shapes**: too-large objects in a small room; too-small objects in a large room; **or normally-sized objects in a medium room but too many of them, so you squeeze between them.**
- **⚠️ Vary the sizes of adjacent pieces.** With a sofa and an armchair together, **let the armrests align in height while the sofa's back sits lower and the chair's higher** — or use a very compact, lower chair. **Better than two pieces at roughly equal height.**
- **⚠️ In a medium room, larger pieces often win: "появляется ощущение, что вы более спокойно, вольготно обращаетесь с пространством, и оно от этого кажется больше."** A counter-intuitive one worth keeping.
- **⚠️ Objects can be well matched by style and by colour and still fail on proportion — "если пропорции нарушены, то это большая проблема."**
- **⚠️ The showroom warning, with an instruction attached: a large sofa looks good in a big salon and heavy at home. "Всегда ходите с рулеткой" — measure the pieces you intend to place next to one another and decide from the measurements.**

## Design Concept — Mistake 3: too much colour

- **Everyone has a personal threshold, and she compares it to a pain threshold** — it differs per person.
- **⚠️ The diagnosis: the problem is usually not that a bright colour is present but that there is too much of it, or that it repeats too often in the same variant and the same shade.** *(The same mechanism as Mistake 1 — sameness rather than intensity.)*
- **⚠️ Her fix: "вышибить клин клином" — put a very bright object of a DIFFERENT colour into the over-coloured interior**, which softens the sense that one colour is bearing down on you. **Her reason: an interior is where you should rest, not be shocked that everything around you is green.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ Mistake 4: too many things

- **Things accumulate.** A new flat is spacious; a long-inhabited one risks becoming *"лавка старьёвщика."* **Parting with things is genuinely hard.**
- **⚠️ The rule she gives: bought one thing → immediately throw out another.**
- **`[restatement]`** People move in, buy new things, then add old things from the old flat, and often the two do not get on. *(This vault already holds the full three-question carry-over method from `bVmE50y33Ls`, Round 3. Recorded only as a second statement of it.)*
- **⚠️ Genuinely new, and it is a budget claim: people buy new things because they have grown tired of the old ones. Do not. "Если вы устали, сначала расхламитесь — возможно, что после этого усталость как рукой снимет, без дополнительных трат."** **Declutter before you diagnose the tiredness as a shortage.**

## Design Concept — Mistake 5: how much decor

- **Two extremes: too little decor reads uninhabited; too much is oppressive. ⚠️ And she notes the second is the more common one *in publications*** — i.e. the reference images people work from are skewed.
- **The right quantity depends on the style.** Her boho example carries bright cushions, tall branches and many books without reading excessive, **because that style presumes it.**
- **⚠️ The rule for the uncertain case: if the interior is laconic and you cannot tell whether you have too much, err toward LESS. In a laconic interior two or three objects are enough "для того, чтобы интерьер заиграл."**
- **⚠️ But do not leave shelves completely bare, or leave many empty surfaces.** The floor exists as well as the ceiling.

## Design Concept — ⚠️ Mistake 6: many small objects, and what to do with a collection

- **⚠️ "Мелкий декор прячет в себе больше опасности, чем слишком крупный декор."** One small object is not enough to decorate with; add more and **suddenly there are too many and it becomes chaos.**
- **⚠️ The specific case is sentimental collections** — small figurines behind glass, arranged at random, *"всё что на полку влезло, там и стоит."* **The consequence she names is the argument: you cannot make out the beautiful details on any individual piece, which devalues the collection.** Displaying all of it is what destroys it.
- **⚠️ The fix, and it is genuinely good: hide part of it, add a few larger decor pieces, and compose a proper group.** And if you regret not seeing everything — **rotate. Swap what is hidden for what is displayed, and you rediscover the pieces you had stopped noticing.**

## Design Concept — Mistakes 7–8: two specific voids, and artificial plants

- **⚠️ Emptiness under a console.** A console tends to leave a hole beneath it. **Fill it easily — a pouffe, decorative baskets or boxes.** *(She puts the question to viewers rather than asserting, showing the same console photographed both ways.)*
- **The same principle for a non-working fireplace** — vases, decorative candles.
- **Artificial plants: "не надо декорировать интерьер пластиковыми листочками."** **⚠️ But she gives the concession rather than just the rule**: if plants keep dying and you forget to water them, **buy the more expensive artificial ones, which now imitate closely.** Her own preference is real plants **because the interest is in watching them grow and shed** — and she notes **self-watering pots** exist.

## Materials / Engineering — ⚠️ Mistake 9: visible cables

**Common among people who bought a flat without renovating, and among renters.** Visible cabling reads as visual chaos.

- **⚠️ Two fixes: narrow plastic trunking painted the wall colour; or hollow skirtings — she names IKEA and notes others make them — into which cables can be partly hidden.**

*(A rare piece of retrofit advice from this channel, and the only one aimed at people who are not renovating.)*

## Design Concept — ⚠️ Mistake 10: art hung too high

**Two distinct problems: hung wrong, or the wrong size.**

- **⚠️ Do NOT align the top of the frame with a window or door opening.** A common instinct and wrong.
- **⚠️ Correct: the centre of the picture at eye level — "причём это уровень глаз не Дяди Стёпы, а уровень глаз хозяев квартиры."** **The qualifier is the load-bearing part: it is calibrated to the people who live there, not to a standard figure.**

## Design Concept — ⚠️ Mistake 11: decorating around an object you already know is wrong

**Her most direct budget argument, and it comes from consultations rather than from theory.**

- **People admit an object is unsuccessful and try to cover it — especially if the purchase was recent — because they do not want to lose the money.**
- **⚠️ But: buy a bad piece and then try to rescue it with a nice rug, more cushions, more decor, and "ситуация не изменится, и в конечном итоге в комнате вас будет раздражать буквально всё."** A throw and scented candles will not help.
- **⚠️ Her recommendation, and the fact that makes it actionable: "сейчас не так сложно продавать мебель, и не обязательно делать это за полцены." Get rid of such pieces rather than decorating around them.**
