## Balcony / Loggia — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Zemskov's technique for joining a loggia with continuous/shared facade glazing (the type an earlier companion video calls essentially impossible to insulate)**: keep only the facade's own aluminum skeleton (the vertical/horizontal structural members that legally define the building's exterior appearance) — **strip out all of its original glass infill entirely**, then install a genuinely new, independent warm PVC frame set inward, fixed directly to the floor and ceiling slabs (not to the old aluminum skeleton) — giving a real, independently-anchored thermal envelope that happens to sit inside the old facade's visual frame rather than depending on it structurally or thermally.
- **Color-matching detail**: the new PVC frame's exterior lamination was ordered specifically to match the surrounding facade aluminum's color, so from outside the building the new glazing reads as a continuation of the original facade system rather than an obvious retrofit — while internally it's plain white PVC, an ordinary window.
- **Zemskov's reported real outcome**: a genuinely warm loggia at -9°C outdoor temperature, described as comfortable even with the room's own supplementary heating not yet running at full capacity (renovation still in progress at time of filming) — offered as evidence the workaround genuinely works, not just a cosmetic fix.
- **A companion technique used on the same project — trading width between the two enlargement methods from prior videos**: removing the exterior insulation from the loggia-to-neighboring-room party wall (since the loggia is now fully joined and warm, that wall no longer needs its own insulation layer) won back ~16cm on one side, while a different area of the same project lost ~8cm to other changes — netting out to a real overall gain. Demonstrates the two width-gaining techniques from companion videos (`yt_jxrd7pZTkvI`, `yt_oIED8ehn2Is`) can be combined/traded off against each other within one project rather than applied in isolation.
- **Party-wall rebuild**: the wall separating this loggia from the neighboring apartment was fully removed and rebuilt thicker, with better soundproofing and thermal performance than the original.
- **Radiator-alcove/niche technique**: built a dedicated, structurally solid alcove for the room's radiator/convector, engineered to double as an extra sturdy work surface (strong enough to sit on) — and shaped so the convector's warm air output serves both the kitchen and the loggia simultaneously, rather than heating only the space directly above it.
- **Additional insulation build-out**: a separate wall section was built specifically to add extra thermal insulation at one particular junction node (the transcript doesn't specify further construction detail for this element).
- **Zemskov's closing framing**: worth pursuing this kind of workaround specifically when the apartment is genuinely small and the extra square meters matter, and when upstairs/downstairs neighbors have already done the same (reducing the chance the management company objects) and there's reasonable confidence the management company won't object to this specific joining.
