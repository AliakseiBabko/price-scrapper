## Walls / Ceilings — Masonry Acceptance Checklist (Foam-Block Partitions)

1. **Priming verification technique (new field method)**: after
   priming, a visible roller-streak mark remains on the dried surface —
   this is how you *confirm* priming actually happened, on floor or
   wall. Cross-check: run a hand across the surface — an unprimed
   surface leaves visible construction dust on your palm; a primed one
   doesn't.
2. **First-row leveling platform ("подложка"/"поду") — corroborates and
   sharpens the existing floor-level-transfer content on this page**:
   floor-slab height can vary 1-2cm between different structural bays,
   especially in secondary/existing housing — laying the first block
   row directly on such a floor risks blocks sitting on different
   planes, degrading the whole wall's quality. Fix: build a leveling
   platform/pad first so the entire first row sits on one true plane.
   For minor variation, a thicker mortar bed under the first row is a
   (slower, harder) alternative to a platform.
3. **Finish-thickness planning caveat**: tile + tile-adhesive thickness
   must be accounted for against the project drawing's stated
   finished-wall dimensions — check the design's dimensions against the
   real as-built result after plastering *and* tiling, not just after
   plastering.
4. **Plumb tolerance, this company's own internal standard**: max **7mm
   deviation across the full wall height**, checked with a bubble-level
   straightedge ("правило") — beyond 7mm the wall is considered
   "заволена" (leaning/out of plumb) and needs correction (extra time
   and cost). Stated explicitly as this company's own held standard.
5. **Joint-fill QC with a named field technique**: every block-to-block
   joint must be fully filled with adhesive — no through-gaps or
   voids allowed. **New checkable technique**: shine a powerful
   flashlight against the opposite face of the wall; if no light passes
   through a joint, it's fully sealed.
6. **Adhesive-layer thickness rule, with a mechanism**: the glue layer
   between blocks must not exceed **3-4mm** — a thicker glue layer
   weakens the wall's structural bond; squeeze-out/drips must be
   cleaned off, and any protrusion left behind must not exceed **3mm**.
7. **Reinforcement rule, corroborating and adding a frequency floor to
   the existing mesh-reinforcement content on this page**: foam blocks
   must be reinforced with either basalt mesh or rebar (both treated as
   equally effective) — **at minimum every 3rd row**, ideally every
   2nd; negotiate the exact frequency with the builders before work
   starts, since it's a real cost/labor tradeoff.
8. **Structural tie-back hardware detail, corroborating and detailing
   the existing "every third course" tie-in rule on this page**: metal
   wall hangers, bent in half — one half anchored to the load-bearing
   wall with wedge anchors, the other half screwed to the new partition
   with wood screws — installed at every 3rd row.
9. **Two distinct deformation-joint specs, new numeric detail**:
   **vertical** joint at the seam between a new partition and an
   existing wall — 1-2cm wide, fully foam-filled, no voids; **horizontal**
   joint between the upper floor slab and the last block row — thickness
   `ASR-uncertain` (heard as "24 см," almost certainly a garbled "2-4
   cm" given the vertical joint's own stated range — do not treat "24
   cm" as reliable), also fully foam-filled. **Purpose stated
   explicitly**: absorbs building settlement as the multi-story
   structure settles under its own weight, preventing cracks — same
   underlying mechanism as this store's existing building-settlement
   deformation-seam content (Petrishin's own plastering-basics video,
   this same trial batch) applied here at the masonry stage instead of
   the plaster stage. Cured excess foam trimmed flush with a utility
   knife.
10. **Block-stagger/bond rule, with a visual explanation of the failure
    mode**: blocks must be laid with at least **1/3-block offset**
    row-to-row ("разбежка") for proper interlocking bond and crack
    resistance. At a wall intersection specifically: a perpendicular
    wall simply butted against a finished wall face is a **structurally
    weaker connection**; the correct method interleaves blocks from
    each wall into the other's course at the junction — demonstrated
    directly on camera as the visibly stronger bond.
11. **Door-opening "ears" ("уши") for casing clearance**: leave a
    protruding block "ear" at each door opening sized to fit door
    casings — skipping this forces cutting casings on-site, especially
    visible/bad-looking when one side of an opening keeps a full-length
    casing and the other gets a shortened stub. **Named root cause**:
    most often happens when renovation starts without a design project,
    or the project itself has small dimensional errors that compound
    into this visible result. **Door zero-reference point** ("нулевая
    точка") must be set by the project lead/foreman — after final
    flooring goes in, the clearance between the door lintel and the
    finished floor may turn out too small to fit a door if this wasn't
    planned for in advance, accounting for the finish-flooring
    thickness.
12. **Door lintel spec**: lintel embedment into the wall on each side
    must be **at least 10-15cm** for stability (the lintel's own
    thickness figure in the transcript, "3мм," is almost certainly ASR-
    garbled and not extracted as a reliable number). **Pre-made
    matching-material lintels** (same gas-block material as the main
    wall) are now used on this company's own projects instead of a
    separately-poured concrete lintel. Lintels must be painted/primed
    against **rust bleeding through the finish** later — ideally fully
    coated before installation, not after.
13. **Chase-routing rule for monolithic structures, corroborating and
    extending the existing "monolithic concrete cannot be chased"
    content on this page**: **horizontal** chases in monolithic
    concrete are explicitly prohibited — the fix is a false wall
    ("фальшстена") built in block, 50-75mm thick, fixed to the main
    structure with mounting "mushroom" fasteners embedded at least 4cm.
14. **Delivered-block quality/quantity QC**: chips larger than **7mm**
    are not acceptable on delivered blocks; leftover unused blocks after
    installation should not exceed **5-10%** of the last delivery batch
    (a reasonable reorder buffer) — if it does, this company's own
    stated practice is to take the excess material back and refund the
    client the difference.
15. **Site-cleanliness handover rule**: the site (including the toilet
    bowl specifically) should be left usable-clean at every stage
    handover between crews, with debris either absent or neatly piled —
    stated rationale: clean stage-to-stage handovers keep the whole
    project clean throughout, better for both crews and the client.
