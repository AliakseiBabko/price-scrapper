## Planning Rules — Replanning-Approval Timing, a Named Real-World Delay

- **⚠️ Named sequencing mistake and its consequence**: many people take a finished design project straight into demolition/replanning work, only realizing afterward that the layout change may be legally unregistered — risking a fine and a forced reversal back to the original layout. **Fix, stated directly**: submit the demolition/construction (montage/demontage) plan for official approval *before* starting any work, not after.
- **⚠️ Named timeline caution**: the approval process itself takes real time and **varies by district** — the speaker's own example cites up to **2 months** as a real, budgetable planning delay, not a formality to squeeze in alongside other early-stage work.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Pre-Demolition Protective Packing, a Real Dispute-Cost Mechanism

- **⚠️ Named packing step and the dispute mechanism it prevents**: before any demolition begins, windows, doors, the elevator, and mailboxes are wrapped/protected as a default practice — not merely as damage-avoidance, but specifically to prevent an unresolvable liability dispute. **Real worked-through scenario**: an unprotected wall gets scratched during demolition/debris hauling; the crew disclaims responsibility ("we only work inside the apartment"); a delivery company disclaims responsibility and demands proof; without a camera or protective packing in place, the owner is left either fixing the damage personally or hoping the management company never notices (risking a bill from them instead). **Even with a security camera identifying the true responsible party** (the speaker's own example: a materials-delivery company's own mover), recovering compensation still requires real, time-consuming effort — determining the correct repair cost and matching the exact developer-original decorative finish for a fair claim — effort that a management company, left unaddressed, will eventually charge the resident for anyway. **Stated conclusion**: default protective packing is cheaper in time, money, and stress than pursuing after-the-fact compensation, even when liability is clearly provable.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Finish-Plumbing-After-Ceiling Rule and Post-Furniture Wall Defect Check

- **⚠️ Named sequencing rule, install order and its rationale**: finish electrical (outlets, switches) and stretch-ceiling installation happen before finish plumbing fixtures (faucets, sinks, glass shower partitions) — explicitly because ceiling installers working in the same room afterward risk damaging already-installed glass/fixtures; finish plumbing goes in only once the ceiling work is done.
- **⚠️ Named final QC step, genuinely new to this vault**: after kitchen/built-in furniture installation (in a classic-design, painted-wall project specifically — not wallpaper), the crew performs a dedicated wall-defect check ("дефектовка") explicitly because furniture installers are known to chip/scratch walls during their own install work — any resulting marks are corrected before the project is considered complete, rather than left for the client to discover.
