## Rules / Heuristics — Fabric Material and Wall-Color Matching

- **Matte fabric with minimal sheen is recommended for a "visually more
  expensive" interior look** — not a ban on glossy fabric, just harder to
  use well; matte fabric more reliably reads as refined. **Natural fiber
  content is preferred (cotton, wool, linen, natural velvet)**, per the
  source's own design-school training, but flagged as higher-maintenance:
  natural silk under constant direct sun exposure can literally weaken
  and split apart over time. Avoid synthetic fabric (analogized to
  plastic corner trim on furniture — a similar "cheap" tell); the
  practical recommendation is a **blended fabric where natural fiber
  content dominates over synthetic**.
- **⚠️ Four named techniques for matching curtain color/texture to
  walls**: (1) **simple match** — curtain color matches wall color,
  suits Scandinavian/minimalist/modern-classic styles; (2) **"nuance"** —
  curtain color matches or is slightly darker than the wall, but the real
  differentiator is *texture*, not color: smooth-painted walls call for
  more texturally pronounced curtains, while textured walls (e.g.
  decorative plaster) call for smooth-textured curtains; (3)
  **contrast**, governed by a **60/30/10 color-distribution formula** —
  60% base (walls/ceiling/some furniture), 30% curtains + some furniture,
  10% accent; (4) **"inspiring fabric"** — the curtain fabric's own
  pattern becomes the starting point for the room's whole color scheme,
  but this only works with genuinely designer-developed patterned fabric
  (with a real design/art/colorist process behind it), not a mass-market
  print.
- **Pattern scale should match room size**: large-scale, many-colored
  prints suit spacious rooms; small-scale patterns with only 2-3 colors
  suit small rooms.

## Rules / Heuristics — Construction Technique for a "More Expensive" Look

- **⚠️ Named as THE differentiator between plain curtains and curtains
  that make an interior read as more expensive: a properly formed pleat
  ("складка")**. Correct pleats create vertical visual lines that lift
  perceived ceiling height, make the window look larger, and establish
  visual rhythm. **Concrete technique: use a lining/backing fabric** —
  even inexpensive mass-market curtains (e.g. IKEA) look and hang
  meaningfully better once lined with an ordinary cotton backing; the
  source explicitly invites viewers to try this as an experiment and
  feel the difference themselves. Applies specifically to medium-weight
  fabrics.
- **Lining fabric as a hidden design accent, real project example**: a
  bold, saturated lining color echoing the room's already-bold wall
  color/texture, while the curtain's visible face fabric stays neutral
  ("mouse" gray) — since the wall alone is already visually rich, the
  curtain face stays neutral and the lining (visible when folded back or
  tied) supplies a coordinated accent pop. Lining color can echo either
  wall color or furniture upholstery color.
- **Mixed-fabric curtains (two or more fabrics joined in one panel)**:
  the seam/division line can be positioned relative to windowsill height,
  nearby furniture height, or a window architectural element — a genuine
  design device, but flagged with a caution: it visually *lowers*
  perceived ceiling height, the opposite effect of the pleat/vertical-
  line technique above, so use it deliberately, not by default.
- **Curtain tiebacks ("подхваты") look best on solid/plain (not
  patterned) curtains and suit classical-style interiors particularly
  well.**
- **⚠️ Real-estate marketing observation used to justify a design
  technique**: developers deliberately emphasize large windows and high
  ceilings in marketing specifically because they read as a status
  marker — interior designers use the same visual tools (curtain
  treatment, rod placement) either to enhance a room's real window/
  ceiling proportions or to visually create the same impression where the
  actual proportions are more modest.

## Numeric Data — Rod/Track Placement and Sizing

- **⚠️ Curtain-rod ceiling-height placement rule, a specific numeric
  "rule of thirds"**: for ceilings up to 2.7m, mount the rod as close to
  the ceiling as possible; for ceilings up to ~3m, divide the vertical
  distance from window-top to ceiling into three equal parts and mount
  the rod at the 2/3 point (i.e. 1/3 of that distance down from the
  ceiling); for ceilings over 3m, mount the rod at the 1/3 point (i.e.
  2/3 of that distance down, closer to the window). A genuinely specific,
  numeric, directly actionable rule not present in either other curtain
  source in this batch.
- **Rod/track selection sequencing rule, load-bearing rationale**: choose
  fabric first, then pleat style and lining, and only *then* select the
  rod/track — because a heavy finished curtain assembly may exceed what
  an arbitrarily-chosen metal rod can support; picking the rod last lets
  it be matched to the actual expected load. **Avoid plastic rods
  entirely; prefer metal or wood** — independently corroborates (a third,
  independent channel) `YT_QnSnO8bVeNs`'s existing "avoid plastic ceiling
  tracks, prefer slim-profile metal" rule.
- **Recessed ceiling-niche track ("карниз в нише")** also visually raises
  perceived ceiling height; niche depth depends on windowsill projection
  and radiator depth — **typical range cited: 150-200mm (15-20cm),
  depending on fabric type**. Independently corroborates, with a specific
  numeric range, the same design device Alla Krol's video covered from
  the AC-concealment angle (≥30cm for that specific, bulkier use case).
- **Rod-length rule for visually enlarging a window**: the rod should
  extend 30-50cm beyond the window's own width (combined, not stated
  per-side) to visually widen the window; for a single-window room, the
  rod is typically run corner-to-corner/wall-to-wall — a specific numeric
  elaboration of the "extend to the wall" coverage decision `YT_QnSnO8bVeNs`
  introduced only qualitatively.
- **Curtain-length options**: a 2-3cm floor clearance is named as one of
  the most common choices; curtains just touching the floor are described
  as technically harder to get right but more visually dramatic; in some
  cases, fabric deliberately pooling on the floor is appropriate.
  **Fabric-specific caution**: linen curtains look especially striking
  pooled on the floor, but linen tends to stretch/lengthen with use over
  time, making a "just touching" length harder to predict and maintain
  long-term. **Named as an unsuccessful choice**: a gap of 5-10+ cm above
  the floor reads as a mistake rather than an intentional style.

## Rules / Heuristics — Hanging Technique and View Consideration

- **Rod-concealment technique**: curtains hung with hooks/rings/clips
  that leave a visible gap between the fabric top and the rod pole look
  worse than curtains hung so the fabric itself covers the rod —
  recommends curtain tape ("шторная лента") specifically to achieve this
  overlap and hide the rod.
- **View-through-window analysis should inform the treatment choice**: a
  genuinely attractive view is worth emphasizing and keeping visible; an
  unattractive view (dense urban development, a busy road interchange)
  should be distracted from/obscured with curtains. In dense residential
  complexes specifically, curtains serve a real psychological privacy/
  personal-space function, not just decoration.
- **Closing design principle**: favor restraint over ornate/pompous
  curtain constructions — analogized to a little black dress reading as
  more elegant than an overly ornate ball gown. Independently
  corroborates `YT_QnSnO8bVeNs`'s existing aesthetic-restraint principle
  from a third, independent channel.
