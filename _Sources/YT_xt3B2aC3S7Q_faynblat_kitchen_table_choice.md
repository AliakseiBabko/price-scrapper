**⚠️⚠️ Two genuine conflicts with what the vault already states, and they pull in opposite directions:**

1. **Her minimum DEPTH (30 cm) is BELOW Neufert's 40 cm** — she treats 40 as the top of the minimum band, Neufert as the minimum itself. **Minor; her band contains the vault's figure.**
2. **⚠️⚠️ Her edge-to-wall clearance (1 m minimum) is 67% ABOVE the vault's 0.6 m non-service minimum, and at or above even the 0.9–1.0 m serving-side tier.** **She applies the high tier to EVERY side.** **→ This is the substantive disagreement and it must not be averaged away.** The vault's existing figures are tiered by function (sitting vs. serving); hers is flat and high. ⚠️ **Note the vault already recorded a real worked example that widened one non-serving side to a full 1 m because it doubled as circulation — which is Файнблат's number arrived at from a case. The two positions may be closer than the tables suggest: her 1 m is a design-stage default, the vault's 0.6 m an as-built floor.**

> **⚠️⚠️ THE GENUINELY NEW ONE: the chair-withdrawal clearances (50–60 stool / 70–80 chair) are a DIFFERENT quantity from edge-to-wall clearance**, and the vault conflated them. **Pulling a chair back and walking behind it are two demands on the same gap**, and the armchair figure (80 cm) is the one that catches people who upgrade from stools to upholstered dining chairs after the layout is fixed.

## ⚠️ 2. Height — and the stool consequence of an island-height table

- Standard table: **70–75 cm**.
- **⚠️⚠️ A table run as a continuation of the worktop («островным за столом») sits at 85–95 cm** — worktop height, not table height.
- **⚠️⚠️ And the consequence she flags: it then requires non-standard seating — bar or semi-bar stools.** *«Вы должны не забывать о том, что под него нужны будут нестандартные стулья или полубарные.»*

> **→ A specific, avoidable, expensive mistake: the space-saving move (table continues the worktop) silently invalidates every ordinary dining chair, and the seating budget is usually settled separately from the kitchen.**

## ⚠️ 3. Shapes — four, with the tradeoffs stated

| Shape | Her case for it | Her case against |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Прямоугольный** | most common; suits room shape; **most placement options — centre, against a wall, lengthwise**; seats 6+ | — |
| **Овальный** | all of the rectangle's advantages, **plus no sharp corners → she names it for families with small children**; **seats more at need** | — |
| **Квадратный** | **ideal for a SMALL kitchen**; **best for conversation — everyone sees everyone**, and shared dishes reach | seats only **four** |
| **Круглый** | *«очень увеличивает пространство»*; normally centred | **⚠️ cannot be pushed into a corner** — *«круглый стол никогда не задвинуть в угол; даже если вы это сделаете, это будет и непрактично, и некрасиво»*; **not every room can accommodate one** |

> **→ ⚠️ The round-table corner point is the useful one and the vault did not have it.** It converts a shape choice into a **layout constraint**: choosing round forfeits the wall-parking option permanently, which in a small kitchen-living is the flexibility that matters most.
>
> ⚠️ **Note the tension with [[_Sources/YT_y7pxU1rfORY_kulekina_dining_table_choice|Кулекина]] in the same batch, who says round and oval seat MORE people than square and rectangular, and recommends round for compact kitchens.** **Файнблат says square is the small-kitchen answer and round needs room.** **→ Not contradictory once separated: Кулекина is counting seats per unit of tabletop, Файнблат is counting the floor the table locks up. Both should appear on the page, framed that way.**

## 4. Seat count, and extension mechanisms

**Seat count: household size, plus one or two if guests are frequent.** ⚠️ **Кулекина independently gives "always one more than your household" in this same batch — two designers, same rule.**

**Extension types, named and distinguished — the vault had no taxonomy of these:**

- **Стол-книжка** — a cabinet with drop leaves both sides; **can store the chairs inside**; reads as a chest of drawers when closed. Soviet-era design, still sold.
- **Раскладной** — a tabletop folded in half.
- **⚠️ Слайдер** — *«появился не так давно, не более десяти лет назад»*: two tops stacked, sliding apart in both directions and latching.
- **Раздвижной** — two halves part and an insert leaf goes between, **stored in a drawer under the table or raised on its own mechanism** as the halves separate.
- **⚠️ С подъёмным механизмом** — *«часто такой стол ставят не на кухню, а в гостиную»*: a coffee table in normal use that rises and extends into a full dining table.
- **Потайной / выдвижной / навесной** — hidden in a niche or in the kitchen run, dropped down on a folding support leg, **for rooms with catastrophically no space**, opened only to eat.

## ⚠️ 5. Tabletop materials — with the failure mode for each

- **ДСП / МДФ / пластик** — cheapest, **least durable**; she ties the decision explicitly to *who uses it and what gets spilled*.
- **⚠️ Стекло** — impact-resistant; **tempered glass will not produce sharp shards or shatter dangerously**; indifferent to temperature swings, dyes and moisture; easy to clean. **Her only stated drawback: you need mats, purely for the NOISE of plates and cutlery.** ⚠️ **Contrast with the vault's existing PRIDE/Алёна entry, which lists FOUR daily-use costs against glass. Файнблат is markedly more positive. Recorded as a disagreement.**
- **Дерево** — fits any style; **массив lasts essentially forever but needs periodic polishing and re-impregnation**; **⚠️ soft species (сосна, ольха) dent and scratch easily and absorb moisture and dyes.**
- **Эпоксидная смола over wood** — easy care, the resin protects the timber and resists almost everything; **⚠️ fine scratches do accumulate and are visible, but it can be re-polished.**
- **⚠️⚠️ Натуральный камень — her recommendation if budget allows, and КВАРЦИТ is named as the best of them**: *«супер износостойкий, не боится температур, красящих веществ, химии, острых предметов»*; stone can be re-ground and restored.
- **⚠️⚠️ Мрамор — conditional: only in NON-LIGHT and NON-POROUS varieties.** Wine and coffee stain it, *«если ваш камень будет идеально белым либо без какого-то ярко выраженного рисунка»*; **a busy pattern hides the accident.** **→ A rare instance of "choose the pattern so the inevitable failure doesn't show" as an explicit design strategy.**
- **⚠️⚠️ The structural rider on stone: it is heavy, so the base cannot be light and cannot be anything but metal, and it will read as visually bulky.** **→ Choosing a stone top forecloses the delicate base you may have been picturing. The vault had nothing on base-vs-top coupling.**
- **⚠️ Металл — she advises AGAINST it for daily dining**: streaks after washing that no amount of polishing removes, dents, scratches, and it can oxidise and change colour. **Fine for small decorative or side tables that are barely used.**
