## Planning Rules / Project Management

- **If hiring a separate execution crew from the one that produced the design documentation, the client must actively verify the crew is following the drawings** — real recurring failure mode: crews reportedly struggled to read/follow the detailed written project documentation (language/literacy friction cited by the client), requiring the client to personally re-visit the site repeatedly to catch deviations (a real example: electrical conduit routed through the floor instead of the ceiling as specified). `single-account`.
- **Always over-provision "crossover"/two-way (проходной, перекрёстный) switch-wiring circuits during rough electrical** — a real project under-wired these, discovered only once finish electrical began, requiring the finish crew to re-cut wall channels (штроба) into an already-plastered wall and pull additional cable runs. Concrete illustration of why rough electrical is worth over-speccing rather than trimming to the exact minimum apparent need.
- **A worksite left unfinished/exposed for an extended pause is a real degradation risk, not just a schedule slip**: a real ~1.5-month work stoppage (during a contractor-transition dispute) resulted in drywall-joint seams separating, plaster falling off box-outs, and — most seriously — a section of ceiling collapsing during a later wall demolition because it had been fixed only to the (now-removed) wall framing rather than to the actual structural ceiling. No injuries in this case, but a genuine safety-relevant defect example.
- **Loggia insulation thickness must be planned before window placement is finalized** — a real project's first-pass loggia insulation was too thin, requiring a full teardown and even repositioning the window itself to accommodate the correct (thicker) insulation buildup needed to prevent the loggia from freezing.
- **A wrong-gauge circuit-cable replacement is a real, hard-to-diagnose failure mode**: a real project found an oven circuit non-functional after installers replaced an under-gauge cable (4mm² instead of the required 6mm²) — during that swap they mislabeled/miswired the new run (crossed neutral and ground from two different cables), which took roughly a month and an independent electrical inspection to correctly diagnose (the panel itself was cleared first, wrongly extending the search). Illustrates the value of an independent electrical inspection when a diagnosis stalls, and the risk of any circuit-cable re-pull done casually.
