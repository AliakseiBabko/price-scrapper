## Balcony / Loggia — Heat-Source and Glazing Prerequisites

1. **Fundamental principle stated explicitly**: insulation conserves
   heat, it does not generate it — without a real heat source (a heated
   floor or a radiator), any amount of insulation is described as
   pointless. **Water/hydronic radiators are illegal on a balcony/
   loggia** — independently corroborates this project's existing
   Zemskov-sourced legal-risk content on this exact restriction with a
   second, unrelated company's own stated practice. **Electric radiator
   is the legal alternative; the company's own recommended combination
   is heated floor + electric radiator.**
2. **Window glazing is a hard prerequisite, independent of insulation
   thickness**: even 10cm of insulation in two layers is described as
   pointless if the window frames are thin and draft-prone. **Choose a
   double-pane ("двухкамерный") glazing unit from a reputable
   manufacturer that genuinely doesn't draft.**
3. **Exterior insulation is the technically correct method but usually
   impossible in a modern ЖК** (facade modification is prohibited) — the
   company states interior insulation is, in practice, just as
   effective, which is why it's the majority method used.

## Balcony / Loggia — Insulation Material Comparison

4. **Three-material comparison, new to this page's existing insulation
   content**: **пеноплекс** (expanded/extruded polystyrene, "Комфорт"
   variant specifically recommended for a loggia among several product
   lines) — the default choice, bonds well to concrete/brick.
   **Mineral wool** (glass/rock/slag wool) — better suited to exterior
   application; needs much greater thickness than пеноплекс to achieve
   the same effect, consuming too much of a small loggia's floor area.
   **Пенофол** (foil-faced reflective roll insulation) — used
   specifically in combination with пеноплекс at heat-source locations
   (behind a radiator, under a heated floor) to reflect heat back into
   the room rather than losing it into the wall/floor mass.
5. **Пеноплекс thickness rule**: minimum **5cm** in a single layer for
   this climate; if doing two layers instead, use **2.5cm per layer**
   (10 layers total... i.e. two 2.5cm layers = 5cm combined), with the
   second layer's seams **staggered/offset (checkerboard) against the
   first layer's seams** specifically to eliminate any straight-through
   cold path at a panel joint that a single layer's seams alone would
   allow.
6. **Heated-floor-over-insulation layer stack, with a named failure
   mode it avoids**: пеноплекс → пенофол → **aquapanel (moisture-
   resistant cement board)** → heated-floor cable/mat → finish flooring.
   **Aquapanel is mandatory, not optional**: installing a heated-floor
   cable directly onto bare пеноплекс risks the insulation itself
   "burning out"/degrading from the direct heat — the layer separation
   exists specifically to prevent this.
7. **Company-owner-added screed rule for this same stack**: screed over
   this insulation stack must be **minimum 5cm** thick, or it risks
   detaching/cracking off the underlying пенофол-topped insulation.
   **Thermal-mass tradeoff, stated explicitly**: a thicker cable-heated
   screed also functions as a heat accumulator/battery — slower to heat
   up initially, but holds heat longer afterward, a genuine tradeoff
   rather than a pure downside.

## Flooring — ⚠️ Real Quartz-Vinyl-on-Heated-Loggia-Floor Failure Case

8. **⚠️ Named real documented failure, with the manufacturer's own
   response**: a client's quartz-vinyl flooring installed over a heated
   loggia floor developed gaps, cupping ("встал домиком"), bumps, and
   squeaking. When the company and client together approached the
   flooring manufacturer, the manufacturer stated the product *can* be
   heated but only up to a certain temperature, and effectively blamed
   the client/installer for exceeding it — no resolution or compensation
   was obtained. **Practical recommendation resulting from this case**:
   use ceramic tile or porcelain tile (керамогранит) over any heated
   loggia floor instead of quartz-vinyl, laminate, or engineered wood —
   cited reasoning: much better thermal conductivity, and no risk of
   cracking, squeaking, or gapping the way the failed case showed. A
   wood-look/vinyl-look porcelain tile exists as a close visual
   substitute if the laminate/vinyl aesthetic specifically is wanted.

## Balcony / Loggia — Installation Technique

9. **Full-contour insulation is required** (floor, walls, *and*
   ceiling) — any uninsulated section is a cold entry point. Sequence:
   dedust/prime the substrate; bond foam-board panels with foam
   adhesive; anchor with dish-head ("тарельчатые"/"грибы") plastic
   anchors — **⚠️ the anchor's own internal stem must be plastic, never
   metal** — a metal stem becomes a literal cold-transfer bridge through
   the insulation layer. Foam-seal every panel-to-panel seam. When doing
   two layers, the second layer is anchored to the *first insulation
   layer*, not the wall, using its own plastic dish anchors.
10. **Loggia-merged-with-living-room detail**: insulation must extend
    **30-50cm into the adjoining living room** past the former
    balcony-door threshold, to avoid leaving a sharp, uninsulated
    thermal-bridge contour line at the join — must be planned at the
    design-project stage, not improvised afterward.
