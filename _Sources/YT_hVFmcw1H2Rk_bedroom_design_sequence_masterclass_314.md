## Rules / Heuristics — The 12-Item Bedroom Design Sequence, in Full

**⚠️⚠️ Core methodology: place bedroom elements in a specific priority order, each one locking in real dimensions the next step depends on** — structural/fixed elements first, furniture next, decor/electrical-detail last. The stated sequence, with each item's own formula:

1. **Interior door** — position against one wall (not centered) to maximize the remaining wall run for the wardrobe. 10cm reveal on the near side; opening width = door-leaf width + 10cm (to fit the frame + foam joint — undersizing forces re-opening the wall with a rotary hammer over finished wallpaper/flooring later; oversizing leaves a foam joint too wide for the casing to cover). **Bedroom door-leaf width: 80cm optimal** (90cm is excessive — heavy, unnecessary for passage, sags prematurely; 70cm is acceptable but tight; 60cm only appropriate for a WC/technical room, not a bedroom). Door swings **inward**, never outward — with the 10cm reveal, it opens past 90° so even the handle doesn't protrude into the corridor; an inward-swinging door's stopper sits safely against a bedroom wall (not endangering fingers in the corridor) and striking someone already in the room is lower-probability/lower-consequence than striking someone passing in the corridor.
2. **Main light switch** — must be **inside** the room (never outside, "despite what designers love to do"), and on the **handle side** of the door (never the hinge side). Height: **90cm from finished floor, 15cm from the opening's edge** (matches the precise formula independently seen in `eezwcNG-1qI` from this same batch — a second corroborating instance). Must be a **pass-through (two-way) switch**, so the main light can be turned off from either side of the bed.
3. **Wardrobe** — placed on the entry wall specifically because doing so makes the room more compact/square, which improves how effectively daylight reaches the space. Custom-built, floor-to-ceiling, **60cm depth**. Length set so it stops **~30cm short of the door opening's edge** — positioned this way so that, once casings are installed, the light switch from step 2 ends up exactly centered between the door and the wardrobe. Door-panel (leaf) width for the wardrobe's own sliding/hinged sections: **35-60cm**.
4. **False wall** (conceals heating risers, recesses the radiator) — **150mm depth**, placed immediately after the wardrobe specifically to lock in the room's true final usable footprint before any furniture placement. Widens the windowsill, hides the radiator (less dust accumulation on it), and keeps the room a clean rectangle without dust-collecting niches. **⚠️ Full radiator-niche formula, the most complete version of this figure found in this store to date**: lower return **15cm** (so the finished baseboard doesn't cover the decorative grille); niche height = the radiator's own **inter-axial (center-to-center) dimension + 20cm** (for adequate convection); upper return **minimum 5cm** — if this comes out under 5cm, choose a different radiator model with a smaller inter-axial dimension and recalculate the whole niche, rather than accepting an undersized upper return.
5. **Curtain zone** — **20cm depth**, placed right after the false wall and wardrobe specifically to lock in the room's final dimension before bed placement. 20cm is wide enough to accommodate even a motorized/electric curtain track without giving up the tulle layer.
6. **Bed** — center point = the exact midpoint between the wardrobe (step 3) and the curtain zone (step 5). **Sleep-surface width: 180cm is the comfortable default**; shrink to 160cm if space genuinely doesn't allow 180cm; grow to 200cm for larger-than-average occupants or when space allows generously. The bed's overall (frame) width usually exceeds the sleep-surface width by 10-20cm (some models by as much as 30cm) — account for this when checking clearances. **Clearance on each side of the bed: minimum 50cm, ideally 60cm** — if a clearance would come out under 50cm, shrink the sleep-surface width (and the bed) rather than accept a cramped walkway; sleeping comfort isn't worth sacrificing the ability to actually use and clean the surrounding floor space.
7. **Nightstands** — placed immediately after the bed, but strictly *before* the bedside electrical points (step 8) are positioned. **Optimal size: 40×40cm** (enough surface area and drawer capacity without wasting floor space). **Optimal height: 50-55cm**, matching the mattress height of most modern beds.
8. **Bedside electrical points** — find the nightstand's own center axis, project it vertically, and place a combined block at **70cm from the floor** on that axis, containing (ordered nearest-to-bed outward): a night-light switch, then a pass-through main-light switch, then 1-2 power outlets. Mirror this exact same block on the opposite side of the bed.
9. **Night light** — individually switched per sleep side (a fully separate switch for each side, not shared). Cable-outlet position: on the nightstand/electrical block's own central axis, exactly **1m from the finished floor**, with **20cm of cable slack** left protruding from the wall for connection. Cable spec: **3-core VVGnG-LS, 1.5mm² cross-section**.
10. **TV zone** — typically a wall-hung console with pull-out drawers. Width ≈ the sleep-surface width from step 6. Centered on the bed's own axis. **Set the console's height only after placing an adjacent dresser or makeup table (step 12)**, ideally matching that piece's height exactly — but if the dresser has 4 drawer tiers, align the console to the **third** drawer's level, not the top, or the console (and the TV mounted above it) ends up too high.
11. **TV-zone electrical points** — two independent groups on the console's own vertical center axis. **Group 1, at 160cm from the floor**: TV outlet, internet/ethernet outlet, power outlet, plus a cable channel running down to Group 2. **Group 2, at 10cm from the top edge of the TV console**: two power outlets plus an internet outlet (for a set-top box/receiver), plus a cable channel running up to the TV itself.
12. **Makeup table or dresser** (whichever the client needs) — placed only after every other item above is positioned, filling the leftover space between the TV zone and the curtain zone. **Minimum width 60cm, maximum 100cm.** For a makeup table specifically, the mirror should start directly at the countertop surface with no gap, for real usability — and hairdryer/styling-tool outlets should be positioned **under** the table, not exposed on the visible face.
