## Genuinely New Content

- **Explicit rejection of two materials as dated/unsuitable for any modern quality renovation**: vagonka (tongue-and-groove wood board cladding) and PVC panels — described bluntly as looking like a shed interior ("сарай"), regardless of budget.
- **Photo-wallpaper vs. mural panel distinction, a named taxonomy not previously on this page**: "фотообои" (multi-roll photo-wallpaper, typically ~5 rolls that must align to form one continuous scene) vs. "панно" (a single oversized roll/sheet printed and hung as one continuous picture, no seam-alignment risk).
- **Fresco material profile, first mention on this page**: a large single continuous painted-fabric mural (worked example up to 5m long × 3m tall) — expensive, must be hung as one uninterrupted piece with zero bubbles/damage; any damage to the installed panel essentially forces full replacement (no patch repair).
- **Substrate-prep cost tiers by material group, prices in RUB/m² (USD normalized)**: plain wallpaper / paintable wallpaper / fresco all share the same prep (plaster, primer, two coats starting putty, inter-coat sanding, primer) — the cheapest prep tier. Paint and decorative plaster share a **more expensive** prep tier: the same wallpaper-prep steps **plus** a fiberglass-mesh (стеклохолст) layer, finish putty (two coats), inter-coat sanding, and a further primer coat before the paint/decorative layer goes on.
- **Simple/facade-style decorative plaster ("короед"-type, textured/bark finish) has the cheapest prep of any decorative-finish material** — just a flat plastered+primed wall, occasionally putty, no finish-putty/mesh layer — rougher final look ("not a wet-silk premium finish") but unconditionally wet-room-safe (facade-rated formulas tolerate any humidity/temperature swing).
- **Installation-cost ladder, RUB/m² (USD normalized)**: plain wallpaper ≈**650 RUB (≈$10)**; paint ≈**800 RUB (≈$10)**; decorative plaster **800-2,500 RUB (≈$10-$30)**, a genuinely wide range depending on complexity/finish.
- **⚠️ Tile installation cost scales dramatically with format, same simple prep across all formats (plaster+primer only)**: regular tile ≈**3,000 RUB/m² (≈$30)**; large-format (≈80×160cm) **5,000-6,000 RUB/m² (≈$60-$70)**; mega-format (e.g. 140×260cm) **>10,000 RUB/m² (≈$110+)**.
- **⚠️ Real cited large-format-tile logistics cost case**: a client living on the 2nd floor was charged ≈**10,000 RUB (≈$110) per panel** just for careful vertical transport (elevator too small for the panel) of ~20 mega-format panels — **≈200,000 RUB (≈$2,200) spent purely on delivery/carrying**, a real, non-obvious cost driver distinct from material or installation labor.
- **Panel material group installation-cost spread, same simple substrate as tile**: prefab soft/upholstered panel, glue-and-mount only ≈**800 RUB/m² (≈$10)**; a gypsum panel with a textured/wavy surface needing hand-painted finishing after mounting (seams filled/sanded, full surface hand-painted to match the texture) **3,000-4,500 RUB/m² (≈$30-$50)** — a labor-intensive "artistic" finish, not a simple mount; plain wood panel cladding needs no further finishing at all.
- **MDF/soft-panel moisture caveat, phrased precisely**: tolerant of ambient humidity, but not of direct water contact — avoid in genuine splash zones (shower area, etc.), distinct from a merely humid room.
- **Flooring-material-as-wall-cladding (laminate/parquet board applied to a wall) explicitly deprecated by the speaker as a practical wall finish**: cheap prep/install, but poor outside-corner detailing and no durability advantage — framed as acceptable only as a small decorative accent, not a wall-finish strategy.
- **Stone veneer (тонкий шпон камня) flagged as a genuinely new, unproven material**: minimal prep (flat wall, two basic putty coats), manufacturer claims wet-room suitability, but the speaker explicitly withholds a recommendation since it's too recently introduced to have real long-term performance data — directly compared in the transcript to the same caution he applies to quartz-vinyl flooring (a separate emerging material, covered in another video).
- **⚠️ Behind-furniture area cost-skip rule with a worked example**: any wall area that will be fully hidden behind built-in furniture (kitchen units, wardrobes) needs no finish layer beyond putty+primer — never decorative plaster or finish putty — since it's permanently invisible; explicitly refutes the "but I might replace my kitchen later" objection (a full furniture swap always triggers a full cosmetic refresh anyway, so pre-finishing hidden wall now doesn't save future work). **Worked example, 50m² apartment (≈150m² total wall area, ≈30m² — 1/5 — behind kitchen units and 1-2 closets)**: skipping decorative finish there saves ≈**60,000 RUB (≈$660)** labor + ≈**20,000 RUB (≈$220)** material = **≈80,000 RUB (≈$880) total**, worth roughly a week off the project schedule.
- **Selection heuristic explicitly framed as visual-plus-tactile, not visual alone**: judge a candidate material by how it actually feels underfoot/underhand (paint's smooth feel vs. a "wet silk"-style decorative plaster's texture), not from photos alone. **Zoning rule**: reserve a more damage-resistant material (e.g. decorative plaster) for heavy-daily-traffic zones like an entryway/hallway even if the rest of the apartment uses a cheaper material like wallpaper.
