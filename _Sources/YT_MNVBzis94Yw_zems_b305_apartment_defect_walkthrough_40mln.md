## Kitchen

- **Tall pull-out cabinet organizers reachable only via a 2-3-motion sequence** (push-release, pull, then a second motion to reach contents behind) — photogenic for a kitchen photoshoot but an active annoyance for daily use; framed as a "looks good in photos, bad to actually cook with" tradeoff. `single-account`.
- **Kitchen electrical-outlet height set too high relative to the countertop, so the outlet body physically collides with the countertop edge** — a plug cannot be inserted normally. Concrete measurable defect, demonstrated on camera. **Cross-reference**: this store already has a 110cm-from-floor kitchen outlet-height standard with a "plug is 90°-to-cable, too-low clearance forces upside-down insertion" mechanism from a different source (`yt_WVfgE0lHgVc`) — this is the inverse failure mode (too high, not too low), corroborating that outlet height vs. countertop clearance is a genuinely error-prone dimension in both directions.
- **Integrated/flush sink cutout (no rim) reaffirmed as "one of the best renovation solutions"** — but the counter strip to its left was cut too narrow, so anything set down there slides directly into the sink. Rule: a flush-sink cutout still needs adequate adjacent counter width; the cutout style doesn't excuse undersizing the surrounding surface.
- **Faucet spout too long relative to a narrow adjacent counter section** — water splashes past the sink onto a nearby leak-protection sensor, triggering a false leak alarm nearly every morning per the walkthrough. Rule: match faucet reach to actual counter/sink geometry, and don't position a leak sensor directly in a faucet's splash path.
- **Decorative dining-area pendant lights demonstrated as insufficient sole primary light source** — the room only reads as adequately lit because a separate large ceiling fixture is also on; with only the pendants lit, the room is visibly dim. Rule: verify a decorative fixture's adequacy as primary lighting by testing it alone, not combined with other sources still on. **Cross-reference**: extends this store's existing kitchen mandatory-task-lighting rule (`yt_WVfgE0lHgVc`) to a second failure mode (decorative pendant as sole *general* light, not just missing task light).
- **Decorative wall panels installed without centering electrical outlets/switches within each panel bay** — visual/functional clash between the panel joint lines and the electrical points.

## Plumbing

- **Water-supply shutoff/distribution node (узел приёма воды) installed inside an entryway coat closet** — flagged as a poor room/function pairing (a coat-storage space repurposed for plumbing access). The closet's leak-protection sensor system on that same node is separately praised as good practice, independent of the placement choice.
- **Extractor-fan switch for a bathroom/WC located physically outside the room it serves** — restated as a standing rule the presenter says he's repeated many times: the switch should be near the room it operates, reachable at the moment of need. **Cross-reference**: directly corroborates this store's existing bathroom-switch-usability pattern ("never place the fan switch outside the bathroom itself," from `yt_s5Gyi8Rd9D0`) — second independent source for the same rule.

## Doors / Trim

- **Two visually identical doors in the same unit installed to swing in different/opposite directions** — flagged as a confusing, avoidable inconsistency; a user expecting the same behavior from twin-looking doors is surprised.
- **A ~75cm-deep built-in storage recess (sized for a full-depth washer plus plumbing behind it) had mismatched trim/reveal widths on its two sides (5cm vs. 15cm)**. Rule: when a deep recess is intentional, plan symmetric trim/reveal widths on both sides in advance, not as an afterthought.

## Materials / Walls / Ceilings

- **Laminate flooring (a floating-floor product) used as ceiling decorative cladding instead of a proper decorative ceiling panel product** — flagged as a material misuse, not just a cosmetic quibble: laminate is not rated for adhesive/overhead mounting, expands and contracts with ambient humidity swings, and was already visibly detaching from the ceiling at time of filming. **Stated safety concern, not only durability**: if the unit were ever flooded from above even lightly, panels risk detaching and falling from the ceiling. `single-account`, demonstrated on camera (visible detachment).
- **Paint-finish (non-wallpaper) wall treatment specifically along an exterior facade wall is prone to cracking**, attributed to that wall's larger temperature/humidity swings versus interior walls. This is a recurring claim from this presenter across multiple already-processed sources in this store — `single-account`/repeated-channel-opinion, not independently corroborated by a non-Zemstandart source.

## Furniture / Built-ins

- **Closet clothing rod (штанга) height set for a tall person specifically** — average- and shorter-height household members can't reach it even jumping. Rule: confirm the actual household's height range before fixing a hanging-rod height; don't default to a single "tall" reference height.
- **A developer-inherited layout niche kept "as-is" by the designer to save space produced a non-standard width that fits no standard furniture size** (either an uncomfortable gap or overflow). Presenter's stated rule of thumb: a kept dead-space niche like this isn't actually space savings — either wall it off entirely or hand the full room dimension back; don't leave an ambiguous in-between gap.
- **Bedside clearance: only 15cm of clear space on each side of the bed to the nightstand** (a stated 30cm total shortfall against what's needed), attributed to a compounding placement/sizing error across bed width, nightstand placement, and electrical-point calculation. Stated practical consequence: an occupant reaching for a bedside light switch while lying down can fall out of bed. The clearance measurement itself is demonstrated on camera (`confirmed`); the fall-risk consequence is the presenter's own characterization (`single-account`).
