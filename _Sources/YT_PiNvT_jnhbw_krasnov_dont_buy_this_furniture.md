## Furniture/Built-ins, Kitchen Appliances, Other Appliances, Mistakes/Warnings

- **⚠️ Corner/sectional sofa depreciation and resale problems — a bundle, all statistics tagged `unverified`/his own claims, no source named**: his own client-base observation (anecdotal, not a market statistic): roughly **6 of 10** studio clients reportedly prefer corner sofas for living rooms, considering them most comfortable/functional. Corner sofas are designed for a SPECIFIC room layout and often don't transfer well to a new home; most come in fixed left/right-handed configurations, expensive/impractical to reconfigure. **Resale-value claim (unverified/uncredited)**: corner sofas reportedly lose up to **75% of original value within 3 years** of use. **Logistics claim (uncredited)**: nearly half of buyers reportedly encounter door/elevator-passage problems moving a corner sofa, sometimes requiring disassembly, window-hoisting, or forced returns. Recommends MODULAR sofas instead for flexibility, or if choosing a corner sofa, models with reconfigurable/swappable sides specifically to reduce future-move risk. States corner sofas are genuinely fine if confident in the floor plan for the next 5-10 years or not concerned about future furniture cost.
- **⚠️ Low-profile sofa ergonomics — a specific, checkable numeric range**: low sofas commonly have a seat height of **30-38cm from floor** — getting up becomes a genuine challenge, especially for guests in restrictive clothing or with back problems; also provide insufficient back/leg support for extended sitting. Recommends **40-50cm seat height** instead for a similar modern look without the ergonomic penalty.
- **⚠️ Backless bar stools — detailed ergonomic critique with a specific posture benchmark**: no back support means back muscles must constantly work to hold upright posture, leading to fatigue and potential lower-back pain over time (cites uncredited "research" on posture/prolonged unsupported sitting — tag `unverified`). A missing or poorly-positioned footrest causes additional leg/back muscle tension. **Optimal seated posture explicitly stated: knees at 90°, feet resting on a solid surface** — a specific, checkable ergonomic benchmark. Recommends models with both back support and a footrest for anyone spending significant time on a bar stool.
- **⚠️ Glass furniture safety — extends existing glass-safety content with injury specifics and a serious personal case**: tempered glass, though more impact-resistant, doesn't deform under a sharp point-impact the way wood/metal does — it cracks/shatters outright, and fracture risk from a heavy object dropped on a CORNER specifically is meaningfully higher than for wood/MDF equivalents (though glass scratches less). Recommends matte glass (fewer visible prints/smudges) and laminated/multi-layer "sandwich" tempered glass (less brittle on impact) as mitigations. **⚠️ Real, specific, serious anecdote**: a friend's child was playing and accidentally broke a glass table, requiring an ambulance call and stitches — Krasnov states this incident is specifically why he personally will never have glass furniture in his own home as a parent, and why his studio rarely uses glass in any element that could face impact or load. A genuinely concrete, documented safety case elevating the existing glass-table caution content.
- **⚠️ Colored/retro appliances (names Smeg specifically) — full critique bundle, flag brand per advertising filter**: replacement/repair difficulty — manufacturers update color palettes over time, so finding a matching replacement color (or matching parts/accessories) years later can be effectively impossible, especially for limited-edition color runs. Color-trend fatigue — a currently-fashionable saturated color (mint, pastel pink) may read as dated once broader interior trends shift, becoming a liability rather than an asset. Cost structure — most of a branded colorful appliance's price premium reportedly pays for brand/design rather than functional upgrades (comparable core function exists in cheaper models), and replacement/repair cost is correspondingly high if it breaks. Krasnov's own personal admission — he genuinely loves Smeg aesthetically, but recommends neutral-toned appliances instead for anyone who can't casually absorb replacement cost or wants flexibility as the surrounding interior evolves.
- **⚠️ Upholstery textured-fabric critique — bouclé and ribbed/textured velvet specifically**: bouclé's knotted/looped surface («узелковая поверхность») is prone to snagging on sharp objects or pet claws over time, degrading appearance. Ribbed-texture velvet («рёбристая поверхность») tends to accumulate dust, crumbs, and pet hair, and can retain odors especially in poorly-ventilated rooms. Recommends smooth-surfaced upholstery fabrics instead — linen, cotton, and a **smooth-finish velvet** is explicitly distinguished as an acceptable alternative to the ribbed/textured kind ("бархат" as smooth vs. "вельвет" as ribbed — the transcript's own distinction) — for durability and easier maintenance.
- **⚠️ Cheap synthetic rugs — detailed material-science critique bundle, genuinely new and useful, record as a full set**: viscose and polyester rug fibers have low mechanical durability — visible wear paths/flattened pile/faded pattern appear within about a year in high-traffic zones (hallways, living rooms, under dining tables). Synthetic fibers don't "recover"/spring back after crushing the way natural fibers do. Poor moisture response — viscose/polyester rugs can lose shape when wet and stains can become permanently set. Poor UV/light resistance — synthetic rugs (especially polyester) can visibly fade within a single season under direct sunlight as pigments degrade, versus natural rugs fading gradually. Recommends natural-fiber rugs instead (wool, cotton, blends with linen or silk) — wool specifically praised for natural elasticity/pile recovery under load plus natural oils providing some inherent stain resistance; cotton/linen rugs praised as easier-care, moisture-tolerant, suitable for households with kids or allergy sufferers.
- **⚠️ Rug quality-check technique, genuinely new and practical**: part the pile and look at the base fiber color — a genuinely good rug has consistent color all the way down the fiber, while a cheap synthetic rug only has surface-level dye/pigment applied on top ("у хорошего ковра цвет одинаковый абсолютно по всей длине волокна, а у дешёвого синтетического пигмент нанесён только сверху"). A specific, actionable, in-store quality test. Acknowledges synthetic rugs have a legitimate place for temporary use or frequent style-changing, but recommends natural fiber for anyone wanting a rug that holds up for years.
