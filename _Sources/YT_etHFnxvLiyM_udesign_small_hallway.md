## ⚠️⚠️ 1. THE METHOD — and the mistake it corrects

> ***«Как правило, проектируя прихожую, мы мыслим категорией, что лишь бы влез шкаф.»***

**Her alternative is to follow the entry sequence and give each step a place:**

1. **⚠️⚠️ FIRST MOVE: free your hands.** Keys, bag, hat, scarf, gloves — so **a small shelf or a wall-hung opening box near the door, plus a hook for an umbrella.** *«Первое движение — освободить руки.»*
2. **⚠️⚠️ Open hooks, not only the wardrobe** — because a **wet coat should not go straight into a closed cabinet**, and because guests are easier to accommodate on a hook than by sending them to hang things inside your wardrobe. **Her preferred detail: hooks concealed BEHIND A PANEL** — still open and instant to use, but not producing visual clutter.
3. **⚠️ A FULL-LENGTH mirror, positioned at the door itself — not several metres down the hall** — because it is used on the way out, to check the whole outfit. *«Маленького зеркала в прихожей нам недостаточно.»* It may be wall-mounted, a mirrored wardrobe door, or **a mirrored panel on the entrance door itself.**
4. **⚠️⚠️ An OPEN shoe rack, one or two steps in** — deliberately open, so wet shoes dry rather than being shut away damp.
5. **Seating follows automatically from the shoe rack** — somewhere to sit and change shoes; **she offers a fold-down bench for a tight hallway.**
6. **Then, and only then, the closed wardrobe** — seasonal coats and shoes, hats, scarves, gloves, spare bags.

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ The transferable finding is the ORDER.** Every item on this list is individually obvious; the vault's hallway pages hold most of them. **What is new is that the wardrobe comes LAST, and that four of the six needs are met by things too small to appear on a furniture plan.** In a hallway where the wardrobe is the only thing that fits, this method says the wardrobe is the wrong thing to fit first.

## ⚠️⚠️ 2. Drying shoes — a services consequence of a furniture decision

- **⚠️⚠️ Underfloor heating in the hallway is recommended specifically because shoes dry better on it** — a functional justification the vault's UFH material does not carry.
- **⚠️ Or a socket positioned for an electric shoe dryer** (the insert type).
- **⚠️ She ties the need to households with children** — *«взрослые как-то поаккуратнее… а вот у детей может такое случаться»*.

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ This is the item with the earliest deadline in the whole source.** Both answers — UFH loop and a socket in the right place — **are settled at first-fix and are unavailable later**, while the shoe rack they serve is bought last. **Belongs in the hallway electrical/heating planning, not only in the furniture list.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 3. Passage widths — stated as starting points and then explicitly relativised

| Passage | Her figure |
| :--- | :--- |
| Minimum / optimal passage | **70 cm** |
| A corridor proper | **90–100 cm** |

> ***«Но я бы сильно не привязывалась к этим цифрам. Я вам их озвучиваю как такие отправные точки… потому что будет зависеть ещё от длины вашего коридора.»***

**Her reasoning, and it is the useful part: a narrow corridor is uncomfortable in proportion to its LENGTH.** A short one can be narrower without penalty — *«зато вы шкаф полноценный поставите»* — while a long narrow one should not be.

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ This converts a fixed clearance into a TRADE, which is what a small hallway actually needs.** The vault holds corridor-segmentation and proportion-control techniques; **this adds the explicit permission to buy wardrobe depth with corridor width when the corridor is short, and the reason it is safe to do so.** ⚠️ **She offers no length threshold, so the trade is judgement, not arithmetic.**
>
> ⚠️ **Compare [[_Sources/YT__uYgVG6qnHU_kulekina_wardrobe_vs_walkin|Кулекина's 80 cm]] minimum aisle in front of walk-in storage, in this same batch. The 10 cm difference is consistent with the different task — passing through versus standing still and choosing.**

## ⚠️ 4. Wardrobe depth, and the ladder down from 60 cm

- **Full depth 60 cm** if it fits — *«очень нам повезло»*.
- **⚠️ Otherwise off-the-shelf wardrobes now exist at 50, 45 and 40 cm deep**, and bespoke at any depth. **→ A specific, checkable ladder; the vault had 60 cm as the figure without the fallbacks.**
- **⚠️⚠️ Full-height to the ceiling, always** — because everything seasonal and everything *«жалко выкинуть»* goes on the antresol. **And the stated failure mode: *«не оставляйте сверху 40–50 см пустого пространства»*, because if storage is short that gap WILL fill with boxes and read as mess.** **→ The argument is not aesthetic — it is that the gap gets used whether or not you designed it.** Ready-made wardrobes in many heights, and models with antresols, are named as the affordable route to this.

## ⚠️ 5. Hinged vs. sliding doors — she argues against sliding, with a reason the vault did not have

**Sliding only for an ultra-narrow corridor** where you cannot step back half a pace to open a door. **Otherwise she prefers hinged**, on four grounds:

1. Looks more current and **does not date**; you can refresh it by changing handles alone.
2. Sliding fronts that suit current taste are **more expensive** than they used to be.
3. **⚠️⚠️ The observational one: *«раздвижные двери, как правило, всегда остаются чуть-чуть открытыми… неудобно каждый раз этот шкаф до конца закрывать. Всё равно какая-то секция будет приоткрыта, и всегда это будет ощущение бардака.»*** Stated from visiting friends' homes, not from theory.
4. Cheaper to buy.

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ Point 3 is the genuinely new one and it is a USE finding, not a spec finding** — a sliding wardrobe fails at "looking tidy" through how people actually operate it, which no datasheet reports. **Corroborates the vault's existing four-reasons-against-sliding entry from a fabricator, arriving from an entirely different direction (a designer observing households).** ⚠️ **Anecdotal by her own framing.**

## ⚠️ 6. Mirrors as the narrow-hallway remedy — with both caveats

- **A fully mirrored wardrobe front** where the wardrobe has made the corridor too narrow; or **the opposite wall in mirror** if the wardrobe is a ready-made one. **She states she has used it on client projects and that it works.**
- **⚠️ Caveat one, hers: with pets or small children it will be permanently covered in handprints — decide how it gets cleaned.**
- **⚠️⚠️ Caveat two, also hers, and it cuts against the technique: she dislikes mirroring a LONG corridor, because it visually doubles the length — *«визуально этот коридор ещё в два раза удлиняем»*.** **→ The remedy for a narrow hallway is a hazard in a long one; the same axis as §3.**
- Mirror is named as **an inexpensive material**, available bespoke or in ready elements.

## ⚠️ 7. Lighting — and the two coordination traps

- **Hallways usually have no daylight, so light must be designed, not left to one fitting.** Several scenarios: **general light covering the whole space, plus local light AT THE MIRROR, positioned to avoid shadows and distortion on the face.**
- **⚠️ Two-way (проходные) switches for a long corridor** — on at the entrance, off from the bedroom, so nobody walks back through a dark hall. **A first-fix wiring decision.**
- **⚠️⚠️ THE COORDINATION TRAP, and it is specific: where wardrobes run to the ceiling, check the ceiling fittings do not obstruct the antresol doors opening, and are not set so close to the fronts that they foul them.** *«Это тоже всё продумывается заранее.»*

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ A real collision between two trades that are specified separately — lighting layout and joinery — and it is only discoverable on a drawing that carries both.** ⚠️ **The vault already holds shadowless face lighting at the mirror from Безверхая; the fitting-versus-antresol clash is new.**

## 8. The colour argument — recorded as attributed opinion

**Her position, which she credits to her own teachers: rooms you pass through rather than dwell in are where you can take a colour risk** — you will not tire of a bold hallway because you are never in it for long, but you get the lift every time you come home. ⚠️ **Attributed opinion. Noted because it is a reasoned position rather than a preference, but it is not a finding.**
