## Kitchen Appliances / Other Appliances — Towel Warmer Selection

- **Named hydronic product**: Sunerzha "Сирокко," a ladder-style,
  bottom-connection design radiator made from food-grade, low-carbon,
  acid-resistant stainless steel (AISI 304L), rated for high
  temperatures, with a "Polimer protect" internal polymer coating stated
  to protect against electrochemical corrosion.
- **Sizing convention**: choose unit size by room size, with margin — this
  project used 100 cm × 50 cm. **A concrete power-sizing rule: ~40-50 W
  per m² of room area.**
- **Towel warmers are a room-heating fixture, not just a towel/laundry
  dryer** — they run continuously in the bathroom, and modern (larger)
  bathrooms need adequately powered units to actually heat the space, not
  just dry items.
- **Electric towel-warmer subtypes, a genuinely useful distinction not
  previously in this store**: (1) **liquid-filled models** — a heating
  element (≥300W) plus a control unit heats an internal heat-transfer
  fluid (household antifreeze), which acts as a thermal-mass buffer,
  continuing to radiate heat after the element switches off; (2)
  **heating-cable-based models** — lower power, continuous (uncycled)
  energy draw with no thermal-mass buffering, so heat output is
  continuous rather than intermittent.

## A Rigorous Electric-Towel-Warmer Operating-Cost Calculation — Materially Higher Than This Store's Existing Figure

- **A common misconception directly rebutted**: an electric towel warmer
  does *not* draw roughly the same as a 60W light bulb — a unit that
  small exists but can't heat a room, only dry "two pairs of socks."
- **Real worked calculation for a 1m × 50cm electric unit**: 300W max

## Hydronic vs. Electric — Cost, Lifespan, Control, and Placement

- **From a reputable brand, hydronic and electric units of comparable
  quality cost about the same** — cost varies far more by *model/design*
  than by type. A cheap "industrial minimalist" hydronic radiator can be
  found for free discarded near any new building's construction dumpster;
  a premium seamless stainless-steel design radiator costs meaningfully
  more.
- **Lifespan**: a hydronic towel warmer lasts at least 10, often 20 years
  — units the speaker installed 10-15 years earlier are still working.
  Electric lifespan is less certain — the heating element (тэн) will
  likely need eventual replacement.
- **The "100% rule" for a hydronic choice**: only install a genuinely
  good-quality unit — a cheap, unreliable one is "a delayed-action bomb"
  (leak risk). Pairing a good unit with a leak-protection system gives
  close to complete (the speaker says "99%, basically 100%") flood
  protection.
- **Electric units carry zero flood risk regardless of quality**, but a
  cheap electric unit still carries real reliability and electric-shock-
  risk concerns — buy a quality electric unit too, for different reasons
  than the hydronic case.
- **Usability/control**: electric is more convenient to operate — power
  is adjustable and a shutoff timer is available. A classic hydronic unit
  has no built-in temperature control, but adding shutoff/control valves
  ("запорная арматура") lets a hydronic unit's temperature be regulated
  too.
- **Placement**: electric can go almost anywhere except directly in a wet
  zone (e.g. a shower) because of its 220V outlet. Hydronic must be
  positioned where water piping can actually reach it, and not every wall
  can have chases cut into it for the supply pipes.
- **Decision heuristic, stated directly**: in a new building with sound
  new pipes, real grounding, and thick walls that can be chased easily —
  recommend hydronic (more economical over the long run). In an old
  khrushchevka with thin walls, old rusty pipes, high leak risk, and no
  grounding — recommend electric, because "it's not the time to
  economize."

## Grounding — A Concrete Mitigation for the Existing Stray-Current Risk

- **Mandatory grounding for a hydronic towel warmer**, framed as a direct
  mitigation for electrochemical corrosion from stray/galvanic currents —
  this is a concrete remediation for the stray-current corrosion risk
  already documented on `Hygienic_Shower_and_Towel_Warmer.md` (which
  previously only described the failure mode, not a fix).
- **Cable spec used**: 4mm² (multi-strand) grounding cable — code calls
  for 6mm², but the speaker couldn't physically fit that gauge under the
  decorative caps. The developer's original grounding wire was only
  2.5mm², implicitly under-spec even against the 4mm² compromise used
  here. Grounding connects both the towel warmer itself and the hot/cold
  risers via КТР (crimp) connectors, soldered rather than just crimped for
  extra reliability.

## Installation Technique and Sequencing

- **Kit contents**: decorative covers, eccentrics, gaskets, a hex key,
  water outlets, plugs, plus top mounting hardware and extenders.
- **Installation sequence**: starts with re-soldering the developer's
  jumper and disassembling/rebuilding the inlet node (already documented
  in earlier episodes this round) — water must be fully shut off and
  confirmed off before starting. The hot riser is cut and rebuilt with a
  new assembly incorporating a quality shutoff valve (replacing the
  developer's with a union-fitting-equipped one), a leak-protection valve,
  and the bypass jumper. **Extends the leak-protection-coverage-gap fix
  flagged in this round's first video**: two additional Neptun valves are
  bought specifically to extend the shared leak-protection module's
  coverage to the towel-warmer branch. Everything is pre-assembled on a
  table before the water shutoff window opens.
- **Connection scheme**: bottom connection, "scheme #2" per Sunerzha's own
  published connection-diagram/common-mistakes reference (an external
  manufacturer resource, not itself extracted in detail here, but worth
  noting its existence for a future cross-check).
- **Pipe/insulation**: basalt-fiber-reinforced polypropylene pipe, with a
  foam insulation sleeve slid onto the pipe before installation.
- **Leveling technique**: outlets set level using quick-setting
  gypsum/plaster; a threaded pipe nipple used as a leveling reference tool
  — first outlet fixed, then the second referenced off it.
- **Temporary anti-tip bracing**: a bent-wire brace holds the unit steady
  on its lower mounting bracket until the top bracket (installed only
  after tiling) is in place.
- **Sequencing dependency, extending the earlier "frame/heater before
  manifold" rule**: the manifold wasn't finally wall-mounted until *after*
  the towel-warmer pipes were routed, due to limited space — all these
  stages are interdependent on this specific job, not strictly linear.
