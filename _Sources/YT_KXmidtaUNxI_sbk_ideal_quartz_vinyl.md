## Materials — Quartz-Vinyl Flooring: Real Terminology and Selection Criteria

- **"Quartz-vinyl" is a Russian-market marketing catch-all, not a real material category**: the term covers at least four genuinely distinct constructions — **LVT** (Luxury Vinyl Tile, stone/tile-look), **LVP** (Luxury Vinyl Plank, wood-look), **WPC** (Wood-Plastic Composite base), and **SPC** (Stone-Plastic Composite base) — Russian retailers/marketers merged all of them under one word to avoid confusing buyers with acronyms, regardless of whether the actual filler is quartz, marble powder, or something else.
- **Glue-down (3-layer) vs. locking (4-layer) construction, real structural difference**: glue-down tile = protective polyurethane top layer + a printed pattern layer + a PVC base — thin and flexible, so it **telegraphs any subfloor unevenness directly into the finished floor**; requires a genuinely flat substrate. Locking tile adds a 4th fiberglass layer that holds its shape/geometry, is laid over an underlay (adds soundproofing, compensates minor unevenness), and tolerates a less-perfect substrate.
- **⚠️ WPC base has a real, named durability weakness**: feels warm/soft/comfortable underfoot and adds sound insulation, but **fears moisture** — over time moisture penetrates the adhesive layer and the planks delaminate; also prone to thermal deformation (direct sunlight through uncurtained windows can cause boards to warp/spread and locks to separate).
- **SPC base is the correct choice for radiant (water) underfloor heating** and for wet zones (showers/bathrooms) — thicker (4–12mm), stiffer, more wear-resistant (thicker top layer), UV-stable, not prone to thermal expansion. Tradeoff: less pleasant underfoot and slightly worse sound insulation than WPC-based product.
- **⚠️ Large-area (≥~150 m²) single-run installations should use glue-down, not locking/floating** — a floating/locking floor across a very large continuous area carries a real risk of the locks separating and the floor "tenting."
- **Uneven-subfloor decision rule**: if unevenness is within building-code tolerance, use locking + underlay and accept the residual risk; if the subfloor is genuinely flat, glue-down is safe and telegraphs nothing.
- **⚠️ Three named buyer mistakes**: (1) **choosing by price alone** — a materially thinner protective wear layer on a cheaper product fails under real foot traffic (the source's own example: office/commercial-grade traffic on residential-grade product); (2) **using LVT (WPC-adjacent) instead of SPC in a house with water-based underfloor heating** — delaminates quickly, gets torn out; (3) **skipping a hands-on quality check** — press on the locking mechanism by hand; if it **crumbles**, the product will crack/fail within 1–3 months regardless of brand claims.
- **Practical buying sequence recommended**: decide your actual use-case requirements first (heating type, room size/shape, subfloor condition, wet-zone exposure) — only then compare price, and always check manufacturer reviews/specs and consult your own installer before committing.
