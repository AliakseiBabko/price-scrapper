## Planning Rules / Design Concept — Whole-Home Layout Philosophy

- **Principle 2 — favor fewer, larger, airier rooms over many small
  rooms ("отказ от цыганского подхода")**: the source explicitly frames a
  design trend from ~15-20 years ago (maximizing room *count* within a
  small footprint, producing many 9-10m² rooms and narrow, dark
  corridors) as outdated. **Modern value shift: prioritize a sense of
  space/openness/air over squeezing in more, smaller rooms** — directly
  generalizable to any compact-footprint layout decision, including a
  renovation replan, not just new construction.
- **Principle 3 — dedicate one bedroom as a true "master bedroom" (own
  en-suite bathroom + walk-in closet) even in a genuinely small home,
  rather than sacrificing it for one more generic room.** Stated
  explicitly as worth the floor-area cost even under space pressure —
  connects to Principle 2's own logic (fewer, better-equipped spaces over
  more numerous, thinner ones).
- **Principle 4 — large-glazing tradeoff, a genuine two-sided design
  tension**: floor-to-ceiling glazing ("walls as glass") maximizes light
  and a sense of air/space, but at real cost to privacy and thermal/
  energy efficiency (heat loss) — the source explicitly frames this as a
  balance to find, not a rule to maximize blindly. Directly relevant to
  window-sizing/glazing decisions in any renovation, not house-specific.
- **⚠️ Principle 6 — built-in furniture as the practical enabler of
  compactness**: recommended specifically because a compact/lean floor
  plan (per Principle 2) leaves no room for separate dedicated closets in
  many cases — built-in furniture (1) hides into the room's own finish
  rather than reading as freestanding furniture, (2) is inherently
  multifunctional (a TV built into a wardrobe front is the source's own
  example), and (3) **can be used to square off an irregular room shape**
  (e.g. an L-shaped room's awkward corner) into a visually regular one —
  this squaring-off use is a specific technique this vault's existing
  built-in-furniture content doesn't already record as explicitly.
- **⚠️ Principle 7 — plan dedicated storage/pantry space at the design
  stage, not as an afterthought**: explicit critique of layouts that look
  good on paper but have no answer for where bedding, kitchenware, or
  bulky equipment (a lawnmower is the source's own example, house-
  specific) actually gets stored — **the underlying planning discipline
  ("a home should be autonomous enough to solve its own future occupants'
  storage needs by design, not by improvising later") generalizes
  directly to an apartment renovation's own storage planning.**
- **⚠️ Principle 9 — the kitchen deserves dedicated architect-level design
  attention, not just post-construction furniture shopping**: explicit
  critique that many clients (and even some architects, deferring this
  entirely to a separate furniture-shop consultation after the building
  is finished) treat kitchen cabinetry as an afterthought, when the
  amount of built-in equipment involved means the cabinet layout has to
  be right from the start. Recommends thinking through the cabinet
  configuration and island options (to offload the main worksurface,
  corroborating this vault's existing kitchen-island content) at the
  actual design stage. Largely corroborates already-recorded kitchen
  content on this vault rather than adding new numeric detail.
- **⚠️ Principle 10 — multifunctional/transformable room use as this
  source's own stated "future" of home design, a genuine independent
  corroboration of this household's own Phase 2 plan**: a living room
  furnished as a normal living room (sofa, coffee table, TV) can convert
  to a guest bedroom at night via a **transforming partition**
  (перегородка-трансформер) — the source names this as the concrete
  worked example of the principle. Also applies the same logic to a
  kids' room (temporary-use by design, expected to become a home office
  once the child leaves) and to small utility functions (a home office/
  study nook that doesn't need its own room, grouped instead with a
  boiler room, bathroom, or even a corridor). **This independently
  corroborates, from a dedicated architect channel rather than a
  bedroom-combination-specific source, the same sofa/bed-transformer
  structural choice already recorded on
  [[04_Living_and_Dining_Room/analysis/Living_Bedroom_Zoning|Living-Bedroom Zoning]]**
  — a different source type (general design-philosophy list vs. a
  room-combination-focused designer) reaching the same conclusion.

## Design Concept — Explicitly Flagged as a Contested Opinion

- **⚠️ Principle 8 — "the time of pass-through kitchens and living rooms
  has come," stated by the source as a controversial position it
  expects pushback on**: in a compact floor plan (per Principle 2), the
  source argues it's acceptable — even inevitable — for a
  kitchen or living room to also function as a circulation path between
  other rooms, rather than insisting every public space be a dead-end
  ("тупиковое") room. Recommends offsetting this with built-in/
  transformable furniture. **Recorded as a single practitioner's
  explicitly self-flagged minority opinion, not settled guidance** —
  relevant to weigh against this household's own open-plan kitchen-
  living-dining circulation decisions, but not elevated to a rule.
