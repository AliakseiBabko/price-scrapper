## Durable Facts (Level 1, `single-account`/`unverified` throughout)

- **Ladders**: keep a minimum of two on site; buy 5-step (usable safely only up to the 3rd step) or 7-step (for work at step 5, so the knee braces against the top platform) depending on task height — never stand at the very top step of a short ladder. Buy wide, reinforced-step ladders only; cheap ladders both fail structurally and cause worker falls. Treated as a consumable: a ladder is considered fully worn out within ~6 months of active use and is bought fresh per project rather than carried between sites.
- **Temporary toilet + sink + shower pan/mixer for on-site workers are billable consumables the customer pays for, not a free perk** — without them, workers living/working on site will urinate into bottles (odor risk) or defecate into bags thrown in the trash or occasionally out windows. Moscow-area building management companies are stated to now inspect new-construction sites for a temporary toilet + sink before allowing a crew to begin work. Companies advertising these as "included free" are, per the speaker, marking up other materials roughly 2x to cover the cost — framed as a transparency argument for itemized billing over "free" bundling.
- **Protective floor/wall sheeting (hardboard/"оргалит") is mandatory along the material-transport path** (elevator to unit) — plastic wrap alone only protects against dust/dirt, not impacts from carts; an unprotected wall-cart collision is estimated to cost a minimum of ~10,000 RUB per damage incident, versus ~5,000 RUB total for hardboard sheeting to cover an entire ~6-month project.
- **Industrial dust extractor for wall-chasing (штробление) work** is called out as a distinct, easily-skipped expense (~120,000 RUB new, in the speaker's example, though the company's unit is shared across ~100 projects) — without one, chasing work fills hidden wall/floor cavities with dust that later causes long-term allergic/respiratory symptoms for occupants. Filter bags for it clog quickly (~7-8 bags needed over 6 months at ~400 RUB/bag, budget ~3,000 RUB total) — one bag cannot last a whole project.
- **Protective plastic sheeting thickness**: 100 micron recommended as the practical optimum (60 micron minimum, 200 micron rejected as too stiff/hard to tape); a mid-size project is estimated to use about one full roll.
- **Buckets — three distinct types needed, not one**: several 60 L (for large plaster/screed mixes — buy 3-5, since they crack under mixing stress), several 20 L (for putty/small mixes — buy multiple, several die per project), and one ~90 L basin/tub. The 90 L basin is recommended *over* a dedicated temporary shower pan for on-site washing, because a shower pan's drain trap clogs repeatedly with sand/plaster grit while a basin is simply dumped out and reused — and the basin doubles as a general-purpose mixing/rinse container.
- **Mixer paddle attachments**: buy heavy-duty/reinforced mixers rather than cheap ones — one heavy-duty unit does the work of roughly four cheap ones (which fail under load) for only about 2x the unit price; always check the shank/shaft type since most require a specific chuck adapter. Keep one cheap backup mixer on hand regardless, since even premium units occasionally fail mid-task.
- **Sabre-saw blade for aerated block**: one blade typically lasts an entire project if the installer avoids pressing hard while cutting (overheating from excess pressure, not blade dulling from cuts, is what kills these blades) — the speaker notes other crews commonly burn through 3-4 blades per project by pressing too hard.
- **Ceiling paint tinting**: always add a small amount of colorant to white ceiling paint (especially before electrical fixtures go in) — raw concrete slab ceilings are light-colored and show through thin uncolored white paint as patchy coverage; a slightly tinted (darker) white gives more even coverage and makes electrical fixtures read more distinctly against the ceiling.
- **Joint sealing rule**: gaps around window/balcony frames and similar structural joints should never be filled with plaster — always use professional pistol-grade (cartridge, not straw-tube) polyurethane foam; straw-tube foam has uncontrolled flow and poor cure at larger volumes, creating internal voids. Always keep foam-gun cleaner/solvent on site, or the gun's foam residue hardens and the gun stops working.
- **Fastener sequencing rule**: buy wood screws (coarse thread) in 4 sizes at the very first purchase — 25mm, 35mm, 45mm, 55mm — plus 71mm and roughly 90mm (ASR-garbled as "90,2 мм," `uncertain`) for joining framing lumber. Deliberately do NOT buy metal/drywall screws in the same first purchase — they're only needed once drywall installation starts, and having both types on site early causes installers to grab the wrong ones. Universal 6mm dowel anchors (35 or 45mm) and universal hanger/suspension brackets should also be on hand from day one.
- **Adjustable/crescent wrench should already be in a hired crew's own tool kit** — stated rule of thumb: "if a contractor asks the customer to buy them a wrench, they're taking advantage of the customer."
- **Temporary electrical is the very first purchase/task after ceiling priming**, before any other work, since no other task is realistically possible without site power/light — cable (1.5mm² and 2.5mm² gauges), temporary bulbs/sockets, extension cords/power strips. High-power work lamps for painting are deliberately *not* included in the very first purchase, since the speaker states they're always damaged/lost and rebought later anyway.

## Numeric Data

- Common wear-item purchase sizing: min. 100 trash bags at first purchase (cheap green bags for early debris; switch to pricier reinforced white bags once sharp drywall-profile offcuts start being discarded, since green bags tear on them).
- Rubber washers for flexible plumbing hose connections: buy 10-15 at once — they frequently fall out/get lost, and a missing one causes a leak.
- Cutting discs for angle grinder: buy 10-15 per major purchase so the site never runs out mid-task.
- Thin (~20mm) foam board recommended to shield the entrance door on both faces from movers/haulers for the whole renovation duration.
