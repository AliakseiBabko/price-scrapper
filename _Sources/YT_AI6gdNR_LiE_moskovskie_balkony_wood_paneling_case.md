## Balcony / Loggia — New Technique: Seasonal Timing for Insulation Work

3. **Practical heuristic, new to this page**: insulate/glaze a loggia in
   **winter, not summer** — a draft or gap is only detectable when it's
   actually cold outside; in summer heat, a poorly sealed installation
   won't reveal where it's drafting, and the installer only finds out
   (and has to redo work) once winter arrives. The company narrates this
   as their own standing advice to clients, illustrated by the current
   client's own experience (doing this job with doors open in winter and
   directly feeling where a draft was still coming from before final
   sealing).

## Balcony / Loggia — New Technique: Wood Paneling ("Вагонка") Installation

4. **Nail-gun/compressor installation, explicitly preferred over clip
   fasteners**: the crew uses a pneumatic nail gun (two nails driven into
   the tongue/groove of each board) rather than кляймеры (metal panel
   clips) — stated reason: clips are unreliable/prone to failure and a
   real injury risk to install by hand ("можно себе пальцы полностью
   отбить"). A genuinely new install-method data point for this page's
   wall-finish content, which so far only covers PVC panel and plaster
   technique.
5. **Board orientation and grading**: horizontal ("имитация бруса"-style)
   board orientation is stated as the current preferred method — vertical
   orientation is called "an outdated approach." Boards are hand-sorted by
   visible defect level before installation: defect-free boards go on the
   visible/exposed wall, boards with minor defects (small knots, minor
   surface irregularities) are routed to the wall section that will later
   be hidden behind a built-in cabinet — a practical technique not yet
   documented on this page's layout/materials content.
6. **Framing detail — reinforcement, not yet documented here**: the
   ceiling/wall wood-frame carcass is tied perpendicular into both the
   rear wall and the side wall, and also braces against the glazing frame
   itself — the glazing's own rigidity is reinforced by more than its
   mounting plates alone once the wood frame is built around it. Presented
   as a deliberate reinforcement step, not incidental.
7. **Deferred baseboard/trim decision, a workflow note**: internal corner
   trim pieces for the wood paneling were deliberately **not** installed
   at job completion because the client hadn't yet decided on baseboard/
   plinth height — since the corner pieces run floor-to-ceiling and get
   cut to match the final plinth line, installing them before that
   decision would mean redoing them. A minor but genuinely practical
   sequencing note (a case for postponing a trim step until a
   client decision is locked in, rather than a schedule-risk framing).
