## Materials (PVC Windows — Maintenance)

- **DIY sash adjustment, two specific screws, one tool (4mm hex key)**:
  (1) **Vertical position adjustment** — a screw located at the bottom
  hinge controls the sash's vertical position; turning it clockwise
  several times raises a sagging sash that's begun catching on the
  frame. (2) **Seal-pressure adjustment** — an eccentric bolt around the
  sash perimeter controls how firmly the sash presses against the
  weatherstripping; rotating the eccentric's thicker lobe toward the
  seal increases pressure. **Paper-test technique to check seal
  pressure**: close the sash on an ordinary sheet of paper at a test
  point, then pull the paper out — pressure is too weak if it slides out
  easily, correct/good if it comes out with resistance or tears; **repeat
  this check at multiple points around the sash perimeter**, not just
  one, since pressure can vary point to point.
- **Explicit scope limit stated by the source**: these two adjustments
  (vertical position, seal pressure) are what a homeowner should do
  themselves; **any other fitting/hardware adjustment should be left to
  a professional**, framed as a deliberate boundary rather than an
  exhaustive DIY guide.
- **Windowsill-drilled directional-vent retrofit for persistent window
  condensation** — corroborates and pre-dates (earliest known telling in
  this store) the existing `yt_U56YIEA3-U4` (2019) entry for the same
  technique: when a wide windowsill blocks radiator warm air from
  reaching the glass (severe enough in this source's stated worst case
  that a rag has to be laid along the frame to catch water), drill a
  hole through the sill directly opposite the center of each glass unit
  and insert a directional grille, one per glass pane — restores warm-
  air flow to the lower glass and stops the fogging. **Explicit caution
  from the source itself**: only drill the sill if condensation is
  actually a problem — don't drill preemptively/unnecessarily.
