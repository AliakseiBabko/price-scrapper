- **⚠️ Acrylic/plastic furniture critique, with named historical design references (flag per the cultural-reference/advertising filter)**: names the **Louis Ghost and La Marie chairs by Philippe Starck**, and the **Panton Chair by Verner Panton**, as culturally iconic examples of the "plastic revolution" that nonetheless behave poorly in real daily use — acrylic clouds/scratches/deforms from load or heat; plastic loses color, becomes brittle, "gives away its composition by touch." Neither ages "nobly" like wood/stone/metal. **Krasnov's stated studio policy: never use acrylic/plastic in active-use zones** — recommends properly-treated glass, solid wood or veneer, or powder-coated metal instead for a similar lightness/transparency effect.
- **⚠️ Ornate/baroque decorative furniture details critique (carved legs, ornate moulding, heavy cornices), named as "pseudo-luxury" especially in factory/mass-production execution — names Jean-Michel Frank and Émile-Jacques Ruhlmann as examples of ornament done well (Art Deco masters, flag per cultural-reference filter)**: ornament in their work is architecturally integrated into a piece's form rather than applied on top. Core stated principle: ornament should reinforce form, never substitute for it or fill empty space.
- **⚠️ Mirrored/glossy furniture-surface durability critique, with concrete timeframes, genuinely new and useful, worth its own entry**: mirrored countertops/cabinet fronts/consoles show first scratches/wear within a few MONTHS of active touch; within **2-3 YEARS** develop a network of micro-defects, cloudy corners, chips — reads as "neglected" rather than luxurious. Recommendation: reserve mirror finish for rare accent use only, never as the primary material for furniture/surfaces subject to regular contact.
- **⚠️ Trendy textured/patterned tile critique bundle, with specific comparative lifespan figures, genuinely valuable, record in full**: cement tile — porous, absorbs moisture/oil/wine even with sealant, needs re-sealing every **6-12 months** or it stains/darkens; glass mosaic — many grout joints, each collects grime, needs constant cleaning; Moroccan/zellige tile — irregular by design (celebrated as an aesthetic feature by top manufacturers themselves) but the irregular joint width traps dirt over time, reads as "dirty" rather than characterful specifically in a kitchen or shower context. **Comparative durability claim, worth quoting exactly**: high-grade porcelain tile/slab lasts **20-25 years** of active use without losing appearance, while a solid stone slab lasts as long as the house itself with minimal upkeep.
- **⚠️ Gold/brass overuse critique, plus a time-sensitive trend observation**: gold/brass on every surface (faucets, handles, furniture bodies, light fixtures) reads as "showy"/"like a Vegas casino" rather than luxurious. Gold works only as a rare accent (one large object — chandelier, art object, a piece of furniture — can carry shine); everything else should stay in calmer materials (matte nickel, black metal, patinated brass). **Explicitly flags brass itself as becoming overused/turning into a new cliché, and states the 2026 trend movement is toward more complex finishes/new materials, with brass receding** — tag as a time-sensitive trend observation, not a durable rule.
- **⚠️ Faux/imitation-texture furniture-finish critique, a strong, specific, comprehensive rule extending the existing imitation-material critiques (wallpaper-imitating-plaster/brick, Rounds 4/7) to FURNITURE FINISHES specifically, with a concrete substitution table worth recording as a set**: MDF painted to imitate concrete/plastic/stone, deliberately-added sparkle paint, faux rust, or deliberately-distressed "aged" furniture reads as cheap imitation regardless of intent (visible via repeating pattern, visible material seams, or a "plasticky" tactile/paint feel). Recommended REAL-material substitutes: instead of paint-imitating-concrete → real mineral plaster or microcement; instead of paint-imitating-metal → real brass or steel that develops genuine chemical-aged patina over time; instead of wood-grain film/vinyl → real veneer or solid wood with brushing/staining techniques; instead of stone-imitation panels → large-format porcelain slab or real stone slab with matched book-matched veining.
- **⚠️ Custom murals/fresco wall art — a nuanced, honestly-uncertain position, worth recording as a genuine open question rather than a flat rule**: acknowledges murals can genuinely elevate a space into a real art object, but explicitly states this "locks" the room into a specific stylistic moment that can read as dated/trend-fixed within a few years. His own honest uncertainty, quoted directly: **"пока не очевидно, как относиться к подобным решениям"** ("it's not obvious yet how to feel about this technique"). Contrasts this with neutral textures + movable/replaceable art objects, which can be swapped over time to refresh a room's feel without capital renovation.
