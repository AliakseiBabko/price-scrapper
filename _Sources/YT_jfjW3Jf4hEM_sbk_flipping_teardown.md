## Mistakes / Warnings — Flip-Renovation Defect Pattern, With Full Cost Breakdown

- **⚠️ Worked example: what a "successful flip" actually costs the eventual buyer**: apartment purchased for **13,000,000 RUB (≈$145,000)**, renovated for a stated **1,200,000–1,375,000 RUB (≈$13,300–$15,300)** (source treats the discrepancy as the flipper's own rounding, not extracted as two separate facts), then resold for **16,800,000 RUB (≈$187,000)** — meaning the buyer's purchase price effectively embeds **≈3,800,000 RUB (≈$42,200)** attributed to "renovation," while the flipper's own genuine cash spend on that renovation was roughly a third of that. **Named conclusion**: at the buyer's actual effective per-m² renovation spend (≈85,000 RUB/m², ≈$950/m²) they are receiving construction quality worth roughly 25,000 RUB/m² (≈$280/m²) — well below this speaker's own stated "genuine quality" floor of 35,000 RUB/m² (≈$390/m²) for the same city/segment.
- **⚠️ No-conduit exposed cable on the ceiling, named as a 100% economy-class tell**: cable run directly on/behind the ceiling without corrugated conduit (гофра) is technically permitted but not done in business/premium-segment work; if done, the cable itself should at minimum be rated for that installation method — its presence in a "finished" flip is read as a direct quality signal, independent of how the ceiling looks once covered.
- **⚠️ Electrical installed before plastering (order-of-operations defect), same mechanism named in this round's video 3's own rough-in-sequencing content and Round 6's `JeRGrHsv07U`**: chasing/wiring finished immediately after demolition, before plaster-to-guide-rail (маяки) plastering — the source infers the crew likely intends to skip guide-rail plastering and just skim-coat over the existing uneven wall instead, meaning the walls will not end up flat despite fresh electrical and finish work.
- **⚠️ Old developer riser/stack pipes left unreplaced, named as a named real risk in an older building**: plumbing labor+materials on this flip totaled only ≈30,000 RUB (≈$330), which the source reads as proof the risers (стояки) were not replaced — a real flood risk to the unit and neighbors over time, and one that becomes far more expensive to fix later once the bathroom is tiled/finished (would require breaking already-finished surfaces to regain riser access).
- **⚠️ Missing leak-protection system and missing manifold (коллектор), both stated as real safety/comfort gaps** for a ≈30,000 RUB (≈$330) plumbing budget: no automatic shutoff system means an undetected leak floods the unit and neighbors until a plumber manually closes the risers; no manifold means a tee-branch (тройниковая) supply layout, so one occupant using a kitchen tap can cause a scalding pressure/temperature spike for someone showering elsewhere in the same unit — contrasted with the source's own standard practice of installing manifold-based plumbing specifically to prevent this.
- **⚠️ No inline/mainline water filters on a ≈30,000 RUB plumbing budget**: named consequence is that any incoming water-quality problem (e.g. rust/discoloration) passes straight through to fixtures and appliances (washing machine, etc.), accelerating their wear and requiring more frequent repair — an ongoing cost not visible at renovation handover.
- **⚠️ Thick foam underlayment under laminate as a floor-flatness workaround, not a soundproofing choice**: floors were never leveled (screed/self-leveling skipped), so a thick (5-7mm) inexpensive foam underlay is used to visually compensate for humps/dips under laminate — looks fine initially, but the source states this predictably fails within 1-2 years (creaking, seams separating) because the underlying unevenness was never actually corrected. **Named implication for material choice**: quartz-vinyl is deliberately avoided in this kind of flip specifically because it has a much stricter subfloor-flatness requirement that a flip's un-leveled floor could never pass — the presence of laminate-on-thick-underlay instead of quartz-vinyl is itself a diagnostic signal of a skipped leveling step.
- **⚠️ Painted-over walls (no wallpaper) framed as a likely future cracking risk, not a stylistic upgrade**: predicted directly by the source before the outcome was shown in the reaction footage, and the source's prediction was confirmed within the same source video (cracks visibly appeared on camera) — attributed to skipping proper crack-isolation/reinforcement prep before painting.
- **⚠️ Undersized electrical panel with no circuit separation, same underlying principle as this round's video 3's master-switch content**: a small panel with all lighting on one breaker and no dedicated circuits (no separate breaker for outlets by room, none for the dishwasher) means a single appliance fault (e.g. a failed dishwasher) can trip a breaker that also serves unrelated outlets across a section of the apartment, cutting power to those until the appliance itself is fixed or unplugged — described as a real day-to-day comfort/reliability cost, not just an inconvenience at install time.
- **⚠️ Standard breaker installed in place of an RCD (УЗО/residual-current device)**: named as a real shock-hazard difference — with an RCD, a fault current to a metal appliance casing trips power before a person completes the circuit through their body; a standard breaker does not provide this protection, and the source states this is a lethal risk specifically for a small child touching an energized appliance casing.
- **Old developer-installed inlet cable (likely aluminum) left in place, not replaced despite the rest of the electrical being redone**: the source reasons the meter/panel's unusually high mounting position is itself evidence the original inlet run was never touched — cable insulation degrades with age (30-50 years cited as the developer-cable age range in older buildings), so leaving it in service keeps a genuinely aged, weaker insulation layer in a "renovated" apartment; stated replacement cost is only "a couple thousand roubles," framed as an inexpensive fix skipped anyway.
- **Cheap furniture failure modes, named mechanisms rather than a vague "gets worse" claim**: a sofa with a gas-lift mechanism installed on only one side pulls unevenly and won't lie flat because the matching lift on the other side was omitted to save cost (cited: a second lift costs only ≈50 RUB extra); a lift-storage bed's stated retail difference between a cheap and adequately-built mechanism is used as an example of durability being traded for a small unit-cost saving; thin frame slats (ламели) and cheap hinges are named as the parts that fail first (sagging, drooping, cabinet doors coming off) under normal use.
