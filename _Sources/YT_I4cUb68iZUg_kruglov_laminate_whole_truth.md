## Genuinely New Content

1. **⚠️ Named regulatory code citation for subfloor flatness before laminate, first for this store**: СП 71.13330 ("Изоляционные и отделочные покрытия," п.8.7) sets a legal maximum subfloor deviation of **2mm per 2 running meters**. Laminate manufacturers' own recommendation goes further: prepare the subfloor to **1mm per 2m** — legally compliant is not the same as manufacturer-recommended.
2. **Self-leveling-compound mechanism, distinct from this page's existing height-matching rationale**: beyond flattening and matching a tile-zone's finish height to a laminate zone's, a thin gypsum-based self-leveling layer also physically "locks down" the screed's own coarse sand-concrete aggregate — laminate is described as a "living floor" that moves slightly during use, and that movement can gradually abrade/damage the laminate's underlayment against exposed coarse screed aggregate if no leveling layer is poured over it first.
3. **Four-layer laminate composition, more granular than this page's existing composition entries**: (1) top wear layer — acrylic or melamine resin, protects against abrasion/scratches/moisture/UV, the primary durability driver; (2) decorative/print layer — pattern only, no structural role; (3) HDF (high-density fiberboard) core — determines lock durability and overall board strength; (4) bottom stabilizing layer — paraffin-impregnated cardboard, plastic, or melamine film, protects against moisture/fungus/mold from below.
4. **⚠️ HDF core density range and a genuine tradeoff, first numeric figure for this store**: HDF density ranges 700-1,200 kg/m³ across the market; the most common quality range sold in Russia is **850-950 kg/m³**. Denser HDF = stronger lock joints, better moisture resistance, and less susceptibility to sagging under furniture weight — but also a more "resonant"/hollow-sounding ("звонкий") floor underfoot. Higher density is not an unconditional win; the source frames it as a real comfort-vs-durability balance to weigh, not "denser is always better."
5. **⚠️ Formaldehyde-class label-reliability caveat, distinct from Kodolov's measured emission comparison already on this page**: E1/E0 are the safe classes, E2/E3 unsuitable for residential use — but reliable E-class labeling is really only consistent from **European manufacturers**; cheaper Chinese, Turkish, or unbranded product may not disclose an emission class at all, making it impossible to verify even if the box claims E1/E0. Stated concern: a producer paying ~200 RUB/m² to make a floor sold at 1,000 RUB/m² (a 5x markup) has an incentive to cut corners a label alone can't catch.
6. **⚠️ Water-damage mechanism, more specific than this page's existing laminate water-intolerance content**: a single laminate board is not itself harmed by isolated water contact — 3-5 drops of water left indefinitely on one board causes no damage, since the top wear layer is fully water-resistant. **The real failure point is the board-to-board lock joint, not the board surface itself**: water pooled across an installed floor for an extended period (worked example: a week-long vacation with a spill) has, per the source, roughly a 99.9% chance of swelling/destroying the whole floor once it reaches the joints.
7. **Water-resistant laminate redefined by joint performance, not the box label**: "влагостойкий" (water-resistant) laminate is not defined by what the packaging claims, but by whether the lock mechanism itself keeps water from passing through at the board joints — the same underlying mechanism as fact 6, framed as a buying-vocabulary correction (don't trust the label alone; the joint quality is what actually matters).
8. **⚠️ Embossing ("тиснение") benefits, not previously on this page**: a textured, wood-grain-matching surface finish (vs. a completely smooth surface where only the printed pattern suggests wood) — three cited benefits: (a) reads as more expensive/premium, close enough to real parquet/engineered board that a non-professional reportedly can't tell the difference by sight; (b) meaningfully less slippery underfoot than a smooth laminate surface, a genuine safety factor; (c) hides surface scratches better than a smooth finish, which shows scratches more visibly especially under raking daylight. The source states a personal preference for embossed laminate specifically because of these three factors, not just aesthetics.
9. **⚠️ Wear-class 20-series-vs-30-series taxonomy, and a marketing-myth debunk, extends this page's existing class-33 content**: the 20-series (class 21-23) is the technically correct residential-use tier by design intent, but the Russian market's own consumer preference has made only the 30-series (commercial-rated) the practical standard even in homes. Within 30-series: **class 32** = low-traffic commercial (offices), **class 33** = high-traffic commercial — both are more than sufficient for an apartment housing 2-4 people, per the source. **⚠️ "Class 34" is stated to not exist as a real technical standard at all — described as a pure marketing invention by Chinese and other budget manufacturers**, not a genuine higher wear tier.
10. **⚠️ Four-type lock-mechanism taxonomy, genuinely new buying vocabulary for this store**: (1) **Click** — the oldest/simplest mechanism (board angled at 45° and snapped down); most reliable, but installation risk is real: boards must first be joined end-to-end into a long connected "snake" across the room's width before the whole assembly is angled and clicked onto the long edge, and this snake can snap/break during that maneuver, especially on large floor areas. (2) **Lock/tongue-groove ("шип-паз")** — either hammered together or simply overlaid; some laminates combine Click on the long edge with Lock on the short edge, letting installers lay one board at a time regardless of room length — the source states he personally wouldn't use a pure Lock-type mechanism. (3) **5G** — an enhanced/upgraded Click, easier and faster to install than classic Click, but **extremely dust/debris-intolerant**: a single trapped speck of dust or debris in a 5G joint can start the floor squeaking almost immediately after installation, with no fix short of pulling up and redoing the affected section. (4) **UniClick/UN Click** — can be either hammered or clicked like a standard Click joint, described as the most balanced/versatile option for both reliability and installation difficulty. **Source's own ranking**: Click = most reliable and simplest overall; 5G = reliable but installation-risk-heavy; UniClick = good balance; plain Lock = avoid.
11. **⚠️ Thickness range, lock-strength independence, and a specific tile-transition height-match, extends this page's existing thickness content**: practical range 8/10/12/14mm (6mm categorically excluded — "only if you want to harm yourself"). **Thickness has zero effect on lock/joint durability** — that's governed by HDF quality (fact 3-4 above), not board thickness; thickness instead affects only soundproofing and thermal performance (thicker = quieter but also slower to warm through). **14mm is called excessive specifically under a heated floor** — a 14mm laminate board would either never fully warm through or take an impractically long time. **⚠️ A specific 12mm transition-height fact, new mechanism for this store**: 12mm laminate plus its underlayment lands at roughly the same combined height as tile plus tile adhesive — making a 12mm laminate-to-tile transition line up almost 1:1 without needing a compensating self-leveling pour, if the buyer hasn't already committed to a self-leveling floor.
12. **⚠️ Named brand tier ladder for laminate specifically, first laminate-brand taxonomy for this store (2025)**: **Economy** — Eger, AGT. **Optimal** — Alpine Floor, Classen, Kronospol ("кроно пол"). **Premium** (five brands, no top-3 cut given) — MyStep, MyFloor, Nofloor/"нотекс" (`ASR-uncertain` on exact brand name), Ro, and Berry Alloc ("Berry АК", `ASR-uncertain` on exact rendering). No prices attached to any tier — the source states laminate price is a function of the buyer's own combined criteria selections (thickness, chamfer, embossing, HDF density, brand), not a fixed per-tier number.
