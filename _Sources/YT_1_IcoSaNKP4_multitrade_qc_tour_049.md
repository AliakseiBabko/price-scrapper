## Plumbing — Pipe Material and Fitting Technique

- **Never use polypropylene pipe for rough plumbing — explicitly called obsolete technology, not a viable modern option.** Explained mechanism: each weld/fusion joint reduces internal pipe diameter (visible when a real polypropylene joint is cut open) — a real narrowing defect, not a marketing myth, contrary to some YouTube demonstrations that only show cutting *along* the pipe (never at a joint) to avoid revealing this. **Always use plastic (PEX/push-fit) pipe with push-fit fittings instead**, laid as continuous single lengths without unnecessary intermediate joints — visible on this site as a direct replacement of a neighbor's inferior original plastic pipework.
- **Push-fit fitting installation, mini-masterclass**: expand the pipe end with the tool twice (once to a standard 90° circular expansion, to avoid immediately reinserting the tool the wrong way and causing later leaks), then insert the fitting immediately (letting the expanded end start contracting before inserting risks a leak) — the source frames this system as removing installer skill/human-error risk almost entirely ("even a monkey could manage it" after one demo), a deliberate contrast with the polypropylene-welding skill dependency above.
- **Tool-rental economics for DIY plastic-pipe installation**: rent the pipe-expansion tool from wherever pipe/fittings are purchased — **~500-1000 RUB/day**, more than sufficient for a full-apartment rough-plumbing job even allowing 2-3 days for a first-timer (**~1500-3000 RUB total rental cost**, `unverified`, 2019 pricing). Framed as trivially cheap insurance against a flood versus hiring a "tourist plumber."

## Electrical — Panel Assembly Quality

- **Factory-assembled electrical panel vs. site-assembled, the real distinguishing factor stated explicitly**: not the quality of individual components inside the panel (a breaker is a breaker regardless of who wires it) — the real difference is **quality of the finished assembly as a whole**: internal wiring/routing discipline, and — critically — **warranty coverage on the assembled product itself**, not just its individual parts. A factory-built panel ships from a specialized manufacturer with its own product passport/certificate, which a site-assembled panel cannot replicate regardless of the individual components used. **Rule: never accept a site-assembled electrical panel — insist on a factory-assembled unit.**

## Plumbing / Materials — Rough-Stage Underfloor Component Replacement

- **Never keep a developer-installed standard water-supply manifold/collector (коллектор)** — explicit mechanism/motive framing: a developer's own incentive is minimum cost, not longevity ("no developer cares if you live 50 years without problems — they care about profit margin"), so the stock manifold is reliably the cheapest available unit and typically fails within roughly 6 months of occupancy. **Always replace the developer-stock manifold during rough works.** Matches this store's existing developer-standard-fixtures-are-cost-minimized framing (applied elsewhere to windows) — extends it explicitly to plumbing manifolds.
- **Manifold-cabinet relocation for accessibility, a specific worked technique**: when relocating a manifold cabinet into a technical/utility zone, it's acceptable to cut into (open up) a shared building duct/riser cavity ("подъездная шахта," a common building service cavity, not a load-bearing structural element) to gain the needed depth — described as safe to open and reclose repeatedly (легко снимается / легко ставится) without a neighbor ever noticing, since it's not load-bearing and carries no risk if handled competently. Framed as a routine, low-risk technique, not a structural violation.

## Windows — Installation Foam-Joint Width Standard, With Mechanism

- **Correct installation foam joint width: 20-50mm, with 25mm cited as the practical optimum** — explicit mechanism for why *not* minimizing this joint even where wind-load isn't a structural concern: the shorter the foam joint, the shorter the frame's own fixing screws/anchors have to be, and shorter fixing elements flex/deform more under load over time than longer ones. A wider joint (within the 20-50mm range) both uses longer, more rigid fixings and leaves more room for insulation, giving better thermal performance too. **Do not minimize the foam joint purely to save foam material or "clean up" the reveal** unless a real structural/wind-load reason specifically requires it.

## Heating — Radiator Wall-Embedding Decision Rule

- **Whether to fully embed a radiator into the wall (vs. a lighter partial recess) depends on two conditions, both must hold to skip full embedding**: (1) the exterior wall must NOT be built from a lightweight/aerated concrete type requiring extra insulation, and (2) there must be no old air-heating-system void/cavity behind the wall to conceal. If neither condition applies, don't bother fully embedding the radiator — a shallow recess (~11-13mm, per the source's own worked figure) is sufficient, since the remaining protrusion becomes visually negligible once plastered.
