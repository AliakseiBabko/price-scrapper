- **A false/furring wall built to match a load-bearing wall segment's thickness should only extend as far as needed to hide the specific service element it's concealing** — worked counter-example: a client extended a false wall across an entire wall segment (matching a load-bearing section's thickness) when only a small strip actually needed concealment, sacrificing space that could otherwise have held a corner utility cabinet (vacuum/buckets/cleaning supplies storage). General "don't over-extend a furring wall past what it needs to hide" caution, distinct from (but related to) this store's existing false-wall/utility-concealment content.
- **A load-bearing exterior/corner condition producing an awkward external wall corner should usually be resolved by extending an adjoining room's wall to absorb it, rather than working around the corner in place** — worked example: a client failed to resolve a stray exterior corner and instead built an undersized corner closet (only 43cm nominal depth, ~35-37cm clear after door/shutter installation — too shallow even for a large vacuum cleaner) plus two additional awkward external corners in the process; the speaker's alternative extends a neighboring room's wall to the full width of the two adjoining load-bearing segments, eliminating all three problem corners at once and enlarging the room in the process.
- **A central corridor/hallway serving multiple rooms is often reclaimable space, not a structural necessity** — worked before/after case: a client's layout retained a central corridor connecting rooms; the speaker's re-plan eliminates it entirely by reshaping adjoining rooms' walls, reclaiming the corridor's full footprint as usable room area (contributes to this video's headline "10.3 m² recovered" figure) and giving one bedroom materially more clearance around the bed/wardrobe than the client's version had.
- **Utility/storage cabinet depth should match its contents** (worked example, extends this store's existing depth-by-purpose framework): a 600mm-deep cabinet for hand tools/small implements is distinguished from a 700mm-deep cabinet needed for a full-size vacuum, buckets, and mops — the 700mm cabinet specifically requires floor space a narrow leftover corridor cannot supply, similar in substance to the same distinction made in this batch's `yt_WVfgE0lHgVc`, suggesting this may be a standard company talking point rather than a one-off observation.
- **Bathtub placement among multiple candidate bathrooms should be chosen for minimal (but sufficient) end clearance plus shortest plumbing run to fixtures** — worked example: a 1.70m tub rejected from two candidate slots (leaving 30cm and 20cm end gaps respectively) in favor of a third slot leaving only 12cm, explicitly reasoned by shortest utility run to the fixtures it serves — same evaluation logic as `yt_WVfgE0lHgVc`'s tub-placement example, reinforcing this as a standard company decision heuristic rather than incidental to one video.
- **Concealed-cistern installation frame requires dedicated wall-cavity space planned into the layout up front, not added after fixtures are otherwise positioned** — worked example: a client's layout positioned toilets correctly relative to stack risers but omitted the wall box needed for the concealed cistern/frame, meaning walls would still need reworking to accommodate it even if nothing else in the layout changed.
- **A sink whose plan-view footprint conflicts with a door-swing/passage opening can sometimes be resolved by shifting the opening itself rather than the fixture** — worked example: shifting a door opening slightly let an 80cm sink cabinet clear the passage entirely; the tradeoff was that a different, narrower fixture ended up protruding slightly into its own (unchanged) opening instead — illustrates that resolving one clearance conflict in a fixed footprint can simply relocate rather than eliminate the underlying space shortfall.
- **Dishwasher should be positioned to the left of the sink for right-handed workflow ergonomics** — stated reasoning: left hand holds the plate, right hand scrapes food waste into the sink/bin, then the plate goes directly into the dishwasher; positioning the dishwasher elsewhere (worked counter-example: to the right of the sink, forced by a cabinet-door conflict) breaks this motion and is described as measurably less convenient, plus costs the kitchen one full cabinet module of storage in the process.
- **TV/bed orientation should be mirrored where needed so the TV ends up directly facing the bed**, not merely nearby — a general placement/orientation rule illustrated across two bedroom worked examples in this apartment.
