- **Door casing/trim should never be cut down to fit a too-narrow entry zone** — the correct sequencing is to size the entry zone to leave real wall reveal visible on both sides of the door casing, not shrink the casing itself to fit an undersized space.

## Mistakes / Warnings — Bathrooms/WC (multiple corroborating instances)

- **⚠️ A tile layout in a small wet room should be centered from the room's own centerline, not begun arbitrarily from one wall — a genuinely useful, specific tiling-planning rule not previously recorded this precisely in this store.** The defect found produced narrow scrap-width cut pieces at both edges of the room because the layout wasn't centered; centering the layout first would have distributed any necessary cut pieces more evenly (or eliminated the narrow scraps entirely).
- **A floor drain (trap) in a shower area should be positioned to one side, not directly underfoot where the user stands while showering** — a real, checkable placement defect, distinct from drain-slope/functionality concerns already covered elsewhere in this vault.

- **A glass shower "screen" that doesn't actually block splash water in normal use provides no real function beyond appearance** — a defect found had a stationary glass pane that failed to contain water during normal showering, unlike a properly sized/positioned splash guard.
- **⚠️ A door stopper/limiter's mounting distance from the wall must be checked against the room's actual passage width — a genuine safety mechanism, third instance of a related access-panel/clearance-direction defect in this chunk.** The defect found had a rubber door stopper positioned far enough from the wall that the door would rebound off it directly into anyone entering the cramped WC — described as capable of striking a small child near eye level.
- **A guest WC/wash-basin needs a minimum ~80cm of width to be genuinely usable, corroborating and slightly extending this vault's existing sink-width findings from other sources in this chunk** — the defect found (~60cm) combined with sharp adjacent hook hardware made the space effectively unused by the household (confirmed by visibly dried-out rubber gaskets from lack of water use).
- **A shower stall needs roughly 15cm more width than a bare-minimum 65cm to avoid the user's shoulders nearly touching both walls** — a specific numeric data point extending this vault's shower-clearance guidance from `nNrZzbTpRi8` (same chunk).
- **⚠️ Access/revision panels opening in an inconvenient or seemingly-arbitrary direction is now a repeated finding across this chunk** (also seen in `78FeaeMZHOI`, same chunk, and `o7lGqaUuGm0` from the earlier trial) — verify a service-access panel's hinge/opening direction against the actual space available to operate it, not just its presence.
- **A WC compartment needs at least ~80cm of width for a person to sit comfortably without knees striking the door/wall** — reiterates this vault's existing WC-clearance convention with a real defect example (~50cm found).
- **A ventilation duct should never be routed visibly through a closet or other finished storage space** — a basic rough-in-planning check, avoid exposing service runs through finished storage.
