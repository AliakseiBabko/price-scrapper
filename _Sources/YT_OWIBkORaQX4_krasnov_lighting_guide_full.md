## Lighting, Electrical, Design Concept

- **⚠️ Explicit critique of a single central ceiling fixture as the sole light source**: "Слава Богу мы избавились от одного-единственного светильника который светит на нас из центра" — restates/reinforces this vault's existing content but as an explicit stated principle worth its own citation.
- **⚠️ Three-tier lighting taxonomy, stated explicitly**: general/ambient (fills the whole room), task (illuminates a specific work zone — counter, desk, dining table), accent (highlights specific objects — art, plants, shelving). States task and accent lighting are rarely done wrong because their purpose is self-evident, but GENERAL lighting is where most people fail. Recommends spot/track lighting distributed across the ceiling instead of a single central fixture, calibrated not to create harsh glare.
- **⚠️ Applied case, real project, real dimension**: a project called "Дом на Львовской" ("House on Lvovskaya") with a real **2.75m** ceiling height — too low for suspended fixtures/dropped ceilings for spot lighting, so the studio used **surface-mounted track lighting** ("накладные трековые светильники") instead, preserving the sense of spaciousness without lowering the ceiling. Same project: multiple lighting SCENES for different zones in an open kitchen-living layout (kitchen task light, accent light over a bar-counter/table).
- **⚠️ Color temperature guidance, a specific concrete range**: prefers ~3000K for residential; below 2000K reads "too orange," reserve for special occasions only; above 4000K reads "too cold/uncomfortable" for residential use, appropriate only for industrial/warehouse spaces simulating daylight. Explicit warning against colored lightbulbs (green/blue-tinted vintage-style bulbs found at flea markets) — recommends avoiding entirely; red/orange-tinted bulbs "might work" but anything cooler is a bad idea.
- **⚠️ Decorative-lamp critique, with a memorable named/described example**: critiques lamps chosen as sculptural/decorative objects first and light sources second. Specifically calls out dome/bowl-shaped translucent glass pendant fixtures (described distinctively in the transcript — "лампы с формой напоминающей грудь," a deliberately blunt visual comparison, preserved verbatim rather than sanitized) as dated AND functionally bad because insects/dust visibly collect inside the translucent glass shade, requiring constant cleaning. Recommends modern equivalent-output alternatives instead.
- **Critique of exposed-bulb fixtures**: a fixture that merely frames a visible bulb (e.g., a pendant over a dining table that's just a decorative cage around an exposed bulb) wastes the opportunity to actually shape/direct the light — calls this "a strange choice."
- **Flame-shaped/candle-shaped bulbs critiqued as tacky unless in a genuine vintage fixture.**
- **Fake/artificial candles critiqued as unconvincing** — recommends real candles for genuine warm ambient glow (with obvious safety caveats), and salt lamps as a good decorative warm-light alternative to fake candles.
- **⚠️ Exposed LED strips — a specific, concrete "hide the strip" rule**: LED strips are designed to be CONCEALED (in a recessed channel/cove) to produce even diffused glow — individual diodes are visible and look cheap when the strip is mounted exposed/visible, as commonly seen in TikTok/Instagram room-decor videos. Also explicitly avoid colored LED modes, use plain white/warm light only.
- **⚠️ Named external reference, flag per the cultural-reference filter**: cites **Rey Sivan** (an *Architectural Digest* home-tour designer, of "FX Studio") using the phrase "suр из света" ("soup of light," "суп из света") to describe deliberately layered, richly-detailed lighting design — attribute to Sivan explicitly, not to Krasnov.
