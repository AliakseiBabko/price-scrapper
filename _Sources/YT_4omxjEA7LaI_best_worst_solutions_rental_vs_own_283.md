## Rules / Heuristics — Genuinely New Content

- **Studio-splitting rental technique** (rental-specific, not applicable to this project's own single-family use): converting a 1-bedroom into two independent studios sharing one entry hall but each with its own locked door, letting the unit be rented to two separate short-stay parties simultaneously (or as two connected studios to one larger group) — roughly doubles achievable rental income when the layout supports it.
- **⚠️ Porcelain-tile-to-laminate transition durability mismatch**: extending porcelain tile from a wet/high-traffic entry zone into an adjoining laminate-inlay pattern creates a real weak point — the laminate swells with tracked-in water and scratches faster, becoming the first thing to fail and forcing a full floor redo sooner than if the whole high-wear zone had stayed in the more durable material. **General rule: keep an entire high-wear/wet-adjacent zone in one consistently durable material rather than mixing in a less durable material at the transition.**
- **⚠️ Paint-color reversibility, directional risk explicitly framed**: going from a lighter wall color to a darker one later is cheap and easy; going from darker to lighter requires many coats of primer/paint and costs significantly more. **General guidance: when unsure about a wall color's darkness for a personal-use renovation, default to lighter** — it's the cheaper direction to correct if wrong.
- **⚠️ Windowsill-material segment-tier guidance**: for personal use, a plain **white** windowsill (with white reveals) is the future-proof default specifically because it's paired with paintable wallpaper walls that may change color later — a laminated wood/stone-effect windowsill can't be easily recolored to match a future wall change. For a rental, a wood-effect windowsill paired with a complementary wall color is a legitimate higher-appeal choice instead, since the wall color there is treated as fixed/non-changing.
- **⚠️ Radiator-grille mounting-mechanism caution**: a specific grille-cover design using tab-and-slot dowel pins is described as nearly impossible to seat correctly — any warp or humidity-driven dimensional change causes the pins to miss their holes. **Prefer a two-component magnetic-mount grille cover instead**, which doesn't have this alignment-sensitivity problem.
- **⚠️ AC "sitting under" clarifying distinction — resolves a genuine common confusion**: "sitting under the AC" in the bad sense means sitting directly in the discharge airflow path (a guaranteed cold draft); "sitting under the AC" in the good sense means being physically located beneath where the unit is mounted but *outside* its direct airflow path, where the air has already mixed to a comfortable temperature. **If a work desk or bed happens to sit in the direct airflow path, that specific position — not necessarily the AC's own mounting location — is the thing to fix.**
- **⚠️ Flooring bevel/chamfer (фаска) recommendation is material-dependent, not a blanket rule**: always choose beveled-edge laminate (hides natural seam gapping as boards shift over time), but do **not** default to beveled quartz-vinyl/LVT — glued LVT rarely gaps at seams, and a beveled edge on some LVT textures can look artificial/cheap. Choose the specific LVT texture based on how it looks matched to furniture/doors first, and only then check whether a bevel helps or hurts that specific texture's appearance.
- **⚠️ "Functional decor" — a named, defined design concept**: an item that is simultaneously genuinely functional *and* a decorative accent (worked example: a quick-access coat-display piece that also reads as an accent element) — praised as an efficient design pattern, but explicitly noted to risk feeling visually tiring in long-term daily personal use even though it photographs and markets well for a short-term rental listing. A useful named concept for evaluating dual-purpose furniture/fixture choices.
- **Compact sink design pattern, a positive concrete example**: a small (~60cm) basin with narrow raised edges and a recessed/sunken counter section immediately around it effectively contains splashing water despite the basin's small size — demonstrates that basin *geometry* (raised edge + recessed surround), not basin size alone, is what determines splash containment. Complements this store's existing narrow-sink-splashes-everywhere negative example with a positive counterexample showing the same small footprint done well.
- **⚠️ Painted-wall-plus-tile combination in a wet room, extended with a specific geometric incompatibility**: beyond this store's existing cracking/maintenance cautions, a painted-wall section recessed ~1cm relative to an adjoining tiled wall plane produces mismatched door-opening reveal widths on either side of the same opening (uneven, not symmetric) — and separately, a painted wall section is specifically incompatible with keeping a revision/access hatch cover looking clean over time (paint texture doesn't hold up against a hatch's repeated opening/handling the way tile does).
- **Dining-zone light + dedicated local switch co-location — flagged as a rare universal (non-segment-dependent) rule in an otherwise segment-tier-heavy video**: a dedicated pendant light over a dining table with its own dedicated switch mounted right there (not lumped into a distant master switch bank) is praised as objectively good regardless of personal-use or rental context — most of this video's other findings are explicitly segment-conditional, making this one of the few flagged as unconditionally good.
- **Closet depth, cleanly tiered**: 60cm minimum for a basic closet; 70cm if the client has a lot of bulky/heavy clothing; 80cm for a sliding-door (купе) wardrobe (10cm consumed by the door-track system); 85cm+ is flagged as wasted floor area beyond what any storage use actually needs.
