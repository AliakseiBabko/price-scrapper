5. **Egger's two structural advantages over other economy-tier competitors**: the largest lamination color palette in the category, and — a real distinguishing feature — lamination applied to **both faces equally**, whereas some competing economy producers only laminate one face to the same standard.
6. **⚠️ LDSP facade limitations, first structured downside list for this material at this store**: edge banding is always visible (1-2mm) and its bond quality determines how quickly the facade fails if poorly applied — though the source notes even budget/garage-level production can now bond banding adequately; a **PUR-glue-bonded edge is described as functionally permanent** but comes at a higher production cost. **A genuinely new, non-obvious limitation**: LDSP cannot be milled/routed into any relief or radiused/curved profile — it stays perfectly flat, with wood-grain texture as the only surface variation available.
7. **⚠️ Mid-tier facade group 1 — plastic-laminate-on-MDF, two named leading manufacturers with a quantified price gap**: **Mateelux** (premium end of the mid-tier) and **AGT** (a comparable-quality alternative, roughly **30% cheaper**) — the source's own framing: "if the name has 'lux' in it, it can't be a cheap producer." MDF substrate (denser/more stable than LDSP particleboard) can be milled/routed, unlike LDSP.
8. **⚠️ Plastic-facade properties, first structured comparison for this store**: laminated on both faces (like LDSP) before being cut to size and edge-banded (same edge-banding-visibility caveat as LDSP applies); available finishes include gloss, matte, super-matte, and soft-touch; decorative-layer thickness is substantially greater than LDSP's lamination, making it noticeably harder to scratch or damage. **Fingerprint-visibility finding**: any dark facade shows fingerprints regardless of material, but plastic specifically (especially light-colored, matte, or soft-touch plastic) shows fingerprints far less than other facade materials at comparable darkness.
9. **⚠️ Mid-tier facade group 2 — film (плёнка) on MDF, two named supplier brands used by the source's own manufacturing partners**: **Adilet** and **Greenwood**, both described as reliable, easy-to-apply film stock. **Total facade thickness: 18-25mm**, varying with whether the facade has a plain flat profile or an additional perimeter frame/rail.
10. **⚠️ Film-vs-plastic-vs-LDSP delamination-risk comparison, a real durability distinction**: LDSP and plastic facades have their finish layer bonded to the board at the factory under high heat (effectively monolithic); film facades are applied separately at the kitchen-company's own local production, carrying a genuinely higher long-term delamination risk than either LDSP or plastic — risk concentrated specifically at high-steam-exposure zones (above a cooktop, near a kettle's steam plume). Risk varies considerably by which film brand and which kitchen-company applied it, so brand/installer reliability matters more for film than for the other two materials.
11. **Film-facade milling/edge-wrap advantage over plastic**: unlike plastic (which stays flat only), film can be applied over a milled/routed MDF profile and wraps fully around every contour including the edges/returns — so a filmed facade needs **no separate edge banding at all**, since the film itself covers the torso/returns. Reverse (rear) side of a film facade is white by default, though some manufacturers can apply film to the rear as well on request.
12. **⚠️ Premium tier 1 — enamel (эмаль) on MDF, first detailed construction/durability content for this material at this store**: MDF is used in ~99% of enamel facades because its density holds enamel with minimal-to-no "orange peel" (шагрень) texture — LDSP-based enamel is explicitly called visibly worse (heavy orange-peel) and not a true premium result. Enamel facades are painted entirely at the *kitchen company's own local production* (unlike LDSP/plastic, factory-applied) — meaning finish quality depends heavily on the specific production shop's spray equipment and paint-booth professionalism, so two "enamel" facades from different shops using the same paint can be dramatically different in quality. **Construction layering, first documented for this store**: primer → base coat → color coat → sealing/clear coat (explicitly analogized to a wall's paint-prep layering).
13. **⚠️ Enamel facade cons, quantified**: scratches extremely easily — the source demonstrates a visible scratch from a bare fingernail-less finger swipe; some enamel formulations fade/discolor under prolonged direct sunlight, a real concern for a sun-facing kitchen.
14. **⚠️ Enamel facade pros justifying its premium-tier position**: color-palette depth of **2,000+ shades** (RAL or NCS systems), far exceeding LDSP/plastic/film's available palette; no visible edge banding at all (painted on both faces as one continuous surface, or one face left unpainted on request); a repair kit (small matching-color paint pot) is available from the manufacturer for touch-up of chips — a real repair option but the touch-up remains visible on close inspection, unlike a full facade replacement.
15. **⚠️ Premium tier 2 — natural/artificial wood veneer on MDF, named leader manufacturer Woodstock**: called the absolute veneer-market leader, with 1,000+ available wood species/texture/color variants. **Natural vs. artificial veneer distinction**: natural veneer is a thin slice of a specific real wood species; artificial veneer starts from a fast-growing, cheaper wood species (poplar, alder named) reconstructed/finished to visually replicate a more expensive species' texture and color — genuinely still a real-wood veneer product, just from a substituted base species.
16. **⚠️ Veneer facade properties and a cost-saving reverse-side technique**: no other material replicates veneer's tactile feel; every facade shows genuinely unique grain (no two trees match exactly) — framed as a feature, not a flaw. Edge banding is done in the same veneer material for a near-invisible seam. **Cost-saving technique**: order the visible (front) face in an expensive wood species' veneer, and the rear (hidden) face in a cheaper species' veneer of matching color — visually indistinguishable to anyone viewing the finished kitchen, while the price difference between the two species can be 3-10x.
17. **Veneer's living-material behavior, framed by the source as a benefit, not a defect**: genuine wood veneer continues to change color/tone over time (unevenly across facades) since it's a living, "breathing" material — a real characteristic to set expectations on before ordering, not itself a flaw.
18. **⚠️ Veneer's one clear con: scratches very easily** — applies to veneer flooring as well as facades, per the source's own cross-reference to this channel's separate kitchen-flooring video.
