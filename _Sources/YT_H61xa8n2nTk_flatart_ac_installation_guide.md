## Durable, Largely Date-Independent Technical Facts (Level 1)

- **Three indoor-unit placement rules** stated directly by the host: (1) don't blow cold air directly on occupants — cool the room evenly instead; (2) leave clear space above and below the indoor unit so it can draw in warm air and release cooled air freely; (3) position the unit so it's visually unobtrusive and doesn't disrupt the room's design. `confirmed`.
- **Condensate drainage must not go straight into a sewer stack**, and a simple water (P-)trap is also inadequate, because the trap's water eventually evaporates during idle periods and loses its seal — both approaches risk sewer odor/bacteria backflow into the room. `confirmed`.
- **Correct condensate drainage mechanism: a "dry trap" (сухой клапан) valve** — condensate enters a small reservoir; rising water lifts a floating ball; once the water rises high enough it overflows through a top port into the sewer connection (a standard ~32 mm pipe cited); when the AC is unused and the water evaporates, the floating ball settles and blocks the opening, preventing sewer gas/bacteria from migrating back up the drain line. `confirmed`, and this is a genuinely new, non-obvious technical mechanism not covered by any prior source in this store.
- **Condensate drain lines run inside walls at a ~2° slope** toward the sewer stack; the host recommends photographing the routing during installation, specifically to reduce the risk of later wall-drilling damaging a hidden line. `confirmed`.
- **Inverter vs. non-inverter (on/off cycling) AC**: non-inverter units cycle fully on and off to hold temperature; inverter units smoothly modulate compressor power instead, avoiding the on/off cycling. `confirmed`, general technical explanation.
- **Rough sizing rule of thumb** (explicitly caveated by the interviewee as an *averaged* estimate, not accounting for extra heat sources): roughly **1 kW of cooling capacity per 10 m²** of room area, with the smallest commonly available unit size being 2 kW, and a roughly 20% capacity buffer added as standard practice. Orientation (sun exposure) and whether a door stays open to a shared space both push actual required capacity up. `confirmed` as the interviewee's stated rule of thumb; explicitly flagged by the interviewee himself as approximate.
- **Warranty structure described**: a bundled 5-year warranty covering both equipment and installation is offered when equipment is purchased and installed through the same company, for most brand tiers (Mitsubishi Electric, Daikin, Electrolux) — except the cheapest/no-name budget tier, which the interviewee says carries only a 1-year *equipment* warranty (installation warranty remains 5 years regardless). Rated minimum lifespan for quality brands cited as 10 years, with breakdowns described as "extremely rare" for the named premium/mid brands. `single-account`, retailer's own claim about his own stock/warranty terms — not independently verified.
- **Filtration — an honestly hedged claim, worth preserving as-is**: basic units include a washable coarse mesh filter; some models add antibacterial/ionic (silver-ion, catechin) filter inserts. The interviewee explicitly says a user will **not** consciously notice any difference from these added filters ("вы однозначно не почувствуете") — any benefit is framed as subtle/subjective at best, not a marketed selling point oversold by the source itself. `single-account`, but notably self-skeptical rather than promotional.
- **Top 3 buyer/installer mistakes**, per the interviewee: (1) undersizing capacity to save money, causing the unit to run constantly at maximum and fail early; (2) poor indoor-unit placement, blowing directly on occupants; (3) **buying equipment from one vendor and hiring a separate, unrelated installer** — makes warranty/fault attribution difficult when something goes wrong. Recommendation: buy and install through the same company for a single bundled warranty. `single-account`, retailer's own stated experience, but a plausible and specific claim (not vague).
- **DIY caution**: the interviewee states AC performance/reliability is roughly 80% dependent on installation quality, not equipment quality, and that installation requires specialized tools (vacuum pumps, flaring tools, manometers/gauges) and skill — explicitly discourages DIY installation based on watching online tutorials alone. `single-account`, professional's self-interested claim (he sells installation services), noted as a potential bias.
- **Seasonal pricing tip**: AC units are reported cheapest to buy in winter (off-season promotions). `single-account`.
- **Real installation example**: for a building where facade-mounted outdoor AC units were prohibited for aesthetic reasons (courtyard-facing units only, to preserve a boulevard/water-view facade), refrigerant line runs became much longer than typical — requiring AC models explicitly rated for the longer run length, to avoid compressor overheating/premature failure from an undersized-for-the-run-length unit. `confirmed`, from a specific project the host describes.
