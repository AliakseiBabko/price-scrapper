## Design Concept / Family Requirements — New Facts

- **Five named root causes of household disorder**: insufficient time
  for cleaning, lack of organization/storage systems, excess possessions,
  no habit of returning items to their designated place, and emotional
  factors (stress/depression/fatigue/mental clutter). Framed as a
  diagnostic starting point before applying any specific storage fix.
- **⚠️ Categorization-first principle, the throughline for the rest of
  this video**: sort everything into explicit categories (books,
  documents, jewelry, etc.) and give each category one consistent home,
  rather than solving clutter room by room without first defining
  categories.
- **Entryway "drop zone" needs a designated place, not a bare surface**:
  the first horizontal surface encountered on returning home
  accumulates a loose pile of daily-carry items by default; deliberately
  designating a proper landing spot (tray, bowl, drawer) for these
  specific frequently-handled items prevents the "growing pile" effect
  rather than fighting it after the fact.
- **⚠️ Practical open-surface declutter method, a repeatable exercise**:
  empty everything from the target surface/shelf into a bin; sort into
  "actually used" vs. "rarely used" (rarely-used items go into a closed
  cabinet); group the remaining "actually used" items by category into
  zones (e.g. cosmetics bottles together, a small decor cluster
  together); physically test-arrange rather than assuming a layout works
  on the first try. Small everyday decor trays/wooden trays help turn
  a naturally cluttered zone into an intentional-looking one.
- **Visually loud product packaging (cleaning products, personal-care
  bottles) can be disguised by decanting into plain black bottles/jars
  labeled "soap," "gel," etc.** — explicit acknowledgment that fully
  decanting everything isn't realistic ("we live in the real world"),
  so treat this as a partial mitigation, not a requirement.
- **⚠️ Skepticism toward trend-driven storage gadgets**: many organizer
  products popularized by social media/marketplace browsing turn out
  impractical, non-durable (not plastic-durable), or simply unattractive
  once actually bought — a caution against over-buying storage
  accessories on trend momentum alone.
- **⚠️ Pre-design-project decluttering exercise, with a real
  measurement implication**: before ordering a design project/
  renovation, physically empty all existing closets/storage and sort
  out genuinely unused items (old, ill-fitting, or unworn-for-years
  items) — real practitioner finding: **once genuinely unused items are
  removed, most clients discover their existing storage volume was
  already sufficient**, despite initially requesting more storage
  capacity in the new design. A real sequencing implication: measure
  actual post-decluttering storage need before specifying new storage
  capacity, not before.
- **Velvet-covered ("flocked") hangers prevent clothing from sliding off**
  compared to standard hangers — a specific, checkable material
  property, not just a nicer look.
- **Deep drawer/storage cells should be pull-out (drawer-style), not
  fixed shelves** — corroborates this vault's existing wardrobe-shelf-
  depth retrieval-convenience rule (see `14_Furniture/analysis/Wardrobe_Vocabulary_and_Sizing.md`)
  from a general-storage angle rather than a wardrobe-specific one.
- **Shoe storage options for a large shoe collection**: angled/slanted
  shoe racks, or individual clear/opaque plastic storage boxes per pair
  (marketplace-sourced) for maximum per-item organization.
- **⚠️ Colored, perforated (ventilated) storage boxes are explicitly
  preferred over clear plastic boxes for stationery/miscellaneous small
  items** — framed as a near-universal "must-have" for keeping loose
  items off open shelves/desks; a woven/wicker basket variant can double
  as visually acceptable open-shelf decor rather than needing to be
  hidden.
- **⚠️ Kid-height toy-box placement, with a real client case**: toy
  storage boxes should sit low enough for a child to reach, retrieve,
  and return items independently — a real observed case where a
  client's children fully self-managed their own toy boxes (get toys
  out, play, put them back, push the box back in) once storage was
  placed at child height, vs. shelving a level a child can't reach
  unassisted.
- **Hollow ottoman/pouf/bench with a lift-top lid** as dual-purpose
  seating + storage — flagged as suitable even for a small apartment,
  not just a spacious one.
- **⚠️ "Chair pile" (clothes draped on a chair) alternative fixtures,
  practitioner's own admitted habit and fix**: an open-frame floor
  valet/clothes stand ("вешалка камердинер") or — the source's own
  stated preference — a decorative ladder leaned against the wall near
  the bed, both let worn-but-not-dirty clothing be set down without
  opening a closet, functioning as a legitimate designed alternative to
  an informal chair pile rather than a moral failing to fix through
  willpower alone.
- **Windowsill decluttering is largely unsolvable through storage
  design** — a real, honest admission that there's little real fix
  beyond accepting plants/decor on a windowsill; **one indirect
  mitigation given**: a natural-stone windowsill visibly shows stains,
  which self-motivates keeping it clearer than a wipeable synthetic
  sill would.
- **⚠️ Balcony storage organization, a specific multi-step method**: (1)
  full inventory/purge pass, (2) use vertical space with floor-to-
  ceiling shelving/racking, (3) buy matching decorative boxes and sort
  contents by category into them, (4) add hanging storage systems
  (hooks, dedicated bike/ski wall mounts), (5) use ceiling storage
  (overhead shelving/cabinets) and storage-integrated furniture (a
  folding table with drawers, a storage ottoman), (6) prefer sliding
  doors/folding fixtures over swing doors to save floor clearance when
  opening storage, (7) add real lighting so stored items are actually
  visible/findable, not just present.
- **Open bookshelf styling notes**: grouping books by color reduces the
  visually "busy"/mismatched look of a typical open bookshelf; leave
  visible gaps/breathing room with small decor objects (a vase, empty
  space) between book groups rather than packing shelves edge-to-edge.
- **Towel storage: hang on rails, don't stack** — stacked towels don't
  fully dry between uses; matching color/design across all towels
  (planned during the original renovation's furnishing pass) lets any
  future replacement towel be bought to match rather than introducing a
  mismatched color.
- **⚠️ Cable/extension-cord clutter prevention is a rough-electrical
  planning decision, not a storage-accessory fix**: specify extra
  outlets with real margin at rough-in stage specifically to avoid
  needing an extension cord later for an unanticipated future device;
  where this wasn't planned for, a small dedicated charging
  cabinet/cubby keeps cables/chargers contained and out of visible
  circulation space instead of scattered.
- **Pet feeding-station integration**: built-in pet food/water bowls
  can be integrated directly into kitchen cabinetry (including a
  pull-out faucet positioned to fill the bowl in place) rather than
  left as a freestanding floor bowl in a room corner.
- **General storage-placement priority principle, applies across every
  zone above**: place rarely/never-used items on the hardest-to-reach
  storage; place frequently-used items closer/more accessible; place
  daily-essential items always within immediate reach, while still
  keeping them visually unobtrusive.
