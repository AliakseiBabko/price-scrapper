## Switches / Sockets — why electrical accessories deserve a deliberate choice at all

Her framing, and the reason the colour block below matters:

- **⚠️ A common mistake among people managing their own design/renovation: not thinking about electrical accessories at all.** When the time comes to buy sockets and switches — **she means the visible outer parts specifically (клавиши, накладки), not the mechanisms** — people buy the cheapest or the most ordinary thing available, and end up with a decent interior and characterless electrical accessories.
- **Her argument for why it shows**: there are always many of them, in every corner and beside every door; **and they are the part of the interior you physically touch every day**, many times a day. So they have to be both aesthetic and functional.

## Switches / Sockets — choosing plate colour

The extractable core of the video, and it both corroborates and sharpens existing vault content:

- **⚠️ Her counter-intuitive headline: the worst colour for electrical accessories is white.** Plain white reads cheap and reads plain "вне зависимости от дизайна."
- **⚠️ The one case where white is right: a snow-white interior.** Her example is a white modern-classic scheme, where white plates on white walls sit well precisely because it is white-on-white.
- **⚠️ The failure case, stated with a tolerance that is tighter than expected: if the walls already have any tint at all** — even slightly milky or slightly greyish, let alone a real colour — **white accessories look "колхозно."**
- **Black is a good option in any interior containing any shade of black** — she points at the studio she is filming in as an example, where black elements already exist. It gives graphic accents, and suits minimalism, all contemporary styles, loft and Scandinavian.
- **⚠️ Greys, and this is the most directly actionable rule: from light grey through to dark saturated graphite, chosen either to match the wall colour or one-to-two shades darker than the wall.**
- **⚠️ Form follows style, and she splits it cleanly**: **rounded-corner** series sit better in styles with any classical inflection and in eclectic styles — Japandi named specifically. **Sharp, pronouncedly rectangular** series suit **only minimalism and ultra-laconic interiors** — not modern classic, not retro, where they would look wrong.
- **⚠️ Different rooms may use different colours, and she names this as another frequent self-managed-design error — the assumption that they must all match.** "Никто не заставляет вас делать одинаковые розетки по всей квартире." Her worked example: something lighter or grey in a children's room, black in the entrance zone, white in a white bedroom.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Relation to existing vault content — this sharpens rather than duplicates.** `12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Switches_and_Controls.md` already holds Игорь Краснов's rule that plate colour should match the wall by default, with contrast permitted as a deliberate accent only when the tone is exact. **Common ground: white-by-default against a tinted wall is a visible error, and matching the wall is the safe rule.** **What she adds**: the explicit *one-to-two-shades-darker-than-the-wall* latitude, the style-to-plate-geometry mapping (rounded vs square-edged), the narrow carve-out where white is actually correct, and permission to vary colour room-by-room — which also nuances Pavel Sidorik's frame-swap lifehack already on that page, since varying frames per room is exactly what that technique makes cheap.

## Switches / Sockets — three brand-independent technical facts

- **⚠️ Back boxes differ by country.** "Подрозетники во всех странах тоже разные, как и сами розетки" — a range made for the Russian market is built around the **standard round back box** used there. Relevant to this project because it means imported accessories are not automatically compatible with installed back boxes.
- **⚠️ Frames are sold by post count and this affects what you must buy**: single-post frames are typically included with the device, so it is ready to install — **but multi-gang blocks (several sockets or switches in one group) require separately purchased two-, three- or four-post frames.** Recorded as a buying-list item: a socket count is not a frame count.
- **Pass-through / two-way switches (проходные выключатели)** — control one fitting or group from several places: light on at the start of a corridor and off at the end, or on at a bedroom entrance and off at either side of the bed. **She recommends them in every video** ("в каждом ролике советую вам обязательно применять").

> **The pass-through recommendation is a third corroborating account and does not change the existing position.** That page already carries both a cost justification (≈2,000–4,000 RUB extra per location, Kruglov/Ontario) and a cognitive-load caution against saturating an apartment with them.
