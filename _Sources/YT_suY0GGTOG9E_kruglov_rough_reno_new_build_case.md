## Detailed Case Study — Real New-Build Rough-Stage Cost, 59 m², Free-Plan

A real, itemized, single-object case: **59 m² free-plan new-build
apartment, zero pre-existing partitions**. Per-stage costs as invoiced
to this client:

- **Partitions**: 77 linear m² of partition wall (with opening
  reinforcement included) — **92,000 RUB (≈$1,000)**.
- **Plastering**: 207 m² of wall area, including priming — **204,000
  RUB (≈$2,300)**.
- **Floor treatment**: 59 m² of floor soundproofing + ceramzite backfill
  + screed — **83,000 RUB (≈$920)**.
- **Plumbing rough-in**: 3 radiators, 18 water points, 75 m of heating
  pipe — **101,000 RUB (≈$1,100)**.
- **Electrical rough-in**: 73 electrical points, a 36-module panel —
  **183,000 RUB (≈$2,000)**.

## Planning Rules / QC — New Acceptance-Checklist Detail

- **Partition acceptance**: check tilt/plumb with a laser level, confirm
  opening heights against the apartment's own reference "zero" mark, and
  confirm reinforcement mesh is present where required.
- **Plastering acceptance ("helicopter pass")**: run a straightedge
  diagonally across every plastered surface, specifically re-checking
  near baseboards, ceiling lines, and openings — **and separately
  re-verify flatness with a laser even where the underlying partition
  was already confirmed straight**, since plastering crews set their own
  leveling beacons and can introduce a new tilt independent of the
  partition itself. Follow with a square-check at every corner meant to
  be 90°, and a laser-tape parallelism check on walls meant to stay
  parallel.
- **⚠️ Named humidity/mold-prevention step after plastering, not
  previously documented in this store's sequencing content**: crack
  windows for ventilation while plastering dries, and actively manage
  indoor humidity according to season/building — in winter specifically,
  this company uses dedicated dehumidifier machines to pull moisture out
  of the apartment, since natural apartment exhaust fans alone can't
  keep up and unmanaged humidity risks mold on walls/floors.
- **⚠️ Floor-soundproofing-membrane protection technique during
  plumbing/electrical rough-in**: pipes and floor-routed electrical
  cable are secured to a net (сетка) laid over the soundproofing
  membrane using cable ties, rather than being screwed or fastened
  directly through the membrane — avoids puncturing its
  waterproofing/soundproofing function.
- **⚠️ A quantified plumbing pressure-test protocol, not previously
  recorded at this level of specificity in this store**: pressurize the
  manifold node to **8 atmospheres** and hold for **30 minutes**,
  confirming no pressure drop, before considering rough plumbing
  accepted. Sewage slope is separately verified with a laser level, and
  the source notes **45° fittings are used throughout instead of 90°
  bends** to reduce flow resistance/clog risk. Check valves are
  confirmed present on the hygienic-shower line and on the heated-
  towel-warmer branch (plus a water-hammer arrestor/compensator on the
  manifold node); a fine-particle filter (cartridge or whole-house
  mainline type) is described as now near-universal in every
  renovation, rarely omitted.
- **⚠️ A named mixed wired/wireless leak-sensor cost-optimization
  strategy, using the "Нептун" system as the worked example**: wired
  sensors are used at each individual water-point risk location (direct,
  cheap per-sensor cost since cabling is short and convenient there),
  while a WiFi-based wireless sensor block is used specifically for the
  radiator/heating-manifold zone (avoids running sensor cable to a more
  awkward location) — an explicit budget-driven mixed-technology choice,
  not simply "wireless is better."
- **Electrical rough-in conduit (гофра) usage rule, previously
  unrecorded at this level of nuance**: for ceiling-routed cable, this
  company uses WGNGLS-type cable inside conduit purely for **mounting
  convenience** (not a code requirement) — but once the cable drops into
  a wall chase, the conduit is removed, since adding conduit to an
  already-cut chase groove would require cutting an even wider channel,
  which panel-building walls (rebar-constrained) won't tolerate.
  Conduit is, however, specifically recommended for low-voltage/Ethernet
  cable, routed in its own separate chase, to avoid electromagnetic
  interference from power lines.
- **Electrical panel/point handover documentation rule**: every
  rough-wiring drop must be labeled/signed by the electrician at
  handover, and the panel itself must be clearly labeled matching each
  breaker to its corresponding outlet/circuit — "no such thing as the
  electrician assembling the panel and leaving unlabeled."
- **⚠️ A named "vacation mode" wiring trick, not previously documented
  in this store**: alongside the main incoming breaker, two circuits are
  wired to bypass it entirely (leak-protection system, WiFi router, and
  refrigerator) — functioning like a secondary master switch specific to
  vacation use: flipping off the main breaker before leaving still keeps
  these critical safety/convenience circuits powered.
- **Screed acceptance checklist**: laser-level check for consistent
  height everywhere, straightedge flatness check, verification that
  expansion/control joints are cut at every external corner and opening
  (crack-prevention), and confirmation of a perimeter damper strip
  (here, built from the soundproofing membrane's own 0.5cm upturn plus a
  separate 0.5cm Energoflex strip, ≈1cm combined).
- **A practical contractor-vetting question, distinct from this vault's
  existing vetting checklists**: ask whether a contractor maintains
  documented technical process cards ("техкарты") for its work, and
  request to see them — offered as a genuine signal of whether a
  contractor's quality process is systematized versus ad hoc.
