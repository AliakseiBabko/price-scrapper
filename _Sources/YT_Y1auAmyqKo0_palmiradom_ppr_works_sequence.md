## ⚠️⚠️ 1. WHAT IT IS, AND HOW IT IS MEANT TO BE USED

**ППР = план производства работ. He says every one of their technical projects contains one, added to the project AS ITS CONTENTS PAGE (содержание).** *«Это основа любого ремонта, и начинать отделку надо именно с него.»*

**⚠️⚠️ AND THE USE INSTRUCTION IS THE TRANSFERABLE PART — it is a dependency lookup, not a reading order:**

> **To do a ceiling plasterboard box: find the related stage (plasterboard work), then look at every step ABOVE it in the contents. If those are complete, start. If not, do them first — themselves in the correct order.**

**⚠️ Your project may not contain every stage, but whatever is in the brief must follow the plan's order.**

### ⚠️ The three criteria he built it on, which explain every decision below

1. **Сохранность выполненного этапа** — protecting work already done.
2. **Соблюдение технологии** — respecting the technology (cure and dry times).
3. **Оптимизация времени выхода мастеров** — minimising how often a trade must return.

**⚠️ And a stated assumption: the plan is written for SPECIALIST trades, each doing only its own work** — the electrician only electrics, the plumber only plumbing.

### ⚠️ The failures it exists to prevent, named up front

- Tiling the bathroom, then painting or wiring elsewhere → **grout joints packed with dust, tile damaged, and the trades using that bathroom.**
- **The floor LEVEL at the bathroom threshold changes once laminate or parquet goes down.**
- **⚠️⚠️ Building a plasterboard box round the room BEFORE levelling walls — the box width changes once plaster adds its layer. So set-out must come off FINISHED walls.** **→ Same finding as [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Small_Bathroom_Constraints|Огурцов's "model to finished faces"]], arriving from the site rather than the model.**
- Renovating room by room «под ключ поочерёдно» → dirt in clean rooms, electrical problems, mismatched floor levels.

| Step | And why it sits there |

| **1. Replace the ENTRANCE DOOR first** | Leaving expensive tools behind a developer's door is risky. **⚠️ If the existing door is sound, you may instead swap it after plastering — that protects belongings AND the new door.** **Wrap it in film or cardboard after fitting.** |
| **2. Preparatory works** | Protect what will REMAIN: entrance door, windows, radiators, panel. Provide light, power and water — bulbs, sockets, **a temporary WC.** **⚠️ Remove any furniture or the trades cannot work efficiently.** |
| **3. Demolition** | Partial (typical new build) or capital (old stock, back to brick and floor base). |
| **4. ⚠️⚠️ Balcony façade glazing / insulation — EARLY, if renovating in cold weather** | **Because the wet trades that follow need temperature: plastering and screed require +5 °C and above.** At minimum a double-chamber unit or a second contour. |
| **5. New block partitions** (пазогребень / aerated concrete) | Before plastering, since they need fibreglass mesh and a plaster coat against cracking. |
| **6. Door openings prepared** | Before plastering, so the mesh laps onto the new made-up jambs. |
| **7. Radiators removed and wrapped** | |
| **8. Plastering** | |
| **9. Screed** | |
| **10. ⚠️⚠️ Flat WINDOWS replaced — AFTER plastering** | **So the window can be set parallel to a levelled wall, giving reveals of even width, and so new windows are not damaged during plastering.** |

**⚠️⚠️ THE CLEANEST PIECE OF REASONING IN THE SOURCE is why steps 4 and 10 are SEPARATED:**

> **Fit the flat's windows early (at façade-insulation time) and the reveals come out uneven after wall levelling. Insulate the façade late (at window-replacement time) and the plaster/screed cycle is broken by low temperature on the balcony, so trades must return.** **In warm weather both can be done together — but if a second contour is planned on the balcony, fit it before the screed that will abut it.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 4. Plastering — three findings, one of them a coordination failure

- **⚠️ Not every room needs true 90° corners** — it costs material and drying time. **But wet rooms and the kitchen corner require it absolutely.** **⚠️ And watch corridor corners so the final tile or laminate cuts do not come out wedge-shaped and conspicuous.** **→ Ties directly to [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Tile_Selection_and_Layout|the tile-cut findings]]: squareness is a tile-layout input.**
- **Plaster drying ≈ 7 days.** Afterwards dry the walls against mould, especially in cold weather — **so refit the radiators.** In poor ventilation or cold weather they run a **dehumidifier.**
- **⚠️⚠️ AND THE COORDINATION FAILURE: plastering CHANGES the wall-to-radiator gap, which must be 3–5 cm for proper warm-air circulation.** *«Часто мы переделываем выводы под радиатор с учётом необходимых требований.»* **→ Plaster thickness eats the radiator gap and the pipe stub-outs then need moving. New to this vault, and it is a rework nobody budgets for.**

⚠️⚠️ **ONE PASSAGE IS UNREADABLE AND IS NOT ADOPTED.** The captions garble the clause stating how plastering relates to electrics and plumbing («штукатурка должна быть сделана при директоре кай и сантехникой»). **His stated REASON is legible — *«для корректного углубления подрозетников и меньшего штробления основных стен»*, i.e. the plaster layer supplies depth so less is chased into structure — and that reasoning implies plaster BEFORE electrics, which is what §5 describes explicitly. ## ⚠️⚠️ 5. Electrics — he argues AGAINST the common order, and the vault already agrees

> **The common version: lay cable → plaster → fit back-boxes. His practice: PLASTER FIRST, then electrics.** **Reasons: wires obstruct the plasterers; electricians otherwise have to return after levelling to set boxes; and *«электрику проще штробить мягкую штукатурку и сразу ставить подрозетники»*.**

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ SECOND INDEPENDENT ARRIVAL. [[11_Budget_and_Planning/Renovation_Sequence|Renovation Sequence]] already carries a practitioner sequence running plaster → waterproofing → UFH → re-verify geometry → screed → *only then* rough electrical, argued from geometry and the zero mark.** **Different reason, same order — and this one adds the labour argument.**

- **⚠️ Electricians attend TWICE: cable and back-boxes now, sockets/switches/luminaires at finish.**
- **⚠️ Cable in the FLOOR is laid BEFORE the screed.**
- **⚠️ For points in plasterboard walls, the plasterboard frame is built FIRST** so the electrician knows where to run cable and the conduit does not obstruct fixing the track.
- **⚠️⚠️ «Правило хорошего тона»: each trade makes good its own chases** — the more so when different crews are involved, and they must be dry before the tiler arrives.
- **⚠️ Agree any METER replacement before electrical work starts; it takes time.**
- **Their spec:** ВВГнг in conduit; **3×2.5 sockets, 3×1.5 lighting, 3×4 or 3×6 for hob and water heater**; **separate breakers for hob, oven, instantaneous water heater, washing machine, dishwasher, fridge — and Wi-Fi.**
- **⚠️ At handover of the stage: confirm temporary sockets exist and cover the panel against dust.** **→ Corroborates [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Rough_Electrical_Sequencing|REMCRAFT's]] temporary-socket practice from a second source.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 6. Plumbing — and a third arrival at "the tile grid governs the services"

- **⚠️⚠️ The boxing FRAME and the ACCESS HATCH are fitted at this stage, so technical supervision can verify convenient access to taps, manifolds, filter, meter and water heater. The tiler only closes the frame once the hatch is confirmed not to need correction.**
- **⚠️⚠️ THE HATCH MUST OPEN ALONG THE TILE JOINTS without harming the layout — so the plumber must allow for the future thickness of finishing leveller and tile when setting outlet heights.**

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ THIRD INDEPENDENT SOURCE for services being set by the tile grid, after [[07_Bathroom/analysis/Tile_Selection_and_Layout|Огурцов]] (mixers and sockets centred on tile or joint) and [[_Sources/YT_0c-QhBDQMWE_shemchuk_technical_design_album_series|Шемчук]] (extract fan centred on a joint).** **This one adds a NEW requirement: the access hatch has to open on joints, which constrains both the hatch position and the tile setting-out.**

- **⚠️ Use a RIGID profile for the boxing frame so the hatch does not warp over time.**

## ⚠️ 7. Ventilation, insulation, and a complete balcony build-up order

**Ventilation** is usually just ducting to the future kitchen hood plus fans in bath and WC. **⚠️ A dedicated supply unit he considers worthwhile from ~80–100 m², or for allergy sufferers.** **⚠️ Common arrangement: a fan in each of bath and WC, switched separately, the switches in the corridor in one frame with those rooms' lights — and then the airflows MUST be separated with non-return valves.** **⚠️ The duct can be sound-insulated. Where poor ventilation causes bathroom mould — common in old stock — a fan paired with a towel rail or underfloor heating adds draught to natural convection.**

**⚠️⚠️ THE BALCONY BUILD-UP, in order** — this vault had the materials but not the sequence:

1. Seal gaps with пеноплекс and foam
2. **Insulate the CEILING** (can add foil-faced изолон)
3. Then the walls
4. Then the floor — **all corner joints hermetically sealed; pre-level the base with finishing leveller so пеноплекс beds tight**
5. Foil-faced изолон on the floor to reflect heat
6. Screed, UFH cord, self-levelling pour
7. Sheath walls in plasterboard or aqua panel, framed or frameless
8. Finish: stretch ceiling, painted walls

**⚠️ Floor build-up depends on the covering: with screed if tile (raises the floor 10–12 cm from base; needs a strong mix, ЦПС 300 or cast concrete); on 50 mm joists with пеноплекс between and plywood over if laminate (60–70 mm total). Can be warmed with infrared film.** **⚠️ And direct sunlight fades materials on a balcony — choose accordingly.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 8. Reveals — a strong recommendation with a mechanism

> **He recommends SANDWICH PANELS over plasterboard for window reveals, because a plasterboard reveal's joint with the frame CRACKS over time. Sandwich panels avoid it through thermal-insulation continuity and material homogeneity with the frame.**

- **⚠️ Fit them at this stage — after plastering, when walls are level — so reveals and the windowsill come out even in width.**
- **⚠️ Order where a balcony step will be tiled: the STEP first (so no joint shows from above), then the top reveal and windowsill, then the sides.**
- **⚠️ Joints formed with LIQUID PLASTIC (e.g. Cosmofen), which unlike silicone actually welds the surfaces — applied in small portions as it sets fast.**
- **⚠️ Cover windowsills with cut plasterboard offcuts: trades will put tools and screws on them.** **⚠️ Corner beads on reveals go on AFTER wallpapering.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 9. The self-levelling pour — before filling, and the mechanism why

> **Pour the finishing leveller BEFORE skim-coating.** Not a disaster reversed, but there is less dust now than after sanding — **and if you skim first, mortar that splashes onto finished walls has to be sanded off, which is awkward because gypsum filler is SOFTER than the cement that landed on it, so you sand pits and grooves into the wall.**

- **⚠️ The leveller does not only level the screed — it COMPENSATES the differing thickness of future coverings so the floor finishes at one level.**
- **⚠️ Prime with diluted primer, better twice; pour after full drying, when a film has formed that stops the mix losing water and so lets it flow evenly.** **⚠️ On a floating screed always fit damper tape against cracking.** **⚠️ Water proportion strongly affects the leveller's properties.**
- **⚠️⚠️ A concrete planning detail: in cold weather radiators come off and go back TWICE — before plastering, and before filling and wallpapering. In warm weather, once.**

**Screed itself**: **⚠️ minimum 4 cm; walkable at 12 h, better 24; full drying ≈ 27–28 days; dry slowly, never forced.** **⚠️⚠️ The flat's floor level should match the COMMON CORRIDOR's level, allowing for finishes.** Compensation joints between rooms.

**Filling**: **⚠️ if the plastering was good, ONE coat suffices. With thin wallpaper, leave no sanded-through scuffs to the plaster — they show through. No filler where tile will go.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 10. The finish order, and the reason it is not the obvious one

> **Wallpaper → stretch ceilings → laminate.** Some lay laminate first, **but then a stepladder or a dropped tool during ceiling tensioning can damage it.** **⚠️ Exception: a covering needing paint or lacquer (solid board, cork) goes first, is covered with hardboard, then wallpaper, then ceilings.**

- **⚠️ Stretch ceilings drop ~40–50 mm from the lowest point the bead meets.**
- **⚠️⚠️ TILE IS FIRST AMONG FINISHES, because it is still dusty** (cutting, mixing).
- **⚠️⚠️ When laying floor tile, check that tile + adhesive matches the abutting covering's thickness (e.g. laminate + underlay). The main compensation happened at the levelling pour, but adhesive can shift it materially.** **→ FOURTH arrival in this vault at the floor-transition level problem.**
- **⚠️⚠️ T-THRESHOLD BASES ARE GLUED DURING TILING, positioned from the CENTRE OF THE DOOR LEAF — for standard doors, 20 mm from the wall plane toward the side the door opens.** **→ Decide during tiling or lose the option.**
- **Painting and wallpapering are one stage because one crew does both — and always PAINT FIRST**, or you cannot avoid splashing finished paper. **⚠️ Paintable wallpaper: paint ceilings and boxes, hang, then paint the paper. The paper-to-ceiling junction can be finished with silicone, which also reinforces the edge.**
- **⚠️ Hang the radiators once wallpaper is up — finally. Then stretch ceilings, so the bead presses the paper and makes an even junction.**
- **⚠️⚠️ SANITARYWARE BEFORE INTERIOR DOORS** — the extra width without doors lets something bulky into the bathroom without risking new doors.
- **⚠️ Splashback after kitchen assembly; cut where dust will not reach clean surfaces; finish visible tile edges with an END PROFILE matched to tile thickness.**
- **⚠️ Skirting last, after doors with architraves and the kitchen, since it abuts them — with the second half of the T-threshold.**
