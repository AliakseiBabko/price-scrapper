## Numeric Data — 2024 Rough-Work and Comfort-Tier Pricing (Russia/Moscow market)

1. **Rough-work-only floor, 2024**: genuine rough-plus-basic-finish labor
   (wall-building, plastering-or-not, wallpaper, stretch ceilings)
   cannot realistically go below **~20,000 RUB/m²** labor, per the
   source's own stated floor.
2. **Designer's own rough-work-cost gradation, partially ASR-uncertain**:
   an investment/"flip" White-Box-scope rough labor figure was stated
   but the digit string is ASR-garbled (`unverified`, not extracted as
   an exact number); a "super-economy" band of roughly 50,000-70,000
   RUB/m², a "mid" band of 70,000-120,000 RUB/m², and a band above
   120,000 RUB/m² were named in sequence — recorded as an approximate
   gradation only, flagged `uncertain` on exact boundaries given the
   ASR quality in this segment.
3. **Comfort segment, 2024, labor only (explicitly excludes design fee
   and furniture)**: 35,000-40,000 RUB/m² for wall-building, plastering,
   electrical, plumbing, screed, and a stretch ceiling. **With furniture
   included**: rises to roughly 100,000 RUB/m²+. Complex/custom-order
   items push the labor-only starting point itself up to 50,000-55,000
   RUB/m² due to added lead time and coordination cost.
4. **Premium-tier big-ticket line items named as concrete cost
   examples**: a dedicated supply-and-exhaust ventilation system
   ("приточно-вытяжная система") can cost 1-3 million RUB on its own
   (worked example: 2 million RUB / 100 m² ≈ 20,000 RUB/m² just for
   this one system); smart-home elements and video surveillance are
   also named as premium-tier-only additions.

## Rules / Heuristics — White-Box Realism, Partition-Material Savings, and Custom-Hardware Lead Time

5. **White Box ("Вайт бокс") realism caution, stated as a personal
   opinion by the company principal**: he states he wouldn't buy a
   White-Box unit himself, and that he hasn't seen a genuinely
   paint-ready ("под покраску") White-Box wall — buying one means
   accepting the walls will need full re-plastering/re-puttying (or
   demolition and rebuild) to achieve a real paint-ready finish; the
   realistic budget scope for such a unit is wallpaper (not paint) plus
   a budget floor (laminate or linoleum). `single-account`, explicitly
   framed as personal opinion, not universal fact.
6. **Partition-material savings tiers, ranked**: foam block
   (`газоблок`, plastered both sides) is called the "golden middle";
   tongue-and-groove gypsum block (`пазогребень`) is denser, also
   normally plastered, but **if wallpaper (not paint) is the finish**,
   it can be built precisely enough to skip full plastering entirely —
   a real savings lever specific to a wallpaper-finish plan. Drywall
   partitions with soundproofing cost more but give the best
   sound-isolation-for-the-money; solid brick is the priciest option
   (material + labor) despite being the most durable/soundproof,
   generally not recommended purely on cost grounds.
7. **Custom-ordered European hardware/fixture lead time risk**: ordered
   items (e.g. a specific fixture/hardware collection) can take 2-3
   months to arrive; the source explicitly recommends the designer and
   builder coordinate this lead time up front so a project doesn't
   reach "renovation finished, contractor wants to be paid and move on"
   with no fixtures on hand to actually close out the job — a second,
   independent instance of the exclusive-material-lead-time caution
   already recorded this round (Round 6 video 2, `8dyPTnmOHKs`), here
   applied to imported hardware rather than flooring.
8. **Investment-flip budget levels are not a safe reference for one's
   own long-term residence**: renovations built cheaply/fast for resale
   ("flip") use different (lower) cost assumptions than a renovation
   meant to be lived in for years — applying flip-level budget
   assumptions to a home you'll occupy long-term risks premature
   material failure, ongoing irritation, and a later redo — net higher
   lifetime cost, not real savings.
9. **Client-expectation-calibration technique at the first design
   meeting**: the source uses a car-brand analogy ("Bentley vs.
   Volkswagen vs. Lada Vesta") to help an undecided client articulate
   what tier of renovation they actually want before a budget is set —
   a concrete communication technique for a design consultation, not
   just "ask the client their budget."
