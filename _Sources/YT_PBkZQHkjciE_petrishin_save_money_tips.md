## Cost Drivers / Labor Prices / Material Prices — Real Per-Unit Market Rates (Numeric Data)

1. **Flooring material-cost comparison, 70m² worked example**:
   engineering board ("инженерная доска") flooring material alone ≈
   300,000 RUB ≈ **$3,700**; quartz-vinyl material alone for the same
   area ≈ 100,000 RUB ≈ **$1,200** — a ≈200,000 RUB (≈$2,500) material-cost
   gap before labor, growing to the video's own headline ≈250,000 RUB
   figure once labor is added.
2. **Installation-labor rate gap for the same two floor coverings**:
   laminate or quartz-vinyl installation from **700 RUB/m² ≈ $10/m²**;
   engineering-board installation from **2,000 RUB/m² ≈ $20/m²** — over
   2× the install cost alone, layered on top of the material-cost gap
   above.
3. **⚠️ Screed-level-planning lifehack, explicit named cost-avoidance
   technique**: when different rooms will get different floor coverings
   of different thickness (e.g. thicker tile in a hallway, thin
   quartz-vinyl in an adjoining living room), have the foreman/project
   pour the screed itself at correspondingly different levels per room
   from the start — this avoids needing a later self-leveling-compound
   top-up layer just to bring the thin covering's surface flush with the
   thick one, a real avoidable added cost. Stated to save "at least
   200,000 RUB" (≈$2,500) in a typical case.
4. **Design-project pricing by type, three tiers**: technical project
   (technical specs only — screed thickness, outlet counts/locations,
   water-point stub-outs, no imagery) from **2,000 RUB/m² ≈ $20/m²**;
   collage project (technical + flat 2D reference images, no true
   dimensional rendering) from **3,500 RUB/m² ≈ $40/m²**; full 3D
   visualization project (photorealistic render accounting for real
   dimensions/colors/furniture placement) from **4,500 RUB/m² ≈ $60/m²**.
   Choosing the technical-only tier over visualization is stated to save
   "more than 150,000 RUB" (≈$1,900) on design alone.
5. **Design-project cost-control tips, beyond tier choice**: ask the
   designer to spec furniture/materials/fixtures from named budget
   retail chains ("Леруа Мерлен" / "Петрович") to keep the smeta down;
   avoid decorative wall elements (stucco/molding) in the design project,
   since these line items meaningfully raise the project's own cost.
6. **⚠️ Labor-tier market rate ladder with an explicit risk/guarantee
   tradeoff, Moscow region**: solo craftsman from **20,000 RUB/m² ≈
   $250/m²** (slowest, since one person can't parallelize trades);
   private crew ("частная бригада") from **30,000 RUB/m² ≈ $370/m²**
   (faster only if the crew is exclusively on your object — the source's
   own stated experience is that private crews commonly juggle multiple
   objects and prioritize whichever pays more, producing excuse-driven
   delays on the others); company from **40,000 RUB/m² ≈ $500/m²**,
   explicitly offering work/timeline guarantees the other two tiers
   don't. Framed candidly as the company's own self-interested case for
   its own tier, but the underlying multi-object-prioritization risk for
   private crews is stated as a real, checkable pattern, not just a
   sales point.
7. **⚠️ Partition-material cost ladder, second independent statement from
   this channel, with a self-disclosed quality caution**: tongue-and-
   groove gypsum block ("позогребень") from **900 RUB/m² ≈ $10/m²**
   (cheapest); aerated-concrete block ("газоблок") from **1,200 RUB/m² ≈
   $10-15/m²**; double-layer drywall partition from **3,000 RUB/m² ≈
   $40/m²** (most expensive). **⚠️ Important caveat volunteered by the
   company itself**: despite listing позогребень as the cheapest option,
   the company states it has stopped using it in its own work due to a
   high complaint/defect rate — real cracking issues reported by past
   clients — and explicitly warns that choosing it means the client
   accepts that risk personally. This meaningfully qualifies the pure
   cost-ranking above; don't read positional-block as a clean recommendation
   despite its price position.
8. **Plastering-method cost/quality tradeoff, real rates on a 220m² wall
   worked example**: strict 90°-square plastering (verified with a level/
   square, thicker material buildup on uneven walls, 3-4.5cm typical)
   from **800 RUB/m² ≈ $10/m²**; "visual" eyeball leveling (minimal
   material use, no square/level verification) from **600 RUB/m² ≈
   $10/m²** — savings of 20,000-150,000 RUB (≈$250-1,900) depending on
   wall area. **Explicit guidance on when NOT to cheap out**: a
   designer-grade renovation with custom furniture and paint-finish walls
   needs the precise method; a budget rental-prep apartment can safely
   use the cheaper visual method.
9. **⚠️ Electrical cost-driver mechanism, two levers, real Moscow-market
   base rate**: cost scales directly with outlet/switch density (halving
   the point count roughly halves the electrical line-item cost) and
   with distribution-panel complexity (fewer breakers/RCDs/voltage
   relays = cheaper panel, up to **80,000 RUB ≈ $1,000** in savings
   depending on the project). **Base electrical installation rate in
   Moscow: from 3,500 RUB/m² ≈ $40/m²** — this is the level-1 region
   evidence for this source.
10. **Plumbing pipe-material cost-neutrality claim, with a
    skill-over-material framing**: the source states polypropylene pipe
    is not objectively worse than PEX ("сшитый полиэтилен") — many
    apartments plumbed entirely in older polypropylene have run leak-free
    for years, and installer skill matters more than material choice for
    a safe, functioning system. **Real per-point labor rates**:
    polypropylene from **2,500 RUB/point ≈ $30/point**; PEX from **4,000
    RUB/point ≈ $50/point** (a "point" = one fixture connection — tub,
    washing machine, sink) — worked 10-point example: 15,000 RUB (≈$190)
    raw labor-rate delta, source states a real-world savings range of
    "20,000-50,000 RUB" (≈$250-620) once material/fitting-count variance
    is folded in. Additional saving lever: keep the utility cabinet
    (сантехшкаф) fit-out minimal (only essential components).
11. **Tile installation-labor rate ladder, real worked bathroom example
    (5m² floor + 24m² walls)**: large-format tile install from **7,500
    RUB/m² ≈ $90/m²**; small-format/mosaic from **5,500 RUB/m² ≈
    $70/m²**; standard mid-size tile from **3,500 RUB/m² ≈ $40/m²** —
    both large-format and mosaic also cost more as raw material, not
    just labor. Choosing plain standard-format tile over either premium
    option saves "more than 100,000 RUB" (≈$1,200) on this worked
    bathroom example.
12. **⚠️ Hidden-vs-standard baseboard/door installation cost gap, named
    consistent pattern**: hidden ("скрытый") baseboard installation from
    **500 RUB/linear m ≈ $6/lin.m**, vs. standard baseboard from **250
    RUB/linear m** (a very small per-unit figure at this exchange rate —
    recorded as ≈$3/lin.m, below this store's usual $10 minimum
    rounding bucket, kept unrounded here since rounding to $0 would
    discard real information). Hidden-mount door installation from
    **6,000 RUB ≈ $70/door**, vs. standard door installation from
    **2,500 RUB ≈ $30/door**. Both hidden-finish options cost
    consistently more than their standard equivalent — worked example:
    ≈17,000 RUB (≈$210) saved on baseboards, and a separate ≈17,000 RUB
    (≈$210) saved on doors, by choosing standard over hidden-mount for
    both.
13. **Ceiling-type cost/speed tradeoff, real rates**: single-layer
    drywall ceiling from **2,000 RUB/m² ≈ $20/m²**; double-layer drywall
    from **3,500 RUB/m² ≈ $40/m²**; stretch ceiling (material included)
    from **1,500 RUB/m² ≈ $20/m²**. Drywall ceilings are also stated to
    be significantly slower to install than stretch. Savings "not
    limited to 100,000 RUB" (≈$1,200), scaling with apartment size and
    material choice.

## Rules / Heuristics — Design-Project Value and General Cost-Saving

14. **⚠️ Design-project value framing, general planning heuristic**: a
    design project's real value is saving time/stress/money through a
    single authoritative document specifying timelines, costs, and a
    clear work sequence — removing the need for constant ad-hoc phone
    negotiation with tradespeople over what happens next, what material
    to bring, or how long a stage should take.
15. **General savings tips, brand-agnostic**: choose off-the-shelf
    furniture over custom-made; choose a non-premium appliance brand to
    avoid pure brand markup; a standard exhaust-only ventilation system
    costs meaningfully less than a large supply-air ("приточная")
    ventilation system.
