## Mistakes / Warnings

- **A shower stall's clearance to the adjoining walkway/passage must be checked against the finished dimension (after wall-leveling + tile), not the raw framing dimension — a real numeric example of the gap.** The defect found: 41cm of clearance before wall-leveling and tile were applied, shrinking to a real 35cm clear passage once finished surfaces were accounted for — described as unusable for the specific (larger-statured) clients even before the shower door itself was factored in. **General rule: always subtract wall-buildup and tile thickness from a raw framing measurement before judging whether a fixture clearance is workable.**
- **A master bathroom's entry point should be accessible directly from the bedroom it serves, not require passing through a shared/public space (e.g. the living room) to reach it.** The defect found: the self-designed layout required leaving the bedroom, crossing through three separate door openings including the living room (where guests might be present) to reach the "master" bathroom — defeating the point of a private ensuite. The replan's fix was simply relocating the bathroom's entry point to open directly off the bedroom. **General rule, worth treating as a standard planning check: verify a master/ensuite bathroom's door opens from the bedroom it's meant to serve, not from a shared corridor or living space.**
- **A protruding wall stub (при стенок) deliberately left uncorrected can be the right call, not a flaw** — the source explicitly defends one such protrusion in this layout as intentional and correct, since removing it would narrow the corridor and force cutting into (shrinking) two bathrooms; the same protrusion also happens to create a clean kitchen-module recess. Framed as a case where an amateur eye might flag something as a mistake that a more experienced planner would deliberately keep. Worth recording as a nuance: not every visible wall irregularity is a defect — check what removing it would actually cost elsewhere in the layout first.
