## Kitchen, Furniture/Built-ins, Electrical

- **⚠️ Drawers vs. cabinet doors for lower kitchen storage — a clear stated preference with reasoning**: drawers let you see/reach everything at once; door-plus-shelf configurations require reaching deep or unloading items in sequence to access the back. Acknowledges some clients still prefer traditional door-and-shelf cabinets out of habit but states drawers are objectively more ergonomic.
- **Over-fragmented drawer fronts on a single cabinet run critiqued**: too many visible drawer faces breaks visual rhythm/adds unwanted "geometry" to the façade. Recommends consolidating to fewer, larger drawer fronts, hiding a secondary utensil tray inside behind the same front.
- **⚠️ Hidden drawer nested behind a single cabinet door critiqued as the WORST configuration**: opening the door fully THEN pulling the drawer, and the drawer can repeatedly knock/scuff the inside of the door over time. **Independently corroborated the same round by `ddRN-vIf3iI` (see cross-reference below).**
- **⚠️ Push-to-open ("tip-on"/"push") mechanism critique, detailed and specific**: more expensive than standard options; fingerprints show constantly on glossy fronts specifically. Explicitly states when push-open IS justified: only for built-in-appliance panel fronts opened rarely, and specifically only when paired with a matte-finish veneer (not glossy). Recommends an integrated finger-pull channel/groove (**"профиль гола,"** quoted inline) as the superior default for both upper and lower cabinets — cheaper, nothing mechanical to fail, preserves clean visual lines. **Independently corroborated the same round by `ddRN-vIf3iI`.**
- **⚠️ Upper-cabinet reachability — Krasnov's own stated personal preference, tagged distinctly, not a universal rule**: as a person of below-average height himself, he deliberately designs upper-cabinet storage to be reachable without a step-stool wherever possible — and reveals his own kitchen has NO upper cabinets at all.
- **⚠️ 800mm upper-cabinet hardware-sizing rule**: an 800mm (80cm)-wide upper cabinet should be split into two 400mm doors, because a single 800mm door is too heavy for the hinge hardware to support reliably long-term.
- **Dishwasher-unloading workflow consideration**: in his own shown project, the upper-cabinet-door layout was explicitly designed so that unloading the dishwasher directly into an adjacent upper cabinet (for plates) is a short, direct motion.
- **⚠️ Task lighting for kitchen work surfaces, with a first-person production detail**: his own head casts a shadow onto the counter while filming/cooking, so an additional light line was specifically added above the primary work surface to fill that shadow. Recommends dedicated task lighting over islands/prep surfaces plus a backsplash light strip as the "finishing touch."
- **⚠️ Built-in vs. freestanding refrigerator comparison, a stated tradeoff**: freestanding models offer meaningfully more freezer/main-compartment volume than built-in models of comparable footprint. For large families/heavy grocery use, recommends TWO built-in fridge columns mirrored side-by-side rather than one larger built-in unit.
- **⚠️ Cabinet-front material comparison, a clear stated ranking with reasoning**: solid wood, ЛДСП (particleboard laminate), and MDF (foil-wrapped/плёнка, painted/эмаль, veneered/шпон), compared on cost, durability, cleanability, visual appeal. Explicitly states his favorites are MDF-enamel and MDF-veneer, MATTE finish only — never glossy (fingerprints/smudges, unpleasant to clean). Recommends veneer as a LOCALIZED accent panel paired against a soft pastel-toned base (his shown example: mint-green base with a wood-veneer accent).
- **⚠️ A practical outlet-count rule of thumb, worth quoting exactly**: count every kitchen appliance you intend to plug in, then add ONE extra outlet as a spare/buffer — states this formula has proven sufficient across his own home and all of his studio's projects. Recommends not being frugal with outlet count anywhere in the apartment. Real example: a switch cluster for main light + task light + under-cabinet light in one location, two dedicated outlets for a kettle, a separate two-outlet section for a built-in coffee machine (named brand **Smeg**, flag per advertising filter) treated as a display "art object."
