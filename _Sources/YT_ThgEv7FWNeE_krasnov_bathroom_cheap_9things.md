## Mistakes / Warnings and Quantities / Measurements — the nine items

- **⚠️ Toilet visible immediately from the door** — the single most common layout mistake he cites: entering a bathroom and seeing the toilet first reads as cramped/utilitarian regardless of finish quality. **Fix**: rotate the toilet away from the direct sightline, screen it behind a partition, or position it so the door swing reveals the sink or tub first. **Explicit exception**: in genuinely small standard-plan bathrooms with the sanitaryware forced opposite the door by minimum footprint, this is treated as unavoidable and normal, not a mistake. First impression forms within a fraction of a second and colours perception of the whole room regardless of the rest of the finish.
- **⚠️ Bold/patterned large-format tile trend-cycle timing, with a quantified comparison**: dramatic tile with a strong pattern/texture across a whole wall or floor visibly dates within roughly **5–7 years** in the studio's own project experience — read afterward as "last season's" or a budget leftover. **Calm/neutral finishes remain current roughly twice as long.** Practical guidance: use dramatic tile only as a point accent (a feature wall, a shower niche, a fragment behind the tub), not across a whole surface.
- **⚠️ Max 3 materials/finishes per bathroom**: mixing too many finishes/tones (his example: chrome + gold + matte black + wood in one room) reads as "current" in isolation but overloads the space — the eye never settles on a focal point and keeps jumping between surfaces, worse in small bathrooms where it visibly fragments the space. Recommended structure: **one base material** (e.g. stone), **one accent**, **one detail material**.
- **⚠️ Mirror/vertical-sconce lighting geometry, layered set of figures**: vertical wall sconces beside the mirror at **eye level, roughly 160–170 cm from floor**, spaced **90–100 cm apart**, gives even face-lighting without harsh top-down shadow (the failure mode of ceiling-only spotlights: shadows under eyes, emphasised wrinkles/nasolabial folds). If using a wall-sconce fixture, **7–10 W bulb wattage at 3000–4000 K colour temperature** gives natural skin tone.
- **General ceiling illuminance target**: **150–200 lux/m²** for a 4–6 m² bathroom. Layer in decorative accent light (under the tub, in niches, under furniture) as an additional soft-glow layer that reduces harsh shadow and adds a usable evening scenario.
- **⚠️ Mirror sizing rules, three figures**: **width ≥ sink width, minus 5–10 cm allowed at most per side** (mirror narrower than the sink, or hung with a large offset above it, breaks proportion and reads generic). **Bottom edge at roughly 110–120 cm from floor**, giving an average-height user a correctly-framed reflection. **Top edge should reach the tallest household member's eye level, typically 180–200 cm from floor.** A correctly-sized mirror also works architecturally — raises perceived ceiling height, adds depth. Full-height (floor/countertop-to-ceiling) mirrors are called a distinct, deliberate design move, not bound by the standard proportions above.
- **⚠️ Hardware/fixture finish durability, restated**: daily-touched items (handles, hooks, mixers) wear fastest; cheap finishes show wear within about 2 years, while **brass or steel with a genuinely good coating lasts decades.** Current market trend (his framing): brass/bronze remain popular, alongside nickel and matte black/graphite, with **classic chrome returning to fashion.** The failure mode is poor coating quality and clashing with the rest of the finish palette, not the metal choice itself.
- **⚠️ Shower floor slope and drain type**: recommended fall **1.5–2% (1.5–2 cm per running metre)** to the drain — too little slope pools water, too much makes the floor uncomfortable/slippery underfoot. **Prefer a linear drain along a wall or the shower entry** over a centre point-drain: a linear drain only needs a single-direction slope, while a centre drain forces a four-sided slope with more geometry errors in practice.
- **⚠️ Dark tones and area-ratio limits, three figures**: dark tones work only as **≤20–30% of surface area** (accent wall or shower zone) in bathrooms **under ~5 m²**. **A fully dark room without natural daylight starts breaking proportion and feeling oppressive from ~6–7 m².** **Fully dark schemes only work well from ~10 m²+**, where enough artificial light and large light-toned surfaces can balance the depth of colour.
- **⚠️ Odour control — ventilation mechanism, with a named cause and named fix**: normative bathroom air-exchange rate is **~6 volume changes per hour** (full air renewal every 7–10 minutes); **most apartment exhaust fans deliver roughly 2–3× less than this in practice.** Consequence: post-shower steam lingers, condenses on tile/grout, and seeds mould/odour — not solvable by cleaning alone. **Condensation mechanism named explicitly**: condensation forms rapidly when surface (wall/floor) temperature is below roughly **18 °C** while room air is at **24–25 °C**. **Fix, stated as the actual solution rather than masking with scent products**: exhaust capacity of at least **90–100 m³/h** for a standard bathroom, plus stable heating that keeps surface temperature from dropping into that gap.
