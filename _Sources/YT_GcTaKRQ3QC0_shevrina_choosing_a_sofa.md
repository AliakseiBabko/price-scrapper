## Furniture / Quantities — Selection Rules

- **⚠️ Bed height: not above 40 cm from the floor** — and she generalises it, stating that any ergonomically correct soft furniture intended for rest should not exceed that. Attached observation: **low beds are the most comfortable**. The only hard number in the source, and the most directly reusable item.
- **⚠️ A short-but-bulky sofa is visually heavier than a longer slim one — the counter-intuitive core of the source.** People buy a compact sofa *because* the room is small, and it makes the room read smaller. Her stated mechanism: **high seat height plus disharmonious side-proportions**. Her named fix: **add legs at the same overall sitting height** — this lowers the cushion mass relative to the floor and lightens the piece visually without losing seat height. She notes the same model can look "стильным, лаконичным, изящным" in its *larger* configuration and wrong in its compact one.
- **⚠️ Headboard proportion: height must be earned by length.** A tall headboard on a normal-width bed reads as "какой-то инородный предмет в интерьере — вроде уже не кровать, но ещё не потолок." (She gives a length figure for when a tall headboard *is* justified; the ASR renders it as "минимум 5 м", which is not credible as spoken and is recorded here only as "must be substantially longer", not as a number.)
- **⚠️ Off-vertical elements clash with the room's real verticals — a compositional mechanism, not a taste verdict.** Slightly angled headboards and bed sides look harmless in a catalogue photo, but a real room is full of true verticals: door openings, wall panels, decorative reveals, track lighting, a floor lamp, a pendant. Set a slightly-leaning headboard next to those and the mismatch reads as an error. This is her stated reason for rejecting angled forms generally, and it is a genuinely transferable argument.
- **⚠️ Don't mix rectangular and round geometry within one piece** — semicircular variants of otherwise-rectangular models "выглядят, знаете, как Котопёс." Applies to sofas specifically but stated as a form rule.
- **⚠️ Repetition of one element at several scales is what makes a modular piece read designed** — her explanation of why one sofa works: seat, arms and back are visibly the *same* element at different sizes, with **identical corner radii and identical forms throughout**. This is the same principle as the "equal radii at every junction" rule in her tables video (`rRQ05JAoLtk`), stated from the positive side.
- **⚠️ Horizontals read as calm, rest and stillness** — and can be reinforced deliberately: horizontal topstitching, horizontally-proportioned cushions, a thin base. Recorded as her stated design rationale, `unverified` as perception claim.
- **Fabric can cheapen or lift the same frame.** Prefer a textured weave with visible stitch (рогожка) or an interesting velour with slight tonal variation over a flat monotone. **But for a piece intended to stay current indefinitely, avoid decorative topstitching along the seams** — it adds period specificity.

## Mistakes / Warnings

- **⚠️ Splayed legs ("ножки в растопырку") and cone/tapered legs — reject; specify cylindrical.** She is emphatic ("самый ужасный элемент любых предметов мебели"), and the practical objection is the same one from the tables video: you strike your little toe on them. **Third statement of this rule from this channel** (also `XGI6FS2ZdCc`, `rRQ05JAoLtk`) — `single-account`, so a consistent studio position rather than corroboration.
- **Complicated forms are a designer's problem, not a buyer's.** Several models are placed in a "leave it to a designer" category — workable, but not safely selectable without one, and hard to restyle later toward a different direction.
