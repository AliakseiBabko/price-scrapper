## Rooms / Zones — Value-Engineering and Layout Changes

1. **Wall-thickness value-engineering, design-vs-execution compromise**:
   a wall projection specified at ~28cm in the client's design project
   was built at only ~15-16cm instead, using a curved drywall segment
   pre-reinforced with a layer of plywood — a real example of trimming
   a design-specified protrusion's depth via a reinforced-drywall build
   rather than full masonry thickness.
2. **Bathroom partition rebuild freeing real space, with soundproofing
   added, not removed**: the shower-stall partition walls were
   demolished and rebuilt (with sound insulation added this time) in a
   way that gained the room significantly more clear width — from the
   originally-designed ~90cm to an actual ~1.36m — a real example of a
   partition-layout change adding both usable space and soundproofing
   simultaneously, rather than trading one for the other.
3. **Client-driven mid-project layout tweak**: the client requested a
   wall section that "sticks out too much" near a door be reduced and
   angled/diagonal instead of a hard corner — a real client-initiated
   change captured mid-construction.
4. **Plywood-reinforced drywall for future heavy-item mounting**: a
   plywood sheet was fixed behind a drywall partition specifically to
   let heavy items (a coat rack, a shelf) be hung there later without
   needing to locate structural backing after the fact.
5. **Design rationale for a window addition in a work zone**: a window
   opening was added specifically so natural sunlight would reach what
   would otherwise be a dark kitchen work zone (countertop/cooktop area)
   — a stated design reason for the addition, referenced back to an
   earlier video in the series.

## Plumbing — Water-Supply Rerouting and Manifold Consolidation

6. **Water-supply rerouting from ceiling to a corridor utility cabinet**:
   the apartment's hot/cold water supply, originally routed under the
   ceiling (per an earlier video in this series), was demolished and
   re-fed instead from the developer's shared corridor utility cabinet,
   tapping the existing heating-riser/water-supply penetration at
   height and dropping down into the unit.
7. **Pipe-material upgrade during the same rerouting**: the original
   polypropylene pipes feeding the unit were removed and replaced with
   cross-linked polyethylene ("сшитый полиэтилен," PEX-A) piping.
8. **Heating-manifold relocation and consolidation**: the heating
   manifold, previously in a different location, was moved into the WC/
   bathroom utility area and consolidated in the same spot as the
   water-supply distribution manifolds, pressure reducers, and filters
   — a single coordinated utility-cabinet zone instead of two separate
   locations.
