## Kids Room / Family Requirements — Durable Facts & Rules

- **Combined parent-child bedroom as a forced compromise, not a
  preference**: this household has only two windows in the entire
  apartment, so the only options were (a) put the child in the
  windowless walk-in closet/gardrobe, (b) combine the child's sleep space
  into the parents' own bedroom, or (c) sell and buy a bigger apartment
  (their actual long-term plan). They chose (b). **Directly analogous to
  this project's own household's Phase 1 arrangement** (also
  child-sharing-adjacent, though this project's case shares between two
  children, not parent+child) — corroborating reference only, not a
  reason to change this project's own plan.
- **Canopy/baldachin ("балдахин") as a soft zoning divider** between the
  parents' bed and the child's sleeping nook inside one combined room —
  explicitly framed by the household as a zoning tool, not pure
  decoration; ordered from a marketplace (Ozon) rather than a mass-market
  retailer specifically for better fabric quality/length options.
- **Bed with soft (padded) side bumpers instead of a hard bed frame edge**
  when a bed sits directly against a wall/room boundary used by a small
  child crossing over at night — the featured child migrates from her
  own nook to the parents' bed most nights; a soft edge avoids bumps/
  bruises versus a hard bed-frame rail. A small nightstand-height soft
  ledge next to the bed doubles as phone/item storage, removing the need
  for a separate nightstand in a tight room.
- **Low, child-height storage drawer for a child's own clothing inside
  the shared room**, kept in the child's own reach so she can dress
  herself (retrieve/put back items independently) — a small dedicated
  chest specifically for the child's clothing, separate from the adults'
  storage in the same room.
- **Bright/saturated-colored children's items ("toys, costumes") are
  explicitly named as visually clashing with a neutral/muted whole-
  apartment palette** — mitigation used here: keep these items genuinely
  accessible to the child (not locked away) but stored inside a closed
  cabinet/drawer rather than left out, so the child can self-serve without
  the bright colors visually competing with the room's overall design.
  Stated as a deliberate, repeatable design principle, not a one-off
  fix.
- **Photo-wall placed directly opposite the bed, at eye level from lying
  down**, specifically so it's the first thing seen on waking — the
  household reports this is noticeably meaningful to their child too, who
  independently comments on it most mornings. Contrast/caution
  (see below): a full-length mirror should NOT be placed visible from the
  bed (uncomfortable seeing one's own reflection while sleeping) — the
  photo-wall works because it's not reflective.
- **Kitchen/dining pass-through window kept closable rather than fully
  open**: a small pass-through opening between kitchen and dining/living
  area, explicitly designed to close (not permanently open like a
  serving hatch), specifically so cooking sounds/smells stay contained —
  motivated originally by a work-from-home partner needing quiet during
  calls, generalizable to any household wanting kitchen isolation options
  without losing all borrowed light/connection.
- **A second door layer (bedroom door + kitchen/dining door) fully blocks
  sound from a sleeping child** in this real case — two doors in series
  between kitchen noise and the child's sleep space, confirmed
  sufficient by the household's own stated experience.
- **Glass panel inserts in an otherwise closed partition wall** preserve
  borrowed light into a windowless-adjacent room while still gaining the
  soundproofing/smell-isolation benefit of a solid partition — the
  household's own stated reason for not building a fully solid wall
  between kitchen and living/bedroom zones.

## Doors/Trim — Durable Facts & Pricing

- **Color-blocked ("colorblock") interior doors as a budget technique**:
  ordinary mass-market laminated doors (not solid wood), painted the same
  single color as the surrounding wall so the door visually disappears —
  avoids the extra cost of a genuinely two-tone-painted solid-wood door
  (needed only if door and wall are different colors, so the door doesn't
  show a "loose tooth" of mismatched color against a colored wall).
  **Real pricing (2025, Moscow, RUB, this household's own project)**: all
  interior doors, color-blocked technique, ran **~180,000 RUB total**;
  the same household estimates a two-tone-painted **solid-wood** door
  alternative would have cost roughly **half a million RUB** for the same
  scope — a large real cost delta attributable specifically to the
  solid-wood + two-tone-paint requirement, not the color-matching idea
  itself (which works with cheap laminated doors). [routes to
  `13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Doors_and_Trim.md`]
