## Switches / Sockets / Cables

Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario says (level 1, spoken directly):

- **⚠️ Socket mounting-type taxonomy**: surface-mounted (накладные, no back-box needed, cable brought out and mounted directly), flush/recessed (встроенные, requires a back-box + routed cable), pop-out/retractable (выдвижные — spring- or button-actuated, e.g. a kitchen-island pop-up socket), and panel-mounted (щитовые — inside the panel itself, clipped to the DIN rail like a breaker; the source explicitly skips detailing these as too specialized).
- **⚠️ Grounding is a hard requirement, not optional**: every power socket should be grounded; the source explicitly recommends against using ungrounded sockets at all.
- **⚠️ Socket functional-category taxonomy**: power (220V/380V), USB, TV, internet/ethernet, HDMI/VGA (video), blank/cap-only, audio/speaker, telephone, and combination sockets (a power socket paired with a USB or USB-C port in one unit).
- **⚠️ Water-protection sockets are visually distinguished only by a hinged cover** — a socket with a cover is water-protected, one without is not; used in damp zones (bathrooms) and outdoor/loggia locations. **Explicit child-safety framing**: shutter-curtain ("со шторкой") child-safety sockets exist (require extra plug pressure to lift an internal shutter) but the source frames good household wiring practice (a correctly-matched breaker/RCD combination) plus parental supervision as the real protection — even if a child does get shocked, correctly-specified protective devices should trip and prevent serious harm.
- **⚠️ Other socket sub-types named**: smart sockets (hub- or network-cable-controlled), lockable-cover sockets (key-actuated cover, no everyday use case given), sockets with an integrated voltage-monitoring relay (very sensitive appliances only — noted as almost never used in apartments), and a decorative illuminated "socket-nightlight" hybrid — this last one explicitly dismissed by the source as a novelty/toy, not a serious recommendation.
- **⚠️ Switch actuation-mechanism taxonomy**: toggle/rocker (single fixed position, on/off), push-button (momentary, springs back after release), rotary/retro-style, magnetic/card-key (hotel-style), dimmer (rotary, rotary-push, or push-only variants), and underfloor-heating thermostats (fully electronic, fully mechanical, or hybrid — source explicitly doesn't dig into thermostat sub-types/brands).
- **⚠️ Switch gang-count and wiring-topology taxonomy, with an explicit worked formula**: a switch can be single/double/triple-gang, and functionally standard, two-way ("проходной"), or crossover/intermediate ("перекрёстный"). A **standard** switch only works from its own single location. **Two two-way switches let one light be controlled from exactly 2 points** — buying two ordinary switches and asking an electrician to wire them as two-way does not work; the devices must physically be two-way switches from the start. For **3+ control points**, keep exactly 2 two-way switches (at the two logical "ends") and add one **crossover/intermediate switch per additional point** — worked examples given: 3 points = 2 two-way + 1 crossover; 4 points = 2 two-way + 2 crossover; 10 points = 2 two-way + 8 crossover. This is a more precise, general version of the existing store's "3+ points adds intermediate switches" note.
- **⚠️ Two devices to avoid specifying, with reasoning**: (1) **triple-gang switches** — each gang is only ~2cm wide (roughly one fingertip-width), making it hard to hit the right gang by feel/in the dark, especially at night; (2) **3+-point crossover wiring on a 2-gang switch** — the cable run required to wire one multi-gang switch across 3+ physical connection points is described as enormous ("kilometers" of cable, hyperbolically) relative to the benefit.
- **⚠️ Avoid a two-way ("проходной") dimmer specifically**: because switch/socket product lines are normally bought as a matched set from one manufacturer/collection, sourcing a two-way-capable dimmer from a different product line than the rest of the apartment's switches/sockets forces a visible style mismatch (different frame shape/finish) across the whole installation — a narrower, mechanism-specific case of the general "buy one manufacturer, one collection" rule below.
- **⚠️ Buy sockets, switches, and cover-frames from one manufacturer and (ideally) one product line/collection, sourced together** — a real documented client case: a client was told to buy "30 sockets, 15 switches" but bought round-bodied mechanisms with square-shaped cover frames from mismatched sources; caught before installation and returned, but the source frames this as a routine, easy-to-make mistake worth warning about explicitly rather than assuming it's obvious.
