## Lighting — the diameter formula

- **⚠️ The rule: take the table's WIDTH — not its length — and subtract 30 cm. That is the pendant's diameter.**
  - Her stated framing: people pick a pendant by eye, on looks. The formula means **you will not hit your head on it when sitting down, while it still gives enough light** — since lamp count usually scales with size, "но это неточно."
  - She grounds it in proportion generally: "не зря же в архитектуре используется принцип золотого сечения."
- **⚠️ Scope limit: this applies to rectangular tables seating no more than three people per long side.**
- **⚠️ For a longer table seating four or more per side, hang two fixtures rather than one bigger one.**
  - **The failure mode she names is specific**: for a 10–12 person table, designers commonly hang one very large many-lamp fixture so the light reaches the end seats — **and it then dazzles whoever is sitting opposite it at the middle of the long edge.**
- **Round table: the same rule — table diameter minus 30 cm.**
- **Small round table up to 90 cm diameter: a single-bulb pendant can work — not only 60 cm but smaller, "от 40 сантиметров."**
- **Square table is effectively the same case as a round one** (it seats half as many people); same rule — width minus 30 cm.

## Lighting — hanging height

- **⚠️ The common error she names: hanging a dining pendant at the same height used for a bedroom or living-room fixture.** Over a dining table the light must sit **lower**, so it does not throw unpleasant shadows on the faces of people at the table.
- **⚠️ With ceilings up to 3 m: 75–90 cm above the tabletop.** With higher ceilings it can be raised.
- **The check that makes the number memorable**: at a 75 cm table height, the fixture's underside then lands **roughly at the eye line of a standing person, or a little above.**
- **Rationale is both compositional and ergonomic**: it will not dazzle a seated person through the whole meal, and it will not sit directly in front of a seated person's face.
- **⚠️ Also size the fixture against ceiling height and room size, not only against the tabletop.** Do not hang a giant fixture in a normal-height room — **"не хватит воздуха,"** it crowds the ceiling and reads unnatural.
  - **The method she gives**: available fixture height = ceiling height − table height − the 75–90 cm gap. **From that remainder you must still subtract the canopy («чашка») and the rod, cable or chain mounting**, the length of which depends on the fixture's style and form. *(Her worked example with a 2.70 m ceiling is arithmetically garbled in the captions and is not reproduced — see Confidence notes.)*

## Lighting — form, and when to break the rules

- **Table shape does not have to dictate fixture shape**, but her own preferences: a round or hexagonal fixture over a round table; over a square table a linear fixture with several straight arms, or a square one.
- **⚠️ Over a rectangular table she dislikes rectangular pendants** — she would use a **linear elongated fixture, two separate pendants, or many small pendants**. She would consider an oval but notes there is very little on the market.
- **⚠️ The rescue for a blocked view: an open frame or lattice («каркасная решётчатая») fixture**, or any type whose form is described by thin lines rather than a solid volume. Use it where the fixture would otherwise block **an interesting window view, interesting wallpaper, or a TV** from some seats. **She notes this also rescues a fixture whose height was not chosen by the rules.**

## Design Concept — a chandelier as decor

- **⚠️ A pendant is frequently decor — a sculptural element that furnishes the room even in daylight — so it must look good switched off.**
- **She endorses buying fixtures that do not give proper light.** Many on the market do not, "и тем не менее я считаю, что их можно покупать и вешать, потому что это украшение интерьера." She observes such fixtures are often especially refined precisely so they sell despite the poor output. *(Worth reading against `9MsEZVjLH2M`, where a lighting engineer argues the same conclusion from the opposite direction — see routing.)*
- **Formality register, as a selection lever**: crystal or glossy metal finishes read **more formal**; wood, woven elements, or a visible bulb **lower the "градус пафоса."**
- **A dining pendant can either support the room's main style or deliberately depart from it** to introduce variety.
- **⚠️ Where the dining zone is combined with a living room, or the kitchen has an island or bar counter as well as a table: work out the relationship between the fixtures deliberately. Keep a hierarchy — the one over the table should be the larger and more prominent — and do not overdo the quantity of decorative light in one zone.**

## Mistakes / Warnings

- **Choosing pendant size by eye.**
- **One oversized many-lamp fixture over a long table** — it dazzles the middle seats.
- **Hanging a dining pendant at bedroom/living-room height.**
- **Sizing only against the tabletop and ignoring ceiling height**, and forgetting the canopy and suspension in the height calculation.
- **Too much decorative light in a single combined zone, with no hierarchy between fixtures.**
