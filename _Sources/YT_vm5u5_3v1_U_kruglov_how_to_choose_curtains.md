## New / Confirmed Content (vs. Existing Page)

**Functions of curtains beyond decoration** — light control (four-tier
fabric taxonomy by transmitted light: sheer/tulle passes maximum light
with slight diffusion; semi-transparent linen/cotton-blend passes
30-60%; "димаут" (dimout) passes 10-25%, best for bedroom/living-room
daytime-sleep or home-theater use; blackout blocks light almost fully,
either as a dedicated fabric or a rear coating applied to any fabric) —
**corroborates** the existing page's BOROSA GROUP dimout (blocks
70-90%, i.e. passes ~10-30%) and blackout figures closely (not merged as
new, recorded as independent confirmation from a 5th source/channel).
Two functions **not previously on this page**: (1) sound absorption —
thick or multi-layer curtains reduce in-room echo and make a room
"acoustically softer" (distinct from the existing page's Хихол
"dedicated acoustic fabric" claim — this is a general density/layering
effect, not a special fabric category); (2) dust filtration — tulle
traps street dust before it enters the room, with the explicit caveat
this is a partial effect only (dust still settles on the windowsill and
inside the curtain folds, requiring more frequent washing) — genuinely
new mechanism for this page.

**Cornice/rod height** — general rule: mount as high as practical, since
this visually stretches the ceiling and enlarges the window; exception
noted for unusually tall ceilings (6m or 4m), where the average-ceiling
rule doesn't apply. **Corroborates** the existing page's more precise
ceiling-height mounting formula (2.7m/3m break points) at the general
level, without contradicting it — recorded as reinforcement, not new.

**Cornice/rod width — a numeric divergence worth flagging**: this
source states the rod should be wider than the window by **at least
30cm on each side** (i.e. ≥60cm combined). The existing page's
"Заметки Дизайнера интерьера"/BOROSA GROUP figure is **30-50cm
combined**. **Recorded as an open, unresolved disagreement** rather than
merged — Kruglov's figure implies a wider minimum than the existing
page's range allows for at its low end.

**Wall-bracket inset detail (new)**: a wall-mounted rod's brackets are
typically inset ~20-30cm from each rod end (e.g. a 2m rod has a
bracket-to-bracket span 40-60cm shorter than the rod itself) — so the
*bracket* span, not just the rod's nominal length, must clear the
window-plus-30cm-per-side target. Not present on the existing page.

**Full-wall-width cornice** (new): mounting the rod across the entire
wall width (not just window-width-plus-margin) creates the illusion of
a floor-to-ceiling, wall-spanning window — a distinct visual technique
from the existing page's "unify two windows" trick.

**Curtain panel width rule (new, distinct vocabulary from the existing
page's gather-ratio formula)**: each panel should be **3/4 of the
window's width** (e.g. a 2m-wide window gets two 1.5m panels) for
correct fold aesthetics when open and no light leakage at the sides when
closed. This is a different calculation method than the existing page's
`rod length × gather coefficient + hem` formula — **recorded as a
distinct/complementary rule of thumb, not merged or reconciled**, since
the two formulas use different inputs (window width vs. rod length) and
weren't cross-tested against each other here.

**Curtain length — four regimes, largely corroborating the existing
page's own four-regime taxonomy, with new cleaning-practicality
framing**: (a) ~1cm above floor — "sterile," least dust buildup, robot
vacuum passes freely; (b) lightly touching floor — most popular current
default, still easy to clean, suits homes with pets; (c) touching +
1-3cm — harder to clean, robot vacuums may snag or skip the zone, less
pet-friendly; (d) pooling 5-15cm on the floor — hardest to clean, only
suited to specific styles. **New, not on existing page**: an explicit
robot-vacuum-compatibility angle across all four regimes, and an
explicit "too long" callout — pooling 20-30cm+ is flagged as a genuine
mistake (heavy dust trap, needs washing roughly monthly especially in
summer, reads as a "heavy," unfinished interior) rather than a
deliberate style choice at any length.

**Radiator/convector clearance (refines existing page's niche-depth
figures)**: standard curtain niche depth is 20-25cm for a two-track
cornice, ≥30cm for a three-track cornice; if the radiator itself is
concealed behind a false wall (flush-mounted), the required niche depth
shrinks correspondingly since the curtain no longer needs to clear the
radiator's own projection. This adds a track-count dependency the
existing page's BOROSA GROUP niche formula (radiator projection +
2-3cm, + 5-8cm/layer) doesn't state explicitly — recorded as a
refinement, not a conflict.

**In-floor convector coverage — a second numeric divergence worth
flagging**: this source states an open curtain should cover **no more
than half** of an in-floor convector, to preserve some heating
performance. The existing page's BOROSA GROUP claim is stricter: an
in-floor convector should **never** be covered by curtains at all.
**Recorded as an open, unresolved disagreement** (partial-coverage-
acceptable vs. zero-coverage-acceptable) rather than merged.

**Curtain type taxonomy (Russian market, supplements existing page)**:
vertical drapes (портьеры), Roman shades, roller blinds, light tulle,
venetian/horizontal blinds (жалюзи), pleated shades (плиссе) — plus
common real-market combinations: tulle + drapes (most common), roller
blackout + drapes, Roman shades + drapes. Broadly corroborates BOROSA
GROUP's existing six-type taxonomy; the combination list is new.

**Outdated/declining treatments (new)**: lambrequins, multi-tier valance
compositions, patterned tulle-plus-drape pairings, "luxury" gold-toned
fabric, and photo-print curtains are named as treatments still
occasionally seen in design projects but functionally obsolete in
current renovations.

**Restated general philosophy (corroborates existing page's aesthetic-
restraint section)**: curtains that read as "expensive" are not
necessarily expensive in price — correct selection and precise
installation matter more than cost. Not re-recorded as new, already
covered by the existing Alla Krol/"Заметки Дизайнера интерьера"
restraint section.
