## Rooms/Zones (Bathroom, Small Bedroom), Engineering/Systems, Mistakes/Warnings

- Glass partitions — minimal soundproofing, need constant cleaning, can feel cold/"office-like." Alternative: sliding wood or combined partitions for privacy plus low maintenance.
- Flush/hidden ("invisible") door panel systems — require perfectly level walls and floor or gaps become visible; cost/install-complexity increases significantly, not viable with cheap materials/rushed installation.
- **⚠️ Smart-home systems — a genuinely new, useful cautionary tech-adoption point, not covered elsewhere in this vault's Krasnov content.** Sensors misfire, companion apps freeze/lag, automations frequently don't behave as configured. Many users report reverting to basic manual switches/thermostats over time and end up using only a few core functions (lighting on/off, climate control) out of a much larger installed feature set. Recommends realistically assessing which specific smart-home functions will actually see daily use BEFORE investing in a full system, rather than installing maximal automation by default.
- **⚠️ Heated-floor scope — a specific, useful scoping rule.** Genuinely worth it mainly in the bathroom and the kitchen's tiled work-zone specifically (keeps a tiled floor comfortable underfoot, helps bathroom surfaces dry faster and resist mould) — rarely worth it in bedrooms/living rooms where wood flooring or laminate is typical and the comfort benefit is minimal.
- **Curbless/flush-to-floor walk-in shower** — reinforces and extends this vault's existing shower-drain-slope content (Round 1) with a specific curbless-design risk framing: praised aesthetically (spa-like, doesn't fragment the room, works well with minimalist/modern styles) but requires PRECISE engineering — an exactly calculated floor slope, quality waterproofing, and professional installation. Any installation error causes water to spread across the bathroom floor instead of draining correctly, with real costly consequences. Explicitly states this is not a place to economize — budget installations frequently get this wrong, and if budget doesn't allow for an experienced installer, this design shouldn't be attempted at all.
- Insufficient outlet/switch count — restates existing outlet-planning emphasis (cross-reference against Round 3's kitchen outlet-count table, `gJfXl9Q3CIw`, don't duplicate) but with a specific critique: designers sometimes hide/minimize visible outlets in renderings for a "clean" look, but real daily electronics needs are usually undercounted at the planning stage as a result, leading to extension-cord clutter later. Recommends counting actual devices needing power BEFORE finalizing outlet placement, not working backward from a rendering's aesthetic.
- **Towel warmer/heated towel rail ("полотенцесушитель") — argued as still largely necessary despite a desire for a clean minimalist bathroom wall.** Without one, towels stay damp and humidity accumulates, risking mould — alternatives (heated walls, standalone electric dryers) don't fully solve this, especially for a multi-person household needing frequent towel turnover. Built-in/concealed towel-warmer models require dedicated plumbing/planning decided at the renovation stage, or there's nowhere to properly install one later.
- **⚠️ Open shelves beside the bed instead of nightstands — a specific tradeoff, distinct from `mqIWre0VXGU`'s "two nightstands mandatory" content in this same round** (that video is about nightstand COUNT for a shared bed; this one is about nightstand FORM — open shelf vs. closed cabinet). Fine only for genuine minimalists with minimal bedside needs (phone charging only) — limited storage capacity, and an open shelf surface collects dust faster than a closed cabinet interior.
- Wall-hung/pedestal-less bathroom sink (no vanity cabinet) — visually spacious but loses ALL under-sink storage for cleaning supplies/spare towels/toiletries; without a basin apron/lip, water can drip onto the floor after use. Extends this vault's existing sink-type content (Round 1), cross-referenced.
- **⚠️ Shower curtain vs. glass partition — a specific, well-reasoned contrarian take worth recording distinctly.** Glass needs constant cleaning and gets visibly grimy quickly, while a fabric curtain can simply be removed, washed, replaced, or cheaply thrown out and replaced new. Explicitly states modern ceiling-track curtain systems in dense/quality fabric don't read as "cheap" the way people assume. Recommends genuinely reconsidering a curtain as the more practical AND still-attractive choice rather than a default budget compromise.
- **⚠️ Painted accent walls — a notable, specific professional-opinion critique, worth its own entry.** Explicitly states that among professional designers, a painted accent wall is increasingly seen as a sign of "architectural poverty" ("архитектурная бедность") — often used to mask a lack of genuine spatial/planning interest or the absence of real architectural detail. States that in genuinely high-end interiors, visual accents are created through material choice, volume/form, or a lighting-and-texture synergy rather than flat paint colour alone. Recommends complex textures, panelling, or decorative techniques instead of a simple painted wall for genuine visual distinction. **Judgment call**: this rises to a new Perspectives-adjacent framing note on the relevant page rather than a full disagreement block, since this vault's other numerous sources on painted accent walls treat the technique neutrally-to-positively without directly addressing "architectural poverty" — flagged as one source's stronger professional opinion, worth surfacing prominently but not (yet) a resolved multi-source dispute.
- **⚠️ Black matte fixtures — see the cross-round Perspectives update below.** Explicitly states black matte faucets/handles show water spots, fingerprints, and scratches quickly and need near-daily upkeep, needing "much more attention" than chrome/stainless.
