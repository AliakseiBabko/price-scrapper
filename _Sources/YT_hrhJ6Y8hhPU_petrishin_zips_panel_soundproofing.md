## Explicit cross-check against Round 10's frame-system soundproofing mechanism (`Soundproofing.md`)

**Confirmed: a genuinely distinct installation approach from Round 10's
wall/ceiling frame system, not a variant of the same construction
method — though both share the same underlying acoustic-decoupling
*principle*.** Round 10 (`rEe5LHWj4fI`/`O4pGx8ESHDU`) builds a
free-standing metal-profile frame off vibration-damping hangers,
decoupled from the structural wall/ceiling via *temporary, removable*
perimeter anchors during construction — the frame itself never rigidly

resilient decoupling element lives *inside each fastening point*, not
in a separate hanger/bracket assembly, and there is no air-gap frame

manufacturer/training source (Acoustic Group). **Conclusion**: ZIPS
panels and the frame system are two structurally different products
sold by the same manufacturer for the same underlying goal (interrupt
rigid structure-borne sound transfer), not the same installation method
applied to two surfaces — this page should present them as a genuine
product-category choice (panel vs. frame), not a single method.

## Walls / Ceilings — ZIPS Panel Wall-Soundproofing Installation Method

1. **Panel construction**: two GVL (gypsum-fiber board) sheets glued
   together with the manufacturer's own adhesive, sandwiching a
   soundproofing insulation layer, plus a resilient insert material
   named "силомер" (silomer) and silicone-filled fastening nodes (6-8 per
   panel) where the panel anchors to the wall — the panel's own built-in
   decoupling mechanism. Tongue-and-groove ("гребень"/"паз") edges let
   panels interlock and screw together edge-to-edge. Total system
   thickness including the finish drywall layer is roughly 5cm.
2. **Real cost figure**: the ZIPS Ultra panel system, including the
   perimeter tape, silicone, and fasteners as a full kit, cost
   **≈2,500 RUB/m² (≈$40/m², trailing-6-month USD/RUB average ending on
   the confirmed 2016-10-13 upload date)** — the practitioner explicitly
   declines to characterize this as cheap or expensive, leaving it to
   the viewer.
3. **Perimeter damper-tape prep, applied before any panel goes up**: a
   named damper tape ("ВИК/VIM100") is applied in two layers around the
   entire wall (and, separately, floor/ceiling) perimeter on silicone,
   and must fully wrap around any obstruction (e.g. an existing drywall
   duct/chase box) rather than stopping short of it.
4. **⚠️ Mistake/warning — excess silicone placement**: when applying
   silicone under the damper tape, keep it close to the wall rather than
   letting excess silicone extend past the tape's edge into the finish
   area — once cured there, it is extremely difficult to remove and
   won't accept putty or paint over it.
5. **Layout-planning rule**: installation always starts from the far
   left corner; measure the run first to determine how many whole panels
   fit, so a corner can be deliberately chosen (in this job, the right
   corner) to avoid needing an awkward partial panel there.
6. **Cutting technique**: use a jigsaw rather than a hand parquet saw to
   trim tongue/groove edges off a panel — slower, but produces far less
   dust.
7. **Socket/outlet handling — a real, mechanism-based caution**:
   drilling through a ZIPS panel for a standard recessed outlet box
   breaks the soundproofing seal at that point; the manufacturer
   (Acoustic Group) advises minimizing such holes altogether — either
   build a same-material buffer insert around any hole that is
   unavoidable, or switch to surface-mounted ("накладные") outlets
   instead of recessed ones.
8. **First-row leveling correction for an unprepared (not beacon-
   plastered) wall**: cut the first row's edge to compensate for the
   wall's own out-of-plumb deviation so every subsequent row stays level
   — the correction is made once, at the first row, rather than
   repeatedly.
9. **⚠️ Real fastener-substitution finding, foam-block substrate**: the
   kit's default dowel anchor didn't hold in this wall's foam-block
   ("пеноблок") substrate when drilled with a hammer drill; the crew
   removed the dowel and fastened with just the self-tapping screw
   directly, relying on its coarse thread pitch — held securely enough
   to proceed.
10. **Utility-avoidance marking practice**: an existing AC line/duct
    route behind the wall was marked with a marker pen before panel
    placement, then re-verified with a chalk/string snap-line after the
    panel was up, specifically to avoid drilling a fastener into it.
11. **Fastening-depth QC rule**: countersink each self-tapping screw
    roughly 1-2mm below the panel surface (checked against a straightedge/
    putty knife) — a protruding screw head can catch under the finish
    drywall layer applied afterward.
12. **⚠️ Row-offset ("разбежка") rule, with a numeric minimum**:
    horizontal seam stagger between panel rows must be **at least 25cm**
    (more is better — this job used ~35-40cm); if a row's natural offset
    comes out smaller, trim the row's starting panel to force compliance
    rather than accepting a tighter stagger.
13. **⚠️ Counter-intuitive fastening-count rule, stated directly by the
    manufacturer**: a whole, intact panel needs only 6 self-tapping
    screws (3 per side) — the two additional corner screws some
    installers assume are needed should specifically **not** be driven,
    because over-fastening measurably degrades the system's
    soundproofing performance. A cut/partial panel, by contrast, should
    be fastened at every fixing node it retains.
14. **Substrate-flatness requirement, with a numeric tolerance**: ZIPS
    panels need a reasonably flat wall — ideally already plastered on
    beacons, but at minimum the wall's own deviation must not exceed
    3-4mm per running meter (checked with a straightedge) — otherwise
    the finished panel surface itself comes out crooked, especially
    visible if the wall will be painted rather than papered.
15. **Pre-drywall QC pass**: after all panels are up and before the
    finish drywall goes on, walk the wall and check every panel-to-panel
    tongue/groove connection node for a protruding screw head, fixing any
    found — a check that materially mattered on this job (a marked AC
    line was avoided specifically because of the earlier marking step,
    not this pass, but the practitioner frames both as standard QC
    discipline).

## Materials — Finish Layer and Sealing Detail

- **Finish drywall board**: a denser, heavier acoustic-specific board
  (named: Акулайн, 12mm GVL) rather than standard drywall — noticeably
  heavier when physically carried by the crew; the added mass is
  credited by the manufacturer with contributing to the system's
  noise-reduction performance (the same product line already recorded
  on `Soundproofing.md` from Round 10's ceiling build, here confirmed
  used on a wall build too).
- **Drywall-screw placement rule**: the finish drywall's own screws must
  not land on top of the ZIPS panel's screws underneath.
- **Damper-tape finishing detail**: cut the perimeter tape at a 45° angle
  at its end specifically so the sealant (named: Вибросил/Vibrosil, also
  an Acoustic Group product) can properly fill the resulting gap.
- **Joint-finishing technique**: sand or float ("тёрка") the screw-head
  seams flush after panels are up; fill deeper joints with Vibrosil
  silicone sealant, wiping excess with a spatula.

## Practitioner's Own Pros/Cons Summary

- **Pros**: (1) relatively simple installation, requiring no special
  prior skill (the crew learned it via a week-long Acoustic Group
  training seminar, no in-depth trade background needed); (2) a notably
  thin overall system (~5cm) versus alternative constructions (frame
  systems, modular "Синема"-type systems) for a claimed comparable
  effectiveness, per the manufacturer's own framing.
- **Cons**: (1) cost — described candidly by the practitioner as "not
  cheap, if not outright expensive"; (2) a genuinely irritant/hazardous
  work environment despite looking simple — cutting GVL and handling the
  damper tape/silicone required masks and gloves, and the crew
  complained; the practitioner explicitly calls it unhealthy work.
