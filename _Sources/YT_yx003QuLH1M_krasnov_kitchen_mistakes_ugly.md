## Kitchen, Furniture/Built-ins, Materials

- **⚠️ Upper cabinets not extending to the ceiling — a specific dual-mechanism critique**: the resulting gap above the cabinets both wastes potential storage AND becomes a hard-to-clean dust/grease trap. Extending cabinets fully to the ceiling reads as more architecturally integrated/finished ("part of the architecture") rather than stopping mid-height.
- **⚠️ Open-shelving critique, with a specific conditional rule**: restates the dust/grease-magnet critique but adds a precise condition — only use open shelves if there is SUFFICIENT closed storage elsewhere to absorb everyday clutter (a burnt pan, a blender); open shelves should hold only curated/decorative items (LED accent lighting, a few art pieces, unique serveware, plants).
- **⚠️ Appliance color/finish coordination principle**: appliances don't need to match brand or product line, but should share a common color/finish family (e.g. black appliances with silver accents should stay consistently black-or-silver, not mix a white fridge + black microwave + grey stove, which reads as visual chaos). If choosing a bold/trend-color appliance line, commit to repeating that SAME accent color elsewhere in the room (towels, small accessories) for rhythm.
- **⚠️ Over-organizing critique — corroborates Round 9's `ddRN-vIf3iI` drawer-divider critique**: too many organizational inserts can paradoxically clutter/waste space. Recommends only tools that solve a REAL problem: pull-out lid racks for pot lids, multi-tier shelving for vertical cabinet space, light under-sink organization; explicitly recommends organizing the REFRIGERATOR too as an underused opportunity.
- Backsplash: avoid loud/trendy colors/patterns since backsplashes are expensive/disruptive to replace; prefer classic/neutral choices that coordinate with changing decor (restates).
- Whole-home style integration: kitchen shouldn't feel visually disconnected from adjoining open-plan living/dining zones (restates).
- Lighting: critiques a single dim central bulb; recommends at minimum a diffusing fixture if only one ceiling point exists, plus dedicated task lighting over the countertop, plus optional decorative wall sconces/pendant over an island (restates).
- **⚠️ Texture-over-color technique for small/monochromatic kitchens**: two worked examples — a small kitchen using ONE base color (light green) but VARYING textures (soft marble vs. matte surfaces) for gentle contrast; an all-white kitchen using a textured geometric backsplash pattern against matte cabinet fronts plus a wood accent for warmth. Record as "vary texture, not just color, to keep a small monochromatic kitchen from reading flat."
- Curated accessory coordination (soap dispensers/cutting boards/towels matched to the room's palette) restates.
- Ventilation: good range hood plus window/vent, maintain filter schedule (restates, generic).
- **⚠️ Hardware scale for small kitchens**: oversized handles are impractical and even a minor bump/snag hazard in a tight kitchen, and visually clutter a small space — recommends minimal/integrated hardware ("almost invisible") specifically for SMALL kitchens; larger kitchens can use whatever hardware style the owner prefers.
- **⚠️ Material-durability red flag**: explicitly warns against adhesive countertop film/vinyl wrap and glue-down flooring in wet zones as failure-prone budget shortcuts; recommends stone/tile/porcelain/wood instead, noting natural stone specifically still needs specialized sealing/maintenance despite being durable.
