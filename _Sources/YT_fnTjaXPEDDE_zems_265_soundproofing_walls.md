## Walls / Ceilings

- **Two stated governing rules for effective wall soundproofing**: (1) **decouple ("отвязаться") the new wall assembly from the neighboring structure entirely** — any rigid connection transmits sound like a taut string, so every fixing point in the assembly (tape, hangers, dowels) is specifically chosen to avoid a rigid path to the shared wall/floor/ceiling; (2) **maximize airtightness** — sound passes efficiently through gaps and microcracks, so every seam, joint, and penetration in the assembly is sealed. `confirmed` as stated governing principles, `single-account`, a genuinely new two-rule framework for this store's soundproofing content (no prior source states these as the two organizing principles of a soundproofing build).
- **Full framed soundproofing wall-assembly build sequence, worked example (bedroom/office adjacent to a neighbor's noisy kitchen-living room)**, stated to reduce noise by **70 dB** for this specific application:
  1. Prime the target wall **and the entire adjacent perimeter** (floor, side walls, ceiling) — not just the wall face itself.
  2. Glue acoustic mat to the primed wall, cut to height with a small allowance, working from spray-can adhesive (aggressive bond, hard to remove once set — apply carefully, brushing into hard-to-reach spots first, rolling elsewhere).
  3. Apply vibration-damping tape around the full wall perimeter, **in two layers**, to soften the rigid contact between the future frame and the surrounding structure — including at the floor even when the floor itself is already separately soundproofed from the neighbors.
  4. Seal every mat seam/joint tightly (airtightness rule) and cut away excess sheet.
  5. Install **vibration hangers ("виброподвесы")** — function as shock absorbers between the wall and the frame's guide profiles, not just structural hangers; laser level used for fixing-point marking, acoustic-rated dowels used specifically to avoid transmitting vibration from the wall into the guide profile.
  6. Install the guide (perimeter) profile using acoustic dowels, then fill each profile channel with mineral wool cut to profile width before installing the vertical stud profiles.
  7. Install vertical stud profiles onto the vibration hangers, using magnetic holders and a laser reference line to plumb/correct each profile; verify straightness across the first several profiles with a straightedge before proceeding; fix vibration hangers to the profile with two self-tapping screws per side once position is confirmed.
  8. **A profile that resists seating into place should never be forced** — forcing it produces a visible bulge/hump in the finished wall; the correct fix is to remove and re-cut the profile rather than force it. `confirmed`, a specific, checkable technique/QC caution.
  9. **Structural reinforcement detail for an outlet box location that falls where a stud profile runs**: cut the section of profile at that point, then build a small reinforcing sub-frame from two additional narrow profile pieces around the outlet box location rather than simply removing a section of the main profile and leaving it unsupported.
  10. Fill the remaining frame cavities with mineral wool (function: absorbs airborne noise — the transcript names dog barking, music, and neighbor conversations specifically as absorbed noise types), including packing wool around the reinforced outlet-box framing, while routing cables out through the wool.
  11. Install sound-insulating panels as the next layer — described as a multi-layer cardboard board filled with quartz and silicone sand, adding both vibration- and airborne-noise damping. **Critical technique**: any cut edge of this panel must be **immediately taped with paper tape**, because an untaped cut edge lets the sand filler spill out, degrading the panel's acoustic performance and effectively turning it into "a very expensive piece of cardboard." `confirmed` as an explicit warning, genuinely new panel-handling technique for this store.
  12. **A specific sound-insulating, modular/sectional electrical outlet box is used, not a standard box** — chosen specifically because it doesn't puncture the sealed sound-insulation contour, and because it can be assembled into whatever module count is needed on site. **Explicit named beginner mistake**: building an expensive, correctly-layered soundproofing system and then puncturing it with an ordinary outlet box — "remember this, and never do it." `confirmed` as an explicit stated mistake, genuinely new and specific for this store.
  13. Seal every panel joint, every panel-to-floor/wall/ceiling junction, every cable penetration, and every screw point with sealant (airtightness rule applied again at this layer), smoothed with a putty knife.
  14. Apply a thin **acoustic membrane layer** over the sealed panel assembly before drywall — described as thin but still effectively damping sound despite its minimal thickness; cut carefully around outlet-box locations and smoothed with a putty knife for full adhesion.
  15. Prepare drywall sheets: bevel cut edges with an edge plane specifically to prevent cracking at the joint after finishing (factory edges already come beveled, so only field-cut edges need this step) — a general drywall-finishing detail, not soundproofing-specific, but stated as part of this same build sequence.
  16. Fix drywall to the frame with a strap/band screwdriver ("ленточный шуруповёрт" — a belt-fed screw gun) along pre-marked lines, screw spacing **no more than 15 cm**; use an oscillating multi-tool ("реновератор") to true up drywall cutouts around outlet boxes; trim excess vibration-damping tape from the perimeter once drywall is fixed.
  17. Final seal of the entire assembly's perimeter and all cable/outlet penetrations with sealant, smoothed with a putty knife.
  - **Closing caution restated as a general principle**: buying expensive soundproofing materials alone is insufficient — the assembly must be installed carefully and correctly, following every step of the technology, for the stated performance to be achieved. `confirmed`, a reasonable closing framing given the step-by-step specificity shown, though the 70 dB figure itself remains `single-account`/`unverified` against any independent acoustic measurement standard.
