## Layout & Zoning

Several real cases show the entryway/hallway boundary itself is
negotiable, not fixed. ИНТЕРЬЕР НАЛИЦО's featured household demolished the
wall between a small, isolated Soviet-era entryway and the living room
specifically to solve a light and circulation problem. Olga Kachanova's
featured household went further: a prior owner had relocated the kitchen
itself into the corridor — that household's own retrospective critique is
worth taking seriously before copying the layout: when a kitchen (or any
prep-space function) sits directly at the entry, verify a genuine
shoe-off/coat-off transition zone still exists ahead of it, or a
multi-person household will end up taking turns dressing at the door (see
also [[01_Entrance/analysis/Storage|Entrance — Storage]] for the
entry-storage-sequencing lesson this case teaches).

For a long, narrow hallway specifically, Anuta Vlady's proportion-control
techniques are the most detailed available: flooring-board direction
(lengthwise reads longer/narrower, crosswise reads wider), line
orientation (horizontal widens, vertical raises ceiling height), and
color-blocking (a dark end wall shortens perceived length; a dark ceiling
avoids a "well" feeling, needing a wall-color extension if the ceiling
exceeds ~3m). A forward-sightline focal point (an accent wall or art
straight ahead) matters more than side-wall treatment. Alla Krol's coarser
companion technique: since a corridor's walls usually can't be physically
moved, divide a long corridor into a **minimum of three visual segments**
so each reads closer to a square proportion. Квартиранты document a third,
different mechanism — a dimensioned, symmetric furniture-and-lighting
composition (mirror, console, lamps, sconces) centered on a doorway,
reinforced with a deliberate color break between zones.

An open bookshelf used as a soft zone divider between an open entry and a
living room is a cheap alternative to rebuilding a wall, doubling as
storage (ИНТЕРЬЕР НАЛИЦО). Independently, a Spain-based practitioner's own
renovation (Alicante, this project's first Spain/EUR-region source)
demolished a non-load-bearing partition wall between the hallway and
living room on the same underlying principle, arrived at independently.

## Wall-Finish Execution Technique

Real corridor-execution technique, as opposed to the layout/zoning
*decisions* above. A Sasha_Life_Vlog individual DIY project documents a
full faux-brick-wall method built from gypsum plaster over a hand-drawn
grid — the key, non-obvious insight being to apply the plaster
**deliberately unevenly**, since an uneven surface reads as convincing 3D
brick while a smooth one works against the effect. Independently, a
tradesperson's full hallway renovation in Alicante, Spain gives this
batch's most complete plaster-through-paint sequence, including a
deliberate wall-texture decision for narrow, light-painted corridors
specifically, and a first-hand account of regional plastering-tool
availability.
