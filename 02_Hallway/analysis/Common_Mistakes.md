# Hallway — Common Mistakes

Part of [[02_Hallway/Hallway_Guide|Hallway Guide]].

- **A two-burner cooktop placed with its long edge against a wall (rather
  than along the countertop's open front edge), under an angled (not
  vertical) range hood, in a compact kitchen-in-hallway layout** — caused
  real, ongoing complaints: large pots/pans touch/heat the wall panel
  behind the cooktop, and steam escapes sideways past the angled hood
  rather than rising into it. Verify cooktop orientation relative to hood
  type at design time, not just the hood's own spec. `single-account`.
  [source: [[_Sources/YT_uvUmZuXgLtU_kachanova_kitchen_in_hallway_case|YT_uvUmZuXgLtU]]]
- **A countertop multicooker sited too close to an active cooktop melted
  its own base/feet** when the cooktop was switched on unintentionally —
  verify small countertop appliances aren't sited within heat/splash
  range of an active cooktop, especially in a tight compact-kitchen
  footprint (relevant to any kitchen-in-hallway or otherwise compact
  layout). `single-account`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_uvUmZuXgLtU_kachanova_kitchen_in_hallway_case|YT_uvUmZuXgLtU]]]
- **No drawers at all in a compact kitchen-in-hallway layout** — an
  acknowledged real design compromise from the tight footprint; even
  cutlery storage becomes inconvenient. Verify a compact-kitchen design
  retains at least one functional drawer rather than optimizing it away
  entirely for cabinet/counter space. `single-account`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_uvUmZuXgLtU_kachanova_kitchen_in_hallway_case|YT_uvUmZuXgLtU]]]
- **Wanted to enlarge a bathroom by borrowing floor area from the
  adjoining corridor, but a load-bearing wall blocked the plan
  entirely** — a real example of a replanning ambition constrained by
  structure, not just preference. `single-account`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_YarbTzTfq6I_interior_nalitso_old_fund_replan_case|YT_YarbTzTfq6I]]]
- **First-time hallway baseboard (plinth) installation, real failure
  modes** (added 2026-08-25): a fixed wall-mounted corner obstruction
  blocked an intended continuous baseboard run, forcing a visibly awkward
  junction — survey the wall for fixed protrusions *before* buying
  baseboard length/corner-piece counts. Separately, **thick electrical
  wiring was discovered inside the wall exactly where a hammer drill was
  needed to seat the baseboard flush** — the household left a visible gap
  rather than risk cutting the wiring. Check for hidden electrical routing
  before hammer-drilling/chiseling a wall for trim; a live-wiring conflict
  can force a permanent visible compromise, not just a delay. A doorway
  threshold miscut by an earlier installer (a separate trade) also could
  not be corrected without replacing it — a real cross-stage QC handoff
  failure. `single-account`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_cCDk-1hthR4_sasha_life_vlog_baseboard_mistakes|YT_cCDk-1hthR4]]]
- **A door jamb left uneven by the installers who fit the door** (added
  2026-08-25) — jamb finishing is not automatically included in door
  installation and needs separate planning/budget even for an
  installation-only job. Attempting to mount a plastering guide-profile
  with double-sided tape (instead of screws, due to lacking drilling
  tools/ability) failed outright — plan for real mechanical fastening
  before relying on this shortcut. `single-account`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_LPcgC50HCMY_sasha_life_vlog_door_jamb_plaster_ep3|YT_LPcgC50HCMY]]]
- **Doing electrical/wiring work energized ("live") purely to keep filming
  a video** (added 2026-08-25) — one source did exactly this and states
  directly, as a caution to viewers: "repeating this and working on live
  wiring is dangerous to health and life." Recorded here as a documented,
  self-flagged unsafe practice, not a technique to copy — de-energize a
  circuit before modifying it, regardless of filming convenience.
  `single-account`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_sAXC1hn8u9A_kvartiranty_hallway_wall_prep_electrical|YT_sAXC1hn8u9A]]]

## ⚠️ Clutter, Concealed Services, and Three Lighting Rules for a Hall (FLAT, added 2026-09-02)

**This page was a single-entry stub before this round.** From a furniture manufacturer's own channel (Михаил, owner of FLAT, St Petersburg); `promotional_ratio: low` — built-in entrance storage is his own trade. RU, 2026-02. **No prices.** **The storage dimensions and material reasoning from the same source are on [[01_Entrance/analysis/Storage|Entrance: Storage]].** [source: [[_Sources/YT_eQdA23i9J8A_flat_ideal_hallway_design|eQdA23i9J8A]]]

### ⚠️ Open shelving and clutter

- **"Чем у вас больше открытых всяких полок, ниш, тем больше хлама всякого. Это вообще стопроцентный проверенный факт."**
- **Second independent account** of the open-shelf-clutter critique already recorded from Игорь Краснов on [[01_Entrance/analysis/Storage|Entrance: Storage]] — **a designer and a fabricator arriving at the same conclusion from different sides.**

### ⚠️ Concealed technical items — and a cost note

- **The hall is where the electrical panel usually sits, along with revision hatches (his example: hatches for transformers serving the building's common lighting). "Вся эта техническая история должна быть от глаз скрыта. Это 100%."**
- **Routes he names: within the wardrobe body; revision hatches made from furniture material; or behind mirrors** — the last being less convenient but admissible.
- **⚠️ And the cost note, which is what makes it actionable rather than aspirational: do not economise on recessing the panel — "не стоит каких-то гигантских денег проштробить эту нишу," not possible everywhere but possible in most cases.** Otherwise it protrudes.

### ⚠️ Three lighting rules

- **⚠️ Use diffuse light, not directional spots.** With spots **you cannot see properly in the mirror, cleaning is uncomfortable, and you get "одно пятно засвеченное, а другое там тёмный угол постоянный."**
- **⚠️ Provide a «дежурный свет» — a low-level duty light — especially where the hall is visible from a child's room.** Arriving home late you would otherwise switch on the main light "и ты будешь просто всем своим мешать." Low-powered, but enough to undress and cross to where you are going.
- **⚠️ A master switch in the hall: "очень классная штука, нужно всем его ставить"** — one key kills every light in the flat, and pressing it on return restores the lights that were on.
  - **⚠️ But he scopes it honestly, and the exemption is the useful half: in a small flat where all the lighting is within your field of view you can skip it, "потому что всё-таки какой-то бюджет он заберёт." It earns its place in a large flat with rooms out of sight, where otherwise "тебе нужно раздеться, опять прийти в комнату, проверить, всё ли ты выключил."**
  - **Corroborates the master-switch content on [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Switches_and_Controls|Switches & Controls]] and adds the small-flat exemption that page lacked.**

### ⚠️ Planning — when a hall is optional, and a real replanning case

- **⚠️ Not having a hall at all is admissible, and the test is the usage scenario.** In serviced apartments where one or two people arrive and drop a jacket it hardly matters. **"А когда ты квартиру используешь каждый день и с семьёй, это уже совсем другое дело."**
- **⚠️ Layouts entering directly into a kitchen-living room get the same test: "если ты там живёшь с семьёй и с детьми — не совсем комфортная история."** **Complements the pass-through-room rules recorded from Безверхая on [[17_Design_and_Ergonomics/analysis/Whole_Home_Planning_Method|Whole-Home Planning Method]] — she gives the numeric ceiling, he gives the household test.**
- **⚠️ A worked case from his own flat, and the resolution is the interesting part.** A wardrobe for outerwear would not fit the available pier — insufficient depth, **about 50 mm to play with** — and forcing it would have been either useless or ugly, **"ты заходишь, у тебя половину входной двери перекрывает торец шкафа."** **The answer was not cleverness within the constraint but replanning: shoe cabinets into the niche, the clothes wardrobe opposite the entrance — which rationalised the hall AND produced a correctly-shaped dressing room for the master bedroom.**
- **⚠️ The general lesson he draws: an initial rough sketch is always potentially wrong. "Ты не можешь сразу всех нюансов учесть, это невозможно. А потом начинает всплывать: вот это я думал, что здесь размещу — это невозможно… изначально твой план нереализуем по техническим причинам."**

### ⚠️ "Нету ничего антивандального"

- **A corrective aimed at a common client expectation: "у нас есть у людей такое, что всё антивандальное должно быть. Иногда такое ощущение, что люди домой с битой заходят… Нету ничего антивандального."**
- **⚠️ With the trade-off attached: "чем более натуральный у тебя материал, тем больше он требует ухода."** His example — **a nubuck sofa: spill wine and the stain is there "до конца своих дней," and you paid for the nubuck.**
- **⚠️ Worth reading against his own four-year retrospective**, where a solid-oak countertop in a wet location cracked and he concluded he should have used stone — **[[07_Bathroom/analysis/Fixtures_Mixers_and_Sinks|Bathroom: Mixers, Toilet & Sinks]]. The principle is borne out by his own flat.**
