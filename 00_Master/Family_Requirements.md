# 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Family Profile, Requirements & Daily Scenarios

This document captures the overall context of the family's needs, preferences, and daily routines to guide the selection of materials, layouts, furniture, and appliances across all rooms.

---

## 🌟 General Family Profile

* **Family Composition**: 2 Adults + 2 Children (ages 3 and 6).
* **Core Philosophy**: Prioritize **reliability, functionality, and convenience** over appearance, brand premiums, or marketing hype.
* **Aesthetic Preference**: Design and color are secondary. Simple, clean options (e.g., standard black/white) are perfectly acceptable if they deliver high performance.

## 🧺 Room-Specific Context & Requirements

### 1. 🧺 Laundry Room
* **User Context**: *"We need a washing machine for a family with two adults and two children, ages 3 and 6. We wash a lot of clothes and bedding. The machines will be stored in the laundry room. Design isn't that important. Color isn't that important either. Black will do. Reliability, functionality, and convenience are more important than appearance."*
* **Key Guidelines**:
  - Focus on **drum capacity** (e.g., 10–13 kg) to wash double bedding sets and heavy children's laundry in single runs.
  - Prioritize **self-cleaning features** (like self-cleaning condensers in dryers) to handle high volumes of lint.
  - Black or standard white gloss finishes are fully accepted.

### 2. 🍳 Kitchen
* **User Context**: 
  - *"In our family, we don't fry in rectangular pans. We only have round pots and pans. A long time ago, I made pilaf in a duck pan on the stovetop. But that's rare. There are fairly thick aluminum and cast iron pans. The advantage of Flex Zones isn't obvious to me. It looks interesting in the video, but when I try to imagine my typical cooktop use case, I think the standard 4-5 coils seem preferable."*
  - *"I don't need a grill [in the microwave]. I have it in my current microwave but never used it."*
* **Key Guidelines**:
  - **No Flex Zones**: Avoid paying a premium for rectangular induction flex induction zones which can cause uneven heat spots on round pans.
  - **Standard Round Coils**: Prefer cooktops with 4-5 dedicated circular induction coils of varying sizes.
  - **Expandable Circular Burners**: A large circular burner (e.g., 28 cm) is highly beneficial for thick-bottomed cast iron/aluminum pans and rare pilaf cooking.
  - **No Microwave Grill**: The family does not use microwave grill features. Standard solo (microwaves-only) models are preferred if they offer better visual, series, or color matching over grill-equipped models.

### 10. 🌅 Balcony
* **User Context**: *"The purpose of this space is storage for hobby/DIY use (fixing things, home projects), relaxation, and occasional light physical exercise. The purpose is NOT to convert it into a real heated room. The plan is to replace the windows and add insulation to extend the usable warm period — make it cold only in the coldest winter months, without dedicated heating. A temporary/portable electric heater can be used occasionally, but underfloor heating or any permanent heating system is not the option. The goal is a closed, well-ventilated, warmer space usable for an extended part of the year — spring, summer, and autumn — in Belarus's subcontinental climate, not the coldest winter period."*
* **Explicit clarification, added 2026-08-10 (verbal, in response to a batch of Zemskov/Zemstandart loggia-glazing sources that raised a join-vs-don't-join decision)**: *"We are not going to fully join the loggia with the smaller bedroom [the adjoining study/small bedroom] by removing any doors or windows. We plan to keep all doors and windows and keep the loggia closed. What we want to achieve by replacing the windows — the cold single-pane glazing — with proper window frames and good insulated glass units, is to create a more comfortable environment inside the loggia for a longer part of the year: not just summer and part of spring/autumn, but from early spring to late autumn. And even in winter, we don't want it to reach freezing temperatures inside — we don't want the ice/freezing damage a cold loggia gets. This is our own decision as the people living here, not Zemskov's recommendation for what to do — his content describes what he does for his own clients, which is useful as reference, but isn't automatically our plan."*
* **Practical consequence of this clarification**: the loggia stays a fully separate, closed room — doors and windows between it and the adjoining small bedroom are kept, not removed, and are not meant to be habitually propped open either (a real failure mode documented in the Zemskov sources: a glazed door left permanently open deforms on its own hinges within ~months and stops closing). The renovation scope is specifically **window/glazing replacement + insulation**, not the more invasive "join two rooms into one" conversion Zemskov's content describes for clients who *do* want that. **The passive-insulation target is now more precise than "make it cold only in the coldest months"**: the household wants the loggia to stay reliably above freezing through an ordinary Belarusian winter without any heating at all (avoiding ice formation/frost damage inside), while the comfortable *usable* season (a space someone would actually want to spend time in) should run roughly early spring through late autumn.
* **Daily Use Scenario**: Three-season-plus (early spring–late autumn) semi-conditioned utility/hobby space (DIY storage and tinkering, light exercise, relaxation) — kept closed and separate from the adjoining small bedroom/study, not year-round heated living space and not merged into the apartment's own heated envelope.
* **Key Guidelines**:
  - Replace the existing single-pane/cold glazing and add insulation to the enclosure — the goal is extending the comfortable-use season (early spring through late autumn) and keeping the space frost-free in winter without heating, not full winter habitability.
  - **Keep the loggia as a genuinely separate, closed room** — do not remove the door/window unit between the loggia and the small bedroom, and do not plan around habitually leaving it open. This is an explicit household decision, distinct from (not derived from) any Zemskov/Zemstandart source's own join-vs-don't-join recommendation.
  - No permanent heating system (no underfloor heating, no hydronic radiator relocation) — a portable/temporary electric heater for occasional use is acceptable, but the space should function passively (well-sealed, well-insulated, well-ventilated) rather than depend on active heating, including staying frost-free through an ordinary winter on passive insulation alone.
  - Durable floor finish to withstand temperature swings — the space is still unheated through winter, even though it should no longer freeze.

Worth flagging if a source specifically addressing this thermal target is found.
