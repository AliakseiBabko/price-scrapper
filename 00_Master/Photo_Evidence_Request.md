# Photo evidence — what is worth shooting, and what a photo cannot settle

**Written 2026-09-04**, answering the owner's question: *"What if I take a general plan, put an
approximate point from which the photo was taken and point the direction of the field of view? Will it
be useful?"*

**Short answer: yes to the orientation map, and the reason is specific — mis-assignment is the dominant
error in this model.** Four figures have been retracted in two days (`G3 = 3315`, `G4b = 1140`, the
`1795`, `V1`'s `490`) and every one was a real number attached to the wrong element. A photo without a
known viewpoint is another chance to make exactly that mistake. **The map is worth more than the photos.**

---

## 1. ⚠️ The one distinction that governs everything here

These are photos of **other completed flats** of the same layout, not of this flat.

| what a photo of another flat is | strength |
| :--- | :--- |
| **building-wide standards** — sill height, head height, vent outlet type and position, pipe stub arrangement, radiator provision, glazing spec | ✅ **strong.** These do not vary flat to flat; they are how the developer builds |
| **what exists at all** — is the towel-rail tail capped, are there two sewer stubs, is the heating distribution visible | ✅ **strong** |
| **this flat's dimensions** | ⚠️ **weak.** Per `Geometry_Variance_Study.md`, another flat's measurements *bound* the error (−45 to +30 mm); they never refine this flat's number |

→ So aim the camera at **standards and existence**, not at dimensions.

---

## 2. ✅ How a photo becomes a measurement

A photo carries no scale — **unless something of known length is in the frame.** Then everything in the
same plane is measurable by ratio.

This model already knows these lengths, so **frame one of them and the photo becomes quantitative**:

| room | known length in frame |
| :--- | :--- |
| middle room, window wall | **O2 opening = 1800** wide |
| living room, window wall | **O3 opening = 1800** wide |
| туалет | wall width **1140**; V1 shaft **700 × 400** |
| ванная | depth **1800** |
| 9.36 room | width **1795** |
| anywhere with a column | concrete **250**, partition **75**, the step between them **175** |

**Shoot square-on to the wall**, not at an angle — an oblique shot needs perspective correction and loses
most of the accuracy.

---

## 3. The shot list, in order of what it unlocks

### 1. ⭐ The window wall, square-on, whole opening in frame — middle room *and* living room
**Settles the sill and head heights for O2 and O3**, which is the single largest gap left in the model.
Sill and head are a *building standard*, so another flat's window answers it for ours. With the 1800
width in frame both heights come out by ratio.

→ This is the highest-value shot in the list by a wide margin. **Everything about the window bands —
plaster area, radiator space below, curtain fixing above, glazing cost — is currently blocked on it.**
The owner has already noted O3's sill is lower and its opening taller than O2's, so **both are needed.**

### 2. ⭐ The туалет, from the door, whole shaft face visible
**Settles whether V1 stands clear of the туалет's east wall or is flush to it** — the model currently
carries 700 long with 290 mm of floor east of it, but 940 flush also fits. It also shows the vent
outlet's height and type, and whether the P1 pipe zone is boxed out or open.

### 3. The P1 zone close up — pipes and stubs
**Settles the plumbing anchors properly:** how many sewer stubs, at what height, whether the hot and
cold are capped, and where the towel-rail tails emerge. The owner has described these; a photo makes them
dimensionable against the 1140 wall width.

### 4. The vent shaft outlet, both shafts
**Settles what the extract actually is** — a hole, a grille, a duct spigot — and its height. This decides
whether kitchen extraction needs a duct run across the ceiling, which is a layout constraint, not a
finish.

### 5. A column-to-partition junction, straight on
**Settles the 175 step visually** and confirms whether the partition sits flush with one column face, as
the model assumes. Also shows whether the 40 mm non-flush setback the owner described is visible.

### 6. The кухня zone looking away from the window, mid-afternoon
**Not a measurement — a check on a claim.** This model wrongly argued that the кухня zone had no daylight
and had to withdraw it. A photo showing how dark that end of the 24.73 space actually gets is the only
honest way to settle how much artificial light the zone needs, and whether the retracted argument had a
grain of truth in it after all.

### 7. The лоджия, glazing and the splay
**Checks the trapezoid finding** — the model derives the лоджия as non-rectangular, splaying 810 mm, with
~2939 mm of glazing. A photo along the glazing line shows the splay directly. Since the frost-free
target rests almost entirely on this glazing, its spec — single, double, frame material — matters more
than any dimension in the flat.

---

## 4. What not to bother with

- ⚠️ **Wide-angle room shots for dimensions.** Barrel distortion makes ratios unreliable at the edges,
  which is where the walls are.
- ⚠️ **Photos of anything already finished** in the other flat. Finishes hide the material change at the
  `R3|G3` split and the junctions — the very things worth seeing.
- ⚠️ **Anything intended to settle `R1a` or the `R3|G3` split point.** Neither is visible, both are
  buried, and another flat cannot answer what this flat's drawing never stated.
- ⚠️ **Areas, in any form.** Standing rule.

---

## 5. What the orientation map itself should carry

Minimum, for each photo: **a numbered point** on the general plan, **an arrow** for the view direction,
and **the photo's filename**. That is enough.

Two things would make it much stronger:

1. **Mark which wall the camera is square-on to**, if any. That is the difference between a measurable
   photo and an illustrative one.
2. **Say which flat each photo is from**, if they are from more than one. A mirrored flat's photo read as
   this layout's would produce exactly the class of error this model has been correcting all week.
