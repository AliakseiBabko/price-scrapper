## Quality Factors & Buying Guidance

**Profile brand has essentially no effect on window quality within the same market tier — the brand-differentiation pitch a salesperson gives is mostly marketing, not a real technical difference.** Classify by market tier (economy/standard/premium) instead of brand name. **Five real quality factors matter more than brand, in stated order of importance**: sealing gaskets (must stay elastic for decades), hardware (one handle turn engaging 5-6 locking points, withstanding thousands of cycles), factory assembly quality, glazing-unit quality, and — the single most important factor of all — **installation quality**: even a top-tier window from the best profile will frost, sag, or lose its seal within roughly six months if installed poorly.

**Buy the window and its slope finishing as one bundled product from one company, not separately sourced** — a company that installs only the window and declines slope work can (and reportedly does) deny warranty claims later by blaming third-party slope work, even when the window itself is at fault.

## Installation & Fastening Technique

**Frames must be fastened with drilled-through direct fasteners (anchor bolts or dowels) into the structural opening — mounting plates alone cannot resist wind load and are for preliminary positioning only**, with one specific exception: **soft base materials (aerated concrete, foam block) flip this rule** — a single direct fastener spins freely in soft material under load, so distributed multi-point mounting plates (4-7 self-tapping screws per plate, ≤60cm spacing) become the *correct* permanent method there, not just a fallback.

**The installation foam seam is this whole system's best-documented weak point, and it needs real technique, not a single bead**: fill only ~25% on the first pass so the foam can expand and cure properly (an all-at-once thick fill reliably leaves void defects — even one the size of a screw head defeats the window's thermal performance and shows up as ice on the slope within 2-3 years), then build up in further passes, trim the cured excess flush, and always protect the seam with exterior weatherproofing tape and interior vapor-barrier tape.

## Measurement

**A zero-reference-point method, not per-window guessing, is what makes multiple windows line up**: find the highest point across all windowsill areas via laser survey, set a zero mark 15mm below it (the minimum tolerable foam-joint thickness), and measure every opening from that shared reference. **A second/adjacent window's height must never be independently measured — match it exactly to the first window's already-established height**, since even 1cm of mismatch between two visually adjacent windows is glaringly obvious, especially under a stretch ceiling.

**The single most consequential measurement trap is loggia-specific**: forgetting the floor will be raised (screed buildup for insulation) means a glazing height measured off the old parapet leaves the new safety parapet too low once the floor comes up. **Minimum safe parapet height from the future finished floor: 1100mm.**

## Slope Finishing

**Plastered window slopes are never appropriate for a PVC window, for four mechanism-based reasons** — thermal-expansion mismatch between PVC and plaster cracks the joint regardless of skill; plaster itself cracks over 5 years against the window's 10-20 year rating; a plastered slope has no closing/retaining contour, so wallpaper's cut edge is always exposed; and plastering forces the frame's first foam joint to be trimmed thin — exactly the wrong compromise on the system's known weakest joint. **The correct default is an insulated sandwich-panel slope**, mechanically fixed with a snap-together two-piece closing contour (never glued) in primary (never recycled) plastic.

**Routing electrical outlets/switches into the newly-widened windowsill created by slope-boxing radiator pipes is one of this channel's most repeated, best-explained ideas** — genuinely useful desk-charging and bedside-switch placements, with the common condensation/electrocution fear addressed directly (the slope cavity sits at room temperature, not below the dew point, so no condensation forms on the contacts).

## Hardware Selection

**Tilt-and-turn is unambiguously the better default over turn-only**, at only a ~1-2% cost premium — it avoids needing a hard fall-limiter, adds trickle ventilation, and critically, protects a large/heavy sash from self-weight deformation on its hinges (tilting transfers weight to the bottom frame instead). **For a tall/panoramic window section below the 1.1m code parapet height, split it into a fixed lower pane plus a small opening transom above**, operated via a remote handle-and-linkage mechanism rather than either a rain-prone low handle or an unreachable high one.
