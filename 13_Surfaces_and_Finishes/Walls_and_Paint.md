# Walls & Paint - Do's and Don'ts

## Gas-Concrete-Block Wall Material Calculation — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-10)

> [!NOTE]
> New topic angle for this page. Two Zemskov/Zemstandart sources, `single-account` throughout — the first his own stated working figures (filmed on an atypical single-wall-thickness clinic build-out); the second a real filmed destructive-test comparison featuring a guest technologist affiliated with a block manufacturer (Bonolit) — that guest's endorsement of his own company's product category is flagged as commercially-affiliated, not neutral. [sources: `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_gas_block_wall_material_calculation_2e558d93.txt`, `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_block_adhesive_destructive_comparison_18922877.txt`]

- **Zemskov's block-quantity formula**: 7 blocks per m² of wall, regardless of block thickness — sum wall areas from a **per-wall labeled-area project sheet**, not a single lump total (which, per Zemskov, only supports a plastering-area calculation and no other material quantity, since a real apartment mixes wall thicknesses — 100/150/200mm — and finish materials by wall). **Never buy the full calculated quantity up front** — order roughly half first, since some second purchase is unavoidable once door openings and on-site cutting are accounted for.
- **Zemskov's adhesive-quantity formula**: one 25kg bag of block adhesive covers 1m³ of masonry, **regardless of block thickness** (thinner blocks need more units but proportionally thinner adhesive layers, keeping volume-based consumption constant) — apply a real-world **+20-25% correction** over the manufacturer's nominal figure. The **first course is always laid on ordinary cement-sand mortar, never block adhesive** (a thin-joint system that can't level an uneven raw floor) — typical layer 1.5-3cm.
- **Only 2 of 6 commonly-used block-bonding materials are actually correct, per a real destructive-test comparison with a guest technologist**: **cement-sand mix** — first course only, ~5× the material use of specialized adhesive beyond that, poor plasticity, slow strength development; **Rotband/gypsum plaster** — technically bonds but needlessly expensive at its required 5mm minimum thickness, and risks a chemistry clash if later finished with cement-based plaster in a humid room; **tile adhesive** — bonds fine, but carries a real ~50% cost premium for tile-specific additives blocks don't need (negligible on one wall, tens of thousands of RUB at full-project scale); **specialized mineral block adhesive — the correct default**, rated -10°C to +35°C (one product for the full seasonal range), achieves joints as thin as ~0.5mm; **specialized polyurethane "foam-glue" — this source's top recommendation**: no water/electricity needed on site, ~3× faster installation, reaches initial strength in 30 minutes and full strength in 24 hours (vs. ~3 days/28 days for any mixed mortar) — caveat: the first course must be leveled with extreme precision, since foam-glue can't correct subsequent-row alignment the way a workable mortar bed can; **ordinary construction/mounting foam — explicitly, categorically wrong** despite looking similar to the specialized foam-glue — its real secondary expansion pushes blocks out of alignment, requiring significant extra plastering to correct.
- **Zemskov's meta-point on why break-testing (as performed in the comparison video) doesn't predict real wall performance**: a finished wall is never subjected to the pulling/twisting force used in such a test, and every candidate material's true strength only develops after ~28 days regardless. **The three criteria that actually matter for choosing a bonding material, per Zemskov**: speed of construction, cost (avoiding capability the application doesn't need), and whether the next course can be built without waiting for full design strength.

## Drywall False Walls — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-10)

> [!NOTE]
> `single-account`, filmed on a real jobsite building a false wall to conceal utility pipes — general drywall-framing technique, not room-specific. [source: `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_drywall_false_wall_masterclass_6d77f6f5.txt`]

- **Zemskov's case for a false wall over exposed pipes**: enables clean baseboard/ceiling-track junctions (neither terminates cleanly against a round pipe), restores the room to a furnishable rectangle, and often yields a wider countertop as a bonus — at the cost of a small floor triangle (his example: roughly A4-sheet sized).
- **Layout method**: find the most protruding element to hide, offset the drywall's inner face 3cm past it, then use a laser plane-builder (bottom marker on the floor mark, vertical-plane mode, adjusted to the wall reference point) to project a true vertical reference plane for the whole run. If the main wall is significantly bowed, cut the perimeter track into segments rather than forcing one piece to bend against it.
- **Zemskov's rule: join framing elements with a professional crimping tool (просекатель), not screws** — he calls "screws are stronger" a myth; every screw head, even flat ones, locally deforms the drywall face. A professional crimper folds/interlocks the metal (verified with a destruction test); an ordinary one just punctures it.
- **Zemskov's rule: use guide-track profile throughout the frame, not stud profile**, even for elements that would normally be studs — stud profile's folded edge blocks freely inserting cross-pieces, forcing special connector clips that would need cutting in half anyway near a window-reveal junction.
- **Convector-niche framing** (relevant to a balcony-block's lower section): build from one continuous bent piece for rigidity; niche sizing exactly 5mm larger than the grille's mounting dimensions; position with 10cm clearance above (not visually centered — the lower gap gets covered by baseboard anyway, and centering leaves awkward regulator access).
- **Bracing rule**: a stud/track frame alone has low rigidity — brace to the main wall with fabricated angle brackets, re-checking verticality throughout (easy to deform the frame slightly while fixing each brace). No fixed brace count — enough that the frame doesn't wobble under a shoulder-lean or wallpapering pressure.
- **Zemskov's sheet-cutting method: cut in place, not by tape-measure-and-pencil** — hold an oversized sheet against the frame, mark by pressing/creasing at the frame edge, then snap/fold/cut along the crease; he frames the tape-measure method as "10 extra actions" for the same eventual result.
- **Safety warning**: never brace a screw with a bare finger behind the drywall as a "stop" — a 35mm screw can drive nearly through the finger. Always wear gloves and safety glasses.

## Do's

| Rule | Applies To | Reason | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Use decorative slats/panels for visual zoning instead of moving walls | Living Room, Bedrooms, Hallway | Significant budget savings over full demolition/rebuilding | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_budget_video_4b421350.txt` |
| Allow wet gypsum plaster to dry completely (2-3 weeks) before applying putty | All Rooms | Prevents plaster detachment, cracking, and mold growth | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_mistakes_video_21ade3f6.txt` |
| Apply paint using airless sprayers for large wall areas | All Rooms | Achieves near-perfect finish uniformity across extensive wall surfaces | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_apartment_renovation_guide_360f4c7c.txt` |
| Choose quality wallpaper over smooth paint-grade plaster finish on tight budgets | Bedrooms, Living Room, Hallway | Saves high wall preparation labor costs while achieving clean, durable wall finishes | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_a8e90887.txt` |
| Install aluminum concealed door frames before wall plastering and puttying | Interior Door Openings | Enables seamless flush plaster/putty alignment; door leaf is hung after final wall finishes | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_2_61e3a372.txt` |
| Dismount developer radiators from walls prior to wall plastering | Rooms with Radiators | Ensures smooth, complete plastering behind radiators rather than uneven patching around mounted units | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_7_b385361e.txt` |
| Apply betokontakt or a scrubbed adhesive tile-glue layer over smooth monolithic concrete before plastering | Monolithic Concrete Walls | Prevents heavy plaster coats from debonding and cracking off smooth concrete substrate | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_7_b385361e.txt` |
| Document per-wall labeled areas, not a lump total, per Zemskov/Zemstandart | Gas-concrete-block walls | A lump total only supports a plastering calc; per-wall areas support block/adhesive quantity by thickness | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_gas_block_wall_material_calculation_2e558d93.txt` |
| Use specialized mineral or polyurethane block adhesive, not tile adhesive or cement-sand mix, per Zemskov/Zemstandart | Gas-concrete-block walls (beyond the first course) | Tile adhesive is a real ~50% overpay; cement-sand mix uses ~5x the material and is slow/weak | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_block_adhesive_destructive_comparison_18922877.txt` |

## Don'ts

| Rule | Applies To | Risk | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Do not demolish walls unnecessarily on tight budgets | All Rooms | Demolition costs accumulate 4x (demolish, haul, rebuild, plaster) | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_budget_video_4b421350.txt` |
| Do not use painted smooth plaster for dark-colored window reveals | Window Openings | Dark paint rapidly fades under direct sunlight and plaster joints crack from frame thermal expansion (use color-matched dark composite or PVC reveals) | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_3_a0e895b1.txt` |
| Do not install window sills or window reveal finishes before wall putty sanding is complete | Window Openings | Heavy airborne plaster and putty sanding dust irreversibly ruins new window sills and reveal surfaces | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1.txt` |
| Do not glue gas-concrete blocks with ordinary construction/mounting foam, per Zemskov/Zemstandart | Gas-concrete-block walls | Real secondary expansion pushes blocks out of alignment, despite looking similar to the correct specialized foam-glue | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_block_adhesive_destructive_comparison_18922877.txt` |
| Do not buy the full calculated block quantity in one purchase, per Zemskov/Zemstandart | Gas-concrete-block walls | Exact final quantity is never predictable in advance (openings, cutting) — a second purchase is unavoidable anyway | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_gas_block_wall_material_calculation_2e558d93.txt` |
