## Style & Finish

attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is archive-only and has no decisive `channel:` field: **Three interior door style categories**: Modern (contemporary but not sharp/futuristic — his own stated default), Hi-Tech (sharp lines, heavy glass, futuristic — only reads correctly in genuinely minimalist spaces), Classic (reserved, in his view, for ceilings above ~3m — "imperial"-scale classic doors look out of place in a typical apartment under standard ceiling height). His rule: doors and furniture should be "from the same era" stylistically.

attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is archive-only and has no decisive `channel:` field: **Panel/finish preferences**: avoid raised door-panel moldings (outdated, adds thickness for no benefit); prefer rounded, not sharp, panel edges (sharp corners visibly collect dust); avoid cheap laminated-paper-wrap surfaces (they peel/delaminate); on light/white doors, prefer a mottled/textured finish over flat gloss (flat gloss shows every fingerprint).

attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is archive-only and has no decisive `channel:` field: **Casing (наличник) convention**: for a Modern-style door, use straight/butt-joint casing corners, not classic 45°-mitered ones — and make the top casing piece slightly thinner than the sides so it sits recessed, hiding the top piece's cut end grain (his stated highest-quality finish detail).

## Hardware Selection

attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is archive-only and has no decisive `channel:` field: **Handle-selection method**: grip and pull the handle firmly ~10 times in a row — reject it if uncomfortable at any point. Advises against gold/shiny-plated handles (plating wears through at contact points, leaving bare-metal patches) in favor of silver-toned finish, and against oversized/ornate handles.

attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is archive-only and has no decisive `channel:` field: **Hinge-type-by-style rule**: Hi-Tech doors, concealed hinges only; Classic doors, visible/traditional hinges only; Modern can use either. Caveat: not every concealed-hinge model opens a full 180° — a door needing a wider-than-90° swing (his example: a bathroom door) must have that specified to the supplier in advance.

attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is archive-only and has no decisive `channel:` field: **Bathroom-vs-office lock distinction**: a bathroom door needs a lock securable from inside but openable from outside in an emergency (e.g. with a coin); an office/study door needs a proper keyed lock instead, since its purpose is securing the room when the occupant leaves. Recommends magnetic (not spring-tongue) door latches — no protruding mechanism to wear out, engages only on closing.

## Project Sequencing

attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is archive-only and has no decisive `channel:` field: **Choose interior doors first → then matching baseboard/casing profile → only then the entrance door's interior-facing trim panel** — even though the entrance door is bought first chronologically (start of project) while interior doors install last, its interior trim and the surrounding wall-reveal finish (откосы) must match the interior doors' eventual style. Easy to get backwards.

## Entrance-Door Hardware

**Recommendations**: vandal-resistant exterior finish, not wood-look (wood-look shows permanent, unrepairable damage); 2-3 hinges (3 preferred for a heavier door); two locks — one behind an armored strike-plate cover, the other a **re-pinnable type** so a lost key (~2,000 RUB) can be replaced without swapping the lock body; **passive deadbolt pins** (always-engaged, hinge-side) to resist a hinge-side grinder attack. **Explicitly recommends against active deadbolt pins** (lock-turn-activated corner pins) — his stated reasoning: frame corners aren't the real weak point they're marketed to protect, and he's seen the connecting linkage rod wear through and snap, jamming the door permanently shut.

attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is archive-only and has no decisive `channel:` field: **Recommended-tier interior door price point (2019, historical — do not extrapolate to current pricing)**: 12,000-15,000 RUB/unit, factory-fitted hardware, which he describes as visually comparable to expensive Italian doors at a fraction of the price.
