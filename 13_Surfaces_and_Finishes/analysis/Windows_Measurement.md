# Windows — Measurement

Part of [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Windows|Windows]]. **One of the densest, most numerically precise sources in this project** — a full worked-example measurement reference for standard windows, balcony blocks, and loggia glazing.

## Standard Window Measurement

- **All windows visible simultaneously in one sightline should have sills at the same height from the floor.**
- **Tool: a laser level on an elevation tripod** (fine-adjustment crank/rod), not a standard tripod (leg-length-only height control, impractically tedious for precise matching).
- **Zero-reference-point method for multiple windows**: find the single highest point across all windowsill areas via laser survey; set the "zero mark" **15mm below** that point (the minimum foam-joint thickness under the sill/base profile needed to avoid freezing — can be more elsewhere if the sill is uneven, never less than 15mm anywhere). Physically mark this zero point at every opening, including hard-to-reach spots.
- **Measurement-from-zero method**: measure from the marked zero point up to the opening's top, then subtract the standard top-joint allowance (30mm) for the frame's actual order height.
- **Sketch-first rule**: sketch the future window unit on paper before measuring, including mullion/pillar (impost) positions.
- **Worked example (height)**: opening height 1600mm − 30mm top joint = **1570mm order height**.
- **Width-measurement rule**: measure width at three points (bottom, middle, top); take the minimum reading; subtract 60mm total (30mm per side).
- **Worked example (width)**: 2000mm minimum measured − 30mm − 30mm = **1940mm order width**.
- **Scope caveat**: this simplified method applies to replacing an existing PVC window with a new PVC window (measured off the old frame's footprint) — replacing a wooden window with PVC needs a materially different, more complex approach.
- **Always independently re-verify every sash/section's real measurement rather than trusting a previous installer's numbers** — specifically to catch a facade-code violation: enlarging a window beyond its original opening on a regulated-facade building can force removing and redoing the new windows at cost.

## Balcony-Block Measurement

- **Reference "zero" offset is taken from the project documentation's stated future-finished-floor level** (worked example: 110mm above rough floor). Set the laser level's horizontal plane at 1m + that offset (example: 1.11m) as a convenient working mark, mark it clearly on the wall, then measure up and down from it, subtracting joint allowances as before.
- **Worked example**: rough floor to future-screed zero = 110mm; +1000mm convenience offset = working mark; measure up and subtract 30mm (top joint); measure down and subtract 15mm (bottom joint) — the frame sits directly 15mm above the future screed with **no base/support profile** in this configuration, covered later by the finished flooring.
- **Critical width rule for a balcony block with an integrated door**: always exactly **two** widths measured — total opening width and door width. **Never** a separately-measured "window width" — it's always the total minus the door width, calculated, never independently tape-measured.
- **Worked example**: total opening width 1600mm − 30 − 30mm = 1540mm; door opening width 800mm − 30 − 30mm = 740mm; window width is the derived remainder.
- **Critical height rule for a second/adjacent window measured against a first**: never independently tape-measure a second window's height — match it exactly to the first window's established height. Even 1cm of mismatch between two visually adjacent windows is glaringly obvious, especially under a stretch ceiling. Cross-check with a tape measure only as a safety verification; if that implies a joint under 30mm, adjust the *second* window's allowance, using the first as the fixed reference.
- **Loggia/balcony-block glazing color must match the building facade exactly** — requires manufacturer color-swatch fans.

## Loggia/Balcony Glazing Measurement (Most Complex Case)

- **The single most catastrophic, effectively unfixable mistake**: forgetting the loggia floor will be raised (screed buildup for insulation, e.g. 11cm). Glazing height based on the OLD (pre-raise) parapet leaves the new safety parapet too low once the floor is raised. **Minimum safe parapet height from the future finished floor: 1100mm (1.10m)** — add a filler panel section if raw glazing falls short.
- **Measure glazing per mullion/section individually** — more measurement steps, more accurate overall result.
- **Tape-measure handling technique**: never let the free end flop loosely — always extend it, brace it against something stable, then read. A professional-vs-amateur tell.
- **Loggia glazing height, worked example**: measure raw height, subtract 30mm top joint and 15mm bottom joint.
- **Critical exterior-insulation-removal trap**: if a loggia's exterior insulation (batting/wool) will be removed (correct practice once glazing itself is insulated), its thickness must be subtracted from the glazing-width measurement, or the finished glazing overhangs past the true facade plane once the insulation is gone. **A measurer who says "no filler strip needed, we'll account for it" is wrong** — removing insulation doesn't move the true facade plane. A proper filler/spacer strip ("добор") is legitimate and necessary. Method to check true thickness: poke through with a long screwdriver until reaching the load-bearing wall, measure that depth, size filler strips to it.
- **Critical corner-loggia positioning trap — the "worst" mistake**: pulling the frame line inward to center it within future parapet insulation shrinks usable interior loggia volume. **Correct: keep the frame at the OLD parapet's outer line regardless of planned insulation thickness** — let insulation buildup create a wider finished windowsill instead of sacrificing floor space.
- **Frame accessibility rule**: the frame and its glazing beads must always remain fully visible/reachable (for future glass replacement) — under-sizing gets absorbed by filler strips, never by burying the frame under flooring.

## Safety Glazing

Once a removable/temporary parapet guard rail is taken down (commonly done well before final glazing installation), lower glazing panels below the original parapet line must be **either tempered or laminated/"triplex" safety glass — no other type acceptable**. **Laminated/triplex is explicitly preferred over tempered**: tempered glass shatters into small pieces (won't cut you, but still leaves an opening someone could fall through); laminated/triplex holds together when broken (like a car windshield), physically preventing a fall-through even after breakage — worth the extra cost specifically for a lower/safety-critical pane.
