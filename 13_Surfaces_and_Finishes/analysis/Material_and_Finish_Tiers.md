# Doors & Trim — Material & Finish Tiers

Two distinct axes — **frame/core material** and **surface finish** — both cross-cut by the same underlying theme: **repairability, not just up-front price, is the real quality differentiator.** Part of [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Doors_and_Trim|Doors & Trim]].

## Frame/Core Material

Four tiers, ascending (retail, one Moscow/SPb retailer, by mount type — separate from concealed vs. classic):
1. **Cheapest MDF-film/cardboard-core doors** — not repairable if damaged; a scratch or dent means replacing the whole leaf.
2. **"Eco-veneer" (экошпон) doors** — a better pine frame with MDF panels wrapped in polypropylene film. Worth knowing: **the "eco"/veneer naming has no actual connection to real wood veneer or ecological materials** — it's a marketing-naming convention, not a material claim.
3. **Solid-wood ("массив") doors** — repairable; a scratch or damage costs roughly ~5,000 RUB for professional restoration rather than replacement.
4. **Concealed doors with aluminum edge trim** — itself two sub-tiers: cheap Chinese aluminum trim on a cheap pine/cardboard core (explicitly warned against by the source) versus full-aluminum-frame construction, which won't warp and is field-repairable by touch-up paint/polish.

One retailer's own stock and stated prices — not verified as market-wide data.

## Surface Finish: Enamel vs. Film

A separate axis from frame material or mount type:
- Sergey Gusev / ПРО ДВЕРИ says: **Enamel** can be locally sanded, filled, and repainted if scratched or chipped (inexpensive, effectively invisible after repair) and can be fully repainted a different color later. One drawback: a cheap 1-2-coat application can show minor "orange peel" texture at an angle — a manufacturing-quality issue, not inherent to enamel as a material.
- Sergey Gusev / ПРО ДВЕРИ says: **Film (PVC/polypropylene/"eco-veneer")** cannot be locally repaired at all — a scratch requires replacing the whole panel or leaf — and cannot be painted over. A manufacturing debris fleck trapped under film stays permanently visible as a raised defect, whereas the same defect under wet enamel can be sanded flush and repainted before the enamel cures. Film-wrapped doors reportedly build up static charge and attract more airborne dust than enamel, especially visible at seams/corners (worse near a busy road with frequently open windows).
- Day-to-day cleaning performance is **not** reported as a differentiator between the two.

## Hardware

- Zemstandart/Alexey Zemskov says: **Door hardware (hinges + handle + lock) should not be bought cheap even on an otherwise budget door** — a quality hardware set costs ~5,000–7,000 RUB and is stated to last ~10 years; low-priced hardware is described as unreliable by default.
- Zemstandart/Alexey Zemskov says: **Hidden vs. exposed hinges — the cost delta is small relative to overall project cost**: a plain exposed hinge costs ~700 RUB vs. ~3,000 RUB for a normal (non-fully-concealed) hidden hinge — a ~2,300 RUB difference framed as ~0.004% of a full renovation budget, argued as never worth economizing on given how visually poor cheap exposed hinges look. `single-account`, `unverified` figure.
- Zemstandart/Alexey Zemskov says: **Thin (~6 mm) door casings require matching ultra-thin baseboards** — a thick baseboard meeting a thin casing leaves a visibly cut/exposed baseboard edge at the joint. Always acceptable to pair a thick casing with a thin baseboard; never the reverse.
