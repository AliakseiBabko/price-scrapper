# Doors & Trim — Entrance Doors

Covers minimum functional width, opening-relocation technique, manufacturer pricing tiers, and a security-spec claim from two competing, self-interested retailers. Part of [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Doors_and_Trim|Doors & Trim]].

## Sizing and Relocation

Zemstandart/Alexey Zemskov says: **Minimum functional clear/light opening width: ~74 cm (740 mm)** — the practical floor below which passing through with furniture (e.g. a non-collapsible armchair) becomes impractical. This floor should take priority over cosmetic rules (e.g. casing standoff — see [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/analysis/Rough_Opening_and_Casing_Sizing|Rough-Opening & Casing Sizing]]) when a hard site constraint (a neighboring wall, embedded electrical conduit) forces a choice.

Zemstandart/Alexey Zemskov says: **A practical maximum leaf width exists too, not just the minimum above: roughly 90cm.** Past about 1 meter, an entrance door leaf becomes genuinely heavy and a real injury risk, without meaningfully improving function beyond that point. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_HLiLNZpVWpg_max_disaster_258|YT_HLiLNZpVWpg]]]

Zemstandart/Alexey Zemskov warns: **A building/HOA can prohibit residents from changing developer-installed entrance-door swing direction at all — verify this constraint before assuming a swing-direction fix is available**, not just a technique choice. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_6syO3dTButw_designer_disaster_66m2_259|YT_6syO3dTButw]]]

Zemstandart/Alexey Zemskov says: **Relocating a developer-installed entrance door opening in a light partition wall is generally low-complexity**: cut/rebuild the wall section, weld a steel angle-iron frame (~8 mm stock) around the new opening for reinforcement, backfill with cement-sand mortar, weld the door frame to the angle-iron, plaster to conceal it, and finish reveals with standard interior-door jamb-extension panels to match. One documented real project shifted an opening ~30 cm this way; the only cited downside was needing anti-vandal stairwell-plaster repair, and no cost figures were given. `single-account`, real project in a light aerated-concrete partition — **may not generalize to load-bearing walls or larger shifts.**

## Installation Process, Real Minsk Service Pricing, and Selection Criteria (added 2026-08-24)

Pavel Sidorik (individual finisher, `single-account`), real new-build
installation, Belarus level 1 (speaker states "у нас в Минске" directly;
all prices in Belarusian rubles):

- **Measurement protocol**: always use a (free) professional measurer even
  if self-measuring is possible — a common mistake is measuring the
  opening with existing casing/liners still attached, giving a wrong
  order size. Remove developer casing first to expose the true rough
  opening; measure width at 3 points, height at 2 points (from the
  opening-swing side, to preserve floor clearance), to ±0.5cm.
- **Real named service price breakdown, Minsk, 2020-12-23** (trailing-6-
  month USD/BYN average, rounded): door installation **90 BYN (≈$40)**;
  old-frame removal preserving the frame for resale **15 BYN (≈$10)**;
  supplemental mounting-plate installation (for a monolithic opening with
  embedded rebar behind planned anchor points, letting anchors be
  repositioned sideways) **15 BYN (≈$10)**; notching stairwell tile
  skirting without removing the tile **15 BYN (≈$10)**. A removed old
  door/frame can itself resell via classifieds for **≈45 BYN (≈$20)**,
  partially offsetting installation cost.
- **Real anchor pattern**: 12 anchors total (6 through the frame directly,
  6 through supplemental mounting plates); frame leveled with plastic
  shims in two planes before any anchor is driven — **level is the single
  most important acceptance criterion** for a newly installed entrance
  door. Foam gap-fill in two passes for full coverage; trim excess foam
  the following day after full cure.
- **Door weight/handling**: a 100-series steel door weighs ≈130 kg — two
  installers with shoulder-slung lifting straps, or four without straps.
- **Protective wrap-during-renovation technique**: PVC tape (named:
  Blue Dolphin) around the frame, plus a cut sheet of the door's own
  cardboard shipping packaging over the face (preferred over the bubble
  wrap it also ships in — cardboard resists impacts like an accidental
  drywall-profile jab better than film alone).
- **Selection-criteria priority order, this practitioner's own stated
  ranking**: (1) soundproofing — leaf "series" number (60/70/80/90/100)
  tracks leaf thickness/mineral-wool fill; a 100-series leaf with 4
  sealing contours (German Schlegel weatherstrip) plus a 16mm MDF exterior
  panel is his top-soundproofing pick; (2) appearance/material continuity
  with the apartment's interior-door line; (3) security — steel thickness
  (1.5mm cited), break-in-resistance class (a 13-class scale, 4 chosen),
  and anti-removal bolts (ригели) specifically resisting hinge-side forced
  removal.
- **Lock-cylinder practicality pattern, worth noting independent of the
  named brands**: install cheap temporary cylinders during renovation
  (many tradespeople need keys), then upgrade to higher-security cylinders
  once renovation completes — the named upgrade brand (abus) also offers a
  master-key system (one key opens several different abus-cylinder locks
  across properties) and cylinder recoding if a key is lost.
- **Maintenance routine**: periodically lubricate the metal latch/deadbolt
  and hinge moving parts (hinges have a lubrication port); always press
  the door fully shut after checking the latch, not just nudge it, so the
  bolt fully seats.
- Commercial mentions flagged, not adopted as neutral fact: specific
  door brand (Стальная линия) and lock-cylinder brand (abus) — a
  practitioner's own stated purchasing pattern (cites 80% of his own
  clients choosing that door brand), not an independent brand comparison.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_TKTKBmTkebg_sidorik_entrance_door_ep10|YT_TKTKBmTkebg]]]

## Apartment vs. House Door Construction, and a Manufacturer's Series Line-up

Pavel Sidorik (`single-account`, corroborating his own earlier disclosure
in the ep10 note above of preferring this manufacturer for ~80% of his
clients), filmed inside the Стальная линия ("Stalnaya Liniya") showroom in
Minsk, Belarus level 1, 2017-08-26 — **flagged high-promotional-density**:
the whole video is a single-manufacturer product walkthrough, arranged in
advance with the company, not an independent comparison:

- **An apartment door and a house (street-facing) door solve different
  problems, per this practitioner**: a house door needs
  weather/UV/moisture resistance an apartment door (facing an indoor
  corridor) doesn't — moisture-resistant MDF facing, zinc-coated metal
  (rust prevention), a stainless-steel threshold (vs. polymer, optional
  upgrade on an apartment door), a multi-layer insulation stack (foam +
  foil-faced polyethylene + an air gap, with stiffening ribs kept clear of
  the MDF to avoid cold bridges), a thermal-break frame, and sometimes a
  glazed insert. An apartment door instead is filled fully with mineral
  wool, prioritizing sound isolation. **Stated selection-priority order**:
  apartment — (1) security, (2) soundproofing, (3) appearance; house —
  frost/weather resistance first. Both need hermeticity (blocking odors/
  sound from a shared corridor).
- **This manufacturer's own 70/80/100 series specs** (series number tracks
  leaf thickness: 7/8/10cm) — `ASR-uncertain` on a couple of exact figures:
  70-series ≈1.2mm metal, 50mm mineral wool, 10mm MDF facing, 2 Russian
  locks + night latch, 2 sealing contours; 80-series adds 12mm MDF facing,
  better weatherstripping, an eccentric leaf-to-frame adjustment, and
  custom sizing; 100-series ≈1.5mm metal, 100mm mineral wool, 16mm MDF
  facing, 3 frame contours + 4 leaf contours, fully custom finish.
- **Break-in-resistance class conflict, unresolved**: this source states
  all of this manufacturer's doors are "class 4," calling that **the
  highest class under Belarusian standards** — this appears to contradict
  the ep10 note's own class figure (a 13-class scale where class 4 was a
  mid-range choice, not the ceiling). Not reconciled here; may reflect two
  different classification systems (a manufacturer's internal strength
  rating vs. an official burglary-resistance scale) or an imprecision in
  one of the two sources. Treat both class-4 claims as `uncertain` until a
  third source or an official standard is checked.
- **This manufacturer's own showroom pricing, Minsk, stated directly in
  USD, 2017-08-26**: 70-series **$250-300**; 80-series **$350-500**;
  100-series **$500+**. Brand-specific catalog pricing, not a market-wide
  benchmark (same caveat as the `Novakey` tier table below) — and dated
  nearly a decade before that 2026 entry, so not directly comparable
  without an inflation adjustment even within the same product category.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_BpqCE2DeN9E_sidorik_choose_metal_entrance_door|YT_BpqCE2DeN9E]]]

## Old-Building Installation Technique and Acceptance Checks

Pavel Sidorik (`single-account`), real old-building (Soviet-era) retrofit
install by the manufacturer's own installers (Стальная линия, same
project/date as the showroom video above — 2017-08-26), region **inferred
level 2** from the companion source (this video itself names no city, but
the two videos are the same real project and were uploaded together, with
the companion video stating "Минск" directly):

- **Old-building opening measurement differs from the new-build protocol
  above**: where a concrete door "rebate"/reveal ledge ("четверть") is
  still in place, measure the opening **from the inside**, not the
  exterior face — an exterior measurement with the rebate present reads
  too narrow, since that rebate gets cut away to match the new frame. Real
  measured opening on this project: 91cm × 208cm. Rebate-removal scope
  varies by building era: this project needed only a small trim, but
  Khrushchevka-era buildings often need the **entire opening widened**.
- **Install sequence**: cut/remove old door → trim the concrete rebate to
  the new frame profile → dry-fit and knock back interference with a
  hammer drill → level the frame (hinge side first, then latch side) →
  clear insulation from anchor points → drill and drive anchors while
  re-checking level continuously → hang the leaf (hinge bearings
  lubricated with silicone grease *before* hanging) → install hardware
  (lock cylinder core also greased) → cut keys and test with the client
  present.
- **Acceptance/QA checks**: the top gap must be even on both the hinge and
  latch sides (confirms the frame is level in both planes); leaf-to-frame
  play is removed via the factory-preset eccentric adjustment (4mm hex
  key) mentioned as an 80-series feature above — this is the actual
  field-adjustment procedure; deadbolts must engage their frame holes
  without binding or rattling.
- **Foam-fill technique**: wet the gap with water first (removes
  anchor-drilling dust, improves adhesion) before foaming in two passes —
  first bead along the casing edge, a brief pause for initial expansion
  before fully filling (avoids overflow), then a light water-mist to avoid
  staining the painted interior frame.
- **Handover briefing example**: bottom lock — key inserted horizontally,
  fully seated, two full turns; top lock — key inserted with its marked
  dot down, also two full turns; **always press the handle fully down
  after closing** so the latch fully seats, otherwise the mechanism
  operates under tension and the deadbolts drag the frame — the same
  underlying maintenance point already recorded above from the Minsk
  new-build source, now with the mechanism explained. Night latch, once
  engaged from inside, makes the door unopenable from outside by any
  means; the separate turn-knob still opens from outside with a key.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_CVTf-a4XIEI_sidorik_install_metal_entrance_door_diy|YT_CVTf-a4XIEI]]]

## Security-Spec Claims — Two Self-Interested Sources, Not Resolved Here

**The question**: does a genuine security upgrade (thicker steel, premium locks, up to 65 dB soundproofing) justify its price premium, or is basic hardware "sufficient"?

**The two positions, both from competing retailers, neither adopted as this page's own guidance:**

| Source | Position | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| A Moscow-area budget retailer | Thin steel (1.2 mm) and standard locks are fully adequate; thicker-metal/high-security doors are oversold marketing | Cites a secondhand press statistic ("Moscow burglaries down 35× in 13 years"; "199 city-wide in 11 months," most reportedly involving unlocked doors, not forced entry). Their own "sufficient" tier prices a basic 1.2mm-steel MDF-panel door at ≈65,000 RUB — an explicit alternative to a 150,000–200,000+ RUB competitor door. |
| The manufacturer above (tier-ladder source) | Sells a full ladder up to 2 mm steel / Italian locks / 65 dB soundproofing, up to 380,000 RUB | Implicit: higher tiers deliver meaningfully more security/soundproofing, worth the premium. |

**Why this isn't presented as a resolved comparison**: both are self-interested claims — one retailer favors selling cheaper stock, the other favors selling expensive tiers. The burglary statistic specifically is a secondhand press citation, not independently verified. Neither framing should be read as neutral market fact.
