# Doors & Trim — Anatomy & Mount Types

Covers door vocabulary and the three-way comparison between classic hinged, sliding-pocket, and concealed-mount doors. Part of [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Doors_and_Trim|Doors & Trim]].

## Key Concepts — Door Anatomy & Vocabulary

**A door unit (дверной блок) has five components**: **leaf** (полотно, the moving panel), **frame** (коробка, the fixed box the leaf hinges/slides against), **jamb extensions** (доборы, panels that extend the frame to match a wall's actual thickness), **casing/trim** (наличники, the decorative trim covering the wall-frame junction), and **hardware** (фурнитура — hinges, handle, lock). Standard leaf height is 2.0 m; standard leaf widths are 60/70/80/90 cm.

**Telescopic jamb extensions are generally preferable to flat-board extensions** — they slide into a channel in the frame and self-adjust to an uneven wall or a slightly twisted frame, producing a cleaner junction than a flat board that can't absorb wall irregularity.

## Mount Types Compared

A two-host structured comparison across 6 criteria (soundproofing, reliability, cost, wall-prep, installation logistics, use-case) is the most systematic single source on this topic — summarized below, supplemented by a single-channel batch's earlier findings and a second batch's independent corroboration.

### Classic Hinged (default recommendation)

Seals on 3 of 4 sides via the frame's rebate — the structural reason it outperforms both alternatives on soundproofing. Full turnkey installation ≈5,000 RUB/door (2026, one company's rate) — the cheapest and simplest of the three to install, with no moving mechanism beyond hinges/handle themselves, so the lowest long-term failure risk. **This page's default recommendation absent a specific reason to choose otherwise** (space constraint, a narrow soundproofing-tolerant use case).

**⚠️ A documented real defect confirms the rebate mechanism from the failure side**: a WC door installed without a proper frame rebate/stop — sitting ~5mm short of the opening's edge on all sides — had near-zero soundproofing despite otherwise looking like a normal door; the source states the frame rebate/stop is what actually creates a door's acoustic seal, and a door installed without one is acoustically almost equivalent to no door at all, regardless of leaf material/thickness. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_ob9iEwc3GWc_35m_170m2_scammed_263|YT_ob9iEwc3GWc]]]

**Magnetic (floor-mounted, flush) door stops have a real hardware-durability tradeoff against a traditional mechanical stop, worth stating to a client rather than assuming a strict upgrade.** They look cleaner (no visible protruding bump), but releasing the door from the magnetic catch requires enough force that it visibly strains/bends the door's own handle hardware over repeated use — one practitioner's own experience: expect to repair or replace the affected hardware roughly every 6-12 months with magnetic stops. Still worth using in a genuinely tight space where a traditional protruding stop would create a real injury risk (e.g. striking fingers in a narrow WC) — a situational exception, not a blanket preference. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_6syO3dTButw_designer_disaster_66m2_259|YT_6syO3dTButw]]]

### Sliding-Pocket

**Materially worse soundproofing than hinged doors, even when hidden behind a false wall** — a basic sliding door has essentially no seal, versus a hinged door's 3-sided rebate seal. First stated by one source (~80% of that speaker's own sliding-door clients complained about sound; complaints stopped once switched to hinged), and **now independently corroborated by a second, unrelated company** (Ontario), which separately ranks sliding-pocket doors as the clear soundproofing outsider for the same underlying mechanism (leaf anchored only at the top rail, minimal seal elsewhere) and recommends caution specifically in bathrooms/WCs and bedrooms — genuinely two-source-corroborated, not just one opinion.

**Mechanism reliability is a second, independently corroborated weakness.** One account: rollers, stoppers, and soft-close dampers commonly begin failing en masse ~3-4 years after installation, and repair requires disassembling substantial finished work to reach the hidden mechanism — the speaker's stated primary reason for abandoning sliding doors in his own practice after using them heavily from ~2009. Ontario's account names a **different specific fragile component** — the opening-limiter/stopper inside the pocket cavity, awkward to reach/repair once damaged (some designs need a wall access hatch planned in for service) — a complementary, not duplicate, mechanism.

**A real height/width penalty**: sliding-door hardware adds ≈5 cm to overall installed height versus a hinged door on the same 2 m leaf; an 80 cm rough opening yields ≈79 cm clear width with a hinged door but only ≈71 cm (≈8 cm less) with a sliding door — in tight corridors this can shrink an opening to as low as 60 cm.

**Appropriate as a narrow exception, not a default**: a private ensuite bathroom/shower serving only 1-2 people, where a hinged door's swing arc has no viable path. The downsides are judged tolerable specifically because a small, intimate user group is affected and usage frequency is low. Outside this case, the standing recommendation across sources is against sliding interior doors. Pricing (material/hardware only, one company, 2026): from ~20,000 RUB.

### Concealed / Hidden-Mount

The most expensive, most demanding, and most contested mount type — see [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/analysis/Concealed_Door_Considerations|Concealed-Door Considerations]] for the full wall-prep/tolerance/schedule breakdown.

**Costs roughly 2–3× an equivalent classic door once frame/hardware/prep/installation are fully counted**, independently stated by two unrelated companies (Ontario's itemized ≈30,000 RUB total install, roughly matching the door's own ≈30,000 RUB material/kit floor; Avalremont's independent 2–3× multiplier framing, compounding to "hundreds of thousands of RUB" across 5–6 doors in one apartment, with design renders typically not disclosing this delta up front) — a real, two-source-corroborated multiplier, not one company's markup.

**Itemized full installation** (one company, RUB, 2026): commonly ≈30,000 RUB/door — 7,000–10,000 structural (frame, concealed hinges, lock) + 5,000–6,000 two-sided painting + 5,000–7,000 flush-side wall-seam finishing + ≈7,000 exposed-reveal finishing.

## Door Casing/Baseboard Consistency & Threshold Installation (added 2026-08-19, remainder-pool Round 2)

**⚠️ Door casings (наличники) and baseboards should be the same color and, ideally, the same manufacturer/material** — reading as one continuous trim element running through a space, not two visually distinct product lines. **Never try to paint a door to match an adjacent wood-textured surface's exact color** — the colors will never truly match under real lighting; use a deliberately contrasting color for a painted door near wood-textured elements instead. **⚠️ Flooring-to-door-frame transition rule**: flooring should meet the door frame directly, with only a small gap filled by color-matched sealant — never a separate visible threshold strip, especially not one with exposed fasteners, a mismatched color, or an angular/asymmetric shape (a real documented failure case combined all three). `single-account`, `unverified`.
