# Windows — Installation & Fastening Technique

Part of [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Windows|Windows]].

## The "Quarter" (Четверть) and Thermal Rationale

The quarter is the reveal/ledge the frame recesses into (brick in brick buildings, poured concrete in panel buildings, wood in wood-frame buildings). It improves thermal performance because the frame sits recessed a few centimeters, protecting the first foam joint on all sides — versus a flush/direct opening, where the frame simply abuts the wall with only a thin foam layer and loses heat faster. **A quarter gives no fastening advantage** — the same wind load acts on the fasteners either way.

## Fastening Method: Direct by Default, Distributed Plates for Soft Materials

**Default rule: frames must be fastened with drilled-through direct fasteners (anchor bolt or dowel) into the structural opening.** Mounting/fastening plates alone cannot resist wind loads and must not be used as final structural fastening — legitimate use is preliminary/temporary positioning only. Frame drain holes near the fastening points are a deliberate manufacturer feature (prevents condensate accumulation, offset so snow can't enter), not a defect.

**Exception, soft base materials (aerated concrete/gas-block, foam block)**: a direct fastener spins freely under load in soft material instead of holding (demonstrated live — a screwdriver bit drills into aerated concrete with no impact action needed at all, confirming how soft it is). **Correct method: distributed multi-point mounting plates, not a single thicker fastener** — load spread across several points holds better in soft material, and is harder to defeat by impact (more points must fail simultaneously).

**Numeric fastening spec for aerated-concrete/foam-block walls**:
- Vertical frame sections into wood-based structural elements: dowel, pre-drilled with an **8mm bit**.
- Frame sections into foam block: mounting plate flush against the profile's structural shoulder (better wind-load resistance), fastened with **4-7 self-tapping screws per plate**.
- Screw spec into foam block: **90mm length, no dowel/plug** — driven directly.
- **Plate spacing ≤60cm** — a 1.5m frame length typically uses 3 plates.
- Stated comparison: 5× 4mm-diameter screws hold more securely and resist impact better than 1× 8mm dowel, in soft material specifically.
- **Hardened ("каленый") screw caveat**: normally discouraged for window fastening (brittle in *hard* materials), but this doesn't apply in soft materials — the soft material absorbs/cushions the impact load that would otherwise shatter a brittle hardened screw. `single-account`, informally demonstrated (a hammer strike on camera), not lab-tested.

**Fastening plates ARE correct at the top of a widened opening** when the header material (e.g. foam-block/plaster infill) can't hold direct fasteners — install the frame, level/true it on one side first using plates, only then fasten the second side; fastening both sides before leveling risks a permanently bowed ("house-shaped") frame.

## Mounting Wedges, Never Wood Scraps

Wedges are cheap, never rot, and level reliably — they stay permanently as load-bearing pads. Cut pieces of old wooden window frame ("дрова") as shims is a bad, historically-common practice. **One narrow legitimate exception**: when the foam-joint gap exceeds **80mm**, treated hardwood timber (not scrap softwood, not construction offcuts) may be used — never as a load-bearing shim substitute, always alongside proper wedges.

## On-Site Leveling and Torque Technique

Set frame in opening, temporarily fix and level with a spirit level and mounting wedges. **Temporary support-block technique** avoids bending the frame while torquing a dowel/anchor — use a support block so the fastener can be fully torqued without deforming the profile. **Correct dowel-torque technique**: many installers stop as soon as the fastener head first touches/dents the plastic — this under-tightens and holds nothing. Correct: continue until the head passes through the plastic and seats against the frame's internal reinforcement, then back off half a turn once deformation is first visible.

## Foam-Joint Technique

**Three-stage perimeter foam-seam technique, not a single application**: pre-fill large gaps/voids first; form the continuous perimeter seam filling **only ~25% initially** so the foam expands properly without over-thickening (over-thickened foam can't cure properly and reliably develops voids/fistulas — even a 3-4mm void, about the diameter of a standard screw, defeats the window's thermal benefit entirely, and a rushed one-pass seam typically has several dozen such voids); trim the cured excess flush. A rushed one-pass seam shows visible ice on the upper slope **within 2-3 years**.

**Two-stage technique specifically for joining a separate door and window frame into one balcony block**: apply foam along the joining edge with the hinge-side frame face-down, wait for the foam's primary cure/skin (slightly springy, non-tacky surface) before assembling — disturbing uncured foam collapses its expansion and leaves a weak joint. Apply a **second, smaller foam bead** on top of the nearly-cured first bead to reactivate/re-bond for a stronger final joint. Remove the connector profile's protective film before install. Join the two frames with self-tapping screws long enough to seat in the second frame's internal reinforcement — never over-tighten (deforms the profile).

**Installer-process minimum, from a separate acceptance-checklist source**: foam the joint in **two passes minimum** — skipping this risks through-voids that grow over time into interior drafts. Prepare external waterproofing (tape or a vapor-permeable membrane) *before* setting the window, not after; apply vapor-barrier tape over the finished joint afterward (protects the foam from interior room humidity).

**Correct foam-joint width, with the mechanism for why not to minimize it (added 2026-08-19)**: **20-50mm, with ~25mm cited as the practical optimum** — even where wind load isn't a structural concern. The reasoning isn't cosmetic: a narrower joint forces shorter frame fixing screws/anchors, and shorter fixings flex/deform more under load over time than longer ones would in a wider joint. A wider joint (within the 20-50mm range) both allows longer, more rigid fixings and leaves more room for insulation — don't minimize this joint purely to save foam material or "clean up" the reveal unless a real structural/wind-load reason specifically calls for it. ## Hardware Quality Checklist (Tape/Multi-Point Hardware)

Described as "the single most important detail" of a PVC window — five criteria:
1. **At minimum 4 positions**: closed; standard open; tilted; and **trickle/slot ventilation** — a distinct locked-but-not-fully-sealed mode letting air circulate around the sash perimeter while all locks stay engaged. **Banknote test**: firmly pinched when fully closed, freely movable in slot-ventilation mode, sash still cannot be opened.
2. Reliability of individual mechanism components — the "tilt scissor" hinge mechanism specifically as a build-quality marker (substantial, not flimsy).
3. Low-maintenance — good hardware needs only a few drops of oil in a dedicated port periodically.
4. **Foolproofing** — should physically prevent combining open+tilted simultaneously (handle can't rotate to tilt until the sash is fully closed).
5. **Anti-burglary resistance** — quality hardware's keeper hardware is visibly substantial even in base configuration vs. generic hardware defeatable by hand-prying and a light strike.

## Water Sill (Отлив) Quality Checklist

1. **Factory-manufactured on precision equipment**, not hand-bent on-site — hand-bent sills have rounded/imprecise edges and **2-3mm** dimensional variance between nominally identical pieces (a genuine factory sill's edge-to-edge tolerance is under **1mm**).
2. Ships with a factory-applied **protective film** that **must be removed immediately after install** — leaving it on ("to keep it looking new") is a documented bad habit even among professional installers; UV/thermal cycling permanently bonds parts of the film while other parts peel/bubble within a few years, becoming impossible to fully remove without replacing the sill.
3. Factory-applied **three-layer sound/vibration-damping backing pad** (adhesive + damping + sound-insulation layers) — without it, rain/wind-induced sill flex generates real noise; a hand-applied pad tends to delay/peel.

## Sound-Insulation Check (Drip Cap)

Tap/knock the exterior drip cap — should sound dull/deadened if properly sound-dampened; a ringing sound means no dampening material was used and rain noise will be audible.
