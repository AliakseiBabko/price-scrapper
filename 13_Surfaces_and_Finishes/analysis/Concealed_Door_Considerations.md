# Doors & Trim — Concealed-Door Considerations

Concealed doors read as part of the wall rather than a framed object — that's the appeal, and also the source of every drawback below. Two independent renovation companies (Avalremont, Forcemontage) and one retailer/comparison channel (Ontario) converge on a consistent picture. Part of [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Doors_and_Trim|Doors & Trim]].

## The Real Costs

**Wall-prep precondition**: unlike a classic door (whose casing hides minor wall unevenness), a concealed door reveals every deviation in the adjacent wall surface. Installing one in an otherwise economy-tier renovation is described as a mismatch ("sport wheels on a car with a broken suspension") — the added wall prep required is itself an extra, often unbudgeted, cost.

**Fabrication-tolerance risk**: concealed doors have essentially no tolerance for fabrication/registration errors — a leaf/frame strike-plate size mismatch is invisible until final installation. One documented real example required ~1.5 months of rework to correct.

**Schedule impact**: concealed door + shadow-gap ("теневой") baseboard combination adds **1.5–2 months** to a renovation's schedule overall — largely because the matching shadow-gap baseboard alone requires up to **14 distinct installation operations**, versus a simple nailed classic baseboard. The fabrication-mismatch defect above added a further ~1.5 months of rework on top of that.

**Shadow-gap baseboard dependency — a structural dependency, not a style-matching preference**: a concealed door is dimensioned assuming its matching shadow-gap baseboard specifically — substituting a plain/classic baseboard causes the leaf to strike or bind against it on opening. A designer who draws a concealed door is implicitly also committing the project to the shadow-gap baseboard's own cost and installation burden.

**Installation logistics/sequencing**: flooring must be scribed/fitted around the pre-installed door assembly — the reverse of the normal classic-door sequence, where flooring goes down first and the door is trimmed to it. Frame boxes need active protection for the renovation's full remaining duration (often ~1 year) since they're installed at rough-in stage. Leaves sometimes arrive from the manufacturer unfinished (needing on-site painting/filing). A full apartment's leaves delivered all at once creates a real on-site storage problem — best practice given is to have the manufacturer hold leaves in their own warehouse until final hang-stage.

**Settling-crack risk**: a concealed door can develop a visible crack at the wall-junction seam months or years after installation, from ordinary building settling (worse in new-construction) — cosmetic/structural-motion, not a manufacturing defect, but alarming to a client expecting a permanently flawless flush surface.

**Daily-wear visibility**: because a concealed door reads as part of the wall rather than a separate framed object, fingerprints, scuffs, and impacts are more visible than on a classic door (whose casing visually "contains" such marks) — worse with dark paint, kids, or pets. **Independently demonstrated on camera by two sources**: both dark and light painted concealed-door surfaces show a lasting fingerprint mark from a single bare touch.

## A Note on the "Avoid Concealed Doors" Framing

Worth flagging lightly rather than overclaiming: the concealed-door regret/hidden-cost content above is real and comes from businesses with real jobsite experience — but those same businesses also sell the alternative (classic doors), so the "avoid concealed doors" framing itself carries a mild structural interest, the same way a competing retailer's security-spec claims do in [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/analysis/Entrance_Doors|Entrance Doors]]. This doesn't discredit the specific mechanisms described above (wall-prep prerequisite, fabrication tolerance, schedule impact are all concrete and checkable, not vague warnings) — but it's a reason to read the framing as "concealed doors have real, documented costs that are often undersold at the design stage," not as "concealed doors are a mistake," which is a stronger claim than the evidence supports.
