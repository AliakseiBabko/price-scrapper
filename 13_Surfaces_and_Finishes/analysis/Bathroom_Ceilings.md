## Bathroom-Specific Ceiling Comparison: Slatted, Cassette, Glass/Mirror, Plastic, Stretch (Петришин-Строй + design blogger Ekaterina Popova, added 2026-08-24, Round 9)

A dual-host Q&A (builder Sergey Petrishin + a named design blogger)
answering real client measurement-visit questions specifically about
bathroom ceilings — complements this page's existing general-room
Round 3 comparison (painted/drywall/stretch) with three options not
covered there:

- **Slatted/rail ceiling (реечный)**: judge quality by metal thickness,
  coating, and brand — Албес/Bart is budget tier (thinner aluminum,
  dents on install); Cesal ("French design," made in Russia) is mid
  tier (thicker, laminated, wider color range); an "America"-branded
  line is premium. **Real 1.70×1.70m bathroom material cost, fully
  fitted**: ≈6,000-9,000 RUB (≈$90-$140) depending on tier/
  configuration, trailing-6-month rate ending 2018-11-21. **Common
  buyer mistake**: substituting a visually-similar hardware-store rail
  for the named brand to save money — the substitute often rusts within
  ~6 months and feels flimsy at install. **Drop needed**: ≈3-4cm with
  only surface-mounted fixtures, ≈7-10cm with recessed spot lighting
  (fixture-housing legs need the extra room). Backing blocks are still
  needed behind the ceiling for any real chandelier/shade fixture.
  DIY-feasible with only metal shears, a hammer drill, and a
  screwdriver.
- **Cassette ceiling (French/Armstrong-style)**: same underlying
  construction as slatted but on a triangular-rail grid that must be
  exactly 30cm to match standard 30×30cm cassettes — cut cassettes at
  the edges should be arranged symmetrically from the center, the same
  underlying principle as this vault's tile centered-layout rule
  applied to a ceiling grid. Both slatted and cassette systems
  disassemble non-destructively for post-leak drying and reassembly —
  a real serviceability advantage neither stretch nor drywall ceilings
  have.
- **Glass/mirror (Armstrong-frame) ceiling**: a grid-frame system (rail
  lengths of 30/60/120cm) with glass panels set into it. Mirror finish
  visually enlarges the room; backlit sandblasted glass gives a
  "glowing sky" effect; printed/stained-glass patterns need designer
  coordination to avoid clashing with a patterned tile choice in the
  same room. **Price point: from ≈3,000 RUB/m² (≈$50/m²)**, explicitly
  not the cheapest tier.
- **Plastic-panel ceiling**: cheapest option; ⚠️ **cheap-grade plastic
  exposed to prolonged hot-water steam can degrade and off-gas an odor
  over time** — quality depends heavily on the specific plastic
  grade/manufacturer. Can be stapled to a wood-batten frame (same drop
  logic as slatted) or glued directly to an existing ceiling if no
  drop/lighting change is needed, and can be shaped to conceal an
  existing ventilation duct.
- **Matte beats glossy for hiding condensation** on any of the glass/
  plastic/mirror options — a real practical (not just aesthetic) reason
  to default to matte in a bathroom.
- **⚠️ Stretch-ceiling mold/leak-resistance clarified**: a stretch
  ceiling's tight perimeter seal genuinely resists ordinary ambient
  humidity, but **it is leak-resistant, not leak-proof** — a real leak
  volume from a neighbor above will still escape through any
  penetration (a recessed-light cutout, or the perimeter itself under
  enough water). Mold risk exists behind *any* ceiling type if a real
  leak persists; the ceiling material only changes how fast the problem
  becomes visible (plastered/painted shows staining sooner; stretch
  hides it longer). **Yellowing** happens over a ~10-year+ service life
  and specifically around a light cutout **if halogen bulbs are used**
  (they run hot) — a competent installer avoids halogen with a stretch
  ceiling; LED eliminates the risk. `single-account` 3-year personal
  no-yellowing/no-mold account from the builder's own bathroom.
- **Design guidance (Ekaterina Popova)**: default to plain white matte/
  semi-gloss slatted or cassette finishes with no decorative inserts or
  gold/silver trim — ornamentation reads as a budget-solution signal; a
  plain white matte slatted ceiling is visually unremarkable rather than
  announcing its own material. A wood-look printed slat finish works
  specifically because ceiling height makes printed film hard to
  distinguish from real wood. **A curtain-track niche (see the concealed
  curtain-track note above) must be flagged to the installer/measurer
  before the initial measurement visit**, since the stretch-film needs
  special shaping for it.
- **Joint verdict**: slatted ceiling for a tight budget/DIY project
  (cheap, minimal skill, but visually dated); stretch ceiling as the
  more modern step-up if budget allows; a drywall ceiling with LED strip
  lighting for a large, high-ceilinged country-house bathroom, worth the
  extra labor cost.
