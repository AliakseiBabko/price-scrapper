# Windows — Quality Factors & Buying Guidance

Part of [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Windows|Windows]].

## Profile Brand Is Not the Real Quality Differentiator

attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is single-account and does not expose a decisive `channel:` field: **Central claim**: PVC window profile brand/model has essentially no effect on window quality within the same market class — a profile is just an extruded plastic shape, and even an experienced practitioner states he can't visually distinguish one brand from another once stickers are removed. `single-account`, delivered with confident, categorical language — not benchmarked against an independent materials-testing source.

**Supply-chain explanation for why the brand myth persists**: profile manufacturer (extrudes the raw profile) → processor/fabricator (buys 6m profile bars, produces finished window units) → dealer (measures, orders, installs — doesn't manufacture) → sometimes a factory's own retail arm sells direct. In the market's early days profiles genuinely differed and lacked common standards, which is when brand-differentiation marketing took hold; the claim is that the technical gap has long since closed as standards matured, but the marketing habit persisted past the point it reflected a real difference.

attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is single-account and does not expose a decisive `channel:` field: **Replacement framework: classify by market tier (economy/standard/premium), not by brand.** Real quality differences exist *between* tiers, not *within* a tier across different brand names.

## Five Real Quality Factors, in Stated Order of Importance

1. **Sealing gaskets** — must stay soft/elastic for decades of service, not just when new.
2. **Hardware** — the mechanism letting one handle turn engage 5-6 locking points simultaneously; must withstand a large number of open/close cycles and the full weight of a glazed sash.
3. **Factory assembly quality** — a good window is assembled at a proper factory on dedicated equipment, "not in a garage."
4. **Glazing-unit (стеклопакет) quality** — even excellent profile/hardware/gaskets won't prevent frosting/condensation if the glazing unit itself is poor.
5. **Installation quality — the single most important factor of all.** Correct installation by skilled hands with good tools is what everything above depends on: without it, even the best window from the best profile starts frosting, sagging out of alignment, or losing its seal within roughly **six months** — this "roughly six months" figure is illustrative/rhetorical, not a benchmarked statistic, `single-account`.

## Buying Caution: Bundle the Window and Its Slope Finishing

**Many companies install only the window unit and decline slope finishing, leaving the client to DIY it or hire a second firm** — and if a problem later surfaces with the window itself, the original installer can (and reportedly does) deny warranty claims by blaming improperly-executed third-party slope work.

attribution: unconfirmed — the cited source is not channel-isolated: **Recommendation: buy windows and slope finishing as one bundled product from one company offering one combined warranty**, not as separately-sourced work. See [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/analysis/Windows_Slope_Finishing|Slope Finishing]] for why slope quality matters as much as the window unit itself.
