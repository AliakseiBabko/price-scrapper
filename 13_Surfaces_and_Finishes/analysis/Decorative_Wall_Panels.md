# Decorative Wall Panels

## What decorative panels are and why they're used

Per Konstantin Kruglov/Ontario: decorative panels ("декоративные стеновые
панели") are prefabricated modules or elements mounted onto a wall to create a
decorative effect. They hide substrate unevenness, add volume/character/style,
create an accent zone (e.g. behind a bed, a TV zone, or an entryway), and
protect walls from mechanical damage (chairs, bags, clothing) in high-traffic
areas. [source: [[_Sources/YT_qzi1LqwsP5k_kruglov_decorative_wall_panels|YT_qzi1LqwsP5k]]]

## Classification axes (beyond material)

Per Kruglov/Ontario, panels vary along several independent dimensions besides
material:

- **Texture**: smooth; relief/textured ("рельефные" — wavy, cube-shaped, and
  slat/rail types all count here); natural-texture imitation (wood, marble,
  other stone); soft/upholstered.
- **Rigidity**: rigid vs. flexible. Flexible panels can follow a wall's
  curvature (semicircular walls, transitions, wavy elements) — either a truly
  flexible base material, or an MDF-core panel faced with real veneer
  ("шпон") where the MDF core is scored/relieved so the veneer wraps the
  angle cleanly.
- **Mounting method**: metal brackets/special fixings (typically for heavy
  panels); glue or "liquid nails" (жидкие гвозди), sometimes reinforced with
  studs/pins ("шпильки"); or self-tapping screws/dowel-nails.
- **Joint style**: butt joint with no visible seam; butt joint with a
  deliberately visible seam; or a gap left between panels, which can be
  painted, finished with a spline/fillet ("шпонка"), or left unfinished (e.g.
  acoustic panels, where the visible gap exposes the acoustic-felt backing).

## Material comparison

Per Kruglov/Ontario — a buyer's-guide-style comparison of the materials this
source's company has actually encountered on real jobs or estimates:

| Material | Moisture resistance | Weight/fragility | Substrate prep | Best for | Avoid in |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **MDF / LDF** (medium-/low-density fiberboard, face laminated/veneered/acrylic/film/enamel) | Moderate | Durable, easy to handle | Minimal | Most popular default — huge décor range, easy install | — |
| **Polyurethane / thermoplastic polymer ("тюрополимер") / polystyrene** | Near-moisture-proof | Very lightweight, strong | Minimal | Moldings, 3D wall constructs; paints well, factory-pre-painted options exist | — |
| **Gypsum (plaster)** | Poor — afraid of moisture | Heavy and fragile, needs skilled installer | Perfectly flat substrate required | Maximum surface detail, takes paint excellently | Humid/wet rooms |
| **Plastic / PVC** | Maximally moisture-resistant | Weak visually, not durable | Low | Bathrooms, toilets, loggias, other high-humidity spaces | Where appearance matters most |
| **Wood + veneer** (plywood/solid-wood base, real veneer face) | Poor — needs stable ambient humidity/temperature | Warps ("plays") most with humidity swings, either direction | High | Natural/premium look | **Least suitable material for bathrooms** |
| **Textile / acoustic** (soundproofing layer + soft upholstery or wood slats) | None (fabric variants) | — | Very flat substrate required, for even soundproofing-layer contact | Bedroom, home office, TV zone (versatile) | Bathrooms, loggias |
| **Cork** (agglomerated cork, cork veneer, natural wax/lacquer) | Reasonably stable in humid rooms | Soft material | Ideally flat substrate required | Adds extra sound insulation | — |
| **LDSP / laminated chipboard** | Can be made moisture-resistant even with a 2mm edge band, but shares MDF's core weakness (moisture penetrates fast once the lamination layer is damaged) | — | — | Maximum-budget option; only smooth panels achievable (texture must come from the LDSP sheet's own surface) | Kruglov's own recommendation: MDF is meaningfully more stable — treat LDSP as the budget fallback, not the default |
| **Bamboo** | Unclear — too new to have a track record | — | — | Newly popularizing, imitates wood/metal/leather/marble/fabric facings; not yet widely available even in Moscow | Durability claims not yet trustworthy — compare to how quartz-vinyl flooring entered this market before establishing a track record |
| **Concrete / cement-composite** | Unclear — rarely used | — | — | Exclusive/expensive niche option | Performance characteristics still not well understood in practice |

## Selection rules

Per Kruglov/Ontario, five stated tips plus one closing general-advice point:

1. **Decide the panel's function first**: pure accent wall → any material the
   client likes; impact/mechanical protection in a hallway → MDF, preferably
   with a dense decorative face layer; a budget option that still projects
   durability/security → economy LDSP panel.
2. **In humid zones, avoid wood, gypsum, and other moisture-sensitive
   materials** — Kruglov's actual best picks are plastic or cork, though he
   notes that "given Russian consumer taste," MDF still tends to end up used
   there in practice anyway (a tier-steering-adjacent admission worth noting,
   not a neutral technical recommendation).
3. **Account for humidity and temperature swings** — e.g. an unheated loggia
   installation: avoid anything sensitive to either moisture or temperature.
   **PVC is named as the only fully suitable option** for that specific case.
4. **Match color/texture to the interior's overall style**: veneer/veneered
   panels for modern-style interiors; gypsum or polyurethane/thermoplastic/
   polystyrene for classical-style interiors, especially where the panel is
   meant to be painted.
5. **Plan decorative lighting for accent walls deliberately** — linear/
   directional fixtures, or backlit false-wall perimeters — since 3D-textured
   panels won't deliver their intended "wow effect" under the wrong lighting.
6. **Don't cut costs on installation labor for complex decorative-panel
   work** — installation skill matters as much as material choice, especially
   for heavier/fragile materials (gypsum) and precise flexible-panel curve
   work.

## Installation Technique — A Real Worked Case (Polyurethane/Durapolymer Panels)

TimRemont, own master-bedroom renovation project (region not stated at
level 1): real installation sequence and two named mistakes for
durapolymer/polyurethane-type decorative panels (a subtype of this page's
existing "Polyurethane / thermoplastic polymer" material row above).

- **⚠️ Buy the longest available panel length to minimize seams**: this
  project bought 2m-length panels when 3m panels were available for the
  same product — the extra seams created required repeated putty/sand
  cycles to blend into the panel's own surface texture, costing far more
  finishing time than planned.
- **Horizontal seams are acceptable if unavoidable; vertical seams between
  panels are explicitly not recommended** — much harder to finish cleanly.
- **Installation sequence**: laser level for placement accuracy + a
  construction adhesive ("жидкие гвозди") to mount the panel, a separate
  specialized panel-joint adhesive to bond panel-to-panel seams, then
  repeated putty + sand cycles at every seam until the joint's texture
  matches the surrounding panel surface, before final painting. The same
  matching baseboard (durapolymer) was painted *before* installation, then
  joint-glued and puttied the same way.
