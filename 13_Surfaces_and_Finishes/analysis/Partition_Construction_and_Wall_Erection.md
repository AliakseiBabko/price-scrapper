## Gas-Concrete-Block Wall Material Calculation — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-10)

> [!NOTE]
> New topic angle for this page. Two Zemskov/Zemstandart sources, `single-account` throughout — the first his own stated working figures (filmed on an atypical single-wall-thickness clinic build-out); the second a real filmed destructive-test comparison featuring a guest technologist affiliated with a block manufacturer (Bonolit) — that guest's endorsement of his own company's product category is flagged as commercially-affiliated, not neutral. [sources: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_gas_block_wall_material_calculation_2e558d93.txt`, `_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_block_adhesive_destructive_comparison_18922877.txt`]

- **Zemskov's block-quantity formula**: 7 blocks per m² of wall, regardless of block thickness — sum wall areas from a **per-wall labeled-area project sheet**, not a single lump total (which, per Zemskov, only supports a plastering-area calculation and no other material quantity, since a real apartment mixes wall thicknesses — 100/150/200mm — and finish materials by wall). **Never buy the full calculated quantity up front** — order roughly half first, since some second purchase is unavoidable once door openings and on-site cutting are accounted for.
- **Zemskov's adhesive-quantity formula**: one 25kg bag of block adhesive covers 1m³ of masonry, **regardless of block thickness** (thinner blocks need more units but proportionally thinner adhesive layers, keeping volume-based consumption constant) — apply a real-world **+20-25% correction** over the manufacturer's nominal figure. The **first course is always laid on ordinary cement-sand mortar, never block adhesive** (a thin-joint system that can't level an uneven raw floor) — typical layer 1.5-3cm.
- **Only 2 of 6 commonly-used block-bonding materials are actually correct, per a real destructive-test comparison with a guest technologist**: **cement-sand mix** — first course only, ~5× the material use of specialized adhesive beyond that, poor plasticity, slow strength development; **Rotband/gypsum plaster** — technically bonds but needlessly expensive at its required 5mm minimum thickness, and risks a chemistry clash if later finished with cement-based plaster in a humid room; **tile adhesive** — bonds fine, but carries a real ~50% cost premium for tile-specific additives blocks don't need (negligible on one wall, tens of thousands of RUB at full-project scale); **specialized mineral block adhesive — the correct default**, rated -10°C to +35°C (one product for the full seasonal range), achieves joints as thin as ~0.5mm; **specialized polyurethane "foam-glue" — this source's top recommendation**: no water/electricity needed on site, ~3× faster installation, reaches initial strength in 30 minutes and full strength in 24 hours (vs. ~3 days/28 days for any mixed mortar) — caveat: the first course must be leveled with extreme precision, since foam-glue can't correct subsequent-row alignment the way a workable mortar bed can; **ordinary construction/mounting foam — explicitly, categorically wrong** despite looking similar to the specialized foam-glue — its real secondary expansion pushes blocks out of alignment, requiring significant extra plastering to correct.
- **Zemskov's meta-point on why break-testing (as performed in the comparison video) doesn't predict real wall performance**: a finished wall is never subjected to the pulling/twisting force used in such a test, and every candidate material's true strength only develops after ~28 days regardless. **The three criteria that actually matter for choosing a bonding material, per Zemskov**: speed of construction, cost (avoiding capability the application doesn't need), and whether the next course can be built without waiting for full design strength.

## Partition Layout & Masonry Technique — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-17)

> [!NOTE]
> `single-account`, earliest-dated (2018-12-14) technique video from this channel processed into this store so far — despite a "how not to" title, a positive step-by-step demonstration, not a critique. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260817_zems_foam_block_partition_masonry_e2150b29.txt`]

- **Laser-projection room layout**: mark the room's four corner points on the floor, place a laser leveling/projection device at each in turn, and it projects the room's exact footprint onto the ceiling plus vertical wall reference lines — snap a chalk line along the projection. Full layout: **~3 minutes**.
- **Metal-profile alignment-frame technique**: install a temporary guide-track profile along the chalk-line layout before laying blocks, then simply butt each course against the profile (checking only mid-span against a taut string) instead of re-measuring every course. **~12 minutes to set up, claimed to save ~3 hours** of masonry time. `unverified` savings figure.
- **Mandatory pre-masonry prep, explicitly flagged as commonly (and wrongly) skipped**: vacuum the base surface, then apply deep-penetrating bonding primer via garden sprayer — skipping this is stated to guarantee eventual course loosening/detachment.
- **Adhesive consistency check**: thick enough that a mixer paddle doesn't sink under its own weight, fluid enough that excess drips freely off it; rest the mix 5 minutes and re-mix once before use.
- **Course-staggering rule**: running bond throughout; every even course starts with a half-block to keep the offset (avoids stacking vertical joints).
- **Top-joint (wall-to-ceiling) technique for soundproofing/strength**: do not try to fully pack the top gap with block adhesive via trowel — a ~5mm ungapped strip reliably remains regardless of care, and delamination reappears over time even if forced full. Instead **wait the full 48-hour adhesive cure**, then fill the top joint with professional polyurethane foam as **two separate continuous beads, one per wall face**, forming an unbroken perimeter seal — even a small gap here is flagged as a real soundproofing failure point. Applying foam before the 48-hour cure risks the foam's expansion pressure deforming the still-curing masonry. Contrasts with ordinary crews' backer-rod-and-plaster top joint, described as materially worse for both soundproofing and mechanical strength.
- **Door-opening worked example**: 2.07m opening height (corroborates this vault's existing 2.07m standard — see the zero-reference/working-reference note below for where this figure itself comes from), 78cm width for a 70cm-leaf door, 21cm corner offset on 10cm-thick blocks leaving an 11cm return so door casing seats without trimming (≥3cm clearance from the corner). Lintel: two rebar rods overlapping the opening ~10cm per side, notched into the top course, wedged, finally set during plastering — corroborates this vault's existing rebar-for-narrow-openings guidance with a specific worked figure.
- **Why 2.07m, not an arbitrary figure**: derived from a standard door leaf's finished-floor mounting clearance (2.05m) plus a typical 2cm finish build-up above the rough screed (1cm self-leveling compound + 1cm finish flooring) — 2.05m + 2cm = 2.07m measured from the rough screed. This derivation, plus the full **zero-reference / working-reference** measurement system underlying it (corridor-finished-floor = zero; a virtual plane exactly 1000mm above zero used for nearly all practical layout), is detailed in [[_Sources/YT_VHfUDBr0p4Q_door_height_zero_reference_109|this source note]] (added 2026-08-19) — see also `12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Mounting_Heights_and_Positioning.md`, which the same reference system underlies.
- **Opening cut-out sequencing**: rather than pre-cutting block to the opening shape, lay full block straight across the future door/window opening during construction, then cut the opening out afterward in one pass with a reciprocating saw, guided by a laser-projected vertical plane along the marked line — simpler than piecing block around a pre-marked void. [source: [[_Sources/YT_g7Cuj1p-0CA_masonry_first_course_leveling_231|g7Cuj1p-0CA_masonry_firs]]]

## Foam-Glue Block Masonry & First-Course Leveling — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-18)

> [!NOTE]
> Two companion sources — a foam-glue bonding technique + client-facing QC checklist, and a detailed prequel on leveling the critical first course that the fast foam-glue method depends on. `single-account`, company-branded. [sources: [[_Sources/YT_Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue_foam_technique_260|note]], [[_Sources/YT_g7Cuj1p-0CA_masonry_first_course_leveling_231|note]]]

- **First course is always laid on cement-sand mortar, never foam-glue, even in a foam-glue-bonded wall** — raw floor slabs are never perfectly level, and foam-glue bonds fast but doesn't correct leveling errors; all leveling work must happen at the first course so everything above it can be laid quickly with no further leveling. [source: [[_Sources/YT_g7Cuj1p-0CA_masonry_first_course_leveling_231|g7Cuj1p-0CA_masonry_firs]]]
- **Survey before deciding whether beacon-based leveling is needed at all**: a small measured spread (~5mm) across a run needs no beacons, just a uniform mortar layer; a larger spread (~3cm) needs full beacon leveling. Don't default straight to beacons without checking first. [source: [[_Sources/YT_g7Cuj1p-0CA_masonry_first_course_leveling_231|g7Cuj1p-0CA_masonry_firs]]]
- **Beacon-leveling method**: find the run's highest point via laser survey, set the first beacon there as the reference/"zero," then match every other beacon's laser-to-top distance to it using a rigid straightedge (not a tape measure, which introduces error). Use a rigid beacon type, never cheap flexible foil beacons. [source: [[_Sources/YT_g7Cuj1p-0CA_masonry_first_course_leveling_231|g7Cuj1p-0CA_masonry_firs]]]
- **Sequencing rule: always start block-laying from an existing fixed wall toward a free corner, never the reverse** — a shortfall/excess at the unconstrained far end is trivially fixed later (plaster if short, saw-trim if long); starting from a free corner risks an awkward sliver cut. [source: [[_Sources/YT_Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue_foam_technique_260|Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue]]]
- **Never pre-cut the first course around planned door/window openings** — lay it continuous, cut openings afterward with a reciprocating saw once the row is set. [source: [[_Sources/YT_Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue_foam_technique_260|Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue]]]
- **Client-facing wall-flatness acceptance threshold, explicit numbers**: gaps up to 5-7mm against a straightedge are acceptable (plaster-correctable); **gaps of 15mm or more should never be accepted — insist on a rebuild.** The raw first-course platform itself, before any further courses are laid, should be noticeably tighter (a couple mm or less). [source: [[_Sources/YT_Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue_foam_technique_260|Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue]]]
- **Never sledgehammer-test a finished wall's strength** — judge qualitatively instead: straight, no cracks, visible foam-glue joints between blocks. [source: [[_Sources/YT_Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue_foam_technique_260|Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue]]]
- **Wall-tie/hanger spacing to an adjoining structural wall: every second course, using flexible ties, never rigid angle brackets** — flexible ties absorb minor building settlement without cracking the partition; rigid brackets don't. [source: [[_Sources/YT_Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue_foam_technique_260|Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue]]]
- **Top-of-wall-to-ceiling gap treatment**: ≤2.5cm, foam directly with standard mounting foam (explicitly distinct from block-glue-foam — don't confuse the two products); larger gaps get blocks cut down first, then foamed. [source: [[_Sources/YT_Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue_foam_technique_260|Qs_FxXdsq40_masonry_glue]]]

## Radiator-Niche Insulation Mechanism — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-18)

> [!NOTE]
> `single-account`, now independently corroborated by a second video from the same channel. [sources: [[_Sources/YT_scOLgA4HPqM_7_key_lifehacks_099|note 1]], [[_Sources/YT_zPR8PGWq5lA_3yr_diy_deviations_case_229|note 2]] (2026-08-19, matches the mechanism below almost exactly)]

- **Never add extra insulation or a heat-reflective panel behind a radiator niche recessed into an exterior wall**, and don't thin the wall further there. **Mechanism**: for a uniform wall of any thickness, the interior/exterior temperature gradient equalizes roughly at the wall's midpoint; insulating or reflecting behind the niche blocks the room's own warm air from reaching and warming that thinned section from the inside — the opposite of the intuitive "protect the thin spot" fix, and it makes that spot colder / condensation-prone instead. A wall thinned ~15cm by a radiator niche still stays warmer overall than most modern panel-building exterior walls without any special treatment — just plaster and drywall-clad it normally. [source: [[_Sources/YT_jrqEbkU4Wj8_developer_worse_than_designer_257|jrqEbkU4Wj8_developer_wo]]]

## Load-Bearing Wall Opening: Cutting & Reinforcement Technique — per Zemsproekt/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-17)

> [!NOTE]
> `single-account`, worked example from this project's own approved engineering spec — reinforcement dimensions are project-specific, but the overall method is general. Real cost comparison: specialized-crew quote >50,000 RUB vs. ~15,000 RUB DIY materials (2019 Moscow pricing, `unverified`). [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260817_load_bearing_wall_opening_technique_017_e28c89d6.txt`]

**USD normalization for the dated load-bearing-opening cost comparison:** ~15,000 RUB DIY materials is ≈$230 and >50,000 RUB specialized-crew work is >$760 using the metadata-confirmed 2019-01-05 source date and trailing 6-month USD/RUB average of 66.08. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260817_load_bearing_wall_opening_technique_017_e28c89d6.txt`]

- **Get replanning approval before cutting, never after** — some openings are structurally prohibited regardless of reinforcement (approval reveals a hard no before work starts), and the approved plan specifies exact reference points and required steel-angle specs, making the later inspection far more likely to pass cleanly. [source: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reason]]]
- **Marking method for a thick wall**: mark reference points on one face, then transfer to the opposite face by drilling through at several points and connecting the resulting holes — don't trust two independently re-measured faces to align. [source: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reason]]]
- **Never use a jackhammer/breaker or any high-impact tool on a load-bearing wall** — impact risks damaging the wall beyond the intended opening. Correct method: drill a dense perimeter of holes (~3-4cm spacing, 10mm diameter) around the outline, then connect them (a wall-saw professionally, or a large angle grinder with a diamond blade + a second person running a construction vacuum at the cut point, swapping roles periodically). [source: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reason]]]
- **Controlled demolition, floor-protection rule**: never remove the freed section as one large piece — divide it into ~10-12 pieces (~40×40cm each) and remove individually. Lay a wood-plank deck on the floor first — a large falling chunk can damage the slab or, in a multi-story building, the ceiling of the unit below. [source: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reason]]]
- **Reinforcement anchoring**: don't drill straight through the angle iron's face into the wall to anchor it — the wall's own rebar sits at a shallow, predictable depth and a through-drilled anchor risks hitting it. Drill anchor pins at an offset angle instead, then weld the angle iron to the pins. [source: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reason]]]
- **Weld-completeness rule**: weld connecting cross-plates and the framing angle irons along both their flat contact face *and* their end/edge faces, not just the face-to-face seam — a commonly missed detail. [source: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reason]]]
- **Rebar-dowel bending technique**: instead of cold-bending a pre-cut rebar tail against the frame (produces uneven bends/gaps), drill the anchor hole at 45° to the wall plane, insert straight rebar, then weld/heat only the segment near the anchor point — localized heat lets that segment bend cleanly by hand at exactly the needed point, then trim to length and complete the weld. [source: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reason]]]
- **Finish**: grind weld seams smooth, paint the full steel assembly for corrosion protection, then fill the gap between frame and masonry with M200/M300 cement-sand mix before the inspection commission's acceptance visit. [source: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reason]]]

## Future-Flexible Planning & Regulatory Workarounds — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-19, Category 5 further-pool chunk 4)

> [!NOTE]
> Three sources from the final chunk of the Category 5 further-pool batch. [sources: [[_Sources/YT_2E7YVK6PhIM_638k_thanks_designer_241|note 1]], [[_Sources/YT_e1HRByaCFv0_420k_designer_disaster_239|note 2]], [[_Sources/YT_M4dxo-6kcDs_540k_thanks_designer_242|note 3]]]

- **⚠️ A glass sliding partition can satisfy a gas-kitchen code requirement (a door must separate a gas kitchen from an adjoining room) while remaining functionally almost fully open.** A fully open-plan gas kitchen-living layout is technically non-compliant; a glass sliding partition technically satisfies "a door exists" for inspection while providing a highly transparent, minimally-obstructive opening in daily use. Distinct from this vault's existing general caution against sliding/swinging glass partitions (falling-glass risk) — here that tradeoff is deliberately accepted as the only way to satisfy both the client's open-plan wish and the code constraint. [source: [[_Sources/YT_2E7YVK6PhIM_638k_thanks_designer_241|2E7YVK6PhIM_638k_thanks_]]]
- **⚠️ A currently-open floor plan can be pre-engineered for a future partition wall or future electrical need — pre-installed anchor points let a later life-stage change (children arriving, a bed needing to become a double) be executed cheaply without demolishing or redoing surrounding finishes.** Two worked examples: (1) a structural box with pre-installed sliding-track mounting points, built now at the future wall's intended location, so a wall can be added later by attaching drywall-frame profiles to the same anchor points; (2) a hidden, capped electrical point run behind wallpaper now, at a location a furniture layout (a single-to-double-bed swap) will need only after a third child arrives. **General rule: when a client's near-term needs favor one layout but a foreseeable future need will require a different one, engineer the eventual change's anchor points (structural, electrical) into the current build now, concealed behind removable/replaceable finish.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_2E7YVK6PhIM_638k_thanks_designer_241|2E7YVK6PhIM_638k_thanks_]]] [source: [[_Sources/YT_e1HRByaCFv0_420k_designer_disaster_239|e1HRByaCFv0_420k_designe]]]
- **"Truncated closets" (усечённые шкафы) — a named, deliberate design pattern**: a closet built to look like a full-depth unit from outside, where only part of its width has genuinely usable depth. **The shallow/unusable section should house wall-mounted utility equipment (an electrical panel, a water-supply manifold), never clothing/general storage** — reserve genuinely deep sections for real storage. [source: [[_Sources/YT_M4dxo-6kcDs_540k_thanks_designer_242|M4dxo-6kcDs_540k_thanks_]]]
- **A single enclosed/glazed loggia can be split into two separate functional zones (e.g. two home offices, each with its own storage) by building a partition wall within it.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_M4dxo-6kcDs_540k_thanks_designer_242|M4dxo-6kcDs_540k_thanks_]]]
- **A furniture-maker's fabrication tolerance for a tapering/wedge-shaped corner cabinet section: at least 4-5cm of material at the narrowest point, never a true zero-width taper.** Reference the guide line from the unit's *depth* dimension to guarantee this, not its width. [source: [[_Sources/YT_M4dxo-6kcDs_540k_thanks_designer_242|M4dxo-6kcDs_540k_thanks_]]]
- **An enclosed kitchen's doorway zone needs its full door-swing clearance counted separately from the cabinet-run wall allowance** — even though a kitchen cabinet run itself needs only 600mm depth + 100mm reveal, an enclosed kitchen's doorway zone needs 900mm total, the extra 200mm being clearance for the door leaf itself to open without striking the cabinet run. [source: [[_Sources/YT_M4dxo-6kcDs_540k_thanks_designer_242|M4dxo-6kcDs_540k_thanks_]]]
- **Before allowing a designer to proceed to rendered visuals, require a stated justification for every wall thickness, opening reveal, and irregular corner** — a designer who can't explain a specific dimension, or treats the question as a trust issue, is a red flag regardless of how polished the eventual renders look. [source: [[_Sources/YT_M4dxo-6kcDs_540k_thanks_designer_242|M4dxo-6kcDs_540k_thanks_]]]
- **⚠️ A fourth documented instance of this technique family**: pre-install reinforced wall backing (закладные) for a wall-mounted Swedish/gymnastic ladder *together with* hidden electrical for a TV zone at the same wall location — lets the client choose (or later swap) between the two without redoing the wall. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_u3UuZN9LHg0_developer_gandon_irregular_geometry_268|YT_u3UuZN9LHg0]]]

## Ventilation-Shaft Debris/Fine Mechanism & Cross-Traffic Elimination (added 2026-08-19, remainder-pool Round 2)

**⚠️ Never chase (штробить) into a true shared ventilation shaft, even a thin section — debris can fall down the shaft into units below, and the responsible party is fined.** Distinct from a decorative duct casing (короб), which can be freely modified — reinforces this vault's existing zashivka-vs-venshakhta distinction with the underlying safety/legal mechanism spelled out explicitly. **⚠️ Cross-traffic-through-a-seating-zone elimination**: when a door opening's position forces foot traffic directly in front of a TV-viewing seating area, relocate the *opening itself* (not just the furniture) to route traffic around the seating zone entirely — a problem often treated as unsolvable but straightforward once the opening, not just the furniture, is treated as the variable to move. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_u3UuZN9LHg0_developer_gandon_irregular_geometry_268|YT_u3UuZN9LHg0]]]

## Layout Marking & Hard/Flexible-Dimension Erection Methodology — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-19)

> [!NOTE]
> Two independent sources on two different real projects (a kitchen renovation, and a commercial dental-clinic fit-out) corroborating the same planning principle. Complements, doesn't replace, the laser-projection+chalk-line layout method above — this is a different marking method plus a planning methodology, not a refinement of the same technique. [sources: [[_Sources/YT_Eq0kg2hD-Ws_wall_layout_tape_marking_224|note 1]], [[_Sources/YT_sJ8UZj36TMQ_wall_erection_step_sequence_114|note 2]]]

- **Painter's-tape first-course marking (alternative to chalk-line)**: mark each wall's footprint with two parallel strips of painter's tape rather than pencil, chalk-and-string, or spray paint. **Mechanism**: the first-course mortar bed that follows doesn't need to bond to the marking — only to the floor beneath it — so tape stays visibly outlined even once mortar is dumped on top, where pencil/chalk/paint would be illegible under it.
- **Squaring a perpendicular without laser-projecting from the corner**: place the laser unit *along* the reference wall (not at the target corner), and use its fine-adjustment screw to rotate it until beam-to-wall distance is equal at two tape-measured points along that wall. The laser's own construction guarantees its front/back beams are exactly 90° apart, so the perpendicular beam is automatically a true right angle once the first beam is parallel — no separate corner check needed.
- **Hard-vs-flexible-dimension planning, the central rule**: never try to hold every dimension on a floor plan to spec. Identify which dimensions are genuinely load-bearing for fixture fit (worked examples: an 1800mm bathtub that simply won't fit if 50mm short; a fixed door-leaf width) and lock those first, sequencing wall construction outward from a fixed reference wall; let every other ("flexible"/"rubber") room dimension absorb whatever variance naturally results from plaster thickness and small measurement error. A "nominal room sizes" summary table — present even in high-end design projects — is explicitly *not* a build reference on its own; it's for material-quantity estimation only.
- **First-course-ignores-future-openings, re-corroborated on a second, independent project**: build continuous walls straight through future doorway locations, then cut the opening out afterward with a reciprocating saw — matches this page's existing opening cut-out sequencing above, confirmed independently two years earlier on a different project type.
- **Riser-plumb-survey pre-framing QC step**: before framing a wall segment that will define one edge of a future duct/pipe-riser concealment box, survey the riser's own plumb deviation with a laser plumb-bob device to find its most-protruding point at any height along it, then reference the wall layout to *that* point, not to the riser's position at floor level. Skipping this risks the riser colliding with the concealment box higher up if it leans, forcing the whole corridor width to shrink to clear it.
- **A fully worked numeric guide-line sequence, from a Category 5 replan case study**: toilet clearance from an adjoining wall (800mm); bathtub-wall-run offsets (50mm → 1,800mm → +100mm); the 110cm doorway-opening formula re-derived a fourth time (900mm rough opening + 100mm reveal each side); built-in-closet dimensions (600mm deep × 1,400mm wide); shower-pan dimensions (1,200mm × 800mm) checked against toilet clearance before finalizing. **Sequencing rule, explicitly stated**: a false wall meant to compensate for a protruding structural element (e.g. a load-bearing corner) should be built at the specific construction stage where its exact required offset is known, not guessed earlier or left uncompensated. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_78FeaeMZHOI_2.5m_thanks_developer_275|YT_78FeaeMZHOI]]]
- **Negotiating with neighbors to shrink a shared entry vestibule (тамбур) and reclaim the freed area into one's own apartment footprint is a real, usable space-recovery technique** — demolish the vestibule-forming walls, then negotiate the reduction directly with the affected neighbors before laying out guide lines. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_G4Vm3wdbGFk_disaster_60m_271|YT_G4Vm3wdbGFk]]]
- **A sliding (купе-style) closet door mechanism consumes roughly 100mm of a closet's own depth — a checkable sizing formula**: hinged-door closet depth = the usable hanging depth needed; sliding-door closet depth = usable depth + ~100mm, to net the same clear hanging space after the slider mechanism's intrusion. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_G4Vm3wdbGFk_disaster_60m_271|YT_G4Vm3wdbGFk]]]
- **A room that must be walked through to reach two other rooms is not a usable isolated room — trace every room's access path(s) before finalizing a layout, not just its floor area.** A documented defect (a self-designed layout) created two large pass-through spaces with no alternate access route to the rooms beyond — fixed by adding one wall with two door openings. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_gtCgn0gOvlA_4.5m_no_one_to_blame_278|YT_gtCgn0gOvlA]]]

## Partition Wall-Plane Offset, Tie-In Cracking, and Thin-Partition Soundproofing — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-19)

> [!NOTE]
> `single-account`, four sources from the Category 5 dunk-format critique batch (chunk 1 of the further-pool re-derivation), corroborating each other on independent projects. [sources: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|note 1]], [[_Sources/YT_3Otf-oBcMqg_lost_30pct_apartment_298|note 2]], [[_Sources/YT_cO56TxjBifo_designer_lost_room_1.9m_291|note 3]], [[_Sources/YT_N0AvLnbDShs_lost_2_rooms_296|note 4]]]

- **A wall-plane offset (a step where two wall segments' faces don't align) has a hard ~40mm threshold for what plaster can fix.** Up to roughly 3-4cm, masonry infill and drywall framing are both too large/thin to use, but a plaster leveling coat still works. Past ~40mm, the required leveling coat becomes too thick to hold (it will detach) and, even if it doesn't, cures too slowly and delays downstream work. **Rule: past ~40mm of wall-plane offset, demolish and rebuild that wall section to the correct plane** rather than trying to level it with any surface material. [source: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reason]]]
- **A partition wall segment built without proper corner tie-in to adjoining walls/lintels (including tie-in to exterior walls, not just other partitions) will develop cracks along every un-tied joint over time, no matter how the seam is later patched or bridged.** Rule: every rebuilt or newly built partition must be tied in at every corner it meets. [source: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reason]]]
- **A pantry/storage-closet or triangular residual room with narrow bounding-wall reveals (roughly ≤45-98cm depending on the adjoining fixture) can become almost entirely unusable for storage even when the room's total floor area looks sufficient** — the deciding factor is reveal width at the opening, not raw square footage. Independently corroborated across two projects (a pantry example and a triangular-room closet example). [source: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reason]]] [source: [[_Sources/YT_cO56TxjBifo_designer_lost_room_1.9m_291|cO56TxjBifo_designer_los]]]
- **Thin partition walls are repeatedly flagged as inadequate for soundproofing at two independently observed thicknesses, with no "adequate" thickness yet established by any source processed so far: 80mm (a paz-greben/tongue-and-groove gypsum-block partition, demonstrated visibly flexing and audibly transmitting sound under hand/foot pressure, eventually cracking even under reinforcing fabric) and 100mm (flagged the same way in a separate project's design review).** Treat any partition at or below 100mm as a soundproofing risk pending a source that states a confirmed-adequate thickness. [source: [[_Sources/YT_p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reasons_demolish_developer_walls_304|p-6OI34C6bw_seven_reason]]] [source: [[_Sources/YT_3Otf-oBcMqg_lost_30pct_apartment_298|3Otf-oBcMqg_lost_30pct_a]]]
- **⚠️ 150mm guide-line spacing for a soundproofing-priority partition is now a 7-source corroborated figure within this store — the strongest candidate for "the adequate thickness" the 80mm/100mm-inadequate findings above leave open.** Not stated by any single source as an explicit general building-code standard (each is a practical choice in its own worked example, `inferred` not `confirmed`), but seven independent worked examples from the same practitioner landing on the same figure — including two videos each using it three separate times for three different room pairs (`59uS6AAxmB4`, chunk 3; `M4dxo-6kcDs`, chunk 4), and a 7th instance from a planning-technique video rather than a defect-critique video (`yt_WmkOC9uKnCQ`, "Best Kitchen-Living Room Life Hack" #154, added 2026-08-19 — this figure isn't only surfacing in the dunk-format sub-genre) — is meaningfully strong repeated-practice evidence. A companion technique: when a nearby structural column's own width would otherwise dictate a thicker wall, deliberately build to the 150mm functional minimum instead and reclaim the difference as usable floor area, rather than defaulting to match the column's width out of habit. `single-account`, `unverified`. [sources: [[_Sources/YT_78FeaeMZHOI_2.5m_thanks_developer_275|YT_78FeaeMZHOI]], [[_Sources/YT_G4Vm3wdbGFk_disaster_60m_271|YT_G4Vm3wdbGFk]], [[_Sources/YT_HLiLNZpVWpg_max_disaster_258|YT_HLiLNZpVWpg]], [[_Sources/YT_59uS6AAxmB4_developer_disaster_92m2_255|YT_59uS6AAxmB4]], [[_Sources/YT_WmkOC9uKnCQ_kitchen_living_loggia_layout_lifehack_154|YT_WmkOC9uKnCQ]]]
- **A redundant second access point onto a loggia/balcony can be converted from a door opening into a window opening** — a loggia only needs one door for access; a second, functionally redundant doorway can instead be repurposed as a window, freeing the adjoining wall for other uses (e.g. a false wall/radiator niche, a corner sofa placement). `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_F5v7eI3ry1M_435k_thanks_designer_266|YT_F5v7eI3ry1M]]]
- **A stated psychological/comfort requirement in a technical brief (e.g. a client's stated feeling that a window is already too small) should override the simplest construction option when the two conflict** — a documented case rejected the easiest false-wall plane specifically to avoid further narrowing a window the client found subjectively too small, at the cost of extra construction complexity. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_F5v7eI3ry1M_435k_thanks_designer_266|YT_F5v7eI3ry1M]]]
- **Corridor width has two distinct figures depending on use case**: 110cm is a general "ideal" baseline; a corridor an adult and a running child need to pass each other in needs 120cm, not 90cm. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_59uS6AAxmB4_developer_disaster_92m2_255|YT_59uS6AAxmB4]]]
- **A tall, narrow shelving/storage unit placed directly in a room's main walking path — rather than against a wall out of the path — takes repeated impact/wear on its exposed outer corner over time**, corroborated across two independent case studies (a kitchen-corner/pantry configuration and a corridor shelving unit).]), 4] [source: [[_Sources/YT_EnSpVCSUiqg_designer_lost_it_apartment_307|EnSpVCSUiqg_designer_los]]] [source: [[_Sources/YT_N0AvLnbDShs_lost_2_rooms_296|N0AvLnbDShs_lost_2_rooms]]]
- **Radiators can be recessed into a false (furring) wall specifically so they don't protrude into the room's usable floor area** — a standard technique for reclaiming floor space, paired with the related observation that false walls are also routinely needed to box in plumbing stacks/risers a developer left with no dedicated enclosure. [source: [[_Sources/YT_3Otf-oBcMqg_lost_30pct_apartment_298|3Otf-oBcMqg_lost_30pct_a]]]
- **A load-bearing wall can force a real fixture-shrinking compromise when a built-in mechanism needs in-wall clearance** — a documented case thickened a load-bearing wall by 12cm to fit a built-in bathtub's mechanism, which then made the tub itself noticeably shorter/less comfortable than a standard installation would have been. **Check whether a wall is load-bearing before committing to a built-in fixture that needs in-wall clearance.** [source: [[_Sources/YT_EnSpVCSUiqg_designer_lost_it_apartment_307|companion note]]]

## Wall-Plane Flush Alignment & Closet-Niche-Beside-Door Sizing — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-19, remainder-pool batch)

> [!NOTE]
> `single-account`, two short technical-design mini-masterclass videos. [sources: [[_Sources/YT_Af8nNyn9a_c_flush_wall_plane_technical_design_113|note 1]], [[_Sources/YT_o4KitYl8vpU_closet_niche_next_to_load_bearing_wall_116|note 2]]]

- **⚠️ A short wall segment out of plane with an adjacent load-bearing wall (an external+internal corner "step") should almost always be rebuilt flush, not left as-is.** Explicitly **not** a space-saving move despite appearances: the resulting pocket is too shallow to ever furnish, collects dust/dirt, and adds real finishing cost (extra corner taping/plastering, wallpaper/paint work around two extra corners, extra baseboard routing, extra stretch-ceiling perimeter routing) that exceeds the cost of fixing it at the rough-in stage. If the misaligned segment is itself load-bearing (can't be moved), fur it out flush or simply skim/level it directly — either fix can shift a nearby door opening by a small offset (~7cm in the documented example) to hide the correction, reading as "more refined" when the door is closed. **Quantified exception: in roughly 10% of cases, the correct move is the opposite** — deliberately move the segment outward to create a genuinely useful niche, rather than leaving a dead, unfurnishable gap. Default is flush-align in the other ~90%. [source: [[_Sources/YT_Af8nNyn9a_c_flush_wall_plane_technical_design_113|Af8nNyn9a_c_flush_wall_p]]]
- **Closet niche beside a bedroom door, built off a load-bearing wall, in one short partition run**: closet niche minimum **700mm** wide (fits a 600mm-deep closet + edge returns); door-opening niche minimum **900mm** wide (fits an 800mm minimum habitable-room door + ~100mm returns each side). Combined run from the load-bearing wall: **minimum 1.60m, ideally 1.70m** — undersizing risks an undersized door, a bad closet/door-frame junction, or a closet shallower than 500mm. Narrower/more specific than this page's free-standing walk-in-closet formulas (see [[14_Furniture/analysis/Wardrobe_Vocabulary_and_Sizing|Wardrobe Vocabulary & Sizing]]) — cross-reference, don't merge. ## Partition-Thickness Defaults, Tile-Buildup Compensation & Wall-Material-Consistency — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-19, remainder-pool batch)

> [!NOTE]
> `single-account`, two dense numeric-planning-logic full-replan videos. [sources: [[_Sources/YT_n7X10oIqugU_full_replan_252|note 1]], [[_Sources/YT_8Xy-h8cS_-s_full_replan_worst_two_room_234|note 2]]]

- **⚠️ Nuance to this page's well-established 150mm soundproofing-partition figure**: when a full-depth closet run is built directly against a partition for its whole length, the closet itself already exceeds a bare 150mm wall's own sound isolation — the partition can be reduced to 100mm without a net soundproofing loss in that specific configuration, freeing 50mm for a wider closet or a shifted door. **Qualifies, doesn't contradict, the 150mm default** — only applies when a full closet run directly backs the partition's whole length. [source: [[_Sources/YT_n7X10oIqugU_full_replan_252|n7X10oIqugU_full_replan_]]]
- **Default partition-thickness matrix, with an explicit infrastructure-priority override**: 150mm between two habitable rooms (bedroom-to-living-room, etc.); 100mm for all other walls (bathroom/WC dividers, closet dividers) by default. **Explicit override**: even a wall separating two closet/storage zones — where a thinner wall would obviously yield more storage space — should stay at 150mm if it must house mandatory infrastructure (a water-supply manifold, an electrical panel). Infrastructure-housing need outranks incremental storage-space gain, even when the "just make it thinner" critique looks correct at first glance. [source: [[_Sources/YT_8Xy-h8cS_-s_full_replan_worst_two_room_234|8Xy-h8cS_-s_full_replan_]]]
- **⚠️ Asymmetric tile+adhesive compensation technique for a raw pre-finish opening**: compensate per bounding wall's own flatness, not with a flat blanket figure — a freshly-rebuilt flat wall needs only ~15mm, an existing crooked surface that can't be demolished (e.g. a ventilation-shaft face) needs closer to 25mm. Worked example: net 1000mm target clear width → 1040mm raw dimension (15mm + 25mm), not a naive 1030mm (15mm + 15mm) that would leave the finished opening narrower than intended on the crooked side. [source: [[_Sources/YT_8Xy-h8cS_-s_full_replan_worst_two_room_234|8Xy-h8cS_-s_full_replan_]]]
- **⚠️ A single continuous wall plane built from materially inconsistent construction is a real, documented developer defect distinct from the already-known wall-plane-offset problem**: one wall combined 200mm-thick and 100mm-thick aerated-concrete-block sections in the same plane; another combined brick with aerated concrete, again in one plane. Verify not just that a wall's plane is flat, but that its actual material/construction is consistent along its full run. [source: [[_Sources/YT_8Xy-h8cS_-s_full_replan_worst_two_room_234|8Xy-h8cS_-s_full_replan_]]]
- **A WC opening's raw clear width can look adequate but leave almost no usable space once finished** — documented worked example: 65.9cm raw clear width (60cm aerated-concrete block) before plastering/leveling/tiling, leaving barely enough room to sit comfortably once finish layers are added. Corroborates this store's existing WC-too-narrow findings with a fully worked before/after-finish example. [source: [[_Sources/YT_8Xy-h8cS_-s_full_replan_worst_two_room_234|8Xy-h8cS_-s_full_replan_]]]

## Wall-Squareness Diagnostic, Curved-Wall Fix & Wall-Thickness-vs-Door-Leaf Nuance — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-19, remainder-pool batch)

> [!NOTE]
> `single-account`, three more videos from the same July-November 2023 narrated-planning-logic cluster. [sources: [[_Sources/YT_HVSZh0lH9hk_craziest_two_room_230|note 1]], [[_Sources/YT_ZVoExA0t6nI_useless_3bedroom_223|note 2]], [[_Sources/YT_1pU60p0Jh3A_worst_two_room_228|note 3]]]

- **⚠️ Wall-squareness DIY diagnostic**: place any true rectangular reference object (a book, a level, a sheet of drywall — anything with real 90° corners) into a suspect corner and rotate it flush against one wall — the gap or overlap visible at the other edge reveals exactly how far the corner deviates from true 90°, no angle-finder needed. [source: [[_Sources/YT_HVSZh0lH9hk_craziest_two_room_230|HVSZh0lH9hk_craziest_two]]]
- **⚠️ Curved/rounded wall corners are a fully mechanism-explained developer defect**: costlier plastering than a flat wall, persistent waviness and wallpaper bubbling regardless of finish quality, a curved curtain track that reliably jams, and a "fix" (a straight rod spanning the curve) that wastes floor area since the standard curtain-zone depth then applies across the full straight-line width. **Convert any curved wall segment into two straight, angled ("faceted") segments instead** — cheaper to finish, doesn't degrade over time, avoids the curtain-track problem entirely. [source: [[_Sources/YT_ZVoExA0t6nI_useless_3bedroom_223|ZVoExA0t6nI_useless_3bed]]]
- **⚠️ Detailed technique for positioning a wall between two fixed load-bearing columns, three options compared**: centering it in the gap leaves an awkward recess needing a large wedge filler; flushing it to the columns' outer face creates a protruding step forcing every adjacent wall to extend (wasting floor area, creating door conflicts, costing extra jamb-extension trim); the correct middle option is flushing the wall's inner face to an already-built-out filler/adjustment segment instead. [source: [[_Sources/YT_1pU60p0Jh3A_worst_two_room_228|1pU60p0Jh3A_worst_two_ro]]]
- **⚠️ Wall thickness beyond 100mm brings no additional soundproofing benefit once an interior door is present in that wall** — the door leaf itself is the binding acoustic constraint once an opening exists, not the surrounding wall thickness. Distinct from, and a nuance to, the 150mm-partition-for-soundproofing default above, which applies to solid partition runs without a door. [source: [[_Sources/YT_1pU60p0Jh3A_worst_two_room_228|1pU60p0Jh3A_worst_two_ro]]]

## Drywall False Walls — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-10)

> [!NOTE]
> `single-account`, filmed on a real jobsite building a false wall to conceal utility pipes — general drywall-framing technique, not room-specific. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_drywall_false_wall_masterclass_6d77f6f5.txt`]

- **Zemskov's case for a false wall over exposed pipes**: enables clean baseboard/ceiling-track junctions (neither terminates cleanly against a round pipe), restores the room to a furnishable rectangle, and often yields a wider countertop as a bonus — at the cost of a small floor triangle (his example: roughly A4-sheet sized).
- **Layout method**: find the most protruding element to hide, offset the drywall's inner face 3cm past it, then use a laser plane-builder (bottom marker on the floor mark, vertical-plane mode, adjusted to the wall reference point) to project a true vertical reference plane for the whole run. If the main wall is significantly bowed, cut the perimeter track into segments rather than forcing one piece to bend against it.
- **Zemskov's rule: join framing elements with a professional crimping tool (просекатель), not screws** — he calls "screws are stronger" a myth; every screw head, even flat ones, locally deforms the drywall face. A professional crimper folds/interlocks the metal (verified with a destruction test); an ordinary one just punctures it.
- **Zemskov's rule: use guide-track profile throughout the frame, not stud profile**, even for elements that would normally be studs — stud profile's folded edge blocks freely inserting cross-pieces, forcing special connector clips that would need cutting in half anyway near a window-reveal junction.
- **Convector-niche framing** (relevant to a balcony-block's lower section): build from one continuous bent piece for rigidity; niche sizing exactly 5mm larger than the grille's mounting dimensions; position with 10cm clearance above (not visually centered — the lower gap gets covered by baseboard anyway, and centering leaves awkward regulator access).
- **Bracing rule**: a stud/track frame alone has low rigidity — brace to the main wall with fabricated angle brackets, re-checking verticality throughout (easy to deform the frame slightly while fixing each brace). No fixed brace count — enough that the frame doesn't wobble under a shoulder-lean or wallpapering pressure.
- **Zemskov's sheet-cutting method: cut in place, not by tape-measure-and-pencil** — hold an oversized sheet against the frame, mark by pressing/creasing at the frame edge, then snap/fold/cut along the crease; he frames the tape-measure method as "10 extra actions" for the same eventual result.
- **Safety warning**: never brace a screw with a bare finger behind the drywall as a "stop" — a 35mm screw can drive nearly through the finger. Always wear gloves and safety glasses. ## Drywall Partition Frame & Board Technique, Beyond Screw-Depth/Plane-Check QC (Петришин-Строй, added 2026-08-24, Round 6)

Region level 2 only, medium promotional ratio (comedic-skit cold open,
settling into a genuine technique-and-acceptance format).

- **Bevel GKL board edges at 45° before installation** so boards fit tightly together without needing corner/edge trim at the seams.
- **Metal profile frame cells must be ≤400mm and uniform** — wider or irregular spacing lets drywall sag under its own weight; guide rails must be installed strictly level, since the whole frame's accuracy depends on that reference line.
- **Use anchor wedges, not nails or plastic dowels, for both wall fastening and ceiling-hanger attachment** — metal anchors don't expand/contract with temperature swings, reducing pull-out risk under sustained load (the video opens with a real collapsed-ceiling case attributed to poor fastening).
- **Profile-to-profile cross joints need a proper crab-style connector plus self-tapping screws, not a simple two-piece butt join** — an improperly joined cross-member develops bulges/waviness over time.
- **Perimeter damper/compression tape under the guide rails** prevents drywall cracking at the wall junction and gives a tighter wall-to-frame connection.
- **Two-layer drywall should be staggered (checkerboard) relative to the first layer's seams**, not seam-aligned, for structural strength.
