# Ceilings Guide - Do's and Don'ts

## L-Shaped / Non-Rectangular Ceiling Sections — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-10)

> [!NOTE]
> New topic angle for this page — a real jobsite case (88.5m² apartment), Alexey Zemskov's own account and cited cost figures, `single-account`, not independently verified against a materials supplier or another installer. Also folded into `13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Wardrobes_and_Storage.md`, `13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Flooring_Guide.md`, and `13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Doors_and_Trim.md` — a single, unusually cross-cutting case. [source: `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_entry_hallway_dividing_wall_case_8963951b.txt`]

- **Zemskov's claim: a stretch ceiling over an L-shaped (non-rectangular) room section is materially harder and more expensive than two independent rectangular sections.** His reasoning: a seamless single piece over an L-shape requires buying oversized material to cover the full bounding rectangle and discarding the unused offcut (usually not reliably reusable, since the fabric/film is cut on-site by an installer working from a stepladder). Alternatively, splitting the L into two pieces means a seam that's long, visible, and — per Zemskov — comparatively short-lived (longer seams fail faster). His stated fix in this project: split the L into two rectangular sections via a dividing wall, avoiding both problems. **Cited cost delta**: more than 10,000, sometimes more than 20,000 RUB (labor + materials) between two simple rectangular sections and one complex L-shaped installation.
- **Fabric vs. film material choice compounds the L-shape waste problem, per Zemskov**: fabric ceilings (matte, doesn't crack/yellow/fade — his preference for living spaces) and film ceilings (glossy, compatible with reflective bathroom surfaces — his preference for wet rooms) are different materials, so a leftover offcut from one room can't be repurposed for a different room using the other material even if otherwise usable.
- **A dividing wall creating a boxed transition also lets a wardrobe's top rail attach directly to a purpose-built box, per Zemskov** — giving the visual effect of the ceiling meeting the wardrobe with no gap, while keeping the two systems independently serviceable (the wardrobe/rail removable without touching the ceiling; the ceiling droppable to access wiring without touching the wardrobe).

## Stretch-Ceiling Seamless-Width Limit — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-10)

> [!NOTE]
> A separate, standalone Zemskov video (real jobsite case, no prior CSV attempt of any kind) — distinct mechanism from the L-shaped-room content above (there the problem is room *shape*; here it's raw *span*). `single-account`, not cross-checked against a manufacturer spec sheet or another installer. [source: `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_stretch_ceiling_seamless_width_limit_25477732.txt`]

- **Zemskov's stated stretch-ceiling fabric seamless-width limit**: manufacturer-rated maximum 5.10m (5m + 10cm install allowance) — but he plans against a flat 5m himself, since real achievable seamless coverage tops out around 5.02-5.03m once installation is accounted for. **A room exceeding this in its longer dimension cannot get one seamless (no-visible-seam) piece over the full span.**
- **Zemskov's real cited case**: a combined living-room/kitchen space measuring 5.92m × 8.5m — both dimensions over the limit. His fix: build a false partition wall (10cm-thick returns on each side) with a portal opening (height reduced 20cm from the ceiling, reveal finished with door jamb-extension trim), splitting the room into two zones each under 5m so each gets its own seamless piece — even though, in his case, the surrounding walls were already prepped for wallpaper. He notes a second benefit: the new partition also protects an exposed exterior corner at the same spot from repeated impact damage.
- **Zemskov's own three-point takeaway**: (1) never forget stretch-ceiling fabric's width limitation when planning a layout; (2) keep watching for correctable issues during construction, not just at the design stage; (3) make corrections at the point where they cost the least — for both client and crew.

## Do's

| Rule | Applies To | Reason | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Coordinate ceiling heights with door openings and cabinet heights | All Rooms | Prevent collision with tall furniture or door trims | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_vid1_transcript_d04723c5.txt` |
| Install full-height cabinetry with top false panel before stretch ceiling | Wardrobe, Kitchen | Enables mounting stretch ceiling track to cabinet false panel for seamless built-in look | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_tips_video_f23c504a.txt` |
| Prime and paint bare concrete ceiling slabs before installing stretch ceilings | All Rooms | Seals concrete surface and prevents mortar dust or debris from flaking onto top of stretch membrane | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1.txt` |
| Construct a rigid drywall framing box above full-height kitchen cabinetry lines | Kitchen | Accepts stretch ceiling perimeter track on its vertical face so cabinet doors open smoothly underneath | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_renovation_guide_mistakes_4_21a6e3c1.txt` |
| Split an L-shaped room section into two rectangular stretch-ceiling zones via a dividing wall, per Zemskov/Zemstandart | Any L-shaped or non-rectangular room section | Avoids costly material waste and a long, short-lived seam — cited real delta >10,000-20,000 RUB | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_entry_hallway_dividing_wall_case_8963951b.txt` |
| Check any room dimension against the ~5m stretch-ceiling seamless-fabric-width limit before finalizing layout, per Zemskov/Zemstandart | Any room over ~5m in either direction | A room exceeding the limit forces either a visible seam or a partition wall to split it into compliant zones | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_stretch_ceiling_seamless_width_limit_25477732.txt` |

## Don'ts

| Rule | Applies To | Risk | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Do not lower ceilings excessively in low-height apartments | Living Room, Bedrooms | Reduces living comfort and space feel | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260727_vid1_transcript_d04723c5.txt` |
| Do not assume a leftover stretch-ceiling offcut from one room can be reused in a different room, per Zemskov/Zemstandart | Rooms using different fabric/film materials | Fabric and film are different materials — an offcut from one isn't usable for the other | `90_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_entry_hallway_dividing_wall_case_8963951b.txt` |
