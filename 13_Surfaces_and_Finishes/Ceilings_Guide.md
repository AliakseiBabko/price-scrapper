## L-Shaped / Non-Rectangular Ceiling Sections — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-10)

> [!NOTE]
> New topic angle for this page — a real jobsite case (88.5m² apartment), Alexey Zemskov's own account and cited cost figures, `single-account`, not independently verified against a materials supplier or another installer. Also folded into `14_Furniture/Wardrobes_and_Storage.md`, `13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Flooring_Guide.md`, and `13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Doors_and_Trim.md` — a single, unusually cross-cutting case. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_entry_hallway_dividing_wall_case_8963951b.txt`]

**USD normalization for the cited L-shaped ceiling cost delta:** >10,000 to >20,000 RUB is approximately >$140 to >$270 using the metadata-confirmed 2020-12-01 source date and trailing 6-month USD/RUB average of 74.12. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_entry_hallway_dividing_wall_case_8963951b.txt`]

Zemstandart / Zemproekt says **a stretch ceiling over an L-shaped (non-rectangular) room section is materially harder and more expensive than two independent rectangular sections.** His reasoning: a seamless single piece over an L-shape requires buying oversized material to cover the full bounding rectangle and discarding the unused offcut (usually not reliably reusable, since the fabric/film is cut on-site by an installer working from a stepladder). Alternatively, splitting the L into two pieces means a seam that's long, visible, and — per Zemskov — comparatively short-lived (longer seams fail faster). His stated fix in this project: split the L into two rectangular sections via a dividing wall, avoiding both problems. **Cited cost delta**: more than 10,000, sometimes more than 20,000 RUB (labor + materials) between two simple rectangular sections and one complex L-shaped installation.
Zemstandart / Zemproekt says **fabric vs. film material choice compounds the L-shape waste problem**: fabric ceilings (matte, doesn't crack/yellow/fade — his preference for living spaces) and film ceilings (glossy, compatible with reflective bathroom surfaces — his preference for wet rooms) are different materials, so a leftover offcut from one room can't be repurposed for a different room using the other material even if otherwise usable.
Zemstandart / Zemproekt says **a dividing wall creating a boxed transition also lets a wardrobe's top rail attach directly to a purpose-built box** — giving the visual effect of the ceiling meeting the wardrobe with no gap, while keeping the two systems independently serviceable (the wardrobe/rail removable without touching the ceiling; the ceiling droppable to access wiring without touching the wardrobe).

## Stretch-Ceiling Seamless-Width Limit — per Zemskov/Zemstandart (added 2026-08-10)

> [!NOTE]
> A separate, standalone Zemskov video (real jobsite case, no prior CSV attempt of any kind) — distinct mechanism from the L-shaped-room content above (there the problem is room *shape*; here it's raw *span*). `single-account`, not cross-checked against a manufacturer spec sheet or another installer. [source: `_Archive/processed_sources/20260810_stretch_ceiling_seamless_width_limit_25477732.txt`]

Zemstandart / Zemproekt says **the stretch-ceiling fabric seamless-width limit is**: manufacturer-rated maximum 5.10m (5m + 10cm install allowance) — but he plans against a flat 5m himself, since real achievable seamless coverage tops out around 5.02-5.03m once installation is accounted for. **A room exceeding this in its longer dimension cannot get one seamless (no-visible-seam) piece over the full span.**
Zemstandart / Zemproekt reports **a combined living-room/kitchen space measuring 5.92m × 8.5m** — both dimensions over the limit. His fix: build a false partition wall (10cm-thick returns on each side) with a portal opening (height reduced 20cm from the ceiling, reveal finished with door jamb-extension trim), splitting the room into two zones each under 5m so each gets its own seamless piece — even though, in his case, the surrounding walls were already prepped for wallpaper. He notes a second benefit: the new partition also protects an exposed exterior corner at the same spot from repeated impact damage.
Zemstandart / Zemproekt says **the three-point takeaway is**: (1) never forget stretch-ceiling fabric's width limitation when planning a layout; (2) keep watching for correctable issues during construction, not just at the design stage; (3) make corrections at the point where they cost the least — for both client and crew.

## Stretch Ceiling vs. Drywall-Boxed Ceiling — Cost, Speed, and Flood Protection (RemProektMD, added 2026-08-24)

> [!NOTE]
> First Moldova/Chișinău-market source in this store, `single-account`, no absolute price figures (only relative comparisons — no currency-normalization question arises). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_OP8ALhLynHE_remproektmd_12_money_saving_tips|OP8ALhLynHE]]]

RemProektMD/Andrei recommends **stretch ceiling over a painted drywall-boxed ceiling for budget renovations**, citing four separate advantages: (1) **flood protection** — a real documented incident where an upstairs-neighbor flood was fully contained by an already-installed PVC-film stretch ceiling (the film bulged but held all the water, protecting the owner's engineered-wood flooring underneath entirely; a drywall or plastered ceiling would have let the water through onto the floor); (2) **installation speed** — a drywall ceiling's full sequence (profile, board, seam-tape, mesh, two putty passes, paint) takes a minimum of 2 weeks, vs. **1 day** for a stretch ceiling; (3) **visual parity** — a matte stretch ceiling looks indistinguishable from a painted ceiling at normal viewing distance; (4) **cost** — stretch ceiling is **2-3× cheaper** than an equivalent painted drywall-boxed ceiling. A fabric ("тканевый") stretch-ceiling variant costs more than PVC film but is still cheaper than drywall — framed mainly as an ecological/tactile preference, not a functional upgrade over film.

**Recessed/point-lighting cost caution**: each individual light penetration through stretch-ceiling film is priced separately (a backing plate/hardware is required under every hole, adding material + labor cost per hole) — prefer 1-2 diffuse fixtures per room (chandelier or a surface-mounted LED "soft-box" type) over many recessed point lights.

The same backing-plate-before-film logic applies to a smoke detector, not just light fixtures — see [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Fire_Safety_Stretch_Ceiling_Installation|Fire Safety: Stretch Ceiling Installation]] for the mounting-platform sequencing, clearance rules, and film-penetration technique specific to this project's own ceiling type.

## Stretch-Ceiling Acceptance Checklist and Pre-Work Liability Act (ДЕЛАТЬ НЕ ПЕРЕДЕЛАТЬ, added 2026-08-28, Round 2)

Vladimir Amelchenko's acceptance checklist plus a broadly-reusable liability-management technique. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_Wbj_6CEgcGU_sbk_stretch_ceiling_acceptance|YT_Wbj_6CEgcGU]]]

- **Acceptance checklist**: membrane (no holes/punctures/creases, visually flat); edge trim tape (straight, no gap from wall or ceiling); corner tightness ("no gap you could fit half a finger into"); recessed-downlight thermal-ring gap must be *consistent* across all fixtures in a room (not just individually present — a 1mm gap at one fixture and 3mm at another reads as sloppy even if each is technically "in spec"); chandelier/fixture base flush with no skew or gap; cycle every switch to confirm it controls the intended zone.
- **⚠️ Post-acceptance liability gap**: damage (wall paint smudges, chipped tile) often surfaces only during post-install cleanup, after the installer is paid and gone — attention during acceptance focuses on the ceiling itself, not the surrounding room. A chipped tile is functionally unprovable after the fact; wall paint damage is more often fixed/compensated on request.
- **Fix: a signed pre-work room-condition act**, executed before the installer touches anything, documenting walls/floors/ceiling/windows/doors as intact with no chemical/mechanical damage. Forces the installer to inspect and photograph pre-existing damage upfront (surfacing it for the owner to raise with their original contractor), and reallocates the burden of proof afterward — any damage found post-install is attributed to the work just performed. Also a screening filter: a confident installer has no reason to refuse signing.

## Painted, Drywall, and Stretch Ceiling — Full Comparison, and a "Practically Free" Loft Option (Петришин-Строй, added 2026-08-24, Round 3)

Region **level 1 — clears the bar directly** ("мы живём в Москве... в
Московской области" spoken directly in this video). Low promotional
ratio. Extends this page's existing stretch-ceiling content and adds
two ceiling types (painted, drywall) not previously covered here in a
general comparison.

- **Painted ceiling**: widely misperceived as the simplest/cheapest
  option — actually one of the hardest/priciest, requiring a dead-flat
  plastered slab before multi-coat paint. **Chasing into the structural
  slab is categorically prohibited** — wiring can only run in the
  panel-to-panel seam, enough for one fixture only, no concealed
  multi-scenario lighting is possible. A settlement crack needs visible
  touch-up; a flood from above is very hard to patch invisibly and
  usually needs a full redo. Genuine advantage: doesn't consume ceiling
  height — common in low-ceiling secondary-market housing.
- **⚠️ Ceiling-specific raking-light failure mode**: poor prep can look
  fine under artificial evening light but reveal visible waviness under
  natural morning/midday/sunset light at a specific angle — a ceiling
  analogue of this project's existing wall raking-light QC theme.
  **Flatness standard**: no gap against a 3m straightedge.
- **Panel-seam paint-prep sequence**: plaster by beacons → putty 2-3
  coats → fiberglass mesh → for a slab-panel ceiling specifically, open/
  rout the seam, foam-fill, apply a stronger filler (Пуфас or Унифлот)
  over the cured foam, reinforce with mesh plus paper bandage tape.
- **Drywall ceiling**: enables any curved line/wave/lighting niche and
  hides any service, but only reads well with a high ceiling — visually
  presses down in a low-ceilinged room. **Real timeline (70m²
  apartment, 2 workers)**: framing+board install alone ~2 weeks, plus a
  further minimum 2 weeks of taping/plaster/finish before paint — a
  full month or more is realistic. Installation labor typically costs
  more than materials. **⚠️ A flood from above is "catastrophic" for
  this ceiling type** — typically needs full demolition and rebuild,
  unlike stretch ceiling's recoverability below.
- **⚠️ Company expert's own ranking**: deprioritizes drywall ceilings
  in most cases in favor of stretch ceiling — anything drywall hides
  can equally hide behind a stretch ceiling, faster and cheaper.
  Exception: when soundproofing is a genuine client priority.
- **⚠️ Flood-recoverability detail, extending this page's existing
  RemProektMD flood-containment finding**: a flooded stretch-ceiling
  membrane can simply be drained and re-stretched, or fully replaced —
  easy and inexpensive relative to redoing a painted or drywall
  ceiling.
- **Two named stretch-ceiling design techniques**: a "floating ceiling"
  (perimeter shadow gap with an LED strip inside it) and a "seamless
  wall junction" (membrane tucked into a specific trim profile for a
  clean, gap-free wall-ceiling line).
- **⚠️ Black perimeter-insert trim as a zero-cost budget approximation of shadow-gap, plus a new "slot" profile named (Kruglov/Ontario, Round 14 triage, added 2026-08-28)**: a plain white perimeter insert trim is now considered dated; switching the same trim to **black** is a zero-extra-cost way to visually approximate a shadow-gap profile's premium look from normal viewing distance (the difference only shows up close). For a genuinely premium result rather than the black-insert approximation, two profile options exist: the shadow-gap ("теневой") profile already on this page, or a **"slot" ("щелевой") profile** — a further profile type not previously named here, producing a stretch ceiling with **no visible seam against the wall at all** (technically present but effectively invisible), distinct from shadow-gap's visible recessed channel. Not yet cross-checked against this page's existing cost ladder. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_xb5pUpTVIJU_kruglov_looks_cheap_10_mistakes|YT_xb5pUpTVIJU]]]
- **⚠️ New ceiling-type content: a deliberately exposed, unfinished
  structural slab as a loft-style choice** — no need to hide services
  or achieve flatness, since visible roughness is the aesthetic itself
  (only works if the whole apartment commits to the style). Near-zero
  installation cost, effectively no timeline, immune to flood/
  settlement damage since there's no finish layer to damage.
  [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_lvixGbwo0Ug_petrishin_ceiling_2026_comparison|lvixGbwo0Ug]]]

## Old Whitewash vs. Paint Ceilings, and a Physical Cornice-Crack Check (Петришин-Строй, added 2026-08-24, Round 5)

- **Whitewash ("побелка") vs. paint — a diagnostic distinction for very
  old (Stalin/Khrushchev-era) ceilings**: chalk-based whitewash cannot
  simply be painted over the way an already-painted ceiling sometimes
  can — it must be fully washed off first, and the old filler layer
  beneath it often needs removing too. Relevant when assessing whether a
  ceiling can be "just recoated" versus needs full rework at estimate
  time.
- **Ceiling molding/cornice cracks can be missed by a visual walkthrough
  alone** — a real example: an already-cracked polystyrene cornice was
  only found by climbing a ladder and testing it by touch/sound, not by
  looking from the floor. Can mean re-installing cornice throughout the
  whole apartment — an easy-to-miss cost driver at estimate time.
  `single-account`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_s27qG_Eg3SY_petrishin_cosmetic_reno_mistakes|YT_s27qG_Eg3SY]]]

## Drywall Screw-Depth Control and Paper-vs-Mesh Joint Tape (Петришин-Строй, added 2026-08-24, Round 5)

- **Screw-depth control**: a drywall screw head should sit slightly
  recessed but must not tear through the paper facing — a torn-paper
  screw hole isn't visible from outside but holds the board poorly and
  risks a crack or the board detaching later. Check drywall ceilings up
  close (from a stepladder) for intact paper at every screw, not just
  from the floor.
- **Seam taping — a stated practitioner preference, not independently
  cross-checked**: this source states paper joint tape works properly
  for drywall seams, while mesh tape does not, and recommends insisting
  on paper tape if a crew proposes mesh. `single-account`, this
  practitioner's own technology preference. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_vKMHNYQYWAI_petrishin_top13_expensive_mistakes|YT_vKMHNYQYWAI]]]

## Bathroom-Specific Ceiling Comparison: Slatted, Cassette, Glass/Mirror, Plastic, Stretch (Петришин-Строй + design blogger Ekaterina Popova, added 2026-08-24, Round 9)

A dual-host Q&A (builder Sergey Petrishin + a named design blogger)
answering real client measurement-visit questions specifically about
bathroom ceilings — complements this page's existing general-room
Round 3 comparison (painted/drywall/stretch) with three options not
covered there:

- **Slatted/rail ceiling (реечный)**: judge quality by metal thickness,
  coating, and brand — Албес/Bart is budget tier (thinner aluminum,
  dents on install); Cesal ("French design," made in Russia) is mid
  tier (thicker, laminated, wider color range); an "America"-branded
  line is premium. **Real 1.70×1.70m bathroom material cost, fully
  fitted**: ≈6,000-9,000 RUB (≈$90-$140) depending on tier/
  configuration, trailing-6-month rate ending 2018-11-21. **Common
  buyer mistake**: substituting a visually-similar hardware-store rail
  for the named brand to save money — the substitute often rusts within
  ~6 months and feels flimsy at install. **Drop needed**: ≈3-4cm with
  only surface-mounted fixtures, ≈7-10cm with recessed spot lighting
  (fixture-housing legs need the extra room). Backing blocks are still
  needed behind the ceiling for any real chandelier/shade fixture.
  DIY-feasible with only metal shears, a hammer drill, and a
  screwdriver.
- **Cassette ceiling (French/Armstrong-style)**: same underlying
  construction as slatted but on a triangular-rail grid that must be
  exactly 30cm to match standard 30×30cm cassettes — cut cassettes at
  the edges should be arranged symmetrically from the center, the same
  underlying principle as this vault's tile centered-layout rule
  applied to a ceiling grid. Both slatted and cassette systems
  disassemble non-destructively for post-leak drying and reassembly —
  a real serviceability advantage neither stretch nor drywall ceilings
  have.
- **Glass/mirror (Armstrong-frame) ceiling**: a grid-frame system (rail
  lengths of 30/60/120cm) with glass panels set into it. Mirror finish
  visually enlarges the room; backlit sandblasted glass gives a
  "glowing sky" effect; printed/stained-glass patterns need designer
  coordination to avoid clashing with a patterned tile choice in the
  same room. **Price point: from ≈3,000 RUB/m² (≈$50/m²)**, explicitly
  not the cheapest tier.
- **Plastic-panel ceiling**: cheapest option; ⚠️ **cheap-grade plastic
  exposed to prolonged hot-water steam can degrade and off-gas an odor
  over time** — quality depends heavily on the specific plastic
  grade/manufacturer. Can be stapled to a wood-batten frame (same drop
  logic as slatted) or glued directly to an existing ceiling if no
  drop/lighting change is needed, and can be shaped to conceal an
  existing ventilation duct.
- **Matte beats glossy for hiding condensation** on any of the glass/
  plastic/mirror options — a real practical (not just aesthetic) reason
  to default to matte in a bathroom.
- **⚠️ Stretch-ceiling mold/leak-resistance clarified**: a stretch
  ceiling's tight perimeter seal genuinely resists ordinary ambient
  humidity, but **it is leak-resistant, not leak-proof** — a real leak
  volume from a neighbor above will still escape through any
  penetration (a recessed-light cutout, or the perimeter itself under
  enough water). Mold risk exists behind *any* ceiling type if a real
  leak persists; the ceiling material only changes how fast the problem
  becomes visible (plastered/painted shows staining sooner; stretch
  hides it longer). **Yellowing** happens over a ~10-year+ service life
  and specifically around a light cutout **if halogen bulbs are used**
  (they run hot) — a competent installer avoids halogen with a stretch
  ceiling; LED eliminates the risk. `single-account` 3-year personal
  no-yellowing/no-mold account from the builder's own bathroom.
- **Design guidance (Ekaterina Popova)**: default to plain white matte/
  semi-gloss slatted or cassette finishes with no decorative inserts or
  gold/silver trim — ornamentation reads as a budget-solution signal; a
  plain white matte slatted ceiling is visually unremarkable rather than
  announcing its own material. A wood-look printed slat finish works
  specifically because ceiling height makes printed film hard to
  distinguish from real wood. **A curtain-track niche (see the concealed
  curtain-track note above) must be flagged to the installer/measurer
  before the initial measurement visit**, since the stretch-film needs
  special shaping for it.
- **Joint verdict**: slatted ceiling for a tight budget/DIY project
  (cheap, minimal skill, but visually dated); stretch ceiling as the
  more modern step-up if budget allows; a drywall ceiling with LED strip
  lighting for a large, high-ceilinged country-house bathroom, worth the
  extra labor cost.

## Deep-Dive Interview with a Professional Installer — Andrey Frolov (Round 5, added 2026-08-28)

Konstantin Kruglov interviews Andrey Frolov, an independent professional stretch-ceiling installer/blogger (no commercial tie to Ontario) — a low-promotional, ~87-minute practitioner interview, genuinely fresh material for this page beyond the general painted/drywall/stretch comparisons above. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_W1PKG4tVw_g_kruglov_stretch_ceiling_truth_frolov|YT_W1PKG4tVw_g]]]

**USD normalization**: using the 2026-04-10 upload date and trailing 6-month USD/RUB average of 79.04, 30,000 RUB ≈ $380; 1,000-1,500 RUB ≈ $10-$20; smaller RUB figures below (200, 350, 300-400, 800) are kept as plain RUB per this project's under-$10 convention.

**Health/off-gassing myth, first detailed treatment for this page**: reputable-manufacturer film and fabric are non-toxic. The plasticizer additives that make PVC film stretchable can off-gas, but only at real heat (**70-80°C+**) — reached briefly during hot-air installation, not by normal room conditions afterward. Cheap/low-quality additives off-gas more readily and are the real source of "new ceiling smell" and installer headaches — an **installer-exposure risk during installation**, not a long-term occupant risk with a quality product; vacate and ventilate the room right after install regardless. "Fabric breathes, therefore healthier" and "PVC doesn't breathe, therefore unhealthy" are both called myths — fabric isn't heated during install (its one real difference) but carries the same additive-quality-dependent risk, and a ceiling has no functional need to be air-permeable at all.

**Fire-rating tier and pricing ladder, new for this page**: above baseline combustible PVC (~200 RUB/m²), a **non-combustible ("негорючий")** grade exists from **~350 RUB/m²** (roughly double) — melts and self-extinguishes when the ignition source is removed, rather than continuing to burn/drip. Most non-combustible-grade PVC carries a strong flame-retardant-additive smell that fades over time, except one named exception below.

**Named manufacturers, first non-Chinese-import producer recorded for this page**: "КНТ" (KNT, Ufa, Russia) makes PVC film domestically (plus inserts/plugs/harpoon strips); "Н-пласт" (N-plast) has a proprietary non-combustible formula with no off-gassing smell and uses virgin (not recycled) plastic pellets, claimed to resist yellowing better over time. **Grade authentication is essentially impossible by touch/sight — certificates are the only reliable check**, and even certificates/anti-counterfeit stamps get counterfeited once a brand/tier is popular enough (installer cites Knauf/Rodman drywall as a precedent in an unrelated material category). The one real hands-on differentiator is film thickness (microns), and even that isn't an absolute rule — requires a side-by-side sample.

**PVC vs. fabric price ratio, first concrete figure for this page**: fabric costs a minimum of **~800 RUB/m²** vs. **~200 RUB/m²** baseline PVC — roughly **4× the material cost**, compounded by fabric-specific tensioning hardware also costing more. Fabric is used in only an estimated **5%** of this installer's own projects.

**Sizing and cold-tolerance, distinct new figures**: PVC film's manufacturer-rated maximum seamless width is **5.6m** — a separate figure from this page's existing Zemskov-sourced 5.10m/achievable-5.02-5.03m figure, recorded separately per this project's non-blending convention, not merged. Fabric's max seamless width (**~5m**) matches this page's existing figure. **Fabric remains usable to -30°C** without losing elasticity (some manufacturers claim to -50°C, unverified) — a real 5-year field example from the installer's own unheated rural house confirms it through repeated -30°C winters. **PVC film is not recommended for unheated spaces** (loggia/garage/terrace) — prefer fabric there, though some cold-rated PVC products exist.

**Flood behavior, a genuine fabric-vs-PVC asymmetry new for this page**: unlike PVC (drainable and heat-gun-reshapeable back to its original form after a flood — already documented elsewhere for this project), **a flooded fabric ceiling must be fully replaced or repainted**, not reused. A first (informal) water-load data point: the installer has personally supported his own body weight (~78kg) standing under a water-loaded PVC ceiling from below without failure — offered as a rough "~78 liters held" proxy, not a rigorous test.

**A finer ceiling-drop (opusk) figure ladder than this page previously had**: concealed wiring/junction box in the void → minimum **~5cm**; no wiring at all → minimum **~3cm**; bare unfinished slab with membrane almost direct against it → absolute minimum **~1.5cm**. Standard/typical room drop **~6-7cm**; complex profile or heavy service routing **~10-15cm**; supply/exhaust mechanical ventilation ducting specifically **~15-20cm** — the single biggest ceiling-drop consumer named.

**Lighting integration, several new items**: purpose-built recessed downlights designed for a stretch-ceiling void ("nahtyashki") run ~300-400 RUB (cheap Chinese) to ~1,000-1,500 RUB (established brand) per fixture at Moscow-area retail. Track lighting is the installer's own stated preference (more functional/flexible, though visually "busier" than a plain track reads in luxury projects) — named brand "Люмфер" (Lumfer), praised on price/functionality. **Backlit translucent "light ceiling" panel**: individually-lensed LED diodes (not ordinary strip) spaced ~10cm apart, minimum **~30,000 RUB (≈$380)** installed even for 1m², averaged across Russia. Genuinely praised — not just aesthetic — for a full-shower-ceiling install: shadow-free diffuse light with the fixture fully concealed/moisture-protected behind the membrane. **Real caution**: unlit, a translucent panel looks visibly different from an ordinary ceiling (semi-see-through) — the installer avoids it in daylight-lit living spaces for this reason, reserving it for the shower use case.

**Access hardware and wiring**: a named plastic overlay access-grille product, "Мультикрафт" (Multicraft, Voronezh, Russia), for driver/transformer access where a full hinged hatch is too bulky for a shallow void. **Voltage-drop caution for low-voltage LED runs**: standard thin 2-conductor wire from a remote driver risks real voltage-drop loss; some installers run 4-conductor 2.5mm² cable instead specifically to cut loss — rough rule of thumb: **~15 running meters** of ordinary low-voltage cable before this becomes a real concern.

**Damage triage, new mechanistic detail**: a puncture/tear at the sheet's edge (near the wall) can sometimes be locally re-cut and re-tensioned by a specialist, salvaging the whole ceiling; the same damage mid-field cannot be repaired this way (no edge to re-anchor to) — forces full replacement or an adhesive decorative-grille/medallion patch glued over the hole as a stopgap only, not a structural repair. Damage can progressively grow if left untreated (installer's own windshield-chip analogy) — call a specialist promptly. Patch adhesive: older Cosmofen-brand cyanoacrylate has been largely superseded by newer Chinese-brand cyanoacrylate in common current use.

**⚠️ Smoke detectors must never be fully concealed behind the membrane** — must sit flush with or only slightly recessed into the ceiling surface, fully exposed, since real-fire detection speed is measured in seconds; a protective grille cover is sometimes installed at a client's explicit request but the installer actively discourages it. **Sprinkler/fire-suppression heads cannot be routed as permissively** — they need a specific manufacturer-made mounting collar/platform at a designated penetration point, distinct from an ordinary wire-penetration reinforcing ring. See [[12_Engineering_and_Systems/analysis/Fire_Safety_Stretch_Ceiling_Installation|Fire Safety: Stretch Ceiling Installation]] for this project's existing platform-before-film sequencing and clearance-rule detail from a different (installation-company) source — this interview adds the detection-speed rationale and practitioner stance, not new mounting mechanics.

**⚠️ Solid baguette framing cancels the membrane's own repairability advantage (Round 15, added 2026-08-28)**: this page's existing repairability content notes a stretch ceiling can normally be locally unclipped and re-tensioned (to add a fixture, fix a leak, etc.) without disturbing the rest of the ceiling. Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario flags that framing the perimeter in a rigid, puttied-and-painted baguette molding removes that flexibility — accessing the membrane later means physically breaking off a baguette section and re-finishing (puttying/sanding/painting) it from scratch. Recommends a shadow-gap or "eurokant" perimeter finish instead, both already compatible with the membrane's repairability. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_kkE25HmFciU_kruglov_worst_solutions|YT_kkE25HmFciU]]]

**Extension, a second Ontario source (Round 15, added 2026-08-28)**: a bare stretch-ceiling membrane replacement (no baguette) takes about **3 hours** with zero mess/dust — the installer unclips the existing profile, tensions new material, and reclips. Where a baguette *is* present, removing it to access the membrane damages the paint/plaster at the wall-ceiling junction, cascading the repair into repainting/re-finishing the walls too — a fuller "why it cascades" mechanism than the entry above gave. `single-account`, `unverified`, Ontario (Nikita Kuznetsov presenting). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_x8cNF81m7-A_kruglov_impractical_solutions|YT_x8cNF81m7-A]]]

**Cross-cutting**: a "stretch walls" (натяжные стены) trend — the same PVC/fabric material and technique applied to walls instead of ceilings, for a soft/cushioned child-safe tactile surface, sound absorption, and a perfectly even defect-hiding finish — is genuinely new territory, flagged for [[13_Surfaces_and_Finishes/Walls_and_Paint]] rather than covered here since it's a wall-finish technique.

## Five-Way Cost, Timeline, and Repairability Comparison (Kruglov/Ontario, Round 5, added 2026-08-28)

Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario gives the first full quantified labor+materials cost ladder across all five ceiling types this page tracks, plus an independent cross-check of this round's own Frolov ceiling-drop figures. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_lhNC30_adGc_kruglov_which_ceiling_cost_comparison|YT_lhNC30_adGc]]]

**USD normalization**: 2024-11-15 upload date, trailing 6-month USD/RUB average of 91.03.

**Labor-only cost per m²**: stretch, ordinary profile **1,000 RUB (≈$10)**; stretch, shadow-gap profile **2,000 RUB (≈$20)**; plastered-for-paint **4,000 RUB (≈$40)**; single-level drywall, one board layer **5,500 RUB (≈$60)**; single-level drywall, two board layers **6,500 RUB (≈$70)** (double joint-finishing work); multi-level drywall, two layers/level **8,000 RUB (≈$90)**.

**Materials cost per m²**: stretch ordinary **~1,500 RUB (≈$20)**; stretch shadow-gap **~2,500 RUB (≈$30, `ASR-uncertain`)**; plastered **~2,500 RUB (≈$30)**; drywall single-layer **~3,500 RUB (≈$40)**, double-layer **~4,500 RUB (≈$50)**, multi-level **~5,500 RUB (≈$60)**.

**Worked 100m² apartment example**: stretch (ordinary) ≈ **250,000 RUB (≈$2,700)**; plastered ≈ **650,000 RUB (≈$7,100)**; multi-level drywall ≈ **1,350,000 RUB (≈$14,800)** — a **~5.4× cost gap** between cheapest and priciest, labor+materials. **Timeline for the same apartment**: stretch ≈ **1 week**; plastered ≈ **1-1.5 months**; multi-level drywall ≈ **2 months**.

**Ceiling-drop ranking, independently corroborating this round's Frolov interview**: plastered loses the least (~1-1.5cm, plaster thickness only — closely matching Frolov's own bare-slab ~1.5cm minimum from a completely different source/format); stretch next (similar regardless of profile tier); single-layer drywall next; double-layer slightly more; **multi-level drywall the most — can easily consume 15-20cm**, matching Frolov's own figure closely.

**Binary (not gradual) compatibility exclusions for a plastered ceiling**: cannot host any recessed/embedded fixture at all (surface-mounted only — chandelier, surface downlight, surface/suspended track); cannot carry more than a single cable to one fixture through the ceiling void (no multi-circuit wiring loom); cannot host any soundproofing layer at all. All three are hard exclusions unique to plastered ceilings — stretch and drywall both support all three. **Concrete motivating scenario**: a low-ceiling (2.65m) secondary-market apartment where the floor screed can't be raised further — route electrical circuits through the ceiling void instead, at some ceiling-height cost (stretch ceiling finishes ~2.45m in this example) rather than raising the floor.

**Flood-repair cost and mechanism, first specific figure for this page**: stretch ceiling — remove (~10,000 RUB/≈$110) then reinstall after drying (~10,000 RUB/≈$110), total **~15,000-20,000 RUB (≈$160-$220)**. **Drywall's flood outcome is qualitatively worse**: water pools inside the metal channel profiles themselves, rusting/rotting over time — essentially unsalvageable rather than a clean repair. **Plastered ceiling usually survives a flood**, with one rare failure mode: water penetrating between plaster and the concrete slab can cause the plaster to delaminate and fall.

**⚠️ Independently corroborating Frolov's micro-draft/billowing mechanism from a completely different source**: a micro-draft (unsealed gap, sometimes from an exterior wall) can cause a stretch membrane to visibly billow in/out — fixed by installing small vent grilles in two opposite corners to equalize air pressure by convection. **New comparative claim**: stretch ceiling is described as non-allergenic and containing less plastic by volume than a single window frame.

**Presenter's own personal ranking** (not framed as universal advice): stretch ceiling is his own favorite outright; drywall second if budget allows (shadow-gap stretch as the value pick otherwise); plastered ceiling ranked last, "not a single upside."

**⚠️ A material-science detail behind the non-yellowing/hypoallergenic claim (same channel, added 2026-08-28)**: the stretch-ceiling membrane is made from **primary (virgin) plastic pellets, melted directly** — this is why it doesn't darken/yellow over time, unlike **recycled plastic**. The source's own comparison: PVC window-frame profiles themselves don't yellow (primary plastic), but plastic insert strips sometimes fitted to windowsills do yellow (recycled plastic) — the same underlying material distinction applies to the ceiling membrane. This video's own cost totals otherwise restate the cost ladder above almost exactly (same channel, same order of magnitude) and are not re-recorded as a separate data point. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_KNY-XfgbGog_kruglov_top8_save_money_tips|YT_KNY-XfgbGog]]]
