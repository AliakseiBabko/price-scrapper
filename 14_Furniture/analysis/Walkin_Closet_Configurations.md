# Furniture — Walk-In Closet Width by Configuration

Part of [[14_Furniture/Wardrobes_and_Storage|Wardrobes & Storage]].

**Single-sided minimum: 135cm** = 10cm door-trim standoff + 70cm rough opening (for a 60cm door) + 55cm zone depth (finishes to 60cm). Reducible to **125cm** (drop the standoff, if no adjacent wall-trim conflict) or **120cm** (standoff dropped + zone shortened 45-50cm using 30-40cm shelving instead) with a still-usable 60cm aisle. **Maximum before double-sided applies: 145cm** (150cm allows an opposite 30cm shelf; 175cm+ upgrades to a proper double-sided layout — 55cm aisle + two 60cm zones). [source: [[_Sources/YT_RyHzFDGgqKA_wardrobe_closet_layout_zones|RyHzFDGgqKA_wardrobe_clo]]]

**Don't shrink a bedroom to carve out a single-sided walk-in** — a plain wardrobe in the same footprint holds the same clothing volume without losing the ~70cm an aisle costs. **Minimum room length to justify one without sacrificing other function: 5m** (at 3m room width) — shorter rooms should default to a plain wardrobe instead. [source: [[_Sources/YT_Xh7uwbKVmfA_two_wardrobes_beat_walk_in|Xh7uwbKVmfA_two_wardrobe]]]

**Double-sided doors typically swing outward**, except into a narrow corridor, where inward-swing is chosen for safety over comfort. [source: [[_Sources/YT_RyHzFDGgqKA_wardrobe_closet_layout_zones|RyHzFDGgqKA_wardrobe_clo]]]

**Three-sided minimum leg length: 80cm.** Shorter legs force overlapping zones that become impractically hard to reach. **Overlap counter-clockwise** (right zone placed first, front overlaps it, left overlaps the front) — most people are right-handed, so compressing hung clothes with the stronger hand while retrieving with the free hand is more comfortable; the same logic extends to reaching into far shoe-storage corners. [source: [[_Sources/YT_RyHzFDGgqKA_wardrobe_closet_layout_zones|RyHzFDGgqKA_wardrobe_clo]]]

**Four-sided minimum width: 260cm** (auxiliary zone left) or **280cm** (auxiliary zone right); 300cm+ opens further configurations, more so above 360cm. [source: [[_Sources/YT_u28Gqg_V7eE_walkin_access_from_hallway_not_bedroom|u28Gqg_V7eE_walkin_acces]]]

**Worked layout for the common ~1.90×1.05m developer-supplied storage room**: only one configuration is arithmetically workable — 60cm zones on both left and right walls with a shallow (~33cm-deep) shoe rack centered at the far end. L-shaped and back-wall-only layouts both fail (leave only 25-45cm for storage after door-opening/aisle allowances are subtracted). [source: [[_Sources/YT_Xh7uwbKVmfA_two_wardrobes_beat_walk_in|Xh7uwbKVmfA_two_wardrobe]]]

**A mirrored walk-in needs a minimum width of 1.7m**: 1m mirror-standing clearance + 60cm clothing zone + 10cm sliding-track allowance. Below that, don't build a walk-in with an internal mirror at all — see [[14_Furniture/analysis/Wardrobe_Common_Mistakes|Common Mistakes]] for why the mirror can't just be skipped or relocated. [source: [[_Sources/YT_kj503mBQXq8_small_closet_layout|kj503mBQXq8_small_closet]]]

## Aisle Width — a Distinct Figure From Zone Width (Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario, added 2026-08-28)

**New source, new channel — the first figure on this page for the actual walking clearance inside a walk-in, distinct from the zone-depth-driven total widths above.** Konstantin Kruglov states the internal aisle (not the door rough opening) should never go below **80cm** — narrower and the room can't be turned around or moved through comfortably, degrading into "just an inconvenient wardrobe." **Comfortable: 90-100cm. Ideal: 110cm+.** This is a genuinely new axis for this page (the existing 120-145cm single-sided figures above are total-width formulas that already bake in a workable aisle; this rule lets a plan be checked directly against the aisle alone, independent of zone configuration). `single-account`, `unverified`.

## Passage-Through Walk-Ins Need Deliberate Ventilation Planning (Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario, added 2026-08-28)

**A walk-in can be built as a "pass-through" — the only route between a bedroom and its ensuite bathroom** — not just entered from a bedroom or hallway as a dead-end room. Kruglov frames this as workable but higher-stakes: because clothing constantly passes through (or sits inside) a room that also carries bathroom moisture/humidity and traffic, **ventilation planning is non-negotiable regardless of which access pattern is used** — see the ventilation tiers below. A closet that is only reachable through a bathroom, or only reachable by walking through the closet to reach the bathroom, should get the same or better ventilation tier than a dead-end walk-in, not less. `single-account`, `unverified`.

**A walk-in's own entry must always have a real door — never an open portal or a curtain.** Kruglov's explicit warning: leaving the walk-in threshold open, or hanging a curtain instead of a door, reads as "colhoz" (makeshift/cheap) and fails the walk-in's own job of keeping bedroom dust out of stored clothing (and vice versa — stored garments themselves shed dust). A hinged (swing) door is his own stated preference; a sliding door (either recessed into a stud-wall pocket or surface-mounted) is an acceptable second choice if it suits the room's layout better. [source: [[_Sources/YT_2rU14i9NqOk_kruglov_walkin_closet_dream_guide|YT_2rU14i9NqOk]]]

## Ventilation — Three Tiers, Worst to Best (Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario, added 2026-08-28)

**No prior source on this page addressed walk-in ventilation at all — genuinely new ground.** Kruglov names three concrete tiers, applicable to any walk-in but treated as mandatory once the closet is a bathroom pass-through:

1. **Passive grilles only** (a vent grille on each side of the closet — e.g. one toward the hallway, one toward the bedroom, or both toward the bedroom at different heights) **plus a 1.5-2cm gap left under a hinged door** for natural air movement. Kruglov is explicit this tier is weak — "there's nothing driving real circulation" — but still better than a sealed room.
2. **Grilles (preferably at the bottom) plus a forced supply/exhaust duct fan** (wall-, ceiling-, or in-duct-mounted) pulling air out of the closet through the ventilation duct, with make-up air drawn in from the bedroom via the grilles or under-door gap. Explicitly the **minimum acceptable tier for any walk-in that doubles as the passage to a bathroom** — Kruglov's own warning: skip this and rely on two passive grilles alone in a bathroom-passage layout, and stored clothing will noticeably absorb odors and excess humidity, losing its "fresh" state.
3. **A full supply-and-exhaust (приточно-вытяжная) ducted system serving the whole apartment**, extended to include the closet — the "no-compromise" tier, nothing better available.

[source: [[_Sources/YT_2rU14i9NqOk_kruglov_walkin_closet_dream_guide|YT_2rU14i9NqOk]]]

## Island Units for Large Walk-Ins (Konstantin Kruglov / Ontario, added 2026-08-28)

**A freestanding central island (like a kitchen island), used for watches, wallets, belts, and small accessories, is only proposed for walk-ins of ~10m² or more** — Kruglov's own explicit floor: below that size there simply isn't room to add a central fixture without blocking circulation. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_2rU14i9NqOk_kruglov_walkin_closet_dream_guide|YT_2rU14i9NqOk]]]

## Master-Suite Bathe-Dress-Exit Flow — an Honest Compromise Admission (Игорь Краснов, added 2026-09-01)

**A proper master-suite flow is the ideal — bathe, then dress in an adjoining walk-in, then exit already dressed — but is often a real compromise even for a design professional's own home.** Krasnov states his own flat, despite its size, lacks a true master-suite adjacency: he must walk the length of the apartment between the shower and the walk-in closet, and says he would design the layout differently if starting from scratch. Recorded as a rare honest admission that even a practitioner's own home reflects a real planning tradeoff, not a showcase of the textbook ideal. `single-account`, `unverified`. [source: [[_Sources/YT_6ttTd82TWPM_krasnov_closets_wasting_space|6ttTd82TWPM]]]
