# Furniture — If a Contractor's Work Turns Out Defective

This is Russian consumer-protection law as explained by a named guest lawyer in one source — **not an independently verified citation of the actual statute text**. Treat the mechanism/process as plausible and worth knowing, but verify the specific percentages against the current law before relying on them in a real dispute. Part of [[14_Furniture/Wardrobes_and_Storage|Wardrobes & Storage]]. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_pVMvsuhrwWs_furniture_dispute_legal_process|extraction note]]]

- **Two legal-expertise categories determine what can be claimed**: **товароведческая экспертиза** (product/goods expertise) applies to a free-standing furniture purchase; **строительно-техническая экспертиза** (construction-technical expertise) applies when the item is built-in and its removal affects the apartment's own interior finishing — the latter additionally allows claiming finish-restoration costs a goods-only claim wouldn't cover. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_pVMvsuhrwWs_furniture_dispute_legal_process|pVMvsuhrwWs_furniture_di]]]
- **The other party must be formally notified of a scheduled expert inspection's date/time in advance** (e.g. via registered telegram) to preserve their documented right to attend — skipping this lets them later contest the report's validity in court. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_pVMvsuhrwWs_furniture_dispute_legal_process|pVMvsuhrwWs_furniture_di]]]
- **Recommended sequence**: engage a lawyer first (to identify contract type — work vs. goods, since the law treats these differently) → determine built-in vs. free-standing → formulate specific questions for the formal expert examination → notify the other party → conduct the examination → calculate the claim. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_pVMvsuhrwWs_furniture_dispute_legal_process|pVMvsuhrwWs_furniture_di]]]
- **An individual entrepreneur (IP) in Russia is personally liable with their own assets** — ceasing IP status doesn't extinguish an existing debt, which can be pursued against the person privately instead. Relevant when evaluating recourse against a small IP-structured contractor specifically. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_pVMvsuhrwWs_furniture_dispute_legal_process|pVMvsuhrwWs_furniture_di]]]
- If unresolved via a pre-court settlement demand, Russian consumer-protection law reportedly adds a **50% penalty on the combined goods/damages/moral-harm total**, plus a **3%/day contractual penalty (неустойка)** on the contract value, capped at a stated maximum — `unverified` against the actual statute text, single secondhand account.
