# Furniture — Upholstery Fabric Selection

Detail page for [[14_Furniture/Seating_and_Tables|Seating & Tables]].

**Which upholstery fabric survives which household — the claw question, the "антивандальность" question, and eco-leather's actual service life.**

> **Why this page exists as its own page (2026-09-14): the claw finding has now arrived from FOUR unrelated sources in four different incentive positions, which makes it the best-corroborated furniture finding in this vault. It was scattered across the bed page, the loose-furniture page and three source notes. Consolidated here.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 1. The organising principle — there is no universal fabric

> ***«Нет плохих инструментов… все материалы хороши, но только на своём месте.»*** — Никита Якоби, re-upholsterer [source: [[_Sources/YT_SwOVFr5YRP8_yakobi_upholstery_fabric_choice|YT_SwOVFr5YRP8]]]

**His instruction is to settle, before choosing anything: who and what will actually be on this piece, and for how long** — children, animals, food, daily versus occasional use — **and then choose to that answer rather than to a category label.**

> **→ Note this is the same shape as [[14_Furniture/analysis/Mattress_Construction_And_Selection|the mattress page's]] conclusion — fit dominates price — arriving independently in a different product category.** **Treat it as the frame for everything below, not as one bullet among them.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 2. THE CLAW FINDING — four sources, four incentive positions, one mechanism

**The mechanism: a loose or open weave gives a claw something to HOOK. A short, dense pile does not.**

| Source | Position | What they name as failing | What they name as safe | |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Шеврина / SMBUREAU** | interior designer | **букле, рубчатый бархат** | — | [source: [[_Sources/YT_GcTaKRQ3QC0_shevrina_choosing_a_sofa|YT_GcTaKRQ3QC0]]] |
| **ARMOS** | bed manufacturer | **рогожка** — *however expensive* | **велюр, флок** | [source: [[_Sources/YT_ZLPknlB-3XI_armos_bed_selection_and_base_construction|YT_ZLPknlB-3XI]]] |
| **⚠️ Никита Якоби** | **re-upholsterer — sees furniture AFTER it fails** | **рогожка** | **флок, велюр** | [source: [[_Sources/YT_SwOVFr5YRP8_yakobi_upholstery_fabric_choice|YT_SwOVFr5YRP8]]] |
| **⚠️ Пинскдрев** | **Belarusian sofa manufacturer** | **plain unbacked шенилл** | **велюр — *«в плане плотности структуры велюр — лидер»*** | [source: [[_Sources/YT_1UGUjGmZPNA_pinskdrev_sofa_fabric_guide|YT_1UGUjGmZPNA]]] |

> **⚠️⚠️ Four unrelated sources, four different commercial interests, the same physical explanation. Велюр is on the safe side in three of the four and contradicted by none.**

### ⚠️⚠️ And the manufacturer supplies the FIX, which the other three could not

**Пинскдрев, explaining why one of their fabrics has a dense backing:**

> ***«Такая подложка нужна для того, чтобы вот этот ворс весь держался очень плотно, чтобы когти с животного не выдирали этот ворс — потому что если бы это был обычный шенилл, то структура была бы нестабильная.»***

- **⚠️⚠️ THE PURCHASING QUESTION THIS CREATES, and the vault had none before: ask whether the fabric is ДУБЛИРОВАННАЯ — backed.** A bonded backing anchors the pile so a claw cannot pull it out.
- **⚠️⚠️ The general property is DENSITY, reached by either of two routes.** The same source on a bouclé: *«здесь такая высокая плотность, что дублированный слой не нужен… плотное букле, без всяких промежутков, без всяких просветов»* — **woven tight, or bonded to a backing. → The inspection heuristic that follows is better than any fibre name: hold the sample to the light and look for gaps.**
- **⚠️ BUT do not read "bouclé is fine" out of this.** Шеврина warns specifically about букле and claws. **They are describing different bouclés — hers loose, his chosen for having no gaps — and the light test is what distinguishes them.**

### ⚠️ Рогожка has a SECOND failure mode, independent of pets

**Якоби:** beyond catching into snags immediately, **a water stain on рогожка spreads into a halo (разводы) and needs dry-cleaning to remove** — not a wipe.

> **⚠️⚠️ A tension inside this vault that must be stated rather than smoothed: [[14_Furniture/analysis/Loose_Furniture_Selection_Principles|Loose Furniture Selection Principles]] recommends рогожка on AESTHETIC grounds — textured weave, visible stitch, better than flat monotone.** **Both are true. Рогожка looks good and fails badly in exactly two conditions: claws, and spills.** **The page must not read as though this vault likes рогожка without qualification.**

### ⚠️ The one dissenting recommendation, and why it is not a fifth vote

**LifeMebel (2020) recommends рогожка and велюр for soft beds, naming велюр *«самый популярный тренд»* and рогожка as adding *«уюта»*, with NO pet caveat at all.**

> **→ He is answering a different question — what looks current — and is silent on the one where the other four agree.** **His велюр recommendation is consistent with all of them.** ⚠️ **Also the oldest source here by three years.**

## ⚠️⚠️ 3. "Антивандальность" — a coating with a shelf life, not a property of the weave

**This vault previously carried only Краснов's position: anti-vandal fabrics are what hotels and rental flats use, and are the answer for pets with big claws. Якоби attacks the category from inside it.**

**His account:**

- **The stain- and claw-resistance being sold is an APPLIED TREATMENT, not a property of the weave.**
- **It degrades within roughly a year** of normal use and cleaning.
- **⚠️⚠️ He states he used to perform the standard showroom demonstration himself** — drawing on flock with a pen and wiping it off — **as a sales trick.**
- **The case that ended it for him:** a customer bought a white flock sofa on that promise, spilled jam on it, and the seat had to be reupholstered. Her complaint was the honest one: *«вы же мне обещали, что это антивандальный материал, с ним ничего не будет»*.

> **⚠️⚠️ THE RECONCILIATION — this does not refute Краснов, it reframes what he is describing.** **A hotel re-covers on a cycle; a household expects a decade.** **"Антивандальный" describes a NEW fabric's behaviour under a showroom demonstration, and the honest planning horizon for that behaviour is about a year.**
>
> ⚠️ **The one-year figure is his trade experience, not a test result, and is uncorroborated.** **The structural claim — that it is a treatment rather than a weave property — is the part that changes a decision, and it is checkable: ask the seller whether the property survives cleaning, and get the answer in writing.**
>
> ⚠️⚠️ **Contrast Пинскдрев, who claims one of their chenilles is soil-repellent *from the weave structure itself* rather than from a treatment. That is a vendor claim about a vendor product with no test cited, and it sits directly against Якоби, who is the better-positioned source on this question. NOT carried here as a finding — recorded so the disagreement is visible.**

## ⚠️ 4. Eco-leather — where it works, and what it costs where it does not

- **⚠️ Service life before it begins to peel: 3–5 years** (Якоби). **The vault's first number for this.**
- **✅ For a kitchen or dining seat he RECOMMENDS it** — it wipes clean, which is the property that actually matters there.
- **⚠️⚠️ The failure case, a living-room sofa in экокожа:** the owner found it uncomfortable to sit on, spent five years covering it with throws that constantly slid off, **and it peeled underneath the throws anyway.** **→ Two costs, not one: unpleasant for the whole period AND it still failed on schedule.**

> **→ Corroborates ARMOS's line already in the vault — экокожа is unsuitable if you want 10–15 years — and now attaches a figure to it.** **Two sources, consistent. The usable form of the rule: экокожа is a WIPE-CLEAN choice with a 3–5 year horizon, not a durability choice.**

## ⚠️ 5. Chenille, velour, and the fabric that is deliberately disguised

**Пинскдрев, describing their own market movement: *«сейчас наступила эра шенилла»*, displacing the velours that dominated earlier collections — on tactile feel and on VOLUME, since a bulky weave upholsters a curved sofa *«мягкая, пушистая, нет лишних складок, каких-то морщинок»*.**

**⚠️⚠️ And the part that matters to a buyer: some velours are deliberately made to READ as chenille.**

> ***«Мода на шениллы есть, и велюры очень успешно мимикрируют под шениллы»*** — explicitly *«для того, чтобы ткань была модная, но при этом практичная»*, and *«неспециалист даже не скажет, что это велюр»*.

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ This resolves the conflict this page otherwise creates.** If velour is the pet-durable choice (§2) and chenille is what looks current, **a mimicking velour gives both instead of trading one against the other.** **And since you cannot identify it by eye — the manufacturer says so — it has to be asked for by name.**

## ⚠️ 6. How a Belarusian manufacturer actually prices upholstery

**Пинскдрев references every fabric by GROUP NUMBER — 18th, 19th, 20th, 22nd — with a lower group meaning cheaper.** A velour praised as *«всего лишь восемнадцатая группа»* is suitable for *«любые недорогие бюджетные диваны»*; a bouclé found *«по двадцатой группе»* replaced one that was *«несколько дороговато»*. **Collections are stated to be universal across their models — *«практически без ограничений»*.**

> **→ ⚠️⚠️ A structural fact about buying a sofa in this market: the price is set by the FABRIC GROUP applied to a model, not by the model alone.** **Consequence: the same sofa quoted twice at different prices may differ only in group — and the group is a lever you can pull, where the frame is not.**
>
> ⚠️ **No money is named anywhere in the source. Group numbers are not prices, so no figure enters this vault under standing rule 2, and none should be inferred.**
