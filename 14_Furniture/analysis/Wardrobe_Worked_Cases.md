## Recessing a Wall to Create a Built-In Niche

Real project, 91m² apartment: recessed an entry-zone wall inward (72-78cm) to create a niche exactly sized for a built-in wardrobe with sliding doors — turning what would otherwise require a bulky free-standing unit into a flush built-in. **Side benefit**: the recess also converts an exposed exterior corner into a protected one once boxed with the wardrobe door front. When this interacts with an adjacent balcony/loggia opening, the opening may need repositioning to keep the wardrobe's sliding-door track plane clear — this project shifted the balcony opening -18cm on one side / +34cm on the other, preserving an 85cm clear opening. **Planning-discipline principle stated explicitly**: precompute the sliding-door rail width and wall-junction detail independent of which side the door panel will ultimately occupy — described as what separates a fully-engineered project from a designer's rendering. `confirmed`, real dimensioned case. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_n0O47DdhATw_builtin_wardrobe_niche_technique|extraction note]]]

## Reconfiguring a Window/Balcony Opening

Real project, 79.5m² apartment: **bed and TV placement should be locked in before wardrobe placement, not after** — once the two highest-priority fixed elements (bed against the only workable wall, TV opposite it) are set, remaining wall space for storage may require reconfiguring the room's own window or balcony opening. Worked dimensioned sequence: offset 700mm from the right wall, +800mm to set the new opening width, offset 500mm from the left wall for window width, raise the balcony threshold to exactly 100mm above the screed, form a radiator niche 850mm down from the ceiling sized 5mm larger than the radiator grille's actual **back-mount** dimensions (not its visible decorative face — e.g. a 1000×600mm grille needs a 1005×605mm niche). Result: a 700mm-wide space fits a 600mm-deep wardrobe with clearance to spare against the window reveal. `confirmed`, precise dimensioned sequence. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_CHCB4KPupyc_bedroom_window_reconfig_for_wardrobe|extraction note]]]

**Bonus — bedroom electrical layout formula from the same source**: center the bed on the room's horizontal midline; symmetric independently-switched bedside lights on each side of the headboard; at each nightstand, group a light switch, a through-switch for the main room light, and 1-2 sockets; project the bed's centerline onto the opposite wall to center the TV on the same axis. Always add at least two outlets inside the balcony/window reveal at the final design step. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_n0O47DdhATw_builtin_wardrobe_niche_technique|n0O47DdhATw_builtin_ward]]]

## Closet-Behind-a-Door Depth-Maximizing Placement (added 2026-08-19, remainder-pool batch)

**Positioning a closet directly behind a door opening (rather than beside it), and pushing the opening itself to one extreme edge of the available wall run, maximizes the closet's own depth** — the entire remaining wall run beyond the opening's reveal goes to the closet, rather than being split between a centered opening and two smaller flanking segments. Worked result: 700mm maximum closet depth from this technique on a run that would have yielded much less with the opening centered. **Bonus idea from the same source**: a wardrobe's overhead storage tier (антресоль) can double as a home-network hub location — a cluster of outlets, every low-voltage cable pass-through, and general equipment storage, integrated into furniture rather than requiring a separate dedicated utility space. `single-account`, `unverified`. ## Wall-Shift Technique: Fixing an Undersized L-Shaped Storage Run (added 2026-08-19, remainder-pool batch)

**An L-shaped storage run that doesn't fit its allotted space (one leg extends into a door opening, the other leg is meaningfully too short) can sometimes be fixed by shifting the bounding wall outward without the corridor-width tradeoff that seems implied.** Documented real case: a 20cm wall shift simultaneously enlarged the wardrobe to full functional capacity (10-15 hanging garments vs. roughly half that before) **and** left the adjoining corridor at a still-generous 1.40m width — worth actually measuring before assuming a wall-shift necessarily costs corridor space. `single-account`, `unverified`.
