# Furniture — Wardrobe Common Mistakes (Real Client-Corroborated)

From a real client's own finished bedroom, walked through on camera — the strongest evidence tier for this topic. `confirmed` throughout. Part of [[14_Furniture/Wardrobes_and_Storage|Wardrobes & Storage]]. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mistakes_client_testimonial|extraction note]]]

- **Never install mirrored wardrobe panels where they'd reflect peripheral-vision motion visible from the bed.** Real case: mirrors were installed facing the room instead of inside the closet as designed, causing genuine nighttime discomfort (any movement produced an unsettling "someone moving beside me" effect); corrected to match the original design. **~65% of this practitioner's clients reportedly request matte/frosted panels over mirrored ones** specifically for this reason — self-reported, `unverified` percentage, but consistent with the underlying mechanism being real. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mistakes_client_testimonial|8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mist]]]
- **A walk-in closet needs its own mirror inside it — never rely on a mirror in another room.** See [[14_Furniture/analysis/Walkin_Closet_Configurations|Walk-In Closet Width by Configuration]] for the resulting 1.7m minimum-width rule. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mistakes_client_testimonial|8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mist]]]
- **Scratch-test glass wardrobe-door panels with a fingernail before ordering.** Cheap glass shows permanent scratch marks from ordinary door-opening contact — real client-reported defect on installed glass, from normal fingernail contact with short nails. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mistakes_client_testimonial|8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mist]]]
- **Never skip soft-close dampers on heavy glass sliding doors.** Without them, a forceful slide can shatter the panel against the end stop. A furniture installer pushed back against including dampers on one project; the practitioner overrode that and kept them. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mistakes_client_testimonial|8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mist]]]
- **Never use recessed spotlights inside an enclosed mirrored closet.** Even in large numbers, spotlights produce a harsh directional light cone that emphasizes every skin/clothing-fold shadow — use a glowing/luminous ceiling panel instead. Real installed mistake (installer defaulted to spotlights despite the plan) with real client-reported discomfort using the space afterward. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mistakes_client_testimonial|8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mist]]]
- **TV height is an inherent compromise, not a mistake with a clean fix.** Low enough for comfortable lying-flat viewing looks visually awkward when off (breaks symmetry with flanking millwork); high enough to look intentional forces semi-upright/angled viewing when lying down. No dimension resolves both — a planning caveat to set expectations around. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mistakes_client_testimonial|8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mist]]]
- **Never place a regularly-used work desk/computer setup inside a bedroom.** Even quiet keyboard use is reported disruptive to a sleeping partner over repeated regular use; acceptable only as a rarely-used decorative surface (e.g. opening a laptop once a month), never a daily workstation. Real client corroboration on camera, plus a stated recurring-complaint pattern across other clients (`single-account` for the recurrence claim). [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mistakes_client_testimonial|8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mist]]]

## Nine Buying/Ordering Mistakes (Sergey Kodolov, added 2026-08-25)

- **⚠️ Deep-shelf "napkin-holder" retrieval problem**: on a deep shelf, pulling one folded item off can drag several neighboring items with it (like pulling one napkin from a dispenser). Pull-out drawers avoid this — items come out bottom-to-top without disturbing neighbors. **Drawer material hierarchy for heavy contents**: cheap MDF/particle board (ДВП) can sag/break under load; ДСП (chipboard) is better; plywood (фанера) is better still — **but drawer-slide/runner quality matters more than drawer-box material**: cheap thin metal slides loosen over time (rattling, poor open/close) regardless of drawer rigidity.
- **Never pre-drill handle holes before handles are actually chosen.** Real case: a client ordered handles from England that looked fine but felt too heavy in hand once received, requiring a swap — handle weight can't be judged from photos alone, only by holding it. Finalize hardware selection before any drilling.
- **Coordinate wardrobe door type with ceiling lighting in the same design pass**: hinged (swing-open) doors can physically collide with ceiling spot lighting if the wardrobe and lighting were designed by separate uncoordinated parties — sliding doors avoid the conflict.
- **⚠️ Door-hinge economy is a false savings with a concrete failure chain**: a heavy door under-specified with 3 hinges instead of a recommended 5 will eventually shed a screw (sometimes tearing loose the plywood/ДСП with it) — starting as slight misalignment/poor closing, escalating to full hinge detachment and a visibly warped cabinet. A perpetually-slightly-open door can also leave a door-triggered light sensor stuck on continuously as a secondary symptom.
- **Design the wardrobe's rod/shelf mix around actual owned-clothing types** (short vs. long hanging items vs. folded-only storage), not a generic template.
- **Hardware-shipping quality signal**: reputable factories ship hinges/slides/connectors in individual boxes, not loose plastic bags (loose hardware in a bag can scratch furniture surfaces in transit). A cheap corner-bracket (уголок) fastening method instead of proper factory dowel/cam fittings is a similar low-quality-shortcut signal.
- **⚠️ Multi-quote "race to the bottom" can produce an unnoticed eurobolt (евровинт) downgrade**: comparing quotes and picking the cheapest can land you on furniture assembled with visible eurobolt heads covered by small round plastic caps instead of hidden-fastener joinery — a real, checkable but easy-to-miss visual quality signal for corner-cutting during assembly.
- **Level-checked assembly is mandatory**: a wardrobe assembled slightly out-of-level causes its own doors to sit permanently ajar or visibly crooked — brand-new furniture assembled poorly reads as old/improperly-made.
- **Plan and wire built-in lighting before assembly, not as a retrofit** — adding lighting to an already-assembled wardrobe (e.g. shelf-embedded auto-lighting) requires substantial disassembly. Rule of thumb for any late wardrobe modification generally: expect roughly **double (×2)** the original cost.
- **Built-in ironing board verdict: not recommended**, from direct installed experience — folding/pull-down ironing boards inside a wardrobe were tried and found inconvenient beyond very small items; a standalone garment steamer recommended instead.
- **Facade trend**: closed, minimalist facades are favored over open/exposed shelving, which "easily and always" creates visible clutter — closed facades judged durably fashionable, conditioned on a fade-resistant material/finish (e.g. UV-resistant natural wood, since unprotected wood veneer with milled grooves is fashionable but fades under sun exposure and needs real design judgment to fit the surrounding interior at typical ~30cm door widths).
