# Furniture — Wardrobe Common Mistakes (Real Client-Corroborated)

From a real client's own finished bedroom, walked through on camera — the strongest evidence tier for this topic. `confirmed` throughout. Part of [[14_Furniture/Wardrobes_and_Storage|Wardrobes & Storage]]. [source: [[11_Budget_and_Planning/_supporting/knowledge/sources/YT_8odGOTVAuIk_bedroom_mistakes_client_testimonial|extraction note]]]

- **Never install mirrored wardrobe panels where they'd reflect peripheral-vision motion visible from the bed.** Real case: mirrors were installed facing the room instead of inside the closet as designed, causing genuine nighttime discomfort (any movement produced an unsettling "someone moving beside me" effect); corrected to match the original design. **~65% of this practitioner's clients reportedly request matte/frosted panels over mirrored ones** specifically for this reason — self-reported, `unverified` percentage, but consistent with the underlying mechanism being real.
- **A walk-in closet needs its own mirror inside it — never rely on a mirror in another room.** See [[14_Furniture/analysis/Walkin_Closet_Configurations|Walk-In Closet Width by Configuration]] for the resulting 1.7m minimum-width rule.
- **Scratch-test glass wardrobe-door panels with a fingernail before ordering.** Cheap glass shows permanent scratch marks from ordinary door-opening contact — real client-reported defect on installed glass, from normal fingernail contact with short nails.
- **Never skip soft-close dampers on heavy glass sliding doors.** Without them, a forceful slide can shatter the panel against the end stop. A furniture installer pushed back against including dampers on one project; the practitioner overrode that and kept them.
- **Never use recessed spotlights inside an enclosed mirrored closet.** Even in large numbers, spotlights produce a harsh directional light cone that emphasizes every skin/clothing-fold shadow — use a glowing/luminous ceiling panel instead. Real installed mistake (installer defaulted to spotlights despite the plan) with real client-reported discomfort using the space afterward.
- **TV height is an inherent compromise, not a mistake with a clean fix.** Low enough for comfortable lying-flat viewing looks visually awkward when off (breaks symmetry with flanking millwork); high enough to look intentional forces semi-upright/angled viewing when lying down. No dimension resolves both — a planning caveat to set expectations around.
- **Never place a regularly-used work desk/computer setup inside a bedroom.** Even quiet keyboard use is reported disruptive to a sleeping partner over repeated regular use; acceptable only as a rarely-used decorative surface (e.g. opening a laptop once a month), never a daily workstation. Real client corroboration on camera, plus a stated recurring-complaint pattern across other clients (`single-account` for the recurrence claim).
