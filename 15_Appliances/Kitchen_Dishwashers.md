### Width

- **⚠️ 60cm is the worldwide standard; 45cm is a CIS-market-only shortened version.** Named
  example: a brand referred to as "AS" doesn't make 45cm units at all; SMEG's own top Italian
  lines are 60cm-only, and SMEG's 45cm CIS-market units are actually ordered under the SMEG
  brand name from Electrolux's factory. **New technology and innovation always debuts in 60cm
  units first**, only reaching 45cm units later if at all — this independently corroborates
  this project's own existing 60cm-standard preference on `Appliance_Preferences.md`.
- 45cm is only justified for a single occupant, someone who rarely cooks, or a short-term
  rental studio for 1-2 guests — buying 45cm to save incrementally across a renovation budget
  is a real false economy for anyone with regular multi-person use.

### Wash Quality — Three Real Drivers

- **⚠️ Driver #1 — internal working water pressure, an undisclosed spec with a named-brand
  comparison**: not the input water-supply pressure (which is published), but the *internal*
  operating pressure the unit washes with — a spec manufacturers don't publish; only
  specialist consultants who've researched it directly know it. **Named example**: MEA-brand
  base models (and derivative brands) wash at 0.8 bar; SMEG's top lines wash at 1.2 bar —
  ~40% higher pressure, translating directly to meaningfully better wash quality.
- **Driver #2 — max water heating temperature**: 70°C+ is adequate; 65°C is insufficient.
  Paradox: even a lower-pressure unit can wash well if its max temperature is 70°C+ —
  temperature can partly compensate for pressure.
- **Driver #3 — detergent chemistry** — see Chemistry section below.
- Secondary/indirect factors (spray-arm shape/trajectory variation, dedicated glass/stemware
  sprayers) are real but minor — the source's advice is to focus on the three drivers above.

### Drying

- **⚠️ Four-type taxonomy, with the genuinely important type last**: condensation drying
  (cheapest, most basic — leaves visible water-spot residue) — "medium uselessness" per the
  source's own words; turbo-drying (adds a fan, faster/more effective, slightly noisier);
  zeolite/mineral drying (expensive, mostly premium models — **not worth buying a dishwasher
  specifically for this feature**, per the source's own opinion). **The genuinely important
  4th type: auto-door-opening at cycle end** — vents residual steam/moisture automatically,
  replaces needing to check an end-of-cycle indicator, removes the risk of forgetting a
  finished load inside. Caveat: steam venting isn't harmful to surrounding
  cabinetry/countertop as long as it's undamaged and properly sealed.

### Programs and Noise

- **Programs**: only 4 core genuinely useful programs — eco, quick, intensive, automatic;
  everything else marketed as additional programs is mostly marketing.
- **⚠️ Noise, context-dependent importance**: less significant for an enclosed kitchen, much
  more significant for an open-plan kitchen-living room or studio. Baseline acceptable:
  42-44 dB; under 40 dB is meaningfully better. Actual real-world noise also depends heavily
  on installation/cabinetry-fit quality, not just the spec rating — check granular per-model
  reviews specifically mentioning noise, since a stated rating isn't guaranteed in practice.

### Detergent Chemistry

- **⚠️ Taxonomy with an explicit Moscow-specific salt rule**: tablets (all-in-one, may bundle
  detergent + rinse aid + salt) — most expensive, most convenient/effective; powder — washes
  well but needs correct manual dosing, may need separately-added rinse aid, and needs
  separately-added salt in hard-water areas; gel — the weakest performer, a compromise choice.
  Rinse aid reduces spotting/improves drying (often built into tablets). **Salt necessity is
  regional (water hardness varies significantly across Russia) — for Moscow specifically, salt
  is stated as mandatory**; with tablets it's often unnecessary since it's frequently built in.

### Price/Brand Tiers (60cm only — 45cm excluded per this source's own stance)

- **Economy: ≈30,000-50,000 RUB (≈$390-$650)** — a single dominant OEM factory (MEA/Midea)
  supplies 50+ rebadged brands off the same internal hardware (named: Samsung, Gorenje,
  Hansa, Coopersberg, and others) — a non-MDEA-branded unit is sometimes cheaper than the
  identical MDEA-branded model.
- **Comfort: from ≈75,000 RUB (≈$980)** — clear market leader SMEG, with a specific
  recommended model (transcribed as "Smac STL362 DQ," likely a SMEG model number — flagged for
  verification given transcript quality) called a genuinely good price/quality/feature
  balance; comfort-tier SMEG units are Polish-assembled base-spec models.
- **Premium: from ≈100,000 RUB (≈$1,300)** — SMEG's Italian-assembled flagship lines, plus a
  second named premium brand (transcribed as "ASCA," likely ASKO — flagged for verification).
  What premium buys: internal build/component quality, higher internal working pressure
  (better wash quality), rack customization flexibility, and control/temperature refinement.

### Mandatory vs. Nice-to-Have

- **Mandatory**: 60cm width; water heating temperature 70°C+; internal pressure ≥0.8 bar
  (economy) / ≥1 bar (comfort/premium); a third rack/tray for cutlery.
- **Nice-to-have**: auto-door-opening, disassemblable racks (ideally height-adjustable top
  rack too), varied spray-arm trajectories.

### Warranty and Reviews

- **⚠️ A real tradeoff with no universally correct answer**: a Chinese-branded unit often
  carries a 5-year official manufacturer warranty, while a European-branded unit often carries
  only a 1-year *retailer* warranty (not manufacturer) — real risk if that retailer closes.
  Recommends checking reviews for the exact model across multiple independent sites/
  marketplaces, not just one retailer's own product page (retailer-page reviews can be bought/
  inflated).
- **A named appliance-matching consulting service**: a personal-shopper-style paid service
  (analogous to a car-buying concierge) recommending specific units matched to stated
  budget/needs — **≈1,000 RUB (≈$10) per consultation**, negligible relative to a renovation
  or appliance budget.
